$$L_{pA}(t)=20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{p_{\rm rms}(t)}{p_{\rm ref}}\right),\qquad p_{\rm ref}=20\,\mu{\rm Pa},\qquad [L_{pA}]={\rm dB\ re\ }20\,\mu{\rm Pa}.$$
Assumption: $p_{\rm rms}$ is a short-window root-mean-square pressure in the occupied enclosure. Breakdown: as $p_{\rm rms}\to0$, the logarithm is singular; a measurement floor or A-weighting filter must replace the ideal equation. The equation also fails if the field is strongly nonstationary within the window.

Cadence-window variance is
$$\sigma_L^2(t)=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(L_{pA}(t_i)-\bar L_{pA}(t)\right)^2,\qquad [\sigma_L^2]={\rm dB^2}.$$
Assumption: the $N$ windows sample the same crowd state. Breakdown: if the crowd transitions between states during the window, the variance mixes regimes and is not a clean second-order statistic.

Cadence detectability is modeled with signal-detection and information operators:
$${\rm SNR}_{\rm dB}=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_s^2}{\sigma_n^2}\right),\qquad [\sigma_s^2]=[\sigma_n^2]={\rm Pa^2},$$
$$d'=\frac{\mu_s-\mu_n}{\sqrt{\tfrac12(\sigma_s^2+\sigma_n^2)}},\qquad [d']=1,$$
$$H(C)=-\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}p(c)\log_2 p(c)\ {\rm bits},\qquad I(C;Y)=H(C)-H(C|Y)\ {\rm bits},$$
$$C_{\rm AWGN}=B\log_2(1+{\rm SNR})\ {\rm bits/s},\qquad [B]={\rm Hz}.$$
Breakdown: $\sigma_n^2=0$ gives ${\rm SNR}_{\rm dB}\to+\infty$; $\sigma_s^2=0$ gives ${\rm SNR}_{\rm dB}\to-\infty$; $d'$ is undefined if both variances vanish. The Shannon capacity form assumes continuous-input AWGN and is only an upper-bound analogy for a human cadence channel.

Reverberation in the dome is approximated by the Sabine diffuse-field relation
$$T_{60}=\frac{24\ln 10\,V}{cA}\approx0.161\frac{V}{A}\ {\rm s},\qquad [V]={\rm m^3},\ [A]={\rm m^2\ sabins},\ [c]={\rm m/s}.$$
Assumption: diffuse field, roughly uniform absorption, occupied dome approximated by an effective $A$. Breakdown: $A\to0$ makes $T_{60}\to\infty$; the diffuse assumption fails in a large occupied stadium with coupled seating decks and distributed crowd absorption. The cadence signal is then a convolution with a non-exponential impulse response, not a single exponential decay.

Derrick Brown’s interior push is formalized as
$$F(t)=ma(t)\ {\rm N},\qquad J=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}F(t)\,dt\ {\rm N\,s},\qquad \bar F=\frac{J}{\Delta t}\ {\rm N},$$
$$p_c(t)=\frac{F_\perp(t)}{A_c(t)}\ {\rm Pa},\qquad \sigma_c(t)=\frac{F_\perp(t)}{A_c(t)}\ {\rm Pa},\qquad \varepsilon(t)=\frac{\Delta L(t)}{L_0},\ [\varepsilon]=1.$$
Linear elastic contact assumes $\sigma_c=E\varepsilon$ with $[E]={\rm Pa}$; that assumption breaks under plastic deformation, pad compression, or joint-stability failure. Breakdown: $A_c\to0$ makes contact pressure singular; real contact has a finite minimum area and tissue/pad nonlinearity.

Pocket-pressure hazard before release is
$$\Pr(\text{pressure before release})=1-\exp\!\left[-\int_0^{T_r}\lambda(t)\,dt\right],\qquad [\lambda]={\rm s^{-1}},\ [T_r]={\rm s}.$$
Assumption: $\lambda(t)$ is a nonnegative hazard for interior pressure and $T_r$ is the QB release time. Breakdown: if $\lambda(t)$ is nonstationary or the release time is censored by scramble/throwaway, the simple exponential survival form fails.

Belief recursion for the offense-defense interaction is
$$P(H_i\mid E)=\frac{P(E\mid H_i)P(H_i)}{\sum_jP(E\mid H_j)P(H_j)},\qquad [P]=1,$$
where $H_i$ is a defensive hypothesis such as “late shift” or “simulated pressure,” and $E$ is pre-snap evidence. Breakdown: if the prior is degenerate or the likelihood is misspecified, recursive theory-of-mind depth adds false confidence rather than information.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is straightforward: Sabine’s enclosed-room acoustics explains why a dome converts crowd noise into a smeared field; signal-detection theory explains why cadence is a threshold problem rather than a raw-loudness problem; information theory explains why a silent count is a low-entropy code that can be learned by the defense; and sports biomechanics explains why interior pressure is an impulse-and-posture problem. The “retrocausal” element is inferential, not literal: the future game state shapes the pre-kickoff prior. If New Orleans plays with a lead, the crowd’s cadence-window variance is likely to rise; if Carolina plays with a lead, the crowd may quiet and the offense’s cadence channel may reopen. Thus pre-kickoff priors should be conditioned on game-script feedback, not treated as fixed stadium constants.

The historical trap is reification of a single noise number. Superdome crowd noise has been treated as a monolithic “12th man” claim, but the useful variable is the joint distribution of level and variance across cadence windows. A second multi-week trap is small-sample penalty counting: false starts are rare, referee crews differ, and silent-count usage changes week to week. A third trap is interior-pressure box-score bias: sacks are rare and noisy, while hurries and altered release times are closer to the mechanism. A fourth trap is opponent adjustment: a line that handled interior pressure against a weak opponent may not replicate against Brown’s alignment and first-step quickness. The correct genealogy therefore runs from room acoustics to temporal coding to protection rules to biomechanical force transfer, with each link independently falsifiable.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when the offense’s normal cadence policy collapses under noise and pressure. The policy $\pi(a\mid s)$ shifts toward high-prior, low-entropy habits: earlier silent count, guard taps, hand signals, and max protection. The defense models this regression and counter-traps with late shifts, stem changes, and simulated pressure. The offense’s counter-trap is snap-time randomization: vary the cadence period, use a hard count when the crowd dips, and keep a hot-route answer. The clock is the binding constraint:
$$t_{\rm snap}=t_0+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_j,\qquad \Pr(\text{delay})=\Pr(t_{\rm snap}>t_{\rm pc}),$$
where $[t_{\rm snap}]=[t_{\rm pc}]={\rm s}$. If cadence variance increases the variance of $\sum\tau_j$, the offense can trade false-start risk for delay risk. That trade is the recursive crux: the defense wants the offense to snap on a predictable silent-count rhythm; the offense wants the defense to reveal its late shift before the snap; the QB must model the defender modeling the QB’s model. The stable counter is not “louder” or “faster” alone, but a lower-variance snap code with enough entropy to avoid defensive timing.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Weather veto: the Superdome is an enclosed fixed-roof venue, so outdoor wind, precipitation, and temperature do not directly boundary-condition the field. The only weather-adjacent operator is indoor air temperature through the sound speed
$$c=\sqrt{\gamma R_{\rm specific}T},\qquad [c]={\rm m/s},\ [T]={\rm K}.$$
HVAC variation changes $c$ weakly; it cannot by itself veto the cadence-variance mechanism unless roof integrity or HVAC failure creates a non-uniform field. Physical veto: if measured cadence-window $\sigma_L^2$ is below the operational threshold and the cadence SNR is high, the noise-variance branch is vetoed. Biomechanical veto: if Brown is inactive, double-teamed at a high rate, or his delivered impulse $J$ is below the offensive line’s postural-stability impulse $J_{\rm stab}$, the interior-push branch is vetoed. Cross-layer veto proof requires both branches to fail: low cadence variance plus low interior pressure means the interaction thesis is not supported; high cadence variance plus high interior pressure means the mechanism is live, but still conditional on the offense’s silent-count and release-clock execution.
