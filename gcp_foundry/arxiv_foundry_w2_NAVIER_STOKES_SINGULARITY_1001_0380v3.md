The paper's operative move is not the force model, it is the choice of observable. Prior blowup results (the four cited works MUK, MP, P, CT) fail on compressible Euler with pressure because they are built on energy or virial functionals, which require mass positivity and produce signed, non-coercive pressure contributions that cannot be closed. This paper instead integrates against the geometric weight $r$ over a *frozen* finite domain, which is licensed by the compact-support hypothesis: $\rho(t,r)=V(t,r)=0$ for $r\ge R$ for all $t$ in the existence interval. Compact support does two things simultaneously. It makes $[0,R]$ time-invariant, so no Reynolds transport boundary term is generated. And it forces every field to vanish at $r=R$, which annihilates the boundary terms produced by integration by parts.

Reconstructing the mechanism (my derivation, constants may differ from the paper's normalization): the radial momentum equation in enthalpy form is $\partial_t V + V\partial_r V + \partial_r h = \delta\, g_+$ with $h = \frac{K\gamma}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}$ and $g_+ = \sigma_d M(t,r)/r^{N-1}$, $M(t,r)=\int_0^r s^{N-1}\rho\,ds$. Multiply by $r$ and integrate. The inertial term is the whole engine:

$-\int_0^R r V \partial_r V\, dr = -\int_0^R \tfrac{r}{2}\partial_r(V^2)dr = \big[-\tfrac{r}{2}V^2\big]_0^R + \tfrac12\int_0^R V^2 dr = \tfrac12\|V\|_{L^2(0,R)}^2 \ge 0$

using $V(R)=0$. Cauchy–Schwarz on the finite interval closes the loop: $\|V\|_{L^2}^2 \ge \big(\int_0^R rV\,dr\big)^2/\int_0^R r^2 dr = 3H^2/R^3$. So $\dot H \ge c\,H^2/R^3$ with $c>0$ — a Riccati super-linear inequality whose solution $H(t) \ge H_0/(1 - cH_0 t/R^3)$ diverges at $T^* \le R^3/(cH_0)$, matching the paper's $T=R^3/(2H_0)$ for $c=2$. Two consequences dominate everything downstream.

First, the pressure term does not resist here. $-\int_0^R r\,\partial_r h\, dr = -[rh]_0^R + \int_0^R h\, dr = \int_0^R h\, dr \ge 0$ precisely because $h(R)=0$. Pressure, which every prior method had to fight, is *non-negative* in this functional. That is the real content of "novel integration method," and it is why $\gamma>1$ is included rather than excluded. Second, the repulsive term $+\delta\int_0^R r g_+ dr = \delta\sigma_d\int_0^R r^{2-N}M\,dr \ge 0$ is also non-negative. Both stabilizers push the same direction as the nonlinearity.

That yields the gray-zone observation worth flagging hard: with $H_0>0$ the theorem is least surprising in the repulsive case, and the terminal object is not a density spike. If $\dot H>0$ persistently, the fluid is accelerating outward without bound, so the ansatz $\text{supp}\subset[0,R]$, $V(R)=0$ is what fails. For repulsive Euler–Poisson the breakdown is kinematic — support-boundary detachment or a vacuum core opening — not gravitational collapse. The theorem's real claim is that the $C^1$ solution class cannot be continued past $T^*$; it is a noncontinuation result, and the physically legible terminal state is cavitation with a divergent velocity gradient at the vacuum edge. Meanwhile the hypothesis $H_0>0$ selects the outward branch outright, so the inward-compression branch that motivates "collapse" intuitions is outside the theorem's reach.
