No source pages were supplied. Therefore no [PAGE N] markers or verbatim quotations are possible. The following are formal analytical operators with explicit assumptions, units, and breakdown regimes.

Let \(\mathcal R\) be a finite set of defensive rush states, and let \(p_R(r)\) be the probability mass on rush state \(r\). The pressure entropy is
\[
H(R)=-\sum_{r\in\mathcal R} p_R(r)\log_2 p_R(r)\quad \text{[bits]},
\]
with the convention \(0\log_2 0=0\). The range is
\[
0\le H(R)\le \log_2|\mathcal R|\quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
A plug-in estimate from \(n\) charted dropbacks has finite-sample bias; under a multinomial approximation the Miller–Madow-style correction is on the order of
\[
\widehat{\mathrm{bias}}(\hat H)\approx \frac{|\mathcal R|-1}{2n\ln 2}\quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
This is not a football ratio; it is an estimator correction.

Let \(L\) be the pre-snap look available to the quarterback, including safety rotation, box count, leverage, and motion response. The conditional rush entropy is
\[
H(R|L)=-\sum_{\ell}p_L(\ell)\sum_{r}p_{R|L}(r|\ell)\log_2 p_{R|L}(r|\ell)\quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
The mutual information between look and rush is
\[
I(L;R)=H(R)-H(R|L)\quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
Simulated pressure is informationally meaningful when \(H(R)\) is high and \(H(R|L)\) remains high after the pre-snap look, equivalently when \(I(L;R)\) is small relative to \(H(R)\). If motion changes the look from \(L\) to \(L'\), the motion-induced clarification is
\[
\Delta I=I(L';R)-I(L;R)\quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
Positive \(\Delta I\) means the motion helped the offense diagnose the rush; negative \(\Delta I\) means the defense used the motion to muddy the read.

The defensive utility is not entropy alone. It is
\[
U_D=-\mathbb E[\mathrm{EPA}_{\mathrm{off}}\mid R,L]\quad \text{[points]},
\]
where \(\mathrm{EPA}_{\mathrm{off}}\) is offensive expected points added in points. A high-\(H(R|L)\) call with \(U_D\le 0\) is not a successful pressure design. The offense’s counter is to choose motion, protection, and route timing that lower \(H(R|L)\) before the rush arrives or that lower \(\tau_{\mathrm{avail}}\).

Assume a finite quarterback decision channel of capacity \(C\) in bits per second. A first-order required processing time is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{H(R|L)}{C}\quad \text{[s]}.
\]
The crisis regime is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{req}}\gt \tau_{\mathrm{avail}}\quad \text{[s]},
\]
where \(\tau_{\mathrm{avail}}\) is the time before contact or throw constraint. This is an information-theoretic abstraction, not a claim that quarterback cognition is literally a Shannon channel; it is useful only as a bounded-rationality constraint.

Weather physics. For dry air, density is
\[
\rho=\frac{p}{R_s T}\quad [\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}],
\]
with \(p\) in pascals, \(T\) in kelvin, and \(R_s=287.05\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\). Aerodynamic drag on a ball is
\[
\vec F_d=-\frac12 \rho C_d A \|\vec v_b-\vec w\|(\vec v_b-\vec w)\quad [\mathrm{N}],
\]
where \(\vec v_b\) is ball velocity, \(\vec w\) is wind velocity, \(C_d\) is dimensionless drag coefficient, and \(A\) is cross-sectional area in \(\mathrm{m^2}\). For a crosswind component \(w_y\) and small ball lateral velocity, the lateral acceleration approximation is
\[
a_y\approx \frac{\rho C_d A}{2m}\|\vec v_b-\vec w\|\,w_y\quad [\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}],
\]
and the lateral displacement over flight time \(T\) is
\[
y(T)\approx \frac12 a_y T^2\quad [\mathrm{m}].
\]
A deep throw is weather-vetoed when
\[
|y(T)|\gt R_c\quad [\mathrm{m}],
\]
where \(R_c\) is the receiver’s effective catch-radius adjustment envelope in meters. For a sealed ball of fixed volume and fixed gas amount, cold reduces absolute pressure approximately as
\[
P_2=P_1\frac{T_2}{T_1}\quad [\mathrm{Pa}],
\]
which is an operational grip and compression constraint, not a betting variable.

Biomechanical veto. For a cut with local speed \(v\) and radius of curvature \(r\), the required lateral acceleration is
\[
a_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{v^2}{r}\quad [\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}].
\]
The surface can supply at most
\[
a_{\max}=\mu_s g\quad [\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}],
\]
where \(\mu_s\) is the dimensionless static friction coefficient and \(g=9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). A pass-rusher bend or offensive cut is biomechanically vetoed when
\[
a_{\mathrm{req}}\gt \mu_s g.
\]
At Lambeau in cold or wet conditions, lowered \(\mu_s\) narrows the admissible cut set for edge rushers and receivers.

Exact singularity and breakdown regimes: \(H(R)\) is undefined if the probability model is not normalized; \(H(R|L)\) is undefined for a look \(\ell\) with \(p_L(\ell)=0\) when conditioning is interpreted literally; \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)=\sum_r p(r)\log_2[p(r)/q(r)]\) diverges to \(+\infty\) if \(q(r)=0\) and \(p(r)\gt 0\); the estimator \(\hat H\) breaks down at \(n=0\); \(\tau_{\mathrm{req}}\) diverges as \(C\to 0\); the ideal-gas density relation fails near liquefaction and is not valid as \(T\to 0\ \mathrm{K}\); the friction veto degenerates at \(\mu_s=0\); and the weather displacement veto is triggered at the crosswind threshold \(w_c\) defined implicitly by \(|y(T;w_c)|=R_c\). These are the exact regimes where the model must stop and defer to measured tracking, surface testing, or active-status data.

The football genealogy runs through the zone-blitz and fire-zone families: Dick LeBeau’s zone blitz, Buddy Ryan’s pressure philosophy, Jim Johnson’s simulated pressures, and Bill Belichick’s pattern-match disguise. Brian Flores belongs to the Belichick-style lineage of showing pressure and changing the post-snap picture. The MIN at GB crucible is therefore a collision between a pressure-disguise genealogy and a motion-and-timing genealogy.

The retrocausal element is this: future scouting changes the present call. If Flores shows a high rate of simulated A-gap pressure on third down in prior weeks, LaFleur’s staff prepares screens, quick slants, and protection slides before the game. That future preparation retroacts on the present value of the simulated pressure. Likewise, if LaFleur’s offense has shown a tendency to answer pressure with play-action, Flores can prepare a sim-pressure drop designed to intercept the play-action window. The current call is selected partly to create a future tendency that the next opponent will overread. This is the multi-week trap structure.

Three multi-week traps are especially relevant. First, the pressure-tendency trap: a defense that lives on simulated pressure on late downs trains opponents to use empty formations, quick game, and hard counts; the defense then overcorrects and loses coverage integrity. Second, the cold-weather overreaction trap: after a windy game, an offense may abandon vertical concepts entirely, allowing the defense to load the box and compress the intermediate field. Third, the clock-and-film trap: a successful pressure week invites repetition, but repetition produces film, and film produces counters. The counter-trap is not a single call; it is a sequence that makes the opponent’s habitual answer expensive. The genealogy therefore predicts not a fixed outcome but a shifting equilibrium: Flores’s deception value decays with motion and film, while LaFleur’s motion value decays if the defense can simulate pressure without declaring.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Level 0: Flores wants pressure to disrupt LaFleur’s timing; LaFleur wants quick game and motion to defeat pressure. Level 1: LaFleur knows Flores pressures on late downs, so he scripts motion, play-action, and quick throws. Level 2: Flores knows LaFleur expects pressure, so he shows pressure and drops the defensive front into coverage. Level 3: LaFleur knows Flores may simulate and drop, so he uses hard counts, screens, max protection, and checkdowns to punish the drop. Level 4 and beyond are mostly unverifiable without in-game evidence; the recursion saturates when the next belief cannot be tested before the snap.

Crisis habit regression is the key failure mode. Under stress, Flores may regress to the pressure call that worked in prior weeks, and LaFleur may regress to the outside-zone and play-action concepts that are his offensive signature. The counter-trap is to make that regression expensive: bait the pressure with a formation that invites it, then throw behind it; or show pressure and drop, then rally to the quick throw. The clock is the adjudicator because it prevents both coaches from waiting for perfect information. Pre-snap motion consumes time, hard counts consume time, and cold/wind reduce the margin for long developing routes. When the play clock and the pass rush compress the decision window, the offense collapses to rule-based answers: hot route, checkdown, protection slide, or scramble. The defense collapses to rule-based answers: rush lane discipline, coverage rotation, and tackle.

The recursive equilibrium is therefore not “Flores wins” or “LaFleur wins.” It is a conditional: if LaFleur’s motion rate and quick-game timing keep \(\tau_{\mathrm{req}}\) below \(\tau_{\mathrm{avail}}\), the simulated-pressure entropy is partially neutralized. If Flores’s sim-pressure looks keep \(I(L;R)\) low after motion, the offense’s diagnosis fails and the pressure value rises. If the weather vetoes deep answers, LaFleur’s counter-trap narrows to intermediate and quick concepts, which makes Flores’s coverage disguises more important and his pressure less necessary. The coach who wins the recursive layer is the one whose habit regression is least exploitable under the clock.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudication rule: a tactic is admissible only if it survives the intersection
\[
\mathcal A=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{info}}\cap\mathcal A_{\mathrm{weather}}\cap\mathcal A_{\mathrm{biomech}}.
\]
If \(\mathcal A=\varnothing\), the call is vetoed regardless of coaching intent. This is the proof structure: each layer supplies an inequality, and any failed inequality removes the tactic from the admissible set.

Information veto: simulated pressure requires residual rush entropy after the pre-snap look. If motion raises \(I(L';R)\) so that \(H(R|L')\) falls below the quarterback’s required diagnosis threshold, the deception is informationally vetoed. Exact breakdown occurs when the defense’s model assigns zero probability to the realized rush state and the offense’s posterior KL divergence diverges; in practical charting terms, the defense has been fully diagnosed.

Weather veto: a deep throw is vetoed when the crosswind displacement exceeds the catch-radius envelope,
\[
|y(T)|\gt R_c.
\]
A long kick or vertical concept is similarly vetoed when the wind-induced lateral error exceeds the operational correction envelope. Cold reduces ball pressure under fixed-volume assumptions and lowers grip margin; if the gauge pressure falls below the operational threshold, the ball-handling layer vetoes certain one-hand and high-velocity concepts. These are physical constraints, not psychological ones.

Biomechanical veto: a pass-rusher’s edge bend or a receiver’s cut is vetoed when
\[
a_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{v^2}{r}\gt \mu_s g.
\]
Cold or wet Lambeau surfaces lower \(\mu_s\), so the same cut that is admissible in dry conditions can become a slip. The exact breakdown is \(\mu_s=0\), where no lateral acceleration can be supplied. This veto is asymmetric: it can remove Flores’s edge simulation while leaving interior power pressure admissible, and it can remove LaFleur’s wide-zone cutback while leaving quick interior routes admissible.

The adjudication is conservative: the strongest veto wins. If the crosswind veto removes the deep answer, LaFleur cannot force the vertical concept merely because the information layer says it would punish a pressure look. If the friction veto removes the edge bend, Flores cannot rely on simulated edge pressure merely because the entropy model likes it. The surviving calls are those that are informationally deceptive, physically controllable, and biomechanically stable. At Lambeau in cold and wind, the surviving set is likely tilted toward quick game, intermediate throws, interior pressure, power run, play-action, and clock-controlling concepts. The vetoed set is tilted toward long free-flight throws, slow-developing vertical routes, and wide-edge cuts that require high friction. This is the cross-layer proof: the weather and biomechanics do not merely modify probabilities; they delete tactics from the admissible set.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR here means reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards style adjudication: each scenario has a binary condition and a post-game verifiable prediction. Reward is \(+1\) if the condition and predicted direction both hold, \(0\) otherwise. These are counterfactual vetoes because the tactic is expected to fail or to be neutralized when the condition fires.

Scenario 1, motion-deception veto. Condition: Green Bay uses pre-snap motion on a high share of dropbacks, for example motion on at least 40 percent of pass attempts, and the average time to throw is at or below 2.6 seconds. Prediction: Minnesota’s residual rush entropy \(H(R|L')\) falls after motion, Flores’s simulated-pressure rate falls relative to his early-down baseline, and quick-game efficiency rises. Verification: chart motion, pressure, time to throw, and EPA. Counterfactual: if motion rate stays below 25 percent and time to throw exceeds 3.0 seconds, the veto does not fire and the simulated-pressure entropy effect should persist. This scenario tests whether LaFleur’s motion-and-timing genealogy actually collapses Flores’s information advantage.

Scenario 2, crosswind deep-pass veto. Condition: the measured crosswind component at the stadium exceeds the threshold \(w_c\) defined by \(|y(T;w_c)|=R_c\) for the relevant throw distance. Prediction: deep attempts decline relative to the calm-weather baseline, and forced deep attempts show lower completion probability and larger tracking error. Verification: compare intended air yards, tracked ball landing point, and receiver adjustment. Counterfactual: if the crosswind is below \(0.7w_c\), the deep-shot veto does not fire and vertical concepts remain admissible. This scenario tests whether Lambeau wind physically deletes the deep answer rather than merely making it less frequent.

Scenario 3, cold-surface cut veto. Condition: surface friction is below the measured or charted threshold \(\mu_{s,\mathrm{crit}}\), or charting shows repeated slip events on edge cuts. Prediction: Flores’s edge-bend pressure wins decline, while interior simulated pressure and power rush share rise; LaFleur’s wide-zone cutback efficiency declines, while quick interior and play-action concepts survive. Verification: pass-rush win chart by rush lane, slip events, and run-concept efficiency by gap. Counterfactual: if the surface is dry and \(\mu_s\) remains above threshold, the edge-bend veto does not fire and Flores can keep the wider pressure menu. This scenario tests whether biomechanics, not intention, determines which pressure and run concepts remain admissible.

All three vetoes are falsifiable and can be checked without any betting market. If the conditions fire and the predicted direction fails, the model loses reward and must be revised. If the conditions do not fire, the model must not claim a veto it did not earn.
