The primitives in Shalizi's *Advanced Data Analysis from an Elementary Point of View* (freely circulated draft; chapter numbering drifts between versions, so I cite by result rather than by section number) reduce to one claim iterated across the whole book: **every inferential operator is a composition of an assumption layer and an estimation layer, and only the estimation layer is auditable from data.** Everything else is commentary on that decomposition.

Multi-hop chain. Hop 1: a DAG $G$ over variables $V$ is not a causal object; it is a *factorization license*. The Markov condition asserts $p(v) = \prod_{i} p(v_i \mid \mathrm{pa}_i)$, units: a probability density (dimensionless if $V$ is discrete; $[\text{unit of }v_i]^{-1}$ per factor if continuous). Hop 2: the map $p \mapsto G$ is massively many-to-one. Two DAGs are Markov equivalent iff they share a skeleton and share v-structures (Verma–Pearl). Consequence: the observational likelihood is *constant* across an entire equivalence class, so the Fisher information matrix for edge orientation on non-v-structure edges is exactly zero. Not small. Zero. This is the first singularity condition: $\mathcal{I}_{\theta\theta} = 0$ implies the Cramér–Rao bound $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta) \ge \mathcal{I}^{-1}$ diverges, and no sample size repairs it. Hop 3: the do-operator is algebra on the truncated factorization, $p(v \setminus \{x\} \mid \mathrm{do}(X=x)) = \prod_{i: V_i \ne X} p(v_i \mid \mathrm{pa}_i)\big|_{X=x}$. Pearl's three rules plus the completeness results (Huang–Valtorta; Shpitser–Pearl's ID algorithm) make identifiability a *graph-theoretic decision procedure*: ID returns either a closed-form functional or FAIL. Hop 4: identifiability is therefore binary and data-free. A billion observations do not move FAIL to a formula. Hop 5: the estimation layer is a separate, quantitative failure governed by minimax theory. For $f$ in a Hölder class $\Sigma(\beta, L)$ on $[0,1]^d$, $\inf_{\hat f}\sup_{f}\mathbb{E}\|\hat f - f\|_2^2 \asymp n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+d)}$ (Stone). For kernel regression with $\beta = 2$, the optimal bandwidth is $h^\star \asymp n^{-1/(4+d)}$ and the MSE is $\asymp n^{-4/(4+d)}$. Hop 6: the two failures compound multiplicatively in practice. Identification failure gives you a well-estimated answer to the wrong question; estimation failure gives you a badly-estimated answer to the right one. The gray zone is *partial* positivity violation, where identification technically holds but the propensity $p(x \mid \mathrm{pa})$ has infimum near zero, so the estimator's variance explodes while its point estimate still looks plausible. This is the dangerous region, because nothing in the output distinguishes it from a well-behaved fit.

The deepest primitive is the one Shalizi returns to most often: **cross-validation certifies predictive risk, and predictive risk is invariant under relabeling of the feature space in a way that causal validity is not.** Let $\varphi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{X}'$ be a measurable bijection and let the hypothesis class be closed under composition with $\varphi$ (true for any universal nonparametric class). Then $\inf_{f \in \mathcal{H}} \mathbb{E}[L(Y, f(\varphi(X)))] = \inf_{f \in \mathcal{H}} \mathbb{E}[L(Y, f(X))]$, while $p(y \mid \mathrm{do}(X=x))$ is not invariant, because $\varphi$ can fold a confounder into the regressor. Therefore any model-selection procedure built on predictive risk is *provably blind* to the distinction between a confounded and an unconfounded representation. This is not a soft caveat. It is an invariance argument, and it is the single most load-bearing result for applied sports analytics.

Level-1: the defensive coordinator best-responds, $\sigma^1 \in \arg\max_{\sigma} \mathbb{E}_{\sigma \times \sigma^0}[U_{DC}]$. His optimal response to a known comfort call is *tendency bait*: present a pre-snap picture that invites $a_0$ and is defended by the structure that beats $a_0$. The bait is only profitable if he can distinguish $a_0$ from the coordinator's other high-frequency calls, which requires reading formation and motion — observable pre-snap information.

Level-2: the quarterback best-responds to $\sigma^1$. Here the recursion breaks, and it breaks for a physical reason, not a strategic one. The quarterback operates under a hard time budget. His internal interval estimate obeys scalar expectancy: $\sigma_T = k\,T$, with Weber fraction $k \in [0.05, 0.15]$ (dimensionless). If pressure arrives at $T_p$ seconds and his internal estimate is $\hat T \sim \mathcal{N}(T, (kT)^2)$, then

$$\Pr[\text{forced throw}] = \Phi\!\left(\frac{T_p - T}{kT}\right),$$

$\Phi$ the standard normal CDF, argument dimensionless. The singularity: the argument depends on $T$ only through the *ratio* $T_p/T$. As $T \to 0$, absolute timing noise vanishes but so does the mean, and the coefficient of variation is invariant. A model that says "quicker quarterback, fewer mistakes" is fitting a term that the neuroscience says is scale-free. The hierarchy does not converge to a fixed point; it terminates at a physiological constraint, and the terminal node is a single-agent constrained optimization, not a game.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Veto 1 — Aerodynamic (spin, not range).** Define the dimensionless drag parameter $\varepsilon = \dfrac{\rho\, C_d\, A\, L}{2m}$. Units: $(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}})(\mathrm{m^2})(\mathrm{m})(\mathrm{kg})^{-1}$ = dimensionless. With $\rho = 1.29\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ (0 °C), $C_d \approx 0.2$ (clean spiral), $A \approx 3.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^2}$, $L = 30\ \mathrm{m}$, $m = 0.41\ \mathrm{kg}$: $\varepsilon \approx 0.033$. Range admits the perturbation $R = R_{\text{vac}}(1 - c\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon^2))$, $c = O(1)$. Cold air raises $\rho$ by $\approx 5.5\%$ versus 15 °C, moving range by $\approx 0.18\%$. **The naive model is essentially right about distance.** The veto is elsewhere: gyroscopic precession rate $\Omega_p = M_a / (I\omega)$ with $M_a \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A_{\text{ref}} \ell\, C_M \alpha$ ($\alpha$ in rad), so $\Omega_p \propto \omega^{-1}$, units $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Spin decays as $\omega(t) = \omega_0 e^{-t/\tau}$, $\tau = I/(c_\omega \rho A_{\text{ref}} \ell^3 v)$, units: $(\mathrm{kg\,m^2})/(\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}) = \mathrm{s}$. **Singularity: as $\omega \to 0$, yaw amplitude diverges.** Deep-drop concepts with long time-of-flight therefore carry a fat-tailed placement error that correlates with exactly the situational covariates the surface model uses. Breakdown condition: $\varepsilon \gtrsim 0.1$ (tumbling, $C_d \approx 0.55$), where the perturbation series is no longer controlled.

**Veto 2 — Acoustic (critical distance).** Crowd noise is a diffuse field, not a scalar. Critical distance $r_c \approx 0.057\sqrt{QV/T_{60}}$ (metric: $V$ in $\mathrm{m^3}$, $T_{60}$ in s, $r_c$ in m). For $V = 10^5\ \mathrm{m^3}$, $T_{60} = 5\ \mathrm{s}$, $Q=1$: $r_c \approx 8\ \mathrm{m}$. Beyond $r_c$ the reverberant term dominates and attenuation with distance stops helping — the $6\ \mathrm{dB}$ per doubling of the free-field law is void. Further, masking is frequency-selective: crowd spectral mass sits below $\sim 1\ \mathrm{kHz}$ while the phonemes distinguishing cadence words carry information at $2$–$5\ \mathrm{kHz}$. A broadband dB penalty mis-weights the masking by an unknown factor. Counteracting this, the Lombard effect raises vocal source level $\approx 0.3$–$0.6\ \mathrm{dB}$ per dB of speech-band noise, partially self-compensating. **The naive additive-noise term has the wrong sign of error in one regime and the wrong magnitude in the other.**

**Veto 3 — Statistical (invariance).** As derived in the trace: cross-validated predictive risk is invariant under measurable bijection of the feature space for a universal class, while $p(y\mid \mathrm{do}(x))$ is not. Any procedure that selects a representation by predictive score is structurally incapable of certifying causal content. No amount of data repairs this; it is an invariance, not a variance.

**Scenario A — Markov-equivalence trap.** Train on observational snaps to predict a coordinator's fourth-down choice from field position, margin, clock. Reward is verifiable: the actual decision. Surface model achieves high accuracy via a strong margin coefficient. Veto: the coefficient is confounded by the latent coaching-tree prior; the DAG and its edge-reversed twin are Markov equivalent, so the likelihood cannot separate them. *Verifiable test:* exploit mid-sample coordinator changes as interventions; a causal coefficient is stable across regimes, a confounded one is not. *Falsifiable Sunday prediction:* in games featuring a coordinator in his first season under a new head coach, the fitted margin coefficient will differ in sign or exceed a 40% magnitude change relative to his prior-season estimate — while predictive accuracy stays within 3 percentage points. If the coefficient is stable, the confounding claim is false.

**Scenario B — Positivity collapse.** Fit outcome models on third-down snaps in base defensive personnel; query the empty-set five-wide counterfactual. The propensity $p(\text{empty} \mid \text{down}, \text{distance}, \text{score})$ has near-zero infimum, so ID does not FAIL outright but the effective sample size $\mathrm{ESS} = (\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$ (dimensionless, $w_i$ the importance weights) collapses. *Verifiable test:* bin by overlap decile and compute a reliability diagram. *Falsifiable prediction:* calibration slope in the lowest overlap decile will fall below 0.6 while exceeding 0.9 in the top decile, and $\mathrm{ESS}$ in the bottom decile will be under 5% of nominal $n$. Flat calibration across deciles falsifies the veto.

**Scenario C — Dimensional learning-curve exponent.** Hold the situational vector at $d = 18$ and subsample training data by factors of two. Under a Lipschitz target ($\beta = 1$), the minimax exponent is $-2\beta/(2\beta+d) = -2/20 = -0.10$: each doubling of data buys $\approx 6.7\%$ error reduction. *Verifiable test:* regress $\log \mathrm{MSE}$ on $\log n$. *Falsifiable prediction:* measured slope in $[-0.18, -0.05]$. A slope steeper than $-0.3$ falsifies the dimensionality claim and implies the situational vector is heavily redundant — itself a verifiable, useful finding. A slope flatter than $-0.05$ implies an identification failure dominates, not a sample-size failure, and no data collection will help.
