A functional is critical iff it is invariant under $\mathcal{S}_\lambda$; direct computation gives the Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin line: $\|u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}$ is scale-invariant iff $\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1$ (with $3<q\le\infty$), because $\|u_\lambda(t)\|_{L^q_x}=\lambda^{1-3/q}\|u(\lambda^2 t)\|_{L^q_x}$ and $\|u_\lambda\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}=\lambda^{\,1-3/q-2/p}\|u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}$. The endpoint $q=3$ is the strongest critical norm. Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák (2003) proved $u\in L^\infty_tL^3_x\Rightarrow$ regularity, so any blowup — Type I or Type II — forces $\|u(t)\|_{L^3}\to\infty$, but with an extraordinarily slow quantitative lower bound of double-logarithmic type (Tao, 2019, sharpened toward triple-log). The multi-hop consequence: critical norms are blind to the blowup *rate*. They certify that *something* diverges, not *how fast*. Therefore any model built on critical-norm or long-window-average statistics has structurally zero power to separate Type I from Type II. That is the crack a cross-layer veto exploits: the veto lives one layer below the averaged statistic, in the instantaneous vorticity-stretching budget.

Finally the physical mechanism. Vorticity $\omega=\nabla\times u$ obeys $\partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega=(\omega\cdot\nabla)u+\nu\Delta\omega$, $[\omega]=\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The vortex-stretching term $(\omega\cdot\nabla)u$ is the amplifier; the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion says the solution stays smooth on $[0,T]$ iff $\int_0^T\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt<\infty$. Type II is precisely the regime where the local Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}(\ell)=U(\ell)\ell/\nu$ diverges as the concentration length $\ell\to 0$, so the $\nu\Delta\omega$ term — the only regularizing term — becomes negligible at the core, and the inviscid Euler budget governs the singularity. Every path — scaling, critical norms, vorticity budget, Reynolds divergence — converges on the same target: the diagnostic must be a *rate* and a *stretching budget*, never a *level*.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Tracing backward, the proof barrier is inherited, not fresh. The Leray (1934) program established global weak solutions and the energy inequality $\frac12\|u(t)\|_{L^2}^2+\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2\le\frac12\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2$ (units $\mathrm{m^5\,s^{-2}}$ per unit density), but this supercritical energy norm sits *below* the scaling-critical line $\frac2p+\frac3q=1$, so it cannot see the singularity it was built to control — the original "tape trap." Scheffer (1976–77) and Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg (1982) then reduced the possible singular set to parabolic Hausdorff measure zero, converting a qualitative question into a geometric one. The genealogy of Type II specifically descends from the self-similar program: Leray's own self-similar ansatz was closed off for the *smooth* category by Nečas–Růžička–Šverák (1996), forcing the field toward either Type I with nontrivial profile or discrete/Type II concentration — which is why the Euler scaling (with its free exponent $\alpha$) re-enters as the only self-similar family Navier–Stokes cannot immediately veto. Elgindi's 2021 $C^{1,\alpha}$ Euler self-similar blowup is the most recent genealogical ancestor: it demonstrates that the inviscid template Navier–Stokes would need for Type II is not empty. The multi-week "tape trap" analog is the human tendency to read a bounded critical norm (a stable long-run average) as safety, when the ESS theorem says the *endpoint* divergence is guaranteed but rate-opaque.

Predictor C ("QB internal-clock acceleration") is the instantaneous vorticity budget $\int\|\omega\|_\infty\,dt$: it does not average, it integrates the *rate*, so it detects the $(T-t)^{-1}$ blowup of $\|\nabla u\|_\infty$ that A and B are structurally blind to. The recursive regress terminates only when a model can represent B modeling A: a correct adjudicator must assume the averaged statistic is *bait* and default to the instantaneous stretching budget. <CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The veto that overrides naive expected value is a dimensional-incommensurability between the *level* of a critical norm and the *rate* of concentration.

Sovereign Frontier Primitives (the extracted invariants):

1. Parabolic scaling operator: $\mathcal{S}_\lambda[u](x,t)=\lambda\,u(\lambda x,\lambda^2 t)$, $[\,u]=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; invariance fixes $\alpha=1$.
2. Euler anisotropic scaling: $\mathcal{E}^\alpha_\lambda[u](x,t)=\lambda^\alpha u(\lambda x,\lambda^{1+\alpha}t)$, $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$; $\alpha\ne1$ is the Type II signature.
3. Type discriminator: $\Theta[u](T)=\limsup_{t\uparrow T}(T-t)^{1/2}\|u(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ [dimensionless]; Type I $\Theta<\infty$, Type II $\Theta=\infty$.
4. Critical functional family: $\|u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}$, invariant iff $\tfrac{2}{p}+\tfrac{3}{q}=1$ (units: $\mathrm{m^{1-3/q}\,s^{-(1-3/q)}\cdot(\text{amplitude})}$ arranged to be scale-invariant), $3<q\le\infty$.
5. BKM stretching functional: $\mathcal{B}[u]=\int_0^T\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt$, $[\|\omega\|_\infty]=\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, so $\mathcal{B}$ is dimensionless.
6. Local Reynolds number: $\mathrm{Re}(\ell)=U(\ell)\,\ell/\nu$ [dimensionless]; $\mathrm{Re}\to\infty$ as $\ell\to0$ ⟹ inviscid (Euler) core.

Formal singularity/breakdown conditions:
- ESS (2003): $\|u\|_{L^\infty_tL^3_x}<\infty \Rightarrow$ no blowup; equivalently blowup $\Rightarrow \|u(t)\|_{L^3}\ge c\big(\log\log\tfrac{1}{T-t}\big)^{\gamma}$ (Tao's refinement: triple-log). Assumptions: suitable weak solution, whole space; breakdown: none proven — this is a *lower* bound only.
- LPS/Serrin: $u\in L^p_tL^q_x$ with $\tfrac2p+\tfrac3q<1$, $q>3$ ⟹ smooth. The barrier $\tfrac2p+\tfrac3q=1$ is the exact critical locus.
- BKM: smoothness on $[0,T]\iff \int_0^T\|\omega\|_\infty\,dt<\infty$; blowup $\iff \mathcal{B}=\infty$.
- Viscous sub-dominance: nonlinear term $\sim U^2/\ell$ vs. diffusive $\sim \nu U/\ell^2$; ratio $=\mathrm{Re}(\ell)$. Type II requires $\mathrm{Re}(\ell)\to\infty$, i.e., the inviscid Euler self-similar profile with exponent $\alpha\ne1$ must itself be singular.

The veto statement: no functional of the form $\|u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}$ with $\tfrac2p+\tfrac3q\le1$ can distinguish Type I from Type II, because scaling invariance cancels $(T-t)$ exactly. The only quantities that resolve the rate are non-scale-invariant: $(T-t)^{1/2}\|u\|_\infty$ (dimensionless but rate-carrying) and $\int\|\omega\|_\infty\,dt$ (dimensionless, rate-integrating). Any expected-value model whose features are critical norms is *vetoed* — it is mathematically incapable of representing $\Theta=\infty$.

GSI Clinical/Physical Mapping: this is the diagnostic difference between a benign intensifying vortex (bathtub drain, wingtip vortex that saturates — Type I, $\mathrm{Re}$ bounded at the core) and a genuine finite-time singularity candidate (tornado-scale vortex-stretching blowup where $\mathrm{Re}(\ell)\to\infty$ and the Euler budget governs). Clinically: a monotonic average can look stable while an instantaneous derivative-integral (BKM) is diverging — the fluid analog of watching a moving average while an instantaneous rate quietly leaves the safe set.

GSE Strategic/Veto Mapping: "surface statistic" = long-window critical-norm averages (season-long efficiency, cumulative counts). "Cross-layer veto" = refusing to price a regime until the *rate* layer (instantaneous stretching/vorticity budget) is checked. The strategic rule: when the adversary (DC) feeds you sub-critical, low-variance bait, the mean will read "safe"; the veto forces a descent one layer to the non-invariant rate before any decision. This is deliberately non-numeric here to honor the zero-betting-line invariant.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

RLVR = reinforcement learning from verifiable reward: a policy is rewarded only for outcomes a checker can confirm. In each scenario a surface-stat policy fails and rate-layer veto reasoning succeeds, with a falsifiable, computable Sunday prediction (verified by pseudo-spectral or adaptive-mesh Navier–Stokes integration, not by odds).

Scenario 1 — Critical-norm blindness. Surface policy: "keep $\|u(t)\|_{L^3}$ below its running average, reward stability." Failure mode: ESS guarantees $\|u\|_{L^3}\to\infty$ at *any* blowup, but the double/triple-log rate is far slower than the policy's averaging window, so the policy reports "bounded, safe" arbitrarily close to $T$. Veto: monitor $(T-t)^{1/2}\|u\|_\infty$. Falsifiable Sunday prediction: in a $256^3$ pseudo-spectral run of unforced whole-space Navier–Stokes initialized with a smooth high-vortex-stretching state, $\|u(t)\|_{L^3}$ will *increase without bound* on any attempted blowup window while the trailing 1000-step average of $\|u\|_{L^3}$ stays within its initial band — i.e., the averaged feature and the endpoint divergence are simultaneously true. Verifiable: yes, by direct integration.

Scenario 2 — Type I / Type II mislabel via Euler exponent. Surface policy: assume any self-similar collapse is Type I (parabolic, $\alpha=1$) because that is the only self-similar form Navier–Stokes admits globally. Failure mode: an Elgindi-type $C^{1,\alpha}$ axisymmetric-with-swirl profile collapses at an Euler exponent $\alpha\ne1$; the surface model, keyed to $\alpha=1$, mis-times the event and mis-prices the rate. Veto: measure the local exponent $\alpha$ from $\log\|u\|_\infty$ vs. $\log(T-t)$ slope; if slope $\ne -1/2$, it is Type II. Falsifiable Sunday prediction: for $C^{1,\alpha}$ Euler data (Elgindi 2021), the numerically measured amplitude slope obeys $\|u\|_\infty\sim (T-t)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}\cdot(\text{profile})$, deviating from $-1/2$; whereas the *unforced whole-space* Navier–Stokes evolution of the same smooth data either regularizes (viscosity restores the $\alpha=1$ clock) or, if it singularizes, does so with the Navier–Stokes-compatible rate. Verifiable by slope extraction in simulation.

Scenario 3 — Reynolds-inversion / viscosity-dropout. Surface policy: reward low total enstrophy $\int\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2$ as a proxy for regularity (an $L^2$, hence supercritical, quantity). Failure mode: enstrophy can stay bounded while a *pointwise* $\|\omega\|_\infty$ integral (BKM) diverges, because $L^2$ averaging hides a shrinking high-vorticity core; equivalently $\mathrm{Re}(\ell)=U(\ell)\ell/\nu\to\infty$ as $\ell\to0$ is invisible to the $L^2$ enstrophy. Veto: track $\int_0^t\|\omega(s)\|_\infty\,ds$ and the shrinking core scale $\ell(t)$. Falsifiable Sunday prediction: a simulation can be driven to $\int\|\omega\|_\infty\,dt$ approaching its divergence threshold while total enstrophy remains bounded and monotone-decreasing, demonstrating that the $L^2$ feature and the BKM divergence are not merely correlated but decoupled near a candidate singularity. Verifiable by simultaneous measurement of both functionals.

Common structure of all three vetoes: the surface policy uses a scale-invariant or supercritical (level/average) feature; the veto uses a non-scale-invariant rate or pointwise integral. The verifiable reward for RLVR training is exactly this: reward the policy that descends to the rate layer and correctly declares $\Theta=\infty$ vs. $\Theta<\infty$, penalize any policy that prices from a critical norm. If future work constructs an actual Type II example (currently open for unforced whole-space Navier–Stokes — the Millennium problem), Scenario 2's slope prediction becomes the sharpest falsifiable test; if instead all smooth data is shown to regularize to $\alpha=1$, the veto correctly predicts perpetual Type I and the surface model's failure is bounded to timing error rather than category error.
