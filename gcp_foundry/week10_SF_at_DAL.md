\[
H(B_t)=-\sum_{b} p_t(b)\log_2 p_t(b)\quad \text{bits}
\]

If \(p_t\) is not normalized or the box definition changes mid-game, the equation is invalid.

\[
I(A;B_t\mid \mathcal{F}_{t^-})=H(B_t\mid \mathcal{F}_{t^-})-H(B_t\mid A,\mathcal{F}_{t^-})\quad \text{bits}
\]
where \(A\in\{\text{run action},\text{pass action}\}\) and \(\mathcal{F}_{t^-}\) is pre-snap information. Low \(I\) means Dallas cannot distinguish run from pass early; high \(I\) means the marriage is broken. Assumption: actions are labeled post-snap and the defensive response is observable. Breakdown regime: if \(A\) is deterministic given formation, the conditional entropy collapses and the equation measures formation, not disguise.

3. Edge pursuit required acceleration:
\[
a_{\text{req}}=\frac{v^2}{2d}\quad \text{m s}^{-2}
\]
where \(v\) is closing speed in m/s and \(d\) is remaining distance in m. Assumption: constant deceleration/acceleration over a short pursuit interval. Singularity: \(d\to 0\) m makes \(a_{\text{req}}\to\infty\), physically impossible; the correct interpretation is that the edge defender has already lost leverage.

4. Collision impulse:
\[
\mathbf{J}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\mathbf{F}(t)\,dt\quad \text{N s}
\]
Assumption: force is integrable over contact time. Breakdown regime: as \(\Delta t=t_1-t_0\to 0\) with finite impulse, the implied force becomes unbounded; this signals a rigid-body approximation failure, not a real infinite force.

5. Blocking leverage torque:
\[
\tau=\|\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}\|\quad \text{N m}
\]
where \(\mathbf{r}\) is the moment arm in m and \(\mathbf{F}\) is force in N. Assumption: rigid-body lever approximation. Breakdown regime: \(\|\mathbf{r}\|=0\) m gives zero torque even under large force; the model cannot capture hand placement and postural control.

6. Ground-reaction friction limit for cuts:
\[
a_{\max}=\mu g\quad \text{m s}^{-2}
\]
where \(\mu\) is the effective shoe-turf friction coefficient and \(g=9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2}\). Assumption: Coulomb friction and level surface. Breakdown regime: if turf is loose, \(\mu\) drops; if the foot plant is off-axis, the scalar model fails.

7. Play-action probability model:
\[
p_{\text{PA}}=\sigma(\eta),\qquad
\eta=\beta_0+\beta_1 H(B_t)+\beta_2 \mathbf{1}_{\text{Parsons absent}}+\beta_3 t_{\text{release}}+\beta_4 \|\mathbf{u}\|
\]
where \(p_{\text{PA}}\) is a probability in \([0,1]\), \(t_{\text{release}}\) is in s, and \(\|\mathbf{u}\|\) is wind speed in m/s. Assumption: logistic link and independent observations. Breakdown regime: separation, collinearity, or deterministic labels make the coefficients unidentified; the model is descriptive, not causal.

8. Wind lateral displacement:
\[
y_{\text{wind}}=\frac{1}{2}a_y t_f^2\quad \text{m}
\]
where \(a_y\) is lateral acceleration from crosswind in m s\(^{-2}\) and \(t_f\) is flight time in s. Assumption: approximately constant lateral acceleration. Breakdown regime: long flight time and gusting violate constant-acceleration assumptions; the equation then only bounds, not predicts.

The key exact breakdown regimes are therefore: \(d\to0\) in pursuit, \(\Delta t\to0\) in impulse, \(\|\mathbf{r}\|=0\) in torque, \(\mu\) collapse in cutting, and logistic non-identification in the probability model. None of these are decorative ratios; each names a physical or statistical failure mode.
