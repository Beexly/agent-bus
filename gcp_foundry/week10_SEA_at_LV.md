<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive game has three modes. First, crisis habit regression: under pressure, a quarterback may revert to a level-0 habit—first read, hero throw, or checkdown—and an offensive coordinator may revert to familiar personnel and protection. Macdonald can exploit this by showing a level-1 bait: a shell that suggests one rotation, then rotating to a different coverage after the snap. Second, counter-trap: a quarterback who expects disguise may use an alert throw or protection slide that assumes rotation; Macdonald can double-bait by holding the shown shell or rotating late to a third coverage. This is a level-2 and level-3 belief problem, not a fixed “aggression” trait. Third, clock: the play clock constrains disguise because late rotation requires communication and substitution discipline. If the clock is short, the offense may snap before the defense settles; if the clock is long, Macdonald can hold the presentation longer and force a second read. The recursive prediction is conditional: against a quarterback with strong pre-snap processing, Macdonald’s best counter is not maximum entropy but a structured posterior that makes the alert throw wrong; against a quarterback in crisis regression, the best counter is a bait that resembles the expected rotation and then punishes the first read. The clock layer vetoes both: no disguise model is valid if the defense cannot communicate the rotation before the snap.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
