1) Signal channel and equivocation (formalizing [PAGE 228 of 276]).
Let plays number $N\approx 30$ ("Many college teams use about thirty plays" [PAGE 160 of 276]). Source entropy:
$$H(S)=\log_2 N \approx \log_2 30 = 4.91\ \text{bits}.$$
A signal is a sequence $X=(X_1,\dots,X_k)$ over an alphabet of size $A$; one secret slot $j^\*$ carries $S$ (optionally via an invertible key, [PAGE 228–229 of 276]), the other $k-1$ are uniform blinds $B_i\sim\text{Unif}\{1,\dots,A\}$. Own team (knows $j^\*$): $H(S\mid X,j^\*)=0$. Eavesdropper (unknown $j^\*$), for $A\ge N$ and uniform blinds, cannot localize the carrier, so residual equivocation
$$H(S\mid X)\to H(S)=\log_2 N.$$
The addition/key schemes are invertible encodings; they add zero entropy — security lives entirely in the key $j^\*$ (Kerckhoffs). Breakdown regime: schedule capture across weeks ("some teams change from game to game" [PAGE 228 of 276]) or a non-uniform tell collapses $H(S\mid X)\to 0$.

2) Punt coverage inequality (formalizing [PAGE 69 of 276]).
Kick point is $x_k$ behind the line of scrimmage ($x_k\approx 10$ yd $=9.14$ m; "Ten yards is a good distance" [PAGE 152 of 276]). Vacuum ballistics: $T=\dfrac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g}$, $R=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$. The end covers $d=R-x_k$ at speed $v_c$. Define coverage margin
$$M=v_cT-(R-x_k).$$
Punt is *coverable* iff $M\ge 0$; else the returner gets a free start. Illustrative: a lofted $\theta=55^\circ$, $R=27$ m kick needs $v_0=16.8$ m·s⁻¹, giving $T=2.80$ s; with $v_c=8.5$ m·s⁻¹, $M=8.5(2.80)-(27-9.14)=+5.9$ m (covered). A flat $\theta=50^\circ$, $R=48.6$ m kick gives $T=3.44$ s and $M=8.5(3.44)-(48.6-9.14)=-9.8$ m (uncovered → returned). This reproduces Wilce's ranking [PAGE 71 of 276]. Breakdown: quadratic drag $\mathbf{F}_d=-\tfrac12\rho C_d A|\mathbf v|\mathbf v$ shrinks $R$ faster than $T$ for high arcs, *widening* $M$ for lofted kicks; wind adds $\pm$ to $R$ ("high kick is best except… against the wind" [PAGE 74 of 276]).

3) Pass lead / intercept (formalizing [PAGE 96 of 276]).
Receiver $r(t)=r_0+v_r t$; flight time $\tau$; aim point $a=r_0+v_r\tau$, lead vector $\ell=v_r\tau$. Catch requires $\lVert \text{ball}(\tau)-r(\tau)\rVert\le\varepsilon$. "Bee-line" low-arc passes minimize $\tau$ (harder to handle [PAGE 88 of 276]); rainbow arcs raise $\tau$ (easier catch, more defender time). Breakdown: a cut at $t_c<\tau$ voids the constant-velocity extrapolation.

4) Down-and-distance value function (formalizing [PAGE 234 of 276]).
State $s=(y,d)$, $y$=yards-to-goal, $d\in\{1,2,3,4\}$. Bellman:
$$V(s)=\max_{a\in\{\text{run,pass,kick}\}}\Big[R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\,V(s')\Big].$$
Failed 4th down flips possession; an intercepted pass carries a large penalty ("equivalent to 40 yards" [PAGE 136 of 276]). Wilce's rules are a risk-averse hand policy; $\gamma\to 1$ near the end (clock), and the interception penalty makes pass high-variance. This is decision theory, not odds.

5) Tackle as a kinematic constraint ([PAGE 48 of 276]).

Rigid bilateral leg coupling sends the stepping DOF to zero, so active forward propulsion $v_{\text{fwd}}\le 0$ (the runner can be driven back but cannot self-advance). Qualitative; no fabricated force numbers.
