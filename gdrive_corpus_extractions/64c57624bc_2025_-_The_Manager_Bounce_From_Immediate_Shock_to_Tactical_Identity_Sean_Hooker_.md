The paper's causal chain runs from raw event streams to a five-dimensional tactical identity vector, then to a convergence clock. First, Hudl events are filtered into coachable micro metrics: pressing line height (longitudinal mean position of pressers and nearby teammates in the opposition half, requiring ≥3 teammates, aggregated by mean), compactness (median normalised convex-hull area of the defending block in its own half, requiring ≥8 outfield players, excluding goalkeepers), directness (share of pass/carry distance moving positively along the x-axis), progression speed (median metres per second over positive pass/carry events with positive OBV), and share of carries (carry count divided by total positive progression events). These are positive continuous or (0,1)-bounded quantities, which motivates the GLM split: Gamma with log link for pressing line height and progression speed; Beta with logit link for compactness, directness, and carry share. Context adjustment follows Skripnikov et al. (2025) but extends it to continuous variables: each metric is predicted under observed context (η_obs) and under a neutralised context (away venue, equal Elo, 11v11, level score; η_neutral). The delta Δ = η_neutral − η_obs is subtracted from the observed linear predictor before back-transformation, yielding y_adj. Robust global z-scores then place all five metrics on a common scale, with log-stabilisation for skewed variables. A diagonal Mahalanobis-type distance D²_t = Σ_j w_j (z_t,j − z_t−1,j)² measures match-to-match tactical shock; weights w_j are inverse functions of normalised model variance plus λ = 0.2 regularisation. An EWMA with α = 0.22 smooths D²_t, and convergence is declared when the smoothed distance stays below THR = 1.57 (the global median of post-8-match distances) for six consecutive matches.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All metrics are computed per match from Hudl event and 360-frame data [PAGE 4]. Pitch dimensions default to 105 m × 68 m when stadium-specific values are unavailable [PAGE 5].

Pressing line height (Hudl units, longitudinal axis x from own goal to opponent goal):  
Let E_press be pressure events in the opponent half. For each event e, let p_e be the pressing player and N_e = {k : |x_k − x_{p_e}| ≤ 10 Hudl units, x_k > x_halfway, k ≠ goalkeeper} the set of ≥3 nearby teammates [PAGE 8].  
\[
\mathrm{PLH}_{\mathrm{match}} = \frac{1}{|E_{\mathrm{press}}|}\sum_{e \in E_{\mathrm{press}}}\frac{1}{|N_e|}\sum_{k \in N_e} x_k .
\]
Breakdown: undefined if E_press = ∅ (possession-dominant teams) or if |N_e| < 3 for all events.

Compactness (dimensionless, normalised area): Convex hull A_hull of defending outfield players (goalkeepers excluded) within the own half during opposition possession/defensive events, requiring ≥8 players [PAGE 9–10]. Area via Gauss' shoelace formula:  
\[
A_{\mathrm{hull}} = \frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i y_{i+1} − x_{i+1} y_i)\right|,\quad (x_{n+1},y_{n+1}) = (x_1,y_1).
\]  
\[
\mathrm{Compactness}_{\mathrm{match}} = \operatorname{median}_{m}\left(\frac{A_{\mathrm{hull},m}}{A_{\mathrm{half\text{-}pitch}}}\right),\quad A_{\mathrm{half\text{-}pitch}} = \frac{105 \times 68}{2}\ \mathrm{m^2}.
\]  
Breakdown: degenerate hull if fewer than 3 non-collinear player positions; the ≥8-player constraint mitigates but does not eliminate sparse-sample variance.

Directness (dimensionless ratio): Over pass and carry actions A,  
\[
\mathrm{Directness} = \frac{\sum_{a \in A,\ \Delta x_a > 0}\Delta x_a}{\sum_{a \in A} d_a},
\]  
where d_a is Euclidean action distance [PAGE 11].

Progression speed (m/s): Hudl-unit distance converted to metres using match-specific pitch length; Δt from sequential event timestamps [PAGE 12].  
\[
v_e = \frac{\Delta s_e\;(\mathrm{m})}{\Delta t_e\;(\mathrm{s})},\qquad
\mathrm{Speed}_{\mathrm{match}} = \operatorname{median}_{e \in E^+} v_e,
\]  
with E^+ = {pass/carry, Δx > 0, positive OBV}. Table 2.1 [PAGE 12] shows mean–median gaps up to 50.55% at P90, justifying the median aggregator.

Share of carries (dimensionless):  
\[
\mathrm{CarryShare} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{carry}}}{n_{\mathrm{pass}} + n_{\mathrm{carry}}}
\]  
over the same positive-progression filter [PAGE 14].

Context adjustment via GLMs. Systematic component η_i = x_i^T β.  
Gamma family (PLH, speed): y_i ∼ Gamma(μ_i, φ), log(μ_i) = η_i, μ_i > 0 [PAGE 21].  
Beta family (compactness, directness, carry share): y_i ∼ Beta(μ_i φ, (1−μ_i) φ), logit(μ_i) = η_i, μ_i ∈ (0,1) [PAGE 22].  
HC3 robust covariance estimators are applied [PAGE 20]. Elo difference is z-scored by season and entered with a quadratic term because OLS residuals showed nonlinearity (Figure 3.1) [PAGE 19].

Neutral context: is_home = 0, equal team strength (z-scored Elo difference fixed at neutral value), 11v11 (Man Net = 0), level score. For Beta models, Δ_i = η_{i,obs} − η_{i,neutral}; adjusted logit:  
\[
\operatorname{logit}(y_{i,\mathrm{adj}}) = \operatorname{logit}(y_{i,\mathrm{obs}}) − \Delta_i.
\]  
For Gamma models, Δ_i is on the log scale:  
\[
\log(y_{i,\mathrm{adj}}) = \log(y_{i,\mathrm{obs}}) − \Delta_i.
\]  
[PAGE 23–24].

Identity vector and distance. Robust global z-scores z_{t,j}; log-stabilise skewed metrics first [PAGE 25–26]. Diagonal Mahalanobis-type distance [PAGE 26]:  
\[
D^2_t = \sum_{j=1}^{5} w_{t,j} (z_{t,j} − z_{t-1,j})^2,\quad \Delta z_{t,j} = z_{t,j} − z_{t-1,j}.
\]  
Weights [PAGE 27]:  
\[
w_{t,j} = \frac{1}{\tilde{R}_{t,j} + \lambda},\quad \lambda = 0.2,\quad \tilde{R}_{t,j} = \frac{R_{t,j}}{\operatorname{std}_{\text{matches}}(R_{\cdot,j})},
\]  
where R_{t,j} derives from GLM dispersion. EWMA smoothing [PAGE 28]:  
\[
\bar{D}_t = \alpha D^2_t + (1−\alpha)\bar{D}_{t-1},\quad \alpha = 0.22.
\]  
Convergence rule [PAGE 28]: THR = 1.57 (global median of \bar{D} over observations after each manager's first 8 matches); a convergence event requires \bar{D}_t < THR for 6 consecutive matches.

Calibration diagnostics: PIT should be U(0,1); KS test rejects calibration at p < 0.05; empirical 95% coverage should approximate 0.95 [PAGE 18]. Reported Gamma models achieve PIT KS p > 0.18 and coverage ≈ 0.95 [PAGE 21]; Beta models achieve KS p > 0.08 and coverage ≈ 0.95 [PAGE 22].

The study sits in a lineage of "manager bounce" research. Ramani (2023) defined the New Manager Bounce across the top five European leagues over six seasons as better immediate results than the predecessor, documenting a short-term uplift concentrated in wins and draws [PAGE 2]. Besters et al., extending Van Ours and van Tuijl (2016), used a cumulative surprise metric (actual versus expected points controlling for opposition strength) and a GLM with team strength and venue to show that mid-season dismissals cluster after poor-form runs, producing a regression-to-the-mean illusion of improvement [PAGE 2]. The present paper explicitly rejects the results-only bounce and pivots to tactical identity.

For identity construction, Fernandez-Navarro et al. (2016) applied factor analysis to 19 performance indicators in La Liga and the Premier League, but Sharpe (2022) criticises such approaches for contextual neglect and subjective variable choice [PAGE 3]. Sharpe instead used Non-Negative Matrix Factorisation on spatial event data, following Franks et al. (2015) in the NBA, to learn playing styles objectively; the current work borrows the manager–identity link but substitutes "coachable" metrics that can shift within days rather than seasons [PAGE 3]. Monte (2024) connects passing and pressing data to managerial style but notes interpretive limits [PAGE 3].

Context adjustment extends Skripnikov et al. (2025), who modelled count-based offensive variables with GAMs and Negative Binomial distributions to isolate game-state effects [PAGE 15]. The paper adapts that logic to continuous metrics. Spatial antecedents include Morgan (2018) on counter-pressing via pressure events within five seconds of turnover [PAGE 8], Rico-González et al. (2021) on spatial-tactical surface metrics [PAGE 9], and Guan et al. (2023) on defensive compactness [PAGE 10]. The Cruyff compactness quotation is sourced from Breaking the Lines (2023) [PAGE 9].

Multi-week traps: the six-match convergence window approximates one month of fixtures [PAGE 28], but fixture congestion, international breaks, and mid-season transfer windows can compress or distort that calendar. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
A new manager enters a squad carrying the predecessor's tactical ghost. Players possess crisis habit regression: under pressure, they revert to previously reinforced positional and pressing patterns even when instructed otherwise. The counter-trap is the opposite over-correction—immediate, large-scale stylistic overhaul that exceeds the squad's technical and cognitive bandwidth, producing confusion and weaker first-game results. Figure 5.8 identifies an optimum: teams too similar to the predecessor (distance 0–5) and teams too dissimilar (distance > 10) both record weaker opening results, while moderate divergence yields the highest first-match points [PAGE 34]. This is a recursive theory of mind: the manager models the squad's expectation of continuity versus change, and the squad models the manager's tolerance for error.

The clock is the binding constraint. Figure 5.7 demonstrates that the fewer days between a predecessor's final match and the new manager's first match, the smaller the identity distance D² [PAGE 33]. With minimal training time, the manager cannot alter pressing line height or defensive compactness meaningfully; only in-game instructions and selection shift. Consequently, the immediate post-appointment "bounce" is predominantly psychological or contextual, consistent with Besters et al.'s null structural finding [PAGE 2]. Tactical identity converges progressively: 31 of 58 eligible tenures converge, typically within 12–15 matches [PAGE 31–32, 40]. Patrick Vieira required 50 games and Eddie Howe 110 [PAGE 31–32], revealing that convergence time is not uniform and that slow developers may be misclassified as non-convergent under a fixed 6-match, THR = 1.57 rule.

Recursive layer: each match updates the manager's belief about player adaptability, which changes future instructions, which changes the identity vector, which changes the distance D²_t. If the manager misreads squad readiness (overestimating coachability of pressing line or carry share), the EWMA \bar{D}_t remains elevated, delaying convergence. Conversely, excessive similarity to the predecessor keeps \bar{D}_t low but may forfeit the moderate divergence associated with better early results. The framework therefore measures not just tactical change, but the manager's recursive calibration of change speed to squad absorption capacity.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Player technical quality imposes a hard ceiling on carry share. Manchester City's high carry ratio is supported by ball carriers such as Doku and Haaland [PAGE 14]; a manager cannot instruct a carry-heavy identity into a squad lacking progressive dribblers. If a context-adjusted carry-share vector implies sustained dribbling volume beyond the squad's demonstrated physical output, the metric is vetoed as physically infeasible.

Biomechanical layer: Pressing line height and compactness are bounded by aerobic capacity, acceleration, and recovery. A high pressing line requires repeated sprints and high-intensity decelerations; if match data show PLH sustained at elite levels across congested fixtures without corresponding sprint metrics, the estimate is suspect. The model excludes pressing effectiveness (success rate) precisely because intent ≠ execution [PAGE 8]; thus a high PLH with low success is an intent signal, not a performance guarantee. Compactness requires coordinated shifting of ≥8 outfield players within the own half [PAGE 9]; injuries or red cards break this coordination. Man Net accounts for numerical disadvantage [PAGE 16], but context adjustment neutralises manpower to 11v11 [PAGE 23–24], which can erase genuine tactical constraints imposed by a sending-off.

Environmental layer: Weather is not modelled as a covariate; pitch dimensions default to 105 × 68 m where stadium data are missing [PAGE 5], introducing spatial scaling error in convex-hull and pressing-line calculations. Wind, rain, and temperature affect passing directness and progression speed but are absent from the GLM context set (Elo, manpower, home/away, winning/losing share, season) [PAGE 16–17]. This is a veto condition: matches with extreme weather not captured by game-state variables may produce outlier identity distances that are environmental rather than managerial.

Veto proof: The neutralised context fixes is_home = 0 and equal Elo strength [PAGE 23], intentionally removing home advantage and opponent strength from the identity vector. However, it cannot remove player availability. If a manager's first match features a depleted back line due to injury, the observed compactness may rise (deeper block) not because of tactical identity but because of personnel. The context adjustment does not model injuries; therefore any convergence event declared within three matches of a major injury wave is vetoed pending roster confirmation.
