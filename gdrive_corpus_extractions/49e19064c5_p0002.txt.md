Defining the dimensionless capital fraction f = Bₖ/Xₖ₋₁, the one-period growth functional is g(f) = p ln(1 + f) + q ln(1 − f), measured in nats per trial. The first-order condition p/(1 + f) − q/(1 − f) = 0 yields f* = p − q = 2p − 1. The second derivative g''(f) = −p/(1 + f)² − q/(1 − f)² is strictly negative, confirming a unique interior maximum whenever p ∈ (1/2, 1). Singularities appear at f = 1, where ln(1 − f) → ln(0) = −∞ on a loss, and at p = 1/2, where f* = 0 and no positive edge exists.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from J. L. Kelly’s formulation of an asymptotically optimal resource-apportionment strategy [cited as [2] in the source], through L. Breiman’s theoretical underpinnings of the Kelly system, to E. O. Thorp’s application of the framework. Rotando and Thorp’s 2006 expository note situates the criterion in the U.S. stock market. Deeper lineage connects Kelly’s logarithmic growth objective to Shannon information theory: maximizing expected log wealth is dual to minimizing the relative entropy between the true outcome distribution and the implied betting distribution, tying capital allocation to channel capacity and entropy rates.

Multi-week traps emerge when this single-period binomial logic is transplanted into serial capital markets. The source’s ruin formula 1 − pⁿ assumes a fixed, stationary edge p across independent trials. Over a multi-week horizon, equity return drift and volatility are regime-dependent; a drawdown week can shift the conditional win probability downward, so the historical p used to size f* no longer describes the next trial. Rebalancing on a fixed weekly calendar creates a look-ahead trap if the edge estimator is computed from the same overlapping window used for execution, because future return information leaks into the current position size. The “infinitely wealthy opponent” assumption also fails under leverage: broker margin limits impose a finite counterparty boundary, so the absorbing state is not only Xₖ = 0 but also the credit-limit breach, which the binomial ruin formula does not capture. These traps are retrospective in the sense that a strategy validated on past i.i.d. bins can appear robust while harboring hidden regime-change sensitivity that only reveals itself over weeks of correlated outcomes.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates along the two extremes explicitly condemned in the source [PAGE 1]. Under drawdown stress, an allocator tends either to regress to expected-value maximization—increasing stake size to “make back” losses, effectively moving toward Bₖ = Xₖ₋₁—or to regress to timid betting—cutting size to the minimum to avoid ruin, which collapses expected average gain. Both regressions are locally coherent but globally destructive under the model’s assumptions.

The counter-trap is the belief that f* = p − q remains a stable parameter during a crisis. In the source’s notation, p is a property of the biased coin, not a structural constant of markets. If a winning streak inflates the allocator’s estimate of p, the Kelly fraction rises toward f = 1, approaching the logarithmic singularity where a single loss wipes out the bankroll. The counter-trap is therefore overconfidence in stationarity: treating an estimated edge as immutable while the underlying regime shifts.

The clock enters explicitly through n in the ruin probability 1 − pⁿ [PAGE 1]. The mathematical clock shows that even a positive edge requires a sufficient number of trials to separate signal from variance. The psychological clock—elapsed drawdown duration—shortens the effective horizon available for recovery. A crisis compresses n, which simultaneously raises the required growth rate to restore capital and raises the probability of hitting Xₖ = 0 before the clock runs out. The recursive theory of mind thus predicts that stressed allocators will oscillate between all-in regression and timid regression, misreading the clock and misestimating p, while the Kelly framework demands a recalculated, smaller f* under regime uncertainty rather than a return to either extreme.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The source model treats capital as a conserved scalar under Xₖ = Xₖ₋₁ + TₖBₖ [PAGE 1]. Real markets introduce transaction costs, taxes, and slippage, which add a non-conservative dissipation term −Cₖ to the recursion. Veto condition: if expected cost per trial Cₖ exceeds the exploitable edge (p − q)f*, the physical layer vetoes the allocation because net worth is no longer conserved across trials and the growth functional g(f) must be adjusted downward, potentially flipping the sign of the optimal fraction.

Biomechanical/human layer: Independent trials require a stable probability assessment p and error-free execution. Under cognitive load, an operator’s conditional loss probability q rises (execution errors, hesitation, discretionary overrides), violating the i.i.d. assumption. Veto condition: if measured execution latency or override frequency during drawdown exceeds a pre-set threshold, the trial sequence is no longer independent and the binomial ruin formula 1 − pⁿ is invalid; the model must be vetoed until the human-machine interface is recalibrated.

Weather/market regime layer: The even-money binomial model assumes bounded outcomes Tₖ ∈ {+1, −1} and stationary bias p > 1/2 [PAGE 1]. Volatility regime shifts and fat tails violate both assumptions: outcomes are not bounded binary, and drift is not constant. Veto condition: if realized kurtosis or implied volatility moves outside the calibration band used to estimate p, the stationary-edge assumption collapses and fixed-fraction Kelly sizing overbets relative to the true conditional distribution.

Adjudication: The model is veto-proof only when all three layers pass simultaneously—conservative capital dynamics after costs, i.i.d. execution under stable operator state, and stationary bounded-edge regime. Any single layer failure is sufficient to suspend fractional-Kelly allocation and revert to a reduced exposure or cash position until the failed layer is remediated.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
