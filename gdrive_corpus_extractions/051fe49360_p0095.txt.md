Clock: the factory’s pacing oscillator shifts from line-shaft RPM (one mechanical clock coupling all machines) to grid frequency (50/60 Hz) and finally to independent motor drives. In crisis, the clock recentralizes: all machines are gated by a single emergency stop or throttled to the slowest remaining unit, quietly reintroducing the mechanical coupling constraint in organizational form. Wiener’s text notes the motor–wiring connection “is not necessarily rigid” [PAGE 171], but organizational rigidity can persist after mechanical rigidity is removed.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: copper conductor loss \(P_{\text{wire}}=I^2\rho L/A\) imposes a hard distance/area penalty. For a dispersed cottage workshop at range \(L\) from a supply, if \(I^2\rho L/A\) exceeds the bearing savings from removing line shafting, decentralized power is energetically inferior; this is a quantitative veto of Wiener’s cottage claim wherever wire runs are long or conductors undersized. Weather does not veto indoor grid-fed motors directly, but exposed overhead conductors in distributed rural workshops raise storm-failure rates; undergrounding raises \(A\)-cost. Biomechanical/human layer veto: textile and assembly processes require material feed, part removal, inspection, and supervision. Human transfer time scales as \(\tau_{\text{transport}} \approx d / v_{\text{walk}}\) with \(v_{\text{walk}}\approx1.2\) m/s; if process takt time \(T_{\text{takt}}\) is shorter than \(N_{\text{handoffs}}\tau_{\text{transport}}\), the cottage layout violates the production rhythm. Wiener’s own reservation—“motives of traffic and convenience” favoring a single factory [PAGE 171]—is precisely this biomechanical veto. Adjudication: the reversion to cottage industry is conditionally valid only for low material-transfer, power-dominated processes; it is vetoed for tightly coupled assembly lines. Electrical non-rigidity removes the mechanical veto [PAGE 171] but does not eliminate wire-loss or human-transport vetoes.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
