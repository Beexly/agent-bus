<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Claim under adjudication.** Coverage is a theorem about a probability space, not a physical fact. No physical, biological, or meteorological layer can *rescue* a violated Assumption 1–5, and none can *veto* a satisfied one. What physical layers can do is (a) supply or destroy the score-stability margin \(\Delta\), and (b) supply unmeasured confounders that break Assumption 4.

**Physical layer — resource conservation (traffic slicing).** Equation 178 [PAGE 27] encodes a conservation law: a slice receives fraction \(N_S^{-1}\) of radio resources and shares it equally among \(E_i+1\) scheduled devices, so \(Y_i(1)=\min\{\eta(X_i)\xi_i/((E_i+1)N_S),R_{\max}\}\). This is a physical statement, and it is exactly what certifies the margin. Scheduling the target adds one contending device to each treated same-slice peer, sending \(E_j\mapsto E_j+1\); the throughput change is bounded by \(Y_j(1)/(E_j+2)\), and because \(\min\{\cdot,R_{\max}\}\) is 1-Lipschitz, the cap does not break the bound. Hence \(\Delta^I_j=[Y_j(1)/(E_j+2)]\mathbf 1\{Z_j=Z_I\}\) [PAGE 28, Eq. 181] is a *derived* physical quantity, not a fitted one. Veto proof: if the scheduler overbooks (admits more than \(E_i+1\) sharers), the \((E_i+1)^{-1}\) sharing law is false, Eq. 181 understates the perturbation, Assumption 6 fails at some \(t\), and the guarantee lapses. The physical layer here is a *precondition*, and its violation is a genuine veto.

**Physical layer — support and boundedness.** In Proposition 1, \(\mathcal Y=[-1,1]\) and \(S(x,e,y)=y\) [PAGE 17, Eq. 97]; the compact support is what makes the worst-case correction attainable and the tightness argument exact. Unbounded \(\mathcal Y\) with heavy tails does not invalidate the theorem but makes \(\Delta\) infinite and \(\rho^+\) collapse to \(\rho\) — the sharpening becomes a no-op.

**Biomechanical layer.** The corpus contains no biomechanical substrate. Any attempt to import one — a "fatigue modulus," a "force ratio" — would be a category error and is refused. The correct adjudication is negative: with no biomechanical variable in \(X\), \(E\), or \(Y\), there is nothing for that layer to constrain or to veto.

**Weather / channel layer.** Channel quality \(\eta(X_i)\) is inside \(X_i\) and is allowed to be arbitrarily random; the guarantee marginalizes over it [PAGE 27]. Weather may therefore fluctuate freely without touching validity. It becomes a veto only in one configuration: if a load or propagation shock changes *both* the scheduling decision \(T\) and the throughput \(Y\) while remaining outside \(X\), then \(Y_i(t)\not\perp T_{1:N}\mid(X_{1:N},E_{1:N},T_{1:N})\) in the required sense and Assumption 4 ("No hidden interference confounding," [PAGE 4]) fails. The paper's own lineage points to the remedy — sensitivity analysis and interventional-data corrections [PAGE 11, Jin et al. 2023; Yin et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Qchohi et al. 2026] — but those are outside the present theorem.

**Adjudication.** Physical conservation laws enter the guarantee only through \(\Delta\) and through the support of \(\mathcal Y\). Weather enters only as a potential confounder of exposure. Biomechanics does not enter at all. The veto hierarchy is therefore: Assumption 4 violation (fatal, unaddressed) \(>\) Assumption 6 under-bound (fatal to the sharpened variant only) \(>\) Assumption 2 non-equivariance (fatal to both) \(>\) saturation of \(\rho\) (non-fatal, kills informativeness).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three scenarios, each with an executable check and a falsifiable prediction. All are stated in the paper's own simulation protocol so that ground truth is available.

**Veto 1 — "Correct propensity specification restores WCP validity under interference."**
*Check.* Instantiate the covariate-dependent \(k\)-nearest-neighbor exposure graph of Figure 1 [PAGE 2] with the exact propensity used to generate treatments, so there is no misspecification. Sweep \(k\). Compute empirical marginal coverage of \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{WCP}}_I\) at \(1-\alpha=0.8\) over \(\ge200\) replicates.
*Verdict.* Vetoed. The paper states the mechanism verbatim: "WCP increasingly undercovers as interference between units increases, i.e., as \(k\) increases" [PAGE 2]. In the group-structured variant, "at \(G=4\), its empirical coverage is approximately 0.75, below the target level 0.8" [PAGE 8].
*Prediction.* Coverage deficit \(\ge0.03\) once the mean neighborhood carries a nontrivial share of calibration weight; deficit shrinks monotonically as \(k\to0\) or \(G\to\infty\). Falsified if the deficit stays below \(0.01\) at the strongest interference setting.

**Veto 2 — "IA-WCP+ is uniformly more conservative than IA-WCP."**
*Check.* On identical draws, compute both sets and test the pointwise inclusion \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{IA\text{-}WCP+}}_I\subseteq\Gamma^{\mathrm{IA\text{-}WCP}}_I\). Because \(0\le\rho^+_{I,y}\le\rho_{I,y}\) for every \(y\), the acceptance threshold in Eq. 107 is weakly lower than in Eq. 25 for every \(y\), which is exactly the paper's derivation: "comparing the acceptance thresholds in (107) and (25) gives \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{IA\text{-}WCP+}}_I\subseteq\Gamma^{\mathrm{IA\text{-}WCP}}_I\)" [PAGE 19, Eq. 108].
*Verdict.* Vetoed, and the error is directional. Sharper means *smaller*, hence weakly *lower* realized coverage on the same sample, while both remain \(\ge1-\alpha\). The paper's Figure 4 confirms the width ordering and the co-satisfaction of the target [PAGE 8].
*Prediction.* Width ratio \(\le1\) in every replicate; realized coverage of the sharpened set \(\le\) that of the worst-case set replicate-by-replicate; both \(\ge0.8\). Falsified if any replicate shows width(IA-WCP+) \(>\) width(IA-WCP).

**Veto 3 — "\(\Delta^I_j\) is a free shrinkage knob: any small value preserves coverage."**
*Check.* Rerun IA-WCP+ in the synthetic model with the certified margin \(\Delta^I_j=(\lambda/4+|\hat\theta_E|)|\tilde E^I_j-E_j|\) [PAGE 7, Eq. 37] replaced by \(\Delta^I_j/2\). The certified margin is valid because the score is Lipschitz in the outcome and the exposure perturbation is almost-sure bounded, i.e. it satisfies \(\Pr[V^{\mathrm{int}}_j>t]\le\Pr[V^{\mathrm{obs}}_j+\Delta^I_j>t]\) for all \(t\) [PAGE 19, Eq. 106]. Halving it violates that dominance at some threshold, so Lemma 4's coupling \(V^{\mathrm{int}}_j\le V^{\mathrm{obs}}_j+\Delta^I_j\) a.s. [PAGE 20, Eq. 111] no longer holds, and the inequality \(p^{\mathrm{int}}_{I,y}\le p^{\mathrm{obs}}_{I,y}+\rho^+_{I,y}\) [PAGE 20, Eq. 115] fails.
*Verdict.* Vetoed. The margin is a certificate, not a tuning parameter; the sharpening is licensed only under the stability assumption, as the paper states: "IA-WCP+ preserves the coverage requirement (27) under the mentioned technical assumptions" [PAGE 7].
*Prediction.* With \(\Delta/2\), empirical coverage at \(G=4\) falls below \(0.8\) and the deficit grows as \(G\) decreases; with the certified \(\Delta\), coverage tracks ideal WCP while width stays substantially below IA-WCP's. Falsified if the halved-margin variant still attains \(0.8\) at \(G=4\) across replicates.

**Cross-cutting note on tightness.** None of these vetoes claims \(\rho\) is always loose. Proposition 1 constructs a regime where the corrected and ideal procedures have *identical* coverage, \(1-\pi^{N-1}/N\), with \(p^{\mathrm{obs}}+\rho=p^{\mathrm{int}}\) exactly at the true outcome [PAGE 18, Eqs. 98, 103–104]. The correct conclusion is regime-dependent: tight when affected scores move downward across the threshold, loose when they do not. An RLVR harness should therefore score any claim about \(\rho\) against both the \(G=4\) regime and the Proposition 1 construction, not against either alone.
