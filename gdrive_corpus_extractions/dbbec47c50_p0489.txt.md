If facial reddening occurs identically at \(T_a = 10^\circ\text{C}\) and \(T_a = 35^\circ\text{C}\) under equal cardiac excitement, thermoregulatory confounding is present but Wundt's specific pressure-relief mechanism cannot be isolated without direct intra-arterial pressure measurement.

Veto proof conditions: A claim of weakened revival is physically vindicated only if (i) the ancestral action is biomechanically possible in the organism's current morphology; (ii) the expression's metabolic cost is affordable under prevailing ambient conditions; and (iii) the expression shows measurable covariance with the outcome \(Y\) in the ancestral context. Where any of these fails, the Jamesian explanation defaults to vestige or epiphenomenon.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions:

Scenario 1 — Effector absence (biomechanical veto). Select subjects with congenital absence or surgical restraint of the effectors involved in anger expression (e.g., inability to bare teeth or clench the jaw). If the expression is a weakened revival of a full action program, the neural command should still issue, but the observable output should be degraded or absent in the affected musculature while preserved elsewhere (e.g., growling, general muscular tension). Prediction: facial EMG shows attempted activation despite no visible movement; self-report of anger remains intact. Veto: if no facial motor command is detected at all, the expression is not a motor-program revival but a socially gated display.

Scenario 2 — Autonomic blockade (James-Lange embodiment veto). Administer a beta-adrenergic blocker (e.g., propranolol) to suppress palpitations and tremors during fear induction [PAGE 479]. Under a strong Jamesian reading, the bodily reverberations are constitutive of the psychical state; blocking them should diminish subjective fear reports. Prediction under embodiment: fear ratings decrease proportionally to heart-rate suppression. Veto: if subjective fear reports remain unchanged despite abolished palpitations and trembling, the bodily revival is not necessary for the psychical state, and the passage's claim that fear "consists of mental representations of painful results" [PAGE 479] is supported over a purely somatic account.

Scenario 3 — Context-gated physical permissiveness (ancestral utility veto). Place subjects in a threat scenario where the ancestral violent action is physically impossible (e.g., verbal social rejection with no possibility of combat or prey-killing). The weakened-revival principle predicts residual expressions (growls, muscular tension, dilated nostrils) because they are echoes of reactions useful in violent dealings [PAGE 479]. Prediction: expression amplitude scales with perceived permissiveness of physical aggression, not merely with threat intensity. Veto: if expressions are fully suppressed in civilized contexts regardless of threat intensity, the revival is context-gated by social affordance, not an automatic discharge of ancestral utility.
