The first hop is to Beschreibung, defined as "pure" introspection: "an immediate description of 'conscious contents' per se" [PAGE 242]. Crucially, in Beschreibung the subject "is not to state the meanings of one's ideas, but merely to describe their immediate texture or stuff" [PAGE 242]. This embodies the Wundtian and Titchenerian structuralist assumption that mental processes possess a "stuff aspect which was independent of their functional characters as meanings" [PAGE 242], and that this stuff can be isolated and described. The ontological claim is strong: consciousness decomposes into elemental qualities (sensory textures) that are separable from what those qualities mean or how they function in behavior.

The second hop is to Kundgabe: "the ordinary everyday sort of introspection," described as "simply the naive report by an inadequately trained individual, psychologist or layman, of what he was thinking about" [PAGE 242]. Tolman links this condemnation to William James's "psychologist's fallacy" [PAGE 242], in which the observer mistakes the object of the subject's thought for the substance of the thought itself. In psychophysical experiments—specifically Tolman's example of "discrimination of lifted weights" [PAGE 242]—this error became so routine that Titchener labeled it the "stimulus error" [PAGE 242]: the subject reports the physical stimulus (the weight is heavy) rather than the subjective sensation it produces.

Tolman's rescue of Kundgabe as proclamation of sign-gestalts is pragmatically attractive, but it raises a regress: who validates that the proclaimed sign-gestalt corresponds to the actual means-end field rather than to the subject's self-narrative? The passage thus exposes a recursive trap—introspection used to study introspection—which structuralism attempted to solve by training, yet which Tolman implicitly acknowledges cannot be fully eliminated, only reinterpreted.

Kundgabe, rather than being excised, is rehabilitated as the report of that cognitive map. This is not a mere terminological shift; it is a retro-causal revaluation of the entire introspective tradition. Multi-week traps. In repeated experimental sessions spanning weeks, subjects typically exhibit a drift from Beschreibung toward Kundgabe. Early in training, the novice introspector attempts to follow instructions to describe "immediate texture" [PAGE 242], but as the task becomes familiar, the report collapses into meaningful narration: "the weight feels heavy," "I am expecting a difference," and so forth. In psychophysical weight-discrimination experiments extending over multiple weeks, this drift coincides with criterion shifts rather than sensory-threshold changes—the subject's report begins to track the stimulus (stimulus error [PAGE 242]) more reliably than the sensation. Tolman's rehabilitation of Kundgabe anticipates this: instead of fighting the regression across weeks, he makes the proclamation of sign-gestalts the object of study.

Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Recursive theory of mind enters because introspection requires the subject to model their own mental states while simultaneously reporting on them. Let \(M\) denote the subject's model of their own cognitive architecture. The introspective act is recursively structured:
\[
\mathcal{I}_t = \text{Report}\big( M( \Phi_t ), \; M( M( \Phi_{t-\tau} ) ) \big)
\]
where \(\Phi_t\) is the target mental process at time \(t\), \(\tau\) is report latency, and the inner \(M\) represents the subject's theory of how their own mind represents the stimulus. This recursion introduces two specific failure modes relevant to [PAGE 242].

Crisis habit regression. Under time pressure, divided attention, or affective stress (a "crisis" in the experimental session), the subject's capacity to maintain the structuralist distinction between stuff and meaning degrades. The introspector regresses from Beschreibung to Kundgabe: instead of describing "immediate texture" [PAGE 242], they produce a naive, meaningful report of "what he was thinking about" [PAGE 242]. In the lifted-weight task, this means reporting "heavy" (the stimulus property) rather than the sensory quality. The regression is habitual because Kundgabe is the default, untrained mode [PAGE 242]; crisis strips away the thin veneer of structuralist training.

Counter-trap. The opposite failure occurs when the subject, desperate to avoid Kundgabe, attempts hyper-pure Beschreibung and invents "texture" descriptors that have no stable referent—"a reddish pressure," "a metallic coolness" in a weight-lifting task where no such qualities exist. This is a counter-trap: the attempt to escape naive proclamation generates confabulated stuff-language that is equally unverifiable. The structuralist assumption that stuff is independent of meaning [PAGE 242] encourages precisely this fabrication, because there is no empirical anchor for a texture detached from its functional context.

Clock. Temporal structure modulates both traps. Conscious contents have short persistence; if \(\tau\) (the delay between experience and report) exceeds the decay constant of the content, the introspector reports a reconstructed state rather than an online one. The experimental clock therefore sets an admissibility window: reports must be elicited within the coherence time of the conscious content (on the order of seconds). Beyond that window, Kundgabe is not merely preferable but inevitable, because the subject must narrate the event from memory rather than describe its texture. Tolman's section 9 on [PAGE 242] implicitly acknowledges this by treating Kundgabe as a proclamation about the current organization of the means-end field—something that can be reported even after the immediate sensory texture has faded.

Veto condition: the regression of report on physical mass has slope indistinguishable from unity (\( \partial r / \partial s = 1 \pm 0.05 \)) while regression on independently measured sensation (via magnitude estimation with physical anchors) has slope near zero. Prediction: under this veto, the subject is not reporting conscious content but echoing the stimulus; any model using \(r\) as a target for sensation will overfit physical mass and fail to generalize to novel weight pairs where sensation and mass dissociate. Verifiable via held-out psychometric curve mismatch.

RLVR scenario 2 — Extraneous Kundgabe content.
Counterfactual setup: During the weight task, the subject produces Kundgabe reports about content outside the means-end field (e.g., "I am thinking about my upcoming lunch" or "the lab feels cold") [PAGE 242]. Veto condition: mutual information between report content and lifting force or discrimination accuracy is statistically indistinguishable from zero (\( I(\text{ReportContent}; \text{Performance}) < 0.01 \) bits), while mutual information between report content and an unrelated environmental variable (room temperature) is significant. Prediction: the report is Kundgabe in the naive sense [PAGE 242], not a proclamation of sign-gestalts relevant to the means-end field. A reinforcement learner using these reports as reward-shaping signals will acquire spurious correlations with temperature rather than with weight discrimination. Verifiable by scrambling report-text against performance labels and observing preserved (null) association.

RLVR scenario 3 — Beschribung confabulation under training pressure.
Counterfactual setup: Subjects are intensively trained to avoid Kundgabe and report only "immediate texture" [PAGE 242]. Veto condition: inter-subject variance in texture descriptions exceeds intra-subject test-retest reliability by a factor greater than 3, and descriptions contain cross-modal or category errors (e.g., describing heaviness as "sharp" or "blue") that cannot be mapped onto any psychophysical dimension. Prediction: the structuralist assumption of separable stuff [PAGE 242] has broken down; subjects are confabulating a texture vocabulary because no such independent aspect is accessible. A learner trained on these descriptions will form a non-identifiable latent space with no convergent representation across subjects. Verifiable by split-half reliability analysis and by cross-modal oddity detection.
