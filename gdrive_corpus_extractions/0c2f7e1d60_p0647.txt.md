James writes that "the only cohesions which experience in the literal sense of the word produces in our mind are, as we contended some time back, the proximate laws of nature, and habitudes of concrete things, that heat melts ice, that salt preserves meat, that fish die out of water, and the like" [PAGE 637]. These are not necessary truths but concrete habitudes.

Hop 2: Such truths are stabilised or destabilised by experience. In the first footnote, James states that "a part of these associations grows confirmed by frequent repetition, another part is destroyed by contradictory experiences; and the world becomes divided for us into two provinces, one in which we are at home and anticipate with confidence always the same sequences; another filled with alternating, variable, accidental occurrences" [PAGE 637]. The mind therefore partitions reality into a province of order and a province of accident.

Hop 3: Strict empiricism cannot generate universal order. James contends that "from the point of view of strict empiricism nothing exists but the sum of particular perceptions with their coincidences on the one hand, their contradictions on the other" [PAGE 637], and that passive association yields only "the belief that law and lawlessness rule the world in motley alternation" [PAGE 637].

Hop 4: The search for deeper order is practically motivated. "The first impulse to look for it proceeds from practical needs: where ends must be attained, we must know trustworthy means which infallibly possess a property or produce a result" [PAGE 637].

Hop 5: Practical need is only the occasion, not the full cause. Reflective motives carry the mind beyond mere association into a conviction that James elsewhere compares to "a religious faith rather than like assent to a demonstration" [PAGE 637].

Gray-zone audit: The boundary between consolidated habit and necessary truth is under-specified. James treats the transition as reflective but does not specify the psychological mechanism by which practical need transmutes into metaphysical uniformity. The text oscillates between naturalistic associationism and quasi-religious description: Accident and Exception are personified as capricious powers, and "Exception has a peculiar fascination; it is a subject of astonishment, a θαῦμα" [PAGE 637]. This is a gray zone where psychological explanation borders on mythopoetic narrative. Breakdown regimes: when repetition is sparse, habits fail to consolidate; when contradiction is dense, the province of order contracts. The 1890 introspective method lacks experimental controls to quantify the habit-to-necessity threshold, so any modern operationalization must treat that boundary as a latent parameter rather than an observed constant.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal apparatus below is analytical and is not attributed to James; it is supplied to make his claims testable.

Let X_t ∈ {0,1} indicate co-occurrence of events a and b on trial t. The frequentist association estimator is
f_n(a→b) = (1/n) Σ_{t=1}^n X_t.
Under assumptions of independent, identically distributed trials and stationarity, f_n converges in probability to P(b|a) as n → ∞. Breakdown regime: non-stationarity, adversarial contradiction, or finite-sample sparsity invalidates the limit; James's "contradictory experiences" [PAGE 637] correspond to shocks that drive the empirical frequency away from the prior estimate.

Bayesian belief revision over hypotheses h ∈ H:
P_{t+1}(h | e_{1:t}) = [P(e_t | h) P_t(h | e_{1:t-1})] / P(e_t | e_{1:t-1}),
with probabilities dimensionless in [0,1]. The surprisal of disconfirming evidence e is
I(e) = −log₂ P(e) bits.
As P(e) → 0⁺, I(e) → +∞ bits, a coding-length singularity that marks predictive breakdown of the habit. Shannon entropy H(X) = −Σ_x p(x) log₂ p(x) [bits/symbol] quantifies uncertainty in the sequence of outcomes.

Domain-boundary formalism: a habit L holds over an environmental parameter region Ω (e.g., fish in water; salted meat at low temperature) and fails at ∂Ω. Let θ be the environmental parameter. The conditional expectation P(y | cue, θ) is stable for θ ∈ int(Ω) and collapses toward the accident base rate as θ → ∂Ω. Singularity: at the boundary, the old model's predictive information diverges and a model switch is forced.

Verbatim anchors [PAGE 637]: "that heat melts ice, that salt preserves meat, that fish die out of water"; "a part of these associations grows confirmed by frequent repetition, another part is destroyed by contradictory experiences"; "law and lawlessness rule the world in motley alternation"; "nothing exists but the sum of particular perceptions with their coincidences on the one hand, their contradictions on the other"; "where ends must be attained, we must know trustworthy means which infallibly possess a property or produce a result."

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage runs from British associationism to Jamesian pragmatism. Hume located necessary connection in a "felt determination of the mind" rather than in an objective impression, establishing custom as the psychological ground of expectation. James accepts that concrete habits arise this way—"the babe [looks] for milk from its nurse and not from its father" [PAGE 637]—but denies that passive association can yield necessary truth or universal physical order. Kant's synthetic a priori categories are thereby bypassed: James locates the impulse toward order in practical needs and reflection, not in pure reason. The pragmatist lineage (Peirce, James, Dewey) treats truth as what survives inquiry and works in practice.

Multi-week trap: short observation windows (days) capture repetition-induced confirmation, whereas over weeks contradictory experiences accumulate and destabilise naive extrapolation. James explicitly notes that associations are "confirmed by frequent repetition" yet "destroyed by contradictory experiences" [PAGE 637]. The trap is to mistake a transient confirmation streak for a stable law. A multi-week observational protocol must stratify by contradiction density, not by repetition count alone, because the province of order can shrink even while repetition continues.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under acute stress, working memory and reflective capacity collapse, and the agent re-enacts early-acquired cue-outcome scripts. James's infant expecting milk from the nurse rather than the father illustrates a regressed, cue-bound associative state [PAGE 637].

Recursive theory of mind: Agent A models Agent B as reverting to habit H_b under crisis; B simultaneously models A as reverting to H_a. If both expect mutual regression, the interaction locks into a habit-trap even when reflective alternatives exist, because each agent's model of the other suppresses exploratory behavior.

Counter-trap: an observer may predict regression when the agent has already undergone James's "reflection on the conditions of a true knowledge" and replaced association with a deliberate means-end rule. Attributing crisis behavior to habit when it is actually a stressed application of a learned rule is a false-regression error.

Clock: associative impulses operate on a fast timescale (sub-second to seconds); reflective correction operates on a slower timescale (seconds to minutes). Crisis compresses the decision window below reflective latency, so habitual output becomes the default unless the habit has been automatized into a refractory pathway.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto hierarchy. Physical/ontogenetic layer: the infant's rooting/sucking reflex is gated by olfactory and somatosensory cues; absent the nurse's specific cue bundle, the association "nurse → milk" is vetoed at the sensorimotor interface [PAGE 637]. Biomechanical layer: learned reaching or avoidance trajectories are bounded by anatomy and dynamics; a physical obstacle or limb immobilization vetoes the motor program irrespective of associative strength. Weather/environmental layer: "fish die out of water" [PAGE 637] makes water a hard environmental parameter. Outside the aquatic medium, gill ventilation fails and the proximate law's domain ends at the shoreline boundary. Salt preservation likewise holds only within a bounded temperature/humidity region; beyond it putrefaction proceeds despite salt.

Adjudication: environmental and physical constraints override associative expectancy. When θ crosses ∂Ω, the conditional expectation collapses from the province of confident sequence to the province of "alternating, variable, accidental occurrences" [PAGE 637]. Singularity: at the boundary, predictive information for the old habit diverges (I → ∞ bits) and the agent must switch models or fail.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 (infant milk cue). Counterfactual: the father offers a bottle visually identical to the nurse's but lacking her olfactory and tactile signature. Veto: the incomplete associative cue bundle prevents reflexive acceptance. Prediction: sucking latency increases significantly and refusal rate exceeds baseline across n = 30 infants in video-coded feeding trials. Verifiable: coded latency and refusal are observable, countable outcomes.

Scenario 2 (salted meat preservation). Counterfactual: meat is salted but stored at 30 °C and 80 % relative humidity. Veto: temperature and humidity cross the preservation boundary, so proteolysis and microbial growth proceed despite salt. Prediction: time-to-spoil collapses nonlinearly relative to a refrigerated control. Verifiable: total viable count (CFU/g) measured daily for 14 days provides a falsifiable trajectory.

Scenario 3 (aquatic fish removed from water). Counterfactual: an obligate teleost and an amphibious fish are both exposed to air. Veto: gill respiration requires water; the law "fish die out of water" [PAGE 637] has a taxonomic boundary. Prediction: obligate fish lose equilibrium within minutes and approach 100 % mortality within hours, whereas the amphibious fish survives. Verifiable: time-to-loss-of-equilibrium and 24-hour survival rate are measurable across species.

None of these scenarios invokes betting lines, spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds.
