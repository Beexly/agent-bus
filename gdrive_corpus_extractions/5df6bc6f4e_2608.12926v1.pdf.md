The derivation chain begins with the sensor layer: Local Positioning Systems (LPS) across the Handball Bundesliga generate tracking data from which event sequences are automatically recognized [PAGE 2]. These sequences are rendered into a SPADL-derived schema with four action types—passes, dribbles, field shots, and seven-meter penalty shots—and pass success is imputed by verifying whether the passing team retains possession for the subsequent action, because explicit pass-success labels are missing in portions of the tracking data [PAGE 3]. The first 30 seconds of each match are removed to exclude pre-match ceremonial passing that would skew transition probabilities [PAGE 3].

Handball-xT (H-xT) is a spatial Markov chain over court zones. Let xT(z) denote the probability that a possession currently in zone z leads to a goal. Transitions are governed by movement and shot probabilities and are solved recursively, as detailed in the football xT reference [19] cited on [PAGE 3]. Any ball-progression action from z_start to z_end is valued by ΔxT = xT(z_end) − xT(z_start) [PAGE 3]. The critical design choice is discretization. Rectangular grids are ill-suited because grid lines intersect the curved 6m goal crease and 9m free-throw arc arbitrarily, destroying tactical interpretability; coaches confirmed this during consultations [PAGE 3]. The authors therefore built a handball-native layout with angular boundaries originating from the goals and concentric divisions matching the 6m and 9m lines, aggregating the entire defensive half into a single zone because defensive-half zone values converge to near-identical expected threat, with residual differences representing spurious noise from action scarcity [PAGE 3].

Robustness is quantified through the simulation methodology of [2], adopted on [PAGE 3]: B = 1000 simulated seasons are drawn by sampling 306 matches (one HBL season) from the 2021/22–2022/23 development set. For each simulation b, model M_b is fit and the maximum zone-level absolute deviation from the ground-truth model M_full is computed as D_b = max_z |xT_b(z) − xT_full(z)|. The robustness metric R90 is the 90th percentile of D_b across simulations [PAGE 3–4]. Applying the decision rule of selecting the most flexible configuration with R90 ≤ 0.03, the native layout supports 67 zones while the grid model is restricted to 45 zones [PAGE 4]. This is not a cosmetic preference: Figs. 1b–1c show that the native surface reveals a low-threat trough directly outside the 9m line (defensive contact raises turnovers) and the highest threat outside the crease in wing corners, where attackers can jump into the 6m crease with restricted defensive contact [PAGE 3]. H-xT excludes 7m penalties due to missing foul coordinates, meaning central 6m zones are likely undervalued [PAGE 3].

Handball-VAEP (H-VAEP) extends this by conditioning on game context. A state S_i is represented by the last k actions, and P_scores(S_i) and P_concedes(S_i) are the probabilities that the possessing team scores or concedes within the next N = 10 actions [PAGE 4]. Action value is V(a_i) = ΔP_scores − ΔP_concedes [PAGE 4]. Feature engineering is sport-specific: exact cumulative scores are replaced with a bucketed score difference (1b) to avoid overfitting sparse high-scoring patterns; polar angle is replaced by the goal angle between rays from the ball to the two posts (2) because handball set plays permit shots from almost any position; and elapsed seconds since gaining possession (3) is added to capture tactical pace under the passive-play rule [PAGE 5]. Model selection favors tuned XGBoost with k = 3 over CatBoost and Random Forest [PAGE 5–6].

Gray-zone audit. Team-identity leakage rises monotonically with context length: a classifier trained to predict the executing team from sequence context achieves Macro-AUC 0.608 at k = 1, 0.664 at k = 3, and 0.698 at k = 6 [PAGE 6]. Held-out evaluation uses a rolling training setup: models trained on season n − 1 value season n, ensuring every reported probability is out of sample across 2.83M valued game states from 2023/24 to 2025/26 [PAGE 6–7]. Base rates are 17.3% scoring and 3.0% conceding [PAGE 7]. Successful shots are deterministically replaced with probability 100% because a goal fixes the outcome by definition [PAGE 7]. Calibration is tight (expected calibration error 0.006 for scoring, 0.001 for conceding), though the conceding model overestimates risk above 80% where fewer than 0.02% of predictions lie [PAGE 7].

\[
R_{90} = \operatorname{quantile}_{0.90}\bigl(\{D_b\}_{b=1}^{B}\bigr), \quad B = 1000.
\]

\[

The scientific lineage of H-xT and H-VAEP is explicitly trans-sport. Expected Threat originates in football analytics (Singh, 2019, cited as [19] on [PAGE 13]), and VAEP derives from Decroos et al.’s action-valuation framework for soccer ([5, 6], [PAGE 13]), operationalized through the SPADL event schema adapted here by omitting body-part features [PAGE 3]. The broader principle of valuing actions via expected possession value has been applied across invasion sports ([20], [PAGE 2]), with methodological divergence between discrete state-space representations (zones or transition matrices in hockey and rugby, [14, 17], [PAGE 2]) and continuous court models such as basketball/rugby EPV ([3, 18], [PAGE 2]). Sport-specific constraints cited as precedents include power plays in ice hockey ([10], [PAGE 2]), shot clocks in basketball ([16], [PAGE 2]), tackle limits in rugby ([17], [PAGE 2]), touch phases in volleyball ([21], [PAGE 2]), and down-and-distance in American football ([22], [PAGE 2]). Handball’s own prior work includes Expected Goals frameworks limited to terminal shots ([1, 11], [PAGE 2]) and the continuous PIVOT framework for tracking-data action valuation ([12], [PAGE 13]). The paper’s novel contribution is the first event-based handball framework incorporating native court zones and passive-play constraints, validated under the structured meta-analytics framework of [4, 7, 13] ([PAGE 2]).

Multi-week traps. The rolling-training design—fit on season \(n-1\), value season \(n\)—assumes tactical stationarity across adjacent seasons [PAGE 6]. This is a retrospective trap: if a club changes coaching philosophy, roster composition, or defensive emphasis between seasons, the out-of-sample probabilities may remain calibrated but lose discriminative validity for the new regime. The paper documents divergent team systems within a single season (SC Magdeburg’s isolation-heavy backs generating 38% of value through dribbles versus SG Flensburg-Handewitt’s passing-dominant backs at 65%) [PAGE 8]. A multi-week trap emerges when context length \(k\) is extended to capture slow tactical shifts: \(k=6\) improves ROC-AUC but pushes team-identity leakage Macro-AUC to 0.698 versus 0.664 at \(k=3\) [PAGE 6], meaning the model begins to memorize team style rather than generalize action quality. Another trap is the 500/250-minute player filter [PAGE 7]: mid-season injuries, suspensions, or rotational crises can push borderline players below the threshold, inducing survivorship bias in cross-season reliability. Finally, the five-season window (2021/22–2025/26) spans LPS deployment maturity; early-season event-detection noise ([PAGE 11]) may non-stationarily inflate transition variance in the development set relative to the held-out set.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under performance pressure, professional handball clubs exhibit a recursive habit of regressing to box-score heuristics: goals, assists, and the Handball Performance Index (HPI), which aggregates defensive and disciplinary actions (blocks, steals, suspensions) outside the offensive on-ball schema [PAGE 1, PAGE 11]. The HPI’s fixed weights cannot credit build-up play, systematically undervaluing players who create scoring opportunities well before the terminal shot [PAGE 1]. The paper’s counter-trap is H-VAEP’s attribution of value across non-terminal actions: in the Fig. 5a fast break, the left back’s long pass receives +0.365, the pivot’s dribble +0.220 and shot +0.292, and the goalkeeper’s outlet pass +0.141—a contribution invisible to box-score statistics [PAGE 9]. In Fig. 5b, the model discriminates among visually similar passes: a neutral centre-back ball (−0.007), a slightly positive return (+0.053), and a hard pass into the left back’s run (+0.163) [PAGE 9–10]. The recursive theory of mind here is that H-VAEP simulates the opponent’s likely response implicitly through \(P_{\text{concedes}}\) and the transition structure, assigning credit to the action that shifts the possession into a higher-threat state even if the eventual shot is missed [PAGE 9].

Clock and passive play. Handball’s temporal structure imposes a hard clock via the passive-play rule, which creates shot pressure as possession elapsed time grows [PAGE 5]. The feature \(t_{\text{poss}}\) (elapsed seconds since gaining possession) distinguishes rapid fast breaks from slower set attacks [PAGE 5]. Additionally, the first 30 seconds of each match are excised to remove ceremonial passing that would corrupt transition probabilities [PAGE 3], and the VAEP horizon is fixed at \(N=10\) actions [PAGE 4]. Context length \(k\) operates as a secondary clock: increasing \(k\) deepens sequential reasoning but monotonically raises team-identity leakage (Macro-AUC 0.608 → 0.664 → 0.698 for \(k=1,3,6\)) [PAGE 6]. The selected \(k=3\) is therefore a metacognitive compromise—deep enough to capture tactical patterns, shallow enough to avoid the model “knowing” the team rather than the action quality.

Recursive failure mode. If a coaching staff in crisis abandons its established system mid-season (e.g., shifting from isolation to structured passing), the \(k=3\) context window may misattribute value because the preceding actions no longer reflect the current tactical regime. Conversely, if the club over-trusts \(k=6\) for its apparent predictive gain, the leakage trap produces rankings biased by team identity rather than individual contribution. The paper mitigates this by reporting out-of-sample held-season performance and face validity with elite-player awards (three IHF World Player of the Year awards among the top ten H-VAEP/10o players) [PAGE 8], but the recursive tension between sequence depth and identity leakage remains unresolved.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Handball is played indoors on a standardized court (length × width with 6m goal creases and 9m free-throw arcs) [PAGE 3]. Because matches occur in climate-controlled arenas, weather is a null veto: no temperature, wind, or precipitation variable enters H-xT or H-VAEP, and the source text contains no weather modulation. The physical veto is instead geometric: rectangular grid lines intersect the curved 6m crease and 9m arc arbitrarily, which coaches confirmed as tactically unintuitive; the handball-native layout uses angular boundaries from the goals and concentric 6m/9m divisions [PAGE 3]. Aggregating the defensive half into one zone is a physical veto against overfitting: defensive-half xT values converge, and residual differences are spurious noise from action scarcity [PAGE 3].

Biomechanical layer. Position-specific biomechanics are reflected in the action-type decomposition [PAGE 8]. Wings generate 55% of value through dribbles and 33% through shots, finishing fast breaks and attacking the crease from narrow angles where they can jump into the 6m zone with restricted defensive contact [PAGE 3, PAGE 8]. Pivots derive 51% of value from shots and 53% from dribbles, but passing is slightly negative (−4%) because a pivot receiving at the 6m line who cannot turn toward goal usually plays backward, preserving possession without improving scoring opportunity while carrying considerable turnover risk in congested space [PAGE 8]. Back players generate 55–63% of value through passing, consistent with playmaking [PAGE 8]. These are hard biomechanical constraints: a model that credited pivot passing positively or treated wing passes as creative would violate the observed geometry of the 6m crease.

Veto register (hard boundaries):
1. Defensive actions are not automatically recognized by tracking systems; defensive events are absent from the schema, so defensive specialists are excluded by the 250 offensive-minute filter [PAGE 7, PAGE 11]. Veto: any rating claiming to evaluate total player value (offense + defense) is invalid.
2. Off-ball behavior (spatial denial in defense, gap creation in offense) requires player trajectories, not discrete event data [PAGE 11]. Veto: H-VAEP cannot value off-ball gravity or screening.
3. Seven-meter foul coordinates are missing; H-xT excludes 7m penalties, so central 6m zones are likely undervalued [PAGE 3]. Veto: xT surfaces near the 6m line are incomplete for penalty situations.
4. Pass success is imputed via subsequent possession retention because labels are missing [PAGE 3]. Veto: turnover probability on passes is inferred, not directly observed.
5. First 30 seconds of each match are removed to exclude ceremonial passing [PAGE 3]. Veto: pre-match possession sequences cannot be used in transition fitting.
6. Event-detection noise and false positives currently prevent robust identification of possession boundaries [PAGE 11]. Veto: any downstream model requiring perfect possession segmentation will inherit false boundary errors.

Adjudication: Each veto is binary and non-negotiable within the current system. If a deployment violates veto 1 or 2, the output ceases to be H-VAEP as validated and becomes an unvalidated hybrid. If veto 6 is breached (e.g., possession-boundary error rate above the empirically observed false-positive level), Brier scores and ROC-AUC from Table 1 no longer apply.
