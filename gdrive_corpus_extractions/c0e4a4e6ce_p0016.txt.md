[PAGE 16] supports the veto that layer (2) does not contain layer (1), and layer (3) does not contain layer (2) as a phenomenal copy. Each recursive step crosses a boundary defined by simple ideas, not by qualia replication.

Physical/neural layer: The nerve-current is a hard veto. From [PAGE 16], "If the nerve-current be not given, nothing else will take its place." Formally, \(N=0\Rightarrow S=\varnothing\). This veto is upstream of all cognitive operations. If the afferent pathway from the tooth or inflamed joint is blocked, no simple idea of sensation concerning that toothache or gout can be generated, and Locke’s boundary \(\sigma(\Sigma)\) receives no sensory input from that object.

Biomechanical/neural transduction layer: Tissue state (gout inflammation, tooth pathology) must transduce into nerve-current. If the biomechanical transducer is damaged or the nerve is severed, the veto holds even if the tissue pathology persists. The text does not specify transduction mechanics, but the necessity of the nerve-current implies that any interruption between the noxious stimulus and the neural signal vetoes conscious sensation of that object.

Therefore, relative to the provided source, the ambient veto set is null: \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{weather}}=\varnothing\). Any claim that weather modulates the Lockean boundary or the nerve-current gate on this page would be an extraneous variable, unverifiable from [PAGE 16], and outside the adjudication domain.

Adjudication: The nerve-current veto dominates all layers because it is the necessary condition for sensation, and sensation is the necessary condition for conception. Without \(N>0\), the biomechanical layer cannot deliver a sensory simple idea, the cognitive layer cannot form a thought about that specific sensation, and the weather layer is irrelevant because the source text contains no such variable. The veto proof is conditional on the brain–consciousness nexus remaining unmodeled: we can prove that no sensation occurs without nerve-current, but we cannot prove that nerve-current alone produces a specific conscious content.

Scenario 1: Peripheral nerve block before the painful episode.
Veto condition: Anesthetic sets \(N=0\) at the tooth/gout site during the episode.
Prediction: The subject reports no sensation of toothache or gout during the block. After the block wears off, the subject may form a thought *about* the numbness or the later pain, but that thought is a new simple idea (of reflection or later sensation), not a copy of the blocked pain. Verifiable by comparing nociceptive reflexes and self-report under block versus placebo.
RLVR outcome: If the subject nonetheless reports pain-level sensation during a verified nerve block, the necessity axiom \(N=0\Rightarrow S=\varnothing\) is falsified relative to [PAGE 16].

Scenario 2: Bainian propositional suppression with sensory exposure.
Veto condition: Subject views scarlet but is prevented from forming any judgment or concept (e.g., pre-linguistic subject, or aphasic subject with blocked verbal report).
Prediction: The subject can behaviorally discriminate scarlet from other colors (sensation operates as a necessary preparation), but cannot truthfully assert "this is scarlet" as knowledge. Sensation of scarlet is not knowledge *about* scarlet, matching James’s correction of Bain on [PAGE 16]. Verifiable by forced-choice discrimination accuracy versus verbal-report accuracy.
RLVR outcome: If the subject produces accurate verbal knowledge of scarlet without any sensory discrimination capacity, Bain’s claim and James’s boundary are both undermined.

Veto condition: Subject is repeatedly asked to think of past gout and to make the thought *feel* as painful as the original sensation.
Prediction: Across trials, the subject rates the retrospective thought as pleasant, neutral, or cognitive, never as phenomenally identical to the original pain. This confirms the non-isomorphism on [PAGE 16]: "the thought of the same gout as bygone is pleasant, and in no respect resembles the earlier mental state." Verifiable by phenomenological rating scales over \(N\ge 20\) trials.
RLVR outcome: If the subject can voluntarily recreate the original painful qualia through thought alone, James’s claim that thought does not contain the original pain is falsified.
