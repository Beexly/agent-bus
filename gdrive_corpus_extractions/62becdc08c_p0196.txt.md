Define contracting set C and outsider set O. Contract vector c ∈ ℝᵐ. Private payoff π_i(c): C → ℝ. Outsider effect e_o(c): O → ℝ. Social welfare W(c) = Σ_{i∈C} π_i(c) + Σ_{o∈O} e_o(c), under quasilinearity and cardinal interpersonally comparable utility (units: utils; numéraire in money). Private FOC for each i: ∂π_i/∂c_j = 0. Social FOC: ∂W/∂c_j = Σ_{i∈C} ∂π_i/∂c_j + Σ_{o∈O} ∂e_o/∂c_j = 0. Divergence Δ_j = Σ_{o∈O} ∂e_o/∂c_j. If Δ_j > 0, private c_j is below the social optimum (positive externality; under-provision). If Δ_j < 0, private c_j exceeds the social optimum (negative externality; over-provision). This is a formal restatement of Knight's claim that voluntary agreements "will not be carried far enough" or "will be carried too far" [PAGE 1].

Breakdown: preference-revelation singularity when agents can misreport willingness to pay; voluntary bounties fail under non-excludability, exactly as Knight notes such services "must be encouraged by bounties or actually taken over... by public agencies" [PAGE 1] or they will not reach maximum benefit.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage: Knight (1921), [PAGE 1], stands alongside Pigou (1920) in the early welfare economics of externalities, though Knight emphasizes institutional "social interference" rather than Pigouvian taxation. The argument prefigures Coase (1960): if property rights over e_o are defined and transaction costs are zero, bargaining internalizes the divergence Δ_j. However, Knight's phrase "practicable way of assessing these benefits" [PAGE 1] anticipates the transaction-cost critique: assessment itself is costly and may be impossible.

Multi-week traps: In a static model, Δ_j is a one-period wedge. Over multiple weeks, an unpriced externality compounds. Week 1: land use imposes uncompensated harm. Week 2: neighbors make irreversible capital adjustments (defensive expenditures, relocation). Week 3: sunk costs create hold-up; bargaining positions harden. Knight's "enormous uncompensated transfers of values" [PAGE 1] thus become path-dependent transfers rather than correctable flow externalities. The trap is that waiting for spontaneous market correction allows physical and legal configurations to entrench, after which the only remedy is redistributive or coercive, not Coasean. A multi-week lock-in arises because the stock of defensive capital and the distribution of entitlements at week N determine the feasible set of contracts at week N+1, making early mispricing of externalities irreversible in practice.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Level-0 reasoning: each contracting party maximizes π_i and ignores e_o, relying on the "mechanical effectiveness" of free contract [PAGE 1]. Level-1 regression: if I believe others ignore outsiders, I also ignore outsiders to avoid being exploited; this is crisis habit regression to pure private contract. Level-2 counter-trap: I anticipate that others will regress, so I support preemptive social interference (bounties, public agencies) to restore alignment. But level-3 recursion introduces capture risk: if agents expect interference, they strategically overstate harm ("thought to do so" [PAGE 1]) or understate benefits to capture transfers.

The clock variable τ is the time before investments sink. Once τ expires, voluntary agreement is infeasible and only public agency remains. Knight's text implies that the window for assessment and bargaining is narrow: without a practicable benefit-assessment mechanism, the clock runs out and uncompensated transfers are locked in [PAGE 1]. Breakdown occurs when higher-order beliefs about the impartiality of the interfering agency are themselves subject to Knightian uncertainty—agents cannot assign probabilities to whether the public agency will correctly internalize e_o, so they default to private-contract heuristics even when those heuristics are socially suboptimal.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three physical/biomechanical/weather layers impose vetoes that no contract can override.

Physical layer: Land use and industrial developments [PAGE 1] generate externalities governed by transport dynamics, e.g. concentration C(x,t) obeying ∂C/∂t = D∇²C − v·∇C + S(x,t). If the spatial support of C exceeds the set of identifiable contracting parties O, the externality is non-local and non-enumerable. Veto condition: the externality field cannot be partitioned into contractible state-contingent claims because affected parties are diffuse or unknown.

Biomechanical layer: Human receptors of neighborhood damage have dose-response thresholds and latent health effects. If damage D_bio is sub-clinical or irreversible, it is unverifiable ex post. Veto when measurement-error variance σ²_meas > |D_bio|, making breach of contract undetectable and therefore unenforceable.

Weather layer: Although not explicit on [PAGE 1], land-use externalities (smoke, runoff, noise) are modulated by atmospheric state ω (wind direction, precipitation). The affected set O(c, ω) is endogenous to weather. Veto when O changes on a timescale shorter than contract renegotiation, so that the set of rights-holders is unstable.

Adjudication: A veto is binding when all three layers hold simultaneously—non-local transport, unverifiable biomechanical harm, and weather-dependent affected sets—rendering private agreement incapable of achieving the social optimum. Only public agency or bounty can act, per Knight [PAGE 1]. If the agency lacks information about ω and D_bio, government failure duplicates market failure; the breakdown regime is mutual opacity across physical and institutional layers.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
