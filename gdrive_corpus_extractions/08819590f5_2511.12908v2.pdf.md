<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The gated reward is, formally, a first-order theory-of-mind device: it forces the policy to hold a *model of its own competence*. The class label $c(\tau)$ encodes "can I (the base policy) solve this from the initial 8 frames?" [PAGE 9]. The RL objective then rewards acting *consistently* with that self-model. This is elegant but fragile along three axes.

**Crisis-habit regression.** Under distribution pressure, a policy tends to collapse to its lowest-entropy habit. Here two attractors exist: (i) *always answer immediately* (maximizes $R_{\text{acc}}$ on the easy majority, forfeits $R_{\text{tool}}$ on Class-A), and (ii) *always retrieve* (forfeits $R_{\text{tool}}$ on Class-B, and risks the disjoint $-0.05$ format cliff plus grounding failure). The asymmetric gate is precisely engineered to make neither attractor globally optimal — the paper states "the model cannot earn $R_{\text{tool}}$ by blindly invoking the tool on every sample, as doing so on Class-B samples yields zero bonus" [PAGE 9]. The 1.4% "Incomplete Exploration" and 42.9% grounding-failure rates [PAGE 13] show the balance holds but does not solve retrieval.

**Counter-trap.** Because $c(\tau)$ is computed *once*, before the 300-step run, it is a stale equilibrium estimate. As $\pi_\theta$ improves, prompts labeled Class-B (base-solvable) may remain correctly labeled, but prompts labeled Class-A (base-failed) could become base-solvable via SFT-induced perception gains — yet the reward still demands a tool call for the $\tfrac12$ bonus. This is a self-model that no longer matches the updated agent: a recursive inconsistency the design cannot detect mid-run.

**Clock.** The temporal budget is the "clock": the anti-redundancy rule ($A_t\neq A_{t-1}$) plus the format-validity gate cap runaway trajectories, and the $\sim1.8$ net retrieved frames/query implies the learned clock mostly reads "stop early." The model has learned *when* far better than *where* — consistent with the error census putting localization (42.9%) far ahead of decision errors (Incomplete Exploration 1.4%).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical and information-theoretic vetoes bound any "reasoning" win, because reasoning cannot recover information the sensor never captured.

**Temporal Nyquist veto.** Let the source video run at $f_s$ fps (frame spacing $1/f_s$ s). An event of physical duration $\delta$ (s) is resolvable only if the retrieved sampling spacing satisfies $\Delta t \le \delta/2$ (Nyquist-type condition). The tool returns $k$ frames over window $[a,b]$ (indices), so $\Delta t=(b-a)/(k\,f_s)$. If the event lies *outside* $[a,b]$, capture probability is exactly $0$ regardless of $k$ or $\theta$ — this is the mathematical form of the 42.9% Tool Grounding Failure: "invoked the tool but retrieved a temporal window that missed the critical event, making the answer impossible" [PAGE 13]. No reward reshaping can violate this; it is a hard veto.

**Spatial-resolution veto.** Input is fixed at "640×360" [PAGE 10]. Fine-grained recognition (jersey color, finger/limb contact) is bounded by pixel density on the acted region. The 37.1% Visual Hallucination rate [PAGE 13] is partly this floor: "retrieved the correct frames but misidentified objects, actions, or attributes." A correct temporal window with insufficient spatial resolution still yields an incorrect answer — resolution vetoes accuracy independently of retrieval.

**Occlusion/pose "weather" veto.** In interactive sports, the decisive contact is often occluded at every captured frame (bodies overlap at the foul instant). This is the domain analog of a weather veto: adversarial capture geometry that no sampling policy or reasoning depth can penetrate. The foul-detection example [PAGE 12, Fig. 3] succeeds only because frames 30–60 *do* contain an unoccluded push; the counterfactual where contact is hidden is unrecoverable.

**Proof of veto (composite).** Given event $(t^\*,\delta)$ and policy emitting window $[a,b]$ with $k$ frames at $f_s$: if $t^\*\notin[a,b]$, then $P(\text{capture})=0 \Rightarrow \max_\theta \mathrm{acc}=$ base rate. If $t^\*\in[a,b]$ but $(b-a)/(k f_s) > \delta/2$, aliasing occurs and $\mathrm{acc}$ degrades toward chance on temporal-order questions. Therefore overall accuracy is upper-bounded by $P(t^\*\in[a,b])\cdot\mathbb{1}[\Delta t\le\delta/2]$, a purely geometric ceiling independent of the RL objective. This formalizes why the authors themselves name temporal precision, not reasoning, as "the primary issue" [PAGE 14].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

The reward is *nominally* RLVR (an accuracy signal), but it is LLM-judged, so "verifiable" is aspirational. Three concrete veto scenarios, each falsifiable:

**Veto 1 — zero-variance group ($\sigma_r=0$).** Counterfactual: for a prompt where all $G=8$ rollouts are correct (saturated-easy) or all malformed (constant $-0.05$), Eq. 2 gives $A_i=0/0$. *Prediction:* these prompts contribute $\approx 0$ gradient and are effectively wasted rollouts; the fraction of wasted compute should be *highest in the first ~dozens of steps* (before the policy differentiates) and should shrink as accuracy spreads. If logged, I predict measurable early-training advantage-norm collapse on easy sports (Soccer, Basketball) before hard ones (Diving, Fencing).

**Veto 2 — judge false-negatives on subjective tasks.** Counterfactual: a semantically valid commentary or a defensible coaching critique scored low by the Claude 4.5 judge yields a spurious low $R_{\text{acc}}$, poisoning the sign of $A_i$. *Prediction:* reward variance on Live Commentary (22.93) and Assessment & Coaching (19.19) [PAGE 11] is dominated by *judge* noise, not policy noise; re-scoring with a second judge should shift these two dimensions more than the two "objective" dimensions (Fine-Grained 49.89, Rule 41.20). If a held-out human re-grade moves these numbers by more than the 1.97-point GPT-5 gap, the SOTA claim is within judge variance.

**Veto 3 — stale-class misalignment.** Counterfactual: Class-A/B labels are frozen pre-RL [PAGE 9]. After SFT-induced perception gains, a Class-A prompt may become base-solvable, yet Eq. 5 still demands a tool call for the $\tfrac12$ bonus, rewarding *unnecessary* retrieval and reintroducing the exact reward hacking the gate was built to kill — now in reverse. *Prediction:* average frames/query should *rise* over the 300 steps on subsets where the base competence improved most, and the net observed $\bar f=9.81$ masks two opposing drifts (down on newly-easy Class-A, up on mislabeled ones). Instrumenting per-class frame counts across steps would confirm or refute this.

**Cross-veto interaction.** Veto 1 and Veto 3 compound: stale labels increase the count of degenerate groups (policy and label disagree → uniform low reward → $\sigma_r\to0$), so mislabeling both misdirects and *silences* learning. The single most diagnostic missing figure in the paper is the per-step distribution of $\sigma_r$ (fraction of zero-variance groups); its absence is the deepest reproducibility and validity gap after the unpublished $\varepsilon,\beta$.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
