Physical veto: actual cautery delivers thermal energy Q [J] to skin. If Q exceeds the necrosis threshold Q_necrosis, protein denaturation and capillary damage occur irrespective of subjective report. The reported "dry scorch... with no aftermark" on the suggested side implies either that Q was below Q_necrosis on that side (a physical dosing difference masquerading as suggestion) or that suggestion suppressed neurogenic inflammation and subsequent edema [PAGE 612]. Without thermocouple measurement of h, A, ΔT, and Δt [W·m⁻²·K⁻¹, m², K, s], the physical layer cannot be cleared.

Biomechanical veto: the stigmata analogy relies on "tracing lines or pressing objects" on skin [PAGE 612]. Mechanical pressure produces transient blanching and reactive hyperemia through capillary compression and release, independent of any mental suggestion. The erythema and bleeding James attributes to line suggestion may be entirely explained by pressure-induced vasomotor response. Because no sham-tracing control (tracing with no suggestion) is described, the biomechanical layer remains unbroken and vetoes the causal attribution.

Weather veto: ambient temperature and humidity govern cutaneous blood flow, sweat-mediated cooling, and blister roof stability. A burn in a dry, cold environment forms a dry scorch; the same burn in a warm, humid environment may macerate and blister [PAGE 612 implicitly via comparative outcomes]. The two sides of Delbœuf's experiment were necessarily at the same ambient weather, but if the subject was moved, or if the suggestion session altered local blood flow enough to change conductive heat loss, the microclimate at each site differed. No meteorological data (ambient T [K], relative humidity [% RH], air velocity [m·s⁻¹]) are recorded [PAGE 612].

Verdict: the claim is not veto-proof. The physical layer lacks dosimetry; the biomechanical layer is confounded by pressure; the weather layer is unobserved. Any one of these can produce the observed asymmetry without invoking suggestion as a tissue-nutrition operator.
