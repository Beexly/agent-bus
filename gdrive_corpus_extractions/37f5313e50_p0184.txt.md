If inverse-probability weighting by estimated fertility-restored sample inclusion restores OR_D > 1 with p < 0.05, the veto is not triggered; if OR_D remains ≈ 1 after weighting, the hereditary claim is vetoed by selection.

Scenario 2 — Shared-environment veto. Verifiable condition: parent-offspring pairs sharing the same occupation or household (e.g., both coal miners, same parish) account for > 60% of D-D co-occurrences, and stratifying by occupation E eliminates the association. Counterfactual prediction: marginal OR_D > 1 but conditional OR_D | E = e ≈ 1 for all strata e, with likelihood-ratio test comparing models with vs. without D^{(1)} → D^{(2)} term non-significant (p > 0.10) after adjusting for E. Under this outcome the observed clustering is fully mediated by home/occupation [PAGE 184], and the genetic/hereditary claim is vetoed.

Scenario 3 — Sex-specific structural veto. Verifiable condition: father→son and mother→daughter transmission are strong (τ^{m→s}_D, τ^{f→d}_D > 1.5) while father→daughter and mother→son are null (τ^{m→d}_D, τ^{f→s}_D ≈ 1), or the reverse pattern. Counterfactual prediction: a homogeneous model τ_D constrained equal across all four parent-sex × offspring-sex cells fails a likelihood-ratio test (χ²₃, p < 0.01), and an autosomal-additive genetic model is rejected in favor of sex-linked or culturally sex-specific transmission. If the data instead support homogeneity, this veto is not triggered; if heterogeneity matches the null-diagonal pattern, the simple Galtonian "disease A descends from disease A" hypothesis is structurally vetoed.
