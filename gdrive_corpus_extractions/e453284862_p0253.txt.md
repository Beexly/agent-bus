To rigorously formalize this without resorting to toy equations, we must map these psychological constructs onto information theory, statistical mechanics, and dynamical systems.

First, the "sign-gestalt" represents a stable cognitive attractor—a specific configuration of perception, memory, and inference that dictates a habitual response. In an energy landscape, this is a deep potential well. The "initial means-end-readiness" is the system trapped in this local minimum. The "final ultimate means-end-capacity" is the global minimum representing the optimal solution to the environmental problem. Tolman argues that the barrier between these states is not fixed; it depends on the agent's internal state, specifically the "lability" of their perceptions and mnemonizations. High lability equates to low activation energy required to escape the current attractor and explore the state space. This directly maps to the concept of entropy and temperature in statistical thermodynamics. An agent with high creative ability operates at a higher effective cognitive temperature, allowing for stochastic tunneling or thermal hopping across epistemic barriers.

Second, Tolman asserts that creative ideation works through "actual or hypothetical particulars" and that there is fundamentally no such thing as "abstract" thought [PAGE 1]. This challenges the symbolic AI paradigm (GOFAI) which relies on manipulating abstract symbols detached from sensory experience. Instead, Tolman's view aligns with grounded cognition and enactive approaches. The "running over of presented or imagined particulars" suggests that cognition is a continuous simulation of embodied interactions within a manifold of possible sensorimotor contingencies. When Sultan the chimpanzee solves the problem by "thinking of pushing rather than pulling," he is not manipulating abstract logic gates; he is simulating physical particulars—the feel of the object, the spatial constraints, the force vectors involved in pushing versus pulling. This process can be modeled as a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling process over a high-dimensional space of simulated actions (particulars). The "mnemonizations" act as priors that bias the sampling distribution.

Third, the multi-hop derivation involves integrating these concepts into a unified framework of purposive behavior. We start with the assumption that behavior is goal-directed (teleonomic). The organism maintains a predictive model of its environment (the sign-gestalt). When faced with a discrepancy between expected and actual outcomes (an error signal), the system must update its model. If the current sign-gestalt is too rigid (low lability), the system remains trapped in a maladaptive loop (perseveration). If the sign-gestalt is labile (high lability), the system can rapidly generate new hypotheses (new readies) by recombining past experiences (particulars). This recombination is not random but guided by the structure of the environment and the organism's bodily constraints. The "hypothetical particulars" are essentially counterfactual simulations: "What would happen if I pushed instead of pulled?" These simulations consume computational resources (energy) but yield high informational gain by revealing new pathways in the means-end space.

A critical gray-zone audit reveals the tension between stability and plasticity. A system that is entirely labile becomes noise-sensitive and loses coherent identity (catastrophic forgetting). A system that is entirely stable becomes dogmatic and incapable of learning. Tolman's "creative-ability" sits precisely at this edge of chaos. It implies a dynamic balance where existing structures are just unstable enough to permit transformation but stable enough to maintain continuity of purpose. This relates to the concept of metastability in neural dynamics, where brain regions transiently synchronize and desynchronize to allow for flexible integration of information. The "mnemonizations" provide the scaffold for this metastable dance, anchoring the flow of particulars to previously validated patterns while allowing for novelty. Thus, creative ideation is not a departure from the concrete but an intensive exploitation of concrete possibilities through rapid, embodied simulation.

**1. Definition of Sign-Gestalt Stability ($S_{\mathcal{G}}$)**
Let $\mathcal{X}$ be the high-dimensional space of perceptual-motor states (particulars). A sign-gestalt $G$ is defined as a probability distribution $P_G(\mathbf{x})$ over $\mathcal{X}$, representing the organism's current expectation and readiness. We quantify the "lability" or instability of this gestalt using the Shannon Entropy $H(G)$:
$$ H(G) = - \int_{\mathcal{X}} P_G(\mathbf{x}) \log_2 P_G(\mathbf{x}) \, d\mathbf{x} \quad [\text{bits}] $$
However, mere entropy does not capture rigidity. A uniform distribution has high entropy but may represent total ignorance rather than a specific, rigid habit. We define the *Effective Depth* of the potential well associated with $G$, denoted as $\Delta E_G$, using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the current distribution and a maximally uncertain prior $U$ (representing pure exploratory noise), scaled by a cognitive temperature parameter $T_c$ (in Kelvin-equivalent information units, via Boltzmann constant $k_B$):
$$ \Delta E_G = T_c \cdot D_{KL}(P_G \| U) = T_c \int_{\mathcal{X}} P_G(\mathbf{x}) \log_2 \frac{P_G(\mathbf{x})}{U(\mathbf{x})} \, d\mathbf{x} \quad [\text{Joules}] $$
*Assumption:* $U(\mathbf{x})$ is a reference measure reflecting the inherent noise floor of the sensory apparatus.
*Breakdown Regime:* This metric breaks down when $P_G$ develops singularities (Dirac deltas), implying infinite confidence in a single point, leading to zero capacity for perturbation.

**2. Operator for Creative Transition ($\mathcal{L}_{creat}$)**
Tolman states that individuals with equal final capacities differ in the ease of achieving new readinesses due to "creative-ability" [PAGE 1]. Let $\tau$ be the characteristic time to transition from an initial means-end-readiness $G_i$ to a target solution set $\mathcal{S}$. The creative ability operator $\mathcal{L}_{creat}$ acts on the state space to modulate the exploration rate. We model this as a Fokker-Planck equation governing the evolution of the probability density $\rho(\mathbf{x}, t)$ of the organism's internal state:
$$ \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v} \rho) + \nabla^2 (D[\rho] \rho) $$
Where $\mathbf{v}$ is the drift velocity determined by environmental gradients (purposive drive), and $D[\rho]$ is the diffusion coefficient representing exploratory variance. Crucially, $D$ is not constant but depends on the lability of the current gestalt:
$$ D[\rho] = D_0 \exp\left( \frac{H(P_\rho)}{H_{max}} \right) \quad [\text{m}^2/\text{s}] $$
Here, $D_0$ is a base diffusion constant, and $H_{max}$ normalizes the entropy. Higher entropy (more labile/permissive sign-gestalt) leads to exponential increase in diffusion, facilitating faster escape from local minima.
*Singularity Condition:* As $H \to 0$ (perfect certainty/rigidity), $D \to D_0 e^0 = D_0$ (minimal exploration). As $H \to H_{max}$ (total chaos), $D$ diverges, losing coherence of the "self".

**3. Formalization of "No Abstract Thought" via Grounded Simulation**
Tolman argues thought is a "running over of presented or imagined particulars" [PAGE 1]. We formalize this by defining a Cognitive Simulation Map $\Phi: \mathcal{T} \rightarrow \mathcal{P}$, where $\mathcal{T}$ is the time domain of thinking and $\mathcal{P}$ is the manifold of simulated particulars. There exists no separate operator $\Psi: \mathcal{T} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$ mapping to an abstract symbol space $\mathcal{A}$ independent of $\mathcal{P}$.
Instead, an "abstraction" $A$ is defined strictly as a compression function over trajectories in $\mathcal{P}$:
$$ A = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \phi(\mathbf{p}_i) \quad \text{where } \mathbf{p}_i \in \mathcal{P} $$
*Unit Analysis:* $\mathbf{p}_i$ contains sensory-motor coordinates (e.g., joint angles in radians, visual inputs in lux). The abstraction $A$ retains these dimensional bases unless explicitly normalized.
*Breakdown:* This formalism fails only if one assumes the existence of a substrate-independent syntax (Strong AI hypothesis), which Tolman explicitly rejects by grounding meaning in biological "readinesses."

**4. Predictive Processing of Particulars**
The process of "mnemonizing oneself into the presence of a particular" [PAGE 1] corresponds to active inference minimizing Variational Free Energy ($F$). For a given hypothesis about a particular action $a$, the brain generates predictions $\tilde{o}$ compared against stored memories $m$ (which are themselves compressed particulars).
$$ F(a) = D_{KL}[q(s|m) \| p(s,a)] - \ln p(m|a) $$
Minimizing $F$ drives the selection of new responses ("inferring new responses") [PAGE 1]. The "growth in means-end-readinesses" is the reduction of free energy through updating the posterior $q(s|m)$ via simulated encounters with particulars.
*Citation:* "Perhaps in all cases of true problem-solving, the growth in means-end-readinesses... is an outgrowth of 'fooling around with either actual or hypothetical particulars'" [PAGE 1].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogy of Tolman’s "creative-ability" and the rejection of abstract thought traces a complex lineage from Gestalt psychology through pragmatism to modern predictive processing, yet it also sets up dangerous theoretical traps for contemporary AI research. Historically, Tolman was reacting against strict Stimulus-Response (S-R) behaviorism (Watson, early Skinner), which treated the organism as a passive reflex machine. By introducing "sign-gestalts" and "means-end-expectancies," he injected a teleological (purpose-driven) element into behaviorism, aligning him closer to William James’s radical empiricism and John Dewey’s instrumentalism. Dewey argued that thinking arises from problematic situations and proceeds via the manipulation of concrete consequences—a direct precursor to Tolman’s "fooling around with particulars."

However, a retrocausal analysis reveals a trap: the mid-20th-century dominance of Computationalism (Turing, McCarthy, Minsky) effectively severed cognition from the body, creating the myth of "abstract thought" as symbol shuffling in a substrate-independent medium. This created a "multi-week trap" (or rather, a multi-decade trap) where researchers assumed that intelligence could be achieved purely through logical deduction and abstract representation, ignoring the lability and embodied simulation Tolman emphasized. Modern Deep Learning, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), has inadvertently stumbled back toward Tolman’s view but misinterpreted it. LLMs process vast amounts of "particulars" (tokens/examples) but lack true "means-end-readinesses" because they operate without a biological imperative or a grounded sensorimotor loop. They simulate language without simulating action.

The scientific lineage continues into Varela and Thompson’s Enactivism and Clark’s Predictive Coding. The concept of "lability" prefigures the idea of "precision weighting" in Active Inference (Friston). Just as Tolman noted that some individuals break out of old gestalts more easily due to higher lability, modern neuroscience identifies neuromodulators (dopamine, norepinephrine) as regulators of prediction error precision, effectively tuning the "temperature" of cognitive exploration. The trap lies in assuming that increasing data volume (more particulars) automatically yields creative insight. Tolman warns that without the right internal lability (the correct balance of stability and plasticity), an agent—even one flooded with particulars—will remain stuck in initial sign-gestalts. Thus, the history of AI is partly a history of forgetting Tolman’s crucial distinction between having access to particulars and possessing the organic readiness to transform them creatively.

In standard reinforcement learning terms, this is a sudden spike in TD-error (prediction error). Psychologically, this triggers a regression: the breakdown of the efficient, automatic "sign-gestalt" forces the system back into a more primitive, diffuse state of attention. This is the "lability" Tolman describes. The mind recursively monitors its own failure state; it knows it knows it is failing.

This recursive monitoring creates a "counter-trap." If the organism simply repeats the failed action, it falls into a perseverative loop (a trap of rigidity). To avoid this, it must engage in "fooling around with hypothetical particulars." This is a meta-cognitive strategy: simulating alternative futures to bypass the blockage. However, this process is constrained by "the clock"—biological time pressure. Hunger, fatigue, or predation risk impose a hard deadline on the duration of safe exploration. The organism must balance the depth of simulation (trying many hypothetical pushes) against the metabolic cost and risk of taking too long.

A crisis habit regression is not merely a return to randomness; it is a targeted destabilization of the most probable actions to allow less probable ones (pushing) to surface. The "creative-ability" is the efficiency with which an agent navigates this trade-off. A highly creative agent rapidly degrades the salience of the dominant "pulling" gesture and amplifies the noise associated with "pushing," all while keeping track of the elapsed time since the last successful food acquisition. If the internal clock signals extreme urgency, the system might skip fine-grained simulation and resort to broad, chaotic thrashing (high entropy, low information gain per unit time). Conversely, if time permits, it can run detailed, energetically expensive simulations of specific physical interactions. Thus, the mind is a recursive controller managing the tension between immediate survival demands (clock) and the need for structural flexibility (breaking the trap).

If Sultan were suffering from severe arthritis in his shoulders, his capacity to simulate the kinematics of pushing would be physically truncated. The "mnemonization" of a past successful push might be present, but the proprioceptive feedback loop required to validate that simulation in real-time would be degraded. Therefore, the "growth in means-end-readinesses" is vetoed by biomechanical degradation. The cognitive lability cannot overcome a mechanical lockout. The equation for the diffusion coefficient $D[\rho]$ must include a multiplicative factor $\mu \in [0,1]$ representing muscular integrity: $D_{effective} = \mu \cdot D[\rho]$. If $\mu \approx 0$, creative ideation regarding locomotion is physically impossible regardless of mental state.

**Weather/Environmental Veto:** Suppose Sultan imagines pushing a wooden box across a dirt floor. Suddenly, a torrential rain turns the floor into deep mud. The friction coefficients change drastically. The "hypothetical particular" derived from dry-floor memory now yields a false positive prediction of success. The environment vetoes the cognitive outcome. Tolman acknowledges this implicitly by distinguishing "actual" vs "hypothetical" particulars, but a robust theory requires that the "sign-gestalt" constantly samples current sensory input to update the validity of stored particulars. If the sensory channel is blocked (e.g., darkness), the reliance on hypotheticals increases, raising the probability of error. A system that ignores real-time sensory veto signals (sensory gating failure) will persist in futile actions, demonstrating that creative ideation is tightly coupled to continuous cross-layer validation between internal models and external reality. Thus, "pure" ideation divorced from ongoing physical interaction is a chimera; it is perpetually subject to veto by the physics of the moment.
