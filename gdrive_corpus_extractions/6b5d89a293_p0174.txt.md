Cylinder-set operator:
\[
C_n(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \{x\in X : x_i = a_i,\; 1\le i\le n\},
\qquad
\mu\bigl(C_n(a_1,\dots,a_n)\bigr) = 2^{-n}.
\]

Singleton null-set derivation (clause 5a):
\[
\{x\} = \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} C_n(x|_n),\qquad
\mu(\{x\}) = \lim_{n\to\infty} 2^{-n} = 0,
\]
valid because μ(X) = 1 < ∞, so continuity from above applies. Breakdown regime: if μ₀ were not finite (e.g. counting measure on an infinite X₀), the product measure would fail σ-finiteness and continuity from above could not be invoked on cylinders of infinite measure.

Eventually-constant tail set (clause 5b):
\[
E = \{x\in X : \exists N,\;\forall i>N,\; x_i = 1\}
= \bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty} \{x : x_i = 1 \;\forall i > N\},
\qquad
\mu(E) = 0
\]
by countable subadditivity, since each tail cylinder fixes infinitely many coordinates and has measure lim_{n→∞} 2^{-n} = 0.

Coding map and measure-space reduction:
\[
X' = X\setminus E,\qquad
\mathcal S' = \{S\cap X' : S\in\mathcal S\},\qquad
\mu'(S\cap X') = \mu(S),
\]
\[
z:X'\to Z=[0,1),\qquad
z(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{x_i}{2^i}.
\]
Units of z are dimensionless (real-valued coordinate coding); z is a Borel isomorphism between (X', ℒ', μ') and (Z, ℬ(Z), λ) modulo null sets.

Measure-preservation identity (clauses 5d–5e):
\[
v(A) \;=\; \mu\bigl(z^{-1}(A)\bigr),\qquad
A\in\mathcal B(Z),
\]
\[
v\bigl([a,b)\bigr) = b - a = \lambda\bigl([a,b)\bigr),\qquad 0\le a<b\le 1.
\]
Because dyadic intervals form a π-system generating ℬ(Z), and both v and λ are probability measures on Z, the extension theorem gives
\[
v(A) = \lambda(A)\quad \forall A\in\mathcal B(Z).
\]

Extension to ℝ:
\[
\lambda_{\mathbb R}\Bigl(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb Z}[n,n+1)\Bigr)
= \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z} \lambda([n,n+1)) = \infty,
\]
equivalently via the product I × Z with I = ℤ carrying counting measure \#(E) = |E|.

Clause (6) alternative space (suspect coding):
\[
X_0 = \mathbb Z^+,\qquad
\mu_0(E) = \sum_{i\in E} 2^{-i},\qquad
\mu_0(\mathbb Z^+) = 1,
\]
but if z(x) = Σ xᵢ/2^{i} with xᵢ ≥ 1 is retained, then
\[
z(x) \ge \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} 2^{-i} = 1,
\]
so z(X) ⊄ [0,1); e.g. xᵢ = 2 ∀i ⇒ z = 2. This is a singularity/breakdown regime: the coding map ceases to be a bijection onto Z and clause (5e) fails literally.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The construction on [PAGE 159] sits at a confluence of three historical lineages. First, Cantor's 1883 ternary set and the subsequent recognition that every real in [0,1] admits a binary (or b-ary) expansion established the set-theoretic skeleton used here: sequences of digits as points, with digit cylinders as the generating measurable sets. Second, Borel (1898) and Lebesgue (1902) extended length from intervals to Borel sets, requiring the countable-additivity and null-set infrastructure that clauses (5a)–(5b) exercise explicitly—singletons and countable tails must be null before a digit map can carry measure faithfully. Third, Carathéodory's 1918 extension theorem and Kolmogorov's 1933 product-measure formalization supply the uniqueness step: once v agrees with Lebesgue measure on intervals, it agrees on all Borel sets.

Halmos's §38 product-space construction (1950) compresses this genealogy into a purely measure-theoretic derivation of Lebesgue measure, avoiding topology entirely until the final identification with [0,1). The multi-week traps embedded in this lineage are durable. Students routinely regress to the pre-1902 intuition that "measure equals length" and attempt to assign measure to every subset of [0,1], inviting the Vitali non-measurable set as a veto; the text sidesteps this by restricting to Borel sets via the cylinder σ-algebra. A second trap is boundary confusion at dyadic rationals: treating the two binary expansions of k/2ⁿ as distinct points of positive mass, or deleting the wrong tail set, destroys the one-to-one correspondence. A third is the uncountable-product overreach: the countable product theorem used here does not extend to ∏_{t∈[0,1]} {0,1}, where every measurable set depends on only countably many coordinates, rendering events such as "all coordinates vanish" non-measurable in the product σ-algebra. Finally, clause (6) presents a generational trap: swapping the fair coin (μ₀({0}) = μ₀({1}) = 1/2) for a geometric law on positive integers while retaining the same binary coding map z breaks the range of z, a silent failure that would propagate into any downstream stochastic simulation relying on uniformity.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: when a reasoner under pressure confronts the map z(x) = Σ xᵢ/2^{i}, the default habit is to treat z as continuous and length-preserving in the Euclidean sense, assuming that small changes in x produce small changes in z and that intervals pull back to intervals of equal length without verifying cylinder generation. This habit regresses to Riemann integration thinking, where partitions are finite and boundaries are innocuous. In the product setting the regression fails at dyadic rationals: z is not a homeomorphism on X (the inverse is discontinuous at dyadics), and the preimage of an interval is a countable union of cylinders, not a single cylinder.

Counter-trap: the explicit recursive refinement clock. At stage n, restrict attention to the first n binary digits. The cylinder Cₙ(a₁,…,aₙ) has diameter 2^{-n} in the z-metric and μ-mass 2^{-n}; the dyadic interval [k/2ⁿ, (k+1)/2ⁿ) has the same length. The error between the finite-stage discrete approximation and the target Lebesgue measure is bounded by the total mass of unresolved tails, namely 2^{-n}, which tends to zero. The counter-trap is therefore the ε/2ⁿ argument: for any Borel A and ε > 0, choose n such that 2^{-n} < ε and approximate A by a finite union of dyadic intervals; the measure discrepancy is controlled uniformly. This defeats the habit regression by replacing geometric intuition with a countable stage-wise sieve.

Clock semantics: the "clock" is the dyadic refinement index n. As n advances, unresolved coordinates recede to the tail. By the strong law of large numbers for the fair Bernoulli product, typical sequences have asymptotic digit frequency 1/2, which is precisely the mechanism that pushes the induced law of z to the uniform (Lebesgue) distribution. If the clock were stopped at finite N, the law of z would be discrete uniform on the grid {k/2ᴺ}, weak-* converging to Lebesgue only as N → ∞; at any finite N the recursion is not yet exact, and asserting exact equality at finite resolution is a vetoed overclaim.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Foundational layer: the product σ-algebra ⊗ᵢ Sᵢ on X = {0,1}^ℕ contains only sets determined by countably many coordinates. Veto: any subset of X depending on an uncountable pattern of coordinates (e.g. in an uncountable power {0,1}^𝔠) is outside ⊗ᵢ Sᵢ. Physical analogue: no finite-resolution sensor can register an infinite binary sequence; the measurable cylinder Cₙ corresponds to a sensor resolving exactly n bits, with irreducible quantization step 2^{-n}.

Measure layer: the null-set arguments in clauses (5a)–(5b) require μ(X) = 1 < ∞. Veto regime: if μ₀ were replaced by counting measure on an infinite X₀, the product measure would not be σ-finite, Fubini–Tonelli would fail, and μ({x}) = lim 2^{-n} could not be derived because cylinders would have infinite measure. This is a hard breakdown: the construction is not robust to non-finite base measures.

Physical/biomechanical layer: human or instrument cognition is a finite-state transducer. It cannot hold or compare two infinite binary sequences x and y differing only at coordinate 10⁶. Veto: the bijection z : X' → Z is theoretically exact but physically unrealizable at infinite precision; any implementation truncates at N, inducing the discrete measure μ_N({k/2ᴺ}) = 2^{-N}, which converges weakly to Lebesgue measure but is never equal to it at finite N. Biomechanical reaction-time limits thus impose a resolution cutoff N_max beyond which the distinction between Lebesgue measure and its dyadic approximation is operationally moot.

Weather/stochastic layer: interpreting each coordinate xᵢ as an i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/2) coin flip (fair weather), the induced distribution of z is uniform by construction. If the coin is biased, P(xᵢ = 1) = p ≠ 1/2, the pushforward of ⊗ Bernoulli(p) under z is a Bernoulli convolution, generally singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. Veto: the uniformity of z's image is not a consequence of the product space alone; it is contingent on the fair-coin parameter p = 1/2. Cross-layer adjudication therefore holds: the measure-theoretic proof on [PAGE 159] is valid, but its physical instantiation is vetoed at finite resolution, non-finite base measure, biased coordinates, or uncountable index sets.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (retain the deleted tail): Do not remove E from X; keep z defined on all of X. Veto: z is not injective because every dyadic q = k/2ⁿ has two preimages, one terminating in zeros and one terminating in ones. Verifiable prediction: μ(z^{-1}({1/2})) = μ({(1,0,0,…)} ∪ {(0,1,1,…)}) = 0 + 0 = 0, equal to λ({1/2}), so the pushforward measure still matches Lebesgue on singletons; however any proof requiring a literal one-to-one correspondence (as in clause 5c's wording) is vetoed at countably many points, and a sampling algorithm that inverts z by choosing the non-terminating expansion will collide with the terminating one on a μ-null but algorithmically reachable set.

Scenario 2 (apply clause 6's coordinate law to the binary coding): Use X₀ = ℤ⁺ with μ₀({i}) = 2^{-i} but keep z(x) = Σ xᵢ/2^{i} and target Z = [0,1). Veto: for every x ∈ X, xᵢ ≥ 1 implies z(x) ≥ Σ 2^{-i} = 1, with equality only at the constant sequence xᵢ = 1; hence z^{-1}([0,1)) = ∅ and z^{-1}({1}) = {(1,1,1,…)}. Verifiable prediction: v([0,1)) = μ(z^{-1}([0,1))) = 0, whereas λ([0,1)) = 1. The measure-preservation identity collapses completely; any downstream prediction that z induces Lebesgue measure under this law is falsified by direct computation of the range.

Scenario 3 (uncountable product extension): Attempt to generalize the construction to Y = {0,1}^𝔠 with the product of fair Bernoulli measures and define a coding z_𝔠(y) via an uncountable "binary expansion." Veto: in the product σ-algebra on an uncountable power, every measurable set depends on only countably many coordinates; the event A = {y : y_t = 0 ∀ t ∈ 𝔠} is an uncountable intersection of measurable cylinders and need not belong to the product σ-algebra. Verifiable prediction: A cannot be assigned a product measure by the theorem used on [PAGE 159]; outer measure is 1 (since every finite-dimensional cylinder fixing finitely many coordinates to 0 has positive measure 2^{-n}) while no measurable subset of A has positive inner measure, so A is non-measurable in the product structure. This scenario falsifies any blanket claim that the product-space construction of Lebesgue measure scales to uncountable dimensions without additional regularity (e.g. Radon measure on compact groups).
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
