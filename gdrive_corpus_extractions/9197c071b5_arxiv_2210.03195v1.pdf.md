- rs-fMRI: TR = 2000 ms, TE = 35.8 ms, 2 mm isotropic, 300 volumes, 10 min eyes-closed [PAGE 38].
- DWI: b = 1000 s·mm⁻² and 2000 s·mm⁻², 30 directions each, TE = 94 ms, TR = 9.8 s, 72 slices, 2 mm isotropic [PAGE 39]. **Only** "the 30 diffusion-weighted volumes with b = 1000 s/mm²" and matched b = 0 were used in FA computation [PAGE 39].
- Parcellation: 8 Yeo + subcortical networks as functional/structure grouping [PAGE 7]; 248 ROIs after cerebellum removal [PAGE 39].

**7. Conjunction floor (multiple-comparison gate). Verbatim [PAGE 10]:**
"equivalent to a conjunction analysis threshold of p ≤ 0.000125 or a Bonferroni correction for 400 tests." Units: probability. **Audit note:** the headline PMe (p_Sobel^perm = 0.007) does not clear this floor; the paper exempts PMe/PMo from further correction [PAGE 12] on the basis of permutation realism — acceptable only if the permutation null is correctly built, which brings the code issue below.

**8. ΔFA aggregation operator (code truth vs. prose).** Prose: "average difference of each of the networks from Pre and Post" [PAGE 40]. Code at [PAGE 55]: "mdelta = sqrt(colSums((m2 - m1)^2))". Formal reading:

$$\boxed{\;\Delta m_{s} = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{248}\left(FA_{k,s}^{post}-FA_{k,s}^{pre}\right)^{2}}\;}\qquad[PAGE\ 55]$$

Units: dimensionless FA-L2-norm per subject s. This is not an "average difference"; it is an L2 norm, and it has no closed-form conversion into mean FA change. This mismatch is a definitional singularity between Methods text and the computational engine.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of this framework runs through four tributaries that converge only in this paper. (i) Luria's neuropsychological syndromic assessment [PAGE 3, ref 4], explicitly transduced into VR modules [PAGE 41]. (ii) Yeo/Amico parcellation-tradition fingerprinting [PAGE 7, refs 119–121], which absorbed Finn's connectome-ID method into a repeated-measures "how stable is the network across the season" metric. (iii) Hayes–Baron–Kenny mediation/moderation in the causal-inference tradition [PAGE 11, PAGE 47, refs 57–58]. (iv) The traumatic-microRNA literature (miR-30d, miR-20a, miR-505) as inflammation surrogates [PAGE 4, refs 42–43]. Each tributary has its own failure mode: Luria's tests assume known lesion-behavior mappings; fingerprinting assumes unitary test-retest without heteroscedastic state shifts; mediation assumes sequential ignorability; miRNA assumes transcriptomic stability that [PAGE 53] itself undercuts ("only miR-30d significantly increased from Pre to Post-season").

Multi-week trap #1 — Do not collapse Pre/Post into a controlled manipulation. The authors acknowledge "no control group with similar body types, athletic plan, lifestyle management, and metabolic needs could be constructed" [PAGE 6–7]. Without out-of-season retest, drift cannot be partitioned from HAE.

Multi-week trap #2 — Do not read "sessions" as dose. It is exposure time [PAGE 8, PAGE 23], and it is uncorrelated with any per-impact magnitude in the reported SVMs. The Bjork/Shephard lineage (accumulated subconcussive exposure) is cited [PAGE 22, refs 111–112, 116] but the paper's own cohort never licenses dose-response monotonicity claims.

Multi-week trap #3 — Do not promote to causal pathology from signaling. The paper's own Discussion [PAGE 22–23] hedges: hyper-connectivity is "short-term compensatory mechanism" and FA elevation is "axonal swelling or inflammation"; both are alternative, non-destructive explanations. No post-season follow-up exists to see whether miR-30d normalizes — that absence breaks the molecular persistence chain.

Multi-week trap #4 — the erratum trap. [PAGE 2] and [PAGE 14] report p_F^perm = 0.036, p_β3^perm = 0.058. [PAGE 16] reverses them: "p_F^perm = 0.058, p_β3^perm = 0.036." A downstream citation that adopts [PAGE 16]'s labeling will claim the interaction is significant while F is the "trending" quantity — the substantive failure shifts from β₃ to F.

Multi-week trap #5 — the code-vs-text trap. Permutation p under-samples when xdelta is not permuted in the actual R loop [PAGE 55]; a re-run correcting this may either shrink or grow the observed p; either way, the published p cannot be replicated as a true permutation p until the code is repaired. The 47% Teff may shift, and with it the >30% gate.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis-habit regression here is the reviewer default of "n = 23, so it's noise" — a reflex that fails to engage the strongest part of the paper: the effect size on the ΔSR → SM-ΔFA arm (β = −0.540, p = 0.025, R²adj = 0.244, 0/17 outliers [PAGE 50]), and on ΔSR → SM fingerprint (β = −0.745, p = 0.001, R²adj = 0.473 [PAGE 50]). R²adj = 0.473 for a single behavioral predictor is not a "toy" correlation at n ≈ 20; it is a signal. The correct reviewer stance denies the paper's *directionality* claim, not the existence of an association.

Counter-trap: conflating "feedback" with "behavior improves white-matter integrity." The paper claims [PAGE 21] "Motor-control behavior clearly affected synchrony of the resting-state fMRI signal across the SM network through its effects on the DTI signal." Directionality rests entirely on secondary-mediation failure, not on an interventional probe. Honest counter-trap reading: if a seasonal, contact-independent developmental or training effect compressed ΔSR (athletes improving) and simultaneously increased SM-ΔFA (via myelin remodeling), we would observe the same three arms. The paper has no out-of-season washout and no counterfactual arm; the reader must treat "feedback" as a generative model, not a causal finding.

Clock layer: the seasonal clock is doing work the statistics do not acknowledge. Between Pre and Post, athletes experience (a) cumulative contact HAE; (b) a coordinated strength/conditioning block; (c) weight, sleep, diet, exam-period stress; (d) scanner variance across ~3–4 months; (e) one acquisition per variable per timepoint, i.e., no within-athlete multiplicity to estimate intra-individual noise. Under a model in which the clock itself produces ΔSR, ΔFA, and ΔfMRI, mediation is unidentifiable without external time anchors. The paper's temporal design is a necessary minimizer, but the clock is the confound, and the "feedback" mechanism is not distinguishable from clock-driven plasticity.

The epistemic recursion the paper implicitly invites: If the reviewer sets one covariate (HoC, YoE) as a control [PAGE 13], HoC and YoE do not survive [PAGE 13], so no dose-by-history buffer is available. Any additional covariate (position, ADHD, defined exercise load) will erode the n = 17–20 effective sample to non-identifiability. The recursive trap is that every "reasonable control" the instinct proposes auto-destroys the design; the honest stance is to accept the paper as a hypothesis-generating structure whose causal direction awaits an interventional/out-of-season study, not to seek a specified DAG on n = 17 subsets.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto proof #1 — Biomechanical layer. The BodiTrak sensor measures only PTA (peak translational acceleration), inner-helmet-mounted, on contact practices only [PAGE 8, PAGE 43]. Thresholds are discretized at 25 G and 80 G with no rotational component, no impact direction vector used in the cHAE sum, and no in-helmet mounting-uniformity QC reported. Verbatim from the paper: "no games were monitored as it could disturb competitive play" [PAGE 8], and "the HAE metrics were only collected at practices, and not during competitive play" [PAGE 23]. Therefore the "dose" observed on the HAE arm (β = −0.582, p = 0.007 [PAGE 52]) is a *lower-bound proxy*, not a true biomechanical exposure. Veto consequence: any claim of the form "head-impact dose X caused ΔSR of size Y" is inadmissible; only the ordinal statement that athletes with more recorded subconcussive hits during practices showed a larger postural-latency penalty survives.

Veto proof #2 — Physiological/transcriptomic layer. The only molecule in the 9-plex showing a statistically meaningful Pre→Post shift is miR-30d [PAGE 53]. If the mediation/moderation had been run against a molecule with stable Pre/Post values, the ΔmiR arithmetic would be dominated by measurement noise and the moderation p_F = 0.036 would be a false-positive artifact of the ddPCR's 1.5–2 droplet-CV. The paper survives this veto *only for miR-30d*, and the transcriptomic claim is localized there; generalizing the moderation to the rest of the 9-plex (miR-20a, -505, -362-3p, -92a, -486, -195, -93p, -151-5p) is vetoed by the same test that supports -30d.

Veto proof #3 — Imaging/biophysical layer. The FA aggregation is L2-norm across 248 ROIs [PAGE 55], while Methods describe "average difference" [PAGE 40]. L2 norm amplifies high-FA voxel outliers (corpus callosum, corticospinal tract) relative to a mean, so the "SM-ΔFA" is biased toward major white-matter fascicles, and small sample sizes (n = 17, 3 Cook's outliers already removed [PAGE 50]) further destabilize it. Veto consequence: the specific numeric claim Teff = 47% cannot be treated as a validated physical quantity until a sensitivity re-analysis under mean-difference aggregation reproduces it.

Veto proof #4 — Weather/environmental/climatic layer (analogous). This paper does not model any environmental covariate (weather, barometric pressure, ambient temperature, or heat exposure), and postural stability is highly weather/vestibular sensitive. Since the study uses a two-point Pre (start of season) → Post (within one week of final game) comparison [PAGE 6], all climate and seasonal effects (heat acclimatization, serum volumes, hydration shifts) are pooled into the ΔSR and ΔFA terms. Veto consequence: any expository derivation that cites "outdoor temperature" or "barometric pressure" as a partial cause of the ΔSR change is unsupported by the paper's own measurements; the paper simply does not have the sensor layer to adjudicate weather.

Combined layer veto: three of four modalities are sessions confounding (HAE: practice-only, imaging: single scan per timepoint, transcriptomics: blood draws within a one-week Pre/Post window). The only way to preserve the paper's headline is to localize it: *in a described male collegiate-lineman cohort, a single-season change in ΔSR covaries sign-consistently with SM-ΔFA, SM fingerprint similarity, and ΔmiR-30d at nominally significant-to-trend levels.* Everything above that — mechanism, direction, magnitude transfer — is vetoed in the absence of an interventional or out-of-season arm.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
**Veto Scenario 1 — Secondary-mediation swap (X↔M flip should fail).** The paper's own gate [PAGE 48] requires secondary mediation (SM-ΔFA as X, ΔSR as M, SM fingerprint as Y) to yield pSobel^perm ≥ 0.05 and Teff ≤ 30%. The Supplemental Tables do not report the secondary values. **Predicted observation if the effect is truly directional:** secondary Sobel is non-significant and Teff_secondary < 30%. **Prediction that must be tested:** rerun the mediation with X = SM-ΔFA, M = ΔSR, Y = SM fingerprint using the same 17-subject common set, S = 100,000 permutations, Cook's prune= 4/n; a pSobel^perm ≥ 0.05 and Teff ≤ 30% is the pass condition. A positive result (secondary also significant) forces the "feedback" directionality to be rewritten as "symmetric association." The published text does not supply the falsifying test, which is itself the primary veto against causal language.

**Veto Scenario 2 — Corrected permutation loop for PMe.** In the shipped R code [PAGE 55], `xdelta = x2 - x1` reuses the unpermuted ΔX inside the q-loop, while `mdelta` and `ydelta` are correctly permuted (`m2q - m1q`, `y1q/y2q`). The Methods text [PAGE 46] specifically describes permuted ΔX′ = x2′ − x1′. **Predicted observation after repair (xdelta = x2q - x1q):** the empirical null for zSobel widens, and pSobel^perm shifts upward from 0.007; expected range under repair: 0.01–0.10 for a widened null. **Falsifier:** if the repaired p remains ≤ 0.01, the original result is robust to the code-path ambiguity and the audit concern is retired. **Second falsifier:** if the repaired z0 shifts the sign or significance in the opposite direction, the entire mediation claim reverses. This is a fully verifiable, executable counterfactual: the paper ships the code, so replication is direct.

**Veto Scenario 3 — Moderation gate strict application + figure-caption erratum.** Applying the paper's own gate [PAGE 11] literally, "moderation is significant if p_β3 ≤ 0.05 (the interaction term) and p_F ≤ 0.05 (for the overall model)," the reported p_β3^perm = 0.058 [PAGE 14] fails. Additionally, the p-F/p-β₃ pair is inverted in the Figure 2 caption [PAGE 16] ("p_F^perm = 0.058, p_β3^perm = 0.036"). **Predicted observation:** a strict-gate reading yields "moderation not significant, trend only"; a caption-reading yields "moderation significant, F trending." These are non-equivalent epistemic states. **Veto prediction:** rerun the moderation with S = 100,000 under the corrected permuted-ΔX code to resolve which labeling is right. **Falsifier:** if both p_F and p_β3 are ≤ 0.05 in the rerun, the figure-caption erratum is cosmetic (only affects the reported number, not the verdict) and the paper's weakest link is retired. **Second falsifier:** if p_β3 rises above 0.058 in the rerun, the paper's own description as a "trend effect" [PAGE 20] is fully vindicated and any abstract-level claim ("interaction predicted ΔmiR-30d" [PAGE 2]) must be rewritten to "the interaction trended toward predicting."

Each of these three vetos is a binary, executable test with a pre-registered pass/fail, and each falsifier is falsifiable by the paper's own shipped assets (code [PAGE 54–60], supplemental tables [PAGE 50–52], and its stated gates [PAGE 10, PAGE 11, PAGE 48]).
