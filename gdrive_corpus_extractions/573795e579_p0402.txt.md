<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 laws emerge from a dialectic between radical behaviorism (Watson) and Gestalt psychology (Köhler/Koffka). Retrocausally, they anticipate modern computational neuroscience: his "cognitive map" prefigures hippocampal place cells (O'Keefe & Nadel, 1978); "expectancy gradients" foreshadow temporal difference learning (Sutton & Barto, 1981). However, a multi-week trap awaits interpreters who project contemporary RL onto Tolman: he explicitly denied that reinforcement strengthens S-R bonds (contrary to Thorndike), viewing it instead as altering the *value* of goals (significance). This distinction collapses if one equates "motivation" with scalar rewards—a common fallacy in DeepMind's model-based RL literature. Historically, Tolman’s framework was suppressed during WWII due to its "mentalistic" implications, later revived in cognitive revolution (Neisser, 1967). The danger lies in conflating his teleonomic "purposiveness" (emergent selection of efficient paths) with conscious intentionality—a category error that still plagues AI alignment discourse.
