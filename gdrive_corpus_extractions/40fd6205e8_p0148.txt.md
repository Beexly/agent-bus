<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates as follows. Under baseline conditions, agents observe Knight’s ex ante rule: investment is destined for capital formation from the outset [PAGE 188]. The theory of mind is “I produce wealth for investment, not for consumption followed by residual investment.” In a crisis, the recursive habit regresses to hand-to-mouth present-biased consumption. Trusts, sinking funds, corporate charters, and legal capital-maintenance rules exist precisely to make \(c^p_t\) legally or contractually infeasible during the crisis window. The agent’s theory of mind is recursively overridden by the institution: “I cannot consume this principal even if I want to.” This restores Knight’s abstinence condition exogenously when individual willpower fails.

Clock: Knight’s decisive temporal claim is that the production clock runs on ex ante destination, not ex post residual saving [PAGE 188]. The clock starts at the decision to produce a capital good, not at the decision to forgo consuming income. During a crisis, agents who watch the wrong clock—the residual saving clock \(S_t = Y_t - C_t\)—will cut investment one-for-one with income because both are treated as the same pool. Agents who watch Knight’s clock—the ex ante capital-budget clock—maintain \(I_t = \alpha_t Y_t\) or protect \(\alpha_t\) even when \(Y_t\) falls, because the capital good was never slated for consumption in the first place. The regression-to-crisis trap is therefore a clock-misalignment error: switching from the production-commitment clock to the cash-flow residual clock precisely when capital preservation is most necessary.
