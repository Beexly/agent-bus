<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week operational traps.

Lineage. The method descends from LeCun et al.'s 1989 backpropagation applied to handwritten Zip code recognition [17], establishing the supervised convolutional baseline for digit recognition [PAGE 10]. Spatial invariance entered via Jaderberg et al.'s 2015 spatial transformer networks [18] [PAGE 10]. Human pose estimation provided the kinematic front end: Toshev & Szegedy's DeepPose (2014) [21], Andriluka et al.'s 2D benchmarks [22], and subsequent 2D/3D pose taxonomies [23-27] [PAGE 10]. Body orientation specifically enters through MEBOW (Wu et al., 2020), monocular estimation of body orientation in the wild [28] [PAGE 10]. The backbone is He et al.'s ResNet (2016) [30], optimized with Kingma & Ba's Adam (2015) [33] [PAGE 10].

Direct JNR ancestry: Vats et al. (2021) introduced multi-task learning for ice hockey jersey numbers but ignored body rotation and digit-interpretation accuracy [6] [PAGE 2]. Liu & Bhanu's pose-guided R-CNN (2019) [4] and JEDE universal detector (2022) [8] used pose to gate regions of interest without explicitly modeling orientation-driven digit visibility [PAGE 3]. SoccerNet (2018) and SoccerNet-v2 (2021) supplied the untrimmed soccer video substrate [19,20] [PAGE 10]. The present paper generalizes beyond ice hockey to soccer, football, basketball, volleyball, and baseball, compiling 58,563 images with 101 categories [PAGE 1, PAGE 4, PAGE 5].

Multi-week traps. Sports analytics operates on a seasonal clock, creating three recurring failure modes not resolved by a static preprint. (1) Class-imbalance drift: rare jersey numbers 60-99 are underrepresented (≈34% of rare-number coverage) [PAGE 5], and week-to-week roster rotations change which numbers appear, so a model trained early in a season faces a shifting long tail; the authors defer rebalancing to future work [PAGE 9]. (2) Orientation distribution drift: body angles depend on game phase (set pieces versus open play), camera position, and player fatigue; a model calibrated on early-season poses may encounter systematic θ-shift by mid-season, breaking the [85°,265°] ADRS prior. (3) Kit and environmental degradation: wrinkles, mud, rain, and stadium lighting introduce deformity and blur non-uniformly across the season [PAGE 8], causing accuracy decay unless the dataset is continuously augmented and re-validated. These are multi-week traps because they compound over the competitive calendar rather than appearing in a single i.i.d. test split.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and inference clock.

Crisis habit regression. The architecture implements a two-path metacognition: a "deliberate" digit-wise path (combined_number via ADRS) and a "habit" holistic path (P₁/A₁). Under perception stress—blur, occlusion, low resolution—the system arbitrates by probability magnitude: if P₁ > P₂ and P₁ > P₃, it regresses to the holistic output A₁ [PAGE 5]. This is a crisis habit regression: when the refined digit-wise representation is uncertain, the model falls back to the entangled holistic class, which is precisely the representation the paper argues is statistically inefficient for rare numbers [PAGE 2]. If P₁ is overconfident but incorrect (a common failure on blurred digits that resemble other holistic classes), the regression actively harms accuracy.

Counter-trap. ADRS is itself a second-order trap. It assumes orientation θ ∈ [85°,265°] correlates with tens-digit visibility and forcibly sets Â₄ ← 2 [PAGE 5]. But a player bending forward or twisted (Figure 6, image 3) may present a side angle while the tens digit is occluded by an arm, another player, or a fold [PAGE 8]. The scheme then commits to a two-digit parse and hallucinates a tens digit, converting a one-digit error into a structured two-digit error. The counter-trap is that orientation guidance, intended to prevent misreads, becomes a source of systematic false two-digit conversion under biomechanical contortion.

Clock. Inference serializes ResNet50 feature extraction, four linear heads, MEBOW/HRNet orientation estimation, and Algorithms 1-2 [PAGE 4-5]. Training used batch size 64 over 100 epochs with Adam [PAGE 7], but no latency benchmark is reported. For live broadcast JNR, the clock constraint is frame rate (e.g., 30 or 60 fps); the serial pose-estimation dependency is a veto for hard real-time deployment unless the orientation branch is distilled or asynchronous.

Recursive theory of mind. Level-0: predict jersey digits. Level-1: predict the number of digits (P₄) to constrain Level-0. Level-2: predict orientation (θ) to correct Level-1 via ADRS. Level-3: arbitrate between holistic and digit-wise hypotheses using P₁ versus P₂/P₃ [PAGE 4-5]. The recursion collapses if any lower level—especially pose estimation—is miscalibrated; the model then holds a false belief about its own digit-count uncertainty and propagates it into the final prediction.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof with adjudication order.

Physical layer veto. The source identifies blurring, occlusion, deformity, and low resolution as core degradations [PAGE 1]. Figure 6 demonstrates failure on extreme blur, clothing wrinkles, and a second person occluding the jersey [PAGE 8]. Veto condition: if projected jersey-digit height falls below the ResNet50 effective receptive-field scale, or motion-blur kernel width exceeds the digit-height distribution in the training set, the physical layer must abstain; no super-resolution or deblur module is present in the architecture [PAGE 4].

Biomechanical layer veto. Orientation θ is produced by MEBOW using HRNet features followed by ResNet and softmax [PAGE 4]. Veto condition: if pose-estimation confidence is low—self-occlusion, truncation, or extreme foreshortening—the angle input to ADRS is unreliable. ADRS only corrects for θ ∈ [85°,265°] [PAGE 5]; outside this band (frontal/rear views) it defers to raw head outputs, so the biomechanical layer supplies no correction where digit distortion from jersey folding is most severe.

Weather/environmental layer veto. Outdoor sports (soccer, football, baseball) are exposed to rain, fog, stadium floodlight flicker, and variable daylight [PAGE 4]. The dataset reports no explicit weather or lighting covariates, and robustness analysis shows failure under blur and wrinkles [PAGE 8]. Veto condition: a measured exposure or visibility shift outside the training distribution (e.g., night-game floodfall, precipitation) triggers domain-shift abstention.

Adjudication order. (1) Sensor/physical veto: resolution/blur/occlusion threshold breached → abstain. (2) Pose-confidence veto: MEBOW/HRNet confidence below threshold → disable ADRS, fall back to the four multi-task heads only. (3) Semantic veto: Â₄ = 0 → output invisible class 100 regardless of P₁/P₂/P₃ [PAGE 5]. (4) Probability-conflict veto: P₁ ≈ P₂ or P₁ ≈ P₃ leaves Algorithm 2's selection undefined [PAGE 5]; require an explicit tie-breaking rule or abstention. Only after all vetoes pass does the system emit a numeric prediction.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) veto scenarios and predictions. All are testable on the existing 4,974-image test split or controlled counterfactuals derived from it, without modifying trained weights.

Scenario 1: Orientation-boundary veto. Construct counterfactual test subsets by rotating jersey images so ground-truth θ ∈ [80°,90°] ∪ [260°,270°] (±5° around the inclusive ADRS boundaries [PAGE 5]) and restrict to one-digit jerseys (ground-truth A₂ ∈ {0,10}). MEBOW noise of a few degrees will flip Algorithm 1's inclusive branch [PAGE 5]. Prediction: Top-1 precision for single-digit jerseys in the boundary band drops by ≥15 percentage points relative to the control band θ ∈ [45°,80°] ∪ [270°,315°], producing a measurable precision cliff at the ADRS threshold.

Scenario 2: Invisible-class override race condition. Select test images with ground truth A₄ = 0 (invisible jersey) but where P₁ > P₂ and P₁ > P₃. Algorithm 2 assigns Predict ← A₁ before the terminal `if A4 = 0` override [PAGE 5]; if P₄ simultaneously mispredicts Â₄ ≠ 0, the override never fires. Prediction: recall for class 100 falls below 20% on this subset while precision remains high, revealing a precision-recall inversion detectable on the current test set and contradicting the table's degenerate precision = recall = accuracy reporting [PAGE 7].

Scenario 3: Occlusion/blur veto. Synthesize counterfactuals by overlaying a second-person bounding box onto the jersey region of clean test images, or by applying Gaussian blur with kernel width σ exceeding digit height. Figure 6 images 4-5 show that neither the proposed method nor Vats[6] recovers under extreme blur plus a second person [PAGE 8]. Prediction: Top-1 accuracy asymptotes to the random baseline of 0.99% [PAGE 7] when occlusion covers >40% of digit area or σ > 0.5 × digit height, reproducing the Baseline (Random) row of Table 3 [PAGE 7]. Each scenario yields a falsifiable numeric threshold and is verifiable from the released corpus.
