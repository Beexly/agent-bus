<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Objective profitability operator, with units and physical domain. From [PAGE 8], “Profitability was calculated for each visit to a feeder as the number of millet grains per total handling time or sum of the delay and consumption time fixed at 4 s.” Let \(a\in\mathbb{N}\) be grains, \(d\in[0,8]\,\mathrm{s}\) the programmed delay, and \(C=4\,\mathrm{s}\) the fixed consumption time. Then
\[
P(a,d)=\frac{a}{d+C}=\frac{a}{d+4\,\mathrm{s}}\quad [\mathrm{grains}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}].
\]
The paper’s worked point is “5/(2 + 4) = 0.83 grains/s” [PAGE 8]. The physical domain has \(d\geq 0\), so the pole at \(d=-4\,\mathrm{s}\) is outside the apparatus range; within range, \(P(a,d)\in[a/(12\,\mathrm{s}),a/(4\,\mathrm{s})]\). If the consumption time is not fixed at \(4\,\mathrm{s}\), the denominator becomes \(d+C\) with variable \(C\), and amount and delay are confounded; that is an exact design-breakdown regime.

Expected value and risk sign. For a binary variable feeder with high and low profitability \(P_H,P_L\) paid “at equal probability (p = 0.5)” [PAGE 10],
\[
\mathbb{E}[P]=\frac{1}{2}P_H+\frac{1}{2}P_L.
\]
The information content of the binary risk is \(H_2(0.5)=1\) bit, with \(H_2(p)=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\). If \(VV_i\) is the subjective value in grains/s, the classification is
\[
VV_i \lt \mathbb{E}[P]\Rightarrow\text{risk-averse},\qquad
VV_i=\mathbb{E}[P]\Rightarrow\text{risk-neutral},\qquad
VV_i \gt \mathbb{E}[P]\Rightarrow\text{risk-prone}.
\]
This is exactly the titration logic: if the fixed feeder must be set below the expected value to match the variable feeder, the variable feeder is undervalued; if above, it is overvalued [PAGE 10, PAGE 17].

Jensen operators. The paper states, for variable delay, “Jensen’s inequality leads to risk proneness in variable delay” [PAGE 5]:
\[
f\!\left(\frac{t_1+t_2}{2}\right)\lt \frac{f(t_1)+f(t_2)}{2}
\]
for convex \(f\) in delay. For variable amount, the paper states the concave case [PAGE 5]:
\[
f\!\left(\frac{A_1+A_2}{2}\right)\gt \frac{f(A_1)+f(A_2)}{2}.
\]
These inequalities require a smooth convex or concave \(f\); if \(f\) is linear, the Jensen gap is zero and risk neutrality follows. If \(f\) is non-smooth or if variance is zero, the sign test is undefined.

Choice model and Bayesian latent variable. The logistic link is
\[
Q(X)=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-X)}\in[0,1],
\]
with \(X\) a dimensionless logit [PAGE 12]. Experiment 2 uses
\[
X_{b,i}=B_i+E_i\left(VF_{b,i}-VV_i\right)
\]
[PAGE 14]. Here \(VV_i\) and \(VF_{b,i}\) are in grains/s; \(B_i\) is a dimensionless individual bias logit; \(E_i\) is the value-difference coefficient. For dimensional consistency of \(X\), \(E_i\) must carry \(\mathrm{s}\,\mathrm{grains}^{-1}\) if \(VF-VV\) is in grains/s, although the source does not report that unit explicitly. \(VF_{b,i}\) is a past-block average over \(K_i\) blocks [PAGE 14-15]; \(K_i\) is a Poisson count of blocks, dimensionless. The model assumes \(VV_i\) “is invariant along block (b)” [PAGE 15]. The posterior classification threshold is \(p(VV_i\gt 0.83)\gt 0.95\) for risk-prone and \(p(VV_i\gt 0.83)\lt 0.05\) for risk-averse [PAGE 17]. MCMC reproducibility requires “Rhat), which must be < 1.2 for all parameters” [PAGE 13]; one individual failed due to two posterior peaks [PAGE 17]. AIC is computed as “a sum of the deviance and the number of parameters” [PAGE 12], with lower values preferred.

Titration update and breakdown. The block-level rule is discrete: if the chick chose the fixed feeder in \(\leq 4\) of 10 test trials, the fixed profitability is incremented; if \(\geq 6\), decremented; at 5, unchanged [PAGE 10]. The step grids were 9 steps for group 1, 5 for group 2, and 9 for group 3 [PAGE 10-11]. Breakdown regimes are exact: a ceiling at 1.25 grains/s censors the equilibrium in group 3 subgroup L [PAGE 16]; a floor at the bottom step censors the opposite direction; and a step too coarse can prevent convergence to a unique indifference point. The group-3 failure to converge is reported on [PAGE 16], and the later re-examination says “A subjective value might not be uniquely given for the feeder with variable delay” [PAGE 18]. Thus the absolute-\(VV_i\) model is misspecified whenever value is alternative-dependent, as the paper concedes: “the conditional nature of the subjective value of the risky feeder violates the assumptions of absolute and independent value assignment” [PAGE 21].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three diagnostic hypotheses compete. Crisis habit regression: under restricted food, the chick’s choice may collapse to a simple budget-driven habit—risk-averse for amount when a positive or stable energy budget dominates, risk-prone when a negative budget dominates. The source cannot cleanly resolve the budget sign: body weight was about 70% of free-fed controls but steadily increasing [PAGE 6], and the paper says “It is possible that the budget was positive” but also “we may argue for a negative budget” [PAGE 19-20]. Counter-trap: the experimenter’s titration creates a comparative context. When the fixed feeder is poor in early blocks, the variable feeder is assigned a paradoxically high value that persists after the fixed feeder improves; the paper reports “chicks in subgroup L showed a paradoxically high profitability in late blocks” [PAGE 16] and later says the value was “conditional and dependent on early experiences” [PAGE 20]. This is a counter-trap because the subject’s valuation model is relational, not absolute; the experimenter’s independent-value assumption is the trap. Clock: the scalar-expectancy and delay-timing account predicts Weber-scaled noise in delay representation, with variable delay overvalued when the value function is convex and with lookback over many blocks. The estimated \(K_i\) “ranged high around 10-20” [PAGE 17], so the chick’s effective clock spans a large fraction of the 18-block history. Recursive theory-of-mind reading: the experimenter models the chick as having a latent \(VV_i\); the chick models the feeder schedule and the alternative’s history. If the chick’s model is context-relative, the experimenter’s hierarchical model is one level too simple. The evidence favors counter-trap/context dependence for delay and leaves crisis habit regression as an unresolved budget-state hypothesis for amount; the clock account explains why delay is especially vulnerable to history and timing noise. The correct recursive statement is not “the chick has a fixed utility,” but “the chick’s choice reveals a value conditioned on the alternative and the recent block history.”
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
