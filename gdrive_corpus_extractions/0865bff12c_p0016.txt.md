Assignment and objective:

Translation recovery (fixed π,R):

Rotation via quaternion eigenproblem:
q*∈S^3⊂R^4, ||q||_2=1 (dimensionless). L(a), R(b)∈R^{4×4} are left/right unit-quaternion multiplication matrices; [x×]∈so(3) is [[0,−x_3,x_2],[x_3,0,−x_1],[−x_2,x_1,0]]. Breakdown: N! grows factorially; real-time use limited to small N unless assignment relaxation (e.g., Hungarian O(N^3)) is substituted—not disclosed.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The method sits at the intersection of three lineages. First, the absolute-orientation / orthogonal Procrustes problem: Schönemann (1966), Kabsch (1976), and Horn's closed-form quaternion solution (1987) for minimizing Σ||y_j−Rx_j−r||^2; the patent's eq. (4) is the assignment-coupled variant of Wahba's problem (1965) from spacecraft attitude determination, solved via the Davenport q-method. Second, the assignment/combinatorial layer: Kuhn's Hungarian algorithm (1955) solves linear assignment in O(N^3), yet the patent elects brute force over N! because N is tiny (3 markers → 6 combinations) [PAGE 11-12]; this is computationally sound only while N remains small. Third, inertial dead reckoning: double integration of accelerometer specific force converts accelerometer bias instability into position error growing super-linearly with time; the quoted 1 meter over 25 seconds [PAGE 11] is a trial-specific empirical figure, not a bounded INS error model.

Multi-week traps. Deployed continuously over weeks, several failure modes accumulate. (a) Gyro bias random walk causes unbounded heading drift unless an absolute heading reference exists; the patent's claim that joint constraints keep heading drift "extremely limited" [PAGE 12] lacks a drift-rate bound, so over multi-week operation yaw uncertainty can grow until the association cost landscape flattens. (b) Gravity-vector estimation is corrupted during sustained linear acceleration because specific force conflates gravity with motion acceleration; a gravity constraint then injects a slowly varying rotation error into R. (c) The rigid-body assumption itself degrades: skin-motion artifact at shoulder and foot marker sites can displace passive optical markers by centimeters relative to the underlying skeleton, violating the fixed rigid transform and inflating residuals; the brute-force statistic may then prefer a wrong permutation if the true association residual exceeds the inter-permutation separation. (d) Thermal changes alter IMU bias and optical marker reflectivity; the 1 m/25 s drift baseline is not transferable across temperature extremes. (e) Repeated donning/doffing risks marker swaps; the system assumes "a correct association ... is in place" [PAGE 11] but does not disclose a re-association trigger after a manual reset, creating a latent trap where a stale J persists across sessions.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the designer's belief about the operator, and the operator's belief about the algorithm, recursively. Level 0 (algorithm): assumes correct association J is in place [PAGE 11]; trusts that the thick continuous line in plot 703 identifies the true permutation at all times despite 1 m of dead-reckoning drift [PAGE 11-12]. Level 1 (operator's model of the algorithm): the operator believes the association is automatic and robust to drift, so during a crisis—e.g., a drift alarm or a perceived tracking jump—the operator habitually regresses to a manual frame reset or re-anchoring action, treating the dead-reckoning origin as the source of error. This is the crisis habit regression: under stress the operator reverts to the simplest explanatory model (origin offset) rather than the permutation model the algorithm actually solves. Level 2 (algorithm's model of the operator under crisis): if the system anticipated manual resets, it would treat a reset as a discontinuity in q_j(t) and re-initialize R,r over a fresh window; the patent discloses no such trigger. Counter-trap: the operator's reset, performed mid-motion, violates the quasi-static rigid-transform assumption and injects a step change comparable to the observed drift magnitude (~1 m over 25 s [PAGE 11]), which can flip the brute-force minimizer to an incorrect association if the post-reset geometry transiently symmetrizes (e.g., both feet near midline). Clock: brute force over 6 combinations is negligible compared to a 25 s trial [PAGE 11-12], but online operation at 100–1000 Hz with N>3 makes the N! search a real-time clock pressure; the operator's crisis manual override consumes seconds, far longer than the association epoch, so the algorithm may lock onto a stale wrong J before the next re-solve. Recursive fixation: designer assumes operator will not intervene because drift is "immaterial" [PAGE 11-12]; operator assumes algorithm cannot confuse shoulder with foot because anatomy is distinct; both assumptions fail simultaneously when a symmetric pose (arms down, feet parallel) makes shoulder-foot distances nearly exchangeable, and the operator's manual reset removes the temporal continuity that would otherwise disambiguate the thick line.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto layer 1 — Physical/observability rank. Compute the centered covariance H=Σ_j x̃_j ỹ_j^T ∈R^{3×3} [m^2]. Veto if rank(H)<2 (equivalently λ_2(H)/λ_1(H) below a numerical threshold), because no unique R∈SO(3) can be recovered; this covers N=2 without constraints [PAGE 12], collinear marker layouts, and optical occlusion dropping visible N below 3 [PAGE 12]. The 1 m absolute drift over 25 s [PAGE 11] is not itself a veto, since r absorbs common-mode offset; the veto must fire on relative inter-unit geometry, not absolute drift magnitude.

Veto layer 2 — Biomechanical range checks. Before brute force, prune assignments violating hard anatomical bounds: inter-foot distance bounded by stance width; shoulder-to-foot distance bounded by limb length plus soft-tissue tolerance; right/left laterality fixed by marker identity. Any permutation π mapping a right-foot prediction to a left-foot measurement beyond the maximum plausible reach is vetoed a priori. This reduces the effective |S_N| and removes symmetric permutations that would otherwise yield multiple minima in eq. (4). Soft-tissue artifact at shoulder/foot sites (skin motion on the order of centimeters) sets the residual acceptance floor; if min_π C(π) exceeds the mounting-repeatability variance, veto and hold last association rather than accept a corrupted rigid transform.

Veto layer 3 — Weather/environmental. Passive optical markers require line of sight; fog, rain, or strong backlight causes missing p_j(t) [PAGE 11 defines positional units as passive optical markers], effectively reducing N. Temperature excursions outside IMU calibration range invalidate the 1 m/25 s drift baseline [PAGE 11] by altering gyro bias instability; veto if the estimated drift rate exceeds the certified envelope for the current temperature. If any layer vetoes, fallback hierarchy: (1) hold last valid (R,r,J); (2) re-solve with gravity and heading constraints [PAGE 12]; (3) declare association loss and request operator re-initiation. Adjudication order: rank veto > biomechanical veto > weather veto > residual threshold, because a rank-deficient geometry makes any further computation meaningless regardless of constraints.

Prediction: the top two eigenvalues of M(π) for the correct and a swapped permutation become near-degenerate, ΔC=C_swapped−C_correct falls below the residual noise floor (e.g., ΔC<1 cm^2), and the thick continuous line jitters between permutations across consecutive epochs.
