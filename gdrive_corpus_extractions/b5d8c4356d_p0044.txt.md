Biomechanical veto: Neoteny requires prolonged caloric investment and neuromuscular maturation; fine motor control and cortical plasticity are bounded by myelination schedules and skeletal development. If the environment cannot support the extended dependency implied by \(\rho_H \approx 0.20\), the learner fraction collapses and the species reverts toward mouse-like rigidity. Weather/environmental veto: Wiener claims feedback renders behavior “relatively independent of the so-called ‘load’ conditions” [PAGE 69], but this is relative, not absolute. Extreme load—network congestion approaching capacity, or environmental stress beyond homeostatic range—constitutes a hard veto that learning cannot override. Adjudication rule: any proposed control law that purports to operate under arbitrary load must first pass an exhaustion test (does it avoid absorbing states?) and a capacity test (\(A < C\) with margin). Wiener’s telephone example passes the capacity argument only if automatic switching actually reduces equipment; his criticism is that it fails the information test by ignoring \(\mathbf{f}\), not that it violates physical law.
