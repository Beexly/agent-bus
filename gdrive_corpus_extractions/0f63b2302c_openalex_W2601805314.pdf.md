**Multi-hop derivation, from photon budget to F-measure, with a gray-zone audit at every joint.**

**Hop 1 — the optical sampling chain.** The paper fixes the geometry early: the camera sits "at a relatively high place (such as a balcony) to observe all of the court's players," and "the distance is about 25 m" [PAGE 1]. It immediately concedes the consequence: "the observed size of each player becomes small" [PAGE 1]. This is a sampling problem before it is a learning problem. A badminton player of stature $h_p$ at range $d$ subtends $\theta_p = h_p/d$; at $h_p \approx 1.8$ m and $d \approx 25$ m that is 72 mrad, or 4.1°. If the 1080-row sensor spans a vertical field $\Theta_V$, the per-pixel angular pitch is $\theta_{px} \approx \Theta_V/N_V$; at $\Theta_V = 30°$ (0.524 rad), $\theta_{px} = 0.485$ mrad, giving a player height of only ~148 px. The player is resolvable; the *net* is not. A regulation badminton net mesh of ~19 mm at 25 m subtends 0.76 mrad, i.e. ~1.57 px per mesh period — below the Nyquist criterion of 2 px/period. The net is therefore not imaged; it is *aliased* into a high-contrast, low-frequency moiré lattice that looks like an object. This is the mechanical origin of every failure the paper later reports, and the authors never compute it.

**Hop 2 — from aliasing to detector score.** The paper's stated mechanism is gradient saliency: "the net region is given a higher score as a tracking-target object in the learning process due to the sharp gradient" [PAGE 2]. That is consistent with Hop 1 but weaker as an explanation, because an aliased lattice produces gradients whose *magnitude* is comparable to a real edge while their *spatial coherence* is higher than a real edge (it repeats). TLD's P-N experts bootstrap a binary classifier from structural constraints; a repeating texture supplies an unbounded supply of mutually-consistent positive examples and almost no negative ones. The net therefore occupies an absorbing state in the template set, not a transient one.

**1. Projected target size (geometric operator).**

$$n_p=\frac{f\,h_p}{d\,s}\quad[\text{px}],\qquad s=\frac{\text{sensor height}}{N_V}\ \left[\frac{\text{m}}{\text{px}}\right]$$

with $f$ focal length [m], $h_p$ player stature [m], $d$ slant range [m], $s$ pixel pitch [m·px⁻¹]. Assumption: paraxial pinhole, frontal plane. Breakdown regime: $n_p \lesssim 20$ px, at which the bounding-box scale estimate is quantised to ±5 % per frame and TLD's median-flow tracker is dominated by quantisation noise. The paper concedes the regime qualitatively — "small observation size (i.e., low resolution)" [PAGE 1] — but never reports $n_p$.

**2. Angular pitch and net-mesh Nyquist ratio.**

$$\theta_{px}\approx\frac{\Theta_V}{N_V}\ \left[\text{rad}\cdot\text{px}^{-1}\right],\qquad \rho=\frac{a_{mesh}}{d\,\theta_{px}}\ \left[\text{px}\cdot\text{period}^{-1}\right]$$

with $a_{mesh}$ mesh period [m]. Resolvable iff $\rho \ge 2$. **Exact singularity:** $\rho < 2$ — the net is aliased rather than imaged, and every downstream claim about "sharp gradients of pixel values" [PAGE 2] is a claim about a moiré artefact, not about the net. This is the paper's unstated regime.

**3. Motion blur (shutter operator).**

$$b=\frac{v\,t_e\,f}{d\,s}\ \left[\text{px}\right]$$

Shutter "$1/60$ seconds" [PAGE 3] gives $t_e = 16.7$ ms; at $v = 4$ m·s⁻¹ and $d = 25$ m, $b \approx 5.5$ px against $n_p \approx 148$ px, i.e. blur/stature ≈ 3.7 %. Assumption: constant velocity within exposure. Breakdown regime: $b/n_p \gtrsim 0.1$, i.e. $v \gtrsim 11$ m·s⁻¹ at this range — reachable by a jump-smash racket head, not by the body centroid, which is why blur is a second-order but non-zero term here.

**4. Gradient magnitude (the operator the paper blames).**

$$g_\sigma(x)=\left|\nabla\!\left(G_\sigma * I\right)(x)\right|\ \left[\text{DN}\cdot\text{px}^{-1}\right],\qquad \max g_\sigma=\frac{\Delta I}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$$

for a step of contrast $\Delta I$ [DN]. Assumption: stationary Gaussian scale $\sigma$. **Exact singularity:** as $\sigma \to 0$, $\max g_\sigma \to \infty$ — an unresolvable high-contrast edge has *unbounded* gradient density, which is precisely the aliased-net case of Eq. (2). The paper states the mechanism — "the regions tend to have sharp gradients of pixel values. However, it is well known that, TLD does not work well in such region" [PAGE 2] — and also the regulatory trap: "the color of the net and the lines is strictly defined. **5. Colour-space gating and mask construction.**

$$\chi_W(x)=\mathbb{1}\!\left[\left(H(x)-H_0\right)^2/w_H^2+\left(S(x)-S_0\right)^2/w_S^2+\left(V(x)-V_0\right)^2/w_V^2\le 1\right]$$

Units: $H$ in degrees or rad, $S,V\in[0,1]$; $r_d$ in px. Assumption: single chromatic cluster for "white." The paper documents this exactly once and qualitatively: "since the near-side player is in white, the Image Inpainting process removed the region with the net and line regions" [PAGE 4]. A second breakdown regime is $\chi_W(x)=0$ on a genuine line — "One reason for the worsening is that the court line is not white" [PAGE 4] — leaving residual $g_\sigma$ in the complement.

**6. Inpainting boundary-value problem (operator unspecified in source).**

$$\nabla^2 u(x)=0,\ x\in\Omega_{r_d};\qquad u(x)=I(x),\ x\in\partial\Omega_{r_d}$$

Units: $u$ in DN; $\nabla^2$ in DN·px⁻². This is the Laplace/Dirichlet form. The source cites Bertalmio et al. Dimensionless. The far-side player's detectability should be reported as a function of $\beta$; it is not.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper's causal ancestry runs through three independent lineages that converge in 2017 at Tsukuba and then diverge again.

*Lineage I — disocclusion as a PDE.* The citation to Bertalmio, Sapiro, Caselles & Ballester, "Image Inpainting," SIGGRAPH 2000 [ref 15, PAGE 6] places the method in a direct descent from Masnou & Morel's 1998 disocclusion work and from the Navier–Stokes analogy Bertalmio borrowed: propagate isophotes (level lines) into the hole, not grey levels. Telea's 2004 fast-marching reformulation [ref 16, PAGE 6] is the fast, approximate descendant and is what most implementations actually execute. Patwardhan, Sapiro & Bertalmio's 2007 video inpainting under constrained camera motion [ref 17, PAGE 6] is the *immediate* antecedent, and here the genealogy inverts: Patwardhan removes *moving* foreground occluders from a *static*-background video; this paper removes a *static* occluder from a video with *moving* foreground. The two problems are duals, and the duality is where the trap lives — removing a static occluder is easier, which is why it was attempted, but it is also riskier, because a static occluder is the only absolute positional reference in the scene.

*Lineage II — P-N bootstrapping.* Kalal, Mikolajczyk & Matas, "Tracking-Learning-Detection," PAMI 34(7):1409–1422, 2012 [ref 14, PAGE 6] descends from Kalal's 2010 P-N learning paper. Its structural assumption is that the P-expert (which proposes corrections for false negatives) and the N-expert (false positives) are *approximately independent* error sources. Aliased repeating texture violates that independence exactly: the net is simultaneously a false-positive generator and, once learned, a false-negative suppressor, because it is the only region that is always present and always self-similar. The experts stop being independent and start reinforcing.

*Lineage III — racket-sport tracking.* The reference list [PAGES 5–6] shows a mature tennis literature (Yan et al. 2014, ref 11; Fang et al. 2014, ref 12; Maruyama & Fukui 2011, ref 13) that the authors deliberately decline to inherit from, arguing badminton is different because "the latter's court is about three times smaller (tennis: 23.77 m × 10.97 m, badminton: 13.4 m × 6.1 m). As a result, occlusion (especially by the net) occurs much more often in badminton videos" [PAGE 2]. That is a defensible claim — 260.8 m² vs 81.7 m², a ratio of 3.19 — and it is the paper's strongest original argument. It is also, retrocausally, the argument that doomed the method: the smaller court means the far-side player is at 25 m while the near-side player is at ~11.6 m along the same axis (court length 13.4 m subtracted from the quoted range), giving a linear scale ratio of ≈0.46 and an *area* ratio of ≈0.21. The far side is not merely occluded; it is one-fifth the pixel budget. No inpainting fixes that.

*Multi-week traps.* (T1) **Resolution pooling.** A lab reproducing at 1080p sees $\Delta F = 0.20$; the same code at 480p, where $\rho \approx 0.70$ px/period, sees aliasing so severe the "net" becomes a low-frequency object detector magnet — a different mechanism masquerading as the same result. Weeks disappear before anyone stratifies. (T2) **The white-tape contradiction.** BWF regulation net tape and net cord are white. The HSV gate of Eq. (5) therefore *always* catches the top band, dilation always grows it, and the mask is never empty. The paper's own remedy — "Since clothing colors can be known in advance, we believe it is possible to avoid such mis-decision using other information such as the shape of the white regions" [PAGE 4] — requires a shape prior that a white tape band and a white shirt both satisfy. (T3) **Threshold portability.** $\chi_W$ is venue-calibrated; a hall with warm-white LED at 3000 K shifts $V$ and $S$ jointly and silently. (T4) **Metric drift.** Improvement and worsening rates share denominator $K$ including "interval times" [PAGE 3]; a longer interval inflates $K$ and deflates both, and nobody notices because both move together.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The authors' model of the system.** Their stated belief is a clean causal chain: net has sharp gradient → TLD scores net as target → remove gradient → TLD scores player. Every sentence in Sections 1–3 supports this chain, and it is a good chain.

**The system's actual model.** TLD does not track "the player"; it tracks whichever region maximises a detector score under an online template set that is updated every frame — "By using the updated learned dataset, the detector finds out the players so that TLD can correct the tracking error" [PAGE 3]. There is no rollback operator, no confidence gate, and no mechanism for *unlearning*. This is the theory-of-mind gap: the authors believe they are removing a distractor; the system is receiving a corrupted training stream and has no way to reject it.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under occlusion stress, the N-expert is supposed to suppress false positives. It cannot, because a repeating texture generates negatives that look exactly like the positives it already accepted. The learner therefore does not fail randomly — it *regresses to its default habit*, which is gradient saliency, the cheapest available feature. This is a stable attractor, not a transient error: once the net enters the template set at frame $k$, it is a positive example at $k+1$, which biases the detector at $k+2$, which re-confirms the template at $k+3$. The failure is **absorbing**. Formal statement: let $\mathcal{T}_k$ be the template set and $\mathcal{A}$ the set of template sets containing any net patch; then $\mathcal{T}_k \in \mathcal{A} \Rightarrow \mathcal{T}_{k+1}\in\mathcal{A}$ with probability $\to 1$ as $r_d$ increases. The paper's own evidence for absorbing behaviour is the near-total elimination of far-side failure in Fig. 4(b2) [PAGE 4] — if inpainting merely *reduced* the attractor's basin, you would see partial recovery, not "accurately tracked."

**Counter-trap.** The intervention creates its own trap, and the paper names it: "when we tracked the near-side player in sequence 3, the F-measure value drastically decreased with II+TLD … since the near-side player is in white, the Image Inpainting process removed the region with the net and line regions" [PAGE 4]. The mask, designed to delete the net, deletes the player. Mechanically: inpainting fills $\Omega_{r_d}$ from $\partial\Omega_{r_d}$, and for a static camera the fill is approximately frame-invariant. Consecutive frames therefore present a *zero-optical-flow* patch exactly where the player is, and TLD's median-flow estimator — which assumes the box interior moves coherently with the target — is biased toward zero displacement. The tracker stalls, not because it lost the target, but because the target's pixels were replaced by a background that does not move. This is a counter-trap of the classical form: **the cure is the disease vector.**

**The clock.** Two clocks, and they disagree. Clock A is acquisition: 30 fps with a 1/60 s shutter — a 50 % duty cycle, so half of every 33.3 ms interval is unobserved. Clock B is the tracking horizon: $K = 18{,}000$ to $36{,}000$ frames per sequence, and a single absorbing event at any $k$ poisons all $K-k$ subsequent frames. The ratio of these clocks is what makes the problem asymmetric: an error budget that is negligible per-frame becomes dominant over 36,000 frames. There is a third, institutional clock — "assistant coaches and players want the analysis data as quickly as possible. Thus, high-speed automatic processing is critical" [PAGE 1] — which is the *real* design constraint and is why the authors chose propagation inpainting over retargeting: "it is another advantage of the former approach that the computational cost is smaller than the latter one" [PAGE 2]. Every methodological choice in this paper is downstream of a latency requirement that is never quantified. That is the deepest theory-of-mind failure: the paper optimises a constraint it does not measure.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Veto 1 — Optical/Nyquist (hard veto, independent of method).** By Eq. (2), the net mesh at 25 m and 1080 rows sits at $\rho\approx1.57$ px·period⁻¹, and at 480 rows at $\rho\approx0.70$. Both are below the $\rho\ge2$ threshold. Consequence: the paper's central causal claim — that the net is a *shape* with a *sharp gradient* — is only literally true after aliasing has already destroyed the shape. Any method justified by "shape gradients" is adjudicating an artefact. **Verdict: the optical layer vetoes the stated mechanism, not the empirical result.** The result may be real while the explanation is wrong.

**Veto 2 — Photometric/illumination (soft veto, venue-dependent).** The paper explicitly converts because of lighting — "To reduce the deleterious effects caused by the lighting condition, we converted the color space from RGB to HSV" [PAGE 3] — but HSV protects against *intensity* variation, not against *spectral* variation. Gymnasium lighting at 100/120 Hz against a 1/60 s shutter produces a beat note: the fractional flux in the exposure window varies frame-to-frame at up to the flicker amplitude, shifting $V$ and $S$ jointly. The gate of Eq. (5) has no temporal hysteresis, so $\Omega_{r_d}$ breathes. **Verdict: photometric layer partially vetoes the transfer claim; the effect is bounded by flicker depth and is testable (see RLVR-3).**

**Veto 3 — Geometric/perspective (hard veto).** The camera "captures the badminton court by looking down in a longitudinal direction" [PAGE 3]. With the court's long axis at 13.4 m and a quoted range of ~25 m to the players, the near-side/far-side linear scale ratio is ≈0.46 and the area ratio ≈0.21. The far-side player has roughly one-fifth the pixels and is *also* the occluded one. This is a double jeopardy that is architectural, not algorithmic. It explains the paper's asymmetry (failures concentrated on the far side, Fig. 2 [PAGE 2] and Fig. 4(b1) [PAGE 4]) better than the gradient story does. **Verdict: geometric layer vetoes any claim that the method is symmetric across the net.**

**Veto 4 — Biomechanical (soft veto).** Badminton articulation is extreme: full lunges, overhead extension, and rapid bounding-box aspect-ratio change between lunge (wide) and jump (tall). TLD's scale search and median-flow estimator both assume moderate inter-frame deformation. The paper concedes the general problem — "large variation of player appearance" [PAGE 1] — but never measures aspect-ratio variance. At $n_p\approx148$ px, a 2:1 aspect swing is a 74-px change, larger than the search radius a 30 fps tracker would use. **Verdict: biomechanical layer sets the recall ceiling that the precision ceiling of Eq. (9) cannot lift.**

**Veto 5 — Weather (near-total veto, stated honestly).** Badminton is indoor; precipitation, wind, and insolation are excluded by construction. The only residual weather channel is HVAC: hall temperature and humidity alter shuttle drag by a few percent, which shifts rally tempo and hence the dwell-time distribution of the far-side player in the occluded band. This is a third-order effect and does not veto anything. **Verdict: the weather layer is properly vetoed out, and claiming otherwise would be overfitting the framework to the sport rather than to the physics.** The dominant physical layer is optics; the dominant process layer is the absorbing-state dynamics of the template set.

*Prediction:* $\Delta F$ is resolution-dependent and *larger* at 480p, because at $\rho\approx0.70$ px·period⁻¹ the moiré lattice is a low-frequency, high-contrast, stable structure that TLD's median-flow tracker accepts more readily than the higher-frequency 1080p version. **RLVR-V3 — The residual-gradient correlation test (tests the single-cause worsening attribution, Page 4).**
*Intervention:* define $E_k = \frac{1}{|\Omega_{r_d}^c|}\sum_{x\notin\Omega_{r_d}} g_\sigma(x)$ per frame — the mean residual gradient energy in the *unmasked* complement, in DN·px⁻¹.
