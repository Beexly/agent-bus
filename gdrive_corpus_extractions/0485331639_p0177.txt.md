The clock problem arises because the perpetual value-income model assumes an infinite decision horizon, while directors and managers have finite tenure and finite lifespans. In crisis, the signal becomes noisy because custom and creation dominate income, so shareholders cannot infer expected profitability from observed investment. The regression is to mores; the counter-trap is misreading normative investment as informational; the clock is finite for the agent but infinite in the model.

Prediction: the correlation between investment volume and expected income approaches zero, \( \operatorname{Corr}(\Delta K, \mathbb{E}[\pi]) \to 0 \), and capital formation tracks social custom and network ties instead of income signals.
