Counterfactual: If an unfamiliar adult presents a terrible or scowling face to an infant under one year, then, per James's doubt and his own observation that infants under one year show at most attention or curiosity [PAGE 404], the probability of a fear response should be \(P(\text{fear} \mid \text{scowling face}, a < 1) \approx 0\), whereas \(P(\text{smile} \mid \text{being smiled at or fondled}) \) should be high. Verifiable prediction: In a controlled presentation, infants under 12 months will not show differential fear (avoidance, crying, withdrawal) to scowling versus neutral faces, but will smile more to smiling/fondling than to still-face. Veto condition: If infants under 6 months consistently cry or withdraw to scowling faces at rates above baseline, James's null claim is falsified and the "instinctive fear" alternative is supported.

Scenario 3 — Proto-speech ordering veto. Counterfactual: If the "peculiar sound expressive of desire" accompanying pointing is the first manifestation of speech, then its onset time \(t_D\) must precede the onset of other significant sounds \(t_W\) by a measurable interval, and pointing without sound should be less frequent than pointing with sound at the same developmental epoch [PAGE 404]. Verifiable prediction: Longitudinal diary or video data will show \(t_D < t_W\) by at least several weeks, and the first referential word will appear only after the desire-sound–pointing combination is established. Veto condition: If a child produces a referential word (e.g., "mama" used to refer to the mother) before ever producing a desire-sound with pointing, the claim that the desire-sound is the first manifestation of speech is falsified.
