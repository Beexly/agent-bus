<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source’s core kinematic operator is angular velocity about the pelvis. Formally, for a joint angle \(\theta(t)\) measured in radians,

\[
\omega(t)=\frac{d\theta}{dt}\quad [\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}],
\]

or in the paper’s reported units,

\[
\omega_{\deg}(t)=\frac{d\theta_{\deg}}{dt}\quad [\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}].
\]

The reported post-test value is “745.20 degrees/second” [PAGE 1], which is

\[
745.20^\circ/\mathrm{s}\times \frac{\pi}{180}=13.006\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}.
\]

For a finite-difference estimate at frame interval \(\Delta t=1/f_s\),

\[
\hat{\omega}_k=\frac{\theta_{k+1}-\theta_{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\quad [\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}],
\]

with approximate angle-noise propagation

\[
\sigma_{\hat{\omega}}\approx \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta}{\Delta t}.
\]

This equation has an exact noise-dominated breakdown regime when

\[
\frac{\sigma_{\hat{\omega}}}{\omega}\ge 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta}{\omega\,\Delta t}\ge 1.
\]

The source reports cameras at “120-240 frames/second” [PAGE 4], so \(\Delta t\) is 8.33 ms at 120 fps and 4.17 ms at 240 fps. At 120 fps, 745.20 deg/s gives 6.21 deg/frame; at 240 fps, 3.105 deg/frame. If \(\sigma_\theta=1^\circ\), the implied velocity noise is about 169.7 deg/s at 120 fps and 84.9 deg/s at 240 fps; if \(\sigma_\theta=2^\circ\), those values double. The paper does not report \(\sigma_\theta\), keypoint confidence, or filtering.

The second operator is kinetic transfer time:

\[
T=t_{\mathrm{kick2,start}}-t_{\mathrm{kick1,end}}\quad [\mathrm{s}].
\]

The reported values are pre 0.58 s and post 0.38 s for the experimental group [PAGE 6], and the system threshold is “>0.40 seconds” [PAGE 11]. The reported improvement is

\[
\frac{0.58-0.38}{0.58}\times 100=34.48\%.
\]

The third operator is accuracy. The paper reports “Accuracy of kicking performance” in “Degree” [PAGE 4; PAGE 6]. If this is angular error, the formal object is

\[
\epsilon=|\theta_{\mathrm{hit}}-\theta_{\mathrm{target}}|\quad [^\circ],
\]

and lower values are better. If it is a score, it is dimensionless and higher is better. The source never resolves this direction-of-scale issue. The reported pre-to-post accuracy change is 6.20 to 9.10, or

\[
\frac{9.10-6.20}{6.20}\times 100=46.77\%,
\]

if higher is better; if “Degree” is angular error, the same arithmetic would mean worse accuracy.

For hypothesis testing, the paired t-statistic is

\[
t=\frac{\bar d}{s_d/\sqrt{n}},
\]

and the independent-samples statistic is

\[
t=\frac{\bar x_1-\bar x_2}
{\sqrt{s_1^2/n_1+s_2^2/n_2}},
\]

with \(t\) dimensionless and p-values in the unit interval \([0,1]\). The source reports \(p=0.000\) [PAGE 6], which should be reported as \(p<0.001\), not as an exact zero. Reliability is reported as Pearson

\[
r=\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}
{\sqrt{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2}},
\]

with \(r\in[-1,1]\). The reported values are 0.89, 0.87, and 0.91 [PAGE 4], but the stability sample is the exploratory sample of three players [PAGE 4; PAGE 5], so the sampling distribution is too unstable for a strong reliability claim.

Biomechanically, the paper repeatedly infers from pelvic \(\omega\) to kick accuracy. A minimal formal link is

\[
v_t=\omega r\quad [\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}],\qquad
a_c=\omega^2 r\quad [\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}],
\]

for a point at radius \(r\) from the rotation axis, under rigid-body and fixed-axis assumptions. Angular momentum is

\[
L=I\omega\quad [\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}].
\]

These equations do not validate the paper’s claim that the system “correct[ed] the ‘radius of rotation’ instantaneously” [PAGE 7], because no radius, moment of inertia, segment mass, or 3-D segment orientation is reported. The exact breakdown regime is when the rigid-body/fixed-axis assumptions fail, as they do in a multi-segment taekwondo kick with trunk tilt, support-foot rotation, and changing joint axes.

The camera-sampling operator has a Nyquist breakdown:

\[
f_s>2f_{\max}.
\]

If the true motion contains frequency content above \(f_s/2\), the measured angular velocity is aliased. The paper gives a range “120-240 frames/second” [PAGE 4] but no actual frame rate per trial, no shutter timing, and no synchronization evidence. The table-level contradiction in Table 7 is also exact: it lists “Trunk angle 12 - 15 درجة 14.2 درجة 21 % Identical” [PAGE 8], so an achieved value inside the stated ideal interval is paired with an unexplained 21% deviation ratio and an “Identical” label.

Physical-layer veto: the measurement claim is vetoed if the camera/keypoint layer cannot support 745.20 deg/s.
