**1. The homography operator (the object being estimated).**
A 2-D projective map acting on homogeneous pixel coordinates:
$$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}' \;\sim\; H\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\1\end{bmatrix},\quad H \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3},\quad H \equiv \lambda H,\ \lambda \neq 0 .$$
Gauge-fixing $h_{33}=1$ leaves $3\times3-1 = 8$ free parameters, matching the paper verbatim: "Estimating the homography requires the eight degrees of freedom afforded by the four matched pairs to compute the homography" [PAGE 5]. Each correspondence supplies 2 scalar constraints; $n=4$ pairs give $2n = 8$ equations, exactly determined.

**2. Direct Linear Transform and its singularity regimes.**
Stacking constraints gives $A\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{2n\times 9}$, $\mathbf{h} = \mathrm{vec}(H)$:
$$\mathbf{h}^\star = \arg\min_{\|\mathbf{h}\|_2 = 1} \|A\mathbf{h}\|_2^2 ,$$
solved by the right singular vector associated with the smallest singular value of $A$. Units: $A$ entries are dimensionless because $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ rows are ratios of pixel coordinates (px/px), so $u,v$ enter in px but cancel in the homogeneous ratio; the residual $\|A\mathbf{h}\|_2$ is therefore dimensionless. Breakdown regimes, stated exactly:
- **Rank deficiency:** if any three of the four correspondences are collinear (in either image), $\mathrm{rank}(A) < 8$ and $H$ is not unique. Detection test: $\sigma_8/\sigma_9 \to 1$ (no spectral gap).
- **Line-at-infinity crossing:** with $h_{33}=1$ enforced, the parameterisation is singular when the true map sends the line at infinity through the image plane, $h_{33}\to 0$. This is precisely the large-$|\theta|$ regime.
- **Support starvation:** if Stage 3 returns $N < 4$ correspondences, no solution exists. This is the mechanism behind the FAST/BRIEF collapse and the 50° wall.

**3. The ratio test (Stage 3 operator).**
For descriptor $d_a$ in image 1 with nearest neighbour $d_b$ and second-nearest $d_c$ in image 2:
$$r \;=\; \frac{\|d_a - d_b\|_2}{\|d_a - d_c\|_2} \;<\; \tau,\qquad \tau = 0.75\ \text{(dimensionless)},$$
verbatim from [PAGE 5]: "When implementing BF kNN, the threshold is set to 0.75, testing the ratio of distance." Descriptor vectors are bitstrings (units: bits per descriptor; length $L$ is **not reported** in the source and is therefore unidentifiable from this drive). Euclidean distance between binary descriptors has units of bits$^{1/2}$ but the ratio is dimensionless. Breakdown: as $\tau\to 1$ the test is vacuous; as $\tau\to 0$ it triggers support starvation ($N<4$). The paper's empirical finding sits exactly in this trade-off: "Finding fewer but more precisely matched pairs can reduce the possibility that a homography matrix is estimated… This suggests that kNN is preferable when enough features, with corresponding descriptors are detected" [PAGE 7].

**4. PSNR (Stage 5 operator, metric 1).**
$$\mathrm{PSNR} \;=\; 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{MAX}_I^{\,2}}{\mathrm{MSE}}\right)\ \ [\mathrm{dB}],\qquad \mathrm{MSE} = \frac{1}{MN}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big[I(i,j)-K(i,j)\big]^2 .$$
Decibels are a dimensionless logarithmic unit; the underlying MSE carries intensity$^2$. **Unit audit:** the paper reports $\mathrm{PSNR} = 93.605$ dB for the top pipeline [PAGE 6]. Under $\mathrm{MAX}_I = 255$ this implies $\mathrm{MSE} = 255^2/10^{9.3605} \approx 2.84\times10^{-5}$, i.e. RMSE $\approx 5.3\times10^{-3}$ intensity levels — indistinguishable to any observer. Under $\mathrm{MAX}_I = 1$ (float normalisation, typical of the Müller toolbox [PAGE 6]) it implies $\mathrm{MSE}\approx 4.4\times10^{-10}$, worse. That value is jointly impossible with a concurrent FSIM of 0.604 on the same warp. Conclusion: **PSNR in Table 1 is range-normalised and/or support-masked in a way the paper does not disclose, and its absolute scale is not a valid similarity magnitude.** Its *ordering* survives only where gaps are large; rank 1 vs rank 2 differ by 0.279 dB, i.e. an MSE ratio of $10^{0.0279}\approx 1.066$ — inside plausible estimator noise.

**5. FSIM (metric 2).**
Phase-congruency / gradient structure similarity, normalised to $[0,1]$ [PAGE 6]:
$$\mathrm{FSIM} = \frac{\sum_{x\in\Omega} S_L(x)\,PC_m(x)}{\sum_{x\in\Omega} PC_m(x)},\quad S_L = S_{PC}\cdot S_G,\quad PC_m = \max(PC_1, PC_2),$$
$$S_{PC} = \frac{2PC_1PC_2 + T_1}{PC_1^2+PC_2^2+T_1},\qquad S_G = \frac{2G_1G_2 + T_2}{G_1^2+G_2^2+T_2},$$
with $T_1, T_2$ stabilising constants and $G$ the Scharr gradient magnitude. FSIM is dimensionless. Unlike PSNR it is contrast-normalised and thus not saturated by large flat agreement regions — which is exactly why Table 2 shows FSIM collapsing to 0.24983 while PSNR retains 80.386 dB [PAGE 7]. **FSIM is the load-bearing operator in this drive.**

**6. Perspective-angle constraint (the operating envelope).**
$$\theta \in [0^{\circ},\,50^{\circ}],\qquad \theta_k = 40^{\circ} + 10^{\circ}(k-3),\ k=3,4,5,6,$$
derived verbatim: "a viewing angle change of 40° between images one and three, with an increase of 10° of change for each subsequent image" [PAGE 5]. Overlap area is monotone non-increasing in $|\theta|$: $\partial A_{\mathrm{overlap}}/\partial\theta \le 0$. The paper's attributed failure mode is exactly this: "the dataset's resolution with a smaller overlapping region affecting the ability to warp the image" [PAGE 8]. Breakdown at $\theta \gtrsim 50^{\circ}$ is empirically demonstrated, not merely conjectured [PAGE 7].

**7. Stage-4 inlier operator.**
RANSAC/USAC maximise
$$\hat{H} = \arg\max_{H}\ \big|\{\,i : \|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}'_i - H\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i\|_2 < \epsilon\,\}\big|,\qquad N_{\mathrm{in}} \ge 4,$$
with $\epsilon$ in px and $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ a dimensionless integer count. Breakdown: when the inlier fraction $w$ satisfies $w^{s} \ll 1$ (with $s=4$ the minimal sample size), required iterations $\approx \log(1-p)/\log(1-w^{s})$ diverge. The paper's verdict on this operator is explicitly *non-identifying*: "there is no definitive data to suggest that there is a significant performance increase in USAC compared to RANSAC" [PAGE 8].

</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The pipeline's ancestry is fully traceable through the reference list [PAGE 8–9] and is a textbook case of *time-indexed* state-of-the-art labelling. Corner detection descends from Trajković & Hedley (1998) [PAGE 9]; scale-space interest points from Mikolajczyk & Schmid (2004, 2005), which also supply the OACRVG benchmark [PAGE 5]; SIFT from Lowe (2004) [PAGE 3]; SURF from Bay et al. (2006) — excluded here "Due to licensing restrictions" [PAGE 4]; BRIEF from Calonder et al. (2010); ORB from Rublee et al. (2011); BRISK from Leutenegger et al. (2011); FREAK from Alahi et al. (2012); AKAZE from Alcantarilla et al. (2013); USAC from Raguram et al. (2013); FLANN from Muja & Lowe (2014) — excluded because "FLANN performed poorly in the past" [PAGE 5]; and the deep-learning successor LIFT from Yi et al. (2016), invoked only to justify the classical route's speed advantage [PAGE 4].

**Trap 1 — reification of "state-of-the-art."** The paper records that "AKAZE is considered a state-of-the-art feature detector and descriptor algorithm, outperforming SIFT, SURF, ORB, and BRISK using a variety of datasets" [PAGE 4], then empirically demotes it: "most of the pipelines involving SIFT and BRISK outperformed their AKAZE counterparts, these findings contrast with [Tong et al., 2021, Sharma and Jain, 2020]" [PAGE 7]. A multi-week trap is treating the SOTA label as a property of the algorithm rather than a property of (algorithm × dataset × metric × year).

**Trap 2 — the licensing exclusion.** SURF's absence [PAGE 4] means the factorial is incomplete by one of the six canonical detectors. Any cross-paper comparison that silently reinstates SURF is comparing different design spaces.

**Trap 3 — the antecedent drift.** "This version of FAST" [PAGE 7] must bind to BRISK, not FAST. Historical naming (BRISK is FAST-derived) makes this an easy mis-extraction with a 13-rank consequence.

**Trap 4 — benchmark monoculture.** A single six-image planar sequence from 2004–2005 methodology cannot license claims about 2026 deployment. Iterating on OACRVG alone reproduces the same 50° ceiling indefinitely.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis habit regression.** The reflexive failure mode for any reader of Table 1 is *rank-worship*: promote "BRISK – FREAK – BF – USAC" (FSIM 0.604, PSNR 93.605) [PAGE 6] to a general law. The habit regresses because the promotion is then defended by the very metric that is broken — PSNR — while the authors' own Table 2 [PAGE 7] shows that same champion pipeline failing outright at image 5 (FSIM 0.24983). A second regression is *architecture attribution*: crediting USAC for the win when rank 3 (identical but RANSAC, FSIM 0.596) is within 0.008, a gap the study cannot resolve without repeated randomised runs.

**Counter-trap.** The symmetric error is wholesale dismissal — "single dataset, saturated metric, therefore uninformative." That over-corrects. Three claims survive scrutiny: (i) the 50° envelope is demonstrated, not asserted [PAGE 2, 7, 8]; (ii) FREAK > BRIEF is consistent across ranks (e.g. rank 1 vs rank 24, 0.604 vs 0.472) and is mechanistically explained — "BRIEF's poor performances can be traced to its inadequate performance with in-plane rotations" [PAGE 7]; (iii) FAST as a *detector* is genuinely last — "FAST is sub-par, producing the worst results, as it could not determine the orientation of a keypoint" [PAGE 7], corroborated by rows 34–46 all sitting in the bottom thirteen.

**Reading the authors' minds (calibration).** The hedging language is informative. "could potentially be a restriction in their ability to handle significant perspective transformations" [PAGE 7]; "there could potentially be"; "This could be due to factors such as…" [PAGE 8]. These are not rhetorical softeners — they mark genuinely unidentified confounds (resolution vs overlap vs descriptor support). Conversely, the one place the authors drop hedging is the USAC/RANSAC verdict, and that is precisely the place where the supporting sentence is textually broken (the "SIFT - SIFT - BF kNN – RANSAC… SIFT - SIFT - BF kNN – RANSAC" duplication spanning [PAGE 7]→[PAGE 8], where Table 1 assigns the second value, 91.637, to the **USAC** row at rank 7). Confidence and evidence are inversely correlated at exactly one joint.

**Reading the reader's mind.** A downstream consumer will (a) quote 93.605 dB as if decibels at that magnitude mean something, (b) cite "row 26" without noticing the duplicated label [PAGE 6], and (c) assume "BRISK" and "FAST" are interchangeable because the paper says BRISK "improves upon FAST" [PAGE 3]. All three are predictable and all three are checkable.

**The clock.** The evidentiary clock is bimodal: 6 images × 6 detector/descriptor families × 2 matchers × 2 robust estimators = 46 realised pipelines, but only 5 usable viewpoint steps (images 2–6) with an effective ceiling at step 4. So the *effective* sample for the headline claim is one image pair at 50°. Compute budget was not the constraint — statistical power was.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical / optical layer — PARTIAL VETO.** A homography is exact only for (a) a planar scene under arbitrary camera motion, or (b) any scene under pure rotation. The paper's benchmark is planar-covariant by construction (OACRVG, 800×640 px, 40°→70° sequence [PAGE 5]), and it *never states* the planarity assumption or tests its violation. Veto: any inference about scenes with depth relief — crowds, stadium tiers, goal frames — is unsupported, because parallax violates $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}' \sim H\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ at the model level, not the estimator level. Secondary veto: lens distortion. The paper's motivating contrast is wide-angle distortion [PAGE 2], yet no radial-distortion term is modelled or removed anywhere in the four stages, so the "no wide-angle distortion" advantage is asserted, not measured.

**Biomechanical / anthropomorphic layer — HARD VETO.** FREAK is "inspired by the human visual system… utilises a retinal circular sampling grid" with "a higher density of points that are closer to the centre" [PAGE 3]. This is an *engineering* borrowing of a sampling geometry; it is not a claim about human or athlete perception and carries no biomechanical content whatsoever. Veto: any reading that treats FREAK's win (0.604 FSIM, rank 1 [PAGE 6]) as evidence about human视觉 physiology, gaze behaviour, or athletic response is category-invalid. The chain of causation runs descriptor-bitstring → match density → homography residual; there is no organism in the loop.

**Weather / environmental layer — HARD VETO.** The dataset is a single static outdoor-artwork sequence with controlled viewpoint change and no reported illumination, precipitation, aerosol, or thermal protocol. The authors themselves scope future work to "multiple datasets with different resolutions and environments, such as sports fields" [PAGE 8] — an explicit admission that environmental generalisation is untested. Veto: no claim about rain, glare, low sun, shadow migration, heat shimmer, or night-time IR transfer is licensed. Note also that FSIM and PSNR are both *appearance* metrics and neither is invariant to global photometric shift, so the paper could not have detected a weather effect even if one were present.

**Institutional layer — DISCLOSURE VETO ON SCOPE.** "This work was funded by a DfE CAST scholarship in collaboration with Metro Surveillance Group LTD" [PAGE 8], and the fourth author is affiliated with that company [PAGE 2]. The commercial interest is surveillance/broadcast stitching; the benchmark is an artwork. No misconduct is implied, but scope claims should be read as industry-motivated rather than deployment-validated.

**Downstream-market layer — ABSOLUTE VETO.** Nothing in this drive constitutes, supports, or informs any priced proposition, contest outcome, or wagering construct. The paper reports image-similarity statistics in dB and in $[0,1]$; those operators have no mapping onto any market quantity. Any such use is void at this layer and requires none of the above analysis to reject.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto scenario 1 — PSNR re-normalisation ablation.**
*Intervention:* recompute Stage-5 metrics with an explicitly declared $\mathrm{MAX}_I$ (255 for 8-bit, 1.0 for float) and a declared support mask, on the identical 46 warped outputs.
*Falsifiable prediction:* reported PSNR values will collapse from the 79.7–93.6 dB band into the conventional 12–35 dB band, and the *relative* ordering of near-tied pairs will change. Specifically, ranks 2/3 (93.326 vs 93.018, gap 0.308 dB), ranks 4/5 (92.224 vs 91.749, gap 0.475 dB) and ranks 5/6 (91.749 vs 91.685, gap 0.064 dB) are each predicted to be unstable, while the FSIM ordering (0.604, 0.596, 0.581, 0.571, 0.563…) will be *more* stable because FSIM is contrast-normalised. *Veto trigger:* if fewer than 3 of those near-tied pairs flip, this counterfactual is falsified and PSNR's ordering is provisionally rehabilitated.

**Veto scenario 2 — viewpoint extension beyond the claimed envelope.**
*Intervention:* synthesise a 60° and 70° pair by extending the OACRVG sequence (or by re-projecting image 1 with the supplied ground-truth homographies composed to $\theta = 60^{\circ}, 70^{\circ}$) and run all 46 pipelines.
*Falsifiable prediction:* **zero** pipelines produce a valid homography with FSIM $> 0.40$ at 60°, and the champion pipeline's FSIM degrades below 0.20 — because Table 2 [PAGE 7] already shows FSIM $= 0.24983$ at the 70° step (image 5) and $0.276183$ at 80° (image 6), with "unable to produce a warped image to beyond image 4 (50° rotation)" [PAGE 7]. *Veto trigger:* if any pipeline achieves FSIM $> 0.40$ at 60°, the "maximum perspective change of 50°" claim [PAGE 8] is refuted as a pipeline-independent bound and must be restated as pipeline-conditional.

**Veto scenario 3 — robust-estimator seed-variance ablation.**
*Intervention:* take the two champion-adjacent pipelines — BRISK–FREAK–BF–USAC (rank 1, FSIM 0.604) and BRISK–FREAK–BF–RANSAC (rank 3, FSIM 0.596) [PAGE 6] — and run each $n = 50$ times with distinct RANSAC/USAC random seeds on the same correspondences, reporting mean ± standard deviation of FSIM.
*Falsifiable prediction:* the between-seed standard deviation will exceed the 0.008 FSIM gap between ranks 1 and 3, making the USAC-over-RANSAC ordering statistically non-identified — which will *confirm* the authors' hedged conclusion "there is no definitive data to suggest that there is a significant performance increase in USAC compared to RANSAC" [PAGE 8] while *refuting* the sentence offered to support it (the duplicated "SIFT - SIFT - BF kNN – RANSAC… SIFT - SIFT - BF kNN – RANSAC" attribution across [PAGE 7]→[PAGE 8], where Table 1 assigns 91.637 to the USAC row at rank 7). *Veto trigger:* if $\sigma_{\mathrm{FSIM}} < 0.002$ across seeds, the estimator ordering becomes identifiable and the parity claim fails.

**Composite acceptance condition.** All three vetoes are independently executable on the released OpenCV 4.6.0 / NumPy 1.24.2 pipeline [PAGE 5] using the OACRVG homographies supplied with the dataset [PAGE 5]. If all three predictions hold, the drive supports exactly three durable propositions: FSIM is the load-bearing metric; the 50° envelope is real but pipeline-conditional; and Stage-1/2 detector-descriptor choice dominates Stage-4 robust-estimator choice. If any prediction fails, the corresponding claim is withdrawn rather than softened.
