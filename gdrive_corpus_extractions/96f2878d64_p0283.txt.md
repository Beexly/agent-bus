<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
When an analyst encounters the boundedness statement on [PAGE 268], the default “crisis habit” regression is to note that $\rho(A,B)\le\mu(A)+\mu(B)=2\mu(E)$ for all translates $xE$ and $E$, and therefore to conclude that $\rho(xE,E)\le\varepsilon$ holds for every $x$ whenever $\varepsilon\ge2\mu(E)$, then to overgeneralize and assume boundedness persists at and beyond the boundary. This regression treats the metric diameter as merely an upper bound rather than as the precise point where the sublevel set jumps from bounded to all of $X$.

The counter-trap is to recognize that the sufficiency proof’s engine—the choice of $\delta$ with $4\delta<2\mu(E)-\varepsilon$—has no positive solution once $\varepsilon\ge2\mu(E)$, so no compact container $C$ can be certified, and indeed the necessity construction shows that for any compact $C$ there exists an $E$ with $C\subset\{x:\rho(xE,E)\le\varepsilon_\star\}$ for $\varepsilon_\star$ arbitrarily close to $2\mu(E)$. Hence boundedness is lost precisely at the diameter, not before.

The clock: parametrize $\varepsilon(t)=2\mu(E)(1-t)$ for $t\in[0,1)$. The admissible slack is $2\mu(E)-\varepsilon(t)=2\mu(E)t$, and the condition $4\delta<2\mu(E)t$ forces $\delta<\mu(E)t/2$. As $t\downarrow0$ (i.e., $\varepsilon\uparrow2\mu(E)$), the window for $\delta$ closes linearly in $t$; the inner-regularity compact set $C$ must satisfy $\mu(C)>\mu(E)-\delta$, so $C$ is forced to exhaust $E$ in measure while the geometric boundedness certificate vanishes. At $t=0$ the clock stops: $\delta=0$ is inadmissible, $C$ cannot be chosen, and the sublevel set becomes $X$. A recursive theory of mind must therefore track not just the inequality $\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$ but the rate at which the $\delta$-window collapses, because that rate is the only early-warning signal before the boundedness veto triggers.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source page [PAGE 268] contains no physical, biomechanical, or weather variables; it is pure measure theory on topological groups. Consequently, the only admissible “veto” layers are measure-theoretic and topological, and they function exactly like physical veto conditions: each one, if violated, invalidates the boundedness conclusion regardless of the other layers.

Layer 1 (measure finiteness): the theorem requires $0<\mu(E)<\infty$ [PAGE 268]. If $\mu(E)=0$, the hypothesis $0\le\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$ is empty; if $\mu(E)=\infty$, then $2\mu(E)=\infty$ and the hypothesis holds for every finite $\varepsilon$, yet the sublevel set need not be bounded. Veto: infinite or zero measure kills boundedness.

Layer 2 (local compactness and Haar existence): $\rho$ and the translation argument require a left Haar measure $\mu$ on a locally compact group $X$ [PAGE 268]. Without local compactness there is no invariant Radon measure and no inner regularity $\mu(E)-\delta<\mu(C)$ for compact $C$; the proof collapses at the choice of $C$.

Layer 3 (left invariance): the identity $\rho(xE,E)=2\mu(E)-2\mu(xE\cap E)$ uses $\mu(xE)=\mu(E)$ [PAGE 268]. A non-invariant measure breaks the simplification; $\mu(xE)$ may vary with $x$, so $\rho(xE,E)\le\varepsilon$ no longer reduces to a lower bound on $\mu(xE\cap E)$, and boundedness is not certified by $\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$.

Layer 4 (boundary diameter): at $\varepsilon=2\mu(E)$, disjoint translates give $\rho=2\mu(E)$, so $\{\rho\le\varepsilon\}=X$; boundedness then holds only if $X$ is compact [PAGE 268]. This is the hard veto: the metric diameter is an absolute ceiling.

Layer 5 (textual/OCR): the garbled relations “$e+28$” and “$E$ U $C^{-1}$D U $D$” on [PAGE 268] constitute a documentary veto: until collated against a clean print, the intermediate inequalities cannot be trusted, and any downstream derivation inherits the uncertainty. Adjudication rule: a veto at any layer voids the boundedness claim; no stacking of partial conditions can rescue it.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 (diameter veto). Let $X=\mathbb R$ with Lebesgue measure $\mu$ and $E=[0,1]$, so $\mu(E)=1$ and $2\mu(E)=2$ [PAGE 268 framework]. For $x\ge1$, the sets $E$ and $x+E$ are disjoint, hence $\rho(x+E,E)=\mu(E)+\mu(x+E)=2$. Thus for $\varepsilon=2$ the sublevel set $\{x:\rho(x+E,E)\le2\}$ is all of $\mathbb R$, unbounded. For any $\varepsilon<2$, symmetry difference gives $\rho(x+E,E)=2|x|$ for $|x|\le1$ and $2$ for $|x|\ge1$, so the sublevel set is $[-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2]$, bounded. Prediction: boundedness holds exactly for $\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$ and fails at $\varepsilon=2\mu(E)$; verifiable by direct computation of the symmetric difference.

Scenario 2 (infinite-measure veto). Let $X=\mathbb R$, $\mu$ Lebesgue, and $E=\mathbb R$. Then $\mu(E)=\infty$, so the hypothesis $0\le\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$ holds for every finite $\varepsilon$ [PAGE 268]. But $\rho(x+E,E)=\mu(\mathbb R\triangle\mathbb R)=0$ for all $x$, hence $\{\rho\le\varepsilon\}=\mathbb R$ is unbounded for every $\varepsilon\ge0$. Prediction: finiteness $\mu(E)<\infty$ is indispensable; without it the boundedness conclusion is false even though the numerical hypothesis is satisfied for all finite $\varepsilon$. Verifiable by direct measure computation.

Scenario 3 (non-invariant-measure veto). Let $X=\mathbb R$ as a group under addition, but replace Lebesgue measure by the finite-on-compacts measure $\mu$ with density $w(t)=e^t$ relative to Lebesgue, so $\mu$ is not translation-invariant. Take $E=[0,1]$. Then $\mu(x+E)=\int_x^{x+1}e^t\,dt=e^x(e-1)\neq\mu(E)=e-1$ for $x\neq0$. The identity $\rho(x+E,E)=2\mu(E)-2\mu(x+E\cap E)$ fails because $\mu(x+E)\neq\mu(E)$; indeed for large $x$, $\mu(x+E)$ grows exponentially while the overlap $\mu(x+E\cap E)$ vanishes, so $\rho(x+E,E)$ grows like $e^x(e-1)+(e-1)$, and no fixed $\varepsilon<\infty$ bounds $x$. Prediction: left-invariance of $\mu$ is a necessary veto layer; without it the condition $\varepsilon<2\mu(E)$ does not imply boundedness. Verifiable by computing $\rho$ explicitly for this weighted measure.
