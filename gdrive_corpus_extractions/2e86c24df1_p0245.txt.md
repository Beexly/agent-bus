Since the player's internal model is optimized for routine, this environmental shift causes a surge in Surprise ($\mathcal{S} = -\log p(s|\theta)$). Furthermore, eating "a little less than they want" aligns with minimizing gastrointestinal blood flow competition and reducing post-prandial fatigue, keeping the autonomic state in a stable parasympathetic dominant mode until the required sympathetic surge at kickoff.

A gray-zone audit reveals potential paradoxes. Roper advocates for extreme isolation to prevent over-arousal. However, modern sports psychology often utilizes "activation" strategies. Roper’s 1920 context lacked understanding of neuroplasticity and rhythmic entrainment; thus, his blanket ban on excitement is a necessary heuristic for an era without controlled activation protocols.

Finally, we look at the collective dynamics. Herding players together creates a coupled oscillator network. <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Roper’s philosophy acts as a retrocausal anchor point, heavily influenced by the late 19th-century "nervous exhaustion" (neurasthenia) theories of George Beard. In 1920, the scientific consensus viewed nerve energy as a finite, non-renewable battery. Therefore, any "excitement" was literally seen as leaking voltage from the nervous system before the main event. 

Historically, this lineage bypassed the early 20th-century advent of mass psychology (Le Bon), which suggested crowd environments amplify emotional contagion. Roper intuitively applied Le Bon's inverse: avoid the crowd to avoid the contagion. 

However, this created a multi-week trap for future coaching paradigms. The "Quiet Room" doctrine became dogma, persisting well into the mid-20th century. It wasn't until the widespread adoption of Hans Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome (1936) and later the Yerkes-Dodson law refinements that coaches realized some stress (eustress) is required for peak enzymatic and neurological firing rates. Roper's strict isolation, while effective for preventing catastrophic collapse (distress), arguably trapped teams in a local minimum of sub-optimal arousal, a blind spot only corrected decades later by modern sports psychologists introducing "activation playlists" and high-energy video reviews on game days.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
