<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source operators are detection, non-maximum suppression, online tracking, Gaussian-process interpolation/smoothing, appearance-free linking, appearance-based merging, and metric aggregation. Units are as follows: confidence is a probability in \([0,1]\), dimensionless; AP and HOTA are dimensionless; image-plane distances are in pixels; temporal windows are in frames; where seconds are needed, one frame equals \(1/\mathrm{FPS}\) seconds. The paper reports image-plane units, not calibrated SI metres, so no metric physical distance can be recovered from the source without camera calibration.

1. Detection threshold. The ball detector is a fine-tuned YOLOv8l “with a low confidence score of 0.05” [PAGE 2]. Formally,
\[
D_t=\{\,d_{t,i}: p(d_{t,i})\geq 0.05\,\},
\]
where \(p(d_{t,i})\) is dimensionless detection confidence. Assumption: high recall is preferred over immediate precision because later temporal filtering removes false positives. Breakdown regime: if the ball is fully occluded or visually merged with “the crowd or white shoes” [PAGE 3], the true detection may be absent; no downstream filter can recover a detection that never exists. If the threshold is raised, recall falls; if lowered, candidate cardinality grows and the smoothing/filter stage bears more load.

2. Non-maximum suppression. The player detector uses “a Non-Maxima Suppression (NMS) IoU threshold of 0.45” [PAGE 2]. With boxes \(B_a,B_b\),
\[
\mathrm{IoU}(B_a,B_b)=\frac{|B_a\cap B_b|}{|B_a\cup B_b|},
\]
dimensionless. Breakdown regime: when players overlap heavily, high-IoU suppression can remove the correct neighbouring player; the paper notes “some errors occur when several players overlap with each other” [PAGE 3]. This is a biomechanical crowding failure, not a tracker-only failure.

3. Average precision. The detector metric is
\[
\mathrm{AP}_{\mathrm{IoU}=\alpha}=\int_0^1 p_\alpha(r)\,dr,
\]
where \(p_\alpha(r)\) is precision as a function of recall at IoU threshold \(\alpha\), both dimensionless. Table 1 reports AP@0.5 from 0.954 on COCO2017 to 0.990 on SoccerNet, and AP@0.5:0.95 from 0.812 to 0.923 [PAGE 3]. The prose instead says “AP@0.5 0.954 to 0.994” [PAGE 3], a source-internal discrepancy. Breakdown/singularity: AP is undefined if the evaluation set has zero positive instances; AP also becomes fragile under label noise, and the paper explicitly used “a COCO2017 pre-trained detector … to select only frames with an IoU>0.7” because of “errors in the ground-truth labels” [PAGE 2]. So the metric is conditioned on a cleaned label subset, not the raw label distribution.

4. Cubic temporal smoothing and 100-pixel gate. The ball centre is smoothed by “3rd order polynomial with a temporal window size of 51 frames” [PAGE 2]. Let \(c_t=(x_t,y_t)\) be a detection centre in pixels and let
\[
\hat c_t=\beta_0+\beta_1 t+\beta_2 t^2+\beta_3 t^3
\]
be the fitted centre over \(t\in[k-25,k+25]\), obtained by
\[
\min_{\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3}\sum_{t=k-25}^{k+25}\|c_t-\hat c_t\|_2^2.
\]
Units are pixels; \(t\) is in frames. The retained detection is
\[
d_t^\star=\arg\min_i\|c_{t,i}-\hat c_t\|_2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\|c_{t,i}-\hat c_t\|_2\leq 100\ \mathrm{px}.
\]
This matches “retained only the detection closest to this smoothed trajectory if its distance was below 100 pixels” [PAGE 2]. Breakdown regimes: the normal equations \(X^\top X\) are singular if the time samples are rank-deficient; more practically, the cubic can lag abrupt acceleration, and the 100-pixel gate can reject the true ball when camera scale or motion exceeds the gate. The threshold is not stated as scale-normalized, so it is resolution- and zoom-dependent. Missing detections are then “linearly interpolated” [PAGE 2]:
\[
c_t=(1-\lambda)c_a+\lambda c_b,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{t-t_a}{t_b-t_a},
\]
with pixels and frames. Breakdown: long gaps and nonlinear ball motion produce a straight-line trajectory that can be farther than 100 px from the true path.

5. Appearance similarity and track merging. The appearance model is OSNet with cosine similarity [PAGE 3]. For embeddings \(e_i,e_j\),
\[
s_{ij}=\frac{e_i^\top e_j}{\|e_i\|_2\|e_j\|_2},
\]
dimensionless. A formal merge predicate consistent with the text is
\[
M_{ij}=\mathbf{1}[\Delta t_{ij}\leq T_{\max}]\;
\mathbf{1}[F_{ij}\geq f_{\min}]\;
\mathbf{1}[s_{ij}\geq\theta_{\mathrm{app}}],
\]
where \(F_{ij}\) is the fraction of track duration with high similarity, \(\Delta t_{ij}\) is the gap in frames, and \(\theta_{\mathrm{app}}\) is an appearance threshold. The source states the qualitative condition: “if a new track is generated within a short time span, an attempt is made to merge it with the previously terminated track if their appearance similarity remains consistently high throughout most of the track duration” [PAGE 2]. The exact \(T_{\max}\), \(f_{\min}\), and \(\theta_{\mathrm{app}}\) are not reported. Singularity: if \(\|e_i\|_2=0\) or \(\|e_j\|_2=0\), cosine similarity is undefined. Breakdown: identical uniforms, occlusion, and lighting changes make high similarity non-identifying; the paper notes “nearly indistinguishable appearances of certain players on the same team” [PAGE 3].

6. GSI and AFLink. GSI is described as “interpolating and smoothing using a Gaussian process (GSI)” [PAGE 1]. A standard formalization is
\[
m(t)=k(t)^\top(K+\sigma^2 I)^{-1}y,
\]
where \(m(t)\) is in pixels, \(K\) has units pixels\(^2\), and \(\sigma^2\) is a pixel\(^2\) nugget. Singular/breakdown regime: \(K+\sigma^2 I\) becomes ill-conditioned as \(\sigma^2\to 0\) with duplicate or near-duplicate time points; the smoother also assumes local temporal continuity, which the ball violates under “considerable acceleration” [PAGE 1]. AFLink is appearance-free linking; the source does not give its internal equation, so any exact AFLink threshold claim would be unsupported by the four pages.

7. HOTA. The reported metric is HOTA [PAGE 4], with the standard decomposition
\[
\mathrm{HOTA}=\int_0^1\sqrt{\mathrm{DetA}(\alpha)\,\mathrm{AssA}(\alpha)}\,d\alpha,
\]
dimensionless. The final tracker reports “HOTA of 66.27, DetA of 70.32 and AssA of 62.62” [PAGE 3]. Breakdown/singularity: if true positives are zero, AssA is undefined; if the evaluation set contains no target instances, the metric is undefined. The paper’s GT-box result is the decisive bottleneck test: “Using GT boxes instead of detections greatly improves the HOTA metric by a notable margin of 21.85 points” [PAGE 3]. That is consistent with detector precision and recall being upstream constraints on both DetA and AssA.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when occlusion or crowding triggers a return to raw confidence thresholding: lower the ball threshold to 0.05, trust the most confident detection, and hope association fixes it. The paper resists that habit by adding a cubic smoother and a 100-pixel gate [PAGE 2], but the regression remains visible in the ball branch because “proprietary filtering” [PAGE 1] is not fully specified. The counter-trap is the opposite error: assuming post-processing creates information. GSI and AFLink can interpolate and merge, but they cannot recover a player or ball that is absent from the detection set; the GT-box jump to 87.85 [PAGE 3] proves the residual gap is largely detector-side. The clock is the online/offline boundary: live tracking cannot use future frames, while post-processing can. Any deployment that removes the delay also removes the retrocausal advantage, so the 66.27 HOTA is not a pure live-tracking number without a latency audit. The recursive model is therefore: detector stress produces ID fragmentation; the counter-trap is to over-merge; the clock decides whether future-frame repair is legitimate. The safe policy is to report online HOTA, post-processed HOTA, and GT-box ceiling separately.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Physical veto: the ball is small, frequently occluded, and can blend with uniforms or crowd [PAGE 1, PAGE 3]. Biomechanical veto: player overlap causes detector errors [PAGE 3], and the ball’s “considerable acceleration” [PAGE 1] violates constant-velocity and cubic-smoothness assumptions. If two players occupy the same image region, NMS at IoU 0.45 [PAGE 2] can suppress one; if the ball accelerates between frames, interpolation can cut the corner. Weather veto: the four pages provide no rain, wind, lighting, or pitch-state variable, so a weather-specific veto cannot be proved from this source. The correct adjudication is “not evidenced,” not “no effect.” The strongest cross-layer proof remains the detector ceiling: GT boxes yield 87.85 HOTA versus 66.00 for DeepOC-SORT++ with YOLOv8 [PAGE 3], so physical and biomechanical detector failures are upstream of tracker association. Post-processing can only repair bounded temporal gaps; it cannot veto a missing detection into existence.
