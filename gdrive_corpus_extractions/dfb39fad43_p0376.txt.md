The Taylor quotation—“moves men’s wills” [PAGE 863]—locates the psychological mechanism in anticipated utility rather than experienced risk.

Historically, Knight (1921) separates insurable, measurable risk from unmeasurable uncertainty, arguing that true profit emerges from the latter. Keynes (1936) later formalizes the same temporal structure through long-term expectation, convention, and liquidity preference, where the beauty-contest logic makes t1 outcomes depend on t0 beliefs about other t0 beliefs. Von Neumann and Morgenstern (1944) axiomatize expected utility under risk; Savage (1954) attempts to reduce uncertainty to subjective expected utility via personal probability; Ellsberg (1961) demonstrates ambiguity aversion, showing that decision-makers distinguish risky from uncertain urns; Gilboa and Schmeidler (1989) generalize with maxmin expected utility over non-singleton priors, directly formalizing the breakdown regime noted above.

Multi-week traps arise when a reader applies this page mechanically over rolling horizons. Trap A: an entrepreneur earns positive \(\Pi\) in week 1 due to an unanticipated \(\omega\), infers skill, expands \(\mathbf{x}\) in week 2, and locks in higher factor prices \(\mathbf{w}\) just as competition adjusts [PAGE 863]; when \(\omega\) reverts, losses compound. Trap B: the phrase “estimated probability” [PAGE 863] is misread as license to assign numerical probabilities to Knightian uncertainty, producing false precision in week-ahead forecasts. Trap C: because “society has to pay a higher price” [PAGE 863] is stated in the aggregate, a week-by-week analyst may wrongly attribute any single firm’s weekly margin to risk-bearing rather than to class-level forecast error.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Each entrepreneur operates with a first-order model of demand and cost and a second-order model of rivals’ models. Under Knightian uncertainty there is no common prior, so higher-order beliefs diverge rather than converge. Crisis habit regression appears when stress reactivates a heuristic: “last period’s fixed-factor contract worked, so expand \(\mathbf{x}\) again.” This is a regression to habit under pressure, not a recalculation of \(\mathbb{E}_{P}[\Pi]\).

The counter-trap is to recognize that rivals are running the same recursion. If every entrepreneur expands fixed-factor commitments because of a recent lucky \(\Pi\), competition bids up \(\mathbf{w}\) until the entire supply is taken out of the market at the established price [PAGE 863]; margins compress and the positive-profit condition fails. The counter-trap is therefore optionality: shorten contract duration, avoid irreversible \(\mathbf{x}\), and preserve liquidity against the t0/t1 clock gap \(\Delta t = t_1 - t_0\).

The clock itself is recursive. Profit realized at \(t_1\) becomes the capital base for the next \(t_0'\). Overconfidence from one cycle raises \(\mathbf{x}\) in the next cycle, lengthening exposure to non-stationary \(\omega\). Eventually a shock pushes \(\omega\) outside the support of the adopted \(P\), making \(\mathbb{E}_{P}[\Pi]\) undefined and triggering a realized loss that cascades into the next round of contracts. Crisis habit regression thus bets the recursive clock on stale expectations; the counter-trap bets on model uncertainty and keeps the clock short.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: productive services are embodied in labor, capital, and energy. Contracting \(\mathbf{x}\) at \(t_0\) does not violate physical law, but realizing \(f(\mathbf{x},\omega)\) does. A hard veto occurs when physical productivity collapses below the break-even threshold
\[
f(\mathbf{x},\omega) < \frac{\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}}{p(\omega)},
\]
which forces \(\Pi(\omega) < 0\) regardless of the ex ante prospect that “moves men’s wills” [PAGE 863]. Example: an agricultural entrepreneur fixes labor and seed; a weather-induced water shortage reduces \(f\) below break-even. This is a physical constraint, not a wagering outcome.

Biomechanical layer: human labor-hours \(x_{\ell}\) are bounded by circadian and fatigue sustainability, say \(x_{\ell} \le x_{\max}\) where \(x_{\max}\) is on the order of 12–16 sustainable hours per day. A contract requiring \(x_{\ell} > x_{\max}\) is infeasible; cost cannot be reduced by substituting more labor, and revenue cannot be expanded by more effort. The veto is physiological and binds ex post.

Weather layer: for transport and agricultural entrepreneurs, \(\omega\) includes temperature, precipitation, and storm events. The breakdown regime is non-stationarity: if realized \(\omega \notin \mathrm{supp}(P)\), or equivalently \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P^* \| P) = \infty\), then the ex ante expected profit \(\mathbb{E}_{P}[\Pi]\) is meaningless and the positive-profit condition cannot be assessed. Weather therefore vetoes the Knightian decision model whenever the realized regime lies outside the historical support used to form \(P\).

Adjudication: these vetoes are ex post physical limits. They falsify the claim that profit compensates for bearing risk; rather, profit is a residual from forecast error that persists until competition adjusts factor prices [PAGE 863]. No amount of “risk tolerance” overrides a biomechanical hour ceiling or a weather-driven production floor.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Contingent factor contracts. Replace fixed \(\mathbf{w}\) with revenue-indexed factor payments \(\mathbf{w}(\omega) = \alpha R(\omega)\), \(0<\alpha<1\). Then \(C(\omega) = \alpha R(\omega)\) and \(\Pi(\omega) = (1-\alpha)R(\omega) - F\) for fixed overhead \(F\). Verifiable veto condition: contract contingency index \(\kappa = 1\) (payments legally tied to realized revenue). Prediction: pure profit is arbitraged to zero ex ante; factor owners capture the residual, and entrepreneurs earn no persistent \(\Pi > 0\).

Scenario 2 — Perfect public forecast. A signal \(s\) reveals \(\omega\) before \(t_0\), so \(I(\omega;s) = H(\omega)\) [bits] and uncertainty collapses. Verifiable veto condition: mutual information equals entropy. Prediction: entrepreneurs bid factor prices up to expected revenue; under competition the price adjusts until the entire supply is taken out at the established price [PAGE 863], yielding \(\mathbb{E}_{P}[\Pi] = 0\) ex ante and \(\Pi = 0\) ex post up to transaction costs.

Scenario 3 — Aggregate forecast-bias correction. Suppose all entrepreneurs share a biased model \(\hat{P}\) with \(\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[C] < C^*\). A regulator imposes ex post cost-truthing audits that reveal \(C^*\). Verifiable veto condition: forecast bias \(b = \mathbb{E}[\hat{C} - C^*] > 0\) is measured and publicly enforced. Prediction: once \(b\) is removed, the condition \(\mathbb{E}_{P}[p f] > \mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\) fails; the entrepreneurial group as a whole realizes zero profit or losses until the model is corrected, and the higher social price [PAGE 863] falls toward the risk-free baseline.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
