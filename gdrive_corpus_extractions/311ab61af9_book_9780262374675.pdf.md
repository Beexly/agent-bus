<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

**Linnaean trunk.** Helmholtz (1860s) splits sensation from percept and frames perception as *unconscious inference* — statistical hypothesis about causes [PAGE 80]. This is the retrodictive seed: every later "prediction" is Helmholtzian inference given a generative model. Oliver's *efficient coding* (1952) then supplies the compression criterion, and Attneave (1954) makes it psychological: "much of the information received by any higher organism is redundant... if we know at a given moment the states of a limited number of receptors... we can make better-than-chance inferences with respect to the prior and subsequent states of these receptors" [PAGE 93]. Attneave's dot-placement experiment [Fig. 5.1, PAGE 94] is the first empirical demonstration that percepts encode *deviations from expectation*.

**Branch A — the cybernetic cerebellum.** Marr (1969) and Albus (1971) model the cerebellar cortex as a supervised learning machine; Fujita (1982) recasts it as an adaptive filter/forward model; Porrill and Dean (2016) preserve interpretive latitude [PAGE 41]. Bell et al. (1997) extend the comparator reading to electrosensory fish, showing anti-Hebbian plasticity at the parallel-fiber→Purkinje synapse. The lineage is *retrocausal*: the modern "prediction error" vocabulary is retro-fitted onto circuitry described in 1969 as pattern classification.

**Branch B — energy networks.** Hopfield (1982) imports spin-glass energy [PAGE 62]; Ackley, Hinton and Sejnowski (1985) add the Boltzmann distribution and contrastive Hebbian learning [PAGE 65]; Smolensky's harmony theory (1986) and Carpenter–Grossberg ART (2003) supply resonance semantics; Hinton & Salakhutdinov (2006) convert the whole apparatus into the RBM and thereby ignite deep learning [PAGE 70]. The genealogical irony: the *least* biologically realistic descendant (long-distance backprop) inherited the field, while the *more* plausible ancestor (local Hebbian CD) was shelved.

**Branch C — predictive coding proper.** Srinivasan, Laughlin and Dubs (1982) coin the term for retinal horizontal-cell compression [PAGE 94]; Mumford (1992) supplies the cortical-column functional interpretation; Rao and Ballard (1999) formalize the hierarchical model [PAGE 99]; Friston (2005, 2010) generalizes to the free energy principle [PAGE 86]; Clark (2016) popularizes. Downing's own lineage runs through Downing (2007a, 2009) on procedural vs. declarative prediction.

**Multi-week traps (accumulated field pathologies, not single errors).**

1. *The locality trap.* Backprop's biological implausibility was expected to be fatal; instead it was repeatedly "rescued" by increasingly elaborate schemes (Lillicrap et al. 2020, cited [PAGE 115]). Downing's own verdict: "only the algorithm's biological plausibility remains suspect" [PAGE 152]. A decade of effort spent defending an architecture the pioneer himself suggested abandoning.

2. *The supervised-label trap.* Hinton's "We clearly don't need all the labeled data" [PAGE 150] is a retroactive indictment of the pretraining→fine-tuning pipeline that made him famous.

3. *The shallow-comprehension trap.* Classifiers that key on backgrounds rather than objects — "what it really learns is that the background for the inanimate objects is often a table or wall with simple patterning" [PAGE 150]. This trap recurs because benchmark accuracy is a *proxy* whose gradient is satisfiable without the target competence.

4. *The neuromodulator-timing trap.* Dopamine must update synapses encoding $V(s_t)$ but not $V(s_{t+1})$, requiring ~100 ms chemical latency discrimination [PAGE 57] — a narrow window that makes any simplified RL-on-basal-ganglia model silently mis-specified.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Three regimes of a predictive agent under surprise.**

**(1) Crisis habit regression.** When the generative model fails, the default is to *lower the gain on reality* rather than revise the model. Downing states the mechanism explicitly: a high error "forces layer 2 to either modify its intentions or reduce its attention to $e_1$, by lowering $g_1$" [PAGE 136]. Clark's definition of attention confirms gain as the control knob: "an organism increases the gain... on those prediction error units estimated to provide the most reliable sensory information" [PAGE 139]. Habit regression is therefore not a bug but a *computable policy*: attenuate the precision on disconfirming evidence. In the FEP formalism this is a legal move on the divergence term of Eq. 4.76, and it produces a stable but epistemically closed agent.

**(2) The counter-trap: active inference as hypothesis confirmation.** Downing is unusually blunt: "good actions are those that confirm the system's favored hypotheses" [PAGE 89], contrasted with the entropy-reduction view that would constitute fair hypothesis testing. The recursive theory of mind here is dark: an agent's model of *other agents* is itself a generative model, and the same accuracy term of Eq. 4.81 rewards acting to make others' behavior conform to the prediction. The counter-trap is that confirmation reduces free energy in the short term while raising model complexity invisibly — complexity is the KL divergence from the prior, which is *also* being minimized, so the agent's ontology calcifies. Downing's phrase "the prior to these (minor or major) breakdowns, the agent can let its own intentions and beliefs run the show, under the practical illusion that it has the full grip" [PAGE 91] is the phenomenology of the counter-trap.

**(3) The clock: oscillatory gating decides which regime runs.** The arbitration between habit, revision, and confirmation is not deliberative — it is *temporal*. Downing's beta–gamma account is the arbitration mechanism: "neurons in layers 2/3 have resonant frequencies in the gamma range, with dominant values around 50–70 Hz, whereas layers 5/6 tend to oscillate more slowly, in the beta range: 20–40 Hz" [PAGE 141]. The slower receiver integrates several fast sender cycles per beat, producing abstraction; the fast sender replays sequences inside the STDP window (0–50 ms), producing chunking [PAGE 142–143]. Phase precession in CA1 place fields makes the clock predictive: place fields skew backward in time so that "$p_d$ will begin firing well before the animal reaches location d" [PAGE 144]. The clock thus *converts* temporal disparity into associative structure — it is the physical substrate of "learning what comes next."

**Recursive loop closure.** The three regimes form a cycle: clock (oscillation) → gain setting (attention) → regime choice (habit vs. revision vs. confirmation) → new predictions → new errors → new clock phase. Buzsaki's "callup" — receiver sends slow wave, sender replies fast [PAGE 141–142] — makes the inner/higher region the *initiator*, which is Downing's "inside-out" reading and Llinas's encephalized motricity. The recursive theory of mind is therefore: *the agent's model of the world is beaten against the world at a frequency the agent itself chooses, and the choice of frequency is the choice of epistemology.*

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Definition.** A veto is a lower-layer constraint that renders a higher-layer predictive strategy *unexecutable* — not merely suboptimal. I adjudicate four.

**Veto 1 — Biomechanical latency floor.** "The price paid for this complexification is latency, which rises by tens to hundreds of milliseconds per neural layer" [PAGE 24]. With $L$ layers, minimal loop delay $\tau_a \gtrsim L \times 50$ ms. Any environment with $\tau_m < \tau_a$ vetoes purely reactive control; the agent *must* predict. Proof structure: if $\tau_a > \tau_m$, the sensed state has already decorrelated by actuation time, so the control law $u_t = \Phi(G - S_t)$ acts on a stale argument. Downing's figure 1.2 labels this exactly: "Read X / Respond to X / But environment is in Y !!" [PAGE 23]. **Consequence:** prediction is not an optimization; it is the only feasible policy above a latency threshold. This veto is *hard*.

**Veto 2 — Metabolic/energetic ceiling.** Stone's conclusion, quoted with approval: brains "maximize the amount of information gained for each Joule of energy expended" [PAGE 129]. Firing rates are bounded (250–1000 Hz), and inhibition is expensive (GABA decay 10–25 ms forces prolonged suppression). This vetoes unbounded free-energy minimization: exact $Z$ is computationally and metabolically impossible, forcing sampling approximations (CD-k, Gibbs) and thereby forcing *bias*. **Proof:** Eq. 4.24 with finite $S'$ is a biased estimator; the metabolic veto is the physical reason the bias exists. A predictive system that insists on exact inference is metabolically vetoed; one that samples is statistically vetoed. There is no third option.

**Veto 3 — Neurochemical specificity.** Dopamine must distinguish synapses active ~100 ms ago from those active now, to update $V(s_t)$ and not $V(s_{t+1})$ [PAGE 57]. This vetoes any basal-ganglia model lacking explicit delayed-inhibitory chemistry: the TD error $\delta_t = V(s_{t+1}) + r - V(s_t)$ [Eq. 3.2] is realizable *only* because fast excitatory and slow inhibitory pathways to SNc are physically separable. Remove the latency asymmetry and the TD error collapses into a reward detector — a different, weaker computation.

**Veto 4 — Weather / thermoregulatory anticipatory regulation.** Sterling and Laughlin: "the core task of all brains: It is to regulate the organism's internal milieu—by responding to needs and, better still, by anticipating needs and preparing to satisfy them before they arise" [PAGE 24]. Environmental thermal load changes on timescales of minutes-to-hours; homeostatic feedback on timescales of minutes. The veto: pure negative-feedback thermoregulation is *always* behind the perturbation. Anticipatory regulation — pre-emptive vasodilation, behavioral shade-seeking before thirst — is mandated by the timescale mismatch, exactly parallel to the sensorimotor case. This extends the veto from the nervous system outward to the whole organism-environment coupling: the brain is a controller whose plant is the body and whose disturbance is weather.

**Adjudication summary.** Vetoes 1 and 2 are *structural* (they shape what architectures are possible); vetoes 3 and 4 are *evidential* (they constrain which models are faithful). No higher-layer predictive claim can override a veto — the veto operates at the implementational tier of Marr's hierarchy [PAGE 27], and Downing's whole argument is that the algorithmic tier must be downgraded to satisfy it (local gradients instead of long gradients; sampling instead of exact inference; relaxation instead of backprop).

**Scenario 1 — Ablate the Schaffer-collateral → interneuron pathway in CA1.**
*Intervention:* silencing CA1 feedforward interneurons removes the inversion that makes CA3's strong excitation net-inhibitory at the soma. The model predicts CA1 loses its comparator property [PAGE 49–50: "the net effect may ultimately be inhibitory due to the ensuing interneuron activity. This has led several researchers to propose CA1 as the comparator region"].
*Verifiable prediction:* (a) Dopamine burst magnitude in VTA should decouple from mismatch magnitude — novel and familiar contexts should elicit statistically indistinguishable dopaminergic responses; (b) hippocampal-dependent one-trial novelty detection should fail while place-field remapping remains intact; (c) prediction error decoding accuracy from CA1 population activity should drop to chance. **Falsifier:** if DA novelty responses persist unchanged, the comparator account is wrong at this locus.

**Scenario 2 — Replace CD-k with CD-1 in an RBM stack and measure the fixed point.**
*Intervention:* Eq. 4.25 [PAGE 72] with $k=1$ versus $k\to\infty$.
*Verifiable prediction:* the learned weight matrix should converge to a distribution whose KL divergence from the data distribution plateaus at a strictly positive value $\epsilon(k)$ monotonically decreasing in $k$; on RBM stacks of increasing depth, $\epsilon$ should grow super-linearly with depth because error compounds per layer. Reconstruction error should show a characteristic floor that extra training epochs cannot breach. **Falsifier:** if CD-1 matches CD-$\infty$ asymptotically on deep stacks, the bias argument is refuted.

**Scenario 3 — Destroy beta–gamma nesting between cortical columns and assay place-field asymmetry.**
*Intervention:* pharmacologically or optogenetically flatten the slow (beta/theta) envelope while sparing gamma, so sequence replay exceeds the 0–50 ms STDP window.
*Verifiable prediction:* per the mechanism at [PAGE 142–145], replay compression fails; therefore (a) CA1 place fields remain symmetric (no backward extension), (b) no phase precession develops across repeated traversals, (c) downstream hippocampal sequence prediction — the "predictive firing" of successor place cells — is absent, even as basic place-field location coding is preserved. The prediction is *dissociable*: location coding $\neq$ predictive skew. **Falsifier:** if asymmetry develops normally without beta nesting, then STDP-window compression is not the operative mechanism and Downing's clock hypothesis at this site is falsified.

**Meta-veto.** All three scenarios share a structure: they test whether a *local* mechanism is necessary for a *global* predictive competence. This is exactly the emergence claim at [PAGE 28] — "There exists no brainwide ledger" — and each scenario is a check that the ledger is genuinely absent rather than merely hidden.
