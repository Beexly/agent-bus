The update uses entanglement matrices \(C_x\) and \(C_y\) with angle \(\theta\) and streaming operators \(S^{01}_{\pm x},S^{23}_{\pm x},S^{01}_{\pm y},S^{23}_{\pm y}\) [PAGE 51-52]. The source says: “There are two essential steps to a QLA; the first step streams the qubits from one lattice site to a neighboring site, and the second step ‘entangles’ two qubits at a given lattice site.” [PAGE 51]. The time advance is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}q_0\\q_1\\q_2\\q_3\end{bmatrix}_{t+\delta t}
=\tilde U_yU_y\tilde U_xU_x
\begin{bmatrix}q_0\\q_1\\q_2\\q_3\end{bmatrix}_{t}
\]
[PAGE 52]. The continuum-limit condition is: “If \(\varepsilon\ll1\) is an ordering parameter, we approach the continuum limit by setting the entanglement angle \(\theta\sim\varepsilon\) and \(dx,dy\sim\varepsilon\).” [PAGE 52]. The recovered equation is second-order: “which, to order \(\varepsilon^2\) is the same as (0.204).” [PAGE 53]. Thus the QLA is not exact; its breakdown regime is finite \(\varepsilon\), nonzero lattice spacing, and non-negligible \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) error.

Quantum information operators include the qubit probability \(P(|0\rangle)=|a_1|^2\) and \(P(|1\rangle)=|b_1|^2\) with normalization \(|a_1|^2+|b_1|^2=1\) [PAGE 40]. These are dimensionless probabilities. The Bell states are
\[
|\Psi\rangle_{B\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle),\qquad
|\Phi\rangle_{B\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle),
\]
and the source notes “Bell states are entangled, but not all entangled states are Bell states.” [PAGE 41]. The parameter-space count is information-geometric: reducible \(n\)-qubit tensor states have \(3n\) real parameters, while irreducible entangled states have \(2^{n+1}-1\) real parameters [PAGE 42-43]. The source states “the state space of entangled, or irreducible, states grows exponentially while that of the reducible states grows linearly with the number of qubits.” [PAGE 43].

The cold magnetized-plasma permittivity is
\[
\epsilon_{ij}=\epsilon_0
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\chi_{11} & -i\chi_{12} & 0\\
i\chi_{12} & 1+\chi_{11} & 0\\
0 & 0 & 1+\chi_{33}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with
\[
\chi_{11}=-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{ce}^2}-\sum_i\frac{\omega_{pi}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{ci}^2},\quad
\chi_{12}=-\frac{\omega_{ce}}{\omega}\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{ce}^2}
+\sum_i\frac{\omega_{ci}}{\omega}\frac{\omega_{pi}^2}{\omega^2-\omega_{ci}^2},\quad
\chi_{33}=-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2}-\sum_i\frac{\omega_{pi}^2}{\omega^2},

for “a thermally cold plasma” [PAGE 54]. Singularity and breakdown regimes are explicit: \(\omega^2=\omega_{ce}^2\) in electron terms, \(\omega^2=\omega_{ci}^2\) in ion terms, and \(\omega=0\) in \(\chi_{33}\). <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage runs backward from the QLA to Maxwell, Minkowski, Lorentz, Dirac-like first-order matrix forms, and the RSW vector tradition. The source itself anchors Section 0.7 in a specific matrix representation: “The material in Section 0.7 is based on, Sameen Ahmed Khan, ‘An exact matrix representation of Maxwell’s equations,’” [PAGE 60]. It also lists RSW references by Bialynicki-Birula and Bialynicka-Birula, Kiessling and Tahvildar-Zadeh, and Sebens, and QLA references by Vahala et al. [PAGE 60-61]. The historical chain is thus: macroscopic Maxwell equations [PAGE 44] → four-vector and Minkowski metric formulation [PAGE 45-46] → Lorentz-covariant field tensor [PAGE 48] → RSW first-order complex field combination [PAGE 49] → gamma-matrix Schrödinger-like form [PAGE 49-50] → unitary streaming-entanglement QLA [PAGE 51-52]. The genealogy matters because each backward step introduces a condition that later steps can silently forget. A multi-week trap is to treat RSW \(F^+\) and \(F^-\) as conjugates; the source explicitly forbids that unless the fields are real [PAGE 49]. Another trap is to treat the homogeneous Schrödinger form as source-inclusive; the source derives it only after ignoring external charge and current densities [PAGE 50]. Another is to assume discrete Gauss laws are automatic because the continuum equations imply them; the source says the opposite for computation [PAGE 44]. Another is to treat the cold plasma permittivity as a universal dielectric response; the source restricts it to a thermally cold plasma and shows cyclotron and zero-frequency denominators [PAGE 54]. Another is to confuse reducible tensor-product qubit states with entangled states; the source says “there are state vectors in \(\mathcal H_4\) which cannot be factored as a tensor product of states in \(\mathcal H_2\)” [PAGE 41], yet not every entangled state is Bell [PAGE 41]. A final long-horizon trap is to use the Minkowski metric as if it were a positive-definite Hilbert inner product; the source calls it pseudo and notes loss of a unique null vector and triangle inequality [PAGE 46]. These traps are multi-week because they survive superficial dimension checks and only appear when one tries to impose unitarity, source terms, boundary constraints, or resonance physics.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive modeling problem is: an observer must track what kind of state the system is in, what kind of evolution it undergoes, and whether a measurement has collapsed it. The crisis-habit regression is classical intuition: treating a qubit superposition as a physically observable classical mixture. The source blocks this: “the superposition (0.147) is not a physically observable state.” [PAGE 39]. A second regression is to assume every two-qubit state is factorizable; the source blocks this with the \(\mathcal H_4\) non-factorization statement [PAGE 41]. The counter-trap is overcorrection: after learning entanglement, the modeler treats all entangled states as maximally entangled Bell states. The source blocks this: “Bell states are entangled, but not all entangled states are Bell states.” [PAGE 41]. The clock layer is the unitary time evolution. For a closed quantum system, Postulate 4 gives
\[
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle=H(t)|\psi(t)\rangle,
\]
and for time-independent \(H\),
\[
U(t,t_0)=\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}H(t-t_0)\right)
\]
[PAGE 38]. The source explicitly says the evolution equation “is valid for a closed quantum system” [PAGE 38]. The QLA clock is discrete:
\[
|\psi\rangle_{t+\delta t}=\tilde U_yU_y\tilde U_xU_x|\psi\rangle_t
\]
[PAGE 52]. Measurement is a different recursive layer: Postulate 3 says a measurement yielding \(\lambda_i\) collapses the state to \(|e_i\rangle\), with probability \(P(\lambda_i)=|c_i|^2\) [PAGE 37]. Thus the recursive theory of mind needs three separate registers: unitary clock register, measurement-collapse register, and model-closure register. The closure register is where plasma physics creates a crisis: the source notes that quantum mechanics deals with closed systems, while waves in a plasma exchange energy with electrons and ions, making self-consistent open-system modeling difficult [PAGE 55]. The counter-trap is to use the source-free Schrödinger form as if it were a closed-system model of a driven plasma; the source derives that form only after ignoring external sources [PAGE 50]. The correct recursive check is: if the target is a source-free linear dielectric, the unitary clock and QLA are admissible; if sources, nonlinearity, or wave-particle energy exchange are present, the model must be enlarged or vetoed. This is a recursive observer problem because the modeler’s own abstraction level determines which operators are legal, and the system’s measurement or open-system status determines whether the clock register is unitary or collapse-like.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The physical layer vetoes are source-grounded. Unit veto: the source uses SI/MKS [PAGE 8], so any formula mixing Gaussian and SI electromagnetic units is inadmissible without conversion. Linearity veto: the source assumes “the polarization density … is a linear function of the electric field amplitude” [PAGE 9]; nonlinear laser-plasma or intensity-dependent permittivity belongs to the open problem stated at [PAGE 55]. Source veto: the Schrödinger-like form is derived only “If we ignore the external charge and current densities” [PAGE 50]; a source-inclusive claim cannot use the homogeneous equation. Discrete-limit veto: the source says discrete Gauss-law preservation is “not necessarily valid” and that one must “solve the four Maxwell equations simultaneously” [PAGE 44]. Continuum-limit veto: QLA recovery requires \(\theta\sim\varepsilon\) and \(dx,dy\sim\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\ll1\) [PAGE 52], and only to \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) [PAGE 53]. Resonance veto: the cold plasma tensor has denominators \(\omega^2-\omega_{ce}^2\), \(\omega^2-\omega_{ci}^2\), and \(\omega^2\) [PAGE 54], so claims at cyclotron or zero frequency are singular or undefined unless a different physics model is supplied. Closure veto: the Schrödinger evolution is for a closed quantum system [PAGE 38], while plasma waves exchange energy with particles [PAGE 55]. Minkowski veto: the metric inner product is pseudo, not positive-definite [PAGE 46], so Hilbert-space positivity claims cannot be transferred to spacetime intervals. Biomechanical layer: no biomechanical variables, forces, or models appear in the 61-page source; a biomechanical veto would be unsupported and is adjudicated as not applicable. Weather layer: no meteorological variables or weather models appear in the source; a weather-based veto is likewise inadmissible. The cross-layer adjudication is therefore: physical and mathematical vetoes are admitted only when tied to explicit source equations and page markers; biomechanical and weather vetoes are rejected as category errors because the corpus supplies no operators for them.
