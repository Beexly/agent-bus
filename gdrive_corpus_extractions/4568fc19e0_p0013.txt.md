<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In the absence of uncertainty (Chapter V), agents operate at Level 0 or Level 1 of the hierarchy: they react to current prices or predict based on stable statistical regularities. There is no need for deep strategic recursion because the "game" is against nature, not other strategic minds adapting in real-time.
However, under uncertainty (Part Three), the environment is dominated by other agents facing the same unknowable future. This triggers a Recursive Theory of Mind (RoTM):
*   **Level 0:** Naive agent assumes others behave according to historical averages (ignores uncertainty).
*   **Level 1:** Agent believes others are naive and exploits their errors.
*   **Level k:** Agent assumes others are at Level $k-1$.
*Crisis Habit Regression:* During extreme stress (crisis), cognitive load spikes. Agents cannot sustain high-$k$ reasoning. They regress to Level 0 or even reflexive behavior (panic selling/buying). This collective regression synchronizes actions, creating liquidity crunches and asset fire sales—exactly the "universal dead-lock" Knight warns about.
*Counter-Trap vs. Clock:* Sophisticated players (hedge funds, central banks) attempt to stay one step ahead (counter-trap). They model the panic of regressors. However, they are constrained by the "clock"—the speed of market feedback loops and the finite processing speed of human/algorithmic decision-making. If the clock ticks faster than the RoTM cycle (i.e., events unfold before higher-order reasoning completes), the counter-trap fails, and the system succumbs to chaotic volatility. The "gray zone" is the precise timing window where high-level reasoning collapses into herd behavior.
