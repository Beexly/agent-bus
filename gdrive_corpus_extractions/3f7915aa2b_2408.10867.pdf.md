<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The methodological lineage traces to Glickman and Stern (1998, 2017), whose state-space model for NFL scores provides the paired-comparison foundation ([PAGE 3]); this extends Bradley and Terry (1952) rank analysis of incomplete block designs ([PAGE 3]). Benz and Lopez (2023) validate paired comparison models as robust for home-advantage estimation, which the authors extend to rest advantages ([PAGE 3]). The historical antecedents for the bye-week question include Sung and Tainsky (2014) and Sung (2020), who identified a bye-week advantage unpriced by markets, though without pre/post CBA comparison ([PAGE 2]). Murray (2018) found a Monday Night Football scheduling disadvantage with no post-2011 bye benefit ([PAGE 2]). Karwan (2021) linked the 2011 CBA to decreased rest impact for home teams but used the pre-game market expectation variable as a control, which the present authors argue would conceal any rest effect already embedded in that metric ([PAGE 2]).

The cross-sport genealogical context includes NBA back-to-back research (Esteves et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021; Charest et al., 2021; Cook et al., 2022; Bowman et al., 2023), NHL rest-differential findings (Campbell, 2011; Squawk, 2015; Luszczyszyn, 2019), and soccer World Cup evidence (Scoppa, 2015; Watanabe et al., 2017) that rest differences did not significantly predict performance ([PAGES 2–3]). The central multi-week trap is the assumption that regular-season rest findings transfer to the postseason. Because the 2011 CBA altered only regular-season bye-week practice rules, postseason byes retain an extra practice week. The data show this distinction sharply: from 2002–2010, 36 postseason bye teams went 22–10 (win percentage 0.611, average point differential +5.17); from 2011–2023, 44 postseason bye teams went 34–10 (win percentage 0.773, average point differential +7.34) ([PAGE 11]). Thus the bye advantage persisted—and possibly grew—in the postseason even while the regular-season edge collapsed, demonstrating that the competitive benefit is tied to preparation time rather than rest alone. A second regulatory inflection, the 2020 CBA, limited post–Thursday Night Football activity by guaranteeing at least three days off, which further suppresses any mini-bye practice advantage going forward ([PAGE 11]).
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes how organizational behavior collapses back to prior routines when external constraints change. Before 2011, NFL coaches treated the bye week as an additional preparation block; after the CBA eliminated organized bye-week practice, staffs shifted to self-scouting—reviewing their own tendencies rather than game-planning for an opponent (Ward, 2023)—while players traveled or spent time away from the facility ([PAGE 10]). Teams regressed to a habit of using the bye for physical and mental disengagement rather than strategic development, which is why the on-field bye effect declined from +2.21 to +0.31 points per game despite unchanged total rest days.

The counter-trap is the divergence between true competitive value and market pricing. Post-2011, the pre-game market expectation variable increased its bye-week valuation from +0.39 to +0.97 points per game while the actual point differential effect fell toward zero ([PAGE 9–10]). If market participants continue to overweight the bye-week narrative, teams can exploit the counter-trap by fading public bye-week sentiment: the expected on-field edge is negligible, yet the market prices in nearly a full point of advantage. This creates an exploitable asymmetry where perceived rest value exceeds measured rest value.

The clock operates at three scales. First, the seasonal clock: the 17-game regular season ([PAGE 1]) with an autoregressive γ pulling team strength toward league average each year ([PAGE 4]), meaning rest advantages must be evaluated within a finite horizon where team strength regresses toward the mean. Second, the intra-week clock: NFL teams receive 4 to 15 days off between games ([PAGE 4]), and research indicates Thursday Night Football with only four days between games does not elevate injury or concussion risk (Baker et al., 2019; Binney, 2020; Perez et al., 2020; Teramoto et al., 2017) ([PAGE 11]), implying that physiological recovery is rapid and the binding constraint is preparation time rather than biological fatigue. Third, the regulatory clock: the 2011 CBA changed bye-week rules, and the 2020 CBA changed post–Thursday Night Football rules ([PAGE 11]), so any static theory of rest advantage is non-stationary across collective-bargaining regimes.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Injury and concussion risk. Multiple studies cited in the paper find that Thursday Night Football—four days between games, the most compressed rest interval in the NFL—does not increase all-cause injury risk (Baker et al., 2019; Binney, 2020; Perez et al., 2020) or concussion risk (Teramoto et al., 2017) ([PAGE 11]). Veto: if physiological recovery were the operative mechanism, the shortest-rest games should show elevated injury rates and performance decrements; they do not, which vetoes a pure recovery hypothesis for rest differentials.

Biomechanical layer: Recovery threshold. The paper notes that the difference between 13 and 14 days of rest is less consequential than between six and seven days ([PAGE 5]), and speculates that some threshold—perhaps two or three days—may suffice for NFL bodies to recover and practice to competitive parity ([PAGE 11]). Veto: a bye week provides six to eight days of rest ([PAGE 2]), far beyond any plausible physiological recovery window, so marginal rest beyond ~72 hours cannot physically explain a +2.21 points-per-game pre-2011 advantage. The surviving mechanism is practice/preparation, not tissue recovery.

Weather and neutral-site layer: 65 games from 2002–2023 were played at neutral sites due to international games or bad weather ([PAGE 4]); these are coded with I(HA) = 0 to remove home advantage ([PAGE 4]). Veto: weather-driven neutral sites remove venue confounding but deplete the sample of standard home/away rest comparisons; estimates therefore rely primarily on conventional stadium environments and may not generalize to extreme weather or international travel windows.

Regulatory layer: The 2011 CBA kept rest constant but removed practice. Because total days off were unchanged yet the competitive benefit collapsed ([PAGE 10]), the physical-rest hypothesis is vetoed; preparation time is the only remaining variable consistent with the pre/post discontinuity.

Epidemiological layer: 52 games (0.9 percent) were dropped, 46 of them in 2020–2021 ([PAGE 8]; [PAGE 29], Table 4). Veto: rescheduled games reflect illness, travel disruption, and abnormal calendars, not typical rest differentials; including them would bias estimates by conflating pandemic-era chaos with ordinary scheduling variation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
