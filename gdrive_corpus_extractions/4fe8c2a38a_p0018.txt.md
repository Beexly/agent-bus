<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock:

The text [PAGE 18] describes two targeting philosophies: demographic group prediction (“predicting user behavior, such as by group”) and profile-specific targeting (“user profiles specific to a user”). Under crisis conditions—specifically when rho approaches 1 and T_auc approaches the 500 ms bound [PAGE 18]—system operators exhibit crisis habit regression: they abandon computationally expensive profile-specific inference and regress to the cheaper demographic heuristic because group-level attributes (age, income, sex, occupation) require less per-request state lookup and can be served from static lookup tables rather than dynamic profile stores. This is a habit regression because it reverts to the older, coarser targeting layer described in the same paragraph of [PAGE 18].

The counter-trap is adversarial or adaptive user behavior. If users perceive that profile-specific targeting is aggressive (tracking paths across sites [PAGE 18]), they may adopt privacy-preserving countermeasures: browser tracking protection, app-transparency opt-outs, or device-level ad identifiers reset. From the ad server’s perspective, this reduces the fidelity of U, collapsing I(U; A) [bits] toward I(G; A). The system then faces a second-order problem: the regression to demographic targeting was triggered by load, but the load itself may be exacerbated by users gaming the system, creating a feedback loop where the crisis response degrades the very data needed to escape the crisis.

The clock imposes a hard bounded-reasoning constraint. The 500 ms auction deadline [PAGE 18] means that recursive theory of mind—the ad server modeling the user, and the user implicitly modeling the ad server’s targeting—cannot iterate to a fixed point within the auction window. Formally, let f: Δ(U) → Δ(A) be the ad server’s targeting policy mapping belief over users to ad distribution, and let g: Δ(A) → Δ(U) be the user’s inferred response mapping. A fixed point μ* satisfies μ* = g(f(μ*)). Computing μ* requires multiple belief-propagation rounds, each consuming network round-trip and processing time. If one round consumes τ [s], the maximum depth is d_max = floor(0.5 / τ). If d_max < d_convergence, the system operates at a non-equilibrium point, and targeting quality is bounded by the truncation error of the incomplete recursion rather than by the information-theoretic optimum. Thus the clock converts what would be a strategic equilibrium problem into a real-time approximation problem, and crisis habit regression is the lowest-depth approximation available.
