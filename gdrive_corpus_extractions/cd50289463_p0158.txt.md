Physical layer: 1915 stopwatches were mechanical (lever or balance-wheel escapement) with temperature-dependent rate error ε̇_watch (unit s/s) and positional error. Timekeeping then reverts to the referee's secondary timepiece or mutual captains' agreement. This veto is physical and cannot be overridden by rule interpretation.

Biomechanical layer: the interval between the referee's order to begin and the ball actually being put in play comprises human reaction latency τ_react (approximately 150–250 ms for auditory-to-motor response), signal-calling, offensive-line set time, and the center's snap mechanics. If the field judge initializes τ at t_sig rather than t_ball, the clock overestimates elapsed play time by λ = t_ball − t_sig ≥ τ_react + τ_snap. Verbal notification alone is insufficient under these conditions.
