When the veto binds, the formal system on [PAGE 58] reduces to a rationing problem, not an equilibrium price problem; predictions based on a single r are invalid until the physical shock dissipates and supplies can be reconstituted.

Veto Scenario 1 — Weather supply shock. Verifiable condition: an exogenous yield index for a staple resource falls by more than one standard deviation while demand \bar{\mathbf{D}} remains inelastic [PAGE 58]. Prediction: the fixed-supply competitive model fails; spot-price variance exceeds the upper bound generated by the fixed-\bar{\mathbf{R}} system; storage and hoarding emerge as margin-adjusting behaviors, and no single capitalization rate r equalizes returns across storage versus production uses.

Veto Scenario 2 — Interest-rate ceiling. Verifiable condition: a regulatory cap \bar{r} is imposed below the competitive market-clearing r* implied by [PAGE 58]. Prediction: credit rationing occurs with strictly positive volume; investment in long-horizon social-progress projects declines despite positive prospective fruits; the second part of Knight’s problem—competition for savings among opportunities—ceases to allocate by price and instead allocates by political or collateral access.

Veto Scenario 3 — Knightian uncertainty shock / panic. Verifiable condition: a discontinuous shift in the consumption share \sigma downward (flight to present goods) not explained by any change in \bar{\mathbf{R}}, \bar{\mathbf{D}}, or insurable risk parameters [PAGE 58]. Prediction: asset prices representing prospective fruits drop discontinuously because the future is no longer capitalizable at a stable r; competitive reallocation of savings fails under non-quantifiable uncertainty; liquidity premiums diverge across ostensibly similar future-fruit assets, violating the equalization condition derived in the equations above.
