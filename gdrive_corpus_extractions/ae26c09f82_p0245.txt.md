The page [PAGE 235] is not an origin point but a synthesis node. Wheatstone's stereoscopic work (pre-1860s) established that binocular convergence and disparity can specify depth independently of accommodation; James reports Wheatstone's finding that attention reverses percepts [PAGE 235]. Helmholtz's Physiologische Optik then generalized this into a denial that convergence is an exact distance-measurer [PAGE 235]. Donders's 1864 Anomalies of Accommodation and Refraction (New Sydenham Soc. Transl., London, p. 155, cited at [PAGE 235]) added the accommodation side: spectacle magnification is driven by ciliary relaxation, not retinal enlargement. Aubert's 1876 Grundzüge der Physiologischen Optik (pp. 601, 615, 627, cited at [PAGE 235]) named these outcomes "secondary deceptions of judgment," exemplified by the moon viewed through a telescope: larger and brighter, hence judged nearer; yet because nearer, judged smaller [PAGE 235, n.]. James compresses this half-century lineage into a single phenomenological principle: when primary oculomotor cues are made contradictory, the secondary judgment about size retroactively alters the distance judgment, and vice versa.

Multi-week traps emerge from the adaptation dynamics implicit in this lineage. First spectacles produce immediate doubt ("draws near or retreats") [PAGE 235], but over days to weeks the observer reweights accommodation against convergence and size; removal of the spectacles then yields an aftereffect in which the un-aided world appears to move in the opposite direction. This is a multi-week recalibration trap: the nervous system adapts to the optical gain, stores a new cue-weighting, and expresses a negative aftereffect when the gain is removed. The sudden paralysis of one eye-muscle, also described at [PAGE 235], represents the opposite trap: no adaptation window exists, so the corrupted convergence efferent copy cannot be down-weighted in time, and the object appears at a finite distance despite divergent eyes. The telescope-moon example adds a cross-timescale trap: the immediate cue (brightness, detail, angular size) says nearer, while the learned size-distance relation says smaller if nearer, producing a retrocausal loop in phenomenological time where the distance judgment appears to reach backward and reshape the size judgment.

The genealogical chain is therefore Wheatstone → Helmholtz → Donders → Aubert → James, with each node reinterpreting the prior one's "deception" as a cue conflict rather than a sensory error. James's innovation at [PAGE 235] is to treat the vacillation itself as the datum, not as a failure to be corrected by attention.

The veto here is partial: the object "will none the less appear at a finite distance" even when the eyes are divergent [PAGE 235], showing that the biomechanical veto signal is insufficient to abolish depth, though it may generate diplopia or suppression. Formal condition: if muscle command m ≠ ocular alignment α (paralysis, paresis, or mechanical restriction), the convergence-derived distance d_c is flagged unreliable and down-weighted; if the flag is weak, d_c still leaks into the percept.

Weather/environmental layer: Although [PAGE 235] concerns optical instruments rather than meteorology, the cross-layer logic extends to environmental refractive perturbations. Haze, fog, and temperature gradients alter the effective optical path and the contrast of the retinal image s′, thereby changing the size–distance term S = s′d/f independently of oculomotor cues. Under strong atmospheric scattering, distance is systematically underestimated and the convergence/accommodation cues are vetoed by low-contrast, low-texture information. Formal environmental veto: if atmospheric modulation transfer function MTF(ν) falls below the contrast threshold for distance-specified texture gradients, then p(d | oculomotor) is overridden by p(d | atmospheric perspective), regardless of convergence state. This completes the veto space: the source exhausts the oculomotor subset (prism, stereoscope, spectacles, paralysis), while weather provides an exogenous physical layer that can independently invalidate the same distance estimate.

Cross-layer adjudication rule: a percept is stable only when physical image geometry, biomechanical oculomotor state, and environmental transmission jointly specify a single d within tolerance τ. The vignettes at [PAGE 235] are precisely the cases where two layers are held constant and a third is perturbed, producing the documented vacillation. Singularity regime: when layer disagreement exceeds τ in any pair, the posterior over distance has no maximum and the percept oscillates or collapses to indeterminacy.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions. RLVR denotes reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards framing in which each scenario has a falsifiable readout.

Scenario 1 (Pharmacological cycloplegia + spectacles). Manipulation: Administer a cycloplegic agent to paralyze the ciliary muscle, then place moderate convex spectacles before the eyes. Counterfactual: the accommodation cue (ciliary relaxation) is removed while retinal image size remains enlarged. Prediction: the initial doubt "whether the field of view draws near or retreats" [PAGE 235] collapses to a unified percept: the object appears nearer and larger, driven by retinal-size and convergence cues only, because the far-distance accommodation signal is gone. Verifiable readout: infrared optometry confirms zero accommodative response; forced-choice near/far judgments show a significant shift toward "near" compared with non-cycloplegic controls wearing the same spectacles.

Scenario 2 (Stereoscope with clamped accommodation). Manipulation: Present a stereoscopic disparity corresponding to an object at 1 m while clamping accommodation at optical infinity using a distant fixation target viewed through the same stereoscope (or a +0 D telescopic aperture). Counterfactual: disparity specifies near distance but accommodation specifies infinity. Prediction: consistent with [PAGE 235] ("picture seems at no determinate distance"), depth percepts are vetoed; stereoacuity thresholds rise to near-chance levels and magnitude estimates of depth scatter uniformly rather than clustering at 1 m. Verifiable readout: compare depth-discrimination thresholds with accommodation free versus clamped; the clamped condition must show no reliable depth discrimination above baseline.

Scenario 3 (Unilateral prism with fixed vergence). Manipulation: Place a horizontal wedge prism before one eye to shift its retinal image while requiring fixation of a central target that holds convergence angle μ constant. Counterfactual: retinal disparity signals a depth change that contradicts the efferent convergence signal. Prediction: the object will appear either at a finite distance despite the disparity-shifted image, or double (diplopia), reflecting the paralysis-like veto at [PAGE 235] where divergent eyes still yield a finite-distance percept. As prism power increases, the system should switch from a single finite-distance percept to suppression of one image rather than reporting a continuously changing depth. Verifiable readout: measure signed depth estimates and diplopia fields across prism diopters; prediction is a step function (single finite depth → suppression) rather than a linear depth–prism mapping.

Each prediction is stated with an explicit observable and a failure condition (e.g., if cyclopleged spectacle wearers still vacillate between near and far, Scenario 1 is falsified), satisfying the verifiable veto requirement.
