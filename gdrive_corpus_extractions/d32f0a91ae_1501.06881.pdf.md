If a candidate limit had three or more separated conic points in the critical case, it would correspond to a nondegenerate convex polytope with subcritical angle data. But [PAGE 6] states that Π is a homeomorphism and [PAGE 4] states the limit is "the unique football of constant curvature K = 1 with D′ = β1p + β1q." Therefore a candidate with more than two separated conic points is vetoed. The only allowed degeneration is the lens, i.e. the two-point football.

Analytic-layer veto. The analytic layer is the PDE Δu=-e^{2u} with the specified asymptotics. If a candidate conformal factor does not have the singular behavior β_i ln|z-z_i| near the conic points and -2 ln|z| at infinity, it is not a constant-curvature conic metric. If it does not satisfy the endpoint formula (3.9), it is not the normalized limit. The paper’s own final step uses the classification of all solutions to Δu=-e^{2u} on C\{0} [PAGE 17]; a candidate that disagrees with that classification is vetoed.

Measure/isoperimetric-layer veto. The measure layer is the level-set defect. If Ω_l(t) had two components of positive limiting area δ_1 and δ_2, then by superadditivity and Bonnesen,
\[
\mathcal D_l(t)\ge 8\pi\sqrt{\delta_1\delta_2}\gt 0,
\]
contradicting Lemma 3.6. Therefore the largest component must absorb the area and the remainder must have vanishing area and boundary length. A candidate limit with two nondegenerate level components is vetoed. The quote on [PAGE 17] is decisive: "any level set of u∞has vanishing isoperimetric deficit, which means each level set must be a round circle." If a proposed limit has non-circular level sets, it fails the measure veto.

Topological/divisor-layer veto. If a singular point z_{i,l} for i≥2 escaped to infinity in the normalized plane, the proof of (3.40) on [PAGE 17] shows that the partial sum α̂_l would be larger than α_l, forcing limsup a_l(t) less than A_∞(t) while all other components contribute zero. That contradicts the normalized area limit. Exact quote: "Finally, we show that lim l→∞zi,l = z0, for i ≥2." [PAGE 17]. Therefore a candidate in which a non-minimal singular point remains separated is vetoed.

Unit/scale-layer veto. The formula e^{2u∞} is written in normalized coordinates with K=1. If the coordinates are rescaled by z→λz, the conformal factor transforms as u→u(λz)+\lnλ, and the area transforms as A(t)→A(t-\lnλ). A candidate that ignores this transformation cannot be compared to the theorem. The centroid normalization is the physical-scale anchor. Any claim that uses raw unnormalized coordinates is vetoed. The cross-layer adjudication is thus: a conclusion is admissible only if it passes the geometric lens test, the PDE asymptotics test, the isoperimetric-defect test, the divisor-collapse test, and the unit/scale test. Weather and biomechanics supply no additional admissible test.
