Crisis habit regression. Scenario 1 — Salience/novelty veto. Replace the cutaneous vibration with an apparatus that produces identical sound and fixture movement but delivers vibration below the dog’s perceptual threshold (verified by absence of orienting response). All other Table 7 parameters remain identical [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: the dog must show no orienting response to the “sham” vibration. Prediction: salivary reaction remains at zero drops; no disinhibition is observed. This falsifies the claim that any “extra” stimulus liberates inhibition [PAGE 1] and supports a salience/novelty-gated disinhibition model rather than a generic extra-stimulus model.

Scenario 2 — Temporal re-inhibition veto. Keep the knocks-under-table disinhibition trial, but extend the interval before the meat-powder-alone probe from five minutes to thirty minutes, with intermediate probes at five and fifteen minutes [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: reaction must be recorded at each interval under identical motivational state. Prediction: if inhibitory reconsolidation has a finite time constant τ_inh on the order of minutes, the thirty-minute alone probe will show nonzero spontaneous recovery, whereas the five-minute probe remains near zero [PAGE 1]. This vetoes the blanket inference that inhibition “returns” within five minutes and instead maps the re-inhibition curve.

Scenario 3 — Measurement-censoring veto. Run an identical extinction/disinhibition schedule on a dog without salivary fistulas, collecting whole-mouth salivation by gravimetric milligram measurement rather than visible drops [PAGE 1]. Calibrate the drop-resolution threshold against milligrams. Veto condition: the gravimetric system must demonstrate sensitivity below one visible drop. Prediction: the same behavioral schedule that yielded “zero” drops at the third extinction trial and after the final alone probe [PAGE 1] will reveal measurable nonzero salivation (e.g., tenths of a milligram), showing that zero drops is a measurement floor rather than biological extinction [PAGE 1]. This vetoes the quantitative claim that the reaction tendency reached an absolute zero.
