<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical, biomechanical, and weather layers veto a strategic claim by changing the admissible strategy set or the effectivity of a coalition, not by rhetorical assertion. First, rules are absolute: “The rules of the game, however, are absolute commands” [PAGE 73]. If a biomechanical constraint makes a move physically impossible, that alternative is not a pure strategy in the game; any claimed strategy using it is inadmissible. Second, weather is a chance move only if it is represented with probabilities p_K(C_K)≥0 and Σp_K(C_K)=1 within the relevant information set [PAGE 94]. Then weather enters through expected payoff in the bilinear form [PAGE 181], not as a deterministic excuse. Third, information-layer failure vetoes the perfect-information theorem: that theorem applies when preliminarity equals anteriority [PAGE 136]; if a player lacks information about anterior moves, the theorem is unavailable and the mixed/minimax analysis must be used. Fourth, coalition claims are vetoed unless effectivity holds: a proposed imputation α cannot dominate β through S unless Σ_{i∈S}α_i≤v(S) and α_i\gt β_i for all i∈S [PAGE 274]. If a physical constraint reduces v(S), the domination claim may fail even though the verbal coalition “exists.” Fifth, any claimed guaranteed value outside the interval bounded by the minorant and majorant games is vetoed; the source defines Δ=v_2-v_1 as the advantage of “finding out” the adversary [PAGE 130]. Weather and biomechanics can enlarge Δ or change v(S), but they cannot make an ineffective coalition effective. The veto proof is therefore: recompute the strategy domains under physical constraints; recompute v(S) under those domains; test the effectivity inequality; if it fails, reject the domination or solution claim; if information is incomplete, reject strict-determinacy claims and use mixed strategies.
