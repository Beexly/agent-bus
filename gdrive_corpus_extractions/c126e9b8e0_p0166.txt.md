Weather / environmental layer veto. Although the source is laboratory-based, ambient temperature and humidity affect running speed v and drive level (Hull’s D = drive). A change in session temperature rescales D_eff = L/v(T) and shifts D_c(ρ). Veto condition: climate control with recorded temperature; otherwise the apparent “absolute magnitude” effect may be a thermoregulatory confound on locomotion rather than a temporal-discrimination effect.

Composite adjudication. The causal identification ℰ = f(D_s, D_l) holds only if all vetoes are cleared: (i) strictly comparable acts with equal path length; (ii) retention chamber separate from food box to suppress secondary reinforcement [PAGE 157]; (iii) constant biomechanical and environmental conditions; (iv) absolute delays within the intermediate discriminable band bounded by σ_a (short-delay floor) and λ⁻¹ (long-delay trace horizon). Failure of any veto renders Corollaries VIII–IX descriptive at best and causally unidentified at worst.

Veto Scenario 1 — Sub-reaction-time short delay.
Setup: Fixed ratio ρ = 4. Sweep D_s from 0.05 s to 2 s while D_l = ρD_s. Path lengths equated; retention chamber separate; temperature controlled. Motor/auditory reaction-time floor is approximately σ_a ≈ 0.15–0.25 s.
Prediction: Discrimination ease ℰ (measured as inverse trials-to-criterion or choice accuracy for the shorter-delay act) collapses to chance-level as D_s → 0⁺, regardless of ρ. The curve follows ℰ ∝ D_s / √(σ_a² + (wD_s)²) near zero and remains below behavioral threshold θ until D_s exceeds roughly 2–3σ_a. Verification: if accuracy stays at 50% (chance) for D_s < 0.15 s even with ρ = 4, Corollary IX’s short-delay boundary is confirmed; if accuracy remains high at D_s = 0.05 s, the additive noise floor assumption is falsified.

Veto Scenario 2 — Long-delay trace extinction.
Setup: Fixed ratio ρ = 2. Hold D_s = 30 s and increase D_l to 60 s, then escalate both delays proportionally across weekly sessions up to D_s = 600 s, D_l = 1200 s (10–20 min). Trace-decay rate λ expected on the order of 10⁻³–10⁻² s⁻¹ based on short-term retention.
Prediction: ℰ exhibits an inverted-U across the escalation, peaking at intermediate absolute delays and declining to indifference as D_s, D_l → sufficiently great values. Because both V(D_s) and V(D_l) approach zero under exponential/hyperbolic decay, ΔV → 0 and the preference criterion is reached in a number of trials not different from random. Verification: if choice accuracy does not decline at multi-minute delays, the trace-decay term λ is effectively zero and Corollary IX’s long-delay impossibility clause is invalidated for this paradigm.

Veto Scenario 3 — Path-length confound veto.
Setup: Two apparatus configurations. Configuration A: acts strictly comparable (turn left vs. turn right, equal runway), D_s = 5 s, D_l = 10 s (ρ = 2). Configuration B: same programmed delays but one act requires a runway twice as long, introducing work/energy expenditure W ≠ 0 (p. 280 ff. confound) [PAGE 157].
Prediction: In Configuration A, ℰ follows the delay-only model with maximum near D_c. In Configuration B, ℰ is systematically lower and shifts as if the effective delay of the long-runway act were D_eff = D_l + βW; if βW is large enough, the organism may prefer the short-runway act even when its programmed delay is longer, producing a preference reversal not explained by D alone. Verification: equating path lengths in Configuration B while keeping programmed delays identical should restore the Configuration A ℰ pattern; failure to restore implies an unmodeled non-temporal variable controls choice.
