<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage runs from Pavlov’s conditioned reflexes (1927) through Hull’s _Principles of Behavior_ (1943), which formalized the generalization gradient as a deductive consequence of association strength, to Hovland’s Yale experimental program on stimulus generalization (1930s–1940s). Hovland’s use of the galvanic skin response and the j.n.d. interval places this work within the psychophysical tradition of Fechner and Weber, where psychological distance is scaled by sensory discriminability rather than by raw physical magnitude. Later descendants include Shepard’s universal law of generalization (1987), which expresses response decay as an exponential function of psychological distance in a multidimensional metric space, and Rescorla–Wagner (1972), which recasts conditioning as prediction-error driven.

Multi-week traps arise when this experimental logic is extended longitudinally. Over repeated weekly sessions, the j.n.d. itself can shift because sensory discrimination improves with practice, meaning that “50 j.n.d. intervals” in week one do not correspond to the same physical intensity spacing in week five. Sensitization accumulates with repeated aversive or novel stimulation, elevating the response floor across sessions. Most critically, the “static stimuli arising from the experimental environment” ([PAGE 1]) are not actually static across weeks: the experimenter, room, and handling routine become conditioned contextual cues. If these contextual cues are not re-baselined or counterbalanced, the apparent asymptote rises over weeks even when the true stimulus gradient is unchanged, producing a false conclusion that generalization has broadened or that learning has degraded.
