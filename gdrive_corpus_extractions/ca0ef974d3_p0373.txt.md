Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions. No betting lines are invoked; all observables are behavioral or statistical.

Scenario 1 — Base-rate veto (rain vs. medicine). Intervention: run \(N\) trials measuring \(p_1 = P(\text{recovery} \mid \text{treatment})\) and \(p_2 = P(\text{rain} \mid \text{ritual})\). If \(|p_1 - p_2| > \alpha\) with \(\alpha = 0.20\), issue a veto on the sorcerer’s analogy. Verification: a separate cohort receives base-rate summaries before judging the Livingstone dialogue. Prediction: base-rate-informed subjects reject the sorcerer’s equivalence at \(p < 0.05\) in a forced-choice validity judgment, whereas control subjects accept it because they match on the rhetorical template “sometimes it works, sometimes it does not” [PAGE 373].

Scenario 2 — Causal-graph veto. Intervention: present participants with explicit causal DAGs for source and target. If the source graph contains a node absent from the target (e.g., “doctor’s duty” versus “conjuration gesture”), veto analogical transfer. Verification: compare transfer rates in an opaque-analogy condition versus a DAG-transparent condition. Prediction: DAG transparency reduces analogical transfer by at least 40% relative to opaque controls, because participants can no longer absorb causal mismatch into an unobserved divine node as in James’s missionary/sorcerer exchange [PAGE 373].

Scenario 3 — Non-stationarity veto (proverbial “bird in the hand”). Intervention: a two-armed bandit with stationary payoffs for weeks 1–2, then a mean shift (drift) at week 3. One agent follows the proverb prior \(\pi_{\mathrm{prov}}\) fixed at “take the certain bird”; another uses a Bayesian change-point detector. Verification: cumulative regret is logged weekly. Prediction: after the week-3 drift, the proverb agent incurs linear regret \(\Omega(T)\), while the change-point agent incurs \(O(\log T)\) regret. The veto is verifiable by observing the regret divergence beginning exactly at the intervention week; James’s account of proverbial philosophy as universally “supreme” at this stage [PAGE 373] fails under this non-stationary counterfactual.
