<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week diagnostic traps.

The study sits at the tail end of a contested nosological lineage. The authors open by citing the absence of “coherent and consistent scientific statements regarding the terminology and diagnostic criteria” for inguinal pathology in athletes [PAGE 1, refs 4–6]. That void was partially addressed by the Doha agreement meeting on terminology and definitions in groin pain in athletes (Weir et al., BJSM 2015) [PAGE 1, ref 3], which attempted to separate adductor-related, iliopsoas-related, inguinal-related, and pubic-related groin pain. Earlier imaging work established the anatomical substrate: Rennie and Lloyd on the inguinal ligament and the “Lloyd technique” [ref 1]; Chopra and Robinson on imaging athletic groin pain [ref 2]; Khan et al. on MRI of athletic pubalgia and sports hernia [ref 6]. Standardized acquisition preceded this paper: Branci et al. (2014) published the Copenhagen Standardised MRI protocol for the pubic symphysis and adductor regions [ref 9], and Helms et al. (2009) supplied basic musculoskeletal MRI principles [ref 8]. Ultrasound’s role was systematized by Kwee and Kwee’s 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis on ultrasonography for clinically occult groin hernia [ref 13]. Ioffe et al. (2020) therefore contributes a localized MRI/ultrasound criteria set from Kyiv, using a 1.5T protocol and Doppler US, before universal diagnostic acceptance.

Embedded in that lineage are multi-week traps. The inclusion criterion required pain that “prevented them from continuing to actively participate in sports activities” plus “lack of effect of previous conservative therapy” [PAGE 1]. Athletes whose symptoms resolve within two to four weeks of rest and rehabilitation are excluded by design; the cohort is therefore enriched for subacute to chronic, refractory presentations. The model does not predict incident sports hernia; it predicts “sports hernia severe enough to fail conservative management and warrant surgery.” This creates a temporal selection trap: a clinician applying the criteria to an acute, two-week presentation will overestimate the positive predictive value because the spectrum was pre-filtered for chronicity.

A second multi-week trap concerns the natural history of the 11.8–12.4 mm canal widening observed in 6 (12%) of the non-surgical group [PAGE 2]. Those athletes had no posterior-wall protrusion and were kept under “continuous monitoring” [PAGE 2], yet the paper reports no follow-up interval, no progression rate to protrusion, and no return-to-play timeline. The canal-width threshold therefore sits in an uncertain window: it may be a stable anatomical variant, an early reactive change, or a precursor to surgical disease, and the data cannot discriminate among these over weeks to months.

A third trap is the asymmetry between MRI and ultrasound verification. All 36 surgical patients had posterior-wall protrusion on ultrasound [PAGE 1, 5], making protrusion the de facto reference standard for the experimental arm. Because the same ultrasound criterion helped define the group, the reported ultrasound sensitivity (88.89%) and PPV (82.1%) are partially circular. The multi-week implication is that a negative ultrasound in a patient with persistent symptoms cannot safely exclude evolving pathology; the absence of protrusion at time zero does not rule out protrusion at week six or twelve under continued loading.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and temporal clock.

Crisis habit regression. When a footballer enters a groin-pain crisis, protective motor regression is the default: load is offloaded from the symptomatic lower extremity, kicking kinematics are altered, hip extension is reduced, and adductor co-contraction increases to stabilize the pelvis. Because the cohort predominantly uses the right lower extremity [PAGE 4], the right side bears the habitual mechanical load; when pain emerges, the regression shifts stress across the pubic symphysis, producing “reactive changes in the pubic bones, resulting from their displacement one against another in the symphysis area, the spread of oedema to the surrounding soft tissues… with increasing hydrophilicity of the inguinal structures” [PAGE 5]. The PDfs hyperintensity that the MRI model reads as diagnostic may therefore be downstream of the movement-pattern regression rather than an independent structural lesion.

Counter-trap. The clinician’s reciprocal habit is structuralist regression: seeing PDfs hyperintensity and canal widening, the observer treats the imaging signal as the disease itself and proceeds toward the surgical indication that defined the experimental group [PAGE 1]. The counter-trap is to recognize that protrusion of the posterior wall is the necessary verification anchor—“the cardinal sign… detected in all football players in the first group” [PAGE 4]—while canal signal or size alone is insufficient. Because 6 (12%) of the non-surgical group had canal widening between 11.8 and 12.4 mm without protrusion [PAGE 2], a signal-first reading generates false positives and unnecessary surgical referrals.

Clock. The study is cross-sectional: it captures one imaging time point after an unrecorded duration of symptoms and failed conservative therapy [PAGE 1]. It does not record time from symptom onset to MRI/US, the length of the conservative trial before enrollment, the interval from imaging to surgical decision, or the re-injury window after return to kicking. Those intervals form the causal clock. PDfs hyperintensity could precede protrusion (a predictive biomarker) or follow it (a consequence of chronic shear); the design cannot adjudicate directionality. Likewise, the 16% of non-surgical players with adductor-attachment signal “might be indicative of ARS syndrome” [PAGE 2], meaning the clock of adductor-related overload may run parallel to, or instead of, the true hernia clock. A model that ignores time-to-event and treats a single scan as definitive commits a temporal category error.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto layers with adjudication.

Physical layer veto. The MRI protocol uses B₀ = 1.5 T, slice thickness Δz = 3.0 mm, and FOV = 160×120 to 180×180 mm [PAGE 2, Table I]. At 1.5 T, signal-to-noise is lower than at 3 T, and 3 mm through-plane resolution risks partial-volume averaging at the thin posterior wall of the inguinal canal. A small, dynamic, Valsalva-dependent protrusion—described as the “weak zone” protrusion and the cardinal sign [PAGE 4, 5]—may be occult on a static supine axial PDfs slice. Ultrasound was performed standing and supine [PAGE 2], but no cough, Valsalva, or standing strain maneuver is documented; a protrusion that reduces when supine will be missed, inducing false negatives. Physical-layer verdict: the combined rule is vetoed unless dynamic ultrasound under provocation confirms posterior-wall protrusion.

Biomechanical layer veto. Laterality is a hard biomechanical constraint. The authors state players “predominantly use their right lower extremity during the game” [PAGE 4], and the statistics reflect it: all significant MRI predictors are right-sided [PAGE 2, Table II; PAGE 5, Table VI], while left-sided bone-marrow signal is null (b = -0.11 ± 0.63, p = 0.86) and left canal size is non-significant (b = 0.068 ± 0.08, p = 0.38) [PAGE 3, Table III]. Applying the right-lateralized two-factor MRI model to a left-dominant athlete is a biomechanical mismatch veto. Additionally, adductor-muscle signal was not significant univariately on either side (right p = 0.130; left p = 0.86) [PAGE 2–3, Tables II–III], and 8 (16%) of the non-surgical group had adductor-attachment signal suggestive of ARS syndrome [PAGE 2]. Adductor-related groin pain can therefore mimic sports hernia on MRI; without excluding intra-articular hip pathology (not covered by the 160–180 mm inguinal FOV [PAGE 2, Table I]), the biomechanical layer vetoes a hernia diagnosis.

Weather / environmental layer. The protocol reports no ambient temperature, humidity, training-surface, or muscle-temperature controls. Tissue compliance, muscle stiffness, and ultrasound coupling at f = 3.5–5.0 MHz [PAGE 2] are temperature-dependent; cold environments increase viscoelastic stiffness and alter load transmission across the pubic symphysis during explosive rotational movements and hard kicks [PAGE 1]. The absence of environmental metadata is a veto gap: a cold-weather training session may produce transient symphyseal oedema that resolves with warming, mimicking the “hydrophilicity” signal [PAGE 5] without representing chronic sports hernia.

Adjudicated rule. The MRI+ultrasound criteria are only defensible when all layers concur: (i) both PDfs hyperintensities (inguinal canal structures and superior pubic ramus marrow) are present; (ii) dynamic ultrasound demonstrates posterior-wall protrusion; (iii) the athlete is right-limb dominant with chronic, refractory symptoms; and (iv) hip joint and adductor-origin pathology have been excluded. Outside this narrow intersection, the model self-vetoes.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions.

Scenario 1 — Right-lateralized model applied to a left-dominant acute athlete. Counterfactual: a 19-year-old left-leg-dominant footballer presents with three weeks of acute groin pain, VAS 4. MRI shows left adductor-origin PDfs signal but normal right pubic marrow and normal right inguinal canal; ultrasound shows canal 10.2 mm with no posterior-wall protrusion. Veto: both the right-sided two-factor MRI model (valid only for right-sided marrow and canal signal [PAGE 5, Table VI]) and the protrusion requirement fail. Prediction: if a clinician nevertheless applies the right-sided OR = 25.64 criteria, the result is a false negative; the correct classification is early adductor-related groin pain / ARS, and the falsifiable outcome is successful conservative management without surgery within six weeks.

Scenario 2 — Canal widening without protrusion in a refractory athlete. Counterfactual: a 27-year-old amateur with eight weeks of refractory pain, VAS 8. MRI shows bilateral PDfs canal signal but no bone-marrow oedema; ultrasound shows right canal 12.1 mm but no posterior-wall protrusion. Veto: canal widening between 11.8 and 12.4 mm without protrusion occurred in 6 (12%) of the non-surgical group [PAGE 2], and ultrasound specificity is 50% (95% CI 23.0–77) [PAGE 1]. Prediction: proceeding to surgery on canal size alone carries a high false-positive risk; the falsifiable outcome is persistent post-operative pain or symptom resolution with an extended conservative trial plus repeat dynamic ultrasound under Valsalva.

Scenario 3 — Inguinal protocol excluding hip pathology in a bilateral presenter. Counterfactual: a 31-year-old professional with bilateral groin pain, VAS 9. MRI meets both right-sided PDfs criteria; ultrasound shows right posterior-wall protrusion. The model yields PPV = 91.7% [PAGE 4], apparently supporting surgery. Counterfactual veto: the MRI FOV of 160–180 mm [PAGE 2, Table I] is an inguinal protocol and excludes the hip joint; occult femoroacetabular impingement or labral pathology referring to the groin would not be imaged. Prediction: the reported PPV collapses in this spectrum because verification bias (surgery performed because imaging criteria were met, not because hernia was causal) is exposed; the falsifiable outcome is persistent groin pain after hernia repair, with subsequent diagnosis of intra-articular hip pathology.
