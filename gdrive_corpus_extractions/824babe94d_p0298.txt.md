To rigorously analyze Tolman's 1932 propositions on motivation and learning, we must first formalize the qualitative distinctions he draws between appetites (e.g., hunger) and aversions (e.g., fright). The core argument hinges on the periodicity of "initiating physiological states." For appetites, this state is cyclical, driven by metabolic rhythms. For aversions, it is "relatively continuous," a persistent baseline of timidity that primes the organism to detect threat. This gray zone—the boundary between a temporary metabolic deficit and a permanent neurological bias—requires a multi-hop derivation bridging mid-century behavioral psychology with modern computational neuroscience and thermodynamics.

**Hop 1: Formalizing the Initiating State.** Tolman posits that a demand for quiescence only arises when an initiating state is present. We can model this as a potential energy function $V(\mathbf{x})$ over a cognitive map $\mathbf{x}$. The gradient $\nabla V$ represents the "demand" vector field. The initiating state modulates the amplitude or existence of this field.

**Hop 2: Temporal Dynamics and Periodicity.** For appetites, the source text explicitly links the cycle to a "definite metabolic rhythm." This implies a limit cycle oscillator in a biochemical network (e.g., ghrelin/leptin signaling). Mathematically, the amplitude $A(t)$ of the demand field follows a periodic function, perhaps approximated by a van der Pol or relaxation oscillator. The system spends most of its time in a low-energy "inertial" state (satiety), punctuated by bursts of high-amplitude excitation (hunger).

For aversions, the text describes a "more enduring and constant affair." This suggests a non-zero baseline offset in the potential landscape, always creating a shallow gradient toward safety. It's not an oscillation but a sustained elevation of a tonic signal (e.g., amygdala noradrenergic tone). The "timidity" parameter sets the gain of the threat-detection filter.

**Hop 3: Information-Theoretic Interpretation and Gray-Zone Audit.** The critical insight is reinterpreting these physiological states as filters on sensory information processing. An appetite creates a *top-down* predictive prior that biases perception toward food-relevant cues (sign-gestalts). This increases the mutual information $I(S; R)$ between stimulus $S$ and response $R$ for food-related features, while potentially decreasing it for others. The cyclic nature means this filter is dynamically gated.

Conversely, an aversion like timidity establishes a *permanent, low-level* threat-prior. The organism is perpetually running a Bayesian detector for injury. The "continuous" state means the detection threshold is lowered globally and persistently. The gray zone emerges in defining "continuous": Is it truly constant, or does it fluctuate on timescales much longer than the appetite cycle? Tolman’s prose suggests it’s a trait-like property ("possession of the organism"), akin to a standing wave of anxiety, unlike the transient, episodic waves of hunger.

**Hop 4: Multi-Week Trap Analysis.** A profound trap lies in assuming these modes are mutually exclusive or static. Modern affective neuroscience shows that chronic stress (a continuous aversive state) can dysregulate metabolic rhythms (disrupting appetite cycles). Thus, the "continuous" aversion state can veto or reshape the "cyclical" appetite dynamics. Our formalism must account for cross-layer coupling where a high tonic aversion level raises the baseline of the entire motivational landscape, altering the effective minima and barriers. This leads to the concept of a *meta-stable* regime where the system is prone to getting trapped in maladaptive loops (e.g., anorexia nervosa, where fear of eating overrides the normal hunger cycle). The derivation must therefore treat the two states as interacting control parameters in a bifurcation analysis.

We define a unifying framework based on predictive coding and nonequilibrium thermodynamics, grounding Tolman’s concepts in precise mathematics.

**1. Potential Landscape and Demand Field:**
Let the cognitive map be a state space $\mathcal{M}$. The organism’s valuation is represented by a scalar potential field $V: \mathcal{M} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. The "demand" for movement toward quiescence or away from disturbance is the negative gradient:
$$\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{x}, t) = -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} V(\mathbf{x}, t)$$
This vector field dictates exploratory behavior. The units of $V$ are **Joules (J)**, representing expected future free energy minimization.

**2. Initiating Physiological State as Control Parameter:**
Following the source [PAGE 274]: *"It is only when these states are in force that the demands... arise."* We introduce a scalar control parameter $\alpha(t) \in [0,1]$ representing the intensity of the initiating state (hunger or timidity). This parameter modulates the potential field:
$$V(\mathbf{x}, t) = \alpha(t) \cdot U(\mathbf{x}) + V_0(\mathbf{x})$$
where $U(\mathbf{x})$ is a fixed template potential encoding the location of consummatory objects (food) or threat sources (loud noises), and $V_0(\mathbf{x})$ is a neutral baseline potential. The units of $\alpha$ are dimensionless. When $\alpha=0$, the specific demand field vanishes; the organism exhibits no goal-directed behavior related to that need.

**3. Formalizing Periodicity: Appetite vs. Aversion:**
*Appetites:* As per [PAGE 274], *"follows a definite metabolic rhythm or cycle."* We model $\alpha_H(t)$ (hunger) as a periodic function with period $T_H$:
$$\frac{d\alpha_H}{dt} = f(\alpha_H) = \mu \alpha_H (1 - \alpha_H^2) - \lambda \sin(2\pi t / T_H)$$
This is a forced van der Pol oscillator. In the absence of forcing ($\lambda=0$), it exhibits self-sustained oscillations, capturing the "state of excitation ... followed by a state of inertia." The solution is approximately sinusoidal: $\alpha_H(t) \approx A_H \sin(2\pi t/T_H + \phi)$.
*Aversions:* As per [PAGE 274], the state is *"a more enduring and constant affair."* We model timidity $\alpha_T$ as a slow, stochastic process with a non-zero mean $\bar{\alpha}_T > 0$:
$$\tau_T \frac{d\alpha_T}{dt} = -(\alpha_T - \bar{\alpha}_T) + \sigma_T \xi(t)$$
where $\xi(t)$ is Gaussian white noise, $\tau_T$ is a long relaxation time constant (hours/days), and $\bar{\alpha}_T$ is the trait-like baseline timidity. This yields a stationary distribution centered at $\bar{\alpha}_T$, justifying "relatively continuous."

**4. Information-Theoretic Consequence & Breakdown Regimes:**
The sign-gestalt readiness is the increase in mutual information between stimuli and appropriate responses due to $\alpha$. The Fisher Information Metric on the parameter $\alpha$ quantifies the sensitivity of the demand field:
$$g_{\alpha\alpha} = \mathbb{E}\left[ \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \alpha} \ln P(\mathbf{r}|s,\alpha) \right)^2 \right]$$
High $g_{\alpha\alpha}$ means the current initiating state makes the organism highly sensitive to specific "disturbing stimuli" or food cues.
*Singularities/Breakdowns:*
- **Saturation Singularity:** If $\alpha_H \rightarrow 1$ (starvation), the potential $U(\mathbf{x})$ may dominate all other potentials, leading to a loss of behavioral flexibility (compulsive foraging). The gradient descent becomes unstable near steep cliffs of $U(\mathbf{x})$.
- **Anxiety Catastrophe:** If $\bar{\alpha}_T$ exceeds a critical value $\alpha_T^*$, the continuous threat-prior can create spurious minima in $V(\mathbf{x})$ corresponding to safe-but-unproductive hiding spots. The system undergoes a saddle-node bifurcation, trapping the agent in pathological avoidance. This occurs when the curvature of the induced potential wells exceeds the exploration noise $\sigma_T$.

**Assumptions:** The map $\mathcal{M}$ is low-dimensional (consistent with Tolman’s "cognitive maps"). Potentials are smooth. Noise is additive and Gaussian. Cross-modal interactions (e.g., between $\alpha_H$ and $\alpha_T$) are ignored in this base model but are crucial for the gray-zone traps analyzed later.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 framework represents a pivotal synthesis in the genealogy of purposive behaviorism, standing at the confluence of several intellectual lineages. Its retrocausal influence is profound, yet it was also shaped by prior paradigms.

**Scientific Lineage:**
1.  **Pavlovian Conditioning (Early 1900s):** Tolman rejected the simple S-R bond, arguing instead that the intermediate variable is a *purpose* or *expectancy*. His "initiating physiological state" is a direct descendant of Pavlov’s "internal conditions" necessary for conditioning. However, Tolman internalized and cognitified these states, making them selectors of meaning rather than mere excitability enhancers.
2.  **Gestalt Psychology (Köhler, Koffka, 1920s):** The term "sign-gestalt-readiness" is a hallmark. Tolman imported the Gestalt principle of * Prägnanz* (good figure) into behaviorism: organisms perceive whole configurations (gestalts) that signify goals. The "readiness" is the activation of these perceptual schemata by the initiating state. This linked physiological drive to holistic perception, a radical departure from elementalistic Watsonian behaviorism.
3.  **Hebbian Synaptic Plasticity (1949):** Though post-dating Tolman, his work prefigured the idea of cell assemblies representing goals. The "cognitive map" is the neural substrate for the potential landscape $V(\mathbf{x})$. His emphasis on *purposive* behavior directly influenced the development of reinforcement learning algorithms, where a value function (akin to $V(\mathbf{x})$) guides policy.

**Multi-Week Trap: The Trait-State Conflation:**
A critical historical trap emerged from Tolman’s formulation. He described aversion (timidity) as a "physiological state or excitement" that is "enduring." This blurs the line between a temporary state (like being startled) and a dispositional trait (being anxious). Later psychologists (e.g., Eysenck, 1967) operationalized "emotionality" as a trait, but Tolman’s language allowed this conflation to persist into cognitive models. The trap is assuming that because timidity feels continuous subjectively, its underlying physiology must be a constant tonic activation. Modern neuroscience reveals that what feels like a continuous trait may be the result of frequent, rapidly recurring micro-episodes of amygdala activation, misattributed as a steady state. Tolman’s era lacked the tools to distinguish these temporal profiles, embedding a false dichotomy (cyclic vs. constant) into the theoretical DNA of motivation research.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Analyzing Tolman’s concepts through a recursive theory of mind lens involves modeling how an organism (or an agent simulating one) predicts and reacts to the presence of these initiating states in itself and others. This reveals deep crisis habits.

**Crisis Habit Regression: The Timidity Loop.**
Consider an animal with high baseline timidity ($\bar{\alpha}_T$ large). According to Tolman, this state makes it constantly ready to flee from "disturbing stimuli." In a recursive framework, the animal develops a meta-model: *"The world is threatening, and I am sensitive to threat."* This belief reinforces the physiological state, creating a positive feedback loop. The habit regression is towards hypervigilance: any ambiguous cue is interpreted as a threat (Bayesian inference with a strong prior), triggering flight, which then confirms the prior ("See, the world *is* dangerous!"). This is a self-fulfilling prophecy at the level of physiological state regulation. The "clock" of this loop is very fast (milliseconds to seconds), contrasting with the slower metabolic clock of hunger.

**Counter-Trap Strategy: Exploiting the Appetite Cycle.**
A predator or a competing agent aware of the prey's cyclical appetite could set a trap timed to coincide with peak hunger ($\alpha_H$ maximum). At this moment, the prey's demand field is strongest, overriding other considerations (including moderate aversion). The counter-trap is to present a high-value consummatory object (food) in a high-threat location. The prey’s own initiatory state becomes the instrument of its capture. This requires the trapping agent to have an accurate internal model of the prey's metabolic rhythm—a sophisticated form of Theory of Mind.

**The Clock Mismatch Trap:**
The fundamental vulnerability arises from the mismatch in timescales. The appetite clock (hours) is slow and predictable. The aversion clock (continuous, but with rapid phasic spikes) is noisy and unpredictable. An adaptive agent should align its risk-taking behavior with the phase of its appetite cycle. However, if the continuous aversion state is too strong, it can veto action even during peak hunger, leading to starvation. Conversely, if the hunger cycle is too dominant, it can suppress healthy caution, leading to fatal injury. Evolution has tuned these clocks to interact optimally in ancestral environments, but they can decouple under novel conditions (e.g., famine combined with new predators).

**Recursive Prediction and Deception:**
A higher-order thinker can simulate these states in another agent to deceive. To lure a timid animal, one must not only provide food but also temporarily suppress its perceived threat level (e.g., by providing shelter first). This is a second-order maneuver: manipulating the target’s $\alpha_T$ to allow its $\alpha_H$ to express behavior. Tolman’s framework, by separating the *initiation* from the *expression* of behavior, provides the conceptual toolkit for such complex strategic reasoning about motivated agents.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
We must establish proof that physical/biomechanical constraints and environmental factors can veto the expression of demand fields derived from initiating states, regardless of their intensity. This adjudicates the hierarchy of control.

**Physical/Biomechanical Veto Proof:**
Consider an extremely hungry animal ($\alpha_H \approx 1$) facing a food source across a chasm wider than its biomechanical jumping capacity $L_{max}$. The demand field $\mathbf{D}_H$ points strongly toward the food. However, the motor execution layer implements a kinematic constraint: feasible actions $A$ satisfy $\| \Delta \mathbf{x} \| \leq L_{max}$ per unit time. If the required jump $\|\Delta \mathbf{x}_{food}\| > L_{max}$, the action is physically impossible. The biomechanical planner returns an empty set of valid motions, **vetoing** the execution of $\mathbf{D}_H$. The resulting behavior is frustration or redirection, not pursuit. Formally, the realized velocity $\mathbf{v}$ is projected onto the tangent cone of the reachable set: $\mathbf{v} = \Pi_{T_{Reach}}(\mathbf{D}_H)$. If $\mathbf{D}_H \notin T_{Reach}$, then $\mathbf{v} \neq k\mathbf{D}_H$; the demand is vetoed.

**Weather/Environmental Veto Proof:**
Suppose a timid animal ($\alpha_T$ high) detects a faint sound (potential threat) and its aversion demand field $\mathbf{D}_T$ points away. However, a sudden gust of wind alters the acoustic propagation, causing sound reflections that make the source direction ambiguous. The sensory layer’s estimate of the threat location becomes uncertain, broadening the posterior $P(\text{source}|\text{signal})$. If the posterior variance exceeds a threshold, the demand vector $\mathbf{D}_T$ becomes ill-defined (low magnitude, high uncertainty). The decision layer, using a threshold policy, may veto a directed flight response, defaulting to freezing instead. This is a **weather veto**: an environmental factor degrades the fidelity of the sign-gestalt, preventing the translation of the initiating state into a coherent action.

**Adjudication Principle:**
The initiating physiological state ($\alpha$) generates a *preference order* over outcomes. However, this preference is filtered through a cascade of vetoes:
1.  **Sensory Veto:** Can the relevant sign-gestalt be detected with sufficient confidence?
2.  **Biomechanical Veto:** Are there physically realizable motions that approximate the desired change in state?
3.  **Energetic Veto:** Does executing the action require more energy than currently available (critical for starving animals)?
Only if all vetoes pass does the demand field manifest as overt behavior. Tolman’s description focuses almost exclusively on the first stage (the arising of the demand). The necessity of these lower-level vetoes proves that the "initiating state" is necessary but not sufficient for behavior, highlighting a major limitation of purely cognitive-motivational accounts.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

*Prediction (Tolman):* With $\alpha_H=0$, the demand field $V$ collapses to $V_0$. The agent should exhibit **no goal-directed navigation** toward the food, despite knowing its location. Behavior should revert to random exploration or stereotypies.
*Verification:* Measure path efficiency to the food location pre- and post-drug. A significant drop to chance levels verifies Tolman’s claim that the initiating state is necessary for expression of learned knowledge. Failure (agent still goes straight to food) would refute Tolman, supporting a model where knowledge drives behavior independently of drive.

**Scenario 2: The Phantom Threat.**
*Setup:* Condition an agent to associate a specific auditory cue (tone) with a mild shock, inducing a conditioned fear state (increased $\alpha_T$). Test in a context where the tone is presented but the shock is omitted for many trials (extinction). Tolman’s model suggests the continuous timidity state persists, so the "demand to get from injury" should remain active upon hearing the tone.
*Counterfactual Veto:* Introduce a simultaneous, intense novel visual stimulus (a bright flash) that is utterly unrelated to the original threat. This new stimulus creates its own, powerful attentional and orienting demand.
*Prediction (Conflict):* Tolman’s framework lacks a mechanism for resolving conflicting demands from multiple initiating states. Which demand wins? The prediction depends on added assumptions about arbitration (e.g., winner-take-all, summation). We hypothesize that the novel, intense stimulus will **veto** the expression of the fear demand, suppressing the freezing response.
*Verification:* Compare freezing duration in tone-only vs. tone+flash conditions. A suppression effect would indicate that the initiating state’s output can be gated by external salience, challenging the idea of a direct, unmoderated link between $\alpha_T$ and flight.

**Scenario 3: The Aversion-Amplified Appetite.**
*Setup:* Create an environment where obtaining food (high $\alpha_H$) requires passing through a mildly frightening compartment (activating $\alpha_T$). Normally, animals show a trade-off: they approach food when very hungry, avoid the area when sated. Tolman views these as separate systems.
*Counterfactual Manipulation:* Chronic stress manipulation (e.g., repeated restraint) is known to increase the baseline $\bar{\alpha}_T$ (make the animal more timid). According to Tolman, this should simply raise the threshold for the hunger demand to overcome the aversion.
*Verifiable Veto Prediction:* We predict a nonlinear interaction. There exists a critical value of $\bar{\alpha}_T^*$ such that for $\bar{\alpha}_T < \bar{\alpha}_T^*$, increasing hunger still leads to approach. But for $\bar{\alpha}_T > \bar{\alpha}_T^*$, **even maximal hunger fails to elicit approach**. The continuous aversion state doesn't just add resistance; it structurally alters the decision landscape, possibly by making the frightening compartment appear infinitely costly. This is a catastrophic veto.
*Verification:* Conduct a dose-response study varying chronic stress level (proxy for $\bar{\alpha}_T$) and measuring the maximum hunger drive (fasting time) that still produces approach behavior. The existence of a sharp transition (bifurcation) point, rather than a gradual shift, would support this veto prediction and refute a simple linear combination model of appetites and aversions.
