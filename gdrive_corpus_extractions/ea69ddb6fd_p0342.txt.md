*Formal Definition:* `φ_M(S) = { a_i | a_i ∈ Ω_S ∧ i ∈ I_task }`, where `I_task` is an index set determined by the current goal `G`.
*Jamesian Citation:* "When we conceive of S merely as M... we neglect all the other attributes which it may have, and attend exclusively to this one. We mutilate the fulness of S's reality." [PAGE 332]
*Units:* Input is a high-dimensional manifold; output is a discrete feature vector. The "mutilation" is quantifiable as the Kullback-Leibler divergence `D_KL(P_true(S) || P_φ_M(S))` between the true distribution of `S`'s properties and the compressed distribution.

**Operator 2: Subsumption (Minor Premise Generation)**
This is the dynamic selection of the optimal `φ_M` at time `t`. It is a search over the space of possible projections.
*Equation:* `M_t^* = argmax_{φ} [ C(φ(S_t), G_t) - β· Complexity(φ) ]`
where `C` is a context-fit cost function and `β` penalizes overly complex conceptions under pressure.
*Jamesian Citation:* "...the second or minor premise, the 'subsumption'... is the one requiring the sagacity ;... the ability to seize fresh aspects in concrete things..." [PAGE 332]
*Breakdown Regime:* When cognitive resources (working memory, attentional bandwidth) drop below a threshold `θ_res`, the search collapses. `M_t^*` becomes a cached, default `φ_default`, often a salient but irrelevant `M` (e.g., seeing only "big linebacker" instead of "linebacker leaking to flat").

**Operator 3: Major Premise (General Rule Application)**
This is a fixed conditional probability distribution or a deterministic mapping learned from past experience.
*Equation:* `P(Rule): M ⇒ P` or `P(Action | M) = w_MP`.
*Jamesian Citation:* "The statement that M is P is an abstract or general proposition." [PAGE 332]
*Sufficiency Condition Failure:* The major premise assumes `M` is a sufficient statistic for predicting `P`. This fails when `S` contains a hidden variable `H ∈ (Ω_S \ M)` that correlates with `P`. James's "infinity of aspects" guarantees such `H` always exists. The error is `ΔP = P(P|M,H) - P(P|M)`.

**Singularity/Breakdown:** The reasoning chain `S → M → P` breaks down at the first hop when `S` is so novel that no `φ_M` in the agent's repertoire yields a high `C(φ(S), G)`. This is the "out-of-distribution" problem, where the system either hallucinates a plausible `M` or freezes.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
James's analysis is a direct descendant of Aristotelian syllogistic logic (`All M are P; S is M; therefore S is P`). However, he inverts its epistemic priority. Aristotle treated the major premise as the site of genuine knowledge (`episteme`), while the minor was mere observation. James, aligning with the rising pragmatic movement (Peirce, Dewey), locates the generative spark in the empirical encounter—the "fresh aspects in concrete things." This shifts the locus of intelligence from library-based deduction to field-based perception.

Scientifically, this presages 20th-century information theory. Shannon’s channel coding theorem deals with lossless compression, but human cognition is inherently *lossy*. Barlow’s "efficient coding hypothesis" (1961) posits that sensory systems evolve to remove redundancy, transmitting only the unexpected. James's "mutilation" is this efficient coding in action—the removal of the redundant, background infinity to transmit the signal `M`.

In the lineage of game theory, this connects to von Neumann-Morgenstern utility maximization, where `M` is the chosen state representation upon which a payoff function is evaluated. However, James introduces a crucial metacognitive layer: the choice of `M` itself is a strategic act governed by "sagacity," a meta-skill above simple utility calculation.

**Multi-Week Trap Genealogy:** In football, this creates the "Pre-Season Schema Trap." During extensive practice, coaches install a vast library of major premises (`If they show Cover 3, run Mesh concept`). Players become fluent in these `M⇒P` chains. By Week 4, against an unfamiliar opponent who uses a novel `S` (a hybrid look), the "fertility of learning" is high—they have many `M⇒P` rules—but "sagacity" is low. They force the new `S` into an old, comfortable `M` (e.g., "It looks like Cover 2!"), triggering a well-practiced but incorrect `P`. The error is retrocausal: the past success of the old schema makes it sticky, causing it to overwrite present perception. The trap deepens as losses accumulate; the system doubles down on refining its major premises (more film study) rather than cultivating the sagacity to see new minors.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
James provides a nascent Theory of Mind (ToM) framework: recognizing that others "conceive" of the same object differently based on their goals ("an army commissary picks out... eating... the general [picks out] courage"). A recursive ToM involves modeling another agent's mode of conceiving, then modeling how that agent models your mode of conceiving, etc.

*Level 0 (Self):* I conceive `S` (QB's posture) as `M_passing` (dropping back to pass). My major premise: `M_passing ⇒ Pressure required`. I commit pass rushers.
*Level 1 (Opponent's view of me):* The QB knows I will see his posture. He fakes `M_passing` but his true `M` is `M_rushing` (scramble design). He relies on my Level 0 error.
*Level 2 (My view of his view):* I know the QB will try to fake `M_passing`. So I watch his hips, not his shoulders. I re-conceive `S` as `M_hip_orientation`. New major premise: `M_hips_square ⇒ Rush`.
*Level 3 (His view of my view):* The QB knows sophisticated defenders watch hips. He now trains himself to keep hips quiet even on a pass. His `S` now perfectly mimics `M_passing` at the hip level.

*Crisis Habit Regression:* Under extreme time pressure (<1.5 seconds) and fatigue, the recursive stack collapses. The system drops to the lowest, most automatic level—often Level 0 or even a pre-motor script. A defender, exhausted, might revert to a "see motion, chase motion" script (a primitive `M_motion ⇒ Pursuit` rule) because maintaining a 3-level recursive model demands working memory resources that are depleted. This is the ultimate surrender of "sagacity" to a crude, overlearned "major premise."

*Counter-Trap Strategy:* Elite performers build "schema interrupts." After a forced fumble (a novel `S` that broke everyone's minor premise), the clock stops. This creates a micro-window where the recursive stack can reset. The key is to use that pause not to reinforce old majors, but to explicitly ask: "What did I *fail* to see about `S` just now? What new `M` should I add?" This cultivates sagacity by treating breakdowns as data for expanding the projection space `φ`.
