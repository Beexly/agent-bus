<THINK_TRACE>
To dissect William James’s foundational claim regarding "crude extensity" within the perception of space, we must trace a multi-hop derivation through phenomenology, information theory, and modern computational neuroscience. James posits that sensations inherently carry an "element of voluminousness"—a spatial or extensive quality that varies across sensory modalities. He notes that thunderstorms are more voluminous than squeaks, and thigh contractions feel more massive than eyelid twitches [144]. This qualitative difference is not merely about intensity (loudness or force) but about perceived extension in space.

First, we must differentiate between subjective phenomenal magnitude and objective physical extent. In psychophysics, Fechner’s law relates stimulus intensity to perceived magnitude logarithmically ($P = k \log S$). However, James is referring to something more fundamental—the intrinsic "spread" or "massiveness" of a sensation before it is mapped onto external coordinates. This "crude extensity" can be formalized as the dimensionality of the sensory manifold activated by a stimulus. A simple pinprick activates a small cluster of nociceptors, yielding a low-dimensional sensory vector with minimal spread. Conversely, a warm bath stimulates a vast area of cutaneous thermoreceptors, creating a high-dimensional, spatially distributed activation pattern. This leads us to define *Sensory Extensive Potential* ($E_{\text{sens}}$), which quantifies the topological footprint of a stimulus on the neural substrate.

Second, we examine the cross-modal consistency of this extensity. James contrasts hearing (reverberating thunderstorm vs. slate-pencil) with somatic senses (boil vs. colic). The auditory system encodes space through interaural time differences and spectral cues, yet the "voluminousness" of a thunderstorm is also a function of temporal duration and spectral density. Here, we see an overlap between spatial and temporal extensity—what James calls "voluminousness" might be a unified cognitive metric of "impact volume," integrating both space and time. In information-theoretic terms, this could be modeled as the entropy of the sensory input over a specific integration window. A complex flavor like plum pudding has a higher chemical complexity (more distinct receptor bindings) than a single taste, resulting in a broader activation map across the gustatory cortex. Thus, $E_{\text{sens}} \approx H(X)$, where $H$ is Shannon entropy over the sensory channel $X$.

Third, we address the "litigation" James mentions regarding the muscular sense (proprioception). Does moving a thigh produce a larger "extensive" feeling than twitching an eyelid? Proprioceptive feedback involves muscle spindles and Golgi tendon organs. A thigh contraction engages a massive motor pool and generates significant proprioceptive inflow, suggesting a high $E_{\text{sens}}$. However, others argue muscles only signal position/force, not extension per se. We can resolve this by distinguishing between *efferent copy* (the command size) and *afferent spread* (the sensory feedback). The "feeling" of extensity likely arises from the integrated afferent spread across multiple body regions, not just the contracting muscle.

Finally, we audit the gray zones. James admits smell and taste have less prominent vastness. Why? Because olfactory and gustatory receptors are less spatially organized compared to the retinotopic visual cortex or somatotopic homunculus. Yet he asserts they are not absent. The "heaviness" of musk or tuberose implies a saturation of the olfactory bulb—a high concentration of activated glomeruli. This suggests that even without precise spatial maps, the sheer number of active neurons creates a feeling of "fullness" or "vastness." The breakdown regime occurs when stimuli are so diffuse they lack boundaries (like a subtle background odor), rendering $E_{\text{sens}}$ indeterminate. Furthermore, the singularity arises in conditions like synesthesia or migraine aura, where extensity is hallucinated in non-spatial modalities, decoupling $E_{\text{sens}}$ from physical stimulus geometry entirely.

**I. Sensory Extensive Potential Operator**
We formalize James's "crude extensity" as the *Sensory Extensive Potential* ($E_{\text{sens}}$), representing the topological spread of neural activation triggered by a stimulus $\sigma$ across a sensory modality $\mathcal{M}$:
$$ E_{\text{sens}}(\sigma, \mathcal{M}) = \int_{\Omega_\mathcal{M}} \rho(x, \sigma) \, dx $$
Where:
- $\Omega_\mathcal{M}$ is the topological domain of the sensory manifold (e.g., retinotopic map for sight, homunculus for touch) [144].
- $\rho(x, \sigma)$ is the local density of neural firing rate at location $x$ induced by stimulus $\sigma$, measured in spikes/s/mm².
- **Units**: Effective Neural Area (ENA), expressed in mm² of cortical/subcortical activation volume normalized by baseline spontaneous activity.
- **Assumptions**: The mapping from peripheral receptor field to central representation preserves topological neighborhood relationships (adjacency). It assumes a linear superposition of localized receptive fields.
- **Breakdown Regime**: Breaks down for "non-topological" senses like certain aspects of pain or visceral discomfort where referred pain disrupts $\Omega_\mathcal{M}$. Also fails under anesthesia where $\rho(x) \to 0$ globally despite intense $\sigma$.

**II. Cross-Modal Entropy Approximation**
For modalities lacking precise spatial maps (smell, taste), James notes extensity manifests as "complexity" or "heaviness" [144]. We approximate $E_{\text{sens}}$ via Shannon Entropy of receptor binding patterns:
$$ E_{\text{sens}}^{\text{(olf)}} \approx -\sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i \log_2(p_i) $$
Where:
- $p_i$ is the probability of activation of the $i$-th olfactory receptor type.
- **Units**: Bits of combinatorial chemical space occupied.
- **Assumptions**: Each distinct chemical feature maps to a unique glomerular activation pattern; "heavier" odors (musk, tuberose) activate a broader distribution of $p_i$ [144].
- **Singularity**: Occurs when $p_i$ becomes uniform (maximal entropy), theoretically yielding infinite "vastness" indistinguishable from white noise.

**III. Muscular Proprioceptive Extensity**
Addressing the "litigation" on muscular sense [144], we define extensity via the divergence of efferent and afferent signals:
$$ E_{\text{mus}} = \alpha \cdot N_{\text{motor\_units}} + \beta \cdot \text{Var}(\text{Proprioceptive Feedback}) $$
- **Units**: Dimensionless index scaled by maximal voluntary contraction (MVC) area.
- **Assumptions**: Larger muscle groups (thigh) recruit more motor units ($N_{\text{motor\_units}}$) and generate higher variance in spindle/Golgi feedback compared to small muscles (eyelid).

**Citation Anchors**:
- "In the sensations of hearing, touch, sight, and pain we are accustomed to distinguish... the element of voluminousness." [144]

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
James’s formulation of "crude extensity" emerges from a specific historical nexus: the late 19th-century debate between nativism (Kantian spatial intuition) and empiricism (associationism). Against T.H. Green and British Idealists who argued space was a pure conceptual construct, James—and contemporaries like J.G. Frazer and R.L. Hodgson—argued that spatial qualities were inherent in raw sensation ("sense-datum"). This lineage is crucial because it positions "voluminousness" not as a cognitive inference, but as an immediate pre-cognitive property of qualia [144].

Scientifically, this genealogy connects to early psychophysics. Fechner had quantified sensation, but James pushed further into the *phenomenal geometry* of those sensations. His distinction between the "fine cobweb" of neuralgia and the "heavy soreness of a boil" anticipates modern multidimensional scaling of pain, where patients rate pain not just by intensity but by spatial diffusion. 

The "multi-week trap" here is the assumption that extensity is purely exteroceptive (about the outside world). James explicitly includes interoception (dizziness/semi-circular canals, colic, lumbago) and proprioception (muscle contraction) [144]. A trap for modern analysts is to dismiss his "litigation" on muscular sense as obsolete. However, modern predictive coding theories echo this exact dispute: does the brain simulate spatial extension based on efference copies (motor commands), or does it derive extension strictly from the convergence of multisensory afferents? James’s refusal to "adjourn its consideration" without admitting the thigh/eyelid asymmetry suggests he intuitively grasped what we now call *active inference*—that acting upon the world (contracting a massive thigh) generates a rich, voluminous sensory prediction error that feels intrinsically extensive. Ignoring this retrocausal link traps one in a passive-receiver model of perception, missing the embodied nature of "voluminousness."

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
When facing a crisis of disorientation (e.g., severe vertigo, dissociation, or sensory deprivation), humans often regress to primitive, somatic anchors to re-establish a sense of existence. James’s taxonomy provides a perfect map of these regression vectors. A person experiencing acute anxiety (a "crisis habit") might instinctively clench their jaw or curl their toes. This is a bid for *minimal extensity*—focusing on the "twitch of an eyelid or some small muscle in the face" [144] to create a sharp, bounded focal point against the overwhelming "vast discomfort of a colic" or the formlessness of panic.

Conversely, in states of grief or existential dread, individuals often seek *maximal extensity*. They might immerse themselves in a hot bath ("gives our skin a more massive feeling") or consume heavy, complex foods ("plum pudding") [144]. This is a counter-trap mechanism: flooding the sensory manifold with high-volume, high-entropy inputs to prevent the mind from collapsing into a void. The "clock" here is the metabolic and neural fatigue associated with maintaining these states. One cannot indefinitely sustain the hyper-arousal required to process "heavy odors like musk" or the intense proprioceptive load of constant muscle tension. Eventually, the system seeks homeostasis, forcing a retreat from extreme extensity back toward neutral baselines. Recognizing whether an individual is spiraling into micro-focus (cobweb pain) or macro-overwhelm (noonday sky) allows for precise intervention, predicting when the cognitive load will hit its singularity threshold and trigger a shutdown or a psychotic break.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Perceptual judgments of "voluminousness" are frequently overridden by hard physical, biomechanical, and environmental constraints—a veto mechanism that proves James’s extensity is grounded in reality, not pure idealism.

*Physical/Biomechanical Veto*: Consider the sensation of a "little neuralgic pain, fine as a cobweb." While subjectively intense, its spatial extensity is physically capped by the anatomical diameter of the trigeminal nerve branch involved. No amount of psychological amplification can make this pain feel "vast" in the way a lumbago does, because the biological generator (the nerve root) lacks the volumetric capacity to recruit widespread myofascial chains. The biomechanical architecture of the nervous system imposes a hard ceiling on $E_{\text{sens}}$. Similarly, the "squeaking of a slate-pencil" is a high-frequency, narrow-band acoustic event. The physics of sound wave interference and the limited surface area of the chalk-slate interface physically limit the reverberative potential, preventing it from ever achieving the "voluminousness" of a thunderstorm, regardless of how loudly one imagines it.

*Weather/Environmental Veto*: Atmospheric pressure and humidity act as vetoes on extensity. On a muggy, high-humidity day, the "warm bath" sensation James describes might be passively induced across the entire body by the environment alone. The air itself feels "massive." Conversely, in a freezing, dry wind, even a feverish internal inflammation might feel sharply localized rather than diffuse, as vasoconstriction limits the spread of inflammatory mediators. The environment physically dictates the boundary conditions for the bodily feeling of space.

*Proof of Adjudication*: If we attempt to cognitively override these vetoes—for instance, by trying to imagine the slate-pencil squeak as having the spatial vastness of a symphony—we fail at the level of raw sensation. The *image* may be vast, but the actual auditory qualia remains pinpointed. The "crude extensity" is resistant to top-down modulation when the underlying physical/biomechanical drivers are too constrained to support a broad neural activation map.
