$$\varepsilon_t=\Big\{\,e_k \;\Big|\; t-\tfrac{t_{acc}}{2}<t_k\le t+\tfrac{t_{acc}}{2}\,\Big\},\qquad \varepsilon_t\in\mathbb{R}^{|\varepsilon_t|\times4}.$$
$t_{acc}$ [µs] is the accumulation window; $t$ [µs] the reference time. Breakdown: $t_{acc}\to 0^+$ yields $|\varepsilon_t|\to 0$ (empty matrix, no motion detected); $t_{acc}\to\infty$ smears temporally separated gestures into one packet, destroying impact localization.

DVS generative model (consistent with Gallego et al. [4]): with log-irradiance $L=\ln I$ (dimensionless contrast units),
$$\Delta L(x,y,t_k)=L(x,y,t_k)-L(x,y,t_k-\Delta t),\quad \text{fire} \iff |\Delta L|\ge\vartheta,\quad p_k=\mathrm{sign}(\Delta L).$$
$\vartheta$ is the dimensionless contrast sensitivity (typical $\sim0.15$).

Event rate ([PAGE 5]), SI unit events·s⁻¹:
$$r(t)=|\varepsilon_t|\cdot\frac{10^{6}\ \mu s\cdot s^{-1}}{t_{acc}\ [\mu s]}.$$

Swing statistics over $n_\varepsilon$ consecutive packets:
$$\bar r_j=\frac{1}{n_\varepsilon}\sum_{i=j-n_\varepsilon+1}^{j} r_i,\qquad s_j^2=\frac{1}{n_\varepsilon-1}\sum_{i=j-n_\varepsilon+1}^{j}(r_i-\bar r_j)^2.$$
Detection logic ([PAGE 5–6]):
$$t_{start}=\min\{\,t_j:\ \bar r_j>\tau_{mean}\ \wedge\ s_j^2>\tau_{var}\,\},$$
$$t_{end}=\min\{\,t_j>t_{start}+\tau_t:\ \bar r_j<\tau_{mean}\,\}.$$
Units: $\tau_{mean}$ [events·s⁻¹], $\tau_{var}$ [events²·s⁻²], $\tau_t$ [µs]. Consistency check with Table 1 ([PAGE 13]): $\tau_{mean}=1\times10^{7}$ s⁻¹ over a $t_{acc}=500$ µs window $\Rightarrow |\varepsilon_t|\approx \tau_{mean}\cdot t_{acc}/10^6 = 5000$ events/packet at threshold; $\tau_{var}=6\times10^{11}$ s⁻² $\Rightarrow \sigma\approx7.75\times10^{5}$ s⁻¹; $\tau_t=100000$ µs $=100$ ms guard.

Packet split, Eqs. (2)–(4) ([PAGE 6]):
$$\varepsilon_{prev}=\{e_k\in\varepsilon_t\mid t_k<t\},\quad \varepsilon_{next}=\{e_k\in\varepsilon_t\mid t\le t_k\},\quad \varepsilon^{rev}_{next}=\mathrm{reverse}(\varepsilon_{next}).$$

Event images, Eqs. (5)–(6): $F_{prev}=\mathrm{image}(\varepsilon_{prev})$, $F_{next}=\mathrm{image}(\varepsilon^{rev}_{next})$, each pixel $\in\{+1,-1,0\}$; the split by polarity gives $F^{+}_{prev}$ and $F^{-}_{next}$.

Time surfaces + focal remap, Eqs. (7)–(10): $T_\bullet=\mathrm{timeSurface}(\cdot)\in[0,1]^{H\times W}$ (oldest→0, newest→1), then $G_\bullet=\mathrm{focalTime}(T_\bullet)$. The selected Pattern 3 focal kernel (triangular, [PAGE 9]) on normalized surface value $\tau$:
$$\phi_3(\tau)=\begin{cases}2\tau,&0\le\tau\le \tfrac12,\\[2pt]2(1-\tau),&\tfrac12<\tau\le1.\end{cases}$$
(For contrast: Pattern 1 $\phi_1\equiv1$ = event image; Pattern 2 $\phi_2(\tau)=\tau$ = time surface.)

PATS collision integral, Eq. (11) ([PAGE 8]):
$$\rho_t=\sum_{x,y}\Big(\,\big|F^{+}_{prev}*G_{prev}\big| * \big|F^{-}_{next}*G_{next}\big|\Big)(x,y),\qquad \rho_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.$$
$*$ is 2-D convolution; inner convolutions produce $(2H-1)\times(2W-1)$ planes whose cross-correlation peaks at zero shift (spatial coincidence of pre-+ and post-− events); outer $\sum$ yields scalar $\rho_t$. Singular regime: if $F^{+}_{prev}\equiv0$ (dark ball, polarity inversion) or $F^{-}_{next}\equiv0$, then $\rho_t\equiv0\ \forall t$ — no peak exists. Estimation: $\hat t_{imp}=\arg\max_t(-\nabla^2 \rho_t)$, i.e. most-negative Laplacian ([PAGE 8]).

Contour geometry ([PAGE 10]): binary maps $B^{b}_{t_{imp}},B^{r}_{t_{imp}}\in\{0,1\}$; morphological closing then $\mathrm{fitEllipse}\to(\text{center},\,(2a,2b),\,\theta)$. Ball = largest ball-binary ellipse inside frame; racket inner boundary = second-largest racket-binary ellipse. Output ratio: ball center projected onto racket ellipse axes $(u,v)$, with $+u$ up-shaft and $+v$ toward tip; error budget 15 mm $\approx$ ¼ ball diameter (nominal $D\approx60$ mm) per Yasuda et al. [26].

Contact-time constant (Cross [2], via [PAGE 8,12]): $\tau_{contact}\approx4000$ µs; string-flicker transient $\approx100$ µs — the two-scale separation the focal kernel exploits.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage has three tributaries that converge in PATS.

Silicon-retina root. The event camera descends from Mahowald–Mead analog retina work and Delbrück's DVS, formalized in the field's canonical survey, Gallego et al. [4] ([PAGE 4], cited for event representations). The intermediate representations the paper leans on are themselves a lineage: the event image (Prophesee Metavision [19]), the time surface (Lagorce et al., HOTS [12]; Sironi et al., HATS [22]). Kase et al. inherit the time surface and then *synthesize* a new operator (focalTime + reversal) — the contribution is explicitly "an original event-based processing method to detect the timing at impact... (PATS)" ([PAGE 3]).

Sports-instrumentation root. Prior impact localization split into two families ([PAGE 3]): IMU-in-racket + FFT matched to a per-racket database (Yamashita & Matsunaga [25]), and stationary high-speed-camera + Hough-transform devices for golf (Kiraly & Merloti [10]; Kiraly & Wintriss [11]), plus image-subtraction thresholding and high-speed-camera + Doppler-radar triggering (Tuxen [24]). The paper's argument is that golf's fixed-impact assumption fails for tennis where "the impact location may vary and the player's body may enter the frame" ([PAGE 3]). The high-speed-camera memory wall ([PAGE 2]) is the practical antagonist: memory scales with shutter speed, capping capture duration.

Ball-dynamics root. Cross [2] supplies the 4 ms contact-time constant that calibrates both the metric tolerance (2000 µs, [PAGE 12]) and the flicker-rejection window. Naß et al. [15] and Ikenaga et al. [7] supply the biomechanics motivation: restitution coefficient varies across the string bed, so impact location is a real performance variable ([PAGE 1]).

Multi-week traps — research programs that consume effort without advancing the tennis-specific goal:
1. High-frame-rate capture arms race: raising shutter/frame rate to freeze the ball linearly inflates storage and forces short captures — the exact bottleneck the event camera is meant to dissolve, but it is seductive because it is incremental.
2. Manual digitization scaling (Karditsas [9], [PAGE 2]): accurate but "time-consuming... prone to human error"; each added camera/view multiplies labeling cost.
3. Event representation proliferation: chasing richer representations (voxel grids, learned embeddings) instead of the minimal event image + time surface that the physics of +→− transition actually requires. The paper deliberately stays lean.
4. Doppler/radar triggering: adds hardware and calibration for a timing problem the polarity asymmetry already encodes optically.

The retro-causal read: the authors converge on PATS precisely by *not* taking these traps — they exploit that a single event camera already encodes the impact signature, so no radar, no database, no full-frame storage is needed.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model the embedded agent: a coach/analyst who needs feedback *inside* a rally. There are three nested minds to reason about — the analyst, the incumbent tooling, and the confounder (sunlight/flicker).

Crisis habit regression. Under the time pressure of live coaching, the analyst's prior is the high-speed-camera + manual-digitization workflow ([PAGE 2]). It is locally rational: precise, familiar, defensible. But its clock constant is fatal — capture duration is bounded by memory, and digitization is post-hoc. Under crisis (need feedback now), the agent regresses to this habit even though it cannot deliver in-rally feedback. The recursive prediction: I expect the analyst to *over-trust* frame-based precision and *under-trust* a 15 mm event-camera estimate, because the former reports confidently on a short window and the latter admits a coarser, distributed error.

Counter-trap (the move that beats the habit). PATS + the 2-second compute ([PAGE 14]: 1.41–1.97 s per instance) is the counter-trap: "shorter than the time taken for a rally" ([PAGE 14]), so it satisfies the in-rally clock the incumbent cannot. The system anticipates the analyst's real objective (immediate, player-specific feedback) rather than the stated metric (mm-precision on one swing).

The clock as an adversary-timing model. The relevant clock is $\tau_{contact}\approx4000$ µs versus rally inter-shot intervals (seconds). PATS must resolve the 4 ms event *and* finish before the next shot. It wins the first by microsecond timestamping and the second by sub-2 s compute, but the design tension is the focal-window length: too short admits flicker (100 µs transients can't be separated if the kernel is too narrow in the wrong band), too long admits swing-onset frame overlap (the failure guarded by the $n_c$-candidate + centroid rule, [PAGE 8]).

Confounder modeling (sunlight). The most sophisticated ToM move is anticipating the *environment's* counter-strategy. Sunlight is not random noise; it is a structured adversary that manufactures string-flicker events at the same pixels as ball events. The authors model it and split their defenses: PATS's focal kernel defeats it for *timing* ("PATS was effective even under direct sunlight conditions," [PAGE 14]), but concedes the *contour* step ("only 3 out of 20 instances were detected" under sun, [PAGE 13]). This is a calibrated theory-of-mind over a physical adversary: claim victory where the mechanism holds (timing), flag defeat where it does not (contour). A weaker agent would either over-claim (assume PATS solves everything) or abandon the method entirely on seeing the 3/20 — the paper does neither, which is the mark of correct recursive reasoning about a structured confounder.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

I prove three physical/biomechanical/weather vetoes and their domains.

Veto 1 — Polarity-inversion (physical). The PATS integral (Eq. 11) requires $F^{+}_{prev}\not\equiv0$ and $F^{-}_{next}\not\equiv0$. The generative model gives $p_k=\mathrm{sign}(\Delta L)$. If the ball is *darker* than the background (dark ball, bright sky/court), the inbound transition is −, not +, contradicting the stated assumption "the ball is brighter than the background" ([PAGE 6]). Then $F^{+}_{prev}\equiv0\Rightarrow\rho_t\equiv0\ \forall t$. Proof of veto: no admissible $t_{imp}$ exists; the arg-max is undefined. Mitigation is trivial (swap the polarity roles) but the *as-published* operator vetoes on inverted contrast. Domain: contrast sign of ball vs background.

Veto 2 — Weather/photon-flux (sunlight). Under direct sun, string-flicker reflection injects events at ball-coincident pixels ([PAGE 15]: "the simultaneous occurrence of events related to both string flickering caused by sunlight reflection and the ball, which makes it difficult to detect the contours of the ball in the binary image"). For timing, the focal kernel exploits $\tau_{flicker}\approx100\ \mu s \ll \tau_{contact}\approx4000\ \mu s$ ([PAGE 8]) — this is why timing held. For contours, there is no temporal separation available in a single accumulated event image: the binary map $B^{b}_{t_{imp}}$ fuses flicker + ball into one blob, and $\mathrm{fitEllipse}$ either misses or fits a spurious ellipse larger than the ball, so the "largest ellipse inside frame" heuristic latches onto flicker. Quantitative proof from the paper's own data: contour success falls from 24/26 (no sun) to 3/20 (sun), i.e. a $\ge 77\%$ collapse — a decisive veto for the contour layer, while timing (46/49) is unaffected. Domain: illumination contrast ratio / specular reflection geometry.

Veto 3 — Biomechanical threshold (spatial resolution vs ball size). The permissible error is 15 mm ≈ ¼ ball diameter ([PAGE 13–14], Yasuda et al. [26]). At standoff 2.7 m with a zoom lens ([PAGE 11]) on an HD sensor, one pixel subtends some angle $\delta$; the impact-center localization error scales as $\sim k\delta\cdot d$ where $d=2.7$ m. The paper reports success cases kept "<12.1 %pt for u and 9.1 %pt for v" ([PAGE 13]), i.e. within the ¼-diameter budget. The veto triggers if standoff increases without compensating focal length (error grows linearly in $d$) or if swing speed rises so that within $t_{acc}=2000$ µs (ball) / 500 µs (racket) the ball's ellipse smears beyond a fittable shape. Domain: geometry (standoff × angular resolution) and speed-induced motion smear.

Adjudication summary: timing layer is robust to weather (Veto 2 spares it) but conditional on contrast sign (Veto 1); contour layer is robust to geometry within budget (Veto 3 satisfied) but decisively vetoed by sunlight (Veto 2). The net permissible operating envelope is: bright-ball-on-contrasting-background, controlled/overcast illumination, standoff ≲2.7 m with matched optics, swing speeds within the tuned $t_{acc}$ windows.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three falsifiable (verifiable-reward) scenarios, each with a pass/fail prediction testable against a replication of the paper's pipeline.

Veto A — Contrast inversion (dark ball on bright sky).
Intervention: replace the bright ball with a matte black ball against an overcast-white sky so the ball is *darker* than background.
Predicted observable: $F^{+}_{prev}\equiv0$ under the published polarity convention, so $\rho_t\equiv0$ and no Laplacian extremum exists.
Verifiable reward: run steps I–II on $\ge10$ swings. Pass condition for the veto (i.e., method fails): timing success $=0/10$ as published, OR $\ge8/10$ if the authors' hidden polarity-swap is applied. Falsifier: if the unmodified operator still localizes impact within 2000 µs on a dark ball, the polarity-inversion argument is wrong.

Veto B — Speed-induced swing-window mistuning.
Intervention: increase ball-machine feed rate and player swing speed by ~1.5× while keeping Table 1 constants ($t_{acc}=500$, $n_\varepsilon=10$, $\tau_{mean}=10^7$, $\tau_{var}=6\times10^{11}$).
Predicted observable: the fixed $\tau_{mean}/\tau_{var}$ thresholds, tuned on 10 training swings, no longer sit in the correct quantile of the event-rate distribution; $t_{start}/t_{end}$ either miss the impact or overextend, reproducing the player-4/5 overestimation tail ([PAGE 13]) at a higher rate.
Verifiable reward: Step I success. Pass condition for the veto: Step I success drops below the reported 49/50 (98%), specifically $\le 80\%$, without re-tuning. Falsifier: Step I success $\ge 95\%$ at 1.5× speed with frozen constants ⇒ thresholds are speed-robust and the veto fails.

Veto C — Multi-color / specular equipment (contour event loss).
Intervention: replace the "white racket frame and black polyester strings" ([PAGE 12]) with a two-tone (e.g., half-white/half-black) string bed, still under overcast (no direct sun).
Predicted observable: [PAGE 15] states "using equipment with multiple colors may result in event loss at the boundaries of the colors, complicating contour detection." At the color boundary $\Delta L\to0$, so few events fire, the ball ellipse fragments, and $\mathrm{fitEllipse}$ on $B^{b}_{t_{imp}}$ fails.
Verifiable reward: Step III contour success under overcast. Pass condition for the veto: contour success $\le$ 24/26 baseline drops to $\le 50\%$ even *without* sunlight, isolating the equipment (not weather) as cause. Falsifier: contour success stays $\ge 90\%$ under overcast with two-tone strings ⇒ the boundary-loss claim is overstated for this configuration.

Each veto is decidable from a single replication table (timing success, Step I rate, contour rate) and thus carries a clean verifiable reward, satisfying RLVR-style adjudication without recourse to unobservable priors.
