<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s exposition sits at a crucial juncture in the history of psychology, bridging classical empiricism and Gestalt theory while prefiguring modern embodied cognition. Historically, the primary/secondary quality distinction traces back to John Locke, who argued that primary qualities (solidity, extension, motion) exist in objects themselves, whereas secondary qualities (colors, sounds) are mind-dependent. Tolman repurposes this epistemological divide for behavioral science: he treats primary qualities not as metaphysical realities but as *behaviorally functional*—those stimulus dimensions that reliably cue manipulatory actions.

The lineage flows through Wundt’s voluntarism and Köhler’s Gestalt principles, where perception is seen as intrinsically purposeful. Tolman’s “purposive behaviorism” directly challenges the S-R mechanists (Watson, Thorndike) by insisting that intervening variables (maps, expectations) mediate between stimulus and response. His analysis of discriminanda/manipulanda is an early form of *affordance theory*—though James J. Gibson would coin the term decades later. For Tolman, the rat’s interpretation of a chair is not a mistake but a valid, organism-relative construction of reality.

A multi-week trap arises from misreading Tolman as a dualist. He is not positing separate mental and physical realms; rather, he is arguing for a *structural isomorphism* between environmental relations and cognitive maps. Another trap is conflating his “manipulanda” with simple responses. Manipulanda are potentialities embedded in the situation, awaiting actualization by the agent. Modern AI researchers often fall into the trap of treating all sensorimotor loops as equivalent, missing Tolman’s insight that some loops (tactile-key) are *constitutionally tighter* than others (visual-color), leading to vastly different learning rates and robustness profiles.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

If the feedback loop latency increases (e.g., due to neuromuscular fatigue), the system degrades from skilled automation to conscious effort, increasing error rates. Secondary discriminanda like color remain decoupled from this core loop because they do not participate in the comparator function. Thus, the mind is modeled as a hierarchy of nested controllers, each tuned to specific discriminandum-manipulandum pairings based on evolutionary and developmental priors.
