<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind concerns what coaches believe about players, what coaches believe players believe about themselves, and how bio-banding intervenes in that recursion.

Crisis habit regression: Under selection pressure, academy practitioners regress to habitual heuristics—selecting earlier maturers because transient stature and strength advantages are visible, salient, and interpretable as "current ability" ([PAGE 5], [PAGE 17]). This is a crisis habit: when the talent-identification clock is tight (contract decisions at 18 years; [PAGE 22], ref. 4), practitioners overweight proximate physical performance and underweight latent technical potential in later maturers.

Counter-trap: Bio-banding is the designed counter-trap. By grouping pre-, circa-, and post-PHV players separately, it removes the overt physical asymmetry that triggers the crisis heuristic, theoretically forcing practitioners to attend to technical and tactical behavior instead ([PAGE 5], [PAGE 7]).

Clock: The biological clock keeps running. Across the three-week study, players age; across a season, they traverse PHV. A player classified "pre-PHV" in week 1 may be "circa-PHV" by week 3, and equation choice reshuffles categories ([PAGE 9]). Thus the counter-trap is only temporarily stable; the practitioner's mental model must be continuously re-calibrated as the same individual moves across maturity bands.

Recursive loop: Coaches observe players in bio-banded SSGs and update their talent mental models. The study finds very few consistent technical/tactical differences between maturity groups in matched or mis-matched fixtures ([PAGE 4], [PAGE 13], [PAGE 15]). If the counter-trap fails to produce separable signals, the coach's recursive model receives corrupted data: a late maturer who performs indistinguishably from an early maturer in a constrained 52.6 m²-per-player pitch may still be downgraded because the coach's prior (physical maturity predicts future senior performance) is not falsified by the observation. Conversely, an early maturer whose physical dominance is suppressed by the small pitch may be overlooked despite latent technical quality. The theory-of-mind failure is mutual: players model coaches as maturity-biased selectors, and coaches model players as fixed maturity categories, while both ignore that the pitch constraint itself has flattened the signal space. The practical implication is that bio-banding, without concurrent coach education about the suppressed physical expression, may not update recursive beliefs at all; it merely changes the grouping label while the underlying selection heuristic persists.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Biomechanical/measurement layer veto: GPS data were collected at 10 Hz with an Optimeye X4 MEMS unit, resampled to remove gaps, smoothed with a 3 Hz Butterworth low-pass filter, and converted to UTM coordinates ([PAGE 11]). The Nyquist frequency is 5 Hz; sharp cutting and acceleration transients in soccer can approach or exceed this band, so the filter and resampling risk attenuating high-frequency positioning variance that SEI and nearest-neighbor distances depend on. [PAGE 18] cites Malone et al. [54] on GPS measurement error and Olthof et al. [55] on match-to-match tactical variability, and acknowledges broad credible intervals (0.0 to 1.49; [PAGE 18]). Veto: measurement noise and temporal smoothing can render genuine tactical differences statistically invisible, inflating HDIs and suppressing pd values below decision thresholds.

Weather/environmental layer veto: Matches were outdoors on a 3G surface ([PAGE 7]), but no weather controls (wind, precipitation, temperature) are reported. GPS satellite signal strength was required ([PAGE 11]), implying outdoor multipath and atmospheric error sources were present but unmodeled. Because tactical metrics derive from coordinate differences over 5-minute windows, unmeasured environmental variance (e.g., wind affecting ball flight and player positioning) is aliased into the maturity-group contrast. Veto: uncontrolled outdoor conditions contribute non-biological variance to the dependent variables, weakening causal attribution to bio-banding.

Biological layer veto: Khamis & Roche EASA carries 2.1% measurement error versus criterion skeletal maturity ([PAGE 9]); Fransen YPHV relies on a Belgian longitudinal normative sample ([PAGE 9]). Eight players changed categories between methods ([PAGE 9]), demonstrating that the "maturity group" independent variable is itself noisy. Veto: misclassification error in the grouping variable biases estimated group differences toward the null (attenuation), which is consistent with the observed scarcity of consistent technical/tactical effects ([PAGE 13], [PAGE 15]).

Adjudication: The veto stack is coherent. Physical, biomechanical, and environmental constraints jointly suppress the very maturity-related variance that bio-banding is intended to isolate. The few significant effects that survive (post-PHV distance to nearest teammate and centroid; SEI for circa-PHV matched groups; [PAGE 12]) are concentrated where density constraints are least binding or where the signal is strongest, supporting the authors' conclusion that restricted pitch size may be the actual attenuator of maturity effects ([PAGE 17]).
