These veto points prove that James's idealized "sliding ruler" is constantly challenged by biological and physical inefficiencies.

The primary forebear is Hermann von Helmholtz, whose *Physiologische Optik* (referenced in the footnote) established the doctrine of *unconscious inference*. This multi-week trap involves the persistence of the Heringian intuition among physiologists who could not easily measure visual processing without appealing to musculature, causing a decades-long stalemate in understanding spatial cognition until behavioral psychology eventually validated the learning-based models.

This triad—Stuff, Ruler, Operation—constructs space dynamically rather than statically.

However, James deduces that they are "devoid of exact space-import per se." Why? Because without a common reference frame, a specific ocular movement might correspond to varying physical distances depending on focus and eccentricity. Therefore, the brain must perform a comparative operation: superposition.
The derivation hinges on the symmetry of comparison. We compare two retinal paths by dragging one object across them (calibrating the eye); conversely, we compare two external objects by dragging the same retinal point across them (calibrating the world). This bidirectional superposition reveals that neither the internal motor command nor the external static image possesses intrinsic length. Length emerges *only* from the successful alignment of the two over time.

The eye rotates about a center; thus, natural oculomotor trajectories form arcs on the sphere of regard. Deviating from these "easy" lines introduces complex torsional components that break the simple superposition model. Consequently, our intuition of "straight lines" is actually a projection of these privileged rotational axes. The "gray zone" arises when we try to imagine straight lines in the periphery far from these axes—the breakdown of the superposition heuristic leads to the known distortions in peripheral spatial judgment. The ultimate conclusion is that spatial geometry is an artifact of the mechanical coupling between eye-rotation and object-persistence, forged through the necessity of superposition.
