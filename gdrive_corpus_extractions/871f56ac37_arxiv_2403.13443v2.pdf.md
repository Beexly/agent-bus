<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

All operators below are transcribed from the DRIVE SOURCE with explicit units and cited pages. Dimensional bookkeeping: Λ(·) carries units of m² in BEV space and m³ in 3D space (PAGE 3); every IoU-family quantity is dimensionless; θ_vm carries units of m; σ, c_t, s_t, CGR are dimensionless; latencies are in ms; throughput in s⁻¹ (FPS).

**O1 — Generalized IoU (baseline affinity).** [PAGE 3, Eq. 1]

$$\mathrm{gIoU}(B_i,B_j)=\frac{\Lambda(B_i\cap B_j)}{\Lambda(B_i\cup B_j)}+\frac{\Lambda(B_i\cup B_j)}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{Hull}}(B_i,B_j)}-1$$

Assumptions: simple polyhedral boxes, finite vertex sets. Breakdown regime: cost is dominated by the hull term — Table II (PAGE 6) attributes 83.0 ms of the 111.3 ms total to "Convex," i.e. 74.6% of the metric cost is convex-hull construction, not overlap.

**O2 — Alignment operator.** [PAGE 3, Eq. 2]

$$\mathrm{A\text{-}gIoU}(B_i,B_j)=\frac{\Lambda(B_i^{A}\cap B_j^{A})}{\Lambda(B_i^{A}\cup B_j^{A})}+\frac{\Lambda(B_i^{A}\cup B_j^{A})}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{Hull}}(B_i^{A},B_j^{A})}-1,\qquad B^{A}=\mathcal{A}(B)$$

with $\mathcal{A}$ the yaw-collapsing map, $B^A$ defined by "the top-left and bottom-right points $p\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 4}$ on the ground plane" [PAGE 3]. Derivable footprint inflation: for half-extents $(l/2,w/2)$ and yaw $\psi$,

$$\rho(\psi)=\frac{\Lambda(\mathcal{A}(B))}{\Lambda(B)}=\frac{(l|\cos\psi|+w|\sin\psi|)(l|\sin\psi|+w|\cos\psi|)}{l\,w},\quad \rho\ge 1$$

Singularity set: $\rho=1 \iff \psi\in\frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{Z}$ (exact). Worst case: $\rho_{\max}=(l+w)^2/(2lw)$ at $\psi=\pi/4+\frac{\pi}{2}\mathbb{Z}$; for a square footprint $\rho_{\max}=2$; for the Car aspect ratio implied by PAGE 3's footnote, $\rho_{\max}\approx 2.39$. **Breakdown regime:** high aspect ratio × diagonal heading (turning vehicles in intersections) → maximal spurious overlap; this is the mechanistic source of the IDS rise 232→342 in Table V [PAGE 7].

**O3 — Cost Gap Ratio.** [PAGE 4, Eq. 3]

$$\mathrm{CGR}=\frac{\min(C_{um})-C_m}{\max(C_{um})-C_m}\in(0,1]$$

Assumptions: one tracklet, one true match, ≥1 false candidate; outliers with $C_m\ge\min(C_{um})$ excluded (≤3% of data). Derived stability radius for the assignment: the true match survives any bounded cost perturbation $\|\Delta C\|_\infty$ satisfying

$$\|\Delta C\|_\infty < \tfrac{1}{2}\big(\min C_{um}-C_m\big)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{CGR}\cdot\big(\max C_{um}-C_m\big)$$

**Singularity:** CGR → 0⁺ as $C_m\to\min C_{um}$ — the bipartite graph becomes degenerate and any tie-breaking rule, not evidence, decides the match.

**O4 — Score refinement (noisy-OR lifecycle).** [PAGE 4, Eq. 4]

$$\text{Predict: } s_{t,t-1}=\sigma\,s_{t-1};\qquad \text{Update: } s_t=1-(1-s_{t,t-1})(1-c_t)$$

Units: dimensionless probabilities; $\sigma$ "empirically ≤0.7" [PAGE 4]. Under conditional independence of survival-evidence and detection-evidence, $s_t$ is a probability of existence. Closed-form fixed point under stationary detection score $c$:

$$s^{*}=\frac{c}{1-\sigma(1-c)}$$

Pure-coasting half-life (setting $c_t=0$): $s_t=\sigma^{k}s_{t-k}$, hence $t_{1/2}=\ln(1/2)/\ln\sigma$ frames. With the per-category $\sigma$ from PAGE 6: Car 0.5 → 1.00 frame (0.50 s at 2 Hz); Moto/Ped 0.6 → 1.36 frames (0.68 s); Bus 0.3 → 0.58 frames; Tra 0.5 → 1.00; Tru 0.2 → 0.43; Bic 0.1 → 0.30 frames. Waymo at 10 Hz: Ped 0.7 → 1.94 frames = 0.194 s; Veh 0.6 → 1.36 frames = 0.136 s; Cyc 0.1 → 0.30 frames = 0.030 s.

**Termination clock.** With online average score the survival condition $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{T}s_0\sigma^{k}\ge\theta_{dl}$ gives

$$\frac{s_0\,\sigma\,(1-\sigma^{T})}{T\,(1-\sigma)}\;\ge\;\theta_{dl}$$

Solving from $s_0=1$: Car ($\sigma=0.5,\theta_{dl}=0.04$) → $T^{*}\approx 25$ frames ≈ 12.5 s of coasting; Ped ($\sigma=0.6,\theta_{dl}=0.1$) → $T^{*}\approx 15$ frames ≈ 7.5 s; Bus ($\sigma=0.3,\theta_{dl}=0.1$) → $T^{*}\approx 4.3$ frames ≈ 2.2 s. **Breakdown regime:** when $T^{*}$ exceeds the count-based max-age, the score clock is inert and the system silently reverts to rigid count logic — the exact rigidity the module was introduced to remove.

**O5 — Voxel mask (Poisson gate).** [PAGE 5, Eq. 5]

$$\text{Coarse: } M_t=\big(\|B_1^{xyz}-B_2^{xyz}\|_2\le\theta_{vm}\big);\qquad \text{Fine: } C_t=1-\mathrm{Aff}(M_t,B_1,B_2)$$

$\theta_{vm}$ in m; $\|\cdot\|_2$ is a 2-norm in m. Under a homogeneous BEV Poisson intensity $\nu$ [m⁻²], the retention probability is $p=1-e^{-\nu\pi\theta_{vm}^{2}}$. At $\theta_{vm}=3$ m, $\pi\theta_{vm}^2=28.27$ m²; with $\nu=10^{-2}$ m⁻², $p\approx 0.246$ (≈75% of pairs pruned). At $\theta_{vm}=1$ m, $p\approx 0.031$ (≈97% pruned). **Breakdown regime:** $p\to 0$ as $\theta_{vm}\to 0$ — candidate space collapses below the prediction-residual scale, producing false negatives; empirically confirmed PAGE 8: "The pronounced decrease in θvm at 1 stems from erroneous matches induced by artificially narrowing the matching space." **Correctness precondition:** the gate is sound only post-prediction, because the gated quantity must be the innovation residual $\|e_t\|_2$, not inter-frame displacement $v\Delta t$ (at 2 Hz and 10 m/s, $v\Delta t=5$ m $>\theta_{vm}=3$ m).

**O6 — Lightweight filter for time-invariant states.** [PAGE 5, Eq. 6]

$$\text{Predict: } X^{ti}_{t,t-1}=X^{ti}_{t-1};\qquad \text{Update: } X^{ti}_{t}=\mathrm{LW\text{-}Filter}(X^{ti}_{t,t-1},D^{i}_{t})$$

with $X^{ti}=(z,w,l,h)$ in m, $l_{lw}=3$ [PAGE 5]. Implemented as a median filter, so the sample breakdown point is $\lfloor (l_{lw}-1)/2\rfloor/l_{lw}=1/3$ — it tolerates exactly one contaminated observation in three. Asymptotic variance inflation versus a mean at Gaussian noise is $\pi/2\approx1.571$ (57% efficiency loss); this is the mechanistic reading of PAGE 7: "the inner limited-step sliding window discards long-term temporal information for time-invariant states, slightly impacting estimation quality and overall tracking accuracy (-0.1% MOTA)." **Breakdown regime:** two consecutive corrupt observations inside the window flip the median entirely — precisely the occlusion/FN pattern the module is meant to absorb.

**O7 — Parallelization (Amdahl reduction).** [PAGE 5, PAGE 7] The reported gain, "improves the inference speed by 8.2% (-3.1ms)," corresponds to $37.7\to34.6$ ms in Table V. Inverting $S=1/(1-p+p/2)$ gives an implied parallelizable fraction $p\approx0.164$ — i.e. only ~16% of frame time is pre-processing. **Breakdown regime:** as the detector's share of the budget grows, $p\to0$ and the speedup saturates at unity; the serial 60% EKF share (PAGE 6) is untouched by this operator.

$$\Delta t(N)\approx \alpha N+\beta\,N^{2}\,\nu\pi\theta_{vm}^{2}$$

so $\partial\Delta t/\partial N=\alpha+2\beta N\nu\pi\theta_{vm}^{2}$ — linear, not quadratic. This is the formal content of PAGE 6: "when the number of objects increases significantly, the frame timing of Fast-Poly exhibits minimal increments (≤30ms)."

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Three competing control policies, one agent, one clock.**

*Crisis-habit regression.* The default habit is the count-based lifecycle: survive until max-age. PAGE 2 frames it as robust but rigid; PAGE 4 calls it out as the maintenance-cost driver: the "rigid count-based lifecycle seriously raises tracker maintenance costs." Under stress (occlusion, FN bursts) a tracker regresses to this habit because it is monotone and requires no score model. The regression is invisible in AMOTA because the metric averages over time; it shows up as FP accumulation. Table VI makes the regression measurable: "Count & Max-age" gives 73.3 AMOTA with FN = 16523 at 23.0 FPS.

*The counter-trap.* The opposite over-correction is terminating on the latest score. The paper's evidence is explicit on PAGE 7: "Confidence [14] & Latest" yields 70.6 AMOTA — a 3.1-point collapse — with FN rising to 19192, despite the highest throughput (45.8 FPS). The false economy is structural: the latest-score rule is *cheaper* because it kills tracklets early. A team optimizing FPS drifts straight into this basin. The paper's diagnosis (PAGE 4) of why [14] fails is the mechanism: the un-decayed predicted score sits in the "over-confidence area ([0.7, 1] interval in Fig. 2 of [14])", producing "hysteresis in score estimates."

*The resolution.* The noisy-OR refinement plus online averaging reconciles both: 73.7 AMOTA, 63.2 MOTA, 28.9 FPS, FN = 15900 — best on three of four axes simultaneously. The claim on PAGE 4 is that "the power function swiftly pulls the tracklet predict score to the [0, σ] interval," which is exactly the removal of the hysteresis basin derived in O4's fixed point $s^{*}=c/(1-\sigma(1-c))$.

*The clock.* There are two clocks and they can disagree. Clock A is the score-average clock $T^{*}$ (O4): 25 frames for Car, 15 for Ped, 4.3 for Bus on nuScenes keyframes. Clock B is the count-based max-age. The lifecycle is well-posed only when $T^{*}<T_{\text{max-age}}$; otherwise the score logic is decorative. A third clock supersedes both in deployment: the frame budget, 1/28.9 s = 34.6 ms on the personal CPU (Table V, Exp5). Any policy that spends more than ~35 ms per frame to resolve a single ambiguous tracklet is losing to the clock regardless of its accuracy.

*Recursive modeling of the operator.* The system's human overseer is also an agent with a habit. Faced with a latency spike, the reflexive response is to shrink the gating radius. The paper pre-empts this (PAGE 8): θ_vm = 1 m produces a "pronounced decrease," because the operator has confused a *spatial* gate with a *residual* gate (O5's precondition). The correct intervention is on $\theta_{fm}$ (the genuinely sensitive knob), not θ_vm. Modeling the overseer's likely mis-attribution is what makes the ablation table actionable rather than descriptive.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A veto here means: a physical or sensor-level condition that renders the tracker's mathematical machinery inapplicable, independent of tuning.

**V1 — Rigid-body veto (biomechanical).** O4's EKF and O6's filter both assume a rigid box with slowly varying extents. Pedestrians violate both: limb articulation changes the effective footprint at gait frequency, and the point-cloud centroid oscillates. The paper's parameter choices encode an implicit concession — Ped receives the loosest association threshold on nuScenes ($\theta_{fm}=1.8$ versus 1.2 for Bus/Car/Tra/Tru, PAGE 5) and the highest decay with Moto ($\sigma=0.6$). On Waymo, Ped receives the highest decay overall ($\sigma=0.7$). The veto proof: a rigid constant-velocity model cannot represent a 1–2 Hz gait cycle; the loosened gates are compensation, not solution. Cyclists add a second violation — roll/lean about the longitudinal axis — while the paper's representation is yaw-only (A‑gIoU collapses to a ground-plane footprint, PAGE 3), so roll is structurally invisible.

**V2 — Occlusion veto.** LiDAR returns are absent, not degraded, under occlusion. The tracker must coast, and the coasting clock is O4's $T^{*}$. Because $T^{*}$ (25 frames for Car at 2 Hz, ≈12.5 s) exceeds typical intersection occlusion durations, the *score* clock does not terminate; only max-age does. The paper's own evidence that this matters is Table VI: switching from latest-score to average-score recovers 2366 FN and 2.7 AMOTA (PAGE 7), i.e. the average is doing occlusion work. Veto condition: an occlusion longer than $T_{\text{max-age}}$ forces ID switch regardless of score modeling.

**V3 — Weather / sensor-physics veto.** nuScenes [ref. 8] and Waymo [ref. 9] contain rain and night scenes, but the paper reports no weather-stratified evaluation. LiDAR return attenuation under rain/fog reduces point counts, which cascades: fewer points → noisier $z,w,l,h$ → O6's median filter is fed contaminated samples → with $l_{lw}=3$ the breakdown point is one contaminated sample in three (O6), so two consecutive wet-frame observations flip the estimate. The veto is quantitative: O6's breakdown point, not the tracker's logic, sets the weather tolerance.

**V4 — Ground-plane / mounting veto.** PAGE 3's footnote fixes "Z-position fixed at 1m," and A‑gIoU operates on "the top-left and bottom-right points on the ground plane." On grade changes, suspension pitch, or curb transitions, the ground plane assumption is violated and the aligned surrogate mis-registers in BEV. The paper contains no slope or pitch stress test; the veto is unadjudicated in the source.

**V5 — Detector veto (upstream).** Since PAGE 6 excludes detection time and PAGE 1 notes the tracker "rel[ies] entirely on the detector input," the tracker inherits the detector's failure envelope. Table VII's DETR3D block shows the ceiling: Fast‑Poly reaches 40.7 AMOTA on the constrained multi-camera detector versus 76.0 with LargeKernel3D. The veto proof is that a 35-point AMOTA spread is produced by the detector alone — no tracker-layer intervention can recover it.
