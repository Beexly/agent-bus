Any runway-configuration prediction \(\hat r_{n,t}\) that assigns operations to a closed runway must be vetoed; the predictor explicitly uses closed-runway information as input [PAGE 11], so a veto fires when the classifier output contradicts the physical runway-status feed.

Biomechanical / human-factor layer: controller sector workload and safe handling rates are not modeled in the source text, but they form a physical upper bound on executable departure and arrival rates. If the classifier predicts available slots \(a_{n,t}\) or \(d_{n,t}\) that exceed the safe controller throughput for the configured runway layout, a veto must cap the executable rate below the model output.

Weather veto: visibility below landing minima, crosswind/component limits, lightning within prescribed radius, or microburst detection force runway reconfiguration or closure independent of the probabilistic scenario output. These meteorological vetoes dominate the classification model because \(g_A,g_D\) are conditioned on weather but cannot override an active weather shutdown.

Adjudication order: vetoes form a precedence lattice. Physical runway status and active weather hazards veto first (hard constraints); human-factor caps veto second; only after all vetoes are applied does the probabilistic change-predictor / change-quantity-predictor output become an advisory for slot scheduling. The smoothing rule may not be applied to override a vetoed transient; a genuine short-duration weather veto must survive the smoother's two-hour threshold.
