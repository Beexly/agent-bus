The trap is sprung not at the political layer but at the biomechanical layer, where the impulse distribution \mathcal{N}(\mu_J,\sigma_J^2) intersects the primer activation threshold J_c. If \sigma_J is large relative to the distance \mu_J - J_c, the probability of detonation becomes a step-like function of microscopic jitter, and the assassin’s recursive model of the target is irrelevant because the bullet never leaves the barrel.

The clock is t_\lambda. Recursive modeling of depth d requires computation time T_{\text{comp}}(d). If T_{\text{comp}}(d) > t_\lambda, the model state is stale by the time it is issued, and the counter-trap misfires because the target’s true trajectory has already diverged beyond the prediction horizon. Wiener’s text places the entire political bifurcation—revolution versus peaceful development—inside a time interval smaller than the latency of social foresight, which means the recursive model must be evaluated faster than the Lyapunov time of the sociotechnical system. When it cannot, the agent regresses to habit, the habit is trapped at the physical layer, and the qualitative outcome flips despite no large quantitative change in circumstances [PAGE 1].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: brittle fracture of the glass pane. The governing veto is the Griffith energy balance \Delta U \ge 2\gamma\Delta A. If the incident kinetic energy \Delta U is less than the critical surface-energy cost, the pane does not break; the ball reflects quasi-elastically and Wiener’s “broken pane” effect never materializes. This is a hard veto because it derives from conservation of energy and the convexity of the elastic energy functional. Breakdown regime: dynamic fracture where crack velocity approaches the Rayleigh wave speed, or ductile materials where plastic work dominates surface energy.

Biomechanical layer: firing-pin impulse and primer detonation. The stochastic impulse delivered to the primer is J = \int F(t)\,dt [N·s]. The veto condition is J < J_c, where J_c is the minimum activation impulse. Human motor control introduces jitter \sigma_J; if \sigma_J is comparable to or greater than the clearance \mu_J - J_c, then even a determined assassin cannot guarantee bullet projection. This veto is probabilistic rather than deterministic: the detonation probability P(J\ge J_c) can be driven to zero by reducing \mu_J below J_c or by increasing \sigma_J through mechanical interference. Breakdown regime: electronic or caseless ignition systems where the mechanical firing pin is absent, invalidating the biomechanical impulse channel entirely.

Weather/climate layer: atmospheric sensitive dependence. Although not explicit in Wiener’s text, the Lorenzian implication of his divergence claim is that any socio-political forecast coupled to weather-sensitive variables (harvest, migration, unrest) inherits the atmospheric Lyapunov time t_\lambda^{\rm atm} \sim \mathcal{O}(10^5\ \mathrm{s}) (several days). The veto condition is \Delta t_{\text{forecast}} > t_\lambda^{\rm atm}; beyond this horizon, initial-condition uncertainty \epsilon grows to O(1) climate variability, and long-range political extrapolation loses skill. Breakdown regime: coupled ocean-atmosphere modes with longer t_\lambda (e.g., ENSO, months to years), where the veto is weakened but not eliminated.

Adjudication order: a physical veto at the fracture layer overrides sociological narrative because energy conservation is non-negotiable; a biomechanical veto overrides assassination intent because the bullet is the necessary physical vector; a weather veto overrides long-horizon societal modeling because the Lyapunov clock expires before the forecast matures. Wiener’s claim that small causes yield large effects is therefore conditional on the absence of lower-layer vetoes; when any veto triggers, the causal chain is severed at its weakest link.
