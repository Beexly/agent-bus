<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
James’s 1890 text [PAGE 459] is the canonical English statement of what became the James–Lange theory, although Carl Lange’s 1884 physiological account of emotion as vasomotor change predates or parallels James’s formulation. The lineage runs backward to Darwin’s 1872 *The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals*, which emphasized the bodily expressive component of affect, and forward to Walter Cannon’s 1927 “thalamic” critique, which argued that bodily changes are too slow and undifferentiated to cause specific emotions. The Schachter–Singer two-factor theory of 1962 reinserted cognitive labeling between arousal and emotion, while LeDoux’s 1996 dual-pathway model distinguished a fast subcortical affective route from a slow cortical appraisal route. Damasio’s somatic marker hypothesis (1990s) revived a Jamesian periphery-to-brain loop but relegated the body to a biasing signal rather than the whole emotion.

The retro-causal trap relevant here is multi-week clinical misattribution. A patient presenting with “absolutely unmotived fear” in an asylum [PAGE 459] may be observed over weeks as having chronic anxiety, when in fact each episode is an acute afferent storm—respiratory restriction, cardiac flutter, epigastric pressure—that the patient retrospectively narrates as causeless because the visceral onset precedes conscious awareness by seconds. The “unmotived” label is therefore a temporal artifact: the bodily cause is real but dissociated from the emotional report in time. A second trap is the “ipso facto” fallacy [PAGE 459]: because deep breathing and erect posture terminate an attack in the moment, clinicians may assume the underlying lability is cured, when the nervous machinery remains “so ‘labile’” [PAGE 459] that the next inappropriate stimulus will re-trigger the syndrome. The multi-week trap is mistaking state-dependent remission for trait-level recovery.

A third lineage trap concerns the footnote’s hallucinatory dread [PAGE 459]. If brain disease or trance can produce dread with a calm pulse, then a century of biofeedback and interoceptive therapies that assume peripheral necessity will fail in precisely that subpopulation. The historical lesson is that James’s theory is a bifurcated theory: peripheral afference is sufficient in the somatic subtype, but central excitation is sufficient in the hallucinatory subtype. Applying the bodily model universally commits the retro-causal error of projecting the somatic mechanism onto centrally generated cases.
