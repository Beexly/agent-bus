The source’s formal object is a binary logistic regression. Let \(Y_i\in\{0,1\}\) be the outcome for game \(i\), with \(i=1,\dots,256\) for the 2015 regular season. The source defines margins as home minus away [PAGE 17]. The Bernoulli likelihood is
\[
\Pr(Y_i=y_i\mid p_i)=p_i^{y_i}(1-p_i)^{1-y_i},\qquad y_i\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The linear predictor and link are
\[
\eta_i=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_j x_{ij},\qquad
p_i=\sigma(\eta_i)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\eta_i}},\qquad
\eta_i=\ln\!\left(\frac{p_i}{1-p_i}\right).
\]
The source describes the link as the component that “quantifies the expected value of the dependent variable with the correlation between the independent variables” [PAGE 11]. Maximum likelihood maximizes
\[
\ell(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[y_i\eta_i-\ln\!\left(1+e^{\eta_i}\right)\right],
\]
with score equations
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i-p_i\right)x_{ij}=0,\qquad j=0,\dots,k,
\]
and Hessian
\[
\nabla^2\ell(\beta)=-X^\top W X,\qquad W=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(p_i(1-p_i)\right).
\]
The five-variable specification is reported as including Pp40, AFP, 3rd, Turn, and FD: “The most accurate five variable model included Pp40, AFP, 3rd, Turn, and FD” [PAGE 30]. In corrected logistic form, it is
\[
p_i=\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\left[-\left(0.3283+1.3075\,\mathrm{Pp40}_i+0.18165\,\mathrm{AFP}_i+5.34172\,\mathrm{3rd}_i-0.61422\,\mathrm{Turn}_i+0.28428\,\mathrm{FD}_i\right)\right]}.
\]
The source’s printed version is OCR-corrupted in the denominator and sign [PAGE 30], so the equation above restores the standard form required by the prose and the monotonic curves.

Units and dimensions: \(p_i\) is dimensionless on \([0,1]\); \(\eta_i\) is dimensionless; each \(\beta_j\) has units inverse to \(x_j\). In the source, AFP is in yards, so \(\beta_{\mathrm{AFP}}\) is per yard; in SI, one yard is \(0.9144\ \mathrm{m}\), so the coefficient would rescale by \(1/0.9144\) per metre. Turn, FD, sacks, and penalties are counts and therefore dimensionless episode counts. Pp40 is points per trip inside the 40-yard line, a count ratio with units of points per trip; PpFD is points per first down. The 3rd-down variable is a fraction; if expressed as percentage points, the coefficient \(5.34172\) per unit fraction becomes \(0.0534172\) per percentage point. The Brier score has units of probability squared:
\[
\mathrm{BS}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(p_i-y_i\right)^2,
\]
and log loss is in nats per game:
\[
\mathrm{LL}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[y_i\ln p_i+(1-y_i)\ln(1-p_i)\right].
\]
These units matter because a coefficient cannot be interpreted without the predictor scale; a “large” coefficient on a fraction is not comparable to a coefficient on a count.

Assumptions: independent Bernoulli outcomes conditional on \(x_i\); correct linearity on the logit scale; no perfect collinearity in \(X\); finite maximum-likelihood estimates; no unmeasured confounders if causal language is used; and exchangeability between training and validation populations if the model is transported. The source explicitly warns against causal reading: “correlation does not mean causation” [PAGE 32]. Exact breakdown regimes are:
1. \(p_i=0\) or \(p_i=1\): the logit is undefined at the boundary; only limits as \(\eta_i\to\pm\infty\) exist.
2. Complete or quasi-complete separation: if a linear combination perfectly orders wins and losses, \(\ell(\beta)\) is maximized only at infinity, so finite MLE does not exist and standard errors diverge.
3. Perfect collinearity: \(\operatorname{rank}(X)\le k\) makes \(X^\top W X\) singular, so \(\beta\) is not identifiable.
4. Saturation: for large \(|\eta_i|\), \(W_{ii}=p_i(1-p_i)\to 0\), so the Hessian becomes numerically singular and Newton-type updates are unstable.
5. Extrapolation: predictor values outside the training convex hull can drive \(\eta_i\) arbitrarily far, producing saturated probabilities.
6. Nonlinear aggregation: \(\sigma(\beta^\top \bar{x})\neq \overline{\sigma(\beta^\top x)}\) in general, so season-average margins are not a valid plug-in for average game-level probabilities [PAGE 34].
7. Selection optimism: choosing the best of 218 specifications on the same data creates upward-biased in-sample accuracy [PAGE 32]; the playoff check drops to 7 of 11, or 63.6% [PAGE 35].

\]

Weather and injury can therefore change \(Y\) while also changing the predictors the model treats as sufficient. Adjudication rule: if a game is outside the training support on weather, injury, rest, or opponent strength, the point prediction must be vetoed or explicitly widened. A stratified model or added \(Z\) covariates would be required. The source’s conclusion that “no model will ever be 100% accurate” [PAGE 38] is correct but too weak; the stronger statement is that this model’s probabilities are not transportable across physical layers it never measured.
