This is a consequential modeling choice: conditioning on remaining counts means the predictive distribution must satisfy the hard consistency inequality \hat{Y}_{a,p,t} \ge Y^{\text{current}}_{a,p,t} for all t, and \hat{Y}_{a,p,T} = Y^{\text{actual}}_{a,p,T} at match end [PAGE 4]. The paper notes cross-action consistency constraints such as shots on target \le shots and team assists \le team goals [PAGE 4], but it does not state that these inequalities are enforced in the loss; they are imposed only implicitly through shared transformer embeddings and marginal output heads [PAGE 13]. This is a gray zone: the model approximates the joint distribution P(Y) through a common representation while optimizing a sum of marginal negative log-likelihoods [PAGE 8, PAGE 14], so global consistency is an emergent property rather than a constrained optimization guarantee.

A second gray zone lies in the axial attention equivalence claim. The authors assert that their additive row/column attention is equivalent to a regular masked sequential self-attention with mask M := M_{row} + P M_{col} P^\top [PAGE 11]. The equivalence depends on the diagonal elements (G)_{ii} being masked in either the row or column operation so that M_{row} and M_{col} are disjoint [PAGE 10, PAGE 22]. If that disjointness precondition is violated, the self-embedding is double-counted across the two Softmax-normalized branches, breaking the claimed equivalence to sequential attention and altering the effective attention entropy. The appendix proof sketch relies on permutation equivariance of row-wise Softmax [PAGE 22], but the temporal mask is strictly upper-triangular (autoregressive) while the agent mask is bidirectional within a time-step [PAGE 13]; combining an asymmetric temporal mask with a symmetric agent mask under a single row-major unravelling creates a mask structure that is neither purely causal nor purely exchangeable, which may distort gradient flow near substitution boundaries.

Calibration exhibits weak support regions. Red cards and own goals are assigned zero-category probabilities no less than 0.84 and 0.97 respectively [PAGE 15], while attempted and accurate passes have high cardinality and diffuse probability mass [PAGE 15]. Because the model outputs marginals per agent and action, rare events can be systematically under-dispersed without affecting aggregate log-probability significantly, creating a failure mode for high-stakes low-frequency actions (red cards, own goals) exactly where game-state shifts are most nonlinear.

The substitution boundary condition introduces another gray zone. A = M + \frac{KQ^\top}{\sqrt d},
\]
where K,Q,V \in \mathbb{R}^{S\times D}, M_{ij}=0 if S_i can attend to S_j and -\infty otherwise, and the row-wise Softmax is [PAGE 9]
\[
\operatorname{Softmax}(A) := \frac{\exp(A)}{\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top \exp(A)},\quad n=\exp(A)\mathbf{1},
\]
with n \in \mathbb{R}^S the per-row normalizer so each attention row sums to 1 [PAGE 9].

Axial attention on grid G \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times D} applies Attention independently to each row and column and aggregates [PAGE 9, PAGE 10]:
\[
R = \frac{n_{\text{row}}}{n_{\text{row}}+n_{\text{col}}}\odot R_{\text{row}} + \frac{n_{\text{col}}}{n_{\text{row}}+n_{\text{col}}}\odot R_{\text{col}},
\]
with element-wise division and Hadamard product \odot [PAGE 10]. Complexity per AxialAttention layer is O((H+W)HW), sub-quadratic in the flattened sequence length HW, versus O(H^2 W^2) for unflattened sequential attention [PAGE 11].

Equivalence mask [PAGE 11]:
\[
M := M_{\text{row}} + P\,M_{\text{col}}\,P^\top,
\]
where P \in \{0,1\}^{HW\times HW} reorders column-major to row-major, M_{\text{row}} = \operatorname{diag}(M_{\text{row}}^1,\dots,M_{\text{row}}^H), and M_{\text{col}} = \operatorname{diag}(M_{\text{col}}^1,\dots,M_{\text{col}}^W) [PAGE 11].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock modulation.

Crisis habit regression. When a team concedes a goal or receives a red card, the game state shifts discontinuously; the paper illustrates this with an opposition red card in the 62nd minute altering expected action totals [PAGE 4, PAGE 11]. Teams under crisis tend to regress to established habit patterns—conservative shape when down a goal, deeper block when down a man—rather than optimizing anew. The model captures this through running totals in X_player and X_team combined with temporal autoregressive attention [PAGE 8, PAGE 13], allowing the embedding for each agent to condition on its own recent trajectory and the opponent's.

Counter-trap. The opposing team observes the crisis-induced regression and adjusts its own pressing intensity and risk profile. Because column (agent) attention permits every agent at time-step i to attend to all agents at that same time-step [PAGE 13], the axial transformer explicitly models recursive inter-agent reaction: Fulham's expected shots drop after a red card while Aston Villa's rise [PAGE 23]. This is a first-order theory of mind—each agent's embedding is updated conditional on the contemporaneous states of all other agents—but it is not a higher-order recursive model of "I know that you know that I am regressing." The shared transformer state approximates this recursion only through multiple layers L [PAGE 13], so deep recursive counter-adaptation (e.g., baiting an opponent into over-committing after a red card) is represented only insofar as it is statistically encoded in the training distribution.

Clock. The remaining horizon T(n) - t directly modulates the Poisson rate for remaining actions [PAGE 8]. As the clock approaches match end, the feasible action count collapses; the model must converge to actual totals [PAGE 4]. Substitution creates an absorbing boundary: post-substitution future actions for that player are zero except yellow/red cards [PAGE 23], so the team-level action budget is recursively reallocated among remaining on-field players. The clock therefore interacts with crisis habits: a red card at minute 62 has a different marginal effect on expected passes than the same red card at minute 88 because the remaining time to amortize the numerical disadvantage differs, and the temporal attention mask restricts each embedding to past time-steps only [PAGE 13], ensuring no lookahead into clock-dependent outcomes.
