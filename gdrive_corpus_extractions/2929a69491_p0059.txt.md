Across repeated weekly measurement, conditioning on return to the lab can also induce a Berkson-type selection artifact if only subjects satisfied with their prior rank reappear, corrupting the very reference distribution the grade depends on.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model two agents: the analyst who reads the chart and the subject whose strength is measured. At zeroth order, the analyst observes s₀ = 74 lbf, reads g = 50° on [PAGE 40], and concludes the subject is median in the reference group. At first order, the subject has a private belief about felt exertion and habitual capacity that may disagree with the single test value.

Crisis habit regression: under stress, fatigue, or time pressure, the subject regresses to a habitual force level s_habit that is typically below a maximal laboratory pull. If s₀ = 74 lbf is a one-off maximum while s_habit < 74 lbf, then the grade 50° is an upper-bound snapshot of capacity, not a regression-stable expectation of everyday performance. Interpreting it as a stable trait overestimates the subject's functional rank under crisis conditions.

Counter-trap: the analyst assumes the grade is fixed and uses it to set future expectations, but the subject, informed that they sit at the median, may train specifically to shift the strength distribution rightward. Because F changes in response to the measurement, the original grade ceases to be a valid predictor—an instance of reactivity in which making a rank a target destroys its measure value.

Clock: there is a timescale mismatch between the measurement occasion (minutes) and the physiological adaptation clock (weeks). Strength reconsolidation and detraining operate on order 1–4 weeks; if the grade is interpreted as week-invariant but \partial F / \partial t \neq 0, the autocorrelation of rank across weeks decays and the 50° grade becomes stale. Stability of the recursive loop—analyst predicts future rank from current grade, subject adjusts training, distribution shifts, prediction fails, re-measurement occurs—requires approximate stationarity of F over the inference window; otherwise the grade is vetoed as a short-horizon estimate only.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The numerical value s₀ = 74 lbf is device-dependent. Handle diameter, grip orientation, arm support, and dynamometer calibration introduce a systematic offset \delta(s) between devices such that s_A = 74 lbf need not equal s_B = 74 lbf. Veto if inter-device concordance (e.g., limits of agreement or concordance correlation) has not been established; otherwise the side-scale input is not commensurable across contexts.

Biomechanical layer: Pull strength depends on forearm musculature activation, tendon stiffness, circadian phase, fatigue, warm-up status, and time since last exertion. A single trial differs from a best-of-three maximum voluntary contraction (MVC) protocol, shifting the empirical CDF. Veto if the protocol lacks standardized rest, warm-up, time-of-day control, and explicit MVC instruction, because \widehat{F}_N then reflects protocol artifacts as much as physiology.

Weather/environmental layer: Ambient temperature T and relative humidity RH alter skin friction, grip compliance, and neuromuscular performance. If the Health Exhibition measurements were taken under uncontrolled or day-varying hall conditions, T and RH are confounders of S. Veto unless T and RH are recorded and shown statistically independent of S (or explicitly adjusted for), since otherwise the curve C on [PAGE 40] is a mixture over environmental states.

Composite veto predicate: V = V_{\text{phys}} \lor V_{\text{bio}} \lor V_{\text{weather}}. If V = true, the centesimal grade \mathcal{C}(s) is not interoperable across devices, protocols, or environments, and any cross-layer comparison of ranks fails. Only if V = false—calibrated device, standardized MVC biomechanics, and recorded/stable ambient conditions—does the grade on [PAGE 40] retain cross-context validity.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Counterfactual: under ties, P(S < 74) ≠ P(S ≤ 74), so the strict "weaker than" reading on [PAGE 40] and the weak ECDF convention yield different grades. Prediction: the true centesimal grade is an interval [100·P(S < 74), 100·P(S ≤ 74)] of width exceeding the chart's line-reading precision; the claim "his centesimal Grade is 50°" is vetoed, and only an interval grade is admissible.
