<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
First-order cognition: The end reads the snap and charges forward immediately, per [PAGE 255]. Second-order cognition: The offense expects this charge and runs a trap inside the end’s original path; the end must now execute the conditional “turning in quickly” [PAGE 255] rather than continuing straight. Third-order cognition: The defense knows the offense expects the charge, so the end holds his outside lane; the offense then attacks the edge because the end is no longer charging to set it. This recursive loop has no fixed point in the text because [PAGE 255] provides no meta-rule for adjusting when the offense counters the charge.

Crisis habit regression: Under crowd noise, fatigue, or late-game pressure, players regress to the simplest available rule. For the ends, that rule is “charge ahead instantly” [PAGE 255]; the conditional turn-in is cognitively more expensive and is the first element dropped. The result is a defensive end who runs past the play, abandons containment, and hands the offense an unblocked edge. Counter-trap: The offensive line deliberately yields the outside shoulder, inviting the end to charge upfield, then traps him behind the line with a pulling guard. Because the text conditions the end’s charge on the snap but not on reading the offensive line’s blocking scheme, the end is vulnerable to being sealed out of the play.

Clock interaction: In the final minutes, the defensive quarterback’s environmental rule \(d_{QB} = g(\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}}, S_{\text{kicker}})\) [PAGE 255] conflicts with the game clock. To prevent a score, the defensive backfield may be drawn shallower than wind and kicker strength prescribe, violating “never be drawn in too close” [PAGE 255]. A shallow \(d_{QB}\) exposes the defense to a quick kick or fake punt into the vacated depth. The clock thus forces a parameter regime outside the source’s stated assumptions, converting an environmental depth problem into a clock-management failure. Recursive theory-of-mind failure occurs when the defense assumes the offense will respect the kick/wind depth model while the offense uses clock pressure to force that very model into violation.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The end’s charge requires horizontal traction. With cleat–ground coefficient of friction \(\mu\), maximum sustainable acceleration is \(a = \mu g\), where \(g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\). On wet or frozen turf, \(\mu\) falls below the critical value needed to reach full speed before the offensive line engages; the “instant” charge [PAGE 255] becomes physically unrealizable. Veto condition: if field measurements give \(\mu < \mu_{\text{crit}}\), the formation cannot execute the prescribed end response.

Biomechanical layer: The center plays “higher” [PAGE 255], elevating his center of mass \(h_{COM}\). A higher \(h_{COM}\) reduces leverage against low double-teams from offensive guards. If opposing linemen maintain \(h_{COM,\text{off}} < h_{COM,C}\), the defensive center loses the leverage battle and is displaced, violating his duty to tackle “on either side of center, or in fact along the entire line” [PAGE 255]. Veto condition: if the center cannot sustain \(h_{COM,C} \leq h_{\text{crit}}\) under load, he must be replaced or the alignment changed.

Weather layer: The defensive quarterback’s depth depends on \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}}\) and \(S_{\text{kicker}}\) [PAGE 255]. Punt trajectory under wind is modeled by \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{v}_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a} t^2 + \mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}} t\). Gusty or variable wind makes \(g\) non-injective: multiple \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}}\) histories map to different landing points for the same kick, so no single \(d_{QB}\) can cover all outcomes. Veto condition: when wind variance exceeds the kicker’s directional control authority, \(d_{QB}\) is indeterminate and the quarterback should not fix a static depth.

Adjudication rule: The “Regular” formation is admissible only if all three layer constraints hold simultaneously. No layer may override another; a failure in traction, leverage, or wind-reading voids the formation’s presuppositions and requires a substitute alignment before the snap.
