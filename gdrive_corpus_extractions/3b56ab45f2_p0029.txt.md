<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under crisis conditions—such as an unexpected injury, red-card event, or abrupt weather shift—the prediction system faces a recursive theory-of-mind problem. The model must anticipate how the opposing team’s habit distribution will regress under stress, while users or downstream consumers of the GUI may themselves over-react to the same crisis. Crisis habit regression occurs when live in-game data deviates from historical aggregates: the boosted-tree component may default to the unconditional historical save percentage or return percentage because the feature vector \(\mathbf{x}\) now lies outside the convex hull of training observations [PAGE 9]. The counter-trap is synthetic overconfidence: the GAN may generate plausible but physically invalid shot trajectories that match the statistical texture of past crises without respecting real-time constraints. The clock layer imposes a hard boundary: predictions for shot winner or shot location must be emitted before the shot is struck, bounded by the shot clock, play clock, or natural event latency. If inference latency \(\tau_{\text{inf}}\) exceeds the decision window \(\tau_{\text{event}}\), the output is causally useless regardless of accuracy. Thus the recursive loop is (1) detect crisis state, (2) regress toward conservative historical priors, (3) counter-trap synthetic GAN hallucinations with physical vetoes, and (4) stamp output with a timestamp and validity horizon before display 110 renders it [PAGE 5].
