- Weakly-informative $\text{HalfNormal}(0,5^2)$ at the boundary 0: if a true variance is near 0 (e.g., $\lambda_1$), posterior piles against the boundary — observed as the "implausibly small" $\hat\lambda_1=0.006$ for all-50-state pooling ([PAGE 21]), i.e., over-shrinkage.
- LOO breakdown: high Pareto-$\hat k$ (influential games) invalidates the $\Delta\text{ELPD}$; the authors invoke the "4 standard errors" rule of thumb ([PAGE 7], citing [43]).
- Additive-vs-multiplicative singularity: under a multiplicative HA data-generating process, the additive $\beta_1$ is not invariant, so the estimated points/year slope is scale-confounded ([PAGE 15]).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The methodological lineage is traceable through the paper's own Table 1 ([PAGE 3]) and references. Harville's linear-model rating work (1977, 1980; refs [28],[22]) established the additive margin decomposition on score differentials; Steffani (1980, [21]) provided early empirical point estimates ($\approx2$ NFL points, 1970–78). Glickman & Stern modernized this into Bayesian state-space models for the NFL (1998, 2017; refs [19],[14]), reporting $3.2$ and $2.4$ points. Lopez et al. (2018, [13]) reframed outcomes via a paired-comparison/state-space "how often does the best team win" lens. The proximate ancestor is Higgs & Stavness (2021, [12]), whose COVID-era analysis concluded the NFL HA "had been declining for multiple years prior to the pandemic" and whose Poisson/Negative-Binomial/Normal comparison informed the distribution choice. Scorecasting (Moskowitz & Wertheim, [31]) supplies the causal folk-hypothesis linking instant replay to the decline in home fumble recoveries; Snyder & Lopez ([32]) quantify an officiating channel ("odds of defensive pass interference fouls were 18% higher when the home team was on offense," [PAGE 2]).

Multi-week (multi-season) traps embedded in any long-window estimate: (1) Rule-change drift — the paper notes going "before 2004 would likely introduce additional confounding by play style" as rules "generally favor increases in offensive production" ([PAGE 15]); older studies ([21],[22]) report smaller HA partly for this reason, a survivorship-of-era artifact. (2) Replay-adoption stagger — NFL 1999/2011/2012/2014/2022, NCAA 2004→2005→2006, HS prohibited until 2019 ([PAGE 13]) — means the "treatment" turns on at different calendar times across leagues, so a single pooled clock is misspecified. (3) The 2020 deletion creates a temporal discontinuity that the linear $t-t_0$ index bridges silently.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The paper contains no explicit strategic-agent (game-theoretic) module, so I extend its stated mechanisms rather than cite a model that isn't there. Three reflexive channels it does name:

- Counter-trap (scheduling agency): stronger programs "pay worse opponents to visit and get blown out" and better teams "host playoff games" ([PAGE 10–11]). This is second-order: the very teams that reduce their own home exposure (buy-games on the road) or manufacture home games (seeding) drive the gap between $\hat\gamma^{\text{empirical}}_{kt}$ and $\hat\gamma_{kt}$. Texas is the natural control — neutral-site playoff rounds after round one ([PAGE 11]) collapse the empirical-vs-model gap, isolating the seeding reflex.

- Crisis habit regression (officiating under crowd state): the social-pressure channel (118 dB crowd ≈ "737 airplane at takeoff," [PAGE 1]) predicts officials partially revert toward home-favoring calls. The replay fix (successful challenges "from 31% in 2004 to 58% in 2022," [PAGE 13]) is the countermeasure that erodes this reflex — strongest exactly where replay was newly introduced (FBS), matching the largest $\hat\beta_{1,\text{FBS}}=-0.097$.

- Clock: the observable is $\beta_{1k}$, the secular drift. The paper's claim is that the counter-trap (replay + charter travel) outpaces the crisis habit in top tiers, while amateur tiers lack both, so their $\beta_{1k}\gtrsim 0$ (38 of 50 states positive, [PAGE 10]).

Honest limit: the "recursive mind" here is reduced to reduced-form trend coefficients; there is no belief-updating or equilibrium object in the source.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical and weather layers are largely held out by design, and I flag that rather than invent them. What the source controls and claims:

- Travel (physical): Figure 5 shows standardized distance vs standardized $z(\gamma)_{kt}$ with $R^2=0.016$ ([PAGE 14]) — "at best a weak association." This is a near-veto of distance as the primary driver. Caveat the authors raise: measurement error from city-level lat/long produces "a disproportionate number of games with incorrect estimates of 0 distance," and 500 flown miles (FBS) ≠ 250 driven miles (Div III), so the physical covariate is under-resolved.

- Weather/biomechanics: the paper does not build a temperature, wind, altitude, turf, or fatigue-state term. The 2020 season is dropped for "irregularities in play, travel, fan restrictions" ([PAGE 6]), which is the only exogenous-shock control. Therefore any adjudication that a specific physical/biomechanical factor vetoes the HA trend is unsupported by this source; the correct verdict is "not modeled — outside scope."

- Statistical veto (what the paper does prove): confounding by team strength is vetoed as an explanation of the raw signal because adjusted $\hat\gamma_{kt}$ diverges from $\hat\gamma^{\text{empirical}}_{kt}$ by $>3$ points ([PAGE 10]). And a distance-driven story is weakly vetoed by $R^2=0.016$. Combined, the paper's physical-layer adjudication is: distance ≈ negligible; other physical layers untested.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsification scenarios derived from the paper's own quantities, each verifiable against the supplied tables, with quantitative predictions.

1. Replay-adoption counterfactual. If referee-subjectivity reduction drives the decline, the magnitude of $\hat\beta_1$ should order by replay-adoption recency: FBS (earliest, most complete) > Div II > FCS/Div III > HS (prohibited till 2019). Check: FBS $-0.097$ > Div II $-0.058$ > FCS $-0.014$ > Div III $-0.009$; HS mean near $0$ ([PAGE 8–10]). Prediction: any future league that installs centralized replay should show $\beta_1$ turn negative within ~5 seasons; a league that never adopts it should retain $\beta_1\gtrsim0$. Veto condition: if FBS's advantage over FCS were absent, the replay story is falsified — currently $P(\beta_{1,\text{FBS}}<0)=1.000$ vs $P(\beta_{1,\text{FCS}}<0)=0.743$, so it holds.

2. Distance-travel veto. If travel distance drove HA, $R^2_{\text{dist}}$ would be material and rural states (AK, MT, WY) would dominate HA. Check: $R^2=0.016$ ([PAGE 14]); yet WY $2.40$, MT $2.33$, AK $2.01$ are high — but SD ($-0.049$/yr), ID ($1.25$), NH ($0.65$) are low ([PAGE 7,10]). Prediction: adding a clean distance covariate changes $\hat\gamma_{kt}$ by $<0.5$ SD across the distance range (the paper's own bound, [PAGE 14]). Veto: a properly measured distance term exceeding $0.5$ SD effect would overturn the claim.

3. Multiplicity/shrinkage veto. If the 38-of-50 rising-state signal were real rather than chance, hierarchical pooling should preserve it. Check: Model 2H shrinks $\hat\beta_1^*\to0.012$ with $\hat\lambda_1=0.006$, and Delaware significance drops $0.968\to0.808$ ([PAGE 21]); large-sample states (TX, CA, PA, OH, ~27% of games) show "much, if any, shrinkage." Prediction: the states surviving both multiplicity and shrinkage are Delaware, Tennessee ($0.999$), Kentucky ($0.977$), Massachusetts ($0.977$); the rest are consistent with noise. Veto: if a small-sample state retained high significance under 2H, the chance-explanation fails.

Net adjudication: the decline is robust for FBS ($P=1.000$) and probable for Div II ($0.999$), NFL ($0.857$); weak for FCS/Div III; and not credible at high school once multiplicity and shrinkage are applied.
