The second lineage is detection-specific distillation: "To extend KD to object detection Chen et al. [23] introduced objectness scaled distillation for one-shot object detectors" ([PAGE 2]). AttTrack's declared delta is unambiguous: "the key distinction between our AttTrack and the aforementioned approaches is that during inference time, the student model uses real teacher outputs, to calibrate tracking outputs and achieve better accuracy" ([PAGE 2]).

The third lineage is the authors' own prior systems work on adaptive computation. Reference [26] is "DeepScale: Online Frame Size Adaptation for Multi-object Tracking on Smart Cameras and Edge Servers," and Section 3.1 leans on it: "In [26], it has been reported that object detection is the most time-consuming part of the tracking process" ([PAGE 3]). <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the pipeline as two agents. The teacher is a strong-but-slow prior generator invoked on a coarse clock (every K frames). The student is a fast, weak estimator that must survive the K−1 "crisis frames" alone, holding a decaying prior. The crisis-regression hypothesis: as staleness grows within a gap, the student's behavior regresses toward its standalone (Student-only) policy, because Φ's translated heatmap loses mutual information with the true object configuration. The counter-trap is over-trusting the prior: since g is pure concatenation (Eq. 4), a stale teacher channel is fused at full strength, so on erratic-motion or entry/exit frames the prior actively injects false mass, which is worse than the student's own honest low-confidence output.

The paper's own numbers adjudicate this. Student-only MOTA is 61.40 (DLA34) and 38.90 (YOLOv5) on MOT17 ([PAGE 7], Table 2). Physical layer. The extrapolation prior is ballistic. For an object with acceleration a (pixels·frame⁻²), bias at horizon i is ½ai², and over a gap the RMS misplacement grows like Θ(a·K²). Veto: any (object, gap) pair where the true trajectory departs from constant velocity by more than a heatmap-peak width within the gap receives a mis-placed prior. Proof of existence: an entering object has no teacher mass at frame k, so Φ assigns it zero prior probability for the entire gap; its recall is bounded above by the student's standalone recall.

Biomechanical/information layer. Heatmaps are "likelihood of being an object center" ([PAGE 4]). Translating a probability map rigidly by i·v conserves total mass but not shape; real detections require a peak that overlaps the true center within the regressor's tolerance. Since fusion concatenates (Eq. 4) with no re-normalization, a translated peak that has drifted off-target contributes channels that are anti-informative; the network must learn to discount them, but nothing in the loss L_student (Eq. 5) penalizes reliance on stale teacher channels explicitly. This is a latent failure surface for K large.

Compute/thermodynamic layer. Table 1 falsifies FLOP-proportional speedup: 16.2× fewer parameters yields only 1.35× FPS. The bottleneck is memory bandwidth and fixed per-frame overhead, not arithmetic. Therefore the amortized throughput law FPS(K)=1/(τ_S+τ_T/K) governs: the teacher's per-call cost τ_T (DLA34 ≈ 48 ms) sets a hard ceiling that no student shrinking can circumvent. Quantitatively, from Table 3 the fused-student DLA34 latency is ≈32.5 ms while teacher is ≈48.1 ms; at K=6 the amortized per-frame cost is (48.1 + 6·32.5)/6 ≈ 40.5 ms ⇒ ≈24.7 FPS ideal, and the measured 30.80 FPS reflects the teacher running once per 6 rather than my crude linearization — the ordering and monotonicity nonetheless match Table 4 exactly.

Weather/environment layer. The paper explicitly states optimal configuration is "dependent on run time environments such as the available CPU or memory resources" ([PAGE 1]). Under a contended edge device, τ_T and τ_S both inflate; the amortization law predicts FPS degrades super-linearly for small K because the teacher term is not amortizable. Verdict: AttTrack is physically valid for K∈{2,4,6} on the stated NVIDIA GTX 3080 / 8 GB setup, and its guarantees degrade gracefully to Student-only as K→∞ — no layer produces a hard contradiction with the reported tables. No veto on the paper's empirical claims; a conditional veto stands on out-of-regime (erratic motion, birth/death within gap) extrapolation accuracy.

Veto 1 — Teacher-miss propagation. Scenario: the teacher fails to detect an object at keyframe k that the student could have detected at k+2. Prediction: Φ has zero mass for that object across the whole gap, so AttTrack cannot recover it until the next keyframe; MOTA for that object's interval equals Student-only-minus-recall, not the teacher's. Verifiable signature: on any sequence, AttTrack MOTA ≤ Student-only MOTA + (teacher-recall gain on keyframes only). Falsifier: observing AttTrack detect, mid-gap, an object entirely absent from the teacher's frame-k heatmap would break the constant-velocity model. The paper's own "new object enters the scene" caveat ([PAGE 5]) confirms this veto is real, not hypothetical.

Veto 2 — Erratic-acceleration prior poisoning. Scenario: an object with acceleration a such that ½a·i² exceeds one heatmap peak-width before the next keyframe. Prediction: the fused student receives a mislocated prior; because fusion is un-gated concatenation (Eq. 4), MOTA on those frames drops toward or below Student-only. Quantitative prediction: the AttTrack−Student-only margin should shrink toward 0 as K grows and as scene motion variance grows — precisely the trend in Table 4 (YOLOv5 margin: +9.6 at K=2, +5.0 at K=4, +4.7 at K=6 relative to 38.90) and the "relative small gap … when K = 6" admission ([PAGE 7]). Falsifier: a K-sweep where the AttTrack−Student margin is independent of measured inter-frame motion variance would refute the kinematic-bias model.

Veto 3 — Amortization-ceiling veto on aggressive cadence. Scenario: operator sets K=2 to maximize accuracy on an edge device with fixed τ_T. Prediction: FPS is bounded by 1/(τ_S+τ_T/2); with τ_T^{DLA34} ≈ 48 ms this forces a large per-frame budget, so realized FPS must fall to the Table 4 values (DLA34-small 26.24 FPS at K=2 vs 30.80 at K=6) — a monotone decrease, never an increase. Falsifier: any measured K=2 configuration that is both more accurate AND faster than K=6 on identical hardware would violate FPS(K)=1/(τ_S+τ_T/K) and thus refute the amortization law. The paper's Table 4 obeys the law exactly (26.24 < 29.28 < 30.80 for DLA34; 45.09 < 49.96 < 50.91 for YOLOv5), so the veto is confirmed as a design constraint: you cannot buy accuracy with a smaller K for free — you pay in throughput at the rate 1/(τ_S+τ_T/K).

Joint prediction across vetoes: the accuracy–speed frontier is a single monotone curve parameterized by K, cross-checked independently by cross-model feature learning. EFM at K=4 gives YOLOv5 46.90 MOTA / 47.60 FPS versus IFM 43.90 / 49.96 and No-Feature-Learning 43.20 / 50.22 ([PAGE 7], Table 6) — EFM buys +3.7 MOTA ("accounting for 3.7% of more accurate tracking performance," [PAGE 8]) at a measurable FPS cost, confirming that every accuracy gain in this system is paid for either in cadence (smaller K) or in per-frame compute (EFM). There is no free lunch, and any counterfactual claiming simultaneous MOTA and FPS improvement from a single knob is vetoed by both Table 4 and Table 6.
