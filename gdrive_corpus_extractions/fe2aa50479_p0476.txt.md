<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman's 1932 work sits at a critical junction between classical reflexology (Pavlov, Watson) and modern cognitive science. His genealogy rejects the simple S-R chain, inserting the "intervening variables" (goals, expectations, cognitive maps) that become the bedrock of later theories.

**Historical Lineage:**
-   **Pre-Tolman**: Pavlovian conditioning (stimulus-response bonds) and Thorndike's Law of Effect (trial-and-error learning). Both treat behavior as mechanically determined by immediate stimuli/reinforcements.
-   **Tolman's Break**: He argued rats in mazes learn *layouts*, not turn sequences. His "purposive behavior" required a latent cognitive structure—a map—that exists prior to reinforcement. This was revolutionary, foreshadowing Bartlett's schemas and modern predictive coding.
-   **Post-Tolman Influence**: Directly inspired Edward Torrance Watson's work on motivation, Neal Miller's drive-reduction theory (though Tolman critiqued pure drive-reduction), and ultimately the cognitive revolution of the 1950s-60s (Newell, Simon, Piaget). His emphasis on *expectancies* prefigured Bandura's social learning theory and modern reinforcement learning's value functions.

**Multi-Week/Month Traps (Historical & Conceptual):**
1.  **The "Black Box" Trap**: Behaviorists dismissed Tolman for positing unobservable "intervening variables." They demanded direct S-R laws. This trap nearly buried his ideas until cognitive science legitimized mental states.
2.  **The Reductionist Trap**: Later physiologists reduced "appetite" to specific peptide hormones (ghrelin, leptin), ignoring the *organismic* integration Tolman described. The "initiating state" became just a hormone level, losing the *meaningful demand* aspect.
3.  **The Computational Trap**: Modern AI agents optimize rewards in Markov Decision Processes, but they lack Tolman's *qualitative* cognitive maps. They don't "understand" space; they compute gradients. This misses the *gestalt* nature of his "means-end readiness."
4.  **The Social Cognition Trap**: Introspective behavior requires modeling other minds—a capability still poorly solved in AI. Current large language models mimic discourse but lack true *shared intentionality* (Tomasello). Tolman's framework exposes this gap: without a genuine theory of mind, communication is just pattern matching, not "purposeful behavior" toward a shared goal.

The retrocausal insight: Tolman’s system implies that *present* purpose is shaped by *future* goals (the "complementing physiological quiescence" or "avoided disturbance"). This teleonomic perspective was suppressed by mechanistic causality but is resurgent in active inference and free-energy principle frameworks.
