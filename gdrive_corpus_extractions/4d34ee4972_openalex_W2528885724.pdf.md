The source is Veliov’s 1992 working paper on guaranteed control of uncertain systems via funnel equations and regulation maps. The multi-hop derivation begins with an uncertain differential inclusion in [PAGE 4]: the paper studies “the differential inclusion” written in OCR as “i E G(x, t) + v(t)” and immediately says it “presents an uncertain control system.” The state is \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), time is \(t\in[t_0,T]\), \(G(x,t)\) is the set of admissible control velocities, and \(v(t)\in V(t)\) is an unknown exogenous disturbance. The controller chooses a velocity from \(G(x,t)\) using exact current state information; the paper states on [PAGE 5] that “Exact knowledge of the current state x(t) (and possibly of the current value of the uncertainty v(t)) is supposed when choosing the velocity from G(x, t) at the current moment t.” The objective is robust: for every initial state in \(X_0\), every measurable disturbance realization in \(V(\cdot)\), keep the trajectory inside a state/viability constraint \(Y(t)\), and reach a target \(M_T\) at time \(T\).

The first inferential step is to replace “find a robust control” by “find a tube \(W(\cdot)\) with weak invariance.” Definition 1 on [PAGE 5] defines weak invariance with respect to an inclusion or a family: for every \(\tau\) and every \(x\in W(\tau)\), there must exist an absolutely continuous trajectory starting at \(x\) that stays in \(W(t)\) for \(t\in[\tau,T]\). For a family of inclusions, [PAGE 5] says the mapping “is weakly invariant w.r. to the family (1.7) if it is weakly invariant w.r. to everyone of the inclusions in this family.” This is already a gray-zone: weak invariance is existential in the trajectory, not necessarily realizable by a single feedback map. The paper later repairs this by constructing a closed-loop regulation map \(\Phi\) and proving stable invariance.

The second step is the infinitesimal characterization by a funnel equation. On [PAGE 15], Theorem 3.1 states: “X(.) E W [to, TI is weakly invariant w. r. to (3.1) if and only if (3.2) is satisfied.” Here (3.2) is a backward-in-time Hausdorff discrepancy condition: for almost every \(t\), the set \(X(t-h)\) must lie, up to \(o(h)\), in the union of \(x-hF(x,t)\) over \(x\in X(t)\). The approximate version is a funnel inequality, and [PAGE 16] says: “The approximate version of (3.2) has the form of a “funnel” inequality.” This is the critical approximation lever: one does not need an exact weakly invariant tube if one can solve a funnel inequality with an integrable residual \(p(t)\); the residual accumulates into an error tube.

The third step is Filippov’s lemma. On [PAGE 8], Lemma 2.1 says that for every \(X(\cdot)\in W[t_0,T]\) the discrepancy function \(p_{X(\cdot)}(\cdot)\) is integrable. The extended Filippov theorem then converts a small funnel residual into the existence of a weakly invariant tube \(W(\cdot)\) lying near \(X(\cdot)\), with an exponential factor depending on the local Lipschitz constant \(\lambda(\cdot)\). This explains why the paper’s stable-invariance estimates are exponential: the Hausdorff distance between tubes can grow as \(\exp(\int \lambda)\).

The fourth step is the closed-loop repair. On [PAGE 29], Theorem 5.1 states: “Under the suppositions A1 and A2 the tube W(.) has the stable invariance property w.r. to (5.2).” The closed-loop inclusion is \(\dot x\in\Phi(x,t)\), where \(\Phi\) is defined via the lower Dini derivative of a distance-like function \(\psi\) and a threshold \(\varphi\). The source admits the constructive difficulty on [PAGE 30]: “The definition of the regulation mapping @ by (4.3), (4.5), (4.6) is not quite explicit since it uses the Lipschitz constant A(-) and the Dini derivative of +.” The paper then defines extremal directions \(\Phi_e\), using only the geometry of \(F(x,t)\) relative to the projection of \(x\) on \(W(t)\), and proves they lie inside the regulation map off a measure-zero set. This is important for practice: one can implement an extremal aiming rule without differentiating \(\psi\) exactly.

The fifth step is the uncertain-system case. On [PAGE 36], Theorem 7.1 gives a necessary-and-sufficient condition: a regulation map satisfying extendibility, state constraint, and target guarantee exists if and only if the funnel equation for the uncertain family has a solution \(W(\cdot)\) satisfying the boundary conditions. On [PAGE 37] the paper notes that the full map may require the current disturbance \(v(\cdot)\), but “the set @(z, t, v) contains a subset that is independent of v” via the extremal map \(\Phi_e(z,t)\). The gray-zone audit is therefore: exact state observation is assumed; disturbances are set-valued but must satisfy A4; the tube must be in the special class \(W[t_0,T]\); exact funnel equations are replaced by inequalities; and time-dependent discretization remains open. Indeed [PAGE 39] states: “The convergence of the Euler scheme for (8.1) in the case of a tube W(.) dependant on the time ... is still an open problem.”

In short, the derivation chain is: uncertain inclusion \(\rightarrow\) family of inclusions \(\rightarrow\) weak invariance tube \(\rightarrow\) funnel equation/inequality \(\rightarrow\) Filippov approximation \(\rightarrow\) stable invariance under a closed-loop regulation map \(\rightarrow\) guaranteed target/state bounds. Breakdown occurs if the tube is not weakly invariant, if the residual \(p\) or Lipschitz \(\lambda\) is nonintegrable, if \(V(t)\) is not convex or not in the \(W\)-class, if exact state is unavailable, or if one asks a naive Euler scheme to certify a time-dependent nonconvex discontinuous feedback map without the constant-\(W\) theorem.

Let \(x(t)\in\mathbb R^n\), \(t\in[t_0,T]\) in seconds, \([\mathrm{x}]\) denote the physical unit of the state coordinate, and \(\dot x\) have unit \([\mathrm{x}]/\mathrm{s}\). The basic uncertain inclusion is, following [PAGE 4],
\[
\dot x(t)\in G(x(t),t)+v(t),\qquad v(t)\in V(t),
\]
with \(X_0\subset\mathbb R^n\), state constraint \(x(t)\in Y(t)\), and target \(x(T)\in M_T\). The OCR on [PAGE 4] prints “i E G(x, t) + v(t)” and calls it an uncertain control system. The paper’s assumptions, stated on [PAGE 7], are:

- A1: \(F:\mathbb R^n\times[t_0,T]\to\mathbb R^n\) is convex and compact valued; \(t\mapsto F(\cdot,x)\) is measurable; for every compact \(S\) there are \(m=m(S)\) and integrable upper semicontinuous \(\lambda(\cdot)=\lambda(\cdot;S)\) with
\[
|F(x,t)|\le m,\qquad F(t,x')\subset F(x,t)+\lambda(t)|x-x'|,
\]
for \(x,x'\in S\). Units: \(m\in[\mathrm{x}]/\mathrm{s}\), \(\lambda(t)\in\mathrm{s}^{-1}\).
- A2: bounded-time confinement of trajectories starting in a compact set.
- A3: \(\operatorname{graph}Y=\{(t,x):x\in Y(t)\}\) is closed [PAGE 7].
- A4: \(V\) is given by (1.9), \(V(\cdot)\in W[t_0,T]\) is convex valued, and \(F(x,t)=G(x,t)+V(t)\) satisfies A1 and A2.

The paper’s tube class \(W[t_0,T]\), defined on [PAGE 7], requires compact-valued upper semicontinuity and a Hausdorff Lipschitz condition \(W(s)\subset W(t)+M(t-s)B\) for \(s\le t\). Here \(M\in[\mathrm{x}]/\mathrm{s}\).

The stable-invariance definition on [PAGE 6] is:
\[
\operatorname{dist}(x(t),W(t))\le
\exp\!\left(\int_\tau^t \rho(s)\,ds\right)
\operatorname{dist}(x,W(\tau)),
\]
for every solution starting near \(W(\tau)\); \(\rho(\cdot)\in L^1([t_0,T];\mathbb R_{\ge0})\) has units \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), so \(\int_\tau^t\rho(s)ds\) is dimensionless. The source says: “If the above requirements are satisfied for 6 = 0 only, the tube W(-) will be (merely) called invariant” [PAGE 6]. This is the exact distinction between stable invariance and mere invariance.

The funnel discrepancy operator is, from [PAGE 8] and [PAGE 15],
\[
p_X(t)=
\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac1h
H_+\!\left(
X(t-h),\,
\bigcup_{x\in X(t)}\bigl(x-hF(x,t)\bigr)
\right),
\]
where \(H_+(P,Q)=\inf\{\alpha\ge0:P\subset Q+\alpha B\}\) is the Hausdorff semi-distance. Units: \(H_+\) is in \([\mathrm{x}]\), division by \(h\in\mathrm{s}\) gives \(p_X(t)\in[\mathrm{x}]/\mathrm{s}\). Lemma 2.1 on [PAGE 8] states: “Let conditions A1 and A2 be satisfied. Then for every X ( - ) E W[to, TI the function px(.)(.) is integrable on [to, TI.” The exact funnel equation of Theorem 3.1 [PAGE 15] is
\[
p_X(t)=0\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(t_0,T],
\]
and Theorem 3.1 says weak invariance with respect to (3.1) holds iff this equation holds. The approximate funnel inequality on [PAGE 16] is
\[
p_X(t)\le p(t)\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(t_0,T],
\]
where \(p(t)\in[\mathrm{x}]/\mathrm{s}\) is an integrable residual. The state-constrained version is Theorem 3.2 with equation (3.6) [PAGE 16], and the uncertain-family version is Theorem 3.3 with equation (3.9) [PAGE 17]: “Theorem 3.3 X(-) E W[to,T] is weakly invariant w.r. to the family (3.4),(3.7) if and only if (3.9) is satisfied.”

The closed-loop regulation operator is defined on [PAGE 20] as
\[
\Phi(x,t)=\{y\in F(x,t):D^-\psi(x,t;y)\le\varphi(x,t)\},
\]
where \(D^-\psi(x,t;y)\) is the lower Dini derivative in direction \((y,1)\). In the main construction, \(\psi(x,t)=\operatorname{dist}(x,W(t))\) and \(\varphi(x,t)=\lambda(t)\operatorname{dist}(x,W(t))\) for the exact weakly invariant tube; for the approximate case, the modified threshold is \(\lambda(t)\operatorname{dist}(x,W(t))+p(t)\), as used in Section 6 [PAGE 34-35]. The stable-bound estimate in Theorem 6.1 [PAGE 35] is
\[
\operatorname{dist}(x(t),Y(t))\le
\exp\!\left(\int_{t_0}^t\lambda(s)ds\right)
\left(
\operatorname{dist}(x(t_0),W(t_0))+
\int_{t_0}^t p(s)ds
\right),
\]
and the terminal bound is the same expression at \(t=T\) with target \(M_T\). Units are consistent because \(\lambda(s)ds\) is dimensionless and both distances and \(\int p\) are in \([\mathrm{x}]\).

The extremal regulation map on [PAGE 30] is
\[
\Phi_e(x,t)=
\bigcup_{z\in P_{W(t)}x}
\operatorname*{Arg\,max}_{y\in F(x,t)}(z-x,y),
\]
where \(P_{W(t)}x\) is the projection set. The source says on [PAGE 30] that “The definition of the regulation mapping @ by (4.3), (4.5), (4.6) is not quite explicit since it uses the Lipschitz constant A(-) and the Dini derivative of +.” The extremal map is the constructive subset. For constant \(W\), (4.8) gives another map \(\Phi_0\) [PAGE 21], and Corollary 5.2 [PAGE 31] asserts stable invariance with respect to \(\dot x\in\Phi_0(x,t)\).

For the uncertain system, [PAGE 36] defines a regulation map \(\Phi(x,t,v)\subset G(x,t)\) and Theorem 7.1 states: “A regulation map @ satisfying 1) and 2) exists if and only if the funnel equation (3.9) has a solution W(-) E W[to,T] satisfying the boundary conditions.” The intended boundary conditions are the initial containment \(X_0\subset W(t_0)\) and the terminal target containment \(W(T)\subset M_T\), with approximate versions \(X_0\subset W(t_0)+r(t_0)B\) and \(W(T)\subset M_T+r(T)B\) [PAGE 35-37].

Singularity and breakdown regimes: (i) if \(p_X(t)\) is not integrable or the limit fails on a positive-measure set, the Filippov approximation and the exponential error bounds do not apply; (ii) if \(\lambda(\cdot)\) has a nonintegrable singularity, \(\exp(\int\lambda)\) blows up and stable invariance degenerates; (iii) if \(\delta=0\), stable invariance reduces to mere invariance and robustness to initial perturbation is lost [PAGE 6]; (iv) if \(V(t)\) is not convex or A4 fails, Theorem 3.3/7.1 cannot be invoked; (v) if the state is not exactly observed, the paper explicitly defers the problem, saying on [PAGE 37] that “The much more complicated case of incompletelinaccurate measurement will be considered in a forthcoming paper”; (vi) if the tube is time-dependent, the Euler convergence theorem of Section 8 is not available: [PAGE 39] says “The convergence of the Euler scheme for (8.1) in the case of a tube W(.) dependant on the time ... is still an open problem.” The Section 8 theorem is only for constant \(W\) and the map (4.8) [PAGE 38].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is explicit in the paper’s introduction and references. On [PAGE 4] the author says, “The present paper is a continuation of the paper [28],” and traces the problem to “the basic works of Krasovskii (see [14]) in the framework of the differential games.” On [PAGE 5] the paper cites weak invariance/viability and invariance of set-valued mappings as central notions, with references to Aubin, Krasovskii, and Kurzhanski. The funnel-equation branch is identified on [PAGE 5] as “an alternative approach, providing a suitable basis for constructive theory” involving “generalized differential equations called funnel equations [16, 17, 22, 23, 27].” The reference list on [PAGE 41-42] confirms the genealogy: Filippov 1967 for the discrepancy lemma, Krasovskii–Subbotin for positional differential games, Aubin’s viability theory, Kurzhanski–Filippova and Kurzhanski–Nikonov for funnel equations, Panasyuk for attainable-set dynamics, Tolstonogov for solution funnels, and Veliov’s own continuation paper [28].

The retrocausal significance is that the 1992 paper converts an older game-theoretic “stable bridge” idea into a set-valued analytic certificate: a boundary-value problem for a funnel equation. The later-looking objects in the paper—stable invariance, exponential tube error, extremal aiming, and Euler discretization of nonconvex u.s.c. feedback—are all prefigured by the older viability and differential-game lineage. The multi-week trap is to read the paper as if weak invariance alone were the guarantee. It is not: weak invariance is existential; stable invariance adds a \(\delta>0\) neighborhood and the exponential estimate. A second multi-week trap is to assume that because a funnel equation has a solution, a robust feedback is automatically implementable. The paper’s own correction is that the closed-loop map may be discontinuous and nonconvex, so Section 4 proves an existence theorem for a special Dini-derivative-structured inclusion; [PAGE 19] notes that general u.s.c. nonconvex inclusions need not have solutions. A third trap is to extrapolate the Section 8 Euler convergence theorem to time-dependent tubes; the paper explicitly flags that as open on [PAGE 39].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The “crisis habit regression” here is the analyst’s tendency, under uncertainty, to collapse the set-valued disturbance \(V(t)\) to a nominal \(v(t)\), convexify a nonconvex feedback map, and then run an Euler scheme as if the closed-loop system were a standard convex differential inclusion. The paper blocks that regression in several places. On [PAGE 19] it says “Well known examples show that such a differential inclusion does not have a solution, in general,” referring to u.s.c. nonconvex right-hand sides. The counter-trap is to keep the full set-valued disturbance model and to verify the funnel residual \(p(t)\), the Lipschitz-integrability of \(\lambda(t)\), and the nonemptiness of the Dini-structured map \(\Phi\). The clock is the endpoint \(T\): the target condition \(x(T)\in M_T\), the boundary condition \(W(T)\subset M_T\), and the accumulated error \(\int_{t_0}^T p(s)ds\) all enter the terminal bound. A controller that optimizes only the instantaneous funnel residual without checking the terminal boundary can be certifiably wrong at the clock even if it looks good on a short horizon.

Recursive theory-of-mind applies to what the controller is allowed to know. The paper assumes exact current state observation and, for the full map, possibly current disturbance \(v(t)\); [PAGE 37] says the regulation map \(\Phi(z,t,v)\) “requires knowledge of the current value of the disturbance v(.).” But it also says the set contains a subset independent of \(v\), namely the extremal map \(\Phi_e\) [PAGE 37]. Thus the counter-trap is: if the disturbance is not observed, do not pretend the full \(\Phi(z,t,v)\) is available; use the extremal subset only if the propositions’ hypotheses hold. The clock also changes the recursive belief: near \(T\), the target tube \(M_T\) dominates the state-constraint tube \(Y(t)\); if \(W(T)\not\subset M_T\), Theorem 7.1’s necessary-and-sufficient condition fails, and no amount of short-horizon tuning repairs the terminal guarantee.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
There is no biomechanical or weather model in the source; the cross-layer veto must be stated in the paper’s own physical/mathematical terms. The actuator/physical layer is vetoed if \(G(x,t)\) or \(F(x,t)\) fails A1: not convex compact valued, not measurable in \(t\), not locally bounded, or not locally Lipschitz in \(x\). The state-constraint layer is vetoed if \(\operatorname{graph}Y\) is not closed (A3) [PAGE 7], because the viability tube and the closed state constraint cannot be identified. The exogenous-disturbance layer—the “weather” analogue—is vetoed if \(V(t)\) is not convex or if \(V(\cdot)\notin W[t_0,T]\), since A4 on [PAGE 7] requires \(V\) to be given by (1.9), convex valued, and such that \(F=G+V\) satisfies A1 and A2. The observation layer is vetoed if current state is not exact; [PAGE 37] explicitly defers incomplete/inaccurate measurement to a forthcoming paper. The discretization layer is vetoed for time-dependent \(W\) because the Euler convergence theorem is only proved for constant \(W\) and map (4.8) [PAGE 38], with the time-dependent case declared open on [PAGE 39].

A proof-level veto adjudication is therefore: if all of A1–A4 hold, the graph of \(Y\) is closed, the tube \(W(\cdot)\) lies in \(W[t_0,T]\), the funnel residual \(p(\cdot)\) is integrable, and the boundary conditions \(X_0\subset W(t_0)\), \(W(T)\subset M_T\) hold, then the paper supplies a regulation map and exponential tube bounds [PAGE 29, PAGE 35, PAGE 36]. If any one of those conditions fails, the theorem does not certify physical realizability; the correct adjudication is “no guarantee,” not “approximately the same.” The paper’s own statement on [PAGE 30] that the regulation map is “not quite explicit” is also a practical veto against claiming a turnkey explicit controller from (4.3), (4.5), (4.6) alone; the extremal map \(\Phi_e\) is the constructive fallback, and Proposition 5.2 places it inside the full regulation map off a measure-zero set [PAGE 30].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
