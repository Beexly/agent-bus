Multi-week traps. Longitudinal validity across weeks of training is threatened by non-stationarities the patent does not address. Sensor mounting drift alters the rigid-body lever arm α × r + ω × (ω × r), so a force baseline from week 1 is not comparable to week 8 without recalibration [PAGE 18]. Athlete physiological adaptation—plasma volume expansion, heart-rate drift, and changed respiratory economy—confounds the interpretation of HR, SpO₂, and lactate trends even when sensors remain attached [PAGE 18]. GPS geometric dilution of precision (GDOP) varies with satellite constellation geometry over days to weeks, introducing slowly varying position bias into the drift-correction loop [PAGE 18]. Battery capacity fade reduces effective sampling duty cycle, and firmware updates can change microcontroller interrogation latency relative to the nominal ≥1 Hz floor [PAGE 18]. Because the device promises "longitudinal data from the training and competition environment" [PAGE 18], any uncorrected sensor drift or physiological adaptation creates a hidden distribution shift that masquerades as athlete performance change.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. When the telemetry chain fails—GPS dropout, accelerometer clipping at the rowing catch, or physiological contact loss—coaches regress to the legacy methods explicitly named in the patent: dedicated sports laboratories, simulated devices, video-footage analysis, indoor tank procedures, and ergometer studies [PAGE 18]. This is habit regression under crisis: the real-time system collapses, and the operator falls back to offline, post-hoc observation.

Counter-trap. The inverse failure mode is automation bias: trusting the "accurate and continuous output" [PAGE 18] so absolutely that subjective athlete reports (perceived exertion, technique feel, pain, and crew synchronisation quality) are downweighted. Because the patent lists physiological sensors as merely "preferable" [PAGE 18], a coach may treat kinematic telemetry as ground truth while ignoring athlete-reported load, producing a mis-calibrated training prescription.

Clock. The system clock defines the causal control cycle. GPS interrogation at ≥1 Hz is the slow clock; accelerometer sampling is the fast clock. Rowing stroke period is approximately 0.5–2.0 s [PAGE 18]. If coach decision latency or radio transmission delay exceeds one stroke period, feedback arrives after the movement is complete, breaking the real-time loop and reducing the device to a logging instrument rather than an intervention tool. Inter-athlete clock synchronisation across a crew must be tighter than the stroke period, otherwise stroke-rate correlation between rowers is spurious.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto. The patent states existing monitoring equipment is "too heavy, expensive, obtrusive or less reliable" [PAGE 18]. For on-water use, GPS L-band signals are attenuated by water and boat structure, and surface multipath from the hull corrupts position fixes; accelerometer housings must be waterproofed against spray and biofouling. Any unit exceeding mass or encumbrance thresholds alters athlete biomechanics and voids the measurement.

Biomechanical layer veto. Rowing force is applied at the oarlock and foot stretcher; a boat-mounted acceleration signal mixes athlete-applied force with hull hydrodynamics and boat speed. Per-athlete force attribution in a crew requires known mounting geometry and time-stamped data with inter-sensor clock offset ≪ stroke period [PAGE 18]; without this constraint, stroke synchronisation metrics are unobservable.

Weather / environmental layer veto. Real-time outdoor operation exposes GPS to rain, spray, atmospheric ionospheric delay, and urban or shoreline multipath [PAGE 18]. Wind and current alter boat speed independently of athlete power, so velocity alone is not a pure performance metric and must be vetoed as a standalone force proxy when environmental forcing exceeds athlete force magnitude.

Adjudication rule. If any layer fails—GPS outage τ > 1 s, accelerometer clipping, physiological sensor contact loss, mounting-frame ambiguity, or environmental forcing dominating the kinematic signal—the system output is vetoed. Fallback is bounded accelerometer-only dead reckoning for ≤1 s, or regression to legacy video/ergometer assessment [PAGE 18]. No output is presented as validated when a veto condition is active.
