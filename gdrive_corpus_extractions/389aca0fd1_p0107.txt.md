If the machine infers “approach light” while the operator intends “rest,” the mutual models diverge and the combined system oscillates at the difference frequency between the two loops. Stable collaboration requires shared state estimation and a common clock for purpose updates.

When V_{sat}=1, issue a hold-current command and disable integration to prevent windup. This veto prevents the growing oscillation Wiener describes when feedback is “overloaded by increasing the amplification” [PAGE 194].

Biomechanical layer veto: human limb impedance Z_m(s)=Ms^2+Bs+K with reflex delay \tau\approx50\text{–}100\,\mathrm{ms}. A prosthetic or rudder controller must not inject energy near mechanical resonance, nor add phase lag exceeding the available phase margin. Veto condition V_{bio}=1 iff predicted closed-loop phase margin PM<PM_{min} (e.g., 30°) at gain crossover, or if commanded bandwidth exceeds the tremor-onset frequency associated with cerebellar injury. This prevents the controller from exciting the intention-tremor analogue [PAGE 194].

Environmental/weather layer veto: illuminance E_v in lux. The photo-tropic loop requires E_v>E_{min} for usable signal-to-noise ratio. “In the absence of light” the machine loses purpose and the postural tremor emerges [PAGE 194]. Veto condition V_{env}=1 iff E_v<E_{min} while a purpose-driven task is active; then switch to an alternative reference (tactile, sonar, or stored waypoint) or halt motion.

Composite veto logic: V=1 \iff V_{sat}\lor V_{bio}\lor V_{env}\lor (r(t)\equiv0\land\text{antagonism active}). When V=1, the system enters a safe hold state and raises an alert. This layered adjudication ensures that no single saturated amplifier, absent light source, or biomechanical resonance can drive the machine into the tremor regimes Wiener maps onto human cerebellar and Parkinsonian pathology.
