Let phenotype x be measured in units U (for example metres for a length trait or an arbitrary trait index). The population mean is mu in U, and a deviation is x - mu in U. For offspring o and mid-parent m = (x_female + x_male)/2, the Galton regression operator is

E[x_o - mu | x_m] = b (x_m - mu),

where b = Cov(x_o, x_m) / Var(x_m). Under the standard additive assumptions—random mating, no dominance, no epistasis, no maternal or cultural effects, constant environment, and normally distributed deviations—b equals the narrow-sense heritability h^2 = V_A / V_P, dimensionless because it is U/U. The breeder’s equation for one generation of selection is R = h^2 S, with selection differential S in U and response R in U per generation. Galton’s b is described in the source as arising from “Fraternal Deviations from their respective true Mid-Fraternities” [PAGE 1]; in modern notation it is a within-family regression/robust scale parameter, not a universal constant. Its units remain dimensionless when both deviations are measured in the same U, but its numerical value collapses if Var(x_m) -> 0, producing a 0/0 singularity in which regression toward M is undefined.

Galton’s “Personal and Latent Elements” can be represented as latent contributions to breeding value. Let descendant d inherit from ancestor set A_d:

x_d = mu + sum_{a in A_d} w_{da} G_a + epsilon_d, epsilon_d ~ (0, V_E),

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The term “retrocausal” here is used only as an epistemological metaphor: Galton infers ancestral states from descendant variation rather than observing future causes acting backward. The supplied page [PAGE 1] belongs to the biometrical tradition in which regression toward M is both a statistical description of parent-offspring scatter and a hypothesis about hereditary transmission. Its lineage runs through Galton’s law of ancestral inheritance, the biometrician-Mendelian controversy, Fisher’s 1918 reconciliation of Mendelian factors with biometric correlations, and the infinitesimal model of quantitative genetics. Within that lineage, “reversion” to zebra-like stripes on a donkey [PAGE 1] is later reinterpreted not as telegony from a previous mate but as expression of conserved developmental-genetic programs or atavistic regulatory states. The weed analogy [PAGE 1] prefigures frequency-dependent invasion and propagule-bank models: rare hardy types persist when not heavily outmatched, which is mathematically analogous to a latent allele persisting at low frequency until competition relaxes.

A multi-week or multi-generational trap is to extrapolate a single-generation regression coefficient b into long-term breed improvement. Response changes as V_A is eroded by selection—the Bulmer effect—and as the population approaches mutation-selection balance or fixation. Another trap is treating the general population mean M as fixed; if the optimum moves because weather, nutrition, or competition changes, regression toward the old M becomes maladaptive. A third trap is assuming equal numbers of Personal and Latent Elements imply equal variety [PAGE 1]; in the infinitesimal model, rare large-effect loci can dominate variance even when common loci are numerous. The source fragment itself truncates before Galton completes the clause about children in the same family taking [PAGE 1], so any genealogy that finishes that sentence is speculative rather than source-grounded.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression is the expression of latent ancestral or developmental phenotypes under stress, analogous to Galton’s reversion and weed persistence [PAGE 1]. Formally, let environmental stress sigma_t increase the probability p(sigma_t) that a descendant expresses a latent ancestral phenotype A even when parents do not display it: p'(sigma) > 0. Under crisis, the population mean can jump toward the ancestral attractor not because genes changed in one generation but because the reaction norm gamma_g(E) exposes cryptic variation. The counter-trap is to assume that selecting against the expressed crisis habit removes the liability. If the liability is latent and segregating, selection reduces its frequency but does not erase the reservoir; recurrence is expected when stress returns. The clock is generational: neutral fixation times scale with effective population size N_e generations, selected response accumulates as R_t = h_t^2 S_t per generation, and h_t^2 itself declines as V_{A,t} is consumed by selection and mutation-selection balance. A recursive model is

V_{A,t+1} = (1 - 1/(2N_e)) V_{A,t} + M - B(S_t, V_{A,t}),

x_{t+1} = mu + h_t^2 (x_t - mu) + eta_t,

where B is the Bulmer reduction in variance due to selection, M is mutational input in U^2 generation^{-1}, and eta_t is environmental noise. Crisis habit regression predicts a temporary increase in expressed ancestral variance; the counter-trap predicts relapse after selection relaxes; the clock predicts that the relapse lag is set by N_e, mutation rate, and the decay of the environmental stress signal. If weather or management changes faster than the genetic clock, the breeder’s equation trained in one regime will mispredict the next.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any hereditary claim from [PAGE 1] must pass three veto layers. Physical/developmental veto: zebra-looking stripes on a donkey [PAGE 1] are permissible only if the donkey genome contains a functional patterning pathway—e.g., a Turing reaction-diffusion or melanocyte-distribution mechanism—and if stripe expression does not violate conservation of mass, energy, or developmental timing. A claimed transmission that requires novel anatomy not present in the developmental repertoire is vetoed. Biomechanical veto: coat pattern per se is mechanically neutral, but if “ancestral qualities” include limb proportions, speed, or load-bearing morphology, the phenotype must satisfy musculoskeletal scaling and joint-load constraints; extreme ancestral morphotypes can be vetoed on mechanical failure grounds even if genetically present. Weather veto: heritability is environment-specific. If V_P = V_A + V_E + 2Cov(G,E), then h^2 = V_A / V_P falls as weather-driven V_E rises. For two environments E_1 and E_2, cross-environment prediction requires the genetic correlation r_{G,12}; response in E_2 to selection measured in E_1 scales approximately as r_{G,12} h_1 h_2 S. If weather makes r_{G,12} -> 0 or V_{E,2} -> infinity, then b_2 -> 0 despite unchanged V_A, and selection in E_1 does not predict response in E_2.

Breakdown and singularity conditions: V_P = 0 makes b undefined; V_A = 0 under strict pure-line fixation makes h^2 = 0 and R = 0 exactly; mutation-selection balance with M > 0 imposes a nonzero floor so regression does not fully disappear; nonzero Cov(G,E) violates the clean partition of Personal and Latent Elements; and non-additive dominance or epistasis makes parent-offspring regression dependent on allele frequency and mating structure. The source’s pure-breed claim that regression disappears “so far as” it is due to mediocre ancestral qualities [PAGE 1] is therefore vetoed for traits where regression is instead maintained by mutation-selection balance, developmental instability, or cross-environment plasticity.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
