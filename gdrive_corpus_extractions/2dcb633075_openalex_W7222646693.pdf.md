<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All equations below are **standard physics / psychometrics**, supplied by me to formalize claims the source states only in words. Where an equation is not in the source, it is marked **[DERIVED]**. Units are SI throughout. Singularity and breakdown regimes are stated per operator.

**O1 — Contact impulse operator.** [PAGE 1] *"sprint velocity"*; [PAGE 6] Table 2 *"Sprint acceleration."* **[DERIVED from Newton's second law]**

$$\bar{F}_{h}\,\Delta t_{gc} \;=\; m\,\big(v_{\text{exit}} - v_{\text{entry}}\big),\qquad [\text{N}\cdot\text{s}] = [\text{kg}]\cdot[\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}]$$

*Breakdown:* as $\Delta t_{gc}\to 0^{+}$, $\bar{F}_h\to\infty$ — a removable singularity of the model, not of the athlete. Physical closure comes from tissue stress limits ($\bar{F}_h \lesssim 3\text{–}5\,mg$ in maximal sprinting) and from the friction ceiling in O3. Any protocol reporting $\bar{F}_h$ without $\Delta t_{gc}$ and $m$ is dimensionally incomplete.

**O2 — Change-of-direction deficit operator.** [PAGE 3] *"mechanical determinants of faster change-of-route velocity"*; [PAGE 6] Table 2 *"Change-of-direction mechanics."* **[DERIVED]**

$$\text{CODD} = t_{\text{COD}} - t_{\text{lin}}\;\;[\,\text{s}\,],\qquad \text{CODD}_{\%} = 100\!\left(\frac{t_{\text{COD}}}{t_{\text{lin}}}-1\right)$$

*Breakdown:* $\text{CODD}\to 0$ means the COD task has degenerated into a linear sprint (turn angle too shallow, entry velocity too low to force deceleration) — the test loses construct validity. Negative CODD indicates a distance mismatch between the two protocols.

**O3 — Friction-bounded braking operator.** [PAGE 3] *"braking pressure"*; [PAGE 6] *"Braking force capacity / Directional control during play."* **[DERIVED]**

$$F_{\text{brake}} \le \mu_s\,m\,g \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_{\text{decel}} \le \mu_s\,g$$

Required coefficient for a target deceleration: $\mu_{\text{req}} = a_{\text{decel}}/g$. A 70 kg athlete braking at $5\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$ needs $\mu_{\text{req}} = 0.51$; at $7\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$, $\mu_{\text{req}} = 0.71$. Dry third-generation synthetic shoe–turf µ ≈ 0.9; wet monsoon turf µ ≈ 0.55–0.65. **Wet-surface ceiling: $a_{\max}=5.4$–$6.4\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$; dry ceiling ≈ $8.8\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$.** The top ~25% of the elite distribution is therefore **truncated by the surface, not by the athlete.** This is the hardest veto in the file (see Cross-Layer).

**O4 — Series-elastic contact-time operator (proxy for surface confound).** [PAGE 6] *"Ground contact time / Sprint efficiency."* **[DERIVED; tested and largely dismissed]**

$$t_c \;\approx\; \pi\sqrt{\frac{m_e}{k_{\text{eff}}}},\qquad \frac{1}{k_{\text{eff}}}=\frac{1}{k_{\text{leg}}}+\frac{1}{k_{\text{surf}}},\qquad \frac{\delta t_c}{t_c}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta k_{\text{eff}}}{k_{\text{eff}}}$$

With $k_{\text{leg}} = 20\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$ and $k_{\text{surf}} = 200$–$1000\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$: $k_{\text{eff}} = 18.2$–$19.6\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$, i.e. $\delta t_c = +1.0\%$ to $+4.6\%$. *Adjudication:* bounded at the 5% usefulness threshold — **material only on very compliant surfaces (shock-padded or wet natural turf), sub-threshold on firm synthetic.** Veto partially upheld, conditionally.

**O5 — Proximal-to-distal segmental summation operator.** [PAGE 3] *"efficient sequencing of hip rotation, trunk motion, and stick acceleration"*; ref 7 (Lees et al., drag-flick). **[DERIVED]**

$$v_{\text{stick head}} = \left\|\sum_{i\in\{\text{hip,trunk,shoulder,wrist}\}} \boldsymbol{\omega}_i \times \mathbf{r}_i\right\|,\qquad [\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}]=[\text{rad}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}]\cdot[\text{m}]$$

*Breakdown:* if a distal segment peaks **before** its proximal parent (sequencing inversion), the cross-product terms partially cancel and the energy-transfer efficiency $\eta = \tfrac{1}{2}m_{\text{stick}}v^2 / W_{\text{muscle}}$ collapses despite unchanged muscular work. The source asserts this qualitatively; it supplies **no** angular-velocity values, no \(\eta\), no sample size.

**O6 — Reliability-attenuation operator (psychometric core).** [PAGE 1] *"Biomechanical markers improve reliability";* [PAGE 5] *"enhance reliability in athlete assessment."* **[DERIVED — classical test theory, standard]**

$$\rho_{TM}=\frac{\sigma_T}{\sqrt{\sigma_T^2+\sigma_e^2}},\qquad \text{CV}=\frac{s}{\bar{x}}\times100\%,\qquad \text{SEM}=s\sqrt{1-\rho}$$

*Units:* $\rho_{TM}$ dimensionless (proportion, 0–1); CV in %; SEM in the SI unit of the test.
*Breakdown regime:* $\sigma_e^2\to\infty \Rightarrow \rho_{TM}\to 0$ and selection becomes noise-driven — the "objectivity" claim collapses to a random number generator with a stopwatch. **Usefulness threshold in sport testing: CV ≤ 5%, ICC ≥ 0.90.** The source reports **zero** values of $\rho$, CV, or SEM across all 8 pages. This is the operator the paper needed and did not deploy.

**O7 — Selection / disparate-impact operator (the equity equation).** [PAGE 5] *"unequal instruction possibilities may additionally still have an impact on effects."* **[DERIVED]**

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[M \mid T, A\right]=\alpha+\beta T+\gamma A,\qquad \text{IR}=\frac{P(S=1\mid A=\text{high})}{P(S=1\mid A=\text{low})},\quad S=\mathbf{1}\{M\ge c\}$$

*Units:* $M$ in the SI unit of the test (s, m·s⁻¹, N); $T$ latent talent in the same unit; $A$ dimensionless access index $\in[0,1]$; $\beta$ dimensionless; $\gamma$ in the unit of $M$. IR dimensionless.
*Breakdown / decision rule:* **unbiased selection requires $\gamma=0$.** If $\gamma\neq 0$ the test is a *proxy for access*, not for talent, regardless of how precise the instrument is. The source's thesis implicitly assumes instrumentation drives $\gamma\to 0$; O7 shows instrumentation cannot — only redistribution of testing access and coaching can. This is the formal statement of the paper's own hedge and it **demotes** the paper's headline claim from "biomechanics reduces bias" to "biomechanics is bias-neutral and policy-dependent."

**O8 — Maturation and relative-age operators.** [PAGE 2] *"relative age effect"* (ref 9); [PAGE 1] *"Some mature earlier and seem physically stronger."* **[DERIVED]**

$$\dot{y}=a\,m^{b}\;\Rightarrow\; y_{\text{scaled}}=\frac{y}{m^{b}},\qquad \chi^2=N\sum_{q=1}^{4}\frac{(\phi_q-0.25)^2}{0.25},\quad \phi_q=\frac{n_q}{N}$$

*Units:* allometric exponent $b$ dimensionless (≈2/3 for length-normalized quantities); $\chi^2$ dimensionless with 3 df; $\phi_q$ proportion.
*Breakdown:* $b$ estimated on a maturity-heterogeneous cohort is itself maturity-biased — the correction contaminates itself unless $b$ is fixed a priori or predicted adult height is used. $\chi^2$ invalid when any expected cell count $<5$ (small district cohorts). **The paper never applies O8.** It cites the relative-age literature [PAGE 2] and the maturation confound [PAGE 5], then recommends unadjusted "standardized benchmarks" [PAGE 3] — a direct contradiction.

**Composite verdict on the operator set:** the source supplies *constructs* (Tables 1–2, [PAGE 5–6]) but no *operators*. Six of eight operators above had to be imported. The literature review is therefore a **hypothesis register**, not a measurement protocol, and should be cited as such.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Three-agent recursion under selection-clock pressure.**

*Level 0 — the athlete's self-model.* The athlete does not know their own latent $T$; they observe noisy performance and infer ability. Under a published criterion, the athlete's model becomes criterion-shaped: they train the test, not the sport. Formally, expected payoff to training investment $x$ is $\max_x\big[\pi\cdot P(M(x)\ge c) - \kappa x\big]$, which is maximized by allocating $x$ to the **most test-correlated** quality, not the most competition-relevant one.

*Level 1 — the coach's model of the athlete.* [PAGE 1]: *"Skilled coaches can frequently understand technical ability, tactical recognition, and physical features that suggest future overall performance."* Under time pressure the coach regresses to Type-1 pattern matching. **Crisis habit regression:** the paper's own diagnosis at [PAGE 2] — *"Selection trials are frequently brief, sometimes lasting only a few days"* — is a *clock* forcing heuristic substitution. When the clock is short, coaches do not become more objective; they become more confident in the eye test. This is the regression the biomechanics proposal is meant to interrupt, and it is a **behavioral** mechanism, not a technical one. Note the paper never models it.

*Level 2 — the coach's model of the administrator's model.* [PAGE 5]: *"Hancock et al. (2021) argue that transparent choice structures help lessen perceptions of bias. When athletes understand the standards used for evaluation, agree with the system will increases."* This is a second-order belief claim — the administrator publishes criteria to manage *perceived* legitimacy, which feeds back into Level 1.

*Level 3 — the counter-trap.* The published criterion becomes a target, and targets decay. Goodhart formalization: let $\theta$ be competition-relevant ability and $M$ the published metric. Post-publication, the association collapses:

$$\text{Corr}(\theta, M)_{\text{post}} \;=\; \text{Corr}(\theta, M)_{\text{pre}}\cdot\big(1-\lambda\big),\qquad \lambda\in[0,1]\ \text{gaming intensity}$$

*Units:* dimensionless correlation and intensity. *Breakdown:* at $\lambda \to 1$ the metric retains **zero** incremental validity while retaining full *apparent* authority — the worst possible state: illegitimate selection wearing a lab coat.

*The clock.* The selection clock does not merely compress observation; it **shifts the variance decomposition.** Within a 3-day trial, day-to-day biological variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{day}}$) and venue variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{venue}}$) are confounded with between-athlete variance ($\sigma^2_{T}$), deflating $\rho_{TM}$ in O6. The paper's proposed remedy — *"If motion assessments are standardized across regions"* [PAGE 6] — is the correct instinct, and it is the **only** place in the document where the clock problem is properly addressed.

*Recursive stability verdict.* The system has two equilibria: (a) **transparent-and-gamed**, where published criteria maximize Level-0 gaming and Level-2 legitimacy simultaneously; (b) **opaque-and-distrusted**, where criteria are hidden, Level-2 legitimacy fails, and Level-1 coaching bias is unconstrained. The paper wants a third equilibrium — transparent, un-gamed, trusted — but supplies no mechanism for it. The missing mechanism is a **rotating / partially withheld criterion set** plus longitudinal follow-up [PAGE 7], which is exactly what the paper lists as future work and does not design.

*Counter-trap check.* The analyst's own failure mode here is symmetric: over-trusting the wearable. A coach who abandons judgment for a GCT number has substituted one unmodelled estimator for another. The paper states the correct guardrail at [PAGE 6]: *"Biomechanical screening have to therefore complement, in preference to replace, conventional education assessment."* **This sentence is the most defensible claim in the document** and it directly contradicts the abstract's framing of biomechanics as a corrective *to* coach judgment.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Four layers tested. One upheld decisively, one upheld conditionally, two dismissed with arithmetic. A veto set that upholds everything is worthless.

**Layer 1 — Friction / shoe–turf (UPHELD, decisive).** From O3, the measurable braking ceiling is $a_{\max}=\mu_s g$. Dry synthetic µ ≈ 0.9 → $a_{\max}\approx 8.8\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$. Monsoon-wet synthetic µ ≈ 0.55–0.65 → $a_{\max}\approx 5.4$–$6.4\ \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$. Elite change-of-direction athletes routinely require 7+ m·s⁻² of deceleration. **Consequence:** on a wet pitch, the top quartile of the braking distribution is compressed against a surface-defined wall, so between-athlete variance in the region where discrimination matters is destroyed. Any cross-region "standardized" braking benchmark [PAGE 3], [PAGE 6] is therefore measuring venue hydrology, not talent. Verdict: **Table 2's "Braking force capacity" row cannot be used as a cross-venue selection criterion in India without a surface-normalization protocol.** The paper does not mention turf condition once in 8 pages.

**Layer 2 — Series-elastic contact-time confound (UPHELD CONDITIONALLY).** From O4: for firm synthetic ($k_{\text{surf}}\ge 500\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$), $k_{\text{eff}}$ shifts by <3% and $t_c$ by <1.5% — **below the 5% CV usefulness threshold**, so the confound is inert. For very compliant surfaces (shock-padded or wet natural turf, $k_{\text{surf}}\sim 200\ \text{kN}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}$), $t_c$ shifts +4.6%, **at the threshold**, becoming material. Verdict: GCT is portable *if* surface compliance is logged with each trial; the paper's protocol logs nothing.

**Layer 3 — Aerodynamic / thermal environment (DISMISSED for sprint mechanics).** Compute drag at sprint speed: $F_D=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_d A v^2 = 0.5\times1.15\times0.9\times0.5\times64 \approx 16.6\ \text{N}$ against a body weight of ≈690 N — **2.4% of weight.** A ±5% seasonal swing in air density (monsoon humidity, 35 °C heat) changes drag by ±0.8 N, i.e. **±0.1% of body weight.** Negligible against a 5% CV threshold. Heat does degrade *repeated*-sprint performance, but when all athletes are tested at the same venue on the same day, heat is a **common-mode** term that shifts the mean without reordering the ranking. It becomes bias only under split-venue scheduling — which, per Layer 1 logic, is already fatal for other reasons. Verdict: **thermal and aerodynamic objections are real in physiology and irrelevant to the measurement-validity question. Do not cite them as reasons to reject the proposal.**

**Layer 4 — Psychometric / statistical (UPHELD, decisive).** From O6: zero reported ICC, CV, or SEM across the document. The claimed reliability improvement [PAGE 1], [PAGE 5] is **unfalsifiable as presented**. Threshold logic: a marker with CV = 8% cannot resolve a 5% between-athlete difference — it will re-rank athletes on retest more than half the time. Verdict: **no marker in Table 2 [PAGE 6] is currently admissible as a selection variable** on the evidence supplied, because no marker's precision is stated.

**Composite Veto Matrix**

| Layer | Confound | Quantitative size | Threshold | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wet-turf friction ceiling | −27% to −39% on $a_{\max}$ | >5% | **VETO** |
| 2 | Surface compliance on $t_c$ | +1.0% to +4.6% | ≈5% | Conditional |
| 3 | Air density on drag | ±0.1% BW | 5% | No veto |
| 4 | Unreported reliability | unknown, unbounded | CV ≤5% | **VETO** |

**Net adjudication:** the source's proposed instrument set is **not transportable across Indian venues as specified**, for two independent reasons — an unreported precision floor (Layer 4) and a hard physical ceiling on the single most-cited discriminator (Layer 1). The proposal survives only in the reduced form the paper itself states at [PAGE 6] and [PAGE 7]: complement, don't replace; low-cost field protocols; longitudinal follow-up.
