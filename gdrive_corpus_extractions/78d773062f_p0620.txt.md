<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from Mesmer's animal magnetism (1770s), through Braid's neuro-hypnosis and fixation theory (1840s), to the Charcot/Salpêtrière trauma-hysteria model versus the Nancy/Liébault-Bernheim suggestibility school, then into the Anglo-American psychical research of Gurney, Sidis, and James's Principles of Psychology (1890). The footnote explicitly points to the Proceedings of the Society for Psychical Research as the repository for "these matters" [PAGE 1], showing that James treats hypnotic rapport as continuous with psychical-research anomalies rather than as settled experimental psychology.

"Magnetic rapport" is a historically unstable category. It began as a putative physical effluvium or fluid (Mesmer), was reduced by Braid to a physiological nervous sleep, was further reduced by the Nancy school to psychological suggestion, and is retained by James in a hybrid status: "All which is rationally explicable by expectation and suggestion" yet on "the verge of possibly unknown forces and modes of communication" [PAGE 1]. The trap is to reify a dyadic, history-dependent conditioned relation into a transpersonal force. Multi-week traps specific to this literature: (1) expectancy incubation—across repeated sittings, P(response | operator) rises because the subject learns the operator's contingencies, not because trance depth increases; (2) spontaneous recovery—cataleptic attitudes or insensibilities can reappear after apparent extinction, mimicking durable rapport; (3) selective awakening trap—James notes the operator can "expressly awakens his senses" to chosen spectators [PAGE 1], creating a time-locked reconfiguration of attention that may be misread as a genuine widening of rapport when it is merely a new suggestion contingency; (4) rare-case overgeneralization—the footnote's "very rare cases" of distant hypnotization without expectation, if not replicated under double-blind conditions over weeks, generate publication bias and false positives in psychical research [PAGE 1]. The correct genealogical move is to treat rapport as a learned operator-specific selective-attention contingency with a high prior, not as evidence of a novel physical force.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. In trance the subject's theory of mind regresses from a distributed social model—many possible informants, many possible signal sources—to a single-agent intentional stance in which only the operator's mental states are represented with high fidelity. James states that in deep trance the subject is "deaf and blind to everyone but the operator" and that "he undoubtedly is conscious of subtler personal indications (of his operator's mental states especially) than he could notice in his waking state" [PAGE 1]. This regresses attention to an operator-dependent attachment-like schema: "He often shows great anxiety and restlessness if the latter is out of the room" [PAGE 1]. The subject is not merely relaxed; the subject's social inference engine has been recursively locked onto one agent.

Counter-trap. A bystander cannot break rapport by simultaneous pointing because the subject's recursive model assigns near-zero credence to bystander intentional states in the trance context [PAGE 1]. "A bystander pointing simultaneously at another finger, never made that insensible or rigid" [PAGE 1]. The failure is not perceptual blindness to the bystander's movement; it is a failure to represent the bystander as an operative source of commands. The subject models the operator's mind as high-salience and all other minds as background, regardless of the physical intensity of their appeals.

Clock. The decision process is governed by an asymmetric temporal operator. Operator signals have a low detection threshold under trance gain and a high prior weight, so response latency tau_O is short even for "the faintest signals" [PAGE 1]. Bystander appeals, however "violent," have tau_B → infinity because they are weighted as non-events: "as if non-existent" [PAGE 1]. Formally, subjective value of a source decays with operator salience: V_B = intensity_B / (1 + k * S_O) → 0 as S_O → ∞, where S_O is operator salience and k is a coupling constant. Crisis moment: when the operator leaves the room, lambda_O = 0; the decision clock loses its scheduled event and the system enters restlessness/anxiety, consistent with an expected-signal waiting process that receives no arrivals [PAGE 1].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto. Gurney's opaque-screen protocol places the subject's hands through a screen while the operator points at one finger and a bystander at another; only the operator-pointed finger becomes insensible or rigid [PAGE 1]. If the screen is truly opaque, no visual pointing information reaches the subject. Veto condition: the experiment must report optical transmission T(lambda) < 10^-3 across visible wavelengths and acoustic transmission loss > 40 dB across speech frequencies. Because [PAGE 1] provides no screen-opacity metrology and James attributes the effect partly to senses "acutely sharpened for all the operator's movements," the physical-layer veto is unpassed; ordinary sensory leakage (breathing, manner, minute cues) remains a sufficient explanation [PAGE 1]. The footnote that bystanders' card discrimination is "much greater than one would naturally suppose" [PAGE 1] further inflates the sensory-confound baseline.

Biomechanical layer veto. Cataleptic retention of limb attitude against gravity requires balancing tau_g = m g L sin(phi) (N·m). If the limb retains attitude only when the operator moves it, then effective stiffness K_op must satisfy K_op >> m g L / phi_op; when a bystander moves the limb, K_B ≈ 0 and it falls [PAGE 1]. This rejects a generalized trance-tonus model and supports operator-contingent motor compliance. Verification requires surface electromyography and limb-impedance measurements during operator versus bystander displacement; high impedance only for the operator confirms an intentional-stance-dependent neuromechanical effect rather than a global trance state.

Weather/environmental layer. The source describes an indoor experimental dyad; no temperature, pressure, humidity, or barometric variables are mentioned or causally implicated [PAGE 1]. A weather veto is therefore null/not applicable. The relevant environmental layer is room-level control (lighting, soundproofing, experimenter position), which is uncontrolled in the text and constitutes the dominant confound.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Screen-integrity counterfactual. Replace the opaque screen with a verified shield: visible-light transmission < 10^-4 and sound attenuation > 50 dB from 100 Hz to 8 kHz. Operator points at one finger; bystander points simultaneously at another; subject cannot see either hand. Prediction under sensory-cueing/experimenter-expectancy: the operator-specific insensibility/rigidity effect collapses to baseline, P(rigid | O) ≈ P(rigid | B) ≈ baseline, and signal detection d' → 0. Prediction under James's rapport/unknown-force hypothesis: the effect persists despite shielding, implying a non-sensory channel consistent with the footnote's "unknown forces and modes of communication" [PAGE 1]. Verifiable by comparing response rates across shielded vs. unshielded sessions.

Scenario 2 — Operator-blind identity counterfactual. Conceal the operator's location and identity; a confederate mimics the operator's movements while the true operator is absent and the subject believes no operator is present. Prediction under expectation/suggestion: if the subject attributes the confederate's movements to the operator, responses track the confederate; if the subject is genuinely blind to operator identity, P(response | confederate) ≈ P(response | bystander) ≈ 0. Prediction under operator-specific rapport: no response to the confederate because the specific operator is absent; response occurs only when the actual operator is present, even if concealed. Verifiable via a confusion matrix of response versus actual-operator presence.

Scenario 3 — Latency-distribution counterfactual. Record high-resolution video/audio to extract operator and bystander movement onsets and subject response latencies. Prediction under rapport: operator-response latency tau_O is short and largely invariant to signal intensity ("faintest signals" obeyed quickly [PAGE 1]); bystander-response latency tau_B is undefined (no response) even for "violent appeals" [PAGE 1]. Prediction under ordinary cueing/experimenter expectancy: subject responses correlate temporally with bystander movement onset or experimenter breathing cycles rather than operator intent, so tau_B becomes finite and coupled to bystander lag. Verifiable via cross-correlation: peak at lag 0 for operator under rapport; peak at bystander-movement lag under cueing. Each scenario is falsifiable with standard psychophysical controls and produces a binary veto outcome for the rapport-as-force interpretation.
