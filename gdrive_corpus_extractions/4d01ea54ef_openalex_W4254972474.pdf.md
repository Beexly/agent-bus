(13)]. The intermediate integral $E_x(e^{-r\tau_0}X_{\tau_0})=\lambda_0 x/(\tilde r-\mu)$ [PAGE 18, eq. (8)] **requires** $\mu<\tilde r$ (Assumption 1, eq. (11)); this is the first gray zone — if $\mu\ge\tilde r$ the gain $g(x)=\beta_1 x-P$ is not integrable and $\beta_1<0$.

Hop 3 — Free boundary. In the continuation region the value is $\tilde r$-harmonic, $\mathcal{L}u-\tilde r u=0$ with $\mathcal L=\mu x\,\partial_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2x^2\partial_{xx}$ [PAGE 25, eq. (36)]. The trial $u=x^q$ yields the indicial quadratic $\tfrac12\sigma^2q(q-1)+\mu q-\tilde r=0$ [PAGE 26, eq. (40)] with roots $q_2<0<q_1=q^\star$; the sign of the quadratic at $q=1$ is $\mu-\tilde r<0$, forcing $q^\star>1$.

Hop 4 — Smooth fit. $u(b)=g(b)$, $u'(b)=g'(b)$, $u(0^+)=0$ give $b^\star=Pq^\star/(\beta_1(q^\star-1))$ [PAGE 26, eq. (42)].

Hop 5 — Verification subtlety (the deepest gray zone). $u$ is only $C^1$ at $b^\star$, so classical Itô fails; one invokes Itô–Meyer [PAGE 27] and proves the global supersolution inequality $\mathcal L u-\tilde r u\le0$ using the identity $\mu q^\star-\tilde r=-\tfrac12\sigma^2q^\star(q^\star-1)<0$ [PAGE 27, eqs. (49)–(50)]. This inequality is what makes $u\ge v$; the reverse $u\le v$ follows from optional sampling on the localized martingale plus Fatou/dominated convergence [PAGE 27–28].

Gray-zone audit across the volume. Paper 1's second singularity is probabilistic, not analytic: even with $\mu<\tilde r$, the hitting time is finite a.s. **only if** $\mu\ge\tfrac12\sigma^2$ (Remark 5, [PAGE 29]): verbatim, "hitting the critical threshold $b=b^\star$ … is only certain when the wage growth rate is large enough, $\mu\ge\tfrac12\sigma^2$." Otherwise $P_x(\tau_{b^\star}<\infty)=(x/b^\star)^{1-2\mu/\sigma^2}<1$ and the "optimal" rule may never fire — the paper's stated motivation for utility corrections (107)–(111). Paper 4 (Moriarty–Palczewski) is the *same* skeleton with a general diffusion: value $v(x)=\sup_\tau E_x(e^{-r\tau}\vartheta(X_\tau)\mathbf 1_{\tau<\infty})$ [PAGE 102, eq. (7)], and the identical trichotomy appears as $L_c=\limsup_{x\to a}(-\vartheta(x))/\varphi_r(x)$ giving cases A/B/C [PAGE 105, Theorem 1]. Paper 2 (Rotondi) is the discrete Bermudan shadow of the same stopping problem, where the continuation value is *estimated* by regression rather than solved. Papers 3, 5, 6, 7 replace "choose a stopping time" with "choose a control": HJB/infinite-horizon (Paper 3), martingale-representation market completion (Paper 5), Bayesian filtering inside a Markowitz objective (Paper 6), and bounded-variation debt control (Paper 7, model absent from excerpt).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The volume's methods form a coherent lineage. The optimal-stopping spine descends from Black–Scholes (1973) and Merton (1973) risk-neutral valuation [Paper 2, PAGE 55], through Merton's continuous-time portfolio rules [Paper 5, PAGE 128; Paper 6, PAGE 160], to the modern free-boundary formulation codified by Peskir–Shiryaev (2006), invoked repeatedly in Paper 1 [PAGE 24, PAGE 25]. Paper 1 explicitly genealogizes its own result to the perpetual American call (Shiryaev 1999, chp. VIII), and von Neumann–Morgenstern (1953) → Borch (1961) → Expected Utility Theory (Kaas et al. 2008) [PAGE 15, PAGE 47]. Paper 2 sits on Longstaff–Schwartz (2001) and its critique literature (Clément–Lamberton–Protter 2002; García 2003; Kan–Reesor 2012) [PAGE 59], itself an application of the same continuation-value operator that Paper 1 solves in closed form. Paper 3's marked-point-process dual predictable projection follows Brémaud (1981) and Ceci–Gerardi, while its HJB verification (Feynman–Kac, Heath–Schweizer 2000) is classical control theory [PAGE 90]. Paper 4 draws the Laplace-transform hitting-time technique from Beibel–Lerche (2000) and Dayanik–Karatzas (2003) [PAGE 101, PAGE 123], generalizing Carmona–Touzi (2008) swing options. Paper 5 rests on Markov-additive-process theory (Çinlar 1972; Asmussen–Kella 2000) and asymptotic-arbitrage theory (Kabanov–Kramkov 1994, 1998; Björk–Näslund 1998) [PAGE 135]. Paper 6 follows Markowitz (1952) → Samuelson (1969)/Merton (1969) → the partial-information filtering school (Lakner 1995, 1998; Rogers 2001) [PAGE 160]. The multi-week "trap" embedded in this genealogy: each paper inherits a *tractability assumption* from its ancestor that later becomes the breakdown axis — GBM constant coefficients (Paper 1), a well-posed regression (Paper 2), premium-principle convexity (Paper 3), a one-dimensional diffusion (Paper 4), a *unique* martingale measure requiring $\mu^j_j=r_j$ (Paper 5), estimable drift (Paper 6). Recognizing that the ancestor's convenience is the descendant's singularity is the genealogical lesson.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Here the "agents" are the modelers and the modeled decision-makers, and the recursion is belief-vs-reality across time. Paper 1 is the sharpest case: the fully rational NPV-maximizing agent adopts the threshold rule $\tau_{b^\star}$, but the authors model a *second-order* agent who anticipates that "the (optimal) waiting time $\tau_{b^\star}$ may be infinite with positive probability … and even if it is finite with probability one, the expected waiting time may be very long" [PAGE 14]. The counter-trap is the utility augmentation $v^\dagger(x)=\sup_\tau E_x(\kappa e^{-\rho\tau}+e^{-\tilde r\tau}g(X_\tau))$ [PAGE 44, eq. (110)], which penalizes impatience and is provably increasing in the preference weight $\kappa$ (Proposition 2, [PAGE 44]) — the "clock" (waiting cost) is internalized rather than ignored. Paper 6 models the recursion explicitly through time: the investor's belief $\hat B_s$ is a martingale in information, $\hat B_s=(\Sigma_0^{-1}+s\Sigma^{-1})^{-1}(\Sigma_0^{-1}b_0+(\sigma')^{-1}Y_s)$, so the *same* prior $b_0$ is progressively overridden as $s\to T$ [PAGE 163]. The counter-trap there is behavioral: the non-learning agent's weight $\Sigma^{-1}b_0$ is frozen, producing a documented reversal tendency at high rebalancing frequency because $w^{NL}$ re-levers losing trades and unwinds winning ones through the $x_{t_0}-x_t$ term [PAGE 171], whereas BL's corrective term $(\psi(s)\sigma^{-1})'\nabla_b R(s,\hat B)$ absorbs deviations. Paper 7's government is a third recursive agent whose *stated* objective (debt-ceiling minimization) may be defeated by a bounded-intervention reality — the title itself, "On the Failure to Reach the Optimal Government Debt Ceiling" [PAGE 178], encodes the gap between intended policy (the 60% Maastricht reference, [PAGE 179]) and achievable policy under limited primary surpluses. Across all three, the recursion is: agent forms a plan under a tractability assumption → the plan's own long-run behavior violates that assumption → a corrected plan internalizes the violation. The trap is stopping the recursion one level too early.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Reinterpreted as the admissibility/feasibility constraints that "veto" candidate solutions; a veto is a proof that a proposed optimum is inadmissible.

Veto 1 (Paper 1, integrability). Any parameter set with $\mu\ge\tilde r=r+\lambda_0$ is vetoed: $\beta_1=\beta\lambda_0/(\tilde r-\mu)$ diverges/negates and $E_x(e^{-r\tau_0}X_{\tau_0})=\lambda_0x/(\tilde r-\mu)$ fails [PAGE 18]. The authors note this is empirically mild — "even if it does [exceed inflation], then it is hardly possible economically that it is greater than the combined inflation–unemployment rate $\tilde r$" [PAGE 18, Remark 2].

Veto 2 (Paper 1, a.s.-finiteness). Conditional on Veto 1 passing, the stopping rule is *probabilistically* vetoed as a practical prescription whenever $\mu<\tfrac12\sigma^2$, because then $P_x(\tau_{b^\star}<\infty)=(x/b^\star)^{1-2\mu/\sigma^2}<1$ [PAGE 29, eq. (65)]. The pathology is worst at $\mu=\tfrac12\sigma^2$ where $E_x(\tau_{b^\star})=\infty$ [PAGE 30, eq. (66)].

Veto 3 (Paper 2, no-arbitrage). Any LSM run whose continuation regression is rank-deficient (ITM paths $N<M$, or ITM payoffs $\to0$) is vetoed because it yields $\pi^A<\pi^E$, violating the early-exercise premium; the sanctioned repairs are the European floor and the non-negativity-constrained regression [PAGE 59–60]. Verified numerically: uncorrected estimates flagged $\star$ in Table 2 (e.g. $1.7515^\star$ vs European $2.2971$) fall outside the confidence interval and are thereby vetoed [PAGE 63].

Veto 4 (Paper 4, sustainability). The pair $S1^\ast:\sup_x h(x)>0$ and $S2^\ast:p_c+K_c<x^\ast$ [PAGE 105, Lemma 2] vetoes contracts that are either unprofitable for the operator or loss-guaranteeing for the system operator; the proof of $S2^\ast$ constructs a riskless-bond arbitrage the system operator could otherwise run [PAGE 119].

Veto 5 (Paper 5, no-arbitrage). Unless $\mu^j_j=r_j$ for every regime $j$, the discounted Markovian-jump security fails to be a local martingale and asymptotic arbitrage appears — a hard veto on the enlarged market [PAGE 138, Theorem 2]. A second, softer veto: the density $\ell(t)$ is a *true* martingale only under Novikov-type integrability (bounded $\psi_0,\psi_j$) [PAGE 137, Remark 2].

Veto 6 (Paper 6, estimability). Any claim that drift is precisely estimated is vetoed by $\mathrm{Var}(\hat a)=\sigma^2/T$, which vanishes only as the horizon $T\to\infty$; the paper quantifies the veto — with $\sigma=0.02,\ T=25$, the 95% margin $\pm0.00784$ is "about twice as big as the value of $a$ itself" [PAGE 33].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three counterfactual interventions, each with a verifiable in-document prediction and a checked outcome.

Veto Scenario A (Paper 1, volatility toggle). Intervention: hold $\mu=0.0004$, $r=\lambda_0=0.0004$, $P=9000$, $\beta_1=30$, and *lower* volatility from $\sigma=0.04$ to $\sigma=0.02$, crossing $\mu=\tfrac12\sigma^2$. Prediction (from eqs. (24), (65), (66)): the hitting probability jumps from $<1$ to exactly $1$, $q^\star$ rises, and $b^\star$ falls. Verified [PAGE 35]: $q^\star:3.864208\to6.728416$; $b^\star:404.74\to352.37$; $P_x(\tau_{b^\star}<\infty)=0.9245906\to1$; and a finite mean hitting time $E(\tau_{b^\star})=91.2$ weeks appears only in the low-$\sigma$ case. This falsifies any claim that the pure-NPV rule is operationally safe across both regimes.

Veto Scenario B (Paper 2, dividend-vs-rate inversion). Intervention: set the American call deep-ish OTM ($K/S_0=1.05$) with dividend yield exceeding the rate ($q=4\%>r=3\%\Rightarrow\mu_Q<0$). Prediction: LSM continuation estimate collapses below the European price. Verified [PAGE 63, Table 2]: at $K/S_0=105\%,r=3\%,q=4\%$, $\pi^E=1.7009$, CRR benchmark $1.7468$, uncorrected LSM $1.0236^\star$ (vetoed, outside CI), corrected LSM $1.7494$. Symmetrically, OTM puts are vetoed when $q$ is low and $r$ high [PAGE 62]. This falsifies "LSM price ≈ true price" whenever drift points away from the strike.

Veto Scenario C (Paper 5, regime-rate mismatch). Intervention: perturb the artificially-set appreciation rate so $\mu^j_j\ne r_j$ for some regime $j$ (the text stresses these are "artificial parameters and can be changed later," [PAGE 135]). Prediction: the discounted Markovian-jump security ceases to be a $Q$-local martingale and the market admits asymptotic arbitrage; equivalently, the martingale condition $\mu_j(t^-)+\sigma_j\lambda_j\psi_j-r=0$ cannot be solved with $\psi_j>-1$. Verified [PAGE 138]: "we have to assume that $\mu^j_j=r_j$ for all $j=1,\dots,N$ to make the market asymptotic-arbitrage-free," and the sign constraint $r>\mu_j-\sigma_j\lambda_j$ [eq. (24)] is exactly the feasibility window for $\psi_j$. This falsifies any claim that regime-switching completeness is parameter-free.

Cross-paper prediction (bonus, Papers 3 vs 6, negative rates). Both explicitly admit $r<0$ (Paper 3, ECB deposit rate $-0.4\%$, [PAGE 75]; Paper 2 footnote, [PAGE 55]). Verifiable prediction: in Paper 3 the optimal retention $\alpha^\ast(t)=e^{-r(T-t)}\ln(1+\theta)/\eta$ is *decreasing* in $r$ [PAGE 86, Remark 6], so as $r$ turns negative, $\alpha^\ast$ rises (less reinsurance ceded); the numerical section confirms wider EVP–VP gaps for $r<0$ [PAGE 90, Figure 4]. Any model assuming $r>0$ is thereby vetoed for the 2016–2019 rate environment the papers target.
