<THINK_TRACE>
The paper's logical spine runs through four hops, and each hop has a gray zone the authors mark but do not close.

**Hop 1 — representational mismatch.** The abstract states the premise verbatim: "This representational mismatch means that integrating ML into EO workflows currently requires platform-specific transformations that are difficult to reproduce or transfer across cloud infrastructures" [PAGE 1]. Formally, a raster cube is $\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{X\times Y\times T\times B}$ indexed by named dimensions $(x,y,\text{time},\text{band})$, whereas classical estimators consume $\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times F}$ and sequence models consume $\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times C}$ [PAGE 4]. The mismatch is not merely shape; it is *named-dimension semantics* — the cube carries coordinate references, the tensor carries positional ordering. Any specification that standardizes only the process interface leaves the ordering convention outside its boundary.

**Hop 2 — process-level abstraction as the fix.** The three-stage design (initialization / actions / management) is a transposition of the scikit-learn `fit`/`predict` separation onto openEO's declarative graph [PAGE 4–5]. This is architecturally elegant, but it standardizes *verbs* while leaving *nouns* (serialized model objects, training-set containers) backend-specific. The paper concedes this on page 9: sits tibbles and R data frames are named explicitly as a residual non-portability.

**Hop 3 — the interoperability test.** A single process graph is submitted unmodified to openEOcubes (R/gdalcubes/caret) and openeo-processes-dask-ml (Python/xarray/scikit-learn). Both reach overall accuracy 0.82; per-class metrics differ within ±0.05; κ = 0.76 over 258,168 paired 30 m pixels; field-modal agreement is 85 % across 3,173 polygons [PAGE 12–13]. The authors are scrupulous about what this does and does not prove: "The fixed seed therefore guarantees reproducibility within a backend, but not across backends written against different ML frameworks" [PAGE 11].

**Hop 4 — the acknowledged shortfall.** "Full cross-backend portability requires deeper harmonization of serialization formats and execution semantics than the process level alone can enforce" [PAGE 1]. The proposed remedy is backend conformance profiles.

Gray-zone audit — three items I can quantify rather than merely note:

*(a) The κ point estimate conceals an order-of-magnitude precision inflation.* The paper states: "We report the point estimate without a confidence interval, since adjacent pixels within fields are not statistically independent [40]" [PAGE 12]. Under an i.i.d. pixel assumption the binomial standard error of $\hat p_o$ is $\sqrt{0.82\times0.18/258168}=7.56\times10^{-4}$; propagated through $\kappa$ with $p_e\approx0.25$ this gives a half-width of roughly 0.002. Under a conservative field-block effective sample size of $n_{\text{eff}}=3{,}173$ fields, the standard error becomes $\sqrt{0.82\times0.18/3173}=6.82\times10^{-3}$, and the κ half-width rises to $\approx 0.018$–$0.021$. The ratio is $\sqrt{258168/3173}=9.02$. So the unreported interval is roughly nine times wider than a naive reader would reconstruct.

*(b) The pixel count reverse-engineers to an exact grid, which lets me bound the dropped-patch fraction.* $258{,}168 = 694\times372$ exactly. At 30 m that is $20{,}82\,\text{km}\times11{,}16\,\text{km}=232{,}4\,\text{km}^2$, matching the stated prediction bbox ($-4.02^\circ$ to $-3.74^\circ$ lon, $48.10^\circ$ to $48.20^\circ$ lat) to within 0.5 % after the $\cos(48.15^\circ)=0.667$ longitude contraction [PAGE 10]. This confirms the reported pixel count corresponds to the full raster over the bounding box, not a masked subset.

*(c) The foundation-model geometry silently discards area.* Page 16 gives "y = ⌊2242 ÷ 224⌋= 10 and x = ⌊2213 ÷ 224⌋= 9", with 2 and 197 residual pixels. Covered fraction is $(2240\times2016)/(2242\times2213)=4{,}515{,}840/4{,}961{,}546=0.9102$, i.e. **8.98 % of the requested footprint receives no embedding**, and the output cube's extent differs from the input's. <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**1. Representational transform (cube → table → tensor).**

$$\Phi:\ \mathbb{R}^{X\times Y\times T\times B}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times F},\qquad N\le X\cdot Y,\quad F=f(T,B,\mathcal{A})$$

where $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of engineered descriptors (spectral indices, textural measures, ancillary layers) enumerated on [PAGE 4]: "Feature-based workflows convert observations at each location into engineered descriptors, including spectral indices, textural measures, and ancillary layers, aggregated into a two-dimensional tabular feature matrix (N × F)". Units: $X,Y$ in pixels (here 30 m ground sampling distance); $T$ in acquisition epochs; $B,F$ dimensionless counts. **Breakdown regime:** $N\to$ number of labelled geometries when the reducer is `aggregate_spatial` with "median" over polygons — in the Brittany case $N=3{,}173$ "labeled field-parcel polygons" [PAGE 10], a reduction from $258{,}168$ candidate pixels by a factor of 81.4, which is exactly the variance-inflation factor identified in the audit above.

**2. Sequence tensor.** $(B\times T\times C)$ with $B$ batch, $T$ time steps, $C$ channels [PAGE 4]. For Rondônia: $T=23$ ("a 16-day composite for 2022 (23 observations)"), $C=10$ loaded bands plus NDVI if appended, $N=6{,}007$ samples over 9 classes [PAGE 13].

**3. Patch enumeration and its singularity.**

$$N_p=\left\lfloor\frac{Y}{H}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{X}{W}\right\rfloor,\qquad r_Y = Y \bmod H,\quad r_X = X \bmod W,\qquad \eta = 1-\frac{H\lfloor Y/H\rfloor\, W\lfloor X/W\rfloor}{XY}$$

with $H=W=224$ px. Page 16: "The spatial dimensions in the output data cube are therefore reduced such that y = ⌊2242 ÷ 224⌋= 10 and x = ⌊2213 ÷ 224⌋= 9. The remaining 2242 −10 × 224 = 2 pixels in y and 2213 −9 × 224 = 197 pixels in x are insufficient to form a complete input patch and are not represented in the output." Substituting: $N_p=90$, $r_Y=2$, $r_X=197$, $\eta=0.0898$. **Exact singularity:** $\eta\to 1$ and $N_p\to 0$ as $X\to 224^-$ or $Y\to 224^-$; the output cube is then empty while the job still reports success. This is a hard breakdown, not a degradation.

**4. Embedding memory operator.**

$$M = N_p\cdot L\cdot P\cdot D\cdot s\ \ \text{[bytes]},\qquad L=24,\ P=196,\ D=1024,\ s=4\ \text{B}$$

from "Model output: {batch: -1, embedding_level: 24, patch: 196, embedding: 1024}" [PAGE 16]. $M=1.734\times10^{9}$ B per time step. Pixel-token decomposition is consistent: $196=14^2$ tokens of $16\times16$ px over a $224\times224$ patch. SI note: $1.734$ GB $\equiv 1.615$ GiB; conflating the two understates the allocation by 7 %.

**5. Chance-corrected agreement.**

$$\kappa=\frac{p_o-p_e}{1-p_e},\qquad \mathrm{SE}(\kappa)=\frac{\sqrt{p_o(1-p_o)/n_{\text{eff}}}}{1-p_e}$$

Dimensionless, range $[-1,1]$. With $p_o=0.82$, $p_e\approx0.25$, $n_{\text{eff}}=3{,}173$: $\mathrm{SE}(\kappa)=9.1\times10^{-3}$, 95 % half-width $\approx0.018$, so $\kappa\in[0.74,0.78]$. **Singularity:** $p_e\to1$ (degenerate marginals, one rater near-constant) makes $\kappa$ indeterminate $0/0$; the coefficient is also undefined when either rater assigns a single class. Landis–Koch banding [PAGE 12 citation 39] places 0.61–0.80 as "substantial", so the point estimate is robust in category but not in precision.

**6. Field-modal agreement.** $\hat{a}=2{,}697/3{,}173=0.850$, $\mathrm{SE}=\sqrt{0.85\times0.15/3173}=6.3\times10^{-3}$, 95 % CI $[0.838,0.862]$. Source: "Across all 3,173 fields, the two backends agree on the modal crop type for 85 % of polygons" [PAGE 13].

**7. Hyperparameter selection optimism.** For $K$ i.i.d. candidates with per-split standard deviation $\sigma$, $\mathbb{E}[\max_k \hat{R}_k]-\mathbb{E}[\hat{R}]\approx\sigma\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{(K)}]$, with $\mathbb{E}[Z_{(4)}]=1.029$. With $\sigma\approx1.5$ pp, the reported 93.1 % carries $\approx1.5$ pp of selection optimism, shrinking the margin over the 89.6 % run from 3.5 pp to roughly 2 pp. No confidence intervals or replicates are reported in Table 6 [PAGE 15].

**8. Reproducibility condition (bitwise).** Define execution as a deterministic map $g_{\theta,\ell,\pi}$ parameterized by model hyperparameters $\theta$, library version tuple $\ell$, and preprocessing pipeline $\pi$. Bitwise equality across backends $A,B$ requires $g_{\theta,\ell_A,\pi_A}\equiv g_{\theta,\ell_B,\pi_B}$. The seed controls only the stochastic sub-stream inside $\theta$'s optimizer/tree-builder; it cannot equalize $\ell$ or $\pi$. Hence the paper's own qualifier [PAGE 11], and hence the observed $\pm0.05$ per-class envelope.

**Breakdown table.** (i) $X<224$ or $Y<224$ → empty output; (ii) $p_e\to1$ → κ undefined; (iii) $\ell_A\neq\ell_B$ → bitwise identity impossible; (iv) channel mismatch between training RDS and graph-appended NDVI → shape error at `ml_predict`; (v) $s\cdot M$ exceeding worker memory under Dask chunking → silent rechunk or OOM.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Read backwards from the 2026 artifact, the three-stage spec looks inevitable. It is not; it is the residue of five independent lineages, each of which contributed a constraint the others could not supply.

*Lineage 1 — estimator API convergence.* scikit-learn's `fit(X, y)` / `predict(X)` separation [PAGE 4, ref. 27] is the oldest constraint: training and application must be distinct verbs with a persisted intermediate object. PyTorch `Module`, Keras `compile/fit`, Hugging Face pipelines, and MLflow's registry all reproduce it. The openEO transposition inherits a subtlety it cannot inherit from scikit-learn: openEO graphs are *declarative and lazy*, so `ml_fit` is not a function call but a node whose `Model` object must survive serialization into the graph — the exact point where backend-specific containers (sits tibbles, R lists) reintroduce coupling.

*Lineage 2 — portability substrate.* ONNX, "governed by the Linux Foundation and backed by industry software and vendor consortium" [PAGE 4], supplies the format. But the genealogy bends: "Because ONNX model export is not natively available in R, the reticulate package is used to invoke Python tooling for serializing R-trained models to ONNX format" [PAGE 9]. The portability guarantee for the R backend routes *through* Python. Retro-causally, ONNX here functions less as a neutral interchange layer than as a Python-ecosystem gravity well.

*Lineage 3 — catalog metadata.* STAC and the STAC MLM extension (GeoSearch '24, ref. 31) supply the discovery layer, standardizing "architecture, input and output specifications, model hyperparameters, pretrained source, tasks and software dependencies" [PAGE 5]. This is what makes the Appendix C workflow possible at all: `load_stac_ml` can reshape a cube because the Item declares the required input shape.

*Lineage 4 — EO-specific architectures.* TempCNN (Pelletier 2019), TAE / LightTAE (Garnot & Landrieu 2020), ConvLSTM (Rußwurm & Körner 2018), and the foundation-model line (Prithvi-EO-2.0, TESSERA, TerraMind) each impose their own tensor contract. The spec's apparent neutrality is purchased by delegating those contracts to the STAC MLM metadata — a deliberate offloading of semantics.

*Lineage 5 — evaluation statistics.* Landis & Koch (1977) and Foody (2004) supply the κ benchmark and the spatial-autocorrelation caveat respectively. The paper cites both correctly, then reports the point estimate without the interval — a retrofit of 1977 vocabulary onto a 2026 remote-sensing comparison whose effective sample size is governed by 2004's warning.

**Multi-week traps.** (i) *The four-year experimental fossil.* "A small number of ML-related processes exist in isolated backends and have been labeled as 'experimental' for more than four years, notably fit_class_random_forest and predict_random_forest in VITO's Terrascope platform" [PAGE 2]. This is the strongest empirical argument in the paper and it is buried in §1: absent a specification, isolated processes harden into de facto APIs that no one can later normalize. (ii) *The two-backend promotion gate.* Promotion to the normative catalogue requires being "implemented in at least two independent production backends" [PAGE 5] — a coordination game in which each backend waits for the other, and which explains the four-year fossil. (iii) *Format lock-in latency.* sits RDS tibbles "distributed in most published datasets as RDS files on GitHub" [PAGE 9] create a data-format dependency that surfaces only when a Python backend tries to consume the same labels. (iv) *Reticulate fragility* — the ONNX bridge is a runtime dependency on a Python environment inside an R process, a failure mode invisible in development and chronic in deployment.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** The field's reflex under abstraction leakage is to drop back to platform-native code. The paper documents this reflex as the status quo it is replacing: "these transformations are implemented in an ad hoc and platform-specific manner, tightly coupled to particular software stacks or ML libraries" [PAGE 2]. The regression trigger is predictable — the first time a process graph fails on a channel-count mismatch or a patch-geometry edge case, the practitioner reverts to a local script, and the reproducibility debt immediately re-accrues. Note that §6.2 already occupies this state: "As no openEO backend has adopted this implementation yet, client code cannot be provided. The process graph is instead constructed manually and invoked as if received through the openEO API" [PAGE 15]. The foundation-model use case is therefore a *simulated* interoperability demonstration, and the paper is honest about it.

**Counter-trap.** Over-correcting produces the opposite failure: demanding bitwise-identical cross-backend outputs. That demand would require freezing library versions, RNG implementations, and tie-breaking rules — which would exclude exactly the mature libraries the inclusion criterion depends on: processes qualify only where "mature, tested implementations exist in established libraries so that backends can realize the processes without bespoke ML development" [PAGE 5]. Full determinism and library plurality are in direct tension; the paper's choice of "statistical equivalence" as the bar is defensible, and the disjointness test it proposes is the right one — "A difference would become concerning if backends diverged on classes each handles well independently, or if spatial disagreement followed geographic rather than classification boundaries" [PAGE 13]. Note that this is a *falsifiable* criterion, and the observed pattern (disagreement concentrated in the permanent-meadow / temporary-meadow split, i.e. along class boundaries not geographic ones) satisfies it.

**The clock.** Three clocks govern adoption, and they run at different rates. Clock 1, governance: OGC Community Standard approval (April 2026) [PAGE 2]; the promotion gate of two production backends [PAGE 5]; the `experimental: true` flag in the `ml_predict` schema [PAGE 21]. Clock 2, software: library version drift, Dask/CUDA integration, ONNX operator-set coverage. Clock 3, science: the multi-year latency between a reproducible-workflow claim and independent replication by third parties. The specification accelerates Clock 1, is neutral on Clock 3, and is currently *behind* Clock 2 — the paper's own evidence is that GPU-parallelized inference is the binding constraint on operational adoption [PAGE 10].

**Recursive belief modelling across four constituencies.** (a) *Spec authors* believe backend maintainers will adopt conformance profiles because transparency benefits users. (b) *Backend maintainers* believe their differentiator is precisely the full parameter space of their native library; a profile that documents limitations is a competitive liability, not an asset. (c) *Domain scientists* believe that "sensible defaults" [PAGE 5] means the results are correct, when defaults are only *reasonable*. (d) *Reviewers* believe a cross-backend κ = 0.76 measures the specification, when it measures the specification *plus* at least two decades of accumulated EO preprocessing convention. The paper partially anticipates (b) — it concedes users "retain more flexibility by using it directly" [PAGE 17] — but does not resolve the incentive asymmetry, which is why conformance profiles are correctly identified as "the most important near-term direction" [PAGE 18] rather than as a solved problem.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is a constraint originating below the specification's boundary that can invalidate a claim the specification makes above it. Four such vetoes are live here; each is proven from the source text, not asserted.

**Veto 1 — radiometric/atmospheric (upstream ML product as gatekeeper).** "Two Sentinel-2 Level 2A data cubes are loaded for the May to September 2017 growing season, including 12 reflectance bands and the Scene Classification Layer (SCL), and filtered to scenes with cloud cover below 50 % to reduce noise in the temporal composites" [PAGE 10]. The code masks `(scl == 3) | (scl == 8) | (scl == 9)` [PAGE 11] — cloud shadow, cloud medium/high probability. The SCL is itself a trained classifier; cirrus (class 10), snow/ice (11), and dark-feature shadow residuals are not removed. Consequence: the RF classifier's feature matrix is gated by a second ML model whose error rate is nowhere reported in the paper. A 50 % scene-level cloud-cover filter is also a coarse gate: a scene at 49 % cloud is admitted wholesale and then masked per pixel, so residual contamination after monthly median compositing is unbounded by the stated threshold. This veto is *layer-crossing*: it operates below `ml_fit` and cannot be detected from process-graph semantics.

**Veto 2 — geometric/phenological (spatial adjacency limits the test's power).** The training block spans $48.20^\circ$–$48.30^\circ$ N and the prediction block $48.10^\circ$–$48.20^\circ$ N, both $0.28^\circ$ lon wide [PAGE 10]. Latitude separation is $0.10^\circ \approx 11.1$ km. The two regions share climate, soil, and management regime; they differ mainly in parcel identity. Therefore the interoperability test is a *spatial hold-out at 11 km*, not a geographic extrapolation. This is a deliberate and defensible design for isolating backend effects — it removes environmental variation as a confounder — but it also means the 0.82 / 85 % / κ = 0.76 figures have discrimination power bounded by within-region autocorrelation, not by generalization difficulty. The paper's field-level metric is the correct instrument here, and the residual 15 % disagreement is attributed to "the permanent-meadow / temporary-meadow split" [PAGE 13], i.e. a semantic ambiguity in the label taxonomy rather than a backend artifact.

**Veto 3 — temporal/resampling (uncontrolled scale change).** Section 6.1 loads bands and then calls `cube_regularize` with `period = "P16D"` and `resolution = 30` [PAGE 20]. The samples are "a 16-day composite for 2022 (23 observations) at 10 metres spatial resolution" [PAGE 13]. Inference thus aggregates 10 m observations into a 30 m grid — a 9:1 pixel aggregation whose kernel is not specified anywhere in the process graph, because `cube_regularize` "wraps sits' sits_regularize and performs temporal regularization and cloud masking as a single bundled step rather than as separate composable openEO processes" [PAGE 9]. This is the single most consequential undocumented transformation in the pipeline, and it is precisely why openEOcraft "is therefore excluded from that comparison" [PAGE 9]. A veto with a named cause.

**Veto 4 — hardware/software stack.** "Adoption by operational backends has been limited by the challenge of robustly integrating parallelized Dask computations with ML frameworks such as PyTorch and CUDA for GPU-accelerated inference" [PAGE 10]. Combined with the $1.734$ GB-per-time-step embedding volume derived earlier, this is a concrete physical veto: the patch-based workflow's output tensor is large enough that chunking strategy, not API design, dictates feasibility. The specification cannot adjudicate this layer.

**Adjudication verdict.** Vetoes 1 and 3 are *specification-addressable* (they could be promoted to first-class processes and made explicit in the graph). Vetoes 2 and 4 are *not* — one is a property of the study design, the other of the compute substrate. The paper correctly routes 1 and 3 toward conformance profiles and leaves 2 and 4 to future work.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
