The veto order is: rule-layer (binary constraints) > referee-judgment layer (spots and jurisdiction) > biomechanical layer (human timing) > physical layer (field and weather). A physical possibility (ball through posts on kick-off) is vetoed by rule; a biomechanically legal kick (drop-kick contact) may still be vetoed by weather if \(e\) becomes unmeasurable; the adjudication is deterministic only within the breakdown regimes stated above.

Scenario 1 — Boundary-spot veto. The ball is declared dead with its forward point exactly on a cross line (the goal line or sideline extension). The determining-point functional \(\mathcal{D}\) [PAGE 278] is discontinuous at the boundary: a 1 mm difference in forward-point placement changes possession (touchback vs. live ball) or scoring status. With no replay available in 1905, the referee’s observation under Rule 2 [PAGE 278] is final, assigning \(P(\text{spot awarded}) = 1\) conditional on the referee’s line of sight. Verifiable prediction: In games where the ball is declared dead within \(\delta_\mathcal{D}=0.05\text{ m}\) of a cross line, the outcome distribution is determined by referee position rather than ball kinematics; the correlation between forward-point trajectory and final spot drops to near zero for \(|x_\text{forward}-x_\text{line}|<\delta_\mathcal{D}\).

Scenario 2 — Temporal-execution veto on drop-kick. A drop-kick is attempted where the ball’s rise time \(t_\text{rise}<\tau_\text{human}+\Delta t_c\) (ball rises faster than the kicker can react and contact within the 10–15 ms window). The operator \(K_D\) is undefined on this domain; the kick is either early (ball still descending from the drop) or late (ball descending after bounce), violating “the instant it rises” [PAGE 278]. Verifiable prediction: Attempted drop-kicks from drops below \(h=0.8\text{ m}\) (yielding \(t_\text{rise}<0.4\text{ s}\)) show a significantly higher rate of mishandled kicks, turnovers, or reclassification to punts compared with drops from \(h=1.5\text{–}2.0\text{ m}\), with the failure rate rising sharply as \(t_\text{rise}\) approaches \(0.3\text{ s}\).

Scenario 3 — Rule-layer goal veto on kick-off. A kick-off from the center [PAGE 278] is wind-aided or deflected such that the ball physically passes through the goal posts (\(G_\text{phys}=1\)). The rule enforces \(G_\text{KO}=0\) regardless [PAGE 278]. Verifiable prediction: No kick-off in the dataset results in a awarded score; all such events are adjudicated as touchbacks or opponent possession at the spot determined by the forward-point rule [PAGE 278], with a 100% veto rate on physical scoring, independent of weather, distance, or kicker skill. The counterfactual—if the rule were removed—would yield a non-zero probability \(P(G_\text{KO}=1)>0\) increasing with wind assistance and leg strength.
