<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when acute drive elevation multiplicatively amplifies the entire habit hierarchy, including weaker, earlier-acquired responses. Under high D, sEr = f(sHr)·f(D) can rise enough to evoke low-habit-strength behaviors that remain suppressed at baseline drive, producing an apparent regression to older response patterns without any change in sHr itself. The counter-trap is the observer's misreading of this drive-driven emergence as new learning or increased habit strength: because sHr is unchanged while D has risen, attributing the behavior to habit growth violates the multiplicative separation asserted on [PAGE 242]. The clock is the 24-hour privation anchor [PAGE 242]; shifting the experimental clock to 12 h or 48 h changes D(t) under the linear assumption, thereby rescaling every sEr prediction. If the experimenter recursively models the subject as representing drive, but misestimates D due to an incorrect clock offset, the resulting prediction of sEr is recursively wrong at both levels—the subject's actual motivational state and the experimenter's inference about it. Thus the theory-of-mind loop contains a single-point failure: errors in the D(t) calibration propagate into false attributions about habit strength, motivation, and learning history.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: neural refractory periods and maximum firing rates impose a hard ceiling on evoked responses independent of sEr; if the multiplicative law predicts a response rate beyond the physical substrate's capacity, the physical layer vetoes the output. Biomechanical layer: muscle glycogen depletion, fatigue, and effector mechanics cap executed behavior; sEr cannot evoke a movement the effector cannot perform, regardless of drive or habit magnitude. Weather/environmental layer: ambient temperature and humidity alter metabolic rate and therefore the speed at which food deprivation accumulates into drive. A hot environment may raise k (du/h) above the value calibrated at the 24-hour anchor [PAGE 242], while thermal stress may suppress locomotion independently of D, decoupling observed behavior from the f(sHr)·f(D) prediction. Veto proof: the molar model is admissible only when all three layers operate below saturation. Any hard cap—motor ceiling, biomechanical failure, or environmental limit—overrides the multiplicative output. Because the equations on [PAGE 242] contain no terms for temperature, fatigue, or motor bounds, the model is not causally complete and must be treated as valid only in the moderate regime where no layer is at its veto threshold.
