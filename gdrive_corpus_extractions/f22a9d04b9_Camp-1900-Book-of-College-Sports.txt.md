\]
where \(\rho\) is air/water density in \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), \(C_d\) is dimensionless, \(A\) is reference area in \(\mathrm{m^2}\), and \(v\) is boat speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Assumptions: steady speed, fully turbulent drag, negligible wind and shallow-water effects. Breakdown: at low \(v\), viscous and form-drag terms matter; at high \(v\), wave-making, cavitation, unsteady blade loading, and “the formula would prove true only within limits, and only approximately so at high speeds” [PAGE 75]. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under stress, the athlete reverts to the motor pattern that was most overlearned, not the pattern most recently instructed. Camp’s rowing passage is exact: “For days, and it may be for weeks, he feels that he must think every time; but then there comes a day when it is more natural and easy to do it the right way than the old way” [PAGE 53]. Before that day, crisis conditions—fatigue, race pressure, a broken oar, a close finish—favor regression. The baseball third baseman illustrates the same law: the ordinary amateur “is turned partially away from first base” because he fails to start sharply, and then the entire play becomes “hurried and inaccurate” [PAGE 220–222]. The coaching countermeasure is a cue that installs the correct first movement: “Jump in front of the ball” [PAGE 221].

Counter-trap. Recursive theory of mind appears when the athlete models the opponent’s model. The fake kick is a clear case: the ball is passed back “as for a kick,” but the receiver “instead of kicking it dodges his man and runs for the end” [PAGE 120]. The 1892 Harvard opening wedge is a higher-order mask: two bodies converge, the ball is played late, and the combined mass can turn right or left, so the opponent cannot infer the outlet from the initial formation [PAGE 121]. The catcher-pitcher signal by mask wire [PAGE 248] and Burdock’s left-arm signal [PAGE 233] are information-theoretic counter-traps: the code is hidden in a cue the opponent does not recognize as a cue. The recursive structure is: I choose an action; I expect you to infer it from my formation; therefore I make my formation compatible with multiple actions and disclose the true action only after your inference is committed.

Clock. The clock changes the optimal level of deception. In football, the source’s general caution is “not to venture such plays when within one's own 25-yard line” [PAGE 123], because a failed trick gives the opponent a short field. In baseball, ninth-inning quick starts must already be habitual: “Such points of play must be made habitual to the player by constant practice” [PAGE 216]. The clock operator is a remaining-time budget \(T_{\mathrm{rem}}\) in seconds: as \(T_{\mathrm{rem}}\) falls, the value of a high-variance counter-trap rises, but only if the underlying habit is already stable. If the habit is not stable, the clock accelerates regression rather than invention.
