**Veto class 1 — Kinematic admissibility (absolute).** A field pair $(\boldsymbol u_\theta,p_\theta)$ with $\lVert\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u_\theta\rVert>0$ is **not a solution of the incompressible system**, irrespective of loss value. **Veto class 2 — Pressure gauge (absolute, but benign).** Any pressure field is determined only up to an additive constant by the momentum equation. A PINN output $p_\theta$ that exhibits a drifting mean is therefore **not wrong physics** — it is under-determined physics correctly reproducing a null direction. Drift in the constant mode is a *missing constraint*, not a training failure.

**Veto class 3 — Shock admissibility (absolute).** A captured discontinuity that violates $s[\![u]\!]=[\![f(u)]\!]$ or the entropy inequality $\partial_t\eta+\partial_x q\le0$ is not an admissible weak solution, even if the network's residual is small away from the front. Each scenario below is stated so that a third party could refute it with a single experiment on a public benchmark. No scenario requires the review's missing tables.

**RLVR-1 — Gauge-fixing counterfactual.**
*Intervention.* On the 2D lid-driven cavity at $\mathrm{Re}=10^{3}$ with velocity-only boundary data, train two otherwise identical PINNs: baseline, and baseline plus an explicit pressure gauge projection (subtract the domain mean of $p_\theta$ from the pressure head at every forward pass, i.e., enforce $\int_\Omega p_\theta\,d\Omega=0$).
*Prediction.* The projected model's pressure-mean drift goes to numerically zero (machine precision, since the shift is exact), while the divergence norm $\lVert\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u_\theta\rVert_{L^2}$ is **statistically unchanged**. Equivalently: gauge-fixing removes the constant-mode artifact and does **not** improve mass conservation.
*Refutation condition.* If divergence error falls materially under projection alone, the pressure null-space is not the dominant error channel and the analysis in this audit is wrong.
*Why it is RLVR-shaped:* the reward signal is a directly measurable residual norm, and the two predicted outcomes are separable.

**RLVR-2 — Activation-swap counterfactual at matched budget.**
*Intervention.* On a 2D cylinder-wake benchmark in the vortex-shedding regime, hold parameter count, collocation count $N_f$, optimizer, and step budget fixed; swap the tanh activation for a sine activation (SIREN-style, with the standard frequency-scaling initialization), and separately for a Fourier-feature input embedding.
*Prediction.* (a) Vorticity-field relative $L^2$ error improves materially, with the gain concentrated in high-wavenumber energy content; (b) **total** error improvement exceeds what an equal-budget increase in width or in $N_f$ delivers; (c) pressure-field relative $L^2$ error improves **little or not at all**, because activation engineering cannot supply the missing inf-sup/gauge constraint.
*Refutation condition.* If pressure error tracks vorticity error improvement one-for-one under activation swap, the pressure weakness is representational rather than structural — contradicting [PAGE 11].
*Source anchors:* "sine-based PINNs demonstrate superior vortex resolution and reduced oversmoothing" [PAGE 33]; "activation enhancements often yield larger accuracy improvements than increasing network depth or collocation density alone" [PAGE 33].

**RLVR-3 — Domain-decomposition counterfactual at iso-parameter, iso-point.**
*Intervention.* Compare a monolithic PINN against an XPINN on a domain with a localized high-gradient region, **matched** on total trainable parameters and total collocation points (not on per-subdomain counts). Sweep $\mathrm{Re}$ across at least three decades.
*Prediction.* XPINN wins on error in the interface-adjacent band at low and moderate $\mathrm{Re}$; the advantage **shrinks monotonically with $\mathrm{Re}$** and becomes statistically indistinguishable once the interface-loss weight itself must be retuned per $\mathrm{Re}$ — i.e., domain decomposition relocates the tuning burden rather than eliminating it, exactly as the review concedes: "interface enforcement introduces additional hyperparameters and constraints, shifting rather than eliminating complexity" [PAGE 29].
*Refutation condition.* If the XPINN advantage is $\mathrm{Re}$-invariant at iso-parameter/iso-point budgets, decomposition is a genuine scaling solution rather than a burden-shift, and the review's own hedge on [PAGE 29] is too pessimistic.
