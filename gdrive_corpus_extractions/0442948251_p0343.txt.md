Let \(G \in \{0,1\}\) denote presence of species-typical genetic endowment for a neutral preparatory skill. Let \(M(t): \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \rightarrow [0,1]\) be a maturation function of developmental time \(t\) measured in days post-conception. Let \(M_{\text{crit}} \in [0,1]\) be the critical maturation threshold below which the neuromusculoskeletal plant cannot express the skill. Let \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}\) denote the biologically provided standard environment, a subset of the full environmental state space with units of physical stimulus intensity (e.g., temperature in kelvin, substrate compliance in pascals, gravitational acceleration in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)). Let \(S \in \{0,1\}\) indicate skill manifestation.

The operative formal relation, extracted from [PAGE 343], is:
\[
S = 1 \iff \left[ G = 1 \right] \land \left[ M(t) \ge M_{\text{crit}} \right] \land \left[ E \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{std}} \right].
\]
Equivalently, in probabilistic terms:
\[
P(S=1 \mid G=1,\; M(t) \ge M_{\text{crit}},\; E \in \mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}) \rightarrow 1.
\]
The breakdown (singularity) regimes are:
\[
M(t) < M_{\text{crit}} \implies P(S=1) \approx 0,
\]
\[
E \notin \mathcal{E}_{\text{std}} \implies P(S=1) \approx 0 \quad \text{(environmental veto)},
\]
\[
G = 0 \implies P(S=1) \approx 0 \quad \text{(genetic absence)}.
\]

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 argument sits in the purposive-behaviorism lineage that rejects strict Watsonian reflexology while remaining behaviorist in method. The immediate genealogy on [PAGE 343] runs through C. P. Stone’s 1927 *Psychological Bulletin* review of “native or congenital behavior,” then forward to Avery (1928) on premature guinea pigs and Carmichael (1926–1928) on amphibian embryos deprived of external stimulation. This is a historical chain of nativist evidence: Stone aggregates congenital literature; Avery demonstrates post-national locomotor competence at term minus four days; Carmichael demonstrates amphibian swimming patterns developing without experiential input.

Multi-week traps are embedded in this lineage. Guinea-pig gestation runs roughly nine to ten weeks; delivery four days prior to term removes approximately 6–7% of gestational duration. Scaling that truncation to human developmental terms is not linear, yet comparative psychologists of the era routinely extrapolated across species. A first trap is the “weeks-to-days” assumption: treating a four-day guinea-pig prematurity as trivial because four days sounds small relative to human gestation, when it may represent a critical fraction of neuromusculoskeletal ossification. A second trap is the “embryonic equivalence” trap: Carmichael’s frog and salamander embryos develop over days to weeks; removing external stimulation for part of that window does not prove independence from all environmental physics, only independence from the specific stimuli tested. Thermal regime, oxygen tension, and gravitational loading during those weeks remain uncontrolled confounds that could gate the expression of swimming movements. A third trap is the “neonatal-to-adult” extrapolation: because a new-born guinea pig can stand, one might assume the adult’s complex locomotion is equally innate; Tolman carefully separates preparatory from consummatory skills precisely to block this slide. The developmental clock (gestational age) and the experiential clock (postnatal practice days) are distinct, and retroactively projecting adult skill onto neonatal capacity creates a false lineage of causation.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes the tendency of an organism under acute threat or failure of higher-order control to default to lower-level, innately structured preparatory skills—locomotion, grasping, vocalization—because these require minimal cortical sequencing and are available immediately after maturation. In Tolman’s framework, consummatory skills are tied to specific appetites, whereas neutral preparatory skills are “more neutral” and “tend to subserve many different appetites and aversions” ([PAGE 343]). Under crisis, the organism regresses from context-specific consummatory execution to these general-purpose primitives.

The counter-trap is the observer’s misreading of this regression as strategic flexibility or resilience. If an animal under stress flees (locomotion) or vocalizes instead of executing a learned consummatory response, an observer may credit the animal with adaptive choice when the behavior may simply be a hardwired fallback that is contextually mismatched. Recursive theory of mind enters when the observer models the animal as modeling the observer: the animal’s regression is interpreted through the observer’s assumption that all behavior is learned, producing a second-order error. The organism is not choosing between learned strategies; it is expressing a maturation-gated primitive because the learned pathway is blocked or too slow.

The clock regulates availability: regression to preparatory skills is only possible if \(M(t) \ge M_{\text{crit}}\). A premature neonate or a developmentally delayed juvenile cannot regress to standing or walking because the physical plant is not online, regardless of crisis urgency. Thus the recursive loop is: crisis triggers attempted regression \(\rightarrow\) maturation clock checks whether \(M_{\text{crit}}\) is passed \(\rightarrow\) if yes, primitive is emitted \(\rightarrow\) observer interprets emission as either adaptive or maladaptive \(\rightarrow\) observer updates model of the organism’s mind. The breakdown occurs when the clock has not struck \(M_{\text{crit}}\); then no fallback exists and the system fails catastrophically rather than regressing gracefully.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: skeletal ossification and muscle-tendon pre-tension set a hard physical gate. Avery’s observation that guinea-pig fetuses delivered at least four days prior to term can crawl, stand, and walk ([PAGE 343]) implies that the ossification threshold for weight-bearing is reached late in gestation, but not arbitrarily early. If delivery occurs before that threshold—say five or more days prior to term—the physical layer vetoes standing regardless of genetic endowment or environmental stimulus. The veto condition is \(M_{\text{ossification}}(t) < M_{\text{crit}}^{\text{bone}}\).

Biomechanical layer veto: locomotion requires ground-reaction force transmission, proprioceptive feedback, and species-specific limb geometry. Even if the nervous system generates a walking command, a substrate with insufficient compliance or a gravitational vector outside the species-normal range vetoes expression. For amphibian embryos, the medium (water vs. air) changes the Reynolds-number regime of propulsion; Carmichael’s embryos develop swimming movements in aqueous external stimulation, but a shift to terrestrial gravity would veto swimming biomechanically even if neural patterning were intact.

Weather/environmental layer veto: “appropriate environmental stimuli” ([PAGE 343]) includes thermal and photoperiodic inputs. Amphibian developmental rate is temperature-dependent (Q10 effects); embryos incubated outside \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}\) may delay or arrest the emergence of swimming movements despite normal genetics and normal gestational stage. Carmichael’s embryos were “experimentally removed from the influence of external stimulation,” but this removal occurred within species-typical thermal bounds; the experiments do not test the veto boundary where temperature or chemistry exits \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}\).

Proof of veto composition: Let \(V_{\text{phys}} = \mathbb{I}[M_{\text{oss}} < M_{\text{crit}}^{\text{bone}}]\), \(V_{\text{bio}} = \mathbb{I}[\text{substrate/gravity} \notin \mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}]\), \(V_{\text{env}} = \mathbb{I}[T \notin [T_{\min}, T_{\max}]]\). Then \(S = (1 - V_{\text{phys}})(1 - V_{\text{bio}})(1 - V_{\text{env}}) \cdot \mathbb{I}[G=1 \land M \ge M_{\text{crit}}]\). Any single veto forces \(S=0\). This is a proof by conjunction: skill manifestation requires passage through all three layers; failure in any one is sufficient and necessary to suppress expression. The breakdown regime is the union of the three veto sets, not their intersection.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Prematurity veto). Veto condition: guinea-pig delivery at \(\Delta t_{\text{preterm}} \ge 5\) days prior to term, exceeding Avery’s observed “at least four days prior to term” tolerance on [PAGE 343]. RLVR prediction: \(P(\text{stand} \mid \Delta t \ge 5) \ll P(\text{stand} \mid \Delta t \le 4) \approx 1\); crawling and standing will be absent or severely delayed even in a biologically standard environment. Verifiable protocol: replicate Avery’s premature-delivery experiment with precise gestational dating at day 3, day 4, and day 5 pre-term, score weight-bearing latency, and reject the null if day-5 subjects show \(>50\%\) failure to stand within the first post-natal hour while day-4 subjects stand.

Scenario 2 (Environmental stimulus veto). Veto condition: frog or salamander embryos raised in Carmichael’s stimulation-removal paradigm but held at a temperature outside \(\mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}\) (e.g., \(>3^\circ\mathrm{C}\) deviation from species-normative range). RLVR prediction: \(P(\text{swimming movement} \mid E \notin \mathcal{E}_{\text{std}}) \to 0\) despite intact genetic program and normal gestational age; movements fail to emerge or emerge with abnormal kinematics. Verifiable protocol: split embryos from the same clutch into standard-temperature and shifted-temperature baths, remove external mechanical stimulation as per Carmichael (1926), and record first onset of swimming; reject the null if the shifted group shows statistically significant delay or absence.

Scenario 3 (Crisis regression mismatch veto). Veto condition: an adult organism with intact consummatory and preparatory repertoires is placed in an acute-threat context where the consummatory/learned response is required but the environment only permits a preparatory output (e.g., escape locomotion blocks the target consummatory act). RLVR prediction: performance error rate on the consummatory task increases; the organism emits locomotion or vocalization instead of the target skill, and the regression is not voluntarily suppressible on short timescales. Verifiable protocol: block the learned consummatory pathway (e.g., sensory occlusion or response-contingent obstruction), introduce a crisis stimulus, and record response topography; reject the null if preparatory primitives dominate the response stream and error rate exceeds baseline by a pre-registered margin. Each scenario yields a binary verification outcome and a falsifiable probability boundary.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
