When the learner encounters an unfamiliar or emotionally intense state, the system regresses from the deliberate coupling regime \(\Lambda(s,e)\) back to the older substrate of reflex interjection and mere contiguity [PAGE 358]. The observer may interpret this as loss of previously acquired signs, but it is actually a layer-shift: the high-level sign-purpose map \(\Phi: S \to E\) is temporarily offline, and the organism is operating from stimulus-bound affect. Because the sign was “dissociated” [PAGE 358] only through prior imitation and repetition, stress collapses the dissociation and returns control to the interjectional cry.

Counter-trap. The instructor, observing regression, may increase drill, structure, and external cueing. This over-scaffolding is the counter-trap: it occupies the “line” that Howe lowers into the “deep sea” [PAGE 358], preventing the learner from spontaneously taking hold of it. James’s mechanism requires waiting for self-initiated uptake; forced repetition during the regression phase fills working memory with contiguity pairs but does not produce the insight that “general purpose imbedded in the different details” [PAGE 358]. The correct counter-intervention is to reduce scaffolding and restore the conditions for spontaneous sign use.

Clock. Two timescales must be distinguished. \(\tau_{\text{contiguity}}\) operates on seconds-to-minutes scales (fast tactile-afferent pairing, \(C_{\text{tactile}}\) in bits/s). \(\tau_{\text{insight}}\) operates on days-to-weeks scales and is heavy-tailed; the hazard rate of the “radiant flash” [PAGE 358] is not constant and cannot be scheduled within a fixed intervention window. Attempting to clock the insight to a curriculum deadline is a formal category error: the reorganization is thresholded on \(I(S;E) \ge \theta_I\), and \(\theta_I\) may be crossed only after an unpredictable latency.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical / biomechanical layer veto. Howe’s method requires the learner to “feel the letters” [PAGE 358]. If digital tactile innervation is vetoed—by anesthesia, peripheral neuropathy, or insensitive covering—then the somatosensory channel capacity \(C_{\text{tactile}} = 0\) bits/s. By the channel-capacity bound, \(I(S;E) = 0\) through that route regardless of repetition; no sign-article association can be encoded, and the system cannot leave the pre-inspection state. Fine-motor apraxia or paralysis likewise vetoes the feedback loop between raised letter and manual exploration.

Environmental / weather layer veto. Although not meteorological in the narrow sense, the stimulus ecology must be stable: the “familiar articles” [PAGE 358] must remain stationary and consistently lettered within the contiguity window \(\Delta t\). A turbulent or rapidly rotating stimulus set destroys the temporal contiguity required for the associative update \(\Delta A(s,e)\), effectively driving the learning rate \(\eta\) to zero for that pairing.

Neurocognitive layer veto. Cross-modal binding of tactile sign \(s\) to emotional referent \(e\) requires intact association cortex. If binding is vetoed, the joint distribution factorizes, \(p(s,e) = p(s)p(e)\), yielding \(I(S;E) = 0\) bits even when both afferent channels are individually intact. The sign and emotion are present but carry no information about each other; the “radiant flash” cannot occur because there is no statistical structure to reorganize.

Adjudication order. Vetoes are assessed hierarchically: (1) physical channel vetoes are absolute and checked first; if \(C_{\text{tactile}} = 0\), the method fails before any higher-layer analysis. (2) Neurocognitive binding vetoes are conditional on channel integrity; they are tested by checking whether \(I(S;E)\) remains at zero despite intact sensation. (3) Environmental instability is a modulatory veto: it does not destroy the architecture but raises the effective \(\eta\) to near-zero, producing a pseudo-plateau that mimics the pre-insight waiting period described by Howe on [PAGE 358].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable prediction stated in information-theoretic and behavioral terms.

Scenario A — Tactile channel veto. During Howe’s raised-letter phase, the learner’s fingertips are anesthetized (or covered with insensitive material) while the familiar articles are still presented. This sets \(C_{\text{tactile}} = 0\) bits/s and blocks the “mere contiguity” encoding described on [PAGE 358]. Verifiable prediction: the confusion matrix between signs and articles remains at chance; \(p(s|e)\) does not diverge from the prior; \(I(S;E)\) stays at 0 bits. The “radiant flash of intelligence” [PAGE 358] never occurs because no associative structure has accumulated. If anesthesia is removed after \(N\) weeks, learning restarts from baseline rather than resuming from a latent symbolic state, confirming that contiguity exposure without transmission capacity is informationally null.

Scenario B — Imitation fidelity veto. The learner has a motor-imitation lesion that prevents repetition of a heard or seen interjectional cry, though the cry is still produced during the eliciting emotion. This sets Bleek’s imitation fidelity \(f = 0\), disabling the fixation loop James attributes to “the great imitativeness of man” [PAGE 358]. Verifiable prediction: the learner never produces the cry “without it” [PAGE 358]—that is, without the concurrent experience; signs remain strictly stimulus-bound; no stock of “determinate specific signs” multiplies [PAGE 358]; the similarity operator has an empty or singleton domain, and language does not progress beyond reflex interjection. \(I(S;E)\) remains bounded by the immediate co-occurrence entropy and never crosses \(\theta_I\).

Scenario C — Deliberate coupling veto. The learner can imitate signs and experience emotions, but cannot voluntarily pair a sign with an emotion in the absence of the eliciting stimulus; the deliberate coupling operator \(\Lambda(s,e)\) has success probability \(q = 0\). Verifiable prediction: the learner recognizes sign-emotion pairs only when both are physically present; when prompted to produce a sign for an absent emotion, performance is at chance. The “general purpose imbedded in the different details of all these signs” [PAGE 358] remains unextracted, and education does not proceed with “extreme rapidity” [PAGE 358] after any putative insight because there is no decoupled symbolic substrate over which generalization can operate. Behaviorally, \(I(S;E)\) measured from a delayed match-to-sample task remains near 0 bits even though immediate contiguity performance is intact.

Each scenario is falsifiable: the intact system should show \(I(S;E) > \theta_I\) bits preceding rapid acquisition, whereas every vetoed scenario predicts \(I(S;E) \approx 0\) bits and absence of the generalized sign-purpose transition described on [PAGE 358].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
