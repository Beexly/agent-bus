\]

where $\tau \in \text{s}$ is the membrane/time constant, and $K(s,s') \in \text{s}^{-1}$ is the interaction kernel. Units: the integral term has units $\text{s}^{-1}$ because $c$ is dimensionless and $ds'$ contributes spatial measure absorbed into $K$; consistency requires $[K] = \text{s}^{-1}\cdot\text{length}^{-d}$ if $c$ is a density, or $[K]=\text{s}^{-1}$ for lumped elements. The fixed point satisfies $c^* = (I - K c^*)\tau$, stable only if the spectral radius $\rho(K) < 1/\tau$.

Compound successive stimuli. Let stimuli $s_1,\dots,s_m$ be delivered at times $t_1,\dots,t_m$ with pauses $\Delta t_k = t_{k+1}-t_k \in \text{s}$. Following Pavlov’s order-dependence quote on [PAGE 48] (citing [PAGE 147-148]), the state-transition operator is non-commutative:

\[
c(t_m) = \mathcal{U}(\Delta t_{m-1})\circ \cdots \circ \mathcal{U}(\Delta t_1)\, c(0) + \sum_{k=1}^{m} \mathcal{U}(t_m - t_k)\, I(s_k,t_k),
\]

where $\mathcal{U}(\Delta t) = e^{A\Delta t}$ and $A = (K - \mathbb{I})/\tau$. Because inputs are injected at different times, reversing the sequence generally changes $c(t_m)$; simultaneity is recovered only in the singular limit $\Delta t_k \to 0\ \text{s}$ for all $k$.

Extinction. From experimental extinction ([PAGE 258 f.] as cited on [PAGE 48]), associative strength $V(n) \in \mathbb{R}$ after $n$ non-reinforced trials obeys:

\[
\frac{dV}{dn} = -\lambda V, \qquad V(n) = V_0 e^{-\lambda n},
\]

with extinction rate $\lambda \in \text{trial}^{-1}$. If $\lambda = 0$, extinction never occurs and the irradiation-of-inhibition trace accumulates without bound, vetoing new conditioning.

Generalization and irradiation. From [PAGE 183] (excitation) and [PAGE 262] (inhibition) as cited on [PAGE 48], define a spatial generalization kernel over stimulus space:

\[
G_{\sigma}(s,s') = \exp\left(-\frac{\|s-s'\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
\]

where $\sigma$ is the generalization width in stimulus-space units (dimensionless if stimuli are encoded as normalized vectors, or meters/degrees if physical). The irradiated excitatory field is:

\[
E_{\text{tot}}(s) = \int G_{\sigma_E}(s,s')\, I(s')\, ds',
\]

and the irradiated extinction/inhibition field is:

\[
X_{\text{tot}}(s) = \int G_{\sigma_X}(s,s')\, \mathcal{E}(s')\, ds',
\]

where $\mathcal{E}(s')$ is the local extinction trace. Units: $[G]=1$ (dimensionless), $[E_{\text{tot}}]=\text{Hz}$ if $I$ is in Hz.

Patterning as secondary. The derived pattern response at test stimulus $s$ is a functional of the four principles:

\[
P(s) = \Phi\!\left( E_{\text{tot}}(s),\ X_{\text{tot}}(s),\ K,\ \{\Delta t_k\}_{k=1}^{m} \right),
\]

where $\Phi$ is unspecified by Hull on [PAGE 48]; any explicit choice (e.g., difference-of-Gaussians or softmax competition) is an extra assumption.

Breakdown and singularity regimes.
1. Runaway excitation: if $\rho(K) \ge 1/\tau$, the homogeneous fixed point loses stability and $\|c(t)\| \to \infty$ in the linear model; biological saturation (e.g., $c \mapsto \tanh c$) is required, marking the breakdown of the linear afferent-interaction approximation.
2. Temporal fusion: if $\max_k \Delta t_k \ll \tau$, successive stimuli are indistinguishable from a single simultaneous compound; the order-dependence principle from [PAGE 48] collapses because the pauses no longer determine distinct outcomes.
3. No generalization: $\sigma_E \to 0$ or $\sigma_X \to 0$ reduces $G$ to a Dirac delta, so irradiation vanishes and patterning cannot be derived from Hull’s four principles; each stimulus remains isolated.
4. No extinction: $\lambda = 0$ yields $X_{\text{tot}}$ monotonic growth, preventing the extinction-of-inhibition balance required for secondary patterning.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week traps.

The lineage moves retrocausally: later frameworks reinterpret earlier principles through their own formal lens. Pavlov (referenced on [PAGE 48] via [PAGE 144] and [PAGE 147-148]) described cortical fusion and order-dependent successive stimulation without molar conditioning laws. Hull (1943), reading backward through the edifice of behaviorism, declared that Pavlov’s patterning could be derived from four primary molar laws ([PAGE 349 ff.] as cited on [PAGE 48]). Köhler’s Gestalt field theory ([PAGE 55] as cited on [PAGE 48]) supplies the continuous-medium metaphysics that Hull implicitly needs but does not formalize. The line continues through Hebb’s cell assemblies (1949), Wilson–Cowan neural-field equations (1972), and modern predictive-coding / active-inference architectures, all of which treat the cortex as a continuous interactive medium where local excitation, lateral inhibition, adaptation (extinction), and generalization (irradiation) generate emergent percepts and behaviors.

Multi-week trap. A system that observes behavior only on a weekly clock faces a specific multi-week trap: it sees the molar outcome of conditioning but not the millisecond-to-second temporal microstructure that Hull says determines the final reaction ([PAGE 48], quoting [PAGE 147-148]). Over two to three weeks, a compound-stimulus protocol may appear stable because the immediate cortical fusion masks the slow drift of extinction and irradiation gradients. If the analyst assumes patterning is primary (Pavlov’s position as critiqued by Hull on [PAGE 48]), they will overestimate the durability of the pattern under perturbation, because they ignore that patterning is actually secondary and therefore dependent on extinction decay ($\lambda \in \text{trial}^{-1}$) and generalization widths ($\sigma_E,\sigma_X$) that evolve across trials. Conversely, if the analyst assumes patterning is purely secondary and ignores the fast afferent-interaction kernel $K(s,s')$, they will underestimate the speed of immediate fusion responses to novel compounds. The trap is therefore a scale mismatch: intra-trial pauses $\Delta t_k \in \text{s}$ govern whether stimuli are fused or successive, yet weekly aggregation smooths those pauses away. A multi-week intervention that changes the stimulus order without resetting extinction traces may produce a behavioral collapse that looks sudden but is actually the delayed emergence of Hull’s secondary derivation failure in the gray zone where $\sigma$ and $\lambda$ have drifted outside the regime that supported the original pattern.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit recursion, counter-trap, and the clock.

Define recursive theory-of-mind depth $\Psi(t) \in \mathbb{N}_0$. Level $\Psi=0$ is the elementary Pavlovian conditioned reflex: stimulus elicits response without patterned integration. Level $\Psi=1$ models the environment as a compound stimulus pattern. Level $\Psi=2$ models another agent as using compound-stimulus patterns, and so on. Crisis habit regression occurs when arousal $A(t)$ crosses a critical threshold $A_{\text{crit}}$; the system drops one or more levels:

\[
\Psi(t) = \max\!\bigl(0,\; \Psi(t^{-}) - 1\bigr) \quad \text{whenever} \quad A(t) > A_{\text{crit}}.
\]

The regression is a reversion to unpatterned, lower-order habits because the secondary derivation of patterning (extinction, irradiation, afferent interaction as detailed on [PAGE 48]) requires intact cortical field dynamics that high arousal disrupts. The agent can no longer maintain the fused representation of a compound pattern and collapses toward elementary S–R bonds.

Counter-trap. An adversary who expects crisis regression may deploy a patterned decoy: a compound stimulus sequence that mimics a level-2 pattern but is actually decomposable into elementary reflexes. The counter-trap succeeds only if the regressed agent, now at $\Psi=0$ or $\Psi=1$, binds the decoy as a unified pattern rather than parsing it into successive elements. This binding is gated by the clock. Let $\tau \in \text{s}$ be the neural time constant from the field equation, and let $T_{\text{crisis}} \in \text{s}$ be the duration of the high-arousal state. The counter-trap must satisfy:

\[
\tau < \Delta t_{\text{decoy}} < T_{\text{crisis}},
\]

where $\Delta t_{\text{decoy}} \in \text{s}$ is the inter-stimulus pause within the decoy. If $\Delta t_{\text{decoy}} < \tau$, the regressed system perceives temporal fusion (simultaneity), which may actually aid binding; but if $\Delta t_{\text{decoy}} > T_{\text{crisis}}$, the crisis ends before the pattern is presented, and the agent returns to $\Psi \ge 2$, defeating the trap. The veto condition is therefore $T_{\text{crisis}} < \tau$: if the crisis window is shorter than the neural integration time, no patterned counter-trap can be instantiated before regression resolves, and the model predicts failure of the counter-trap. The clock is thus not merely a parameter but a hard boundary condition on recursive mind-reading under stress.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

A neural-field model of afferent interaction is veto-proof only if every lower layer permits the assumed dynamics. Four veto layers apply.

1. Neuronal refractory layer. The absolute refractory period $\tau_{\text{ref}} \approx 1\text{--}2\ \text{ms}$ imposes a maximum afferent interaction frequency $f_{\max} \approx 1/\tau_{\text{ref}} \approx 500\text{--}1000\ \text{Hz}$. If the stimulus repetition rate $f_{\text{stim}} > f_{\max}$, cortical cells cannot respond to successive inputs as distinct events; the simultaneous/fusion principle on [PAGE 48] is physically vetoed because no independent excitation can be generated.

2. Conduction-delay layer. Axonal conduction velocity $v \in [0.5,120]\ \text{m/s}$ across cortical distances $d \in [1,10]\ \text{mm}$ yields delays $\Delta t_{\text{cond}} = d/v \in [8\ \mu\text{s},\,20]\ \text{ms}$. Hull’s order-dependence ([PAGE 48], citing [PAGE 147-148]) requires that pauses $\Delta t_k$ be resolvable. If $\Delta t_k < \Delta t_{\text{cond}}$, successive stimuli arrive at the target element within a single integration window and become physically indistinguishable; the successive-stimulus formalism breaks down and the prediction of order-dependent reactions is vetoed.

3. Biomechanical effector layer. Muscle contraction time $T_{\text{CT}} \approx 50\text{--}100\ \text{ms}$ imposes a minimum separable interval between behavioral components. If Hull’s derived pattern requires two response elements separated by $< T_{\text{CT}}$, the motor layer cannot express them; the pattern is vetoed at the output stage regardless of cortical field integrity.

4. Weather / environmental noise layer. Ambient noise raises the detection threshold for afferent input. Let signal power be $P_{\text{signal}}$ and noise power spectral density be $N_0$ (units $\text{W/Hz}$). Afferent excitation occurs only if $\text{SNR} = P_{\text{signal}}/N_0 > \theta$ for some threshold $\theta$. Under adverse weather (e.g., heavy precipitation, thermal agitation, electromagnetic interference), if $\text{SNR} < \theta$, cortical cells are not “undergoing excitation” in Pavlov’s sense ([PAGE 48], citing [PAGE 144]); no afferent interaction, no irradiation, and no patterning can be initiated. Formal veto: $\mathcal{V}_{\text{env}} = 1 \iff \text{SNR} < \theta$, which nulls the kernel $K$ in the field equation.

Adjudication rule. The neural model is conditionally veto-proof iff all of the following hold simultaneously: $f_{\text{stim}} < f_{\max}$; $\Delta t_k > \Delta t_{\text{cond}}$ for every inter-stimulus pause; adjacent pattern elements are separated by $> T_{\text{CT}}$; and $\text{SNR} > \theta$. Violation of any single inequality triggers a layer-specific veto and falsifies the prediction of patterned behavior derived from [PAGE 48].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1—Temporal-fusion veto of successive-stimulus order effects. Counterfactual: If the inter-stimulus pause $\Delta t$ is driven below the conduction-delay floor $\Delta t_{\text{cond}}$, Pavlov’s order-dependence claim on [PAGE 48] (“the order … and the pauses between them are the factors which determine the final result”) must vanish because the stimuli physically fuse. Verification: present paired audiovisual compounds to human participants with EEG at $\Delta t = 2\ \text{ms}$ versus $\Delta t = 60\ \text{ms}$, counterbalancing order A-B vs B-A, and decode reaction-topography differences. Prediction: at $\Delta t = 60\ \text{ms}$ (> $\Delta t_{\text{cond}}$), multivariate EEG patterns discriminate order; at $\Delta t = 2\ \text{ms}$ (< $\Delta t_{\text{cond}}$), order discrimination falls to chance. Veto: if order effects remain statistically significant at $\Delta t = 2\ \text{ms}$, the successive-stimulus mechanism is vetoed by refractory/conduction limits.

Scenario 2—Generalization-width veto of extinction irradiation. Counterfactual: If the generalization width for inhibition is zero ($\sigma_X = 0$), extinction of a conditioned stimulus $s^*$ should not suppress responses to nearby stimuli $s'$; Hull’s secondary derivation from irradiation of inhibition ([PAGE 262] as cited on [PAGE 48]) would fail. Verification: fear-conditioning protocol with a morph continuum of face stimuli; extinguish the center exemplar $s^*$, then test startle responses across the continuum. Prediction: suppression follows $G_{\sigma_X}(s,s') = \exp(-\|s-s'\|^2/2\sigma_X^2)$ with $\sigma_X > 0$ (units: normalized morph distance); the gradient is smooth and nonzero. Veto: if suppression is spatially flat (identical at all $\|s-s'\|$), the irradiation-of-extinction principle is vetoed at the $\sigma_X \to 0$ singularity.

Scenario 3—Arousal-dependent recursive theory-of-mind veto. Counterfactual: Under high crisis arousal ($A > A_{\text{crit}}$), agents regress from $\Psi=2$ to $\Psi=1$ or $\Psi=0$, so a level-2 counter-trap that depends on modeling the opponent’s model should fail. Verification: nested belief-task with monetary-free points (no betting lines); manipulate arousal via public speaking anticipation and index with heart-rate variability. Prediction: high-arousal subjects show significantly reduced sensitivity to second-order deception and are more easily captured by elementary patterned decoys than low-arousal subjects. Veto: if high-arousal subjects still exhibit level-2 counter-trap success at rates indistinguishable from low-arousal controls, the crisis-habit-regression axiom is falsified.
