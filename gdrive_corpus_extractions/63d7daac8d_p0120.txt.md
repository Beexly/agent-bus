A second multi-week trap concerns the curiosity prerequisite. Historically, this explains why radical scientific paradigms (Kuhn, 1962) require crises that make old heads visibly inadequate before new heads can be constructed; without the crisis, the distortion \(d\) remains above \(\epsilon_{\text{reject}}\) and the novelty is treated as not taken account of at all.

Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

Recursive theory of mind here means modeling another agent’s apperception as a function of their schema space \(H_i\) and distortion metric \(d_i\), then recursively modeling how they model one’s own \(H\). Under crisis—threat, novelty, or cognitive load—agents regress to habit regression: they collapse \(A(x)\) to the nearest existing head, even when the fit is pathological. A crisis stimulus that violates \(H_i\) is either misclassified (horse as pig) or deleted (“not taken account of at all”) [PAGE 110]. Because the agent believes their own apperception is veridical, they will attribute the misclassification to the world rather than to their own \(H_i\), producing a second-order error: they model others as sharing the same misclassified head.

The counter-trap is deliberate unhabitual perception, James’s genius faculty [PAGE 110]. Operationally, the agent must temporarily suspend \(A(x) = \arg\min d(x,h)\) and tolerate high-distortion states long enough to instantiate \(h_{\text{new}}\). The pedagogy counter-trap is to connect novel material to pre-existing curiosity, thereby lowering effective \(d\) before presentation [PAGE 110]. Both counter-traps are effortful and are precisely what crisis habit regression suppresses: under stress, cognitive resources are allocated to minimizing alteration, not to schema expansion.

The clock is the twenty-four-hour decay window. James’s observation that coerced admission vanishes “twenty-four hours later” [PAGE 110] defines a consolidation deadline \(t_{\text{deadline}} \approx 24\,\text{h}\). Any counter-trap that does not produce a stable trace \(M(t_{\text{deadline}}) > M_{\text{threshold}}\) is functionally null. Multi-week applications must therefore be organized in \(\le 24\,\text{h}\) consolidation cycles: each new head must be anchored to personal experience and pre-existing curiosity within one diurnal period, or it regresses to the old head. The regression-versus-counter-trap contest is thus decided by the clock: habit regression wins by default unless assimilation is achieved and consolidated before trace decay erases the admission.

Veto condition: if \(d(x,h) > \epsilon_{\text{reject}}\) for all pre-existing \(h\) and no \(h_{\text{new}}\) is constructed, the memory trace must satisfy \(M(24\,\text{h}) \approx 0\) per James’s twenty-four-hour clause [PAGE 110]. Prediction: the argument-only group shows above-chance recognition at \(t=0\) but chance-level performance at \(t=24\,\text{h}\), whereas an associative-anchoring group (new fact knitted to pre-existing curiosity, per the pedagogy maxim [PAGE 110]) shows \(M(24\,\text{h}) > M_{\text{threshold}}\). Veto: any model predicting durable belief change from argument alone without assimilation is falsified.

Scenario 2: Stimulus outside the hypothesis space (null curiosity/wonder condition). Verifiable setup: expose participants to a structured but conceptually foreign stimulus domain for which they possess no referents or standards (e.g., an unfamiliar formal symbol system with no semantic anchor). Measure curiosity ratings and wonder ratings on continuous scales, plus orienting behavior (dwell time, saccades). Veto condition: because \(H=\emptyset\) or standards are absent, James’s null regime requires \(C(x)=W(x)=0\) [PAGE 110]. Prediction: participants report near-zero curiosity and wonder and exhibit no systematic orienting beyond low-level novelty. A control domain with at least one available anchor yields \(C>0\) and \(W>0\). Veto: any model claiming that mere novelty generates curiosity or wonder in the absence of conceptual referents is falsified.

Scenario 3: Unhabitual-perception training (genius-faculty condition). Verifiable setup: randomize participants to either (a) schema-expansion training that explicitly rewards perceiving familiar objects in unhabitual ways (e.g., re-categorization drills, analogical remapping), or (b) an active-control task matched for effort but without schema expansion. Pre- and post-test categorization error rates on novel items that are nearest-head ambiguous (horse/pig, orange/ball). Veto condition: genius as unhabitual perception [PAGE 110] predicts that training increases \(H\) or alters \(d\) so that previously rejected or misclassified items satisfy \(d(x,h_{\text{new}})\le\epsilon_{\text{assim}}\). Prediction: the training group shows reduced nearest-head misclassification errors and increased correct novel-category assignments at post-test; the control group does not. Veto: any model attributing post-test improvement to practice, fatigue, or generic arousal without schema-space change is falsified, because the source mechanism is specifically alteration of apperceptive heads [PAGE 110].
