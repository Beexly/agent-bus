The first hop is counting: an extensive-form tree with two players acting $K$ times each over $U$ actions has $U^{2K}$ terminal paths (page 1, "the game tree complexity is $U^{2K}$"). The collapse is not a discretization trick; it is a *structural* theorem about where the equilibrium lives. Following Thm 4.1 (page 5), P1's equilibrium behavioral strategy concentrates on at most $I$ action prototypes, so P1 contributes only $I$ branches per step and $I^K$ paths when P2 best-responds (pure, no branching). Dually, P2 is $(I{+}1)$-atomic, giving $(I{+}1)^K$.

The second hop is *why* atomic. The mechanism is value convexification (page 5, proof sketch step 3): P1 controls a pushforward measure $\nu(dp')$ over posterior beliefs subject to the martingale/marginal constraint $\int p'\,\nu(dp')=p$. The Bellman operator becomes $\min_\nu \int_{\Delta(I)} \tilde V_\tau(p')\,\nu(dp')$, which is exactly the *largest convex minorant* (convexification) of the non-revealing value $\tilde V_\tau$. Here is the decisive dimensional argument: a point in the simplex $\Delta(I)$ has affine dimension $I-1$, so by Carathéodory it is a convex combination of at most $I$ vertices → $I$-atomic. In the dual, $\hat p\in\mathbb{R}^I$ is *unconstrained* (full $I$-dimensional, not on the simplex), so convexification needs $I+1$ points → $(I{+}1)$-atomic. This asymmetry ($I$ vs $I{+}1$) is the quiet crux; the paper states it verbatim on page 5: "Since $\hat p$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}^I$ rather than constrained on $\Delta(I)$, $\zeta^\dagger$ is at most $(I+1)$-atomic."

Hexner analytic core (page 24–25): payoff $J=\mathbb{E}_\theta[\int_0^T(\|\tilde\theta_1-\theta\|^2 d_1-\|\tilde\theta_2-\theta\|^2 d_2)d\tau]$; critical time $t_r=\arg\min_t\int_0^t(d_1(s)-d_2(s))\,ds$; equilibrium $\tilde\theta_{1,2}=0$ on $[0,t_r]$, $=\theta$ on $(t_r,T]$. Here $\theta\in\{-1,1\}$ dimensionless, $d_j$ a per-second weight.

Football contact/tackle (page 33, 35): sticky weight $w_{ij}=\sigma\!\big(k_{\text{tackle}}(r_{\text{thr}}^2-\|X^{(1)}_i-X^{(2)}_j\|^2)\big)$, $\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})$; merge probability $p^{(1)}_{m,i}=1-\exp(-\sum_j w_{ij})$; tackle probability $p_{\text{tackle}}=1-\prod_{j=1}^N(1-w_{\text{rb},j})$; running loss $\ell_{\text{run}}=\tfrac{0.1}{2}\tau(\text{vec}(U_1)^\top R_1\text{vec}(U_1)-\text{vec}(U_2)^\top R_2\text{vec}(U_2))+\lambda_{\text{tackle}}p_{\text{tackle}}$; terminal $L^{\text{run}}_{\text{term}}=-(x_{\text{rb}}+\alpha_{\text{in}}|y_{\text{rb}}|)$ with $\alpha_{\text{in}}=-0.8$, and $L^{\text{throw}}_{\text{term}}=-\max_i X^{(1)}_{i,x}$.

Breakdown regimes: (i) Isaacs fails → $\tilde V$ undefined; (ii) stochastic dynamics → convex operators lower-bound $V$ (page 5); (iii) $K\to\infty$ → $O(K^3C^{2K})$ blow-up (Thm 5.1); (iv) interior-NE assumption fails, breaking GDA convergence guarantees (page 6).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage runs Harsanyi (1967) → Aumann–Maschler–Stearns (1995) → De Meyer (1996) → Cardaliaguet (2007, 2009) → Ghimire et al. (2024) → this paper. Harsanyi recast incomplete information as a nature chance move at $t=0$. Aumann et al. proved the repeated-game "Cav $u$" theorem: the informed player's value is the *concavification* of the non-revealing value — the direct ancestor of Thm 4.1's convexification. De Meyer introduced the dual reformulation making P2's behavioral strategy Markov, and connected belief dynamics to a central-limit scaling. Cardaliaguet lifted these to *differential* games, proving value existence (viscosity solution of the HJI PDE) and, in 2009, the uniform convergence $V_\tau\to V$ (Lemma C.1) that Thm 4.2 leans on. Hexner (1979) supplied the analytic target-concealment benchmark. The genuinely new contribution is not convexification per se — the authors credit Aumann/De Meyer — but its *operationalization*: recognizing atomicity as a complexity-collapsing prior for MARL/MPC.

The solver-side genealogy is orthogonal but load-bearing: regret matching (Zinkevich 2007; Lanctot 2009; Tammelin CFR+ 2014; Brown & Sandholm Deep-CFR), gradient descent–ascent / regularized QRE (McMahan 2011; Perolat et al. 2021; Sokota MMD 2022; Martin & Sandholm JPSPG 2024). Multigrid for HJ PDEs descends from Braess & Hackbusch (1983) and Trottenberg et al., applied via Han & Wan (2013); the value-approximation analysis borrows NTK theory (Jacot et al. 2018) and Zanette et al. (2019).

Multi-week traps. Trap 1 — the "policy-gradient-works" trap: Rudolph et al. (2025) reported PG methods (PPO/MMD) solving imperfect-info games well, yet here PPO/MMD *fail* (Fig. 6). The reconciliation is subtle: GDA convergence guarantees require the NE to lie in the *interior* of $\Delta(U)$ (Perolat et al. 2021), but 2p0s1 equilibria are atomic → they sit on *vertices/faces* of the simplex. Anyone rebuilding on the Rudolph claim without checking atomicity will misattribute the failure to hyperparameters (the paper's own App. H sweep of learning rates $\{2.5\text{e}{-}5\ldots2.5\text{e}{-}1\}$ × entropy $\{0.01\ldots0.2\}$ is exactly that trap being exhausted). Trap 2 — the "abstraction is free" trap: discretizing $U$ and using CFR-style solvers injects abstraction error and $U$-scaling cost; the whole point is to *never* discretize. Trap 3 — the "value approximation inherits the collapse" trap: atomicity helps path-based solving ($I^K$) but the *whole-space* value approximation still pays $C^{2K}$ (Thm 5.1); conflating the two claims is a multi-week misread.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The equilibrium is a mutual-modeling fixed point: P1 best-responds to P2's *belief* trajectory, P2 best-responds to the *distribution over P1's types*, and neither can profitably deviate — that is the Nash condition ({η†}, ζ†) attaining $V$. Concretely, P1's optimization is over a pushforward of the *public* belief $\nu(dp')$ (page 5), i.e., P1 is literally choosing what P2 will infer. This is third-order ToM compressed into an operator: P1 selects action prototypes $\{u_k\}$, which via Bayes move $p\to p_k$, which changes P2's maximand $\max_v[\cdots]$, which P1 anticipated. P2, being uninformed, runs the symmetric dual over $\hat p$, interpreting $\hat p[i]$ as "the loss type-$i$ P1 would suffer under best response" (page 4) — a risk functional, "P2's equilibrium strategy minimizes the worst-case risk across all game types."

Crisis-habit regression. The empirical signature is *target/play concealment until a critical clock*. Hexner: $\tilde\theta=0$ on $[0,t_r]$ then $=\theta$ — P1 deliberately "plays dumb" (moves toward $\mathbb{E}[\theta]$) to deny P2 information, then reveals. CAMS recovers $t_r=0.60\pm0.06$ s vs analytic $0.5$ s (page 9). Football: concealment of $0.5$ s (page 10). The habit-regression analog is that a naive/exploitable agent reveals type early (maximizes immediate downfield gain) and is punished; the equilibrium *suppresses* that habit — a deliberate, clock-gated withholding of information.

Counter-trap vs clock. P2's counter-trap is Bayesian inference on the observed action sequence; P1's counter-counter is to make the *revealing action itself* maximally informative only at $t_r$, keeping the pre-$t_r$ signal type-independent. Time is the scarce resource: page 3 notes that "differential games may have multiple critical time-state-belief points where belief manipulation is required," unlike repeated games (single first-round manipulation). The concealment window is not cosmetic — it is the quantitative price of the information asymmetry, and it directly bounds how long P1 can hold the convexification advantage before the belief martingale is forced to concentrate on a vertex.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/biomechanical veto. The model is engineered for *differentiability*, not physical fidelity, and the paper is explicit about the substitution. Page 10: "Physical contact is captured by a smooth 'merge' weight that grows as opponents approach, softly blending their velocities and attenuating their ability to apply new acceleration, so tackles emerge continuously rather than through hard impulses." A real tackle is an impulsive, momentum-conserving collision (impulse $J=\int F\,dt=\Delta p$); the soft convex blend $\hat V^{(1)}_i=(V^{(1)}_i+\sum_j w_{ij}V^{(2)}_j)/(1+\sum_j w_{ij})$ does *not* conserve momentum — the blended velocity is a weighted average, injecting/removing momentum as $w_{ij}$ varies. This is a deliberate biomechanical veto: contact mechanics are replaced by a sigmoid coupler so that $\partial(\cdot)/\partial\theta$ exists for DS-GDA autodiff.

The double-integrator kinematic layer (page 32) also vetoes force–velocity physiology. Real sprint acceleration follows a force–velocity–power profile (Hill-type), $a$ decreasing with speed and bounded by metabolic power; the model instead uses componentwise clamping to `BOX_VEL`, `BOX_ACC` (page 34), yielding "globally piecewise control-affine" dynamics (page 34) with almost-everywhere differentiability. This piecewise-affine structure is exactly what makes Isaacs' condition (Eq. 2) hold so that atomicity (Thm 4.1) follows — i.e., the *tractability* is bought by *simplifying the physics*.

Weather/environment veto. There is no weather, footing, or aerodynamics layer: the game is a 2D plane with `x` downfield, `y` sideline-to-sideline. Wind would add a state-dependent drift $d(x,t)$ to $f$; wet footing would make $\mathcal{U}$ state- and time-varying and stochastic, breaking both A1 (compact fixed $\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}$) and the deterministic-belief-control property. Under the paper's own admission (page 5), stochastic dynamics collapse the convex Bellman operators to *lower bounds* — so any weather-induced noise voids the exact-NE guarantee. Adjudication: the physical layer is *consistent within its stated idealization* (smooth contact + double integrator + box clamping) but is *not* a validated biomechanical or meteorological model; it is a control-affine surrogate constructed to satisfy A1–A5. The atomic-NE result is a theorem about the surrogate, not about real 11-vs-11 contact dynamics.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Veto 1 — Stochastic-dynamics veto (checkable via page 5 concession). Counterfactual: inject process noise $\dot x=f(x,u,v)+\omega$, $\omega\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$. Prediction: atomicity degrades because P1 can no longer place beliefs at exact vertices $\{p_k\}$; CAMS's operators become strict lower bounds, and measured exploitability will floor at a positive gap scaling with $\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)$. Falsifiable test: sweep $\|\Sigma\|$ and show exploitability gap monotonically increasing from ~0, contradicting the deterministic 30-min equilibrium claim.

Veto 2 — Isaacs-failure veto (checkable via Eq. 2 / page 34). Counterfactual: replace the smooth merge with a hard-impulse (non-differentiable, momentum-conserving) tackle so that $\min_u\max_v\neq\max_v\min_u$. Prediction: the non-revealing value $\tilde V$ ceases to exist as a pure saddle, Thm 4.1's convexification has no pure inner solution, and DS-GDA (which "guarantees sublinear convergence on nonconvex-nonconcave problems," page 6) will oscillate rather than converge. Falsifiable test: measure NashConv vs iteration; a plateau/divergence under hard contacts confirms the veto, whereas the paper's convergence (Fig. 6c) requires the soft-affine surrogate.

Veto 3 — Large-$K$ value-approximation veto (checkable via Thm 5.1 vs Table 1). Counterfactual: attempt *whole-space* value approximation (not fixed-initial MPC) at growing $K$. Prediction: worst-case cost $O(K^3C^{2K}I^2\epsilon^{-4}\delta^{-2})$ is exponential in $K$; runtime should scale like $C^{2K}$. Empirical check from Table 1 (page 9): no-multigrid runtimes are $9.3,\,27.6,\,46.2$ h at $K=4,10,16$ — near-linear, i.e., $C\approx1$ empirically and multigrid (2.3/10.9/17.8 h) tames the low-frequency error. So the *worst-case bound is loose*; the verifiable prediction is that pushing $K$ much higher (e.g., $K=30$–$40$) with a poor value hypothesis class will eventually reveal the $C^{2K}$ wall, vindicating Thm 5.1 while showing the $K=10$ football demonstration sits safely below it because it uses $I^K=2^{10}=1024$ path-based rollouts, not full-space approximation.

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
