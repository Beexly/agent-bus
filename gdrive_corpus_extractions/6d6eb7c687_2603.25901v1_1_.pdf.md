<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All three losses are categorical cross-entropy expressed in **nats** (natural log; divide by ln 2 for bits). Logits are $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times(R+1)}$, $z\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times C}$, etc.

Matchup loss, [PAGE 18], Eq. (1) — per-defender independent multi-class over receivers plus a "no matchup" class:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{matchup}} = -\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{r=0}^{R} z^{*}_{d,r}\,\log\!\left(\frac{\exp(Z_{d,r})}{\sum_{r'=0}^{R}\exp(Z_{d,r'})}\right)$$
Units/args: $D$ = number of defenders (11); $R$ = eligible receivers; index $r=0$ is the "no matchup" class; $z^{*}_{d,r}$ is the (transposed) ground-truth one-hot; rows that zero-sum in the target are forced to the "no matchup" label = 1.0 [PAGE 17–18].

Coverage loss, [PAGE 18], Eq. (2) — per-defender multi-class over $C=20$ assignments:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{coverage}} = -\frac{1}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\mathbb{1}[c=c^{*}]\,\log\!\left(\frac{\exp(z_{d,c})}{\sum_{c'=1}^{C}\exp(z_{d,c'})}\right)$$
$\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator (Iverson bracket); $c^{*}$ the ground-truth class for defender $d$; averaged over the 11-defender unit.

Target-defender loss, [PAGE 18], Eq. (3) — single-label categorical over $N$ players:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{target}} = -\log\!\left(\frac{\exp(z_{d})}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(z_{j})}\right)$$
$N = \text{num\_defenders} + \text{targeted receiver}$; the offensive logit is masked to $-10^9$ before softmax [PAGE 18].

Breakdown / singularity regimes:
- **Mask underflow (benign):** the $-10^9$ sentinel makes $\exp(z)\to 0$ exactly in float32, so the receiver leaves the denominator cleanly. A true $0/0$ singularity would require *all* candidate logits masked to $-10^9$; that cannot occur because defenders retain finite logits. This is why the mask is safe rather than NaN-producing.
- **Cascade instability (structural, not numerical):** because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{coverage}}$ averages correlated per-defender errors, a shell error is not a bounded single-class mistake — it produces a heavy-tailed loss contribution (the "6+ errors" tail, [PAGE 11]). The metric is therefore non-Lipschitz in scheme correctness.
- **Rare-event regime (double coverage):** ground-truth base rate is $1{,}179/21{,}567 = 5.5\%$ [PAGE 14]. Per-receiver counts live in a small-$n$ Poisson-like regime: $\mathrm{SE}\approx\sqrt{p(1-p)/n_{\text{plays}}}$ on the *rate* understates instability because the *count* itself is small (Mike Evans: 10 all season [PAGE 15]; Tre Tucker: model 9 vs human 0). A handful of "judgment call" flips swing per-player rates by tens of percent — visible at the individual level, invisible in the 5.5% aggregate.
- **Disguise operator:** defined qualitatively as (pre-snap accuracy) vs (full-play accuracy) [PAGE 13, Fig. 7]. It is a *difference of two accuracies on the same head*, not an independent measurement, so it inherits the scheme-feature dependency noted in THINK_TRACE.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is a clean four-generation descent toward frame-by-frame individual assignment. Generation 0: post-hoc, team-level coverage classification — the ensemble CNN-attention model of Song et al. [12], "achieved 88.9% accuracy across eight coverage types" [PAGE 2], plus a CNN-LSTM trajectory model [6, 10] that established defensive backs as the hardest class to model due to "long-distance travel and frequent direction changes based on ball perception and receiver movement" [PAGE 2]. Generation 1: the factorized-attention idea itself is imported from Scene Transformer [11] and AutoBots [9] (interleaved temporal/social attention) and, most directly, from HoopTransformer [13], which "appl[ied] attention along the agent axis at each timestep, then along the time axis for each agent independently" [PAGE 2–3]. The basketball lineage [13] is the explicit template.

The "multi-week trap" the authors had to escape was the field's default framing: coverage as a *static, team-level, post-hoc* label. Every prior system answered "what shell was this?" once per play. The trap is that a single-label answer structurally cannot represent rotations, handoffs, and disguises — so errors that are actually label artifacts get scored as model failures. This paper's escape is the truncation-augmentation regime: "11 fixed truncation strategies" plus a random strategy, "fixed truncation or random truncation at a 60% and 40% split" [PAGE 17]. That augmentation is what converts a play-level classifier into a *temporal* predictor, and it is the specific mechanism that unlocks the derivative metrics (disguise rate, double-coverage rate) that have no analog in the CNN-LSTM generation.

Second-order lineage point: the target-defender head's most direct ancestor is a "rule-based approach... nearest defender to the targeted receiver at pass arrival" [PAGE 7]. So one of the three tasks is measured against a *heuristic baseline* (76.4%) rather than a learned one, which is why the +12-point lift is emphasized as the headline for that task [PAGE 8, 12].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reframed to the object at hand, this section is about the adversarial inference the model performs — and how its "priors" (habit), "counter-trap detection," and the play "clock" interact.

Theory-of-mind layer: defense runs a signaling game. It shows an alignment (observable) while holding a true coverage (hidden); the QB must infer intent from cues. The model is a *third-party observer solving the QB's inference problem* from tracking alone. Its ToM competence is demonstrated by the Invert Cover 2 example [PAGE 10, Fig. 3]: pre-snap it reads the conventional shell ("two high safeties... deep left and right halves, outside cornerbacks cover the flats"), then detects that "the outside cornerbacks drop back... the high safeties step forward... the slot corners shift to the flats" and updates by pass arrival. That is the model attributing and *revising* an inferred intention — a genuine recursive-mind step, not pattern-matching a static label.

Habit (regression to prior): the model carries a strong symmetry/shell prior — "most coverage schemes exhibit spatial symmetry, such as flat left and flat right assignments... and defaults to this principle unless the play dynamics indicate otherwise." [PAGE 11] This prior is usually correct (hence 70% clean plays) but is exactly what makes the model *disagree* with legitimate asymmetric/poaching looks [PAGE 11], and what makes it "bias toward symmetry" on the Cover 4-vs-Cover 2 play [PAGE 11, Fig. 5].

Counter-trap (deception detection): disguise rate is the model out-reading human perception. The sharp twist is Tampa Bay: Bowles is "known for his more unpredictable blitz packages," yet the model finds him *readable* — "his coverage rotations are easily identified by the model based on his secondary's timing of their movement before the snap." [PAGE 14] So the model separates "unpredictable to human eyes" from "predictable to a movement-timing tracker," while flagging Kansas City/Spagnuolo as the hardest to read (lowest pre-snap accuracy). That is a model of *model-readability of deception*, one recursion deeper than human scouting.

Clock: the play clock (snap → pass forward → pass arrival) governs how much ToM the model can exercise. On sub-2-second "quick game," coverage cannot mature, so both human labelers and the model fall back to priors — and disagree — as in the 2.1s play [PAGE 10, Fig. 4] and the <2s Cover 2/4 play [PAGE 11, Fig. 5]. Habit dominates when the clock is short; counter-trap detection dominates when the clock is long enough for rotations to reveal intent.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/schematic vetoes constrain which labels and predictions are even admissible.

1. Body-constraint veto (physical): a defender occupies one location at one time, so any label set that requires simultaneous disjoint deep responsibilities for one player is inadmissible. This is the exact basis for rejecting the "quarters inside left" annotation: it "violates the charting guidelines since no teammate is assigned the outside quarters area on that side of the field." [PAGE 10–11] The four deep zones must *partition* the deep field; an unpaired "quarters inside" leaves "outside quarters" uncovered — a partition violation, hence vetoed. The model's own alternative (deep-half) satisfies the partition; the human label does not.

2. Biomechanical timing veto: DBs are the hardest trajectories because of "long-distance travel and frequent direction changes" [PAGE 2]. A prediction requiring a defender to traverse two zones within the available window (e.g., 2.1 s in Fig. 4) is physically suspect; this is why compressed-timeline plays are flagged as "inherently difficult" and why the model conservatively defaults to the shell.

3. Coupling veto (schematic, cross-layer): assignments are "tightly coupled to the overall shell," so a scheme-level disagreement vetoes per-defender correctness en masse — the cascade regime. The most extreme is Prevent, where "the model assigns Prevent labels to all coverage defenders, concentrating misclassifications on a single play." [PAGE 11] The veto here is that you cannot grade individual assignments independently of the (possibly wrong) shell.

Net adjudication: the model is *more* consistent with physical/schematic constraints than the ground truth in the documented gray-area plays (Fig. 4, Fig. 5), which is the paper's core claim that reported accuracy understates true accuracy — corroborated by Top-2 = 97% [PAGE 12].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable scenarios, each checkable against the 2024 holdout or the promised ablations [PAGE 15].

1. Scheme-feature corruption veto. Prediction: replace the upstream team-scheme input [PAGE 3] with a deliberately wrong scheme and coverage-assignment accuracy collapses *disproportionately* through cascade errors (multi-defender per play), while Top-2 stays near 97%. Falsifiable form: top-1 accuracy drop ≫ the ~4-point pre/post gap; Top-2 largely intact. Verifiable in the planned ablation "isolating the contributions of... the upstream coverage feature" [PAGE 15]. If Top-2 also collapsed, the cascade-only hypothesis is falsified.

2. Disguise-extremes veto. Prediction: on the most-deceptive defenses (lowest pre-snap coverage accuracy, e.g., Kansas City, Fig. 7) the pre-snap→full-play coverage gap exceeds the league-average ~4 points; on the most-predictable (San Francisco / Tampa Bay) it falls below. Falsifiable by binning per-team pre-snap accuracy from Fig. 7 against per-team gaps. If the gap is team-independent, the disguise-rate interpretation is falsified.

3. Quick-game clock veto. Prediction: for time-to-throw < ~2 s, coverage cannot develop, so the model regresses to the shell prior and disagreement concentrates on *deep-zone* distinctions (Cover 2 vs Cover 4), not on flat/hook assignments. Falsifiable by binning plays by time-to-throw and measuring (a) error density rising as throw time shrinks and (b) errors being deep-zone-typed. A flat error profile across throw times would falsify it.

Corollary (double-coverage small-sample prediction): model-identified double coverages will over-index on low-reputation, man-heavy receivers relative to human labels (Tre Tucker 9-vs-0 [PAGE 14]), because human labels carry "reputation bias" toward stars. Falsifiable by checking whether model-minus-human double-coverage deltas correlate negatively with receiver fame/target share. Note the invariants: these are model-behavior predictions on the dataset, not any market claim — and no such market is present in the source.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
