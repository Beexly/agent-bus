(1)]. The public’s random home bet share is \(B_h(s)\in[0,1]\), with visitor share \(B_v(s)=1-B_h(s)\). Crucially, \(B_h(s)\) is allowed to be statistically dependent on \(M\). Conditional losing-side shares are defined as \(b_{h,L}(s)\equiv E[B_h(s)\mid M<s]\) and \(b_{v,L}(s)\equiv E[B_v(s)\mid M>s]\) [PAGE 2, eq. (2)].

The single-game profit under symmetric vig \(\phi\) and no pushes is \(\pi(s)=B_h(s)-\phi B_v(s)\) when the home side loses, \(\pi(s)=B_v(s)-\phi B_h(s)\) when the visitor loses, and zero on a push. Taking the total expectation over the two outcome partitions yields eq. (3) on [PAGE 2]. Substituting \(B_v=1-B_h\) and the conditional shares collapses the two conditional expectations to \((1+\phi)b_{h,L}(s)-\phi\) and \((1+\phi)b_{v,L}(s)-\phi\), respectively. Collecting terms and using \(F_m(s)+\bar F_m(s)=1\) produces the profit–bias identity:

\[
E[\pi(s)]=(1+\phi)Q(s)-\phi,\qquad Q(s)\equiv b_{h,L}(s)\Pr(M<s)+b_{v,L}(s)\Pr(M>s)
\]

[PAGE 2, eqs. (4)–(6)]. Thus expected profit is affine and strictly increasing in \(Q(s)\), the expected fraction of handle on the losing side. This is the first gray-zone boundary: the identity assumes \(\pi_{\text{push}}(s)=0\) and symmetric vigs; asymmetric vigs \(\phi_h,\phi_v\) and positive push probability are deferred to Supplementary Sec. S1 [PAGE 2, PAGES 18–19], and the main-text bound \(E[\pi]\le(1+\phi)Q(s)\) becomes exact only under those restrictions.

Proposition 1 decomposes \(Q(s)\) into three channels [PAGE 3, eq. (9)]:

\[
Q(s)=\frac12+2\theta(s)\lambda(s)+2F_m(s)\bar F_m(s)\delta(s),
\]

where \(\theta(s)\equiv F_m(s)-\tfrac12\) is shading, \(\lambda(s)\equiv b_h(s)-\tfrac12\) is lean, and \(\delta(s)\equiv b_{h,L}(s)-b_{h,W}(s)\) is the outcome covariance of the bet share [PAGE 3, eqs. (7)–(8)]. Substituting into the identity gives the margin decomposition [PAGE 3, eq. (10)]:

\[
E[\pi(s)]=\underbrace{\frac{1-\phi}{2}}_{\text{hold}}+\underbrace{2(1+\phi)\theta(s)\lambda(s)}_{\text{shading}\times\text{lean}}+\underbrace{F_m(s)\bar F_m(s)\delta(s)}_{\text{outcome covariance}}.
\]

Two necessary corollaries follow: lean without shading yields zero excess profit (Corollary 2, [PAGE 3]), and shading without lean yields zero excess profit (Corollary 3, [PAGE 3]). The third term equals \(2\operatorname{Cov}(B_h(s),\mathbf 1\{M<s\})\) [PAGE 3, eq. (11)], so it is outcome-covariant and only resolvable after the event.

This links bookmaker profit directly to misclassification error.

Key breakdown regimes: pushes invalidate the main-text identity (requires SI S1); asymmetric vigs require Proposition S1 [PAGE 20]; if \(b_{\max}(s)\le\tau\), the book never profits regardless of \(F_m(s)\) [PAGE 5]; if \(b_{\min}(s)>\tau\), it always profits; detection power is limited—5 pp of aligned mispricing is detected 88% of the time versus 6% when orthogonal to lean, and a calibrated book can generate local departures up to 9.4 pp by chance at this sample size [PAGE 10]. Margins are reconstructed from shares, not observed dollars, so volume distribution is unseen [PAGE 13]. All results are observational except the local projections, so causal claims about informed money are limited [PAGE 13].

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators and equations extracted from the source, with units and page citations.

1. Outcome CDF and push probability [PAGE 2, eq. (1)]:
\[
F_m(s)\equiv\Pr(M<s),\qquad \pi_{\text{push}}(s)\equiv\Pr(M=s),\qquad \bar F_m(s)\equiv\Pr(M>s)=1-F_m(s)-\pi_{\text{push}}(s).
\]
Units: dimensionless probabilities in \([0,1]\). \(M\) is the realized game statistic (margin, total, or player prop). \(s\) is the posted threshold.

2. Conditional losing-side bet shares [PAGE 2, eq. (2)]:
\[
b_{h,L}(s)\equiv E[B_h(s)\mid M<s],\qquad b_{v,L}(s)\equiv E[B_v(s)\mid M>s],\qquad B_v(s)=1-B_h(s).
\]
Units: fractions of total handle, dimensionless in \([0,1]\).

3. Single-game expected profit, symmetric vig \(\phi\), no pushes [PAGE 2, eq. (3)]:
\[
E[\pi(s)]=F_m(s)E[B_h(s)-\phi B_v(s)\mid M<s]+\bar F_m(s)E[B_v(s)-\phi B_h(s)\mid M>s].
\]
Units: profit per unit of handle (dimensionless margin). \(\phi\) is the net payout per unit wagered on a winner; at standard pricing \(\phi=100/110\approx0.909\).

4. Profit–bias identity [PAGE 2, eqs. (4)–(6)]:
\[
E[\pi(s)]=(1+\phi)Q(s)-\phi,\qquad Q(s)\equiv b_{h,L}(s)F_m(s)+b_{v,L}(s)\bar F_m(s).
\]
\(Q(s)\) is the expected share of handle on the losing side. Breakdown: assumes \(\pi_{\text{push}}(s)=0\) and \(\phi_h=\phi_v\equiv\phi\); general asymmetric-vig case in SI S1 [PAGES 18–19].

5. Independence test [PAGE 3, eq. (7)]:
\[
\delta(s)\equiv b_{h,L}(s)-b_{h,W}(s)=0,
\]
where \(b_{h,W}(s)\equiv E[B_h(s)\mid M>s]\). Independence holds iff \(\delta(s)=0\). Units: percentage points (pp) in empirical application.

6. Shading and lean definitions [PAGE 3, eq. (8)]:
\[
\theta(s)\equiv F_m(s)-\frac12,\qquad \lambda(s)\equiv b_h(s)-\frac12.
\]
Units: probability units; \(\theta>0\) means the posted threshold implies home is more likely than the true median suggests.

7. Anatomy of the margin, Proposition 1 [PAGE 3, eqs. (9)–(10)]:
\[
Q(s)=\frac12+2\theta(s)\lambda(s)+2F_m(s)\bar F_m(s)\delta(s),
\]
\[
E[\pi(s)]=\underbrace{\frac{1-\phi}{2}}_{\text{hold}}+\underbrace{2(1+\phi)\theta(s)\lambda(s)}_{\text{shading}\times\text{lean}}+\underbrace{F_m(s)\bar F_m(s)\delta(s)}_{\text{outcome covariance}}.
\]
The book exceeds hold iff the bracket is strictly positive.

8. Covariance equivalence [PAGE 3, eq. (11)]:
\[
Q(s)=\frac12+2\theta(s)\lambda(s)+2\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(B_h(s),\mathbf 1\{M<s\}\bigr).
\]
The third term is twice the covariance between bet share and the home-loss indicator.

9. Public-belief model [PAGE 4, eq. (12)]:
\[
Y=X+\varepsilon+V,\qquad M=X+U,
\]
with public backing home iff \(Y>s\), so \(B_h(s)=\mathbf 1\{Y>s\}\). Units: \(X\) latent expected margin, \(Y\) public belief, \(\varepsilon\) systematic bias, \(V\) belief noise, \(U\) outcome noise independent of \(V\).

10. Profit driver as misclassification probability, Proposition 4 [PAGE 4, eq. (19)]:
\[
Q(s)=\Pr(Y>s,\,M<s)+\Pr(Y\le s,\,M>s)=\Pr(\text{public backs the losing side}).
\]
Thus book profit is affine in the public’s Bayes-error rate for classifying \(\operatorname{sign}(M-s)\).

11. Book break-even threshold [PAGE 5, eq. (13)]:
\[
\tau\equiv\frac{\phi}{1+\phi}\in\left(0,\tfrac12\right),\qquad 1-\tau=\frac1{1+\phi}.
\]
Units: dimensionless. At \(\phi=100/110\), \(\tau\approx0.476\).

12. Book-profit condition [PAGE 5, eq. (14)]:
\[
Q(s)>\tau.
\]
Because \(Q(s)\) is a convex combination of \(b_{h,L}\) and \(b_{v,L}\) weighted by \(F_m(s)\) [eq. (15)], \(Q(s)\in[\min(b_{h,L},b_{v,L}),\max(b_{h,L},b_{v,L})]\) [eq. (16)].

13. Proposition 5 regimes [PAGE 5]:
(i) Always: \(b_{\min}(s)>\tau\Rightarrow Q(s)>\tau\) for all \(F_m(s)\).
(ii) Never: \(b_{\max}(s)\le\tau\Rightarrow Q(s)\le\tau\) for all \(F_m(s)\).
(iii) Threshold: \(b_{\min}(s)\le\tau<b_{\max}(s)\), crossing at
\[
F^*(s)=\frac{\tau-b_{v,L}(s)}{b_{h,L}(s)-b_{v,L}(s)}\in[0,1].
\]

14. Bettor positive expected value [PAGE 5, eq. (18)]:
\[
E[\text{home bet}]>0\iff F_m(s)<\tau,\qquad E[\text{visitor bet}]>0\iff F_m(s)>1-\tau.
\]
Bettor set \(B\) is the union of these tails [eq. (19)].

15. Goldilocks Zone [PAGE 5, eq. (17)]:
\[
G\equiv\{s:Q(s)>\tau\}\cap B.
\]
Sufficient: \(b_{\min}(s)>\tau\) and \(F_m(s)\) in a tail. Necessary: \(b_{\max}(s)>\tau\) and \(F_m(s)\) in a tail.

16. Devigged implied probability [PAGE 14, eq. (20)]:
\[
d_V(\phi_h,\phi_v)=\frac{1/(1+\phi_h)}{1/(1+\phi_h)+1/(1+\phi_v)}.
\]

17. Temporal mixed model [PAGE 15, eq. (21)]:
\[
B_{g,t}=\beta_0+\beta_h h_{g,t}+\beta_W W_g+\beta_{hW}(h_{g,t}\times W_g)+u_g+\varepsilon_{g,t}.
\]
Here \(h_{g,t}\) is hours before onset, \(W_g\) indicates home win. Dependence movement is \(\delta(h)=b_{h,L}(h)-b_{h,W}(h)=-\beta_W-\beta_{hW}h\).

18. Jordà local projections [PAGE 16–17, eq. (22)]:
\[
B_{g,t+h}-B_{g,t-1}=\beta^{B\leftarrow\ell}_h\Delta\ell_{g,t}+\gamma_h\Delta B_{g,t-1}+a_g+\varepsilon_{g,t},
\]
\[
\ell_{g,t+h}-\ell_{g,t-1}=\beta^{\ell\leftarrow B}_h\Delta B_{g,t}+\gamma'_h\Delta\ell_{g,t-1}+a'_g+\varepsilon'_{g,t}.
\]
Empirical result: \(\beta^{B\leftarrow\ell}_4\approx-0.20\) (\(t=-3.11,p=0.002\)); \(\beta^{\ell\leftarrow B}_h\approx0\) (\(|\\beta|\le0.010,p>0.27\)) [PAGE 11].

19. Per-game reconstructed profit [PAGE 16, eq. (23)] and margin decomposition [PAGE 16, eq. (24)]:
\[
\pi_i=C_i\bigl((1-b_{h,i})-\phi_{h,i}b_{h,i}\bigr)+(1-C_i)\bigl(b_{h,i}-\phi_{v,i}(1-b_{h,i})\bigr),
\]
\[
\pi_i=h_i+D_i\lambda_i(p^*_i-C_i),\qquad D_i=2+\phi_{h,i}+\phi_{v,i},\qquad h_i=\frac{1-\phi_{h,i}\phi_{v,i}}{D_i}.
\]
Units: percent of handle.

20. Asymmetric-vig general identity, Proposition S1 [PAGE 20, eq. (S5)]:
\[
E[\pi(s)]=h+D\theta(s)\lambda(s)+DF_m(s)\bar F_m(s)\delta(s),\qquad D=2+\phi_h+\phi_v,\quad p^*=\frac{1+\phi_v}{D},\quad h=\frac{1-\phi_h\phi_v}{D}.
\]
Reduces to main-text eq. (10) when \(\phi_h=\phi_v=\phi\) [PAGE 20].

Empirical bounds from the source [PAGE 10, Figure 5c–d]: aligned shading upper bound \(\le3.7\) pp; outcome-covariance upper bound \(\le5.2\) pp; mean profit \(5.20\%\) (95% CI [0.88, 9.35]) vs hold \(4.39\%\); per-game s.d. \(73.8\%\); \(n=1{,}139\).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The theoretical lineage begins with Levitt (2004), who modeled bookmaker profit as a function of the favorite’s win probability and the fraction of handle on the favorite, implicitly treating bet share as independent of outcome [PAGE 1]. The present paper treats Levitt’s expression as the independence special case of a broader identity [PAGES 2–3]. The adverse-selection counter-lineage runs through Shin (1991, 1992, 1993), who attributes margins to privately informed insiders rather than public bias [PAGE 1], and is recently re-weighed against ordinary bettor disagreement by Whelan (2024, 2025) [PAGE 1]. Empirical tests of Levitt’s model by Paul and Weinbach (2007, 2008, 2009, 2011, 2012), Humphreys (2010, 2011), and others are cited as the literature that inherited the independence assumption without examining it [PAGE 1]. Earlier market-efficiency work includes Woodland and Woodland (1994) on baseball, Gandar et al. (2002), and Ottaviani and Sørensen (2008) on favorite–longshot bias [PAGE 1]. The public-belief model draws on Snowberg and Wolfers (2010), Jullien and Salanié (2000), and Avery and Chevalier (1999) for noisy biased signals [PAGE 1], while the Goldilocks existence condition connects to Shleifer and Vishny (1997) and Hubáček et al. (2019) on limits of arbitrage [PAGE 4]. Dmochowski (2023) supplies the individual-bettor positive-expected-value conditions used to define set \(B\) [PAGE 4]. Kuypers (2000) supports the view that books do not move lines to equalize money [PAGE 11]. The broader efficiency debate is anchored by Sauer (1998), Gray and Gray (1997), and Vandenbruaene et al. (2022) [PAGE 13].

Multi-week traps: the empirical window spans 2026-05-31 through 2026-08-28, described as an eleven-week duration [PAGE 8]. The first trap is pooling: aggregating all games creates a spurious bet-share–outcome correlation (\(b\delta=+5.8\) pp, \(p=0.0004\)) that vanishes when stratified by favorite identity (home favorites \(-1.5\) pp, \(p=0.51\); away favorites \(+0.6\) pp, \(p=0.79\)) [PAGE 9]. The second trap is temporal: for home underdogs, outcome-conditioned dependence \(\delta(t)\) is significant early in the pre-game window (\(t=-24\) to \(-18\) h: \(b\delta=-8.9\) pp) but converges by first pitch, with a significant time\(\times\)outcome interaction (\(p=0.038\)) [PAGE 10]. This implies a square bias that strengthens as the clock approaches game onset, a dynamic Levitt’s static independence assumption cannot capture. The third trap is causal direction: local projections show the posted price leads public share, but share does not lead price [PAGE 11], so any model that assumes the book chases money is backwards. A fourth trap is misattribution of margin: a large public favorite lean (\(+29.6\) pp / \(-24.5\) pp) coexists with a margin indistinguishable from hold, because Corollary 3 requires shading to multiply lean, and no shading is detected [PAGE 10].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: as the pre-game clock winds down, the public does not become more informed; in the home-underdog stratum, the outcome-conditioned gap \(\delta(t)=b_{h,L}(t)-b_{h,W}(t)\) evolves from an early sharp pattern (more home money on the eventual winner at \(t=-24\) to \(-18\) h) toward convergence, which the authors interpret as increasing square betting closer to first pitch [PAGE 10]. Under the public-belief model \(Y=X+\varepsilon+V\) [PAGE 4], the representative bettor acts on a noisy biased signal and regresses to habitual favorite loyalty as decision time compresses, rather than incorporating new private information.

Counter-trap: the book faces a choice between extracting structural profit via \(\theta(s)\lambda(s)\) (Corollary 3) or accepting the hold. Because the data bound aligned shading to \(\le3.7\) pp and show no detectable calibration departure (\(+0.48\) pp, 95% CI [\(-2.42,+3.38\)], \(p=0.54\)) [PAGE 10], the book avoids the counter-trap of over-shading into the public lean. It accepts enormous per-game variance (s.d. 73.8% of handle) without increasing mean return above the 4.39% hold [PAGE 10, Figure 5a]. This is a risk-without-reward equilibrium: the book takes on uncompensated idiosyncratic variance because the profit channel requires both lean and shading, and only lean is present.

Clock: the temporal layer is the 24-hour pre-game window sampled hourly and aggregated into non-overlapping 6-hour bins [PAGE 8, PAGES 10, 15]. The clock operates through two coupled processes: (i) book pricing \(\ell_{g,t}\) evolves via payout adjustments (the handicap is fixed at \(\pm1.5\), so line movement is price movement) [PAGE 14–15], and (ii) public share \(B_{g,t}\) responds to price innovations with a small fade of \(\approx-0.20\) points per one-point implied-probability increase at horizon \(h=4\) [PAGE 11]. The reverse impulse response is flat (\(\beta^{\ell\leftarrow B}_h\approx0\), all \(p>0.27\)) [PAGE 11]. Thus the recursion is one-directional: the book posts a price anticipating the lean; the public updates its bet share in response; the book does not chase the share. The game clock \(h\) indexes this recursion, and the breakdown regime occurs when \(F_m(s)\) sits in the bettor tail while \(b_{\max}(s)\le\tau\), making the Goldilocks Zone empty [PAGE 5].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto layer 1 — Outcome (physical) layer: the main-text identity assumes \(\pi_{\text{push}}(s)=0\) [PAGE 2]. A push (\(M=s\)) voids all wagers and breaks the affine identity; the general treatment requires Supplementary Sec. S1 with \(\pi_{\text{push}}(s)>0\) [PAGES 2, 18–19]. Weather or biomechanical events that shift \(M\) (e.g., precipitation altering scoring, pitcher fatigue altering the margin distribution) change \(F_m(s)\) and can push the system across the Proposition 5 threshold \(F^*(s)\) [PAGE 5], vetoing book profitability if \(Q(s)\le\tau\).

Veto layer 2 — Pricing (economic) layer: asymmetric vigs \(\phi_h\neq\phi_v\) invalidate the symmetric hold \((1-\phi)/2\) and require Proposition S1 with \(D=2+\phi_h+\phi_v\) and \(p^*=(1+\phi_v)/D\) [PAGE 20]. If the book does not shade (\(\theta=0\)), Corollary 2 shows lean alone cannot generate excess profit regardless of \(\delta\) [PAGE 3]. If the public does not lean (\(\lambda=0\)), Corollary 3 shows shading alone cannot generate excess profit [PAGE 3]. The empirical veto is explicit: the upper bound on aligned shading is 3.7 pp and direct calibration testing rejects detectable mispricing (\(p=0.54\)) [PAGE 10], so the \(\theta\lambda\) channel is vetoed.

Veto layer 3 — Betting (behavioral) layer: outcome covariance \(\delta(s)\) must satisfy \(b_{\max}(s)>\tau\) for the book to ever profit, and \(b_{\min}(s)>\tau\) for it to always profit [PAGE 5]. The empirical bound on \(\delta\) is \(\le5.2\) pp [PAGE 10, Figure 5d], and stratification by favorite identity removes the pooled dependence entirely (\(p=0.51\) and \(p=0.79\)) [PAGE 9], vetoing the covariance channel in both strata.

Veto layer 4 — Goldilocks (bettor-book intersection) layer: \(G=\emptyset\) unless \(b_{\max}(s)>\tau\) and \(F_m(s)\) lies in a tail (\(F_m(s)<\tau\) or \(F_m(s)>1-\tau\)) simultaneously [PAGE 5]. Since the reconstructed margin is indistinguishable from hold [PAGE 10], no spread satisfies both book profitability beyond hold and bettor positive expected value in a way that exceeds the risk-free hold.

Veto layer 5 — Reconstruction and inference layer: margins are reconstructed from handle shares, not observed dollars, so the volume distribution across games is unseen [PAGE 13]. All estimates are observational except the local projections, so causal identification of informed money is vetoed [PAGE 13]. Sample-size veto: a calibrated book can produce local mispricing departures up to 9.4 pp by chance, and detection power against aligned mispricing is only 88% for 5 pp versus 6% when orthogonal to lean [PAGE 10].

Composite verdict: every excess-profit channel (shading\(\times\)lean, outcome covariance) is vetoed by the observed confidence intervals; the hold channel remains the only statistically supported margin source, and it comes with higher variance, not higher expectation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Pooled square-bias arbitrage. Counterfactual: an RL agent observes the pooled estimate \(b\delta=+5.8\) pp (\(p=0.0004\)) and infers a square public, betting against the public side expecting excess profit from the outcome-covariance channel \(F_m\bar F_m\delta\). Veto: stratification by favorite identity removes the effect (home favorites \(b\delta=-1.5\) pp, \(p=0.51\); away favorites \(b\delta=+0.6\) pp, \(p=0.79\)) [PAGE 9]. The pooled correlation is a Simpson’s paradox driven by favorite identity confounding both public preference and outcome frequency. Prediction: wagering against the public in either stratum yields no systematic excess profit beyond the hold; the covariance term \(F_m\bar F_m\delta(s)\) is statistically zero within strata.

Scenario 2 — Lean exploitation without shading. Counterfactual: an agent observes the large favorite lean (\(\lambda\approx+29.6\) pp when home favored, \(-24.5\) pp when visitor favored) [PAGE 10] and assumes Levitt-style shading \(\theta>0\) aligned with the lean, expecting profit from \(2(1+\phi)\theta\lambda\). Veto: Corollary 3 requires non-zero same-sign \(\theta\) and \(\lambda\); the data bound aligned shading to \(\le3.7\) pp [PAGE 10, Figure 5c] and direct calibration testing finds average mispricing \(+0.48\) pp with 95% CI [\(-2.42,+3.38\)] (\(p=0.54\)) [PAGE 10]. Prediction: the shading\(\times\)lean channel contributes indistinguishable-from-zero excess profit; realized margin remains at the 4.39% hold (observed mean 5.20% within CI [0.88, 9.35]) [PAGE 10].

Scenario 3 — Share-to-line front-running. Counterfactual: an agent monitors hourly bet-share movements and places wagers anticipating that the book will move the posted price to chase unbalanced money, profiting from the ensuing line adjustment. Veto: Jordà local projections show price leads share (\(\beta^{B\leftarrow\ell}_4\approx-0.20\), \(p=0.002\)) while share does not lead price (\(\beta^{\ell\leftarrow B}_h\approx0\), \(|\beta|\le0.010\), all \(p>0.27\)) [PAGE 11]. The book does not reprice in response to observed share changes; the public fades price moves instead. Prediction: share-initiated line-chasing strategies generate no predictable price response and no exploitable edge; the causal arrow runs from posted price to public bet share, not the reverse.
