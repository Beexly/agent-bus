<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The 24 lbm shot (m = 10.886 kg) [PAGE 1] imposes a static shoulder torque τ ≈ m g L_arm sin α. With L_arm ≈ 0.70 m and α ≈ 30°, τ_static ≈ 37.8 N·m; dynamic release adds acceleration and centripetal terms, pushing rotator-cuff and glenohumeral loads toward tissue tolerance limits. The 16 lbm shot reduces this torque by one-third, representing a physiological veto boundary: if the athlete’s maximum voluntary isometric shoulder torque is below the dynamic demand, the implement cannot be accelerated to competitive v₀ without injury risk.

Biomechanical layer. The release must occur “in front of the shoulder” [PAGE 1], constraining the release point to an anatomical cone anterior to the glenoid. Violation changes the effective moment arm of the implement about the shoulder and may alter the release velocity vector beyond the admissible region. The four-inch board is a hard geometric veto: “no part of the competitor's person must be on this board” [PAGE 1]. Contact is binary and discontinuous; no gradual penalty exists. Stepping out of the limited space before the put is measured is likewise an absolute fault [PAGE 1], meaning kinetic energy gained from a follow-through outside the enclosure cannot be harvested.

Weather layer. Air density ρ(T,P) and wind **w** modify the effective relative velocity **v**_rel = **v**_shot − **w**, so drag power scales as P_d ∝ ||**v**_rel||³. A headwind reduces the forward component of v₀ relative to the ground, decreasing range by an amount approximately ΔR ≈ −(C_d A ρ / 2m) ∫₀^{t_f} ||**v**_rel|| v_x dt. Crosswind introduces azimuth error δφ, and because measurement is strictly along the right-angle normal to the front line [PAGE 1], the recorded distance is d_recorded = d_true cos δφ, a cosine loss that grows nonlinearly with wind angle.

Veto proof. A legal attempt requires the logical conjunction: physical capacity ∧ anatomical release admissibility ∧ board/space rule compliance ∧ environmental tolerance. Failure in any single layer nullifies the attempt irrespective of the mark achieved; distance is only computed after all vetoes are cleared [PAGE 1].

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Board contact for forward momentum.
Veto condition: “no part of the competitor's person must be on this board” [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: if the athlete were allowed to plant the toe on the top of the four-inch board and drive forward, the added horizontal impulse Δp = m Δv would raise release speed by Δv, increasing vacuum range by ΔR ≈ (∂R/∂v₀) Δv ≈ (2 v₀ sin(2θ)/g) Δv for small Δv.
RLVR prediction and verification: The actual attempt is scored as a foul; no distance is recorded or measured, regardless of how far the shot landed. Verifiable outcome: the official scorecard marks the trial “fault” with no numeric entry, and video review confirms shoe contact with the board top.

Scenario 2 — Two-handed put.
Veto condition: “It must be put with one hand only” [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: a two-handed push increases the effective force–time integral and the stabilized mass of the athlete–implement system, plausibly raising v₀ by 10–15% over a one-hand release for the same athlete.
RLVR prediction and verification: Under the veto, the attempt is void even if the mark exceeds the current best. Verifiable outcome: the field judge observes both hands on the shot at release and disqualifies the put; the measured distance is discarded.

Scenario 3 — Oblique measurement.
Veto condition: “Puts are measured in a line at right angles from the front line of the space” [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: measuring along a diagonal line at angle δ from the normal overestimates the true orthogonal distance by factor 1/cos δ; for δ = 15°, the overstatement is ≈ 3.5%.
RLVR prediction and verification: The measurement is invalidated and must be redone along the perpendicular to the front line or its prolongation [PAGE 1]. Verifiable outcome: a theodolite or laser alignment confirms the measurement tape was not perpendicular; the re-measured distance is shorter than or equal to the original, and the shorter mark stands.
