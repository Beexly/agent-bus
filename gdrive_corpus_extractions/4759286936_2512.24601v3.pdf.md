<THINK_TRACE>
The paper's spine is a single information-flow claim: an LLM's context window is a fixed-size integration surface, and tasks that require integrating more information than the window can hold will degrade, but the *rate* of degradation depends on how much of the prompt must be jointly integrated. Page 1 states the thesis directly — "arbitrarily long user prompts should not be fed into the neural network (e.g., Transformer) directly but should instead be treated as part of the environment" (p.2) — and the mechanism is that the prompt is loaded as a REPL variable `context`, so the root model manipulates it symbolically and delegates semantic reading to bounded sub-calls. The multi-hop reasoning I audit is: (1) if information demand $I(P)$ exceeds window capacity $K$, a single forward pass cannot integrate it; (2) but total work need not be bounded by $K$ because the REPL variable state is external memory whose capacity is decoupled from $K$; (3) therefore the binding constraints migrate from "window size" to "iteration count, per-call output-token ceilings, and cost." This migration is the paper's real contribution, and it is formalized only loosely (the $\Omega(|P|)$ / $\Omega(|P|^2)$ language on p.3, and the $K/c$ iteration bound in footnote 2). The empirical deltas are large: OOLONG-Pairs moves from a base F1 of $\le 0.1\%$ to $58.0\%$ at depth 1 (p.6, Table 1), which is the headline because base models "make little progress" there. The task taxonomy (S-NIAH $O(1)$, OOLONG linear, OOLONG-Pairs quadratic; pp.4–5) is the conceptual key: degradation is governed by *processing complexity per unit length*, not length alone.

Gray-zone audit — where the claims are softer than they read: (a) The compaction baseline is asymmetric — "for the GPT-5 experiments ... we use GPT-5-nano for compaction and GPT-5 to provide the final answer" (p.5), so compaction's GPT-5 numbers are not apples-to-apples with RLM's GPT-5/GPT-5-mini split. (b) The "$1.50–$2.75" cost for a hypothetical GPT-5-mini ingest of 6–11M tokens is an extrapolation, and the paper labels it as such ("linearly extrapolated cost," p.6); RLM's $0.99 is measured. (c) Runtime figures are explicitly caveated: "all LM calls are blocking / sequential" (p.7), so the tail-latency story is implementation-bound. (d) OOLONG-Pairs is "20 synthetically generated tasks" (p.26) — small-N, so the $76.0$ vs $58.0$ depth gap rests on 20 items. (e) An internal unit inconsistency: the 8B prompt says the sub-LM "~32k token limit (approximately ~24k characters)" (p.20) but also "around ~100k chars, roughly 32k tokens" (p.20) — these cannot both be right (~32k tokens ≈ ~100k+ chars at 3–4 chars/token). <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**System constants.** Base model $M$ with context window $K$ tokens; prompt $P\in\Sigma^\*$, length $|P|$. Per the paper: GPT-5 $K\approx 2.72\times10^{5}$ tokens (Fig. 1 caption, p.1), Qwen3-8B $K\approx 3.2\times10^{4}$ (p.20). Information units: $1\text{ token}\approx 4\text{ chars}\approx \log_2|V|$ bits; $6\text{–}11\text{M}$ tokens $\approx 2.4\text{–}4.4\times10^{7}$ chars.

**Root-iteration bound (verbatim from footnote 2, p.3):** "if we trim each turn to $c$ tokens, we will have at most $K/c$ root iterations, each of which can launch arbitrarily many sub-calls." Hence
$$T_{\text{root}}\le \left\lfloor \tfrac{K}{c}\right\rfloor,\qquad S_{\text{total}}=\sum_{i=1}^{T_{\text{root}}} S_i,$$
where $S_i$ (sub-calls in iteration $i$) is *not* bounded by $K$ — the design's escape hatch.

**Sub-call coverage.** A sub-LM integrates up to $w$ tokens/call ($w\approx 500\text{K chars}$, p.18, for GPT-5-mini; $w\approx K$ for same-family sub-calls). One full pass over $P$ needs $\lceil |P|/w\rceil$ calls; a quadratic (pairs) task needs up to $\Theta\!\big((|P|/w)^2\big)$ unless code-side filtering collapses it (which is exactly what Example E.2 does, pp.34–35).

**Complexity classes (pp.4–5).** S-NIAH $\Theta(1)$, OOLONG $\Theta(|P|)$, OOLONG-Pairs $\Theta(|P|^2)$; the paper targets "$\Omega(|P|)$ or $\Omega(|P|^2)$ semantic work" (p.3).

**Analytical extension (mine, NOT stated in the paper) — aggregation-decay model.** If per-item label accuracy is $a\in(0,1)$ and errors were independent, the probability of a fully-correct aggregation over $N$ items is
$$\Pr[\text{correct}]\approx a^{N},\qquad N=\begin{cases}\Theta(1)&\text{S-NIAH}\\[2pt]\Theta(|P|)&\textOOLONG\\[2pt]\Theta(|P|^2)&\text{OOLONG-Pairs.}\end{cases}$$
This qualitatively reproduces the collapse (base GPT-5: OOLONG $44.0$ vs OOLONG-Pairs $0.1$, Table 1, p.6). **Breakdown regime:** independence fails under context rot (one degradation event flips many labels → correlated errors → exponent softer than $N$); and when the RLM offloads aggregation to *code* (deterministic pair enumeration, pp.34–35) the $a^{N}$ bottleneck is replaced by $a^{S}$ where $S\ll N$ is the sub-call count — the architectural claim.

**Cost model.** $C_{\text{RLM}}\approx T_{\text{root}}\,c_{\text{root}} + S_{\text{total}}\,c_{\text{sub}}$. Datum: BrowseComp+ (1K), measured RLM(GPT-5, depth=1) $=\$0.99$ vs extrapolated GPT-5-mini $\$1.50\text{–}\$2.75$ (p.6).

**RLVR hyperparameters (pp.8, 16–17):** train on $32\text{k–}64\text{k}$-token split, 2 needles, 150 steps, batch 128, 4 rollouts/example, $\le 4096$ output tokens/turn, $\le 20$ RLM iterations; eval every 50 steps on $512\text{k–}1\text{M}$ tokens, 8 needles.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The Related-Works map (p.9) traces four lineages converging on RLMs. (1) **Lossy context management / compaction**: Baleen "condensed retrieval" [Khattab et al. 2021], iterative summarization [Wu et al. 2021], ReSum [Wu et al. 2025], Codex CLI compaction [OpenAI 2025b], OpenHands condensation [Smith 2025] — the "summarize-when-full" family the paper argues "presumes that some details ... can safely be forgotten" (p.2). (2) **Explicit memory hierarchies**: MemGPT [Packer et al. 2024], Mem0 [Chhikara et al. 2025], G-Memory [Zhang et al. 2025] — external stores with retrieval policies. (3) **Program-of-thought / sub-LM delegation**: ViperGPT [Surís et al. 2023], THREAD [Schroeder et al. 2025], ReDel [Zhu et al. 2024], Context Folding [Sun et al. 2025], AgentFold [Ye et al. 2025], and DisCIPL [Grand et al. 2025] — the closest ancestors; the paper's differentiator is that DisCIPL "generates programs ... in a single-step and cannot recover from generation mistakes," whereas RLMs use "iterative ... execution feedback from the persistent REPL" (p.9). (4) **Expressive-power theory**: reasoning/CoT adds formal power to vanilla Transformers [Merrill & Sabharwal 2024] (p.2), and bootstrapping reasoning [STaR, Zelikman et al. 2022; QSTaR 2024] motivates treating "RLM trajectories ... as a form of reasoning" (p.9).

**Multi-week traps in this lineage.** (i) *The longer-window trap*: the architecture branch — ALiBi [Press et al. 2022], S4/SSM [Gu et al. 2022], Infini-attention [Munkhdalai et al. 2024] (p.9) — keeps pushing $K$ upward, but the paper's premise is that degradation is task-relative, so a bigger $K$ defers rather than removes the problem for dense tasks. (ii) *The forgetting-is-safe trap*: compaction's implicit assumption is empirically false on dense aggregation, which is the paper's whole reason for existing (p.2). (iii) *The verbalized-delegation trap*: prior self-delegation "verbalize[s] sub-calls autoregressively rather than producing them programmatically" (p.3), capping the number of delegations to what fits in an output stream — RLMs break this by looping in code.
