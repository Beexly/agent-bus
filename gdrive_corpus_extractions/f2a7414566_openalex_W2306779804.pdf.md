<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The source contains no cognitive psychology and no empirical theory of mind. It does, however, contain the exact formal machinery for recursive multi-agent modeling: set-valued composition. If agent \(A\)’s possible responses are \(G:X\rightrightarrows Y\) and agent \(B\)’s possible reactions are \(H:Y\rightrightarrows Z\), then the usual product is \((H\circ G)(x)=\bigcup_{y\in G(x)}H(y)\), while the square product is \((H\square G)(x)=\bigcap_{y\in G(x)}H(y)\) [PAGE 51]. The inverse formulas are also stated: \((H\circ G)^{-1}(y)=G^-(H^{-1}(y))\) and \((H\square G)^{-1}(y)=G^+(H^{-1}(y))\) [PAGE 52]. This is the formal “recursive theory of mind” layer: an agent modeling another agent’s set of possible replies is not composing single-valued functions; it is composing correspondences, and the difference between union and intersection is the difference between “some reply” and “every reply.”

“Crisis habit regression” can be represented as a breakdown of the regularity that licenses equality. In a crisis, a model under time pressure tends to reuse the habitual local linearization \(D(H\circ G)\approx DH\circ DG\). But the source only gives an inclusion in general; equality requires pseudo-convexity, finite-dimensional transversality, or stability conditions [PAGE 54–56]. If those fail, the habitual equality is a regression to an unjustified convex/linear picture. The formal signature is either \(\alpha\ge1\) in the inverse stability condition, or failure of the constraint qualification \(C_{K_1}(x)-C_{K_2}(x)=X\) in intersection formulas [PAGE 22, PAGE 24]. In such a regime, the correct prediction is not “the derivative is the composition”; it is “the composition is only an outer/inner estimate.”

“Counter-trap” is the adversarial use of the square product. If an opponent can choose \(y\in G(x)\) after observing your model, then your usual-product prediction \(\bigcup_{y\in G(x)}H(y)\) may be too permissive or too narrow depending on whether you need a guaranteed outcome or an existential outcome. The square product \(\bigcap_{y\in G(x)}H(y)\) is the guaranteed common reaction; the usual product is the possible union. The source’s inverse formulas make this operational: \(G^-\) uses “intersects” while \(G^+\) uses “is contained in” [PAGE 52]. A counter-trap audit must therefore ask whether the opponent’s choice is existential or universal.

“Clock” is the \(h\to0^+\) limit and the observation schedule. Contingent, adjacent, and Clarke derivatives differ precisely in their sequence quantifiers: contingent uses existence of some \(h_n\to0^+\), adjacent uses all \(h_n\to0^+\), and Clarke uses all \(h_n\to0^+\) and all \(x_n\to_K x\) [PAGE 10]. A fast clock that samples only one subsequence can mistake a contingent direction for an adjacent direction; a slow clock that averages over states can approach a Clarke object and lose contingent information. The recursive model must therefore report which clock and which quantifier it used. The source’s “lim sup inf” machinery for adjacent and circatangent derivatives is exactly a warning that two-variable limits do not commute [PAGE 46–47].
