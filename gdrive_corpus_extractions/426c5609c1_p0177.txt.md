<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under elevated fatigue F(t) > F_c and approaching the Thanksgiving terminal node, a 1915 coaching staff tends to regress to crisis habits: the playbook contracts to the previous season's high-probability calls—mass line plunges, punt-on-third-down conservatism, and wide-end sweeps relying on the heaviest available linemen. In 1915, with limited substitution and minimal protective equipment, regression is biomechanically rational: it reduces decision load and preserves the few freshest bodies. Let H_regress(τ) denote the probability that a coach calls a 1914-style conservative play at game time τ. H_regress increases with dF/dt and with clock pressure C_rem(τ), the time remaining in the contest.

Counter-trap. The opposing coaching staff, observing the same date schedule on [PAGE 177], can compute the opponent's F(t) and anticipate H_regress. The counter-trap is therefore a second-order response: if Coach A expects Coach B to regress to line plunges because B played Saturday, October 16 and again Saturday, October 23 with a depleted line, Coach A stacks the line, blitzes the fullback, and forces a punt or a turnover on downs. This is a recursive theory-of-mind loop: Coach A models Coach B as fatigued; Coach B models Coach A as expecting fatigue and thus prepares a spread or reverse counter to the stacked line. The equilibrium is not a fixed play but a belief distribution over formations that updates after each observed game in the October sequence.

Clock recursion. The 1915 game clock was a piecewise-stopped process C(t) with stoppages σ_k for out-of-bounds, penalties, injuries, and (in some rule interpretations) incomplete passes. A coach's decision at time t depends on C_rem = C_end − C(t) and on the inferred model M_opp(C_rem) of the opponent's clock management. As C_rem → 0 in a close game, crisis habit regression intensifies: the trailing team punts instead of attempting the then-risky forward pass, while the leading team kneels or runs up the middle. The clock thus couples physical fatigue to strategic recursion—short rest (from the October 16–23 cadence) raises F, which raises H_regress under clock pressure, which invites the counter-trap, which further drains F through high-intensity defensive plays. The fixed point of this recursion is a late-October game in which both teams abandon their October playbooks and revert to 1914 muscle memory, precisely when the Thanksgiving terminal node makes such regression most costly.
