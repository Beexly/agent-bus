The white-scrap procedure [PAGE 24] is especially exposed because the scrap is interposed and removed while the eye is fixed; environmental drift between placement and screen removal invalidates the subtraction logic.

Adjudication: The contrast-color is admitted only if Layer 1 (optical fusion + isoluminance), Layer 2 (fixation without stabilization singularity), and Layer 3 (illuminance constancy) all pass. If any layer vetoes, the datum is discarded or reclassified, not averaged.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios derived from [PAGE 24], each with a falsifiable prediction. No betting lines or odds are invoked; all thresholds are empirical decision boundaries to be set by the experimentalist.

Scenario 1 — Black enclosing lines. Counterfactual: Helmholtz claims black circles bordering the gray ring weaken the contrast [PAGE 24]; Hering denies they can eliminate it under favorable conditions [PAGE 24]. Veto: surround the gray ring with high-contrast black annuli and measure the contrast index \(\mathcal{C}\) via hue cancellation or heterochromatic photometry. Prediction: \(\mathcal{C}\) does not collapse to zero; it remains above the measurement noise floor \(\epsilon_{\mathcal{C}}\) (e.g., \(\mathcal{C} > 0.10\), with \(\epsilon_{\mathcal{C}}\) determined by instrument precision), though variance increases. If \(\mathcal{C}\) falls below \(\epsilon_{\mathcal{C}}\), Hering's veto is falsified and Helmholtz's claim is reinstated for that condition.

Scenario 2 — White scrap with careful fixation. Counterfactual: Place the white scrap over the colored field; contrast-color appears initially, but careful fixation makes it diminish rapidly on both scrap and ring [PAGE 24]. Veto: record \(\mathcal{C}(t)\) during maintained fixation without saccades. Prediction: \(\mathcal{C}(t)\) decays approximately exponentially, \(\mathcal{C}(t) = \mathcal{C}_0 e^{-t/\tau_{\mathrm{fix}}}\) with \(\tau_{\mathrm{fix}} < 10\,\mathrm{s}\), and recovers within \(\sim 2\,\mathrm{s}\) after a saccade or brief blanking. If contrast remains constant during fixation, the adaptation counter-trap is absent and Hering's account fails for this observer/stimulus.

Scenario 3 — Saturation–luminance trade-off. Counterfactual: Increase saturation of the green field to strengthen contrast while avoiding brightness-contrast disturbance; this requires a darker gray field, but darker fields reduce contrast-color visibility [PAGE 24]. Veto: map \(\mathcal{C}\) over a two-dimensional design of field chroma \(c\) and gray-ring luminance \(L_g\). Prediction: the ridge of maximal \(\mathcal{C}\) shifts to lower \(L_g\) as \(c\) increases, following a von Kries-type gain control rather than a monotonic improvement in \(\mathcal{C}\). If the ridge moves to higher \(L_g\) with chroma, Hering's saturation–darkness interaction is falsified.
