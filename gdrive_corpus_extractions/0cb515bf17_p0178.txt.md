Hull's Corollary IV claims an asymptote "appreciably above zero in the case of certain receptors" [PAGE 1]. If the physical layer saturates at maximum receptor discharge, further increases in E_CS cannot raise ∂H/∂t; Corollary III's ∂g/∂E_CS > 0 hits a hard ceiling. This veto is binding whenever E_CS approaches or exceeds the receptor's dynamic range, a regime not specified on [PAGE 1].

Biomechanical layer veto: the new section opening on [PAGE 1], "HABIT STRENGTH AS A FUNCTION OF THE DURATION OF THE STIMULUS TRACE AT THE TIME OF ACTION OCCURRENCE," makes action occurrence the critical deadline. Even if neural habit strength H is positive, the musculoskeletal effector has a minimum conduction and contraction latency. If biomechanical action latency exceeds the stimulus-trace duration, the habit cannot be expressed at the moment of action, voiding the molar prediction regardless of H magnitude. This layer vetoes the acquisition equations when the trace decays before the response can be executed.

Weather/environmental layer veto: Hull's equations assume controlled application of conditioned-stimulus energy [PAGE 1]. Ambient sensory noise degrades effective E_CS at the receptor and adds jitter to Δt, shifting the system out of the "optimal temporal relationships" precondition [PAGE 1]. In field settings, environmental degradation effectively reduces E_CS below E_min or decorrelates CS and US onset, invoking Corollary IV or V asymptotes even when the trainer intends optimal timing. This veto is external to Hull's formalism but binding in any non-laboratory deployment.

Adjudication rule: a prediction from [PAGE 1] is accepted only if all three layers simultaneously permit it. If the physical layer saturates, the biomechanical latency exceeds trace duration, or environmental noise breaks temporal contiguity, the equation is vetoed and the observed behavior will asymptote toward the lower bound dictated by the binding layer, not by Hull's corollary.
