When s_i and s are both large positive (confident detection), σ(-s) ≈ e^{-s}, so the gradient vanishes exponentially — the likelihood is flat at the peak, and the particle weights are dominated by numerical noise. **Hop 1 — from the detector to the risk.** The paper defines the proposal scorer as \(P(x\mid z_t;w)=(1+e^{-w^\top\phi_t(x)})^{-1}\) [PAGE 4], i.e. a logistic log-odds model with \(w,\phi_t(x)\in\mathbb{R}^d\). The negative log-likelihood of a label \(y\in\{-1,+1\}\) is exactly \(\ell_t(x,y,w)=\log(1+\exp(-y\,w^\top\phi_t(x)))\) [PAGE 4], measured in nats because the logarithm is natural. So minimizing \(\ell_t\) is maximum-likelihood estimation, not an ad hoc surrogate. That much is standard; the interesting derivation begins with the coupling.

**Hop 2 — the multi-task objective is not a hierarchical posterior.** Equation (1) minimizes \(L_t+\lambda\Omega_t\) with \(\Omega_t(W)=\frac{1}{2N_t}\sum_i\|w_i-\bar w^{(t-1)}\|_2^2\) [PAGE 5, Eq. 2]. A proper Bayesian reading would place a prior \(w_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\bar w,\lambda^{-1}I)\) and give \(\bar w\) its own hyperprior. The consequence is that \(\lambda\) is fixed while the effective prior variance shrinks as the \(w_i\) converge, so the penalty becomes relatively stronger over time — a second, unannounced drift-limiting mechanism.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Habit.** The tracker’s habit is the warm-start plus mean penalty: every new instance model begins at the category model and is pulled back toward \(\bar w\). This is a prior, and priors are habits.

**Crisis.** The crisis is an appearance discontinuity — occlusion, lighting change, a hard turn — where the instance evidence is briefly inconsistent with both the motion prediction and the category prior.

**Regression.** Under crisis the instance model has few samples \(n_i\), so the gradient \(O(1/n_i)\) in Eq. (2) cannot overcome the penalty \(\lambda\|w_i-\bar w\|^2\). The model regresses to \(\bar w\), i.e. the tracker re-detects a *generic* car rather than *the* car. That is precisely an identity switch, and the paper reports “404” IDS and “1304” FRG on KITTI [PAGE 8] and admits “our tracks contain many ID switches and are generally fragmented” [PAGE 10].

**Counter-trap.** The designer’s natural response is to raise \(\lambda\) to suppress drift, which accelerates regression; or lower \(\lambda\) to force specialization, which lets the model latch onto background or a distractor. The paper’s own framing of the trade-off — “our multi-task objective tends to favor conservative updates to prevent drift” [PAGE 9] — is a confession that the counter-trap has no interior optimum without a schedule.

**Clock.** The only mechanisms that move the balance over time are the two cooling schedules: \(\alpha_t=N_t/(\bar N_{t-1}+N_t)\to 0\) and \(\sigma_0/k\to 0\). Both are monotone conservative. For a non-stationary crisis, a monotone conservative clock is the wrong direction: the system becomes *less* able to adapt exactly when adaptation is most needed.

**Recursive model of the other.** The algorithm models “what would the category detector say” and “what would the instance detector say,” but it does not model other targets. Inter-target reasoning is a post-hoc merge rule: “the tracker with the lower score is terminated” [PAGE 7]. In a crowd, the lower-score track is often the partially occluded true target, so the rule systematically deletes the harder instance — another route to ID switches.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto is a physically or statistically grounded condition under which the paper’s causal claims cannot hold, independent of hyperparameter tuning.

**Optical/physical layer.** The velocity is a median of DeepFlow vectors [PAGE 6]. Under motion blur, rain, low sun, or textureless road/sky, the flow field is corrupted; the median then reports background motion, and the linear extrapolation \(x_t=x_{t-1}+v\Delta t\) steps off the target within one to two frames. Veto condition: mean endpoint error of the flow inside the box exceeds half the box diagonal. No such diagnostic is reported.

**Sensor layer.** The state is a monocular image-space box \((x,y,w,h)\), so “velocity” is pixels/frame, not m/s. Without calibration there is scale ambiguity: a small near car and a large far car can produce similar box sizes and similar image velocities. Any physical interpretation (braking, lane change) is a category error; the model is an image-kinematic model only.

**Kinematic/biomechanical layer.** Road vehicles are non-holonomic with yaw rate and lateral acceleration. The first-order flow extrapolation has no yaw state, so a hard turn produces a curved trajectory that a straight-line prediction misses. Veto condition: on KITTI sequences containing turns above a threshold yaw rate, predicted-box IoU with ground truth falls below the detection threshold before the appearance model can recover.

**Weather/lighting layer.** The paper’s own Figure 2 caption notes “striking differences in visual appearance between the two datasets” [PAGE 9]. A single-Gaussian Fisher vector has one mean and one covariance; a lighting shift moves the mean, and because the category model is an equal-weight running average with memory \(\Theta(t)\), it cannot track the shift. Veto condition: a monotone illumination ramp causes recall to decay monotonically rather than recover.

**Statistical layer.** The headline “ranks third” [PAGE 8] is vetoed as a controlled claim by the offline/online mixture and by the small P2K margins: ODAMOT vs CIT is 23.6 vs 22.8 MOTA (R-CNN) and 19.7 vs 18.2 (mini-FV) [PAGE 9], with only 21 target videos. With sequence-level bootstrap, a 0.8-point MOTA difference is unlikely to exclude zero. Until a confidence interval is reported, the causal claim “our multi-task online adaptation allows to gradually discover and track more targets” [PAGE 9] is not adjudicated.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
