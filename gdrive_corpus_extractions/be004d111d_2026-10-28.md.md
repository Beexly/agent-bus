Physical layer. Pitch-control and space-ownership models assume isotropic, continuous player motion. The TTA quantile regression paper demonstrates that human sports motion is player-specific and anisotropic, with an MLP outperforming six physics models on PFF FC World Cup 2022 data [Roundup §1, row: “Learning Human Sports Motion Feasibility…”]. Veto condition: if tracking data lack inertial measurement or player mass profiles, the time-to-arrival lower quantile is physically unidentifiable near set pieces and tight defensive pressure, because acceleration limits vary by athlete. The model is singular wherever the physics assumption of constant velocity conflicts with measured foot-plant dynamics.

Biomechanical layer. The roundup explicitly skipped a throw-distance pose-modelling paper (2610.16383) as outside sports analytics proper, but its existence signals a boundary: pose-only estimation omits ground-reaction forces and joint loading [Roundup, Seen and skipped]. Veto condition: any injury-risk or workload model built from pose estimation without force-plate or instrumented-insole validation is biomechanically inadmissible for load management; the absence of force covariates makes the model blind to non-contact injury mechanisms.

Weather / environmental layer. The TTA training data come from PFF FC World Cup 2022, held in climate-controlled stadia [Roundup §1, row: “Learning Human Sports Motion Feasibility…”]. No wind, precipitation, or turf-temperature covariates appear in the roundup. Veto condition: deploying that quantile surface to open-air venues (MLS, Premier League, NFL outdoor stadia) without recalibrating \(\tau\) for ball drag and player thermoregulation violates portability [Roundup §1, row: “From Metrics to Decisions…”]. The breakdown regime is low-tail \(\tau\) under headwind: drag increases time-to-arrival nonlinearly, so lower-quantile estimates become overconfident.

Software supply-chain layer. Python 3.10 remains pinned to Pillow 12.3.0 with open advisories [Roundup §2]. Veto condition: running sdv-py v0.2.0b1 or image-based Grad-CAM audits on Python 3.10 exposes the pipeline to CVE-mediated image-decoding failures; the hard break is upgrading to Python 3.11+ before processing broadcast frames.
