Berkeley's treatises on vision held that distance is not seen directly but suggested by prior tactile-motor associations; Kant's transcendental aesthetic made space an a priori form of intuition rather than a retinal sensation; Helmholtz synthesized these by treating perception as unconscious inference from ambiguous cues weighted by past experience. Loeb contributes the specific biomechanical mechanism—muscular adaptation for near and far distance—as a determinant of relief form [PAGE 257]. James, writing in 1890, occupies the middle position: he will not "throw out retinal sensibility altogether as a space-yielding function" merely because ambiguity appears, because "the former way is cheap, wholesale, shallow; the latter difficult and complicated, but full of instruction in the end" [PAGE 257].

Multi-week traps: (1) Perceptual rivalry dominance durations are non-stationary; repeated daily exposure over weeks induces neural adaptation and shifts the switch-rate distribution, so a single-session flip rate cannot be extrapolated to multi-week baselines without modeling consolidation. (2) Accommodative tone and vergence plasticity exhibit slow day-to-day drift (ciliary fatigue, vergence adaptation), so a subject tested on day 1 versus day 14 may show different relief dominance even with identical retinal input, violating the assumption that [PAGE 257]'s "fixed retinal impression" remains fixed across weeks. (3) The voluntary attention trick James describes—"attend to one of the angles" and imagine solid versus hollow [PAGE 257]—can consolidate into a demand-characteristic habit: over weeks, subjects report reversals that reflect compliance with the experimental instruction rather than image-driven dynamics, creating a hysteresis trap in which the percept is controlled by the subject's model of what the experimenter wants.

Crisis habit regression: under ambiguous, fixed retinal input [PAGE 257], the observer regresses to the dominant first-order prior (for example, convex-over-concave, the hollow-face bias). The first-order model is "the world is solid"; when the crisis of ambiguity strikes, the system retreats to this habit instead of computing the percept from the image. Counter-trap: James's instruction to attend to an angle and imagine it solid or hollow [PAGE 257] is a metacognitive intervention, but repeated use converts it into a second-order habit: the subject expects to reverse at will, and reported flips may reflect compliance with that self-model rather than perceptual necessity. Clock: reversal timing supplies an objective clock. Let dwell time T_dwell follow hazard λ(t) = λ_0 exp(−t/τ_adapt) + λ_att, with τ_adapt in seconds (neural adaptation) and λ_att an attentional-switch term. Recursive theory of mind operates at three orders: first order p(h | r); second order p(h | r, 𝒜(α)), where 𝒜(α) is the attended angle; third order p(𝒜 | experimenter demand). Under crisis, the first-order prior dominates; under the counter-trap, the second-order attentional term dominates; the clock—measured by switch-interval distributions—reveals which layer is in control. Prediction: when demand characteristics are strong, switch intervals shorten and become bimodal (forced reversals), decoupling the observed clock from the biological τ_adapt.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/retinal layer: r is held constant while the percept flips [PAGE 257]. Veto proof: if spatial content were determined by retinal sensation alone, constant r would entail constant percept; observed flips under invariant optical conditions falsify pure retinal sufficiency. Biomechanical/oculomotor layer: accommodation A(t) in diopters (m^{-1}) and vergence V(d) in radians [PAGE 257, Loeb footnote]. Veto proof: pharmacological paralysis of accommodation or extraocular-muscle paralysis removes the near/far muscular cue; if relief perception collapses or its flip frequency changes, the retinal image is insufficient and oculomotor signals are necessary co-determinants of relief. Weather/ambient-optics layer: varying illumination E in lux (cd·sr·m^{-2}) and atmospheric scattering change the luminance L reaching the retina while perceived object form remains constant (perceptual constancy). Veto proof: because the percept is invariant across illumination changes that alter r, the percept cannot be a direct readout of L; it must be an inference invariant to the weather/optics layer. Adjudication: the consistent verdict across all three layers is that space perception is a multi-layer inference (retinal likelihood × oculomotor prior × environmental invariance), not a simple sensation. James's refusal of the "cheap, wholesale, shallow" denial of spatial sensation [PAGE 257] is vindicated: retinal sensibility is necessary but not sufficient, and relief depends on conditions beyond the retinal image itself.
