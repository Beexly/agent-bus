**Eigenvalue-assignment operator (Ackermann).** $K=(p_1-a_1,\dots,p_n-a_n)\,\tilde W_r W_r^{-1}$ (6.21, [PAGE 192]); reference gain $k_r=-1/\big(C(A-BK)^{-1}B\big)$ (6.13, [PAGE 188]) — dimensionless only if $CB$ carries units making $C(A-BK)^{-1}B$ reciprocal to $u$.

**LQR.** $u=-Q_u^{-1}B^TPx$, with $P$ solving $PA+A^TP-PBQ_u^{-1}B^TP+Q_x=0$ (6.27, [PAGE 203]). **Breakdown:** no $P>0$ if $Q_x\ge0$ fails or $(A,Q_x)$ unobservable.

**State evolution (convolution).** $x(t)=e^{At}x(0)+\int_0^t e^{A(t-\tau)}Bu(\tau)\,d\tau$ (5.14, [PAGE 157]); $y(t)=Ce^{At}x(0)+\int_0^t Ce^{A(t-\tau)}Bu\,d\tau+Du$ (5.15). Impulse response $h(t)=Ce^{At}B$ (5.18, [PAGE 148]). Stability iff $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i(A)<0$ (Thm 4.1, [PAGE 118]).

**Lyapunov operator.** $V=x^TPx,\ P>0$; $\dot V=-x^TQx$ via $A^TP+PA=-Q$ (4.14, [PAGE 126]).

**Finite escape / non-uniqueness (singularity regimes).** $\dot x=x^2,\ x(0)=1\Rightarrow x(t)=1/(1-t)$, singular at $t=1$ [PAGE 109]; $\dot x=2\sqrt{x},\ x(0)=0$ has infinitely many solutions $x=(t-a)^2\mathbf 1_{t>a}$ [PAGE 109–110]. Lipschitz failure: $\partial F/\partial x\to\infty$ at origin breaks uniqueness [PAGE 110].

**Model validity bound.** Uncertainty lemon [GPD59], Fig 2.15b [PAGE 63]: "rare to find models that are accurate for signal ranges larger than $10^4$" [PAGE 62] — a hard amplitude×frequency breakdown envelope.

**Feedback linearization.** $u=B^{-1}(q)\big(M(q)v-C(q,\dot q)\big)$ (5.39, [PAGE 175]); **breakdown** if $p\neq n$ or $B(q)$ singular — cancellation impossible.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
**Scientific lineage (as recorded in-source).** Watt's centrifugal flyball governor as the industrial-revolution enabler [PAGE 14]; Black's negative-feedback amplifier (1927) trading gain for robustness [PAGE 29, 178]; the Nyquist criterion as the frequency-domain stability engine (Ch 9, [PAGE 6]); Kalman's state-space + reachability/observability program (1960–61, [PAGE 209]) and the quadratic-loss state feedback that became LQR; Wiener's cybernetics (1948) and Tsien (1954) [PAGE 23]; Hodgkin–Huxley voltage clamp (1963 Nobel) [PAGE 22–23]; Forrester's supply-chain loops (1961) [PAGE 27]. The chapter's own genealogy places mechanics (state-space) and electrical engineering (input/output) as two streams that "gradually merged into... the state space representation of input/output systems" [PAGE 43].

**Multi-week traps (pedagogical breakdown regimes).** (1) *Controllability vs reachability*: "in most textbooks the term 'controllability' is used instead of 'reachability,' but we prefer the latter term because it is more descriptive" [PAGE 209] — Kalman distinguished reaching the origin from reaching an arbitrary state; conflating them costs weeks. (2) *Stabilization vs tracking*: $k_r$ sets steady-state value by precise calibration [PAGE 192], whereas integral action achieves it structurally — confusing the two leads to brittle designs. (3) *Linearization is only defined at an equilibrium*: attempting to linearize a polynomial $\dot x=a_0+a_1x+\dots+u$ at the origin with $a_0\neq0$ produces a model that is both wrong and immediately vacated by the trajectory [PAGE 172]. (4) *Canonical-form conditioning* [PAGE 187] as above.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Nearest rigorous referent: the **reactive-vs-anticipatory duality and loop delay**, not adversary psychology. The book's axiom — "Feedback is reactive: there must be an error before corrective actions are taken" [PAGE 34] — is the "habit": a pure error-driven regulator. The "clock" is loop delay/lag. The thermostat must "do a bit of anticipation, turning the heater off before the error actually changes sign" to avoid cycling [PAGE 17–18] — i.e., the reactive habit must be augmented by prediction. The formal counter-habit is the derivative term as a linear predictor $e(t+T_d)\approx e(t)+T_d\,\dot e(t)$ [PAGE 36], and feedforward that acts *before* the disturbance enters [PAGE 34]. **Regression-to-instability under delay** is explicit in the congestion model, where window dynamics are evaluated at $t-\tau_i$ and drop probability carries a backward delay $\tau_{li}^b$ [PAGE 91–92]: increase loop gain or delay and the reactive habit overshoots into oscillation (TCP sawtooth / limit cycle). So the "counter-trap vs clock" content is genuine: reactive control + delay + high gain $\Rightarrow$ limit cycle; anticipation (derivative/feedforward) or reduced gain restores stability — and integral action only helps if a steady state exists at all ("there may not always be a steady state because the system may be oscillating," [PAGE 36]).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Reinterpreted as **physical/actuator/model-validity/environmental vetoes that override nominal design**. (1) *Actuator-magnitude veto*: a design that is theorem-valid (arbitrary $\lambda$ placement) can be physically infeasible — the balance controller demanding ~3× gravity in input [PAGE 201], with the explicit rule that actuator capability constrains achievable eigenvalues [PAGE 200]. (2) *Actuator-rate / bandwidth veto*: cruise-control torque is bounded $0\le u\le1$ and the model is "not valid for very rapid changes of the throttle" [PAGE 68]; achievable rise time is capped by initial control authority $u(0)=k_r=\omega_c^2 r$ [PAGE 190]. (3) *Model-validity veto (uncertainty lemon)*: any design is only licensed inside the amplitude×frequency envelope of Fig 2.15b [PAGE 63]; beyond $10^4$ signal range, or at frequencies near unmodeled modes, the model — hence the controller synthesis — is void. (4) *Fundamental-limit veto*: Bode's relations and "fundamental limits of performance and robustness" (Ch 11.5, [PAGE 10]) impose performance bounds no $K$ can beat. (5) *Environmental-disturbance veto* (the "weather" analog): road grade $mg\sin\theta$ in cruise control [PAGE 78], wind in insect flight entering via drag [PAGE 46], packet loss in networks — all handled by feedback/feedforward but bounded by the above physical limits.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three **verifiable** claims (provable from the source, with definite predicted outcomes):

1. **Finite-escape-time blow-up.** System $\dot x=x^2,\ x(0)=1$. *Prediction:* every convergent integrator outputs $x(t)=1/(1-t)\to+\infty$ as $t\to1^-$; no control law keeps it bounded past $t=1$ without changing the vector field [PAGE 109]. *Verification:* closed form exists; substituting confirms. **Veto:** any "settling-time" spec is meaningless here — the breakdown regime is at $t=1$, not $\infty$.

2. **Pole placement is impossible on a non-reachable system.** Two identical pendula on one cart driven by one force [PAGE 183–184] give $\operatorname{rank}W_r<n$. *Prediction:* for any $K$, $\det(sI-A+BK)$ cannot be assigned arbitrarily; the invariant subspace $H$ with $Hx=\text{const}$ cannot be moved [PAGE 184]. *Verification:* construct $H$ in the left null space of $A$ and $B$; then $HW_r=0$. **Veto:** a claim of "arbitrary eigenvalue assignment" is false unless reachability is first proven.

3. **Integral action gives zero steady-state error only if the closed loop is stable.** *Prediction:* augmenting with $\dot z=y-r$ (6.28, [PAGE 195]) forces $\dot z=0\Rightarrow y=r$ *at equilibrium* — but only exists if $A-BK$ is Hurwitz; if the loop is unstable or oscillatory, no steady state exists and the zero-error guarantee is void [PAGE 36, 195]. *Verification:* at equilibrium $0=y-r$ algebraically; existence of the equilibrium requires stability. **Veto:** "integral action ⇒ zero error" is conditionally true, not absolute.

(Bonus, feedback linearization): exact cancellation via $u=B^{-1}(q)(M(q)v-C(q,\dot q))$ is verifiable only when $B(q)$ is square-invertible ($p=n$) [PAGE 175]; otherwise the residual nonlinearity is uncancelable — a structural veto.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
