<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of this experiment traces back to the foundational debates of early 20th-century psychology. It stands in direct opposition to Watsonian behaviorism, which posited that behavior was solely determined by stimulus-response bonds strengthened through repetition. Tolman, influenced by Gestalt principles (Wertheimer, Köhler), introduced the idea that behavior is mediated by internal expectations and goals.

Historically, this 1932 experiment served as a precursor to the "Latent Learning" experiments (often cited with the rat maze studies), proving that learning can occur without immediate reinforcement. By showing that subjects did not "learn" the misspelling despite frequent practice, Tolman argued retrocausally that the *meaning* of the action determines its impact on future behavior.

**Multi-week Traps in Behavioral Studies:**
A common trap in early conditioning studies was the assumption that any repeated behavior automatically forms a habit. Tolman exposed this trap: if a student practices "recieved" ten times, a behaviorist might predict they will eventually spell it that way automatically. However, Tolman's genealogy shows that unless the *meaning* is altered (i.e., the student actually believes it is correct), the cognitive map remains intact. This insight prevents modern AI training loops from simply overwriting weights with noisy data; it emphasizes the need for "ground truth" alignment alongside frequency.
