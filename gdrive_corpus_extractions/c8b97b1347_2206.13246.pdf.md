\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{|V_k|}\sum_{i\in V_k}\bigl|y_i - \hat{y}_i\bigr|\quad [\text{k€}].
\]
These are the only validation metrics; no probability calibration or scoring-rule metrics are reported [PAGE 13].

LightGBM additive ensemble at iteration $t$ [PAGE 4–5, citing Ke et al. (2017)]:
\[
F_t(\mathbf{x}) = F_{t-1}(\mathbf{x}) + \eta\, h_t(\mathbf{x}),
\]
with leaf-wise growth selecting the split that maximizes loss reduction, regularized by $\lambda_{\text{L2}}$ (lambda l2) and $\text{min\_split\_gain}$ [PAGE 14]. Categorical features are grouped internally without one-hot expansion using the Fisher (1958) homogeneity criterion [PAGE 4].

TPE Bayesian optimization [PAGE 5–6, citing Bergstra et al. (2011)] models the generative density of hyperparameters $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ conditioned on the objective:
\[
p(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mid y < y^*) \equiv l(\boldsymbol{\lambda}),\qquad
p(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mid y \ge y^*) \equiv g(\boldsymbol{\lambda}),
\]
optimizing expected improvement over the incumbent threshold $y^*$. The search space for LightGBM includes learning rate $[0,1]$ and $n_{\text{estimators}} \in [50,3000]$ [PAGE 13–14].

SHAP additive attribution [PAGE 14–15] for feature $j$:
\[
\phi_j = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{j\}}
\frac{|S|!\,(|N|-|S|-1)!}{|N|!}\,
\bigl[f(S \cup \{j\}) - f(S)\bigr],
\]
where $\phi_j$ is expressed in k€ units because the model output is market value.

Calculated ability attributes from Table 3 [PAGE 7], all dimensionless ratings on $[1,99]$ except aggregates:
\[
\text{PAC} = \frac{\text{Sprint Speed} + \text{Acceleration}}{2},\quad
\text{SHO} = \frac{\text{Finishing} + \text{Long Shots} + \text{Shot Power}}{3},
\]
\[
\text{Total stats} = \sum_{m=1}^{35} a_m \in [39,3500],\qquad
\text{Growth} = \text{Potential} - \text{Overall Rating}.
\]
Potential satisfies $\text{Potential} \ge \text{Overall Rating}$ with mean gap $5$ points and range $[0,23]$ [PAGE 7].

Singularity/breakdown regimes: (i) Leaf-wise LightGBM overfits when leaf sample counts fall below the minimum child sample threshold, producing high-variance leaves for rare positions such as goalkeeper [PAGE 14]. (ii) TPE degrades when the objective is non-stationary across the monthly SOFIFA update cadence [PAGE 2]. (iii) RMSE is unstable because $\sigma_y > \bar{y}$, so the metric is dominated by the upper tail $[10^4, 10^5]$ k€ [PAGE 8].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage runs from classical regularization to modern gradient boosting: Tibshirani's lasso (1996) [PAGE 3,16], Welling's kernel ridge regression notes (2013) [PAGE 3,16], and Zou & Hastie's elastic net (2005) [PAGE 3,16] form the linear baseline family. Friedman's gradient boosting machine (2001) [PAGE 4,17] establishes GBDT; Ke et al.'s LightGBM (2017) [PAGE 4,17] introduces leaf-wise growth and histogram-based split finding; Fisher's (1958) grouping method underpins LightGBM's native categorical handling [PAGE 4,17]. Bayesian hyperparameter optimization enters via Bergstra et al.'s TPE (2011) [PAGE 5–6,17] and is operationalized through Optuna (Akiba et al., 2019) [PAGE 5–6,16], which the authors prefer over Hyperopt for pruning and search-space expressiveness [PAGE 5–6].

Domain-specific antecedents reveal the traps. He et al. (2015), Müller et al. (2017), and Majewski et al. (2016) relied on Transfer Market, WhoScored, and weak linear models [PAGE 1–2,4,16]. Ćwiklinski et al. (2021) mixed WhoScored, TransferMarket, and Sofascore with Random Forest, Naive Bayes, and AdaBoost but omitted SOFIFA ability depth [PAGE 2–4,16]. Yigit et al. (2019, 2020) used Football Manager attributes with linear and XGBoost baselines [PAGE 2–3,16]. Game-data studies—Prasetio (2016), Pariath (2018), Rajesh (2020), Soto-Valero (2017), and Behravan & Razavi (2020)—used SOFIFA alone, with Behravan & Razavi applying PSO/GWO/IPO/WOA-tuned SVRs across 55 attributes for position-specific subsets [PAGE 2–5,16].

The multi-week trap is temporal: SOFIFA attributes are refreshed monthly based on actual competition performance [PAGE 2], while the training target is a static 2022 snapshot merged with a 2021–2022 season table [PAGE 6]. A model trained in week $t$ and deployed in week $t+4$ faces attribute drift because pace, shooting, and overall ratings are re-evaluated weekly by EA producers and the 6,000-person reviewer community [PAGE 2]. Furthermore, "potential" and "growth" are season-relative constructs: potential incorporates current-season age, international reputation, and game history, and is bounded below by overall rating [PAGE 7]. If a player suffers a red-card suspension or injury mid-season, the WhoScored violation attributes (yellow card range 1–76, red card 1–38) [PAGE 8, Table 4] change abruptly, but the SOFIFA overall rating may lag by one monthly update cycle, creating a stale-feature trap that the cross-validation design does not explicitly guard against.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes the club's reflex under transfer-market pressure: when fees become "astronomical" [PAGE 1], decision-makers revert to familiar, interpretable linear tools—multiple linear regression, lasso, ridge, elastic net—because they offer coefficient stability under scrutiny [PAGE 3]. The paper identifies this as a performance trap: on average, LightGBM is 3.8 times better in RMSE and 6.6 times better in MAE than the linear and regularization regression baselines, and hyperparameter optimization does not rescue the linear family; in some cases HPO worsens lasso, E-Net, and KRR [PAGE 14].

The counter-trap is the authors' optimized LightGBM + TPE pipeline. By searching boosting type, feature fraction, bagging fraction, lambda l2, and min split gain [PAGE 14], the model escapes the linear habit and captures non-linear interactions among Overall, Release Clause, Age, and BOV [PAGE 14–15]. The counter-trap succeeds empirically: I-TPE LightGBM reaches RMSE 609.42 and MAE 211.17 k€ versus the LM baseline of RMSE 2,334.65 and MAE 1,467.45 k€ [PAGE 13–14, Table 6]. Yet the counter-trap contains its own regression risk: SHAP ranks "Release Clause" second despite its 0.96 correlation with the target [PAGE 15], meaning the model may be regressing to a contractual proxy rather than learning footballing causality.

The clock is the depreciation axis. Age enters as a top-four SHAP feature [PAGE 14–15], and the Potential−Overall Rating gap ("growth") averages 5 points with range 0–23 [PAGE 7], encoding the expectation that younger players appreciate while older players depreciate. Because SOFIFA updates monthly [PAGE 2], the model's predictions are valid only within a narrow temporal window around the 2021–2022 season snapshot [PAGE 6]; beyond that window, age advances, potential decays, and the 72-attribute feature vector drifts out of distribution. A club that treats the trained model as static commits a clock error: it prices a 22-year-old using a 2022 potential curve while the player's actual match record (goals, assists, cards) evolves weekly in WhoScored [PAGE 8].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: height (converted to cm), weight (converted to kg), and BMI are ratio-scale demographic attributes [PAGE 8, Table 4]. A veto fires if BMI lies outside physiological bounds for a given position—e.g., a center back with BMI inconsistent with the defending/physical attribute cluster [PAGE 7, Table 3]—because the training distribution covers only players from top European clubs [PAGE 6].

Biomechanical layer: PAC is defined as (Sprint Speed + Acceleration)/2 on [1,99] [PAGE 7, Table 3], and movement aggregates acceleration, agility, sprint speed, reactions, and balance [PAGE 7]. A veto fires when pace-related attributes are missing for wide positions (LW/RW/LB/RB) [PAGE 11, Table 5], since leaf-wise splitting in LightGBM [PAGE 4–5] cannot impute speed from shooting or passing statistics without violating the causal order of physical attributes.

Weather/exogenous layer: The dataset contains no weather, pitch-condition, or atmospheric variables [PAGE 6–8, Tables 3–5]. Therefore the model is vetoed for any prediction contingent on match-day weather (rain, heat, cold) because no feature in the 72-attribute inventory [PAGE 8] or the 124-feature SHAP space [PAGE 14] encodes it. Goal difference, win/draw/lose counts, and team standing (ordinal 1–20) capture club context [PAGE 8, Table 4] but are season-aggregated, not match-day-specific, so they cannot adjudicate a single fixture's weather effect.

Positional layer: 27 positions across attacker, midfielder, defender, and goalkeeper categories [PAGE 11, Table 5] are dummy-encoded [PAGE 7–8]. A veto fires for any player whose best position and suitable positions are mutually inconsistent with the calculated ability attributes—e.g., a goalkeeper assigned non-zero attacking/skill movement stats—because the Overall Rating and BOV are position-weighted averages plus international reputation [PAGE 7, Table 4]. Cross-layer adjudication: if the physical (BMI/height/weight), biomechanical (PAC/DEF/PHY), and positional layers all pass, the LightGBM prediction proceeds; otherwise the prediction is abstained rather than extrapolated.

This tests whether the model is learning football value or regressing to a contractual price signal. Verifiability: retrain I-TPE LightGBM with release clause excluded; observe MAE shift on the held-out 20% test partition [PAGE 13].
