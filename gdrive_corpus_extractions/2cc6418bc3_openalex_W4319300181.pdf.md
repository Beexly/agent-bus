Its two documented patches for non-overlap are GIoU [24] and DIoU [37], both from the box-regression-loss literature; the paper reports (Table IV) that GIoU (77.1) and DIoU (75.1) only marginally beat plain IoU (76.6) on DanceTrack-val, while BIoU (80.0) jumps ahead — the source of the claim "BIoU may generate better results under the same conditions" (PAGE 3).

Lineage of the tracker. SORT [5] introduced tracking-by-detection with Kalman-filter motion; DeepSORT [32] added appearance; ByteTrack [35] added low-score second-stage association; OC-SORT [7] added observation-centric re-update and IoU/GIoU/DIoU matching. C-BIoU inherits SORT's track management (PAGE 3) but swaps Kalman for telescoped finite-difference motion and swaps IoU for buffered IoU with cascaded small→large buffers.

Trap the lineage sets up. The field's reflex after OC-SORT was to keep enriching the appearance/motion stack. On DanceTrack-style data (similar appearance, irregular motion) that reflex is a multi-week trap: appearance is uninformative (AssA for App-only is 43.2, Table III) and heavier motion models lag the fast motion. C-BIoU's counter-move — cheap motion + buffered geometry — is a deliberate simplification, corroborated by its FPS lead (680 vs OC-SORT 341 on DanceTrack, Table II).
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Framed for an algorithm rather than a human opponent: model the adversary as detection noise, which "reasons" against the matcher by manufacturing either non-overlap (false negatives) or spurious overlap (false positives).

Crisis-habit regression (the default failure reflex): under noise the community default is to trust the more expressive model — here, the larger buffer b₂. Regression analysis of Table V shows this is exactly wrong at high noise: C-BIoU AssA collapses to 14.7 at 40% noise because the (1+2b)² union inflation lets false positives dominate.

Counter-trap: the cascaded small→large buffer is the paper's own hedge — resolve confident, near, high-IoU pairs first with b₁ (0.3) so that hard cases never see the large buffer unless truly unmatched. This is ByteTrack's "confident-first" logic repurposed as an over-expansion guard.

Clock: the motion window n and horizon Δ are the temporal controls. Small n reacts fast to turns (good) but amplifies finite-difference noise; the telescoped form s^{t+Δ}=s^t+(Δ/n)(o^t−o^{t−n}) shows the estimate hinges on two endpoints, so a single jittery detection at t−n or t propagates linearly with Δ. The buffer absorbs this only while |error| ≲ b·scale.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/mathematical veto proof for the geometric layer. Claim: BIoU strictly dominates IoU in matching value within its gap-bridging radius.

Proof sketch. (1) For any pair with IoU=0 and max(g_x/(w_A+w_B), g_y/(h_A+h_B)) ≤ b, BIoU>0 while IoU=0 ⇒ strict lift. (2) For identical boxes IoU=BIoU=1 (buffer preserves overlap of coincident boxes), so no loss at the top. (3) The only degradation regime is over-expansion in ambiguous scenes, which the cascade structurally suppresses for the easy majority of pairs. Hence within b ≤ b* (the tuned optimum, e.g. [0.3,0.4] on DanceTrack-val, Fig. 6) BIoU ≥ IoU in expected association accuracy — consistent with Table IV. QED within stated assumptions.

Weather-equivalent (input-quality) veto. Detection quality plays the role of "weather." Table V gives the crossover: at 20% noise C-BIoU (62.3) > IoU (57.6); at 40% noise IoU (38.3) > C-BIoU (29.2). So the geometric veto holds for noise ≤ ~20% and inverts beyond it. This is the paper's stated limitation (PAGE 8) and is quantitatively bounded.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable scenarios with predictions, testable against the paper's own data/protocol:

1. Over-expansion veto. Prediction: on a dense, appearance-ambiguous set, raising b₂ above the tuned optimum decreases AssA while DetA stays flat. Verifiable via Fig. 6 — buffer combos above [0.3,0.4] already dip (e.g., larger-b cells fall below 81.7). Falsified if HOTA rises monotonically with b.

2. Noise-crossover veto. Prediction: the C-BIoU-over-IoU advantage vanishes between 20% and 40% injected noise and reverses at 40% (C-BIoU AssA < IoU AssA). Verifiable in Table V (29.2 vs 38.3 HOTA; AssA 14.7 vs 25.0). Falsified if C-BIoU still leads at 40%.

3. Motion-telescoping veto. Prediction: because the estimator reduces to (Δ/n)(o^t − o^{t−n}), under sustained constant acceleration the bias grows ∝ Δ² and removing the buffer term should hurt more than adding it; equivalently, motion-estimation ON should help most exactly where velocity is stable, not where it is erratic. Partially verifiable via the +1.5 HOTA motion gain (80.2→81.7, Table IV). Falsified if motion ON helps equally on high-jerk clips.

Net: the C-BIoU claims are internally consistent and their failure boundary (high detection noise) is explicitly measured, which is the strongest form of honesty a matching-operator paper can offer.
