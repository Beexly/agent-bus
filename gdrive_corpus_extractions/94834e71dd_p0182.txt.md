Maintenance is bounded below by biomechanical limits (inspection, repair, replacement labor-hours per unit of capital) and above by the physical decay rate of the capital goods themselves. Agents cannot consume capital at an arbitrary psychological rate; inadequate maintenance is constrained by the time it takes to neglect an asset to failure and by the irreversible point at which scrap value is reached. This vetoes models that treat capital consumption as a frictionless flow choice.

Weather/exogenous-productivity layer veto. MPK shifts with exogenous productivity conditions (weather analogs for agriculture, energy disruptions, supply-chain shocks). Because r(t₀) = MPK(t₀) is causal and productivity-determined [PAGE 107], an exogenous productivity shock moves r independently of saving psychology. Any theory that subordinates the interest rate to intertemporal preference at the moment is vetoed by the productivity layer: r responds to the physical output capacity of existing capital, not to the desire to save.

Adjudication: Across all three layers, the invariant is that instantaneous r is set by the physical capital stock and its marginal productivity, while ρ governs the flow \dot{K} and hence future K and future r. The veto chain is complete: physical fixity blocks same-moment psychological determination; biomechanical maintenance bounds block infinite capital consumption; exogenous productivity shifts block pure preference-based rate setting.

Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Veto of same-day interest response to saving-psychology stimulus. Counterfactual: an exogenous announcement shifts household time preference or saving sentiment at t₀. Veto: Knight’s fixity condition [PAGE 107] implies dK/dr = 0 at the moment and ∂r/∂s ≈ 0. Prediction: within the same trading day (or week), no statistically significant movement in r attributable to the sentiment shock; any r movement must await changes in the physical capital stock or realized productivity.

Scenario 2 — Veto of a homogeneous capital–product ratio for installed capital. Counterfactual: an analyst computes r as the ratio of current product to existing capital for a heterogeneous installed base. Veto: footnote 2 at [PAGE 107] states capital goods once created are valued by capitalization, which presupposes r, so the ratio is not a direct primitive. Prediction: newly installed capital (homogeneous with anticipated product) earns r ≈ MPK, whereas existing heterogeneous capital earns an imputed r derived from capitalized earnings; the two measured ratios will diverge systematically, and using the latter as a causal productivity rate will produce specification error.

Scenario 3 — Veto of finite-horizon convergence to equilibrium interest. Counterfactual: a model assumes r(t) → r* within a fixed window T (e.g., 4–8 weeks) after a disturbance. Veto: Knight asserts “no limits to the time” required for equilibrium adjustment, even with all else static [PAGE 107], and notes r has remained above equilibrium during persistent rapid accumulation. Prediction: economies with positive persistent net investment will exhibit r > r* for durations exceeding any pre-specified T; convergence, if it occurs, happens only after capital accumulation slows, not on an exogenous clock.
