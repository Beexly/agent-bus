The LLM is not a free-standing policy: it receives the situation and top-k retrieved candidates and is told “not to mention specific player names or team names in the output and not to provide explanations or alternatives, only a single formatted call” [PAGE 10 of 19]. The gray-zone audit has several nontrivial checks. First, the “held out situations” are “derived from the same source as the dataset” [PAGE 10 of 19]; the paper does not report a strict temporal split, team-disjoint split, or week-forward split, so the 41% Hits@10 may reflect within-season, within-team, and same-source overlap rather than true forward generalization. Third, the MRR interpretation is ambiguous: if MRR is averaged over all queries with misses counted as zero, then \(\mathrm{MRR}/\mathrm{Hits@10}=0.3950/0.41\approx0.963\), implying conditional reciprocal rank near 1 and average conditional rank near 1.04, not “around rank 2–3” [PAGE 12 of 19]. <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The lexical scorer is standard BM25Okapi. With query terms \(q_i\in Q\), document \(D\), term frequency \(f(q_i,D)\), document length \(|D|\) in tokens, corpus average document length \(\mathrm{avgdl}\) in tokens, inverse document frequency \(\mathrm{IDF}(q_i)\) dimensionless, and hyperparameters \(k_1,b\) dimensionless, the paper states:
\[
\mathrm{BM25}(Q,D)=\sum_{q_i\in Q}\mathrm{IDF}(q_i)\cdot
\frac{f(q_i,D)(k_1+1)}
{f(q_i,D)+k_1\left(1-b+b\frac{|D|}{\mathrm{avgdl}}\right)}.
\]
Citation: [PAGE 9 of 19]. Units: \(f, |D|, \mathrm{avgdl}\) are token counts; \(\mathrm{IDF}\), \(k_1\), \(b\), and the resulting score are dimensionless. Breakdown regimes: if \(\mathrm{avgdl}=0\), the length-normalization term is undefined; if all candidate BM25 scores are equal, min-max normalization has zero denominator; if \(f(q_i,D)\) is very large, the term saturates toward \(\mathrm{IDF}(q_i)(k_1+1)\), so exact-token repetition cannot dominate without bound.

Dense retrieval uses E5-base-v2, a 12-layer transformer producing a 768-dimensional embedding, L2-normalized so cosine similarity reduces to inner product [PAGE 8 of 19]. Formally, for encoder \(f_\theta\):
\[
v(x)=\frac{f_\theta(x)}{\|f_\theta(x)\|_2},\qquad
s_{\cos}(q,d)=v(q)^\top v(d).
\]
Units: embedding components are dimensionless; \(s_{\cos}\in[-1,1]\) for normalized real vectors. Singularity: if \(\|f_\theta(x)\|_2=0\), normalization is undefined; in practice this is avoided by nonzero embeddings, but the code should guard it. FAISS HNSW parameters are \(M=32\), \(\mathrm{efConstruction}=200\), \(\mathrm{efSearch}=64\) [PAGE 8 of 19][PAGE 9 of 19]; these are dimensionless graph/search parameters. Breakdown regime: HNSW is approximate, so recall can degrade as corpus size, dimensionality, or graph connectivity changes; the paper’s 7,381-document corpus is moderate, so the reported latency is not a proof for league-scale multi-season corpora.

Hybrid fusion is:
\[
s_h(d)=0.7\,s_{\cos}(q,d)+0.3\,\tilde{s}_{\mathrm{BM25}}(d),
\]
where \(\tilde{s}_{\mathrm{BM25}}\) is min-max normalized to \([0,1]\) across the union of dense and lexical candidates [PAGE 9 of 19]. Units: both terms are dimensionless; weights sum to 1. Breakdown regimes: if \(\max \mathrm{BM25}=\min \mathrm{BM25}\), min-max normalization is undefined; if the dense candidate list is empty, the union and weighting logic must define fallback behavior; if the query has no BM25 tokens after tokenization, the lexical term is zero or undefined depending on implementation.

Retrieval effectiveness is:
\[
\mathrm{Hits@K}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{rank}_i\le K],
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{MRR}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{rank}_i}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{rank}_i\le K]
\]
under the convention that misses contribute zero; if misses are excluded, the denominator is the number of hits instead of \(N\). Units: Hits@K is a probability in \([0,1]\); MRR is reciprocal rank, dimensionless in \([0,1]\). The paper reports Hits@10 = 41.0%, MRR = 0.3950 [PAGE 12 of 19], but does not specify the MRR denominator. This matters: with misses counted as zero, conditional mean reciprocal rank is \(0.3950/0.41\approx0.963\), implying conditional rank near 1.04; with misses excluded, \(0.3950\) implies conditional rank near \(2.53\). The paper’s “around rank 2–3” [PAGE 12 of 19] is therefore only consistent with the hits-only convention.

Latency is reported as p50 and p99 for retrieval: 38.47 ms and 59.43 ms [PAGE 12 of 19]; end-to-end p50 = 865.91 ms and p95 = 1.31 s [PAGE 13 of 19]. Units: milliseconds and seconds. The NFL play clock is 40 s in ordinary situations and 25 s in specified administrative situations, with an effective decision window “between 15 and 25 seconds” [PAGE 4 of 19]. Thus p95 end-to-end \(1.31\ \mathrm{s}\) consumes \(1.31/15\approx8.7\%\) to \(1.31/25\approx5.2\%\) of the effective window, before stadium-network and communication overhead. The “approximately 8%” retrieval-share statement [PAGE 2 of 19] is not reproduced by the reported medians: \(38.47/865.91\approx4.44\%\).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is a straight line from probabilistic lexical retrieval to dense retrieval to hybrid retrieval to retrieval-augmented generation. BM25 is the long-standing lexical baseline built on term frequency, inverse document frequency, and document-length normalization [PAGE 3 of 19][PAGE 9 of 19]. Dense Passage Retrieval and the E5 family then establish learned embeddings as a complementary retrieval signal; the paper notes that E5 is trained with weakly supervised contrastive pre-training and is designed to provide a single vector suitable for retrieval [PAGE 4 of 19][PAGE 8 of 19]. Hybrid fusion is the next historical layer: the paper explicitly grounds its 0.7 vector + 0.3 BM25 fusion in hybrid-retrieval work and in industry systems such as Vespa, Elasticsearch, and OpenSearch [PAGE 3 of 19][PAGE 4 of 19]. The LLM stage is a RAG-style synthesis layer constrained to the retrieved neighborhood, not a standalone decision maker [PAGE 10 of 19]. The football lineage is equally explicit: call sheets organized by down, distance, field zone, hash, personnel, red zone, third down, two-minute, and four-minute situations [PAGE 4 of 19]; Bruce Arians’ roughly 150 plays and Carson Palmer’s 171-play wristband examples [PAGE 4 of 19]; and the operational reality that coordinators must scan multiple sections under pressure [PAGE 5 of 19].

The retrocausal trap is that the paper’s success metric is a single analyst-designated inspiration, not a counterfactual outcome. Over multiple weeks, three failure modes compound. First, same-source evaluation can overstate forward performance: the paper says experiments use “held out situations derived from the same source as the dataset” [PAGE 10 of 19], but it does not report a week-forward or team-disjoint split. A strict weekly deployment audit would reindex only prior weeks and test on future weeks; if Hits@10 drops materially, the 41% figure is a within-source retrieval ceiling, not a forward-looking memory. Second, opponent adaptation creates distribution shift: a coordinator who repeatedly uses the same retrieved families becomes more predictable, and the defense’s counter-adjustments are not in the 2020 corpus. Third, feedback loops contaminate future logs: if coaches act on recommendations, the next season’s play-by-play is no longer a natural observational corpus, so future retrieval evaluation needs propensity logging or randomized holdouts. The red-zone beachhead is also a generalization trap: the paper itself limits the study to “red zone plays from a single NFL regular season” [PAGE 15 of 19], so midfield, backed-up, two-minute, and four-minute contexts remain unproven. The historical lineage therefore supports the architecture but not the claim that a single-season red-zone retrieval score transfers automatically to a full-field, multi-week, adaptive NFL environment.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory-of-mind layer is where the paper is strongest conceptually and weakest empirically. A coach is not choosing a play in a vacuum; the coach is modeling the defensive coordinator’s model of the offense’s tendencies, while the defensive coordinator is modeling the offense’s likely response to front and coverage. The paper captures the human failure mode well: under time pressure, “coordinators to fall back on a small set of familiar calls rather than systematically evaluating the full space of options keyed by situation” [PAGE 5 of 19]. That is crisis habit regression. A retrieval system can counter it by surfacing league-wide precedents that are outside the coach’s habitual call sheet. But the same system can create a new habit trap: if the top-ranked retrieved play family becomes the default, the offense’s recommendation distribution collapses toward the retriever’s most similar historical cluster. The counter-trap is therefore not merely “retrieve more plays”; it is to preserve a calibrated mix of analogues, including lower-ranked but situationally distinct plays, and to expose uncertainty rather than a single authoritative call.

The clock is the binding constraint on this recursive loop. The paper states that the effective decision window is “between 15 and 25 seconds for routine situations” [PAGE 4 of 19], and the end-to-end p95 is 1.31 s [PAGE 13 of 19]. On pure arithmetic, that leaves roughly 13.69 s to 23.69 s for human deliberation, communication, personnel, and line-of-scrimmage adjustment. But the system’s output is a play family, not a full call with protection, route adjustment, and cadence; the human still has to map the family to personnel and to the defense’s current structure. The recursive risk is automation bias: a coordinator under clock pressure may over-weight the retrieved family because it is fast and syntactically clean, even when the defense’s live structure contradicts the historical analogue. The LLM constraint helps here: it is told to output “only a single formatted call” and not to explain alternatives [PAGE 10 of 19], which reduces hallucinated structures but also removes the uncertainty signal that a recursive decision-maker needs. A safer design would keep the LLM output constrained but add a separate uncertainty and counterfactual layer: “this family is retrieved, but the live front/coverage resembles these three historical counters.” That is the difference between a memory aid and a policy that inadvertently trains the coach to stop modeling the opponent.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto is mandatory because the paper’s corpus does not contain the variables needed to prove physical, biomechanical, or weather feasibility. The dataset fields are “identifiers for game and play, offensive and defensive team abbreviations, down, distance to first down, yard line, quarter, play type, nominal direction, yards gained, and a free text description” [PAGE 6 of 19]. The context adds situation and a history window of up to five prior offensive plays [PAGE 6 of 19]. The appendix example even shows `"formation": null` [PAGE 18 of 19], so formation is not reliably populated. There is no weather field, no wind speed, no precipitation, no temperature, no field-surface state, no injury report, no personnel availability, no quarterback arm-strength measurement, no receiver separation, no pressure rate, and no biomechanical load. The paper mentions Next Gen Stats tracking, separation, top speed, and completion probability as ecosystem capabilities [PAGE 5 of 19], but those signals are not used in the reported retrieval or evaluation. Therefore a retrieved play family is only a historical analogue; it is not proof of physical admissibility.

A formal veto adjudication should treat a recommendation \(r\) in state \(s\) as callable only if independent predicates pass: \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}(r,s)=1\), \(V_{\mathrm{bio}}(r,s)=1\), \(V_{\mathrm{weather}}(r,s)=1\), and \(V_{\mathrm{clock}}(r,s)=1\). These are Boolean gates, not invented performance ratios. \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}\) checks personnel package, formation compatibility, quarterback availability, route-depth versus arm strength, protection count, and down/distance legality. \(V_{\mathrm{bio}}\) checks injury status, snap load, fatigue, and movement capacity; units would be player-availability counts, load in arbitrary team units, and speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). \(V_{\mathrm{weather}}\) checks wind speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), precipitation in \(\mathrm{mm\,h^{-1}}\), temperature in \(^\circ\mathrm{C}\), and field surface; illustrative team-set thresholds such as wind \(>10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) or heavy precipitation should force downweighting or abstention on deep-boundary families. \(V_{\mathrm{clock}}\) checks whether the remaining play-clock budget exceeds the end-to-end latency plus communication overhead; with p95 \(1.31\ \mathrm{s}\) and a 15–25 s effective window [PAGE 4 of 19][PAGE 13 of 19], the raw latency passes, but stadium-network and headset delays must be measured. The proof obligation fails in the current paper because the required fields are absent; the correct behavior is abstention or a “retrieval-only, unverified” label, not an implicit claim that the historical analogue is physically and meteorologically valid.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
1. **Temporal and team-disjoint holdout veto.** Counterfactual: rebuild the index using only 2020 weeks before the test week and exclude the test team from the index, then evaluate the same Hits@K and MRR pipeline. Verifiable reward: if Hits@10 falls by more than 10 percentage points from the reported 41.0% [PAGE 12 of 19], the deployment reward is vetoed and the paper’s same-source “held out” evaluation [PAGE 10 of 19] is classified as leakage-sensitive. Prediction: Hits@10 will drop materially, most likely into the mid-20% to low-30% range, with MRR dropping below 0.30; if it does not drop, the within-source result is more robust than the audit expects.

2. **Normalization and empty-query singularity veto.** Counterfactual: unit-test the hybrid scorer on (a) a query whose BM25 scores are all equal across candidates, so \(\max \mathrm{BM25}=\min \mathrm{BM25}\), and (b) a query with zero lexical tokens after tokenization. Verifiable reward: the current min-max fusion [PAGE 9 of 19] must not emit NaN, infinity, or an exception; if it does, the release is vetoed until a guard returns a defined fallback (e.g., lexical term = 0, dense term only). Prediction: without a guard, the equal-score case produces a zero-denominator normalization failure; with the guard, the hybrid score reduces to \(0.7s_{\cos}\) and remains finite.

3. **Physical/weather feasibility veto.** Counterfactual: construct an expert-labeled test set of situations with high wind, heavy precipitation, extreme temperature, backup quarterback, missing personnel, or formation-null records [PAGE 18 of 19], and require the system to either abstain or downweight infeasible deep-boundary families. Verifiable reward: expert-labeled infeasible recommendations must be zero on the veto-enabled system; the current system, which lacks weather and biomechanical fields in its corpus [PAGE 6 of 19], should show a nonzero infeasible rate. Prediction: the unvetoed system will recommend at least some deep or personnel-dependent families in weather/personnel-mismatch cases because retrieval has no field to penalize them; the veto layer will eliminate those invalid outputs at the cost of lower coverage and more abstentions.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
