Biomechanical veto: Even if the command is neurally generated, muscle-tendon force-velocity properties, joint capsule limits, and articular contact enforce $y(t) = y(t-1)$ despite $u(t) \neq 0$. This veto operates in the plant dynamics, not in the sensory cortex, and is expressed as a constraint set $\mathcal{Y}_{\text{feasible}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{n_y}$ such that $y(t) \in \mathcal{Y}_{\text{feasible}}$.

Weather veto: None. The experiment is conducted indoors; no atmospheric pressure, temperature, humidity, or wind variable enters the causal structure. Any appeal to “weather” would be a category error with zero coupling coefficient to the variables $u$, $y$, $\hat{y}$, or $\varepsilon$.

Veto proof: The correct sensation under an active veto is “movement blocked.” The illusion arises only when the veto signal’s sensory precision $\lambda_y$ is driven to zero by anesthesia or occlusion, so that $P(M=1 \mid u, y) \approx P(M=1 \mid u) \approx 1$. If proprioception is intact and vision is available ($ \lambda_y > 0$), the comparator resolves $\varepsilon = y - \hat{y} \neq 0$ and the veto stands: no hallucinated movement. Thus the illusion is not a property of intention per se but of intention unopposed by a verified veto, exactly the regime James describes: “unless the sensation of the part’s not moving is a strong one, we are apt to feel as if the movement had actually taken place” ([PAGE 115]).

Scenario 1 (Anesthesia + visual feedback veto). A participant’s fingertip is anesthetized; they are instructed to flex against a fixed obstruction while viewing their hand through a live video feed. The veto is the continuous visual display of zero displacement. Prediction: vividness of illusory movement (0–10 verbal report) is significantly lower in eyes-open/video-feedback trials than in eyes-closed trials; effect tested by paired Wilcoxon signed-rank, $n = 30$, $\alpha = 0.05$. Breakdown regime: if video latency exceeds the comparator integration window ($\tau > 100$ ms), the visual veto is temporally decorrelated from $u$, and the illusion rate should revert to the eyes-closed baseline.

Scenario 2 (Intact limb + delayed reafference veto). An intact participant performs the Exner finger-flexion attempt with eyes closed, then receives veridical proprioceptive and visual feedback after a programmable delay $\Delta$. The veto is the temporally aligned reafference confirming stillness. Prediction: for $\Delta < 80$ ms, felt-movement reports remain at baseline ($<15\%$); for $\Delta > 150$ ms, the mismatch between efference copy and delayed feedback reintroduces the illusion, with felt-movement frequency rising to $\ge 40\%$. Verifiable via simultaneity-judgment psychophysics and binary report logistic regression on $\Delta$.

Scenario 3 (Phantom limb + mirror-therapy veto over four weeks). A unilateral amputee receives either (A) mirror therapy providing congruent visual reafference for intended phantom movement, or (B) motor imagery alone with no visual feedback. The veto in condition A is the mirror image that cancels the unopposed motor prior by supplying $y_{\text{visual}} \neq 0$ correlated with $u$. Prediction: by week 4, condition A shows a statistically significant reduction in phantom vividness ($V$ in bits via report entropy) and pain (VAS 0–100 mm) relative to condition B, while condition B shows stable or increased vividness due to the unopposed prior $P(M=1 \mid u)$. Verifiable via daily ecological momentary assessment and pre/post fMRI S1/M1 map overlap; breakdown occurs if the mirror is removed, at which point the veto is withdrawn and vividness rebounds toward baseline.
