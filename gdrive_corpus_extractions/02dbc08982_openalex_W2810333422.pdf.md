The pipeline in Israni & Mewada can be modeled as a cascaded decision process with discrete gateways: foreground extraction (GMM) → blob centroid → Euclidean cost matrix → Hungarian assignment → occlusion flag (bounding-box growth) → demerge test (cost-difference threshold µ=60) → feature verification → identity retention. Each hop is conditional on the previous, so an error at hop one propagates deterministically unless later gates intervene.

First hop: GMM foreground extraction [PAGE 4, Eq. (1)]. The background model assumes stationary mixture weights ω_{i,t} over Gaussian components η(X_t; μ_{i,t}, Σ_{i,t}). If stadium illumination shifts or the camera pans, the stationary assumption collapses and the foreground blob may fragment or vanish. No blob means no centroid, which means no entry in the Hungarian assignment matrix D_{k,k+1} [PAGE 6, Eq. (10)], and the player simply drops out of the state space until a new blob is detected. This is not a soft degradation; it is a hard structural break because the algorithm has no re-identification primitive outside the reliable-feature cache.

Second hop: Hungarian assignment minimizes total Euclidean distance between centroids in consecutive frames [PAGE 6, Eq. (11)]. When two players cross at similar velocities, their centroids become approximately equidistant to the two candidate previous positions. The assignment matrix then admits near-degenerate minima, and the Hungarian solver returns an arbitrary permutation. The source acknowledges that Kalman prediction fails under nonlinear soccer motion [PAGE 8], but the proposed Hungarian cost has no semantic term to break this symmetry; it relies entirely on spatial proximity. At 1080 × 1920 resolution, distant players produce small inter-centroid distances, so the permutation ambiguity is magnified in the telephoto regime typical of broadcast soccer.

Third hop: occlusion detection [PAGE 7, Eq. (13)] triggers when the nearest neighbor’s bounding box height or width increases beyond a symbolic threshold. The numerical value of that threshold is never given in the source, creating an irreproducible gray zone. Worse, the rule requires the occluded player’s centroid to be known in order to test adjacency [PAGE 7, Eq. (12), “PV = 0 and OM = 0”], yet by definition a fully occluded player has no reliable centroid. This circularity means the system can mark “No Occlusion” precisely when total occlusion occurs, then continue tracking a ghost bounding box via linear extrapolation—a crisis habit regression to motion priors.

There is no depth or scale normalization, so the threshold is not dimensionless across camera zoom settings.

\[
D_{k,k+1} = \begin{bmatrix}
d_{1k,1k+1} & d_{1k,2k+1} & d_{1k,3k+1}\\
d_{2k,1k+1} & d_{2k,2k+1} & d_{2k,3k+1}\\
d_{3k,1k+1} & d_{3k,2k+1} & d_{3k,3k+1}
\end{bmatrix}
\]
where \(d_{ik,jk+1} = \text{Euclidean distance}(P_{i,k},P_{j,k+1})\). Units: pixels. Breakdown: rectangular matrices occur when player count changes between frames; dummy nodes and gating thresholds are not specified in the source.

[PAGE 6, Eq. (11)] — Hungarian optimal mapping:
\[
f = \min\bigl(\{D_{k,k+1}\}\bigr),\qquad
\{f: (P_{1,k+1}\to P_{1,k}),(P_{2,k+1}\to P_{3,k})\}\mid P_{i,k},P_{i,k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^2
\]
Units: dimensionless permutation over \(\mathbb{R}^2\) positions. Breakdown: when cost differences fall below numerical precision, the mapping is non-unique; the solver choice becomes an uncontrolled source of identity switches.

[PAGE 5, Eq. (5)] — Diagonal Laplacian descriptor:
\[
\mathrm{LAP}_D = |I*L_x| + |I*L_y| + |I*L_{x1}| + |I*L_{x2}|
\]
with \(L_x=[-1\ 2\ -1]\), \(L_y=L_x^T\), \(L_{x1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\0&-2&0\\1&0&0\end{bmatrix}\), \(L_{x2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&-2&0\\1&0&1\end{bmatrix}\). Units: absolute intensity response (arbitrary focus units). Assumption: descriptor is rotation/scale/translation invariant [PAGE 3]. Breakdown: uniform jersey regions yield near-zero response, destroying discriminative power between teammates.

[PAGE 5, Eq. (6)] — Standard deviation descriptor:
\[
S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\mu)^2}
\]
Units: intensity levels (0–255). Assumption: \(N\) pixels span the player blob; \(\mu\) is the mean blob intensity. Breakdown: low-texture kits produce \(S\approx0\), making different players indistinguishable.

[PAGE 5, Eq. (7)] — Image curvature descriptor:
\[
\emptyset = |c_0|+|c_1|+|c_2|+|c_3|
\]
where \(c_0=M_1*I\), \(c_1=M_1^T*I\), \(c_2=\frac{3}{2}M_2*I-M_2^T*I\), \(c_3=\frac{3}{2}(M_2^T*I)-(M_2*I_{\mathrm{rev}x})\), with \(M_1=\frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix}-1&0&1\\-1&0&1\\-1&0&1\end{bmatrix}\), \(M_2=\frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\1&0&1\\1&0&1\end{bmatrix}\). Units: inverse-pixel-squared curvature proxy. Breakdown: convolution boundary artifacts at blob edges create spurious curvature when players are near the frame boundary.

[PAGE 5, Eq. (8)] — Gradient energy descriptor:
\[
\emptyset_{X,Y} = \sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega(x,y)}\bigl[I_x(i,j)^2 + I_y(i,j)^2\bigr]
\]
Units: intensity-squared per pixel. Assumption: \(I_x,I_y\) are first-order spatial derivatives; \(\Omega\) is the blob neighborhood. Breakdown: motion blur from high-speed sprinting suppresses first derivatives, collapsing gradient energy for fast-moving players precisely when tracking is most critical.

[PAGE 5, Eq. (9)] — Vollath’s autocorrelation descriptor:
\[
\emptyset_{x,y} = \sum_{(i,j)=\Omega(x,y)} I(i,j)\,I(i+1,j) - \sum_{(i,j)=\Omega(x,y)} I(i,j)\,I(i+2,j)
\]
Units: intensity-squared. Assumption: local pixel autocorrelation captures texture structure. Breakdown: striped or checkered jerseys introduce periodicity that produces false autocorrelation peaks, causing mismatches between same-team opponents.

[PAGE 7, Eq. (12)] — Self-occlusion centroid metric:
\[
\mathrm{Sb}(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{\sum_j\bigl(Ac(j)(x,y)-Ac(i)(x,y)\bigr)}, & PV=0\ \text{and}\ OM=0\\
\text{undefined}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
Units: pixels. \(Ac(j)\) = centroid of occluded object; \(Ac(i)\) = centroids of detected players. Breakdown: circular dependency—requires the occluded player’s centroid \(Ac(j)\), which is unavailable precisely when the player is fully occluded; the condition \(PV=0\) and \(OM=0\) cannot both be satisfied under total occlusion, so the metric is undefined in the worst case.

[PAGE 7, Eq. (13)] — Occlusion declaration:
\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\text{Mark Occluder and Occluded}, & x\|y > \text{threshold}\\
\text{No Occlusion}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
with \(x=\mathrm{Sb}(x)/\mathrm{Sfb}(x)\), \(y=\mathrm{Sb}(y)/\mathrm{Sfb}(y)\). Units: dimensionless ratios compared against an unstated numerical threshold. Breakdown: the threshold value is never provided [PAGE 7], so Eq. (13) is not reproducible; moderate occlusion producing sub-threshold box growth is misclassified as “No Occlusion.”

[PAGE 7, Eq. (14)] — Demerge detection:
\[
x = [A]-[B] < \mu,\qquad \mu=\text{threshold}=60
\]
\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\text{Demerge Detected}, & x=1\ \text{and}\ OM>0\\
\text{No Demerge}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
Units: Hungarian cost difference in pixels; \(OM\) is an occlusion mark counter. Assumption: \([A]\) and \([B]\) are minimum assignment costs for the two tracks. Breakdown: if both costs are large but their difference happens to be \(<60\) pixels, a false demerge is declared; if the true cost difference exceeds 60 pixels under telephoto compression, a real demerge is missed.

[PAGE 8, Eq. (15)] — Identity retention:
\[
\mathrm{sum1}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}[fd_1(i)-fr_1(i)] + \sum_{i=1}^{4}[fd_2(i)-fr_2(i)]
\]
\[
\mathrm{sum2}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}[fd_1(i)-fr_2(i)] + \sum_{i=1}^{4}[fd_2(i)-fr_1(i)]
\]
\[
\mathrm{IdentityRetention}=
\begin{cases}
fd_1=P_1,\ fd_2=P_2, & \mathrm{sum1}<\mathrm{sum2}\\
fd_1=P_2,\ fd_2=P_1, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
Units: summed descriptor differences (mixed units; no normalization across Laplacian, standard deviation, curvature, gradient energy, autocorrelation is specified). Note: the source describes five descriptors [PAGE 5] but sums \(i=1\) to \(4\) [PAGE 8], an indexing inconsistency. Breakdown: \(\mathrm{sum1}=\mathrm{sum2}\) yields an undefined identity; scale mismatch across descriptor families makes the comparison sensitive to arbitrary feature ordering.

[PAGE 8, Eqs. (16)–(17)] — Precision and recall as printed:
\[
\text{Precision}=\frac{(\text{PlayerId retained correct})\cap(\text{total player occlusion})}{\text{total player occlusion}}
\]
\[
\text{Recall}=\frac{(\text{PlayerId retained})\cap(\text{total player occlusion})}{(\text{player ID retained correct})}
\]
Units: dimensionless ratios in \([0,1]\). Breakdown: the notation conflates set intersection with scalar counts; Recall’s denominator appears inverted relative to standard definition, so the exact operating point cannot be audited from the text alone.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The algorithm’s intellectual ancestry forms a backward chain through computer vision and developmental psychology. The object-permanence principle—“an occluded object will re-emerge near its occluder”—is imported from Baillargeon’s work on five-month-old infants [15], cited on [PAGE 2]. This is a strong, domain-specific prior: it assumes re-emergence is spatially local and temporally immediate. The Hungarian assignment step traces to Munkres’ 1957 algorithm for assignment and transportation problems [31], cited on [PAGE 11], which provides a globally optimal one-to-one mapping but contains no appearance model. The Kalman baseline traces to Welch & Bishop’s 1995 tutorial [29], cited on [PAGE 6], which assumes constant-velocity motion—explicitly invalidated for soccer by the authors on [PAGE 8]. The five focus-measure descriptors (Diagonal Laplacian, standard deviation, curvature, gradient energy, Vollath autocorrelation) are repurposed from Pertuz et al.’s 2013 microscopy focus-operators survey [27], cited on [PAGES 3 and 5]; these operators were originally optimized for controlled illumination in light microscopy, not outdoor stadium floodlights. Performance is scored with CLEAR MOT metrics (MOTA, MOTP, ID-S) from Bernardin & Stiefelhagen (2008) [42], cited on [PAGE 10], and ground truth follows D’Orazio’s semi-automatic soccer annotation system [13], cited on [PAGES 1 and 8].

Retrospectively, this lineage embeds several multi-week traps if the system were deployed across a competitive season. First, identity switches compound: because the tracker has no global re-identification primitive outside the descriptor cache, a single misassignment in week one persists whenever the two players remain visually similar, and the “self-healing” claim on [PAGE 8] only operates if demerge occurs while both players are still adjacent and within the feature-cache window. Second, descriptor drift: jersey dirt, sweat, and fabric wear alter gradient energy and Diagonal Laplacian responses across weeks, while the threshold \(\mu=60\) [PAGE 7] was calibrated on the fixed ISSIA geometry and does not transfer to different stadium zoom levels. Third, illumination seasonality: matches under floodlights versus daylight change the mean intensity distribution, invalidating the microscopy-derived focus measures without any stated re-calibration protocol. Fourth, temporal compounding of ID-S: CLEAR MOT penalizes identity switches heavily, so a small per-match swap rate accumulates into large MOTA degradation over weeks, particularly in derby matches where teammates wear identical kits and inter-player occlusion is frequent. The genealogy therefore shows a system assembled from stable components (Hungarian, GMM) and context-mismatched priors (microscopy focus measures, infant object permanence) whose composition has not been validated for longitudinal deployment.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The tracker implicitly maintains a second-order model of each player: it believes that each player is an intentional agent whose bounding-box state is hidden during occlusion, and it must infer which hidden agent corresponds to which visible re-emergence. This is a recursive theory-of-mind problem because the tracker’s belief about Player A depends on its belief about what Player A believes about being occluded by Player B.

Crisis habit regression occurs at the moment of occlusion. When direct observation is lost, the system regresses to its most deeply entrenched prior: geometric adjacency and motion continuity. Equation (13)’s bounding-box growth test and Equation (14)’s cost-difference test are habits of spatial reasoning—they assume that the nearest neighbor is the occluder and that demerge preserves proximity. Under intense occlusion, these regressions dominate because the feature channel is silent. If both players wear identical kits, the regressed habit cannot distinguish them; the Hungarian cost matrix supplies a permutation but no semantic identity, so the tracker literally regresses to a state where it “knows” two positions changed but not who moved where.

The counter-trap is the feature-verification layer in Equation (15). It interrupts the geometric habit by forcing comparison against reliable-frame descriptor caches (\(fr_1, fr_2\)) rather than extrapolated motion. The counter-trap succeeds only if the descriptors are truly RST-invariant and illumination-stable, conditions not guaranteed by the source. If the descriptors fail—e.g., gradient energy collapses under motion blur [PAGE 5]—the counter-trap becomes worse than the regression: it issues a confident identity swap instead of a hesitant “unknown,” converting a recoverable tracking gap into a permanent ID-S error.

The clock is the 25 fps temporal grid and the ten-frame reliability window [PAGE 5], i.e., 0.4 seconds. Soccer players execute changes of direction on sub-second timescales, so a player can exit the reliable-blob state mid-action. The demerge test \(\mu=60\) pixels [PAGE 7] has no temporal decay term; a demerge occurring in a single frame between reliable snapshots can be missed entirely because the identity was never committed to long-term memory. In crisis, the clock compresses: fast demerge within one frame skips feature computation because the reliable-blob precondition is unmet, and the tracker is forced back into habit regression. The recursive loop therefore oscillates between trust in geometry (habit regression) and trust in appearance (counter-trap), governed by a 0.4-second commitment window that is often shorter than the biomechanical event it is trying to resolve.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: The monocular pipeline projects 3D players onto a 2D image plane. When two players’ centroids project to identical or near-identical pixels, the Euclidean cost matrix contains near-zero entries for both possible assignments, making the Hungarian solution degenerate. A physical veto must fire whenever centroid separation falls below the camera’s angular resolution limit, forcing the tracker to output “unresolvable occlusion” rather than a guessed permutation. The bounding-box growth rule in Equation (13) also fails physically when a smaller player is fully hidden behind a larger one: the occluder’s box may not grow beyond threshold while the occluded player vanishes entirely, producing a false “No Occlusion” and a ghost track. Without stereo or depth input, this physical ambiguity is irreducible; the correct veto is to freeze identity and suppress position updates until separation exceeds the resolution limit.

Biomechanical layer veto: Soccer players execute cuts, sprints, and accelerations that violate the smooth-bounding-box and constant-velocity assumptions implicit in the Hungarian cost and the reliability model. The ten-frame reliable window (0.4 s at 25 fps) [PAGE 5] is shorter than many change-of-direction cycles; a player can drop out of reliable status during a sprint, causing the fragmentations illustrated in Figure 6 [PAGE 5]. A biomechanical veto should trigger when consecutive invisible count exceeds one action cycle (~25 frames, ~1 s) without a descriptor match, because at that horizon the object-permanence adjacency assumption [PAGE 2] no longer holds and re-emergence may be far from the last occluder.

Weather/illumination layer veto: The source explicitly lists illumination variation as a challenge [PAGE 8], yet the five descriptors are microscopy focus measures designed for controlled lighting [PAGE 5]. Stadium floodlights, passing cloud shadows, and rain on the camera housing shift global intensity statistics. Vollath autocorrelation and gradient energy are especially sensitive to global contrast changes; a 30% mean-intensity drop between the reliable frame and the detected frame can invert sum1/sum2 ordering in Equation (15) even when geometry is unambiguous. A weather veto should compare the global histogram mean \(\mu_I\) between reliable and detected frames; if \(|\Delta\mu_I|\) exceeds a calibrated delta (e.g., 15 intensity levels on an 8-bit scale), descriptor-based identity retention must be suspended and the tracker must fall back to geometry-only with low-confidence flagging.

Adjudication hierarchy: Physical veto (projection degeneracy) overrides Biomechanical veto (action-cycle horizon) overrides Weather veto (histogram shift) overrides Feature match (Equation 15). Any veto forces identity to “unknown” rather than a forced swap, trading MOTA for a durable reduction in ID-S. This hierarchy is veto-proof because it blocks identity commitment at the layer where the generative assumption first fails, rather than attempting to correct a swap after it has been written to the track state.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-verifiable-reward (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions:

Scenario 1 — Same-team crossing below pixel threshold. Counterfactual: two teammates in identical kits cross in ISSIA sequence S3 with centroid separation below 60 pixels at demerge. Veto: suspend identity retention because the Hungarian cost difference cannot fall below \(\mu=60\) pixels in a way that disambiguates identical appearance. Prediction: with veto active, ID-S for this event is 0 but one fragmentation is recorded; without veto, the baseline swaps identities with probability approximately 0.5, yielding expected ID-S = 0.5 and lower MOTA. Verifiable on Table 2/3 same-team occlusion rows by injecting controlled crossings at varied focal lengths and measuring ID-S conditioned on centroid separation < 60 px.

Scenario 2 — Long-term occlusion beyond cache horizon. Counterfactual: an occluder blocks the target for 35 consecutive frames (~1.4 s), exceeding the ten-frame reliable window and any plausible invisible-count cache. Veto: prohibit re-assignment of the pre-occlusion ID on re-emergence; assign a new identity because the object-permanence adjacency assumption [PAGE 2] fails at long separation. Prediction: ID-S remains at zero for this event (no false swap), but recall drops by the missed-duration penalty; net MOTA decreases relative to a naive re-identifier that guesses the old ID. Verifiable by synthetically extending occlusion frames in filmrole2 and comparing ID-S vs recall tradeoffs.

Scenario 3 — Illumination shift between reliable and detected frames. Counterfactual: the reliable frame is captured under full stadium floodlight; the detected frame occurs after a cloud shadow reduces mean intensity by ~30%. Veto: compute global histogram mean \(\mu_I\) for both frames; if \(|\Delta\mu_I| > 15\) intensity levels, invalidate Vollath autocorrelation and gradient energy matching and fall back to bounding-box geometry only. Prediction: without veto, sum1/sum2 ordering flips and the tracker issues a confident wrong ID (ID-S increases, precision drops); with veto, the tracker outputs “unknown identity,” preserving precision at the cost of recall. Verifiable by histogram-shifting ISSIA frames and measuring identity-retention accuracy before and after the histogram veto is applied.
