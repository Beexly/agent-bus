A counter-inference that ignores this clock mismatch will attribute between-subject differences to stable trait differences when they may simply reflect different phases of the oscillation process at the moment of measurement.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers govern whether an internal potential becomes an overt act.

Layer 0 — Antecedent / neural potential. sER is anchored to objectively observable antecedent conditions [PAGE 398]. If the antecedent configuration is absent or degraded, \(sER = 0\) (or undefined). Veto: no antecedent, no potential, no response, regardless of sOR or sEB.

Layer 1 — Oscillation gate. Even with \(sER > 0\), the continuous sOR process applies an absolute depressing action [PAGE 398]. The veto condition is \(sOR(t) \ge sER - \theta\), forcing \(sEB(t) \le \theta\) and suppressing the response. This is a hard, stochastic veto that operates moment-to-moment and is uncorrelated across different reaction potentials at a given instant (Postulate 10, p. 319, cited on [PAGE 398]).

Layer 2 — Biomechanical / effector execution. Hull's text confines itself to reaction potential and does not specify peripheral transmission, muscle fatigue, or effector damage. Nevertheless, a cross-layer veto proof requires that Layer 2 can veto Layer 1: an organism may have \(sEB(t) > \theta\) yet fail to respond because the neuromuscular or biomechanical effector cannot execute the act. In formal terms, response \(R(t) = \mathbb{I}[sEB(t) > \theta] \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{effector available}]\). If the second indicator is 0, the neural veto analysis is moot.

Environmental and weather factors enter only insofar as they alter the antecedent conditions that anchor sER [PAGE 398]. Extreme temperature, precipitation, or wind may change the observable antecedent configuration, thereby shifting sER; they do not appear in the source as direct modifiers of sOR. The text explicitly roots sOR in a "little-understood physiological process" rather than environmental input [PAGE 398], so treating weather as a direct sOR modulator would be an unsupported extension.

Veto adjudication order: Layer 0 (existence of sER) is checked first; if it fails, Layers 1–2 are irrelevant. If Layer 0 passes, Layer 1 (sOR threshold crossing) is checked next; if it fails, no act occurs even if the effector is ready. Layer 2 is checked last and can veto a fully prepared sEB. This hierarchy prevents misattribution: a failure to respond must be decomposed into antecedent absence, oscillation suppression, or effector failure before any conclusion about habit strength is drawn.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Oscillation saturation veto. Set \(sER\) high but finite, and posit a single-trial sOR draw from the upper tail such that \(sOR(t^{\ast}) \ge sER - \theta\). Prediction: \(R(t^{\ast}) = 0\) despite the habit being strong enough to evoke response under mean oscillation. Verification: record single-trial response latencies in a repeated-choice task; trials with zero response should cluster after periods of high physiological arousal (proxy for sOR excursion) and should not be predictable from sER alone.

Scenario 2 — Decorrelation veto. Present two alternative actions with equal \(sER\) at the same instant \(t\). By characteristic 4 [PAGE 398], their sOR realizations are uncorrelated. Prediction: the probability that exactly one alternative is evoked is strictly greater than zero, and the joint suppression probability equals the product of the marginal suppression probabilities (under independence or merely zero covariance with Gaussian assumption). Verification: use a simultaneous-go/no-go paradigm; observe that equal-strength alternatives do not fail in lockstep, rejecting a perfectly correlated global suppression model.

Scenario 3 — Pooling veto. Collect \(N\) repeated trials of the same action under identical antecedents. Prediction: the single-trial response variance is inflated by \(\sigma_{\text{OSC}}^{2}\), but the block-averaged response rate converges to the sER-determined rate with standard error \(\sigma_{\text{OSC}}/\sqrt{N}\). Verification: compare single-trial response variance to variance of trial-block means; a \(1/\sqrt{N}\) reduction in standard error confirms that oscillation is the noise source being averaged out, as the text claims is necessary for empirical laws to emerge [PAGE 398].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
