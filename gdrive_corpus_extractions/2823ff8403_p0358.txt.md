<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let each idea be represented by a feature vector x_i ∈ ℝ^d. Abstract characters are extracted by a projection φ: ℝ^d → ℝ^k with k ≪ d, where φ is learned through experience. Define the similarity operator between ideas i and j as the dimensionless Gaussian kernel
S_{ij} = exp( - || φ(x_i) - φ(x_j) ||_2^2 / (2 σ_s^2) ),  σ_s > 0 [feature units].
Define the contiguity operator as
C_{ij} = 1{ |t_i - t_j| < Δt } · exp( - |t_i - t_j| / τ_c ),  τ_c > 0 [seconds],
where Δt is the temporal contiguity window. The transition probability from idea i to j is
P(j | i) = ( β_c C_{ij} + β_s S_{ij} ) / Z_i,   Z_i = Σ_j ( β_c C_{ij} + β_s S_{ij} ),
with dimensionless non-negative weights β_c, β_s.

Brute regime (per James [PAGE 358]): β_s → 0, β_c > 0 ⇒ P(j|i) ∝ C_{ij}; associations are "almost exclusively by contiguity" [PAGE 358], and thoughts pass from "one concrete object to its habitual concrete successor" [PAGE 358]. Reasoned-thinking regime: β_s ≫ β_c ⇒ similarity drives transitions across non-contiguous pairs, enabling the "extricating of characters" [PAGE 358].

Breakdown and singularity regimes: (1) If φ is undefined because no abstract characters are attended to, S_{ij} is singular and the model collapses to pure contiguity. (2) If τ_c → 0 and Δt → 0, C_{ij} → 0 for j ≠ i, breaking the associative chain. (3) If Z_i = 0, the transition kernel is undefined. Units: probabilities are dimensionless; time is in seconds; feature distances are in abstract psychological units (a.p.u.). Citations and verbatim anchors: James attributes the genius–similarity link to Bain, referencing Bain's "Study of Character, chap. xv" and "Senses and Intellect" [PAGE 358]; he asks whether dogs would scatter on ice "for the first time together" [PAGE 358]; he describes tropic men "Recognizing cracking to be a sign of breaking, and seizing immediately the partial character that the point of rupture is the point of greatest" [PAGE 358].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage: The associationist tradition runs from Aristotle's law of contiguity through Hartley, Hume, the Mills, and Alexander Bain. James explicitly invokes Bain's "Study of Character, chap. xv; also Senses and Intellect, Intellect, chap. I, the latter half" [PAGE 358] to establish that genius tracks association by similarity. He then imports Darwin's Descent of Man and Dr. Hayes's Arctic dog observations [PAGE 358] to test whether animal sagacity reduces to contiguity. Forward descendants include Thorndike's law of effect (contiguity + consequence), Pavlovian conditioning (CS–US temporal contiguity), Tolman's cognitive maps (similarity-based inference), Tversky's contrast model of similarity, and contemporary hierarchical predictive processing, in which abstraction is the extraction of invariant partial characters across contexts.

Multi-week traps: In longitudinal animal-cognition studies, a subject that solves a novel problem in week 4 may appear to reason by similarity, yet the behavior can be the terminal link of a contiguity chain reinforced across weeks 1–3. The trap is attributing week-4 similarity abstraction to a mechanism that was actually built by week-1 to week-3 contiguous reinforcement. A second trap is the "naive subject" contamination: testing an animal on ice for the "first time" [PAGE 358] after weeks of handling by experimenters means the subject is not naive to the experimental context, so contiguous associations with the experimenter, apparatus, or acoustic setup may already exist. A third trap is retrospective reinterpretation: observing scattering after cracking and labeling it "reason" ignores that the same motor pattern could have been reinforced by prior contiguous crack–break sequences. These traps require within-subject counterbalancing and pre-training controls that James's anecdotal citation does not provide.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under time pressure or physiological stress, an agent's available decision time t_dec falls below the time τ_φ required to compute the abstract projection φ(x). When t_dec < τ_φ, the similarity channel is gated offline and β_s collapses toward zero; the system reverts to β_c-dominated contiguous association—the "brute-like" mode James describes [PAGE 358]. This is habit regression under crisis: the mind falls back on the habitual concrete successor rather than extricating abstract characters.

Counter-trap: Extensive similarity training under deadline compression can shorten τ_φ, but it creates a counter-trap. The agent becomes prone to over-generalized similarity, applying abstract characters to situations where only local contiguity is valid. For example, a dog trained to scatter on any cracking sound may scatter on benign thermal cracking that poses no rupture risk—a false positive of similarity-based reasoning mistaken for insight.

Clock: The physical clock imposes a hard upper bound. If ice rupture occurs at t_rupture and the animal's earliest detectable motor response latency is t_response, then any cognitive attribution requires t_response < t_rupture − t_detection, where t_detection is the latency of transducing the cracking stimulus. If the available interval is shorter than the minimum time for multi-synaptic abstract inference (on the order of hundreds of milliseconds in mammals), the "reasoning" explanation is temporally impossible, and only contiguous reflex or fixed action pattern remains. James's question—whether dogs would scatter on first ice exposure [PAGE 358]—is therefore a clock test: if latency is too short for abstraction, the behavior cannot be reasoned sign-recognition.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Ice fracture is governed by linear elastic fracture mechanics. The stress intensity factor K_I = Y σ √(π a) must exceed the fracture toughness K_IC for crack propagation. Ice thickness h, flexural stress σ from sledge loading, and temperature gradient ∂T/∂z determine whether cracking precedes rupture. If h < h_crit, catastrophic failure is physically inevitable regardless of any cognitive state.

Biomechanical layer: A sledge team distributes load across multiple contact points. Scattering reduces local pressure and keeps the system center of mass within the support polygon. This load redistribution can be produced by a local postural reflex to tilt or vibration without any abstract representation of "breaking."

Weather layer: Rapid cooling generates thermal stress σ_thermal = E α ΔT. When σ_mechanical + σ_thermal ≥ σ_fracture, cracking occurs deterministically. The animal's detection of acoustic or vibratory cues is downstream of this physical threshold.

Veto proof: Attributing the dogs' scattering to "Recognizing cracking to be a sign of breaking" [PAGE 358] is vetoed if the response latency falls within the spinal/reflex arc (< 200 ms) or if the stimulus is transduced by mechanoreceptors before cortical abstraction can occur. Formal veto: let t_min_abstract be the minimum latency for trans-synaptic abstract inference. If t_response < t_min_abstract, then the similarity-based "sign recognition" explanation is physically vetoed; only contiguity or reflex survives. Because James's anecdote provides no latency measurements, the reasoning attribution is unverified and potentially vetoed by the physical layer.
