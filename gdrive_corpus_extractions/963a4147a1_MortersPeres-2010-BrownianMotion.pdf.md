All equations below use the book's normalization (generator $\tfrac12\Delta$), so $[B]=\text{length}$, $[t]=\text{length}^2$; the invariant $x\sim\sqrt t$ is Brownian scaling [PAGE 22].

- Lévy construction [PAGE 19]: $B(d)=\tfrac12[B(d-2^{-n})+B(d+2^{-n})]+2^{-(n+1)/2}Z_d$, with $\mathbb E[B(t+h)-B(t)]=0$, $\mathrm{Var}=h$.
- Scaling/time-inversion [PAGE 22–23]: $a^{-1}B(a^2t)\stackrel{d}{=}B(t)$, $\;tB(1/t)\stackrel{d}{=}B(t)$.
- Lévy modulus [PAGE 26]: $\displaystyle\limsup_{h\downarrow0}\sup_{0\le t\le 1-h}\frac{|B(t+h)-B(t)|}{\sqrt{2h\log(1/h)}}=1$ (a.s.). Units: numerator and $\sqrt h$ both $\sim$ length.
- Quadratic variation [PAGE 32]: for nested partitions with mesh $\to0$, $\sum (B(t_j^{(n)})-B(t_{j-1}^{(n)}))^2\to t$ a.s.; breakdown: for arbitrary partitions $\limsup=+\infty$ [Remark 1.36, PAGE 33].
- Cameron–Martin density [PAGE 37]: $\dfrac{dL_F}{dL_0}(B)=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\int_0^t F'(s)^2\,ds+\int_0^t F'\,dB\big)$. Regime: $L_F\!\perp\!L_0$ (singular) iff $F\notin D[0,1]$, i.e. $\int_0^1 F'^2=\infty$; equivalent iff finite Dirichlet energy.
- Reflection / maximum law [PAGE 55]: $\mathbb P_0\{M(t)>a\}=\mathbb P_0\{|B(t)|>a\}$, giving $\mathbb P_0\{M(t)>a\}\le \tfrac{\sqrt{2t}}{a\sqrt\pi}e^{-a^2/2t}$.
- Wald II [PAGE 66]: $\mathbb E[B(T)^2]=\mathbb E[T]$ for $\mathbb E[T]<\infty$; exit of $[a,b]$: $\mathbb E[T]=|a|b$ [PAGE 67].
- Stable-$\tfrac12$ subordinator [PAGE 61]: hitting-time transition density $p(a,t,s)=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi(s-t)^3}}\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{a^2}{2(s-t)}\big)\mathbf 1_{\{s>t\}}$.
- Cauchy process from planar BM [PAGE 62]: $p(a,x,A)=\dfrac1\pi\int_A \dfrac{a}{a^2+(x-y)^2}\,dy$.
- Annulus exit (harmonic $|x|^{2-d},\log|x|$) [PAGE 82]: $\mathbb P_x\{T_r<T_R\}=\dfrac{R^{2-d}-|x|^{2-d}}{R^{2-d}-r^{2-d}}$ ($d\ge3$), $\dfrac{\log R-\log|x|}{\log R-\log r}$ ($d=2$); transience threshold: $\mathbb P_x\{T_r<\infty\}=(|x|/r)^{2-d}$, $d\ge3$ [Cor 3.19].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage fixes who proved what, when — a common source of misattribution. Bachelier [Ba00] modeled stock fluctuations first; the physical phenomenon is Brown [Br28], explained by Einstein [Ei05]; the *first rigorous construction* is Wiener [Wi23] ("in his honour Brownian motion is sometimes called the Wiener process" [PAGE 33]). Lévy's interpolation is a *construction of Wiener's process*, not a prior object — a frequent retro-causal error is to date "Lévy's construction" before Wiener's existence proof. Paul Lévy owns the modulus of continuity [Le37, PAGE 26], local time [Le48], and the $M-B$ identity [Le48, PAGE 60]. Nowhere differentiability is Paley–Wiener–Zygmund [PWZ33], with the modern proof due to Dvoretzky–Erdős–Kakutani [DEK61, PAGE 34]. Recurrence of random walk is Pólya [Po21]; its BM extension Lévy [Le40] and Kakutani [Ka44]; the probabilistic Dirichlet solution and harmonic measure are Kakutani [Ka44a,b, PAGE 94]. Dimension theory rests on Hausdorff [Ha19], Frostman [Fr35], with graph/range dimensions by Taylor [Ta53] and Lévy [Le51], McKean's image theorem [McK55], Kaufman's simultaneous doubling [Ka69]. Local time's continuity is Trotter [Tr58]; Ray–Knight [1963] independently by Ray and Knight; the Hausdorff-measure representation is Taylor–Wendel [TW66] with the sharp constant by Perkins [Pe81]. Skorokhod embedding [Sk65], Donsker [Do51], Strassen [St64], and Pitman's $2M-B$ [Pi75] complete the transfer machinery; Spitzer's winding law is [Sp58]; the SLE appendix (Schramm–Werner) closes the modern arc [PAGE 5]. Multi-week trap: believing the arcsine "second law" predates the "first," or that Donsker's principle is a corollary of the CLT rather than its functional lift.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A "veto" here is a rigorous impossibility proof; each depends on stated physical/analytic hypotheses.
1. Point-polarity veto (planar): points are polar [Cor 2.26, PAGE 57], so the punctured-disc Dirichlet problem *fails*: with $\phi(0)\ne v(0)$, $u(0)=v(0)\ne\phi(0)$ because planar BM never hits $\{0\}$ [Example 3.15, PAGE 80]. Hypothesis: continuity + $d=2$; the Poincaré cone condition is violated at the puncture.
2. Differentiability veto: for every fixed $t$, a.s. nondifferentiable (Thm 1.27); simultaneously for all $t$, a.s. nowhere differentiable with $D^*B\in\{\pm\infty\}$ (Thm 1.30) via $\sum_{k}\mathbb P(\Omega_{n,k})\le (7M)^3 2^{-n/2}<\infty$ and Borel–Cantelli [PAGE 31]. Hypothesis: continuity of paths and independent Gaussian increments; the veto of "$\alpha$-Hölder for $\alpha>1/2$" is the same mechanism (Remark 1.31).
3. Recurrence veto in high $d$: transience for $d\ge3$ from $\mathbb P_x(A_n^c)=(1/n^2)^{d-2}$ summable $\Rightarrow$ finitely many returns a.s. [PAGE 83]. Breakdown at the critical dimension $d=2$ (neighbourhood-recurrent, not point-recurrent, Thm 3.20).
4. Martingale veto: $\mathbb E[|B(t)|^{2-d}]\to0$ ($d\ge3$) contradicts the constant-expectation a martingale would require [Example 7.19, PAGE 211]. Hypothesis: the localizing integrability $\mathbb E\int_0^{t\wedge T}|\nabla f|^2<\infty$ is *absent*.
All vetoes lean on the strong Markov property [PAGE 53] and Brownian scaling [PAGE 22]; remove either and the veto proof collapses.
