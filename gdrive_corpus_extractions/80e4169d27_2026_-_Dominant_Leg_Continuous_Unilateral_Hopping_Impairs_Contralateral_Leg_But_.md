Biomechanical layer: the dominant leg was identified by ball-kicking preference (16/18 right-leg dominant); arm dominance was writing hand (17/18 right-arm dominant). The rebound push-up test diverged by sex: males standard (feet), females modified (knees), altering relative load from ~64–66% to ~49–53% of body mass (Ebben et al., 2011; Gouvali & Boudolos, 2005). This is a hard veto on direct sex-comparative inference for upper-body power; the authors pooled sexes because n_female = 5 precluded adequate power, and Table 3 shows only trivial-to-small raw differences, but the modified-versus-standard technique change remains an unmeasured biomechanical confound.

Measurement layer: the Chronojump contact mat estimates power indirectly from flight-time assumptions rather than direct force-plate measurement. A veto applies to absolute Watt values; relative within-subject pre/post changes are the only defensible inference. Contact time was unchanged (p > 0.05), which strengthens the interpretation that the observed deficits are central (force-expression) rather than mechanical (contact-strategy), but only if mat calibration and foot-placement consistency were held constant—procedural standardization was enforced by a single investigator and verbal encouragement every 2 seconds during testing.

Physiological layer: no direct fatigue or neurophysiological measures were obtained during the 60-s continuous hopping intervention; fatigue was inferred from post-intervention remarks and visual inspection. A veto applies to any definitive claim about peripheral accumulation (metabolites, H+ release) versus central drive; the discussion’s central-mechanism inference is plausible but not empirically verified by this dataset. Upper-body loading was estimated via a plank-on-scale hand pressure reading, not actual segmental mass, vetoing precise upper-body loading normalization.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 (Structure substitution): Replace the continuous 60-s dominant-leg hopping bout with the intermittent structure (2 × 30 s, 10 s rest) while keeping all other procedures identical. Counterfactual claim: if the temporal structure is the causal factor, the Session × Time interaction for contralateral hop height, RSI, and power should vanish (p > 0.05 at all post-tests), and no significant difference between intermittent and control should emerge. Prediction: replicate the study’s null finding for intermittent hopping—no contralateral impairment at 1, 5, or 10 min—thereby vetoing a generic "dominant-leg hopping causes crossover fatigue" claim and isolating continuous duration as the necessary condition.

Scenario 2 (Temporal extension): Extend post-intervention assessment to 15 min and 20 min after the continuous session. Counterfactual claim: if the effect is transient and predominantly central, contralateral hop height and power should return to pre-test baseline by 10 min and remain equivalent at 15 and 20 min (paired comparisons vs. pre, p > 0.05). Prediction: significant suppression at 1 and 5 min (as observed), non-significant difference from pre at 10, 15, and 20 min. A persistent deficit beyond 10 min would veto the transient-central mechanism and suggest structural/metabolic crossover.

Scenario 3 (Neurophysiological add-on): Add twitch-interpolation voluntary activation and surface EMG of the contralateral vastus lateralis during the hop test at 1 min post-continuous hopping. Counterfactual claim: if contact time is unchanged but power/height are depressed, central motor drive (voluntary activation %) should be significantly reduced at 1 min versus pre (p < 0.05), while peripheral contractile properties remain intact. Prediction: reduced voluntary activation and/or altered EMG amplitude coinciding with lower hop height/power; if voluntary activation is unchanged, the central-veto fails and a peripheral or measurement artifact must be invoked.
