Formal operators and units. Let X ∈ {L, R} denote which spark appeared first, and let Y ∈ {L, R} be the observer’s binary judgment. Let Δt be the inter-stimulus interval in seconds. Let M ∈ {L→R, R→L} denote apparent-motion direction. Spatial binding holds when ‖x₂ − x₁‖₂ < 2r_irr, where r_irr is the irradiation-circle radius.

Empirical boundaries from [PAGE 1]:
Δt_TOJ ≈ 44 ms (temporal-order judgment collapses to chance)
Δt_AM ≈ 15 ms (apparent-motion direction remains above chance under spatial binding)

Psychometric model for temporal order:
P(Y = X | Δt) = ½ [ 1 + erf( (Δt − μ_t) / (√2 σ_t) ) ]
where μ_t is the point of subjective equality and σ_t is temporal jitter. At Δt = 44 ms, P ≈ 0.5, hence mutual information I(X; Y) → 0 bits.

Psychometric model for apparent motion, conditioned on spatial binding:
P(ŝ_M = M | Δt, ‖x₂ − x₁‖₂ < 2r_irr) = ½ [ 1 + erf( (Δt − μ_m) / (√2 σ_m) ) ]
with μ_m ≈ 15 ms.

Mutual information (units: bits):
I(X; Y) = Σ_{x,y} p(x, y) log₂ [ p(x, y) / (p(x) p(y)) ]

Breakdown and singularity regimes:
1. Δt → 0: sparks are simultaneous; X is undefined and I(X; Y) is void.
2. ‖x₂ − x₁‖₂ ≫ 2r_irr: irradiation circles do not overlap; the percept splits into two discrete flashes, M is undefined, and I(M; ŝ_M) → 0.
3. If spark duration τ_spark ≥ Δt, the two stimuli are not physically separable and the temporal-order premise is void.

Assumptions: (1) “0.044"” and “0.015"” denote seconds; (2) binary judgments are unbiased by motor or response criteria; (3) irradiation circles are circularly symmetric and stationary during the flash pair; (4) the observer is a stationary binary channel across the trial block.
Units: time [s], probability [dimensionless], information [bits], spatial extent [m] (r_irr and d not numerically specified in [PAGE 1]).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from Exner (Sitzungsberichte der k. Akad. Wien, Bd. Lxxi., Abth. 8, 1875) and Vierordt (Zeitschrift für Biologie, xii. 226, 1876) to James’s 1890 synthesis. Exner supplies the quantitative temporal dissociation (44 ms order vs. 15 ms motion); Vierordt supplies the referential illusions that motion is computed relative to an external frame and can invert when that frame is destabilized. James combines them into the claim that movement is a “primitive form of sensibility,” not derived from position or succession.

Scientific successors include Helmholtz’s unconscious inference (spatial perception as inference from ambiguous cues), Mach’s work on contrast and movement perception, Wertheimer’s phi phenomenon (1912), Kolers’ apparent-motion experiments (1972), and the modern neurophysiology of direction-selective cortical areas (MT/V5) and Reichardt correlation detectors / Adelson–Bergen motion-energy models. The 15 ms versus 44 ms dissociation anticipates a division between fast, spatially bound motion computation and slower, explicit temporal-order comparison.

Multi-week traps. If this psychometric mapping were repeated longitudinally, three confounds would accumulate. First, temporal recalibration: sustained exposure to a skewed distribution of asynchronies shifts the PSE μ_t over days to weeks, so the 44 ms boundary would drift without any change in sensory resolution. Second, criterion drift: signal-detection bias (β or λ) can move P(Y = X) away from 0.5 while sensitivity (d′) stays constant, masquerading as threshold change. Third, physiological drift: pupil diameter and ocular media alter r_irr, sporadically violating d < 2r_irr and converting apparent motion into successive flashes. A multi-week protocol must therefore re-measure r_irr, τ_spark, and the observer’s criterion daily; otherwise the claimed double dissociation between motion and order becomes confounded by decisional and environmental drift.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the observer as a recursive agent maintaining two competing hypotheses: H_pos (judgment based on static positions) and H_mov (judgment based on a motion primitive). Under cognitive load, time pressure, or stimulus ambiguity—a “crisis” state—the agent regresses to the habitual H_pos, because spatial localization is the default, evolutionarily older code. But H_pos cannot resolve Δt < 44 ms, so under crisis the observer reports chance-level temporal order at 20 ms and cannot report motion direction at 15 ms.

Counter-trap. The experimenter presents spatially overlapping sparks at Δt = 15 ms. An observer regressed to H_pos expects two discrete positions and will either report “two flashes” or guess direction randomly. An observer using H_mov reports a single moving spark and identifies direction correctly. Because H_pos cannot generate the motion percept under these parameters, the counter-trap isolates H_mov: correct direction reports at 15 ms with overlapping irradiation circles imply active use of the motion primitive.

Clock. The internal temporal comparator for explicit order has resolution τ_clock ≈ 44 ms (variable X), whereas the motion detector integrates over a shorter window τ_mov ≈ 15 ms (variable M). Formally, the observer selects:
ŝ_H = argmax_{H ∈ {H_pos, H_mov}} p(D | H) p(H | C)
where C is crisis/context and p(H_pos | C) increases under load. Reaction time decomposes as:
RT = τ_enc + τ_dec(H) + τ_mot
with τ_dec(H_mov) < τ_dec(H_pos) when Δt < 44 ms, because the motion channel bypasses the slow order comparator. The “clock” thus comprises at least two distinct temporal resolutions, and crisis habit regression forces the system back onto the slower, less granular one.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three veto layers determine whether the psychological inference on [PAGE 1] survives physical, biomechanical, and environmental scrutiny.

Physical (stimulus generation). Electric sparks have finite rise and decay times. Define V_phys = 𝟙[τ_spark < 15 ms]. If τ_spark ≥ 15 ms, the two flashes temporally overlap and Δt is not well-defined; the inference of a 15 ms apparent-motion threshold is vetoed because the stimuli are not physically separable.

Biomechanical (retina and skin). Retinal irradiation circles depend on pupil diameter and ocular optics; skin two-point perception depends on mechanoreceptor afferent spacing (SA1, FA1, Pacinian) and uniform contact pressure. Define V_bio = 𝟙[‖x₂ − x₁‖₂ < 2r_irr ∧ uniform contact pressure]. Pupil drift changes r_irr; non-uniform pressure on the skin creates artifactual spatial gradients. If either fails, spatial binding collapses and the apparent-motion condition is vetoed.

Weather/environment. Indoor luminance and temperature affect pupil size and spark conductivity. Define V_env = 𝟙[luminance stable ∧ temperature stable]. Humidity or temperature drift that increases spark jitter σ_spark above roughly 5 ms blurs the 15 ms boundary and vetoes the precise temporal claim.

Composite veto: V = V_phys · V_bio · V_env. If V = 0, the conclusion that movement is a primitive sensibility with 15 ms resolution cannot be sustained from [PAGE 1] alone; the result is limited to the specific, tightly controlled conditions Exner and Vierordt described.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual scenarios, each with a veto condition and a prediction.

Scenario 1: Controlled flash duration. Replace electric sparks with LEDs of τ_flash = 5 ms. Present two spatially overlapping flashes at Δt = 20 ms (>15 ms, <44 ms). Veto: if temporal order is above chance (I(X; Y) > 0 bits), the 44 ms barrier is not absolute. Prediction: order remains at chance (P ≈ 0.5, I ≈ 0 bits), while apparent-motion direction is reported correctly (P > 0.75), verifying the motion/order dissociation.

Scenario 2: No spatial overlap. Separate the two sparks by d > 2r_irr at Δt = 15 ms. Veto: if apparent-motion direction is still reported above chance, spatial binding is not necessary for the 15 ms threshold. Prediction: the observer reports two successive flashes with no motion (I(M; ŝ_M) → 0 bits), vetoing the claim that irradiation-circle overlap is required for sub-44 ms motion perception.

Scenario 3: Temporal recalibration. Adapt the observer for 30 minutes to a constant 40 ms asynchrony. Veto: if both temporal-order and apparent-motion thresholds shift equally, a single clock underlies both. Prediction: the temporal-order PSE μ_t shifts toward 40 ms, while the apparent-motion threshold μ_m remains ≈ 15 ms. This double dissociation confirms distinct primitives; if μ_m also shifts to ~30 ms, the primitive-motion claim is vetoed.

All scenarios are verifiable by recording binary judgments and estimating mutual information I(X; Y) and I(M; ŝ_M) in bits.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
