L0 offense: runs the habitual “smash into the center of the line” because it is cognitively available under pressure. L1 defense: anticipates L0 and stacks the interior (Yale’s error versus Harvard). L2 offense: anticipates L1 and deploys the Harvard-type play that “offered relatively little chance of a successful opposition” even when anticipated. L3 defense: anticipates L2 and widens the front to squeeze outside lanes. Crisis habit regression occurs when τ is low, Δ is unfavorable, or possession is rare: working memory narrows and the offense regresses from L2 back to L0, because the habitual smash feels “safe” under cognitive tunneling. The formal regression trigger can be written as p_reg = σ(α·(τ_threshold − τ) + β·(−Δ) + γ·(possessions_remaining)), where σ is the logistic function, τ in s, Δ in pts; when p_reg > 0.5 the offense selects the habit action with probability > 0.5.

Counter-trap. Because the defense expects L0 regression in crisis moments, the offense can run the counter-habit: the outside/Harvard play exactly when the defense stacks for the smash. The counter-trap payoff is highest precisely at moments of maximal defensive certainty (q → 1 toward smash), because the defense’s anticipation becomes its own exploitability. Clock interaction: the play clock (40 s / 25 s depending on code) and game clock compress decision time; as τ → 0, the offense’s ability to execute a “wider repertory” declines because substitution and signaling consume τ. Thus the text’s field-position gradient ∂Φ/∂x > 0 is multiplied by a clock-feasibility factor κ(τ, personnel) ∈ [0,1]; if κ → 0 late in the half, the wider repertory is unrealizable regardless of x.

Breakdown regime: when the offense’s non-habit play is physically vetoed (see CROSS_LAYER), recursion collapses from level-k game theory to brute-force interior attack, and the defense, anticipating this, maximizes run-stopping payoff. The “safe play” then becomes unsafe not because of field position but because of predictable execution failure.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Strategic preference from [PAGE 148]—“a wider repertory near the opposing goal”—is a soft recommendation subject to hard physical and biomechanical vetoes. The adjudication hierarchy is: (1) physical law, (2) biomechanical feasibility, (3) weather/environmental constraint, (4) strategic game-theoretic preference. If any lower layer vetoes an action, it is removed from A(s) before the Bellman maximization.

Physical veto (interior smash). A center smash requires the offensive line to produce a ground-reaction impulse J = ∫₀^Δt F(t) dt exceeding the impulse needed to move the ball carrier backward against defensive resistance: J_required = m_bc · Δv_required + F_defense · Δt, with J in newton-seconds (N·s), mass m_bc in kilograms (kg), Δv in m/s. If the offensive line’s maximal sustainable force F_OL,max yields F_OL,max · Δt < J_required, the smash is physically infeasible and vetoed, independent of its strategic safety.

Biomechanical veto (outside run / cut). A lateral cut outside the tackles requires coefficient of friction μ between shoe and turf sufficient for centripetal demand: μ ≥ v² / (r · g), where v is ball-carrier speed in m/s, r is cut radius in m, g = 9.81 m/s². On wet or muddy turf, μ drops; to maintain the cut, r must increase, slowing the play and allowing pursuit. If measured μ < μ_crit for the required r, outside runs are vetoed and A(s) collapses toward interior actions.

Weather veto. Precipitation rate P > 0.1 in/hr or standing water increases observation noise Σ in defensive play recognition and reduces ball security; complex timing plays (option pitches, reverses, quick passes) become unreliable. Wind speed w > 20 mph increases angular error in passing σ_angle(w), degrading pass accuracy. If σ_angle(w) exceeds the throw window for a given route, the pass action is vetoed.

Adjudication result. Define feasible set F(s) = { a ∈ A(s) | physical(a) ∧ biomechanical(a) ∧ weather(a) }. Roper’s “wider repertory” holds only if |F_near_goal| > |F_midfield|. In cold, wet, or muddy conditions inside the opponent 20 yd, F often narrows to {interior smash}, precisely inverting the text’s prescription. The veto is verifiable: play-by-play and player-tracking data can show run-direction splits and EPA by weather condition, testing whether the strategic layer or the physical layer governs actual calls.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a veto condition, the overridden action from [PAGE 148], and a testable prediction.

Scenario 1 — Precipitation / turf-friction veto. Condition: game-time precipitation rate P > 0.1 in/hr or field μ measured below critical threshold for 45° cuts. Vetoed action: outside running plays and cutback runs near the goal line (part of Roper’s “wider repertory”). Forced action: interior center smash. Verifiable prediction: Inside the opponent 20-yard line in precipitation games, run-play share between the tackles increases by at least 15 percentage points versus dry-weather baseline, and EPA per outside run falls below EPA per interior run by more than 0.3 pts. Data source: play-by-play with weather fields and Next Gen Stats run direction.

Scenario 2 — Goal-line defensive-front / pass-protection veto. Condition: defense deploys goal-line personnel with ≥6 defensive linemen and offensive-line pass-block win rate < 60% (verifiable tracking metric). Vetoed action: play-action pass and spread passing concepts from inside the 10-yard line. Forced action: interior run. Verifiable prediction: Against goal-line fronts, passes from inside the 10 yield negative EPA (≤ −0.5 pts per attempt) and sack probability > 10%; the offense’s optimal action under the Bellman equation shifts to interior run despite Roper’s preference for variety. Data source: PFF pass-block win rate and EPA by defensive front.

Scenario 3 — Clock-scarcity / cognitive-tunneling veto. Condition: trailing by Δ ≤ −8 points with τ < 120 s remaining in the half or game. Vetoed action: conservative “safe” plays predicated on field-position safety near midfield; forced action: high-variance deep passes (>20 air yards) even from x ≈ 40–50 yd. Verifiable prediction: In the final two minutes trailing, deep-pass share from own 40–50 yard line increases by at least 20 percentage points versus early-game baseline, and win-probability added per play is higher for high-variance deep passes than for conservative short passes. Data source: play-by-play air yards and win-probability model outputs.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
