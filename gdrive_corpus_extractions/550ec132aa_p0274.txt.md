1. Mid-parent / filial regression operator (pointers: "Filial relation, 19 ; regrussion, 95" and "regression in, 108", [PAGE 274]):
\[
\mathbb{E}[X_o \mid M] = \mu_o + \beta (M - \mu_M), \qquad \beta = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_o,M)}{\operatorname{Var}(M)}
\]
Units: [X_o] = L (length, e.g. metres for stature), [M] = L, [β] = dimensionless. Assumptions: bivariate normality of (X_o, M), no assortative mating, independent residuals. Breakdown/singularity: Var(M) = 0 collapses the operator; Cov(X_o,M) = 0 yields β = 0 (no regression information); heavy-tailed deviations invalidate the moment-based β (cf. "cause of deviations, 55", [PAGE 274]).

2. Ancestral contribution operator (pointer: "Ancestral contributions, 134", [PAGE 274]):
\[
\mathbb{E}[X_o \mid \mathbf{A}] = \sum_{g=1}^{G} w_g \bar{A}^{(g)} + \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\epsilon^2)
\]
Units: [w_g] = dimensionless, [\bar{A}^{(g)}] = L, [σ_ε] = L. Assumptions: additive contributions across generations, independent ancestral channels, finite second moment. Breakdown: epistasis or dominance violates linearity; [PAGE 274] provides no w_g values, so the model is under-identified from this artifact alone.

3. Fraternity-as-unit operator (pointers: "to be treated as units, 35" and "regression in, 108", [PAGE 274]):
For fraternity f with n_f siblings,
\[
\bar{X}_f = \frac{1}{n_f}\sum_{i=1}^{n_f} X_{fi}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{f,t+1} \mid \bar{X}_{f,t}] = \mu + \lambda (\bar{X}_{f,t} - \mu)
\]
Units: [\bar{X}_f] = L, [λ] = dimensionless. Assumptions: siblings exchangeable conditional on fraternity mean, n_f large enough for CLT regularity. Breakdown: n_f small (n_f < 5) invalidates Gaussian approximation of \bar{X}_f; shared household environment induces correlated errors, violating independence.

4. Error-law / normal frequency density (pointers: "Curve of Frequency, 40, 49", "of Distribution, 40, 54", "Error... law of, 55", [PAGE 274]):
\[
p(\epsilon) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma} \exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad [\sigma] = L,\; [p] = L^{-1}
\]
Assumptions: deviations are additive, symmetric, and possess finite variance. Breakdown: kurtosis ≠ 3 or skew ≠ 0 (cf. "schemes of deviations, 51, 60", [PAGE 274]) invalidates the normal density; "probable error, 57" implies a specific quantile functional that requires the same regularity.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The index on [PAGE 274] traces a historical lineage from Galton's biometric program to modern quantitative genetics. "Ancestral contributions, 134" and "Contributions from separate ancestors, 134" are the conceptual ancestors of Fisher's (1918) infinitesimal model and the additive genetic relationship matrix; "Filial relation, 19" prefigures parent-offspring regression, which Pearson later formalized as the correlation coefficient. The entry "regression in, 108" (fraternities) establishes that Galton's centripetal operator was defined on group means before it was defined on individuals, a fact often lost in retrospective accounts that treat regression as purely an individual-level phenomenon.

Multi-week trap one: In a longitudinal anthropometric panel with weekly measurements, selecting the tallest 5% of fraternities at week 1 and re-measuring at week 4 guarantees a centripetal shift toward the grand mean regardless of any intervention. Because [PAGE 274] indexes "regression in [fraternities], 108" as a standing property of sibships, an observer who attributes the week-4 decline to a treatment, diet, or climatic exposure commits a post hoc regression fallacy. The trap is "retrocausal" in the sense that the extreme week-1 value (the effect) is used to infer a cause, when the week-4 value is statistically determined by the week-1 extremity itself.

Multi-week trap two: "Blends... in hazel eyes, 145" and "blends... have no effect on statistical results, 17" create a dual trap. If a researcher treats hazel eyes as a stable ontological blend across weeks, they will model eye-color inheritance as a fixed mixing proportion. If they instead treat it as a statistical mixture, the weekly variance remains unchanged by blending. Confusing these two registers produces spurious "stability" or "change" claims in multi-week pedigree tracking. The index warns that the statistical operator is blind to ontological blend status; the researcher must specify the register before interpreting longitudinal eye-color data.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression is the within-lifetime analogue of Galtonian filial regression. Let π_t denote a behavioral policy under acute stress at time t, with crisis peak π_peak at t_c and baseline habit π_0. A formal reversion model is:
\[
\pi_t = \pi_0 + (\pi_{\text{peak}} - \pi_0) e^{-(t - t_c)/\tau}, \qquad t \ge t_c
\]
Units: [π] = dimensionless policy simplex coordinate, [τ] = time (weeks). This is structurally identical to the fraternity regression operator on [PAGE 274]: an extreme state (π_peak) is pulled toward a population or individual baseline (π_0) with a characteristic time constant τ.

Counter-trap: An observer who expects crisis habit regression may apply a corrective intervention during the crisis window [t_c, t_c + τ] and then attribute the natural exponential reversion to the intervention. Because the index on [PAGE 274] establishes regression as a property of the relational operator ("Filial relation, 19"), not of the agent's will, the counter-trap is a category error: the observer confuses a statistical centripetal tendency with causal efficacy.

Clock: The recursive theory of mind must track the mismatch between the observation window T and the reversion constant τ. If T < τ, the agent appears "stuck" in crisis and the regression is unobserved; if T ≫ τ, the intervention appears potent because it coincides with completed reversion. Galton's "regression in fraternities, 108" implies that the same clock logic applies at the group level: a crisis-shifted fraternity mean reverts on its own τ, and any weekly counter-measure applied during that window is confounded with the statistical clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers must independently pass before any regression claim indexed on [PAGE 274] is admitted.

Physical / measurement layer: Galton's anthropometric apparatus (pointer: "anthrop. laboratory data, 43, 46, 79", [PAGE 274]) has finite resolution Δx. The frequency curve (pointer: p. 49, [PAGE 274]) can be resolved only if Δx < σ/5, where σ is the error scale from the law of error. Veto condition: if Δx ≥ σ/5, the observed distribution is aliased and the regression slope β is not identifiable.

Biomechanical layer: Stature regression is a population-level centripetal operator, not an individual growth law. Epiphyseal plate closure imposes an absorbing boundary at approximately age 18–25 years. Veto condition: regression forecasts for post-closure individuals are invalid; the operator applies only to pre-closure growth strata. This is a hard physical veto because no statistical centripetal force can reopen closed growth plates.

Weather / environment layer: Diurnal spinal compression and ambient temperature alter stature by roughly 1–2 cm. Veto condition: anthropometric comparisons are invalid unless measurements are time-of-day normalized and climate-controlled; otherwise the "law of error" (p. 55, [PAGE 274]) absorbs weather variance into σ, inflating the error scale and attenuating β.

Adjudication rule: A statistical claim survives only if all three layers pass. If any layer vetoes, the index-referenced regression claim ("regression, 95"; "regression in fraternities, 108", [PAGE 274]) is invalid for that case, regardless of the apparent fit of the frequency curve.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions. RLVR here denotes reward/validation from verifiable longitudinal outcomes.

Scenario 1 — Fraternity selection counterfactual. Select the top 5% of fraternities by mean stature at t_0. Prediction from the fraternity operator ([PAGE 274], p. 108):
\[
\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{t_1} \mid \text{selected}] = \mu + \rho (\bar{X}_{t_0} - \mu), \qquad 0 < \rho < 1
\]
Verification: weekly anthropometric log; compute \bar{X}_{t_1} for selected fraternities. Veto: if \rho ≥ 1 (no centripetal regression) or \rho ≤ 0 (reversal away from mean), the Galtonian regression operator is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Blending cross counterfactual. Cross parents with intermediate hazel phenotypes (pointer: "hazel eyes, 145", [PAGE 274]). Prediction: offspring eye-color variance Var(X_o) > 0 and not equal to the parental blend variance; segregation produces outliers beyond the parental range. Verification: pedigree eye-color records across siblings. Veto: if offspring are phenotypically identical to the mid-parent blend with zero variance, the index claim "blends... have no effect on statistical results" (p. 17, [PAGE 274]) is contradicted at the micro-level and the blending-stability model is vetoed.

Scenario 3 — Ancestral information counterfactual. Condition offspring prediction on mid-parent M alone versus mid-parent plus grandparental means G (pointer: "Ancestral contributions, 134", [PAGE 274]). Prediction: if ancestral contributions are non-redundant,
\[
\mathbb{E}[X_o \mid M, G] - \mathbb{E}[X_o \mid M] \neq 0
\]
Verification: nested regression on multi-generational pedigree data; grandparental terms must add out-of-sample predictive information. Veto: if grandparental terms add zero predictive information conditional on M, the additive ancestral contribution model is vetoed and the filial operator reduces to a mid-parent-only regression.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
