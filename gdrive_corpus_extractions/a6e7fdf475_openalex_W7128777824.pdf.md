Formal operators, units, and exact page citations.

1. Projection geometry and calibration estimation [PAGE 3].
The camera operates under the standard full-perspective projection model. Initial intrinsic and extrinsic parameters are computed analytically without iteration. Nonlinear refinement maximizes a likelihood function over 3D-to-2D field correspondences. The plane-to-image homography is extracted from the camera projection matrix using DLT (Direct Linear Transform) with RANSAC applied to a subset of keypoints. A heuristic voting mechanism selects the solution with minimal reprojection error. The integrated refinement module of the parent PnLCalib method is omitted in the present work because of its substantial runtime impact [PAGE 3].

2. Segment true-positive condition [PAGE 3, Eq. 1].
Let s be the projected polyline of a field segment and ŝ the annotated reference polyline. Segment s is a true positive iff
\[
\forall p \in s,\quad \min_{q \in \hat{s}} d(p,q) < \gamma,
\]
where d(·,·) is Euclidean distance in pixels and γ is the pixel error threshold. Otherwise s is a false positive; annotated segments missing from projection are false negatives.

3. Jaccard Index [PAGE 4, Eq. 2].
\[
\mathrm{JaC}_{\gamma} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_{\gamma}}{\mathrm{TP}_{\gamma} + \mathrm{FP} + \mathrm{FN}}.
\]
Units: dimensionless ratio in [0,1]. The working threshold is γ = 5 pixels.

4. Completeness Rate [PAGE 4].
\[
\mathrm{CR} = \frac{N_{\text{successful}}}{N_{\text{images with } \ge 4 \text{ annotated field lines}}}.
\]
Units: dimensionless ratio in [0,1].

5. Final Score [PAGE 4, Eq. 3].
\[
\mathrm{FS} = \mathrm{CR} \times \mathrm{JaC}_{5}.
\]
Units: dimensionless composite in [0,1]. No disaggregated CR or JaC5 values appear in Tables 1–2 [PAGE 6].

6. HRNet channel and parameter topology [PAGE 4–5].
W48_orig: 77.5 million parameters; channel sequence 48 → 98 → 192 → 384 across four stages; throughput 8.56 FPS (MV setting, no PnL module); FS = 0.581 [PAGE 4]. W6: four-stage scheme preserved, but channel sequence compressed to 6 → 12 → 24 → 48; one block per stage; throughput 22.973 FPS; FS = 0.455 [PAGE 5]. W18 contains three times fewer parameters than W32 and six times fewer than W48 [PAGE 5].

7. Hardware and timing units [PAGE 4].
Inference and FPS measurements on NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU, Intel Core i9-14900K CPU. W48_orig training time assessment was performed on RTX 5090 GPU: one keypoint-subnetwork epoch ≈ 25 minutes; one line-subnetwork epoch ≈ 100 minutes [PAGE 4]. Epoch counts per architecture are listed in Tables 1–2 [PAGE 6].

8. Dataset [PAGE 3].
SoccerNet 2023: over 21,000 frames from multiple synchronized broadcast cameras (typically 3–5 cameras per match) in professional stadiums. Semantic annotations of field markings and rigid 3D landmarks (goalposts) as ordered 2D polylines from known 3D coordinates. Annotations follow the ProCC protocol, covering planar field components and non-planar landmarks [PAGE 3].

Breakdown and singularity regimes:

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to assume that because W6 is the fastest model (22.973 FPS [PAGE 5]), it is the correct production choice. W48_MTL delivers FS = 0.500 at 11.292 FPS [PAGE 6], only 2.7 FPS above W48_dyn but with higher accuracy than W6 [PAGE 6]. If the broadcast pipeline already buffers frames for human review, spending the latency budget on W48_MTL’s extra accuracy dominates W6’s marginal speed. The counter-trap is speed-maximization without matching the decision-loop clock.

Clock. The physical clock is the broadcast frame interval. At 30 FPS capture, one frame arrives every 33.3 ms. W6 at 22.973 FPS requires ≈ 43.5 ms per frame, so it cannot process every incoming frame in real time on a single RTX 4070 [PAGE 5–6]; it must drop frames, parallelize across cameras, or accept latency. W48_orig at 8.56 FPS requires ≈ 116.8 ms per frame, clearly non-real-time [PAGE 4]. W18_MTL at 21.118 FPS requires ≈ 47.4 ms per frame [PAGE 6], also sub-30 FPS but closer. The clock mismatch means none of the compact variants natively satisfies a strict per-frame 30 FPS budget on the tested hardware; W6 merely reduces the deficit. Deploying W6 as "real time" without a multi-GPU or frame-skip scheduler is a clock violation.

Biomechanical layer. During sprints or tackles, player motion blur and rapid camera panning violate the static-homography-per-frame assumption implicit in the DLT/RANSAC pipeline [PAGE 3]. The paper does not model shutter speed or motion-blur kernels. At W6’s 22.973 FPS [PAGE 5], a fast-moving player displaces multiple pixels within the exposure window, smearing Gaussian heatmap peaks [PAGE 3]. Because W6 has only one block per stage [PAGE 5], it lacks the representational depth to deblur or to distinguish a blurred line from a player limb, raising FP and FN counts.

Weather veto. Rain, snow, floodlight glare, and uneven illumination were noted as failure modes for classical feature extractors [PAGE 2]. Deep models are more robust, but W6’s compressed sequence (6 → 12 → 24 → 48 vs. 48 → 98 → 192 → 384 [PAGE 5]) narrows the dynamic range of feature activation. Under heavy rain or night floodlights, water droplets on the lens act as additive noise that shifts keypoint heatmaps beyond γ = 5 pixels [Eq. 1, PAGE 3]. Once JaC5 approaches zero, FS = CR × JaC5 collapses multiplicatively even if CR remains high [Eq. 3, PAGE 4].

Veto adjudication rule: A deployment is vetoed if (a) the effective γ = 5 pixel threshold cannot be maintained under observed weather/occlusion, or (b) per-frame latency exceeds the broadcast clock budget without parallelization. W6 passes the speed check on RTX 4070 but fails the weather-robustness check relative to W48_MTL or W18_KD because its narrow channels cannot absorb environmental perturbation without accuracy loss [PAGE 5–6].
