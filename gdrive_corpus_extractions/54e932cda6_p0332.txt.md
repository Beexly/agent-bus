<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Under Knightian uncertainty—where agents cannot assign objective probabilities to outcomes—crisis habit regression takes the form of precautionary hoarding: labor is supplied not to increase consumption but “to get rich” as a buffer against unknowable future loss [PAGE 319]. The agent regresses to a saving-dominant habit because the utility of present consumption is discounted by existential uncertainty. This is rational at the individual level but recursively destructive at the aggregate level: aggregated hoarding raises capital supply, which, via the production operator \mathcal{P}, generates new wants and expands the indirect means of production, thereby deepening the very uncertainty that triggered the regression.

The counter-trap is the belief that accumulation itself provides safety. Because “the increase in wealth is to a large extent an end in itself” [PAGE 319], the agent treats the growing stock \mathbf{K} as the object of security, not the flow C it could purchase. This creates a recursive loop: more uncertainty → more accumulation → more production of wants → more perceived need for accumulation → more uncertainty. The agent mistakes the means (capital) for the end (security), and the habit becomes self-justifying.

The clock is the intertemporal discount structure \rho. As standards of life advance, Knight notes, wealth-as-end-in-itself grows “to a rapidly increasing degree” [PAGE 319]. This implies the effective discount rate on future consumption falls relative to the discount rate on future wealth, or equivalently that the psychological clock compresses: future capital is valued more highly than present goods. The acceleration of accumulation shortens the perceived planning horizon in utility terms while lengthening it in capital terms. The agent lives on a capital clock, not a consumption clock. Crisis habit regression therefore locks the agent into a time preference structure where the marginal utility of deferred wealth dominates the marginal utility of immediate consumption, making the autotelic regime temporally self-reinforcing.

Physical / thermodynamic layer: Capital reproduction is a dissipative structure. It requires a net positive flow of exergy to maintain \mathbf{K} against depreciation \delta. If the energy required to extract, refine, and deploy new capital exceeds the energy yielded by that capital, \dot{E}_{\text{net}} \le 0, then no motivational preference for “getting rich” can sustain accumulation. The physical layer vetoes the autotelic loop absolutely. Knight’s enumeration includes “discovery of new natural resources” as a phase of progress [PAGE 319]; this is precisely the variable that determines whether the physical veto is active or dormant.

Biomechanical layer: Labor supply \mathbf{L} is bounded by human physiology. The working day cannot exceed 24 hours, and recovery, caloric intake, and musculoskeletal limits impose L_{\max}. If accumulation requires labor beyond L_{\max} without substitution by automation or energy slaves, the biomechanical layer vetoes capital deepening. The “work to get rich” motive hits a hard ceiling when disutility of labor diverges as L \to L_{\max}, regardless of the stated desire for wealth [PAGE 319].

Weather / exogenous shock layer: Climate anomalies, resource depletion, and ecological shocks alter the production possibilities frontier F(\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{L}). A multi-season drought or resource shock can reduce F below the level needed to cover \delta \mathbf{K}, forcing decumulation. Because Knight treats discovery of new natural resources as one of the independent phases [PAGE 319], a negative shock to this phase is an exogenous veto that no amount of wealth-as-end motivation can override.

Adjudication: The cross-layer veto is hierarchical. Physical energy balance is first-order; if it fails, accumulation ceases. Biomechanical labor limits are second-order; they determine whether capital can be deepened via labor or must shift to automation. Weather and resource shocks are third-order triggers that can activate either of the first two vetoes. The motivational factor (work to get rich) is necessary for the autotelic regime but not sufficient; it operates only within the feasible set carved out by the physical, biomechanical, and exogenous layers.
