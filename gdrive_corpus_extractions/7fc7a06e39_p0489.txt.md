<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Tolman’s theory can be formalized as a dynamic information-processing system operating over a cognitive map. We define the following operators and state variables:

1.  **Cognitive Map State ($M_t$):** A probabilistic graph $G=(V,E)$ representing the agent's knowledge of environmental structure at time $t$. Nodes $V$ represent loci (spatial/temporal points, objects), edges $E$ represent learned transitions with associated probabilities $p_{ij}$ and expected rewards $r_{ij}$.
2.  **Sign-Gestalt Encoder ($\mathcal{E}$):** Maps raw sensory input $S_t$ (a vector in some modality-specific space) into a discrete "sign" token $z_t = \mathcal{E}(S_t; \theta_\mathcal{E})$, where $\theta_\mathcal{E}$ are learned parameters. The encoder groups features into holistic percepts (gestalts).
3.  **Expectation Generator ($\Phi$):** Given current sign $z_t$ and cognitive map state $M_t$, produces a predicted future distribution over next signs $z_{t+1}$ and outcomes (rewards/goal-states). Formally, $P(z_{t+1}, o | z_t, M_t) = \Phi(z_t, M_t)$.
4.  **Valuation Function ($V$):** Assigns scalar value to states based on current drive states $d_t$ (first/second-order) and predicted outcomes. $V(s_t) = \mathbb{E}_{\Phi} [ \sum \gamma^k r_{t+k} | z_t, M_t ] + \text{drive\_modulator}(d_t)$.
5.  **Behavior Policy ($\pi$):** Selects action $a_t$ to maximize expected utility: $a_t = \arg\max_a \mathbb{E}[ V(s_{t+1}) | s_t, a_t, M_t ]$. In deterministic environments, this reduces to choosing actions leading to high-value nodes in $M_t$.

**Formal Learning Dynamics:**
Upon transition $(s_t, a_t) \rightarrow s_{t+1}$ with observed reward $r_{t+1}$, the cognitive map is updated via Bayesian inference or temporal difference learning:
$\Delta p_{ij} \propto \alpha ( \mathbb{I}(j|a_t) - p_{ij} )$
$\Delta r_{ij} \propto \beta ( r_{t+1} - \hat{r}_{ij} )$
where $\mathbb{I}$ is indicator function, $\alpha, \beta$ are learning rates. Crucially, updates occur only if the transition confirms or disconfirms an *existing expectancy* ($\Phi$). Pure novelty (no prior expectancy) may trigger exploration but not immediate $M_t$ update.

**Breakdown Regimes & Singularities:**
*   **Expectancy Collapse Singularity:** If prediction errors $\delta = r_{t+1} - \hat{r}$ become persistently large and unpredictable, the valuation function $V$ diverges, and policy $\pi$ becomes random or stuck (analogous to learned helplessness). This corresponds to Tolman’s "unpleasantness" overwhelming the organism.
*   **Map Overfitting Catastrophe:** In highly non-stationary environments, aggressive updating ($\alpha, \beta \to 1$) causes $M_t$ to memorize noise rather than extract generalizable structure. The model loses its predictive power outside training conditions (poor transfer).
*   **Symbol Grounding Failure:** If the sign encoder $\mathcal{E}$ fails to bind arbitrary symbols (words) to their significates via reliable sensorimotor contingencies, the system cannot form true "symbols (words)" as Tolman defined them (p.240). Communication breaks down into meaningless ritual.
*   **Drive Saturation Lock:** Second-order drives (e.g., obsession with a "sign magic" object like money) can saturate the drive modulator, causing the agent to pursue signs devoid of ultimate biological significance, leading to maladaptive loops (akin to addiction).

**Units:** All equations operate in continuous time/discrete steps. Probabilities are dimensionless. Values/rewards have units of subjective utility (arbitrary scale). Learning rates are dimensionless fractions. The cognitive map $M_t$ has units of "expected transitions per unit time/distance."

**Assumptions:** 
(A1) Organisms possess innate capacity for forming sign gestalts ($\mathcal{E}$ exists).
(A2) Reinforcement works primarily through confirmation/disconfirmation of expectations ($\Phi$), not direct S-R strengthening.
(A3) Motivation is separable from learning (drives affect $V$, not $\mathcal{E}$ or map formation directly).
(A4) Cognition involves representation ($M_t$) distinct from response tendencies.

These assumptions allow Tolman's framework to explain phenomena like latent learning (learning without reinforcement), insight, and purposeful detours—phenomena that violate strict S-R contiguity laws.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Edward Tolman’s work sits at a pivotal junction in the history of mind. His genealogy branches back to three major streams, each leaving a trace in the provided index:

1.  **Gestalt Psychology (Köhler, Koffka, Wertheimer):** The terms *Sign gestalt*, *Round about-solutions (Umwege)*, *Spatial distances*, and *Topologue* directly import Gestalt principles. Tolman adopted the idea that perception is holistic (*gestalt*) and that problem-solving often involves sudden reorganization of the perceptual field (insight). However, he radicalized it by insisting these gestalts must be *operationalized* and tested via behavioral experiments, particularly with animals. The index entry for *Tool using in Kohler's apes* (pp.220-226) shows Tolman integrating Köhler's famous chimpanzee studies into his own experimental paradigm, arguing they proved the existence of expectancies and cognitive maps, not just drive reduction.

2.  **Pavlovian Conditioning & Hullian S-R Theory:** Tolman engaged deeply with mainstream behaviorism but turned it on its head. Entries for *Responsiveness to stimuli*, *Stimulus response psychologies*, and *Thorndike's kitten* mark this dialogue. While accepting conditioning as a fact, he rejected the notion that it created direct S-R bonds. Instead, he proposed Pavlovian CS-US pairings created *sign-gestalt expectations*: the CS comes to signify the US, generating an anticipatory state that guides behavior. This reinterpretation allowed him to explain why extinction occurs faster when the US is omitted during conditioning (the expectancy isn't confirmed) versus when it's merely suppressed—a nuance S-R theories struggled with.

3.  **Psychoanalysis (Freud, Jung):** The inclusion of *Repression*, *Sublimation*, *Transference*, *Symbols (psychoanalytical sense)*, and the explicit mention of the *Freudian unconscious* (p.299f) is remarkable for a behaviorist. Tolman attempted a naturalistic account of these phenomena. He viewed "symbols" in the psychoanalytic sense as sign gestalts whose significates are emotionally charged memories or drives. Repression, for instance, could be modeled as the active blocking of certain sign-significate associations from entering conscious awareness (defined operationally as reportability), perhaps due to conflict with other dominant expectancies. This bold synthesis aimed to bring the depth of psychoanalysis under the umbrella of objective science.

**Multi-Week Traps & Historical Blind Spots:**

*   **The Cognitivist Anachronism Trap:** Assuming Tolman was a precursor to computer-based cognitivism. While he used terms like "map" and "expectation," his foundation was thoroughly biological and phenomenological (*raw feels*, *visceral sensations*). He lacked any notion of algorithms or digital computation. His "cognitive map" was an analog, embodied spatial topology.
*   **The Mentalism Misreading:** Seeing "purpose," "goal," and "consciousness" (in relation to thought/speech) and concluding Tolman smuggled dualism back into behaviorism. His ambition was a unified theory spanning phylogeny and ontogeny. The index entries for *Speech* (as tool behavior, set of signs) show he saw human language as an elaboration of the same fundamental sign-learning processes seen in animals, albeit vastly more complex and recursive.

Understanding Tolman requires seeing him not as a failed S-R theorist or a proto-computationalist, but as a synthesizer trying to build a bridge between the objective methods of biology/psychology and the rich, purposive reality of lived experience, all while navigating the intellectual politics of the early 20th century.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Tolman’s framework invites a recursive analysis of the mind modeling itself and others, crucial for understanding crisis, habit, and strategic interaction.

**Crisis Habit Regression vs. Counter-Trap:**
In a stable environment, an organism operates via well-worn pathways in its cognitive map $M_t$, driven by habitual expectancies ($\Phi$). A *crisis* (e.g., sudden change in reward location, traumatic shock) creates massive prediction error, destabilizing $M_t$. According to Tolman, organisms don’t revert to pure S-R panic (though it can happen); instead, they engage in heightened *exploration*—testing alternative paths in the map, actively seeking new sign-gestalts that restore predictability. This is not regression to simpler mechanisms but a shift in strategy within the same cognitive architecture. The "habit" being broken is the specific route expectancy, not the capacity for expectation itself.

A *counter-trap* scenario arises when an organism learns that certain cues previously signaling safety now signal danger (reversal learning). Tolman’s theory predicts this should be difficult because the old sign-gestalt-expectation link is strong. Breaking it requires forming a new, competing expectancy. This process is slow and prone to interference—the old map doesn't vanish but coexists weakly until overridden. Modern neuroscience supports this (separate neural ensembles for original and reversed rules). Tolman would interpret this as evidence for multiple, context-dependent cognitive maps, not a single overwritten S-R bond.

**The Clockwork Mind vs. Purposive Agent:**
The deepest tension Tolman addressed is between the mind as a deterministic automaton (clockwork) and as a teleonomic agent (purposive). His answer was nuanced: At the level of moment-to-moment neural firing, there may be clockwork determinism. But at the level of *behavioral sequences over time*, the system exhibits apparent purpose because it is guided by internal representations (maps, expectancies) that encode distal goals. The "purpose" is emergent from the dynamics of the cognitive map interacting with the environment. There is no homunculus inside making choices; the choice is computed by the policy $\pi$ evaluating options against $V(s)$ derived from $M_t$.

However, Tolman acknowledged a residual element: *Valence (Aufforderungscharakter)* (p.179), borrowed from Gestaltists. This is the inherent "affordance" or "invitation-character" of certain stimuli to act upon them. While largely learned via sign-gestalt associations, some basic valences (sweet tastes, bright lights) might have innate biases. This introduces a minimal layer of biologically programmed "attractor states" into the otherwise learned map, preventing complete tabula rasa. For Tolman, even this valence operates through the same informational channels—it influences which expectancies get prioritized in attention and learning.

Thus, the recursive theory of mind implied is one where an agent maintains a self-model (part of $M_t$) including its own drives, current expectancies, and likely actions. It uses this meta-knowledge to plan (simulate future states in $M_t$) and potentially deceive others (if it models their $M_{others}$). This sophistication was implicit in his discussions of deception in animals and the social nature of second-order drives.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Tolman’s grand integrative project rests on the assumption that cognitive explanations (expectancies, maps) are sufficient to account for behavior once lower-level physical/biomechanical constraints are satisfied. We must adjudicate whether lower layers can issue a veto over cognitive predictions.

**Proof of Principle: Physical/Biomechanical Constraints.**
Consider the classic puzzle box experiments. A hungry cat eventually escapes by pulling a string. Tolman argues the cat forms an expectancy linking the lever-pull gesture to escape. But suppose we make the string infinitesimally thin, requiring fine motor control far beyond the cat’s paw morphology. Even with perfect cognitive map knowledge, the cat fails. Here, biomechanics vetoes the cognitive plan. Similarly, if a rat’s hind limb is paralyzed, its elaborate maze-running map becomes partially useless. The physical substrate imposes hard limits on executable policies $\pi$.

**Weather/Environmental Stochasticity Veto.**
Imagine a desert ant navigating via path integration and landmark vectors (a cognitive map). A sudden sandstorm erases landmarks and scatters odors. The ant’s internal map remains intact, but environmental noise vetoes its ability to execute navigational plans reliably. Its behavior appears erratic (not "purposive") despite unbroken cognition. Tolman accounted for this indirectly through his emphasis on *responsiveness to stimuli*—even with expectancies, immediate stimulation modulates behavior. But his framework under-specifies how noisy transduction or effector failure interacts with cognition.

**Adjudication Procedure within Tolman’s Framework:**
Tolman didn’t formally define such vetoes, but his operationalism provides clues. He distinguished between:
1.  **Intervening Variables (IVs):** Like expectancies, purposes, cognitive maps – theoretical constructs explaining covariation.
2.  **Respondent Processes:** Reflexes, fixed action patterns – considered more automatic, less influenced by IVs.
3.  **Operant Responding:** Goal-directed behavior shaped by consequences.

A plausible veto rule emerges: **Lower-layer processes (respondent, biomechanical) can override higher-layer IV-guided operant responding ONLY when the lower-layer demand exceeds a threshold intensity AND the operant goal is not immediately survival-critical.** For example:
*   A powerful startle reflex (respondent) may freeze a rat mid-run toward food (operant), especially if the threat is novel and intense.
*   Severe muscle fatigue (biomechanical) will halt running regardless of hunger strength.
*   However, mild pain might not stop a desperately hungry animal from continuing a painful detour if the cognitive map indicates short distance to reward.

This hierarchy suggests cognition sets broad intentions, but execution is gated by bodily states and reflexive systems. Modern hierarchical reinforcement learning models implement similar arbitration modules.

**Conclusion:** Tolman’s genius was elevating cognition to causal status. Yet, his index hints at boundaries (*Timidity*, *Visceral sensations*) where non-cognitive factors dominate. A complete theory requires specifying the interface protocols—when does the map yield to the body’s immediate cries? This remains a vibrant research frontier in neuroethology and embodied AI.
