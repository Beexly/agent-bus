If the rat pushes through on the basis of \(f\) alone, the recursive model is validated: the subject has modeled the experimenter's rule as "feature \(f\) implies outcome \(o^*\)." If the rat hesitates or chooses randomly, the model is falsified; the expectancy was bound to the complete sign complex \(s\), not its feature \(f\).

The clock is the delay interval as a measurable temporal structure. Rats may solve the task not by holding expectancy "in leash" but by learning an interval-timing function \(t \mapsto P(\text{release} \mid t)\), effectively using an internal clock rather than working memory. A recursive theorist must therefore distinguish between expectancy maintenance and timed response initiation; the two produce identical choice data but implicate different neural architectures (persistent activity versus ramping accumulator).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Physical layer: The maze apparatus [PAGE 158] requires the rat to push through a curtain. If the white curtain offers different mechanical resistance (mass, drape, friction) than the black curtains, the rat may select by force-feedback rather than visual sign. Veto: measure curtain displacement force in Newtons; if \(\Delta F\) exceeds 5% between conditions, the visual-confound null is rejected.

Biomechanical/sensory layer: Rattus norvegicus has limited visual acuity and dichromatic vision; white versus black curtains may differ in luminance contrast but also in volatile compounds from dye or fabric. Olfactory marking from prior trials can linger on the apparatus. Veto: counterbalance curtain positions across trials, sanitize the maze between subjects, and equate olfactory salience using unscented fabric controls. If performance persists under odor-controlled conditions, the visual-feature veto is survived.

Weather layer: The experiment is indoor and climatologically controlled, so no meteorological variable directly affects curtain color or door mechanics. However, extrapolating to outdoor or semi-natural enclosures introduces humidity-induced changes in paper/fabric opacity and thermal effects on rat activity. Veto: the lab result is valid only within HVAC-stable bounds (approximately 20–24°C, 40–60% relative humidity); outside this envelope, curtain drape and rat motivation become uncontrolled confounds.

Cross-layer adjudication: The experiment passes only if (i) mechanical forces are equated across curtains, (ii) olfactory cues are nullified, and (iii) testing occurs within climate-stable bounds. Failure at any layer vetoes the claim that the condensed sign is purely visual.
