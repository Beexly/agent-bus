This establishes three recursive layers.

First, crisis habit regression: under novelty, time pressure, or stress, A reverts to a previously successful classification \(\pi^{\text{habit}}\) rather than recomputing class boundaries from scratch. This is computationally rational given the finite intelligence bound \(I_{\max}\) [PAGE 1], but it freezes the model of B at an outdated partition \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{old}}\).

Second, counter-trap: B, anticipating A's regression, deliberately emits cues that activate \(\pi^{\text{habit}}\) while behaving according to a different class \(\mathcal{C}^{\text{true}}\). Because B is adaptive and reflexive [PAGE 1], B can manipulate the classification boundary itself. Formally, B chooses an observation \(o^*(t)\) to maximize A's loss \(L(A,B)\) subject to A's policy being constrained to \(\pi^{\text{habit}}\):
\[
o^*(t) = \arg\max_{o} \; L\bigl(A,\; B;\; \pi_A = \pi^{\text{habit}}\bigr).
\]
The counter-trap is effective precisely because Knightian uncertainty denies A an objective probability distribution over B's possible classes; A cannot weight the hypothesis "B is counter-trapping" against "B is stable."

Third, the clock: a decision deadline \(T_{\text{dec}}\) truncates the time available for re-classification. If \(T_{\text{dec}} < \tau_{\text{reclassify}}\), where \(\tau_{\text{reclassify}}\) is the time required to estimate a new partition within tolerance \(\epsilon\), A is forced to act under the regressed habit schema. The interaction is multiplicative: crisis habit regression raises exploitability, the counter-trap converts that exploitability into systematic loss, and the clock removes the escape option of delayed judgment. In Knight's terms, the decision-maker is left with entrepreneurial judgment under non-measurable uncertainty rather than with a calculable risk [PAGE 1].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any predictive system predicated on Knightian classification is subject to cross-layer vetoes when the assumptions of finite, unchanging kinds are falsified by physical, biomechanical, or atmospheric conditions.

Physical layer: Even "rocks and planets" do not preserve strict unvarying identity [PAGE 1]. At molecular scales, thermal fluctuations of order \(k_B T\) (joules) induce state transitions; at quantum scales, measurement uncertainty imposes a finite resolution \(\delta x\). Below \(\delta x\), objects are indistinguishable; above it, microscopic heterogeneity accumulates until the class-conditional distribution \(P(B \mid \mathcal{C}_k)\) broadens beyond tolerance \(\epsilon\). Veto condition: signal-to-noise ratio \(< 1\) or positional uncertainty \(\sigma_x > \delta x\).

Biomechanical layer: Knight explicitly identifies men and animals as exceptions to strict invariance [PAGE 1]. Their behavior map \(B\) is non-stationary because of learning, motivation, fatigue, and adaptive goal-seeking. Within-class variance \(\sigma^2_{B|\mathcal{C}_k}\) is not a fixed parameter but a time-varying function of internal state. Veto condition: rolling-window within-class variance \(\sigma^2_{B|\mathcal{C}_k}(t) > \theta_{\text{bio}}\), where \(\theta_{\text{bio}}\) is a domain-specific tolerance in action-space units.

Weather/atmospheric layer: The atmosphere is a complex, chaotic system whose "circumstances" are composed of other things (air masses, moisture, thermal gradients) whose behavior exhibits sensitive dependence [PACE: see regress on PAGE 1]. The Lyapunov time \(\tau_L\) (seconds) bounds the prediction horizon; beyond \(\tau_L\), class-conditional probabilities become effectively uniform, maximizing Knightian uncertainty. Veto condition: decision lead time \(L_{\text{dec}} > \tau_L\).

Veto proof: The veto is absolute because it targets the ontological precondition, not a parametric estimate. If any layer violates its condition, the assumption that there exists a finite, practically manageable number of unchanging kinds [PAGE 1] is falsified, and no recalibration of class probabilities can restore valid prediction. The system must abstain or switch to a robust, distribution-free criterion (e.g., info-gap satisficing or maxmin expected utility) rather than issuing a classification-contingent forecast.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Prediction without veto: mean-squared prediction error grows as \(\mathcal{O}(\sigma^2 \Delta t)\); with veto: prediction error is bounded by the worst-case regret of the minimax policy, at the cost of increased abstention frequency.

Prediction without veto: a Goodhart loop in which in-sample accuracy rises while out-of-sample outcomes deteriorate; with veto: A switches to a game-theoretic solution concept (e.g., Stackelberg commitment), sacrificing short-term fit for strategic robustness.
