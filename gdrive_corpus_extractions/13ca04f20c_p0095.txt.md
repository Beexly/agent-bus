The complementary hue emerges when peripheral excitation saturates, consistent with James’s “excessive excitation of the periphery of the optic nerve” and “subjective and durable sensation, that of the complementary color” [PAGE 1].

Binding. Let R_friend be the reminiscence retrieved by the laws of association [PAGE 1]. The phantom is P = R_friend ⊗ K, where ⊗ denotes spatio-chromatic binding over the visual field; outlines, faces, and clothes take on the corpse-like tint K [PAGE 1].

Visibility gating. Let ρ ∈ [0,1] index reflection/examination intensity. Phenomenon persistence is T_p(ρ) = T₀ / (1 + κρ), with a singularity at ρ → ρ_crit where T_p → 0 (“All grew suddenly pale … had disappeared”) [PAGE 1]. Open-eye visibility satisfies V_open ≪ V_closed because exogenous light dilutes endogenous K (“only saw them faintly, of no determined color”) [PAGE 1].

Units and assumptions. Time in seconds (s) or minutes (min); probability dimensionless on [0,1]; opponent-channel signals dimensionless; irradiance W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹ if physicalized, though James works with phenomenological hue categories [PAGE 1]. Assumptions: (1) prior chromatic adaptation is necessary for K ≠ 0; (2) eyelid closure raises endogenous signal-to-noise; (3) reflection acts as a competitive inhibitor with critical threshold ρ_crit. Breakdown: if ρ > ρ_crit, or eyes are open under photopic illumination, or F_c ≈ 0, then P_visible = 0. All James citations refer to the provided source page [PAGE 1] (Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, p. 85).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The immediate lineage is James’s own Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, “The Perception of Things,” section “Illusions,” p. 85 [PAGE 1]. Fig. 47, referenced but not reproduced here, had previously been framed in terms of brain-processes; James now re-treats A and B as objects to study reference apart from physiology [PAGE 1]. The associationist inheritance runs through Aristotle, Locke, Hartley, Hume, James Mill, and Bain, culminating in James’s “laws of association” by which the inward reminiscence of the absent friend arises “by a very simple necessity” [PAGE 1].

The color-vision lineage runs from Goethe’s Theory of Colours (1810) and Purkinje’s subjective phenomena through Helmholtz’s physiological optics to Hering’s opponent-process theory (1878). James’s phrase “excessive excitation of the periphery of the optic nerve” and his appeal to the “complementary color” [PAGE 1] place him squarely in this tradition: prolonged chromatic stimulation fatigues opponent channels, and withdrawal yields the complementary after-image. The mountain’s “violet, brown, and dark green” [PAGE 1] are atmospheric-perspective hues whose complement is the “greenish yellow gray” corpse-like field [PAGE 1].

Multi-week traps. First, a longitudinal study that treats veridical perception and illusion as categorically distinct processes violates James’s explicit process-identity claim (“the process is the same”) [PAGE 1]; any model assigning separate generative mechanisms to errors versus successes over weeks is trapped in a false dichotomy. Second, introspective reports are not stable across sessions because the act of reflection terminates the phenomenon (“the influence of reflection and examination had been too powerful”) [PAGE 1]; repeated testing over weeks would exhibit reactivity and decay rather than stationary baselines. Third, attributing the phantom solely to top-down expectation or solely to bottom-up after-image misses the binding operation P = R ⊗ K [PAGE 1]; a multi-week design that fails to control chromatic adaptation history, eyelid state (open vs. closed), and reflective load will confound the associative layer with the sensory after-image layer and misattribute causality.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under fatigue and sensory withdrawal (eyes closed after mountain gazing), the perceiver regresses to an associative habit: an interrupted thought thread—James had been looking for the “Waldbruder” when the thread broke [PAGE 1]—spontaneously retrieves an absent friend, and this reminiscence binds to the dominant sensory residue, the complementary after-image, producing the corpse-like phantom [PAGE 1]. The crisis state lowers the threshold for endogenous binding because exogenous competition is removed.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to assume that the same perceiver under non-crisis conditions would continue to see the phantom. James reports the opposite: with eyes open, “I did not see phantoms, or at all events only saw them faintly, of no determined color” [PAGE 1]; and deliberate inquiry collapses the image: “the influence of reflection and examination had been too powerful. All grew suddenly pale” [PAGE 1]. The counter-trap is therefore the assumption of cross-attentional stability of the illusion; the correct model is attentional gating in which open eyes and high reflection veto the endogenous image.

Clock. The phenomenon carries a time constant. James notes it “might have lasted some minutes longer” had reflection not intervened [PAGE 1], implying a natural decay clock τ_phantom on the order of minutes. Under time pressure or crisis, there is insufficient time for the reflective clock to run down the phantom; under leisurely examination, ρ rises and T_p(ρ) → 0 before natural decay completes. The clock is thus competitively inhibited by reflection rather than running autonomously.

Recursive theory of mind. The perceiver meta-represents the first-order event: “I asked myself at once how I had come to think of my absent friend” [PAGE 1]. This second-order model of the associative retrieval is precisely the reflection that terminates the first-order illusion. The theory-of-mind loop is self-terminating: thinking about the illusion kills the illusion, because the recursive monitoring raises ρ above ρ_crit and triggers the pale-collapse singularity [PAGE 1].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Optic-nerve peripheral excitation and the complementary after-image are necessary conditions [PAGE 1]. Veto: if the pre-closure field is achromatic or neutral, F_c ≈ 0, so K ≡ 0 and no corpse-like hue can be generated by rebound, regardless of the strength of R.

Biomechanical layer. Eyelid state is a mechanical gate. Closed eyes permit the endogenous field to dominate (“the whole field of sight … covered with the same corpse-like hue”) [PAGE 1]. Open eyes admit exogenous illumination that dilutes or masks K; phantoms appear only faintly or with no determined color [PAGE 1]. Veto: in photopic open-eye conditions, P is below perceptual threshold unless the after-image is exceptionally strong.

Weather/atmospheric layer. The mountain’s apparent colors—violet, brown, and dark green [PAGE 1]—are produced by atmospheric perspective and distance. Changes in illumination, fog, or time of day alter I_m(λ,t_e), thereby changing F_c and hence K. A gray overcast day would not saturate the same opponent channels as a sunlit violet-brown mountain. Veto: if atmospheric conditions do not produce saturated chromatic excitation, the after-image layer fails.

Veto proof. Define Veto = 1 iff (EyeState = Open ∧ Illuminance > Φ_thresh) ∨ (Reflection ρ > ρ_crit) ∨ (ChromaticAdaptation F_c ≈ 0). When Veto = 1, P_visible = 0. James’s own termination by reflection (“All grew suddenly pale … had disappeared”) [PAGE 1] is a direct empirical instance of the ρ > ρ_crit clause; his open-eye report (“only saw them faintly”) [PAGE 1] instantiates the exogenous-masking clause; and the absence of a phantom before mountain gazing instantiates the F_c ≈ 0 clause. The three layers are jointly sufficient to explain why the corpse-like phantom appears only under closed-eye, post-fatigue, low-reflection conditions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Achromatic control (physical-layer veto). Subject gazes at a neutral gray field of mountain-like luminance for 5 minutes (no chromatic saturation), then closes eyes and recalls an absent friend. Prediction: F_c ≈ 0, so K ≡ 0; no greenish-yellow-gray after-image forms; therefore no corpse-tinted phantom P = R ⊗ K emerges. Verifiable veto: if a subject reports a vividly hue-specific corpse phantom under purely achromatic adaptation, the complementary-color binding mechanism is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Reflective load (recursive/attentional veto). Subject gazes at violet-brown-dark-green mountain colors, closes eyes, and immediately performs continuous serial subtraction (high ρ). Prediction: reflection rises above ρ_crit within seconds; T_p(ρ) → 0; the phantom collapses before its natural minute-scale decay. Verifiable veto: if the phantom persists unchanged for > 2 minutes under continuous high-load reflection, the attentional-gating singularity is falsified.

Scenario 3 — Open-eye photopic competition (biomechanical veto). Subject gazes at mountain colors, then keeps eyes open in bright daylight while attempting to recall the friend. Prediction: exogenous illumination competes with K; V_open ≪ V_closed; at most faint, color-indeterminate phantoms, matching James’s report that with eyes open he “only saw them faintly, of no determined color” [PAGE 1]. Verifiable veto: if a vivid, color-saturated corpse phantom superimposes on the open-eye visual field, the biomechanical masking veto is falsified.

Predictions are anchored to James’s parameters: τ_phantom ~ minutes (“might have lasted some minutes longer”) [PAGE 1]; V_open/V_closed ratio qualitatively < 0.2 (“faintly” vs. “whole field of sight … covered”) [PAGE 1]; ρ_crit is the point at which “reflection and examination” turns the field “suddenly pale” [PAGE 1].
