Verbatim: “the exertion of the will is followed neither by actual movement of the eye, nor by contraction of the muscle in question, nor even by increased tension in it” [PAGE 517]. Any theory asserting that direction perception requires afferent feedback from muscle contraction is physically vetoed, because the percept persists while contraction is absent.

Biomechanical veto. During excessive lateral rotation, muscular strain \(\sigma\) (Pa) rises, yet James states such feelings are “too faint and vague to be of use in the perception of direction” [PAGE 517]. Formally,
\[
\frac{\partial \hat{\theta}}{\partial \sigma} \approx 0,
\]
so the biomechanical afferent channel is perceptually gated out. A theory that routes direction sense through proprioceptive strain is biomechanically vetoed.

Environmental / weather-layer veto. The phenomenon occurs with unchanged retinal image and unchanged object position [PAGE 517]. Varying illuminance \(E_v\) (lux) or atmospheric conditions does not alter \(\mathcal{E}\), because the volitional command is centrally generated and independent of photic input. A purely retinal or environmental-luminance theory of directional perception is vetoed by this invariance.

Veto synthesis. The conjunction of (i) zero motor output, (ii) unchanged afferent retinal image, and (iii) persistent directional percept forms a logical veto of any afferent-only spatial-perception model. The sole breakdown regime is binocular: when \(|\theta_L-\theta_R|>\delta_{fusion}\) rad, double images emerge [PAGE 517], and the single efference copy cannot reconcile two divergent plant states. Outside that singularity, the veto proof holds across physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers.

Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Effort modulation under complete paralysis.
Intervention: In a patient with paralyzed right external rectus (\(\theta=0\) rad, \(F=0\) N [PAGE 517]), parametrically vary volitional effort \(\mathcal{E}\in\{0.2,0.5,0.8\}\) while the sound eye remains covered and the retinal target is stationary.
Prediction: Reported perceived direction \(\hat{\theta}\) increases monotonically with \(\mathcal{E}\) (e.g., \(\hat{\theta}\approx\eta\mathcal{E}\) rad) despite zero ocular displacement.
Veto target: Pure afferent / retinal model \(\hat{\theta}=h(\theta,\text{retina})\), which predicts constant \(\hat{\theta}\) for all \(\mathcal{E}\). Verifiable via forced-choice direction reports.

Scenario 2: Paresis adaptation clock.
Intervention: After onset of paresis with fixed gain \(\alpha=0.3\), measure \(\hat{\theta}\) at \(t=1\) day and \(t=30\) days for the same volitional command \(\mathcal{E}\).
Prediction: At \(t=1\) day, \(\hat{\theta}\approx\eta\mathcal{E}\) (overestimates actual \(\theta=\alpha\mathcal{E}\)); by \(t=30\) days, \(\hat{\theta}\to\theta\) as the forward model recalibrates on the \(\tau\)-clock (“before many days or weeks are over” [PAGE 517]).
Veto target: Static mapping model \(\hat{\theta}=g(\theta)\) with time-invariant parameters. Verifiable via longitudinal psychophysical direction estimates.

Scenario 3: Binocular conflict without occlusion.
Intervention: Leave both eyes open, paralyze the right external rectus (\(\theta_R=0\) rad), and instruct the patient to will rightward gaze while fixating a central object.
Prediction: Diplopia emerges with angular separation \(\Delta\phi\propto\mathcal{E}\), not \(\Delta\phi\propto\theta_R=0\); the two perceived images separate in proportion to the willed efference command [PAGE 517].
Veto target: Retinal-image-correspondence / fusion-only model, which predicts no direction shift because the right retinal image is unchanged. Verifiable via diplopia-field mapping as \(\mathcal{E}\) is varied.
