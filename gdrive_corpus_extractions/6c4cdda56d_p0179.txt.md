Multi-week traps. If this procedure were extended over weeks, several traps would invalidate a simple sign-condensation reading. First, repeated touching of the same curtain could overtrain a motor habit, so the response becomes an S-R habit directed at a location rather than a flexible sign for food; reversal of the baited door would then produce perseverative errors. Second, proactive interference across sessions would accumulate, making the sign representation trial-history dependent and producing non-monotonic accuracy across τ. Third, contextual drift—experimenter position, room odor, lighting, or start-box cues—could become correlated with the target door, so the rat solves the task by context rather than by the continuing door feature. Fourth, the 7–45 s window [PAGE 1] cannot support claims about longer delays; over weeks, very long intervals might engage long-term spatial memory or habitual routines, decoupling accuracy from the short-term retention clock. Fifth, individual differences noted as “some animals” [PAGE 1] would likely amplify with extended training, separating fast sign consolidators from slow habit developers. Any multi-week interpretation must therefore test for habit perseveration, contextual control, and interval generalization before attributing performance to purposive sign-condensation.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the situation recursively: Level 0 is the rat’s behavior; Level 1 is the experimenter’s model of the rat; Level 2 is the rat’s presumed model of the food location; Level 3 is the experimenter’s model of the rat’s model of the food location. Tolman’s inference operates at Level 3: the experimenter claims the rat treats the door as the continuing sign for food because the overt touch response condensed the sign from the white-curtain-plus-door complex to the door feature [PAGE 1].

Crisis habit regression. Under delay, occlusion, and running-about interference, the system may regress from a sign-based, purposive choice to a crisis motor habit: perseveration at the previously touched curtain, a side bias, or a return-to-start tendency. If such regression dominates, errors will cluster at a preferred door or side independent of τ, and accuracy will not track the retention decay P_ret(τ). The “high percentages” reported for “some animals” [PAGE 1] could reflect animals that did not regress, whereas failures could reflect habit regression rather than absence of sign-condensation.

Counter-trap. The overt touch response is being interpreted as evidence of sign-condensation, but the counter-trap is that the touch may simply be a conditioned orienting or approach response whose memory is carried by idiothetic path integration, vestibular self-motion, or proximal cues rather than by the door sign. Because the rat runs about during the delay [PAGE 1], self-motion cues could allow it to update the target location even when all curtains are black. A valid counter-trap test is to rotate the start position or the room cues between presentation and choice; if accuracy collapses under rotation despite black curtains, the rat was using idiothetic or contextual cues, not a door-centered sign. Conversely, if accuracy survives rotation but collapses when the screen is not dropped or curtains remain white, the original sign-condensation account gains support.

Clock. The delay τ is the independent variable acting as a retention clock. Under the sign-condensation model, accuracy should be a decreasing function of τ with asymptote p_0 = 1/3 once P_ret(τ) is exhausted. Deviations from monotonic decay—flat accuracy across 7–45 s, sudden recovery at long delays, or abrupt side-bias errors—indicate that the clock is not governing behavior and that sensory cues, habits, or context are driving choices. The recursive point is that the experimenter’s conclusion depends on the rat “remembering that it touched the door because the door meant food,” but the observable data only constrain “the rat chose the previously touched door after τ,” which is consistent with multiple recursive models.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Define veto variables for the three layers required to attribute delayed choice to sign-condensation rather than sensory, biomechanical, or environmental confounds.

Physical layer. V_board = 1 iff stiff boards behind all curtains prevent passage and immediate food delivery [PAGE 1]; V_screen = 1 iff the dropped screen fully occludes all three curtains during the delay [PAGE 1]; V_blackout = 1 iff all curtains are changed to black and no differential reflectance, glare, or residual white cue distinguishes the former target curtain [PAGE 1]. The physical veto is V_phys = V_board ∧ V_screen ∧ V_blackout. Failure of V_board drives τ → 0 and converts the task to instrumental consumption; failure of V_screen or V_blackout leaves a visual cue available, making delay irrelevant to the sign variable.

Biomechanical layer. V_locomotion = 1 iff the rat can approach, contact, and touch the curtain; V_vestibular = 1 iff running about during the delay does not impair spatial updating; V_motor = 1 iff no fatigue, injury, or motor impairment prevents the overt choice response. V_bio = V_locomotion ∧ V_vestibular ∧ V_motor.

Environmental/weather-proof layer. The chamber is indoor, so meteorological weather is excluded by design; however, HVAC failure, temperature extremes, air currents, or odor plumes constitute an environmental veto analogous to weather. V_odor = 1 iff no odor cue marks the target door; V_light = 1 iff lighting is uniform and black curtains are not discriminable by brightness; V_sound = 1 iff no local sound cue identifies the door; V_climate = 1 iff chamber temperature and airflow are stable. V_env = V_odor ∧ V_light ∧ V_sound ∧ V_climate.

Adjudication rule. Accept the sign-condensation inference only if V = V_phys ∧ V_bio ∧ V_env = 1 and A(τ) is statistically above p_0 = 1/3 with a parametric decay consistent with P_ret(τ). If any component is 0, the conclusion is vetoed and the high accuracy must be attributed to the failed layer: persistent curtain passage (V_board = 0) yields immediate reinforcement; visible curtain residue (V_blackout = 0) yields visual discrimination; odor at the target (V_odor = 0) yields olfactory guidance; motor impairment (V_motor = 0) invalidates the overt-response requirement. No inference about purposive sign-condensation can survive a vetoed layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (remove the overt-touch commitment). Revert to Hunter-style restraint at the entrance while preserving the delay, screen drop, and curtain-to-black transformation [PAGE 1]. Verifiable prediction under sign-condensation: delayed accuracy should fall to chance p_0 = 1/3 even at τ = 7 s because no motor commitment binds the sign to the door feature; under a pure sensory-residue account, accuracy could remain high if any white-curtain cue survives. Observation of A(7 s) ≈ 1/3 in the restrained group and A(7 s) > 1/3 in the Honzik group would veto the sensory-residue alternative and support the sign-condensation/motor-commitment mechanism.

Scenario 2 (retain the white-curtain cue during delay). Keep all curtains white or otherwise differentially visible during the delay period rather than changing them to black [PAGE 1]. Verifiable prediction: accuracy should remain near ceiling at τ = 45 s for both restrained and overt-choice animals, because W itself continues to specify the target and sign-condensation to the door is unnecessary. If A(45 s) remains high without door condensation, the original Honzik advantage is vetoed as a failure to remove the presentation cue rather than evidence of delayed sign retention.

Scenario 3 (extend the delay beyond the tested domain). Increase τ from the tested 7–45 s range [PAGE 1] to 300 s while keeping all veto layers intact. Verifiable prediction under the retention-clock model: A(τ) should decay monotonically toward p_0 = 1/3 as P_ret(τ) → 0; if accuracy remains high at 300 s, performance is supported by long-term spatial memory, habit, or an uncontrolled external cue rather than by the short-term sign trace under investigation. A flat or non-decaying curve at long τ would veto the claim that the 7–45 s effect indexes the same working-memory sign-condensation process.
