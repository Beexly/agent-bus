**1. Primary vs. Secondary Drive Dynamics:**
Let $M_p(t)$ denote the magnitude of a primary drive (appetite) at time $t$. It follows a homeostatic oscillator model:
$$ M_p(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi) + C $$
where $A$ is amplitude, $\omega$ is the biological frequency, and $C$ is the baseline need threshold [PAGE 1: "cyclical and spontaneously aroused"].

Conversely, let $M_s(x_t)$ denote a secondary aversion, triggered by a threatening stimulus feature vector $x_t$:
$$ M_s(x_t) = \sigma\left( \mathbf{w}^\top \phi(x_t) - \tau \right) $$
where $\phi(x_t)$ maps environmental inputs to a threat-feature space, $\mathbf{w}$ encodes the learned vulnerability to unfamiliarity/lack of dominance, $\tau$ is the activation threshold, and $\sigma$ is a sigmoid. As noted [PAGE 1], these are "continuously ready to go off, whenever such threatening... stimuli are presented."

**2. Sign-Gestalt Readiness as Value Function Approximation:**
Tolman's "sign gestalts" are cognitive maps linking stimuli to expectations. We model this as a state-value function $V_\theta(s)$ parameterized by a neural network or kernel expansion $\theta$.
$$ V_\theta(s) \approx \mathbb{E} \left[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty \gamma^k R(s_{t+k}, a_{t+k}) \mid s_t = s \right] $$
A "readiness" state corresponds to a high gradient norm $\|\nabla_a Q(s,a)\|$, indicating a strong, pre-computed policy bias.

**3. Pathological Distortion Operators (Repression, Fixation, Sign Magic):**
Personality mechanisms modify the standard Bellman update $\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$.

*Fixation:* A reduction in entropy regularization of the policy $\pi(a|s)$. In Soft-Actor-Critic frameworks, the objective includes $\mathbb{E}[\log \pi(a|s)]$. Fixation occurs when the temperature parameter $\alpha \to 0$, collapsing the policy into a deterministic, rigid loop:
$$ \pi_{fixed}(a|s) = \arg\max_a Q(s,a) \quad (\text{zero exploration}) $$

*Repression:* An active manipulation of the backward pass gradient. Let $L$ be the reconstruction or TD-loss. Repression applies a masking function $\mathcal{M}_{rep} \in \{0,1\}^{d}$ to the gradient w.r.t specific traumatic features:
$$ \nabla_\theta L_{repressed} = \mathcal{M}_{rep} \odot \nabla_\theta L $$

*Sign Magic:* A complete decoupling of the latent representation $z$ from the true physical state $s$. The agent optimizes against a hallucinated reward signal derived purely from internal symbol interactions. Mathematically, the mutual information $I(z; s)$ approaches zero while variance in $z$ remains high.

**Breakdown Regimes:** These equations break down when the system enters *chaotic attractors*, where infinitesimal changes in stimulus $x_t$ cause discontinuous, unpredictable shifts between multiple personality-operator basins. This represents the absolute failure of the sign-gestalt to predict reality.

His lineage traces retrocausally to Kantian notions of synthetic a priori synthesis—the mind actively structures the world—but filters it through the rigorous lab-coat empiricism of the 1920s rat mazes. He builds directly upon Thorndike’s laws of effect but rejects pure trial-and-error, proposing instead that rats form cognitive maps ("expectancies"). 
From a forward-looking vantage point, Tolman's "sign gestalts" are the direct intellectual ancestors of Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and later, Predictive Coding theories (Friston). However, his discussion of "personality mechanisms" reveals a multi-week trap for modern interpreters: the temptation to conflate psychoanalytic metaphysics (Edipus complex, transference) with operationalizable cognitive architecture. While he mentions these psychoanalytic terms, his intent is to reduce them to "distortions" of basic learning rules. The trap is assuming Tolman validates Freudian structuralism; rather, Tolman is aggressively naturalizing it, stripping away the mystical Oedipal theater to reveal a malfunctioning Bayesian inference engine. He views repression not as a psychic guardian, but as a learning algorithm failing to propagate error signals due to overwhelming hedonic cost.
