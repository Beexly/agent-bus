If \(T_a > 30^\circ\text{C}\), thermoregulatory sweating raises baseline skin conductance by \(>10\,\mu S\), vetoing any attribution of a \(G(t)\) increase to psychic stimulus. Relative humidity \(RH > 70\%\) impairs evaporative cooling, further confounding conductance. Biomechanical veto: Mosso and Pellicani’s bladder plethysmography cannot distinguish detrusor smooth-muscle contraction from abdominal wall skeletal-muscle EMG artifact; isolated cystometry with EMG subtraction is required. In pregnant women, fetal movement is confounded by autonomous fetal motility and myometrial hormonal tone, vetoing a pure maternal-reflex interpretation. Weather veto: pupil diameter is enslaved to retinal illuminance \(E_v\) in lux. At \(E_v > 10^4\) lux, \(d\) is clamped near \(d_{\min}\approx2\) mm, vetoing fear-induced dilatation; at \(E_v < 1\) lux, \(d\) is at \(d_{\max}\approx8\) mm, vetoing further dilatation (ceiling effect). Photoperiod changes across seasons shift baseline \(d_{\mathrm{eq}}\) and pupillary light-reflex gain.

Adjudication rule: a visceral or glandular response is attributable to the sensorial/psychic stimulus described on [PAGE 878] only if (i) \(\Delta T_a < 1^\circ\text{C}\) and \(\Delta E_v < 100\) lux across baseline and stimulus epochs; (ii) abdominal-wall EMG is recorded and subtracted from bladder pressure traces; (iii) no autonomic pharmacology (atropine, anticholinergics, adrenergic blockers) is active; and (iv) response latency exceeds \(0.5\) s, consistent with central reflex arcs rather than mechanical artifact. Failure of any condition triggers a veto and nullifies the emotional attribution.

Scenario 1 — Thermal veto: Identical emotional film presented in two sessions at \(T_a = 22^\circ\text{C}\) versus \(T_a = 34^\circ\text{C}\), with skin conductance recorded. Veto condition: if thermal \(\Delta G > 5\,\mu S\) at \(34^\circ\text{C}\) exceeds the emotion-linked increment, the emotional attribution is voided. Prediction: at \(34^\circ\text{C}\), EDA tracks skin temperature and correlates with \(T_a\), not film valence; \(p(\text{emotion} \mid T_a=34^\circ\text{C}) \to 0\).

Scenario 2 — Photopic pupil veto: Fearful stimulus delivered under \(E_v = 500\) lux versus \(E_v = 10{,}000\) lux. Veto condition: if \(d\) is photically saturated (\(d \le d_{\min}+0.5\) mm) at high illuminance, dilation cannot be observed. Prediction: \(\Delta d \approx 0\) mm at \(10^4\) lux despite subjective fear; \(\Delta d > 1\) mm at 500 lux. The high-illuminance null result reflects a physical ceiling veto, not absence of fear.

Scenario 3 — Neuroanatomical bladder veto: Same abdominal sensorial stimulus applied to neurologically intact subjects versus subjects with complete spinal cord transection at T6. Veto condition: if afferent conduction is abolished, detrusor response must be zero regardless of stimulus intensity. Prediction: intact group shows detrusor contraction probability \(p > 0.5\); SCI group shows \(p = 0\) and no sphincter ani response. The null outcome confirms an anatomical veto rather than a psychological absence of sensation. All three scenarios are verifiable with standard psychophysiological instrumentation and yield binary veto/accept decisions.
