This is a self-consistency/consensus condition, not a global optimum — there is no potential function guaranteed for this multi-population flow, so "equilibrium" ≠ "correct". **6. Covariance descriptor.** Feature vector \(\;z_i = [\,x\;\; y\;\; H\;\; S\;\; V\;\; G_x\;\; G_y\;\; \operatorname{mag}(x,y)\;\; o(x,y)\,]^{\top}\) [PAGE 3, Eq. 4], with \(\operatorname{mag}=\sqrt{G_x^2+G_y^2}\) and \(o=\arctan(G_y/G_x)\). Covariance \(C=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_i-\mu)(z_i-\mu)^{\top}\) [PAGE 3]. **Dimensional-homogeneity defect:** \(x,y\) are in pixels (integer, large magnitude), \(H\) in degrees or [0,1], \(S,V\in[0,1]\), \(G_x,G_y\) in intensity·pixel\(^{-1}\), \(\operatorname{mag}\) likewise, \(o\) in radians wrapped to \((-\pi,\pi]\). \(C\) therefore mixes incommensurable units, and because the downstream distance is built on *generalized eigenvalues*, the entire similarity is not invariant to pixel-unit rescaling or hue-scaling. The paper reports no normalization of these channels, and this is an unreported free parameter.

**7. Geodesic distance.** \(\;\rho(C_i,C_j)=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\ln^2 \lambda_k(C_i,C_j)}\) [PAGE 3, Eq. 5]. Units: nats (\(\rho^2\) in nats²). Validity is conditioned on: "It is proven that ρ satisfies the metric axioms, positivity, symmetry, triangle inequality, for positive definite symmetric matrices." [PAGE 3]. **Exact singularity regimes** for this operator:
- **S1 (flat patch):** \(G_x=G_y=0\Rightarrow o=\arctan(0/0)\) is undefined; and \(\operatorname{mag}=0\) makes the last two channels constant, dropping rank by ≥2.
- **S2 (n = 1):** \(C = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum(\cdot)\) divides by zero.
- **S3 (rank deficiency):** if the patch's feature cloud is supported on a subspace of dimension \(r<d\), then \(C\) is singular (PSD but not PD), some \(\lambda_k = 0\), and \(\ln 0 = -\infty \Rightarrow \rho = +\infty\); positivity and triangle inequality both break. The metric axioms the paper relies on are *false* on the actual data distribution.
- **S4 (graph singularity):** \(\tilde W = D^{-1/2} W D^{-1/2}\) with \(d_{ii}=\sum_j w_{ij}\) [PAGE 4]; any isolated node (\(d_{ii}=0\)) makes \(D^{-1/2}\) undefined and the normalized similarity NaN. An isolated node's payoff is identically zero, so its equilibrium is the entire simplex — the readout \(\arg\max\) is then a tie broken by floating-point noise.
- **S5 (strategy-set collapse):** if \(c\) is unknown or a target leaves the frame permanently, the strategy set must change mid-game — explicitly deferred: "to handle a variable number of classes C" [PAGE 5].
- **S6 (dynamical cycling):** the flow is not a potential game; interior equilibria in cyclical configurations are not attracting, so convergence is not guaranteed for arbitrary \(W\).

**8. Reported performance, with units and provenance.** Precision/recall/F are dimensionless fractions in \([0,1]\):

The abstract-level superiority claim is dataset-conditional, not general.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The intellectual lineage of this six-page note runs on three independent strands that only meet at the 2012 junction paper, and the paper's own reference list [PAGE 5–6] is the primary evidence for the genealogy.

**Strand A — equilibrium existence.** "J. Nash, 'Non-Cooperative Games,' The Annals of Mathematics, vol. 54, no. 2, pp. 286–295, Sep. 1951" [PAGE 6, ref. 22]. Nash's existence theorem is what licenses the sentence "For the Nash equilibrium theorem [22] the GTG always has equilibrium in mixed strategies that corresponds to a steady state" [PAGE 2]. Note the retrocausal asymmetry: Nash guarantees *existence*, Daskalakis–Goldberg–Papadimitriou [25],[26] [PAGE 6] establish that *computing* one is PPAD-complete. The 1951 theorem is used to justify a 2018 algorithm, but the 2006 complexity result — also cited by the same authors — undercuts any claim of efficient exact solution. The algorithm's defence is that the replicator flow is a heuristic that converges on the benign instances actually tested, not that it solves the general problem. That distinction is suppressed in the prose and should be resurrected.

**Strand B — polymatrix specialization.** "L. Quintas, 'A note on polymatrix games,' International Journal of Game Theory, vol. 18, no. 3, pp. 261–272, 1989" and "J. T. Howson, 'Equilibria of polymatrix games,' Management Science, vol. 18, pp. 312–318, 1972" [PAGE 6, refs. 23–24]. The polymatrix restriction (pairwise edge games, additively separable payoffs) is what permits \(A_{ij} = w_{ij} I_c\). This is a *seventeen-year-old-at-time-of-writing* structural simplification, and it is the reason the payoff collapse in §3 above is possible. Without polymatrix structure there is no per-edge matrix and the whole graph analogy evaporates.

**Strand C — evolutionary dynamics.** "J. W. Weibull, 'Evolutionary game theory,' 1995" [PAGE 6, ref. 21] plus the Daskalakis complexity papers [25],[26]. The paper's methodology sentence — "The dynamic interpretation of Nash equilibria through the evolutionary approach imagines that the game is played repeatedly, generation after generation, during which a selection process acts on the multi-population of strategies, thereby resulting in the evolution of the fittest strategies." [PAGE 2] — is a near-verbatim restatement of the Weibull program. The crucial inherited commitment is *population thinking*: the "fittest strategy" language implies selection pressure, but the flow here has no mutation term and no finite-population noise, so it is a deterministic best-response-averaging process masquerading in biological vocabulary.

**Junction node.** "A. Erdem and M. Pelillo, 'Graph transduction as a noncooperative game,' Neural Computation, vol. 24, no. 3, pp. 700–723, 2012" [PAGE 6, ref. 20]. Everything theoretical in Sections II is imported from this single source; the present paper's contribution is explicitly the application: "To our knowledge this is the first application of transductive learning to multiple target" tracking [PAGE 1–2]. The retrocausal chain is therefore: Nash 1951 → Howson 1972 / Quintas 1989 → Weibull 1995 → Erdem–Pelillo 2012 → this 2018 application.

**Strand D — covariance geometry.** "F. Porikli, O. Tuzel, and P. Meer, 'Covariance tracking using model update based on lie algebra,' CVPR 2005" [PAGE 6, ref. 27]; "W. Forstner, B. B. Moonen, and C. Gauss, 'A metric for covariance matrices,' 1999" [PAGE 6, ref. 30]; "Y. Liu, G. Li, and Z. Shi, 'Covariance tracking via geometric particle filtering,' EURASIP J. Adv. Signal Process, pp. 1–22, 2010" [PAGE 6, ref. 29]; and "D. Coppi, S. Calderara, and R. Cucchiara, 'People appearance tracing in video by spectral graph transduction,' ICCV Workshops 2011" [PAGE 6, ref. 14]. The 1999 Förstner metric is being applied nineteen years later to a descriptor (HSV + Sobel orientation) that Förstner never contemplated, on a domain where positive-definiteness is not guaranteed. The genealogy reveals an *unexamined transplant*: a metric proven for PD matrices is deployed on empirically PSD-or-singular matrices, and the qualifier in "for positive definite symmetric matrices" [PAGE 3] is the only trace of the gap.

**Strand E — transduction lineage.** "X. Zhu, 'Semi-supervised learning literature survey,' 2006" [PAGE 6, ref. 1]; "T. Joachims, 'Transductive learning via spectral graph partitioning,' ICML 2003" [PAGE 6, ref. 2]; "D. Zhou, J. Huang, and B. Schölkopf, 'Learning from labeled and unlabeled data on a directed graph,' ICML 2005" [PAGE 6, ref. 3]. The paper positions itself against spectral solutions — "in contrast to common solution based on the spectrum of the graph" [PAGE 2] — but note it still *uses* the normalized Laplacian-adjacent object \(\tilde W = D^{-1/2}WD^{-1/2}\) [PAGE 4], so the break from spectral methods is rhetorical in the pre-processing and substantive only in the solver.

**Multi-week / multi-month trap genealogy.** Three traps are inherited, each with a documented temporal signature:
- **Trap 1 — drift by online model update.** "TT SpUpd" (spectral update) is described as "an improved version where the labelled data point are iteratively updated with an evolutionary spectral clustering" [PAGE 5]. Iterative label refresh accumulates a self-reinforcing bias; the paper exploits this by comparing its own *off-line* method against online updaters and winning on 3dPes (0.93 vs 0.64) [PAGE 5]. The trap: any future port to online operation inherits exactly this failure mode, which the authors themselves flag as future work.
- **Trap 2 — long-horizon degradation.** "especially with the 3dPes dataset we stressed the algorithm increasing the number of frames from 200 to 430 and even with the highest number of frames the results were satisfactory with the F-measure of roughly the 90%" [PAGE 5]. But Table I already reports 0.92 at 280 frames — so extending to 430 frames yields no measured gain and possibly a loss. The multi-week extrapolation ("long term tracking" [PAGE 5]) is asserted without an error-accumulation model.
- **Trap 3 — complete-graph blow-up.** "the adjacency matrix W is built over the complete set of people patches extracted from all the images composing the video" [PAGE 3–4]. \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\) with \(n \approx 280 \times 5 = 1400\) patches for 3dPes implies \(\sim\!10^6\) pairwise geodesic evaluations, each a \(9\times9\) generalized eigenvalue problem. Memory and time both scale as \(O(n^2)\); the largest reported run (430 frames × 5 targets ≈ 2150 nodes) implies \(\sim\!4.6\times 10^6\) eigenvalue solves. The paper reports **no** timing. Multi-month extension is therefore computationally, not just statistically, unbounded.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Modeling the *beliefs the system holds about itself* exposes three failure archetypes. Treat the pipeline as three agents: the **Detector** \(D\) (HOG), the **Game** \(G\) (replicator flow), and the **Evaluator** \(E\) (precision/recall accounting).

**Level 0 — Detector's self-model.** \(D\) is assumed infallible in the sense that its output patches *are* the node set. The paper concedes the counter-evidence only obliquely: on 3dPes "the targets are not always correctly identified by the detector due to occlusions" [PAGE 4], and false negatives are defined as "a missing estimation (e.g the ith target label is non assigned in the frame even if that target is present)" [PAGE 4]. The recursive problem: a false negative *removes the node*, so \(G\) cannot err on it, and \(E\)'s recall penalty is a purely detector-side loss attributed to the tracker. The tracker is blamed (or credited) for beliefs it never formed.

**Level 1 — Game's model of the Detector.** \(G\) has no such model. It treats \(w_{ij}\) as ground truth similarity. If \(D\) systematically shifts boxes (a known HOG behaviour near occlusion boundaries), the covariance descriptor \(C\) changes, \(\rho\) changes, \(W\) changes, and the equilibrium moves — with no mechanism to detect that the *measurement* changed rather than the *target*. The paper's "robustness" claim "even with a heavy unbalance between the number of labeled and unlabeled input patches" [PAGE 1] is a claim about label *count*, not label *position* fidelity.

**Level 2 — Evaluator's model of the Game.** \(E\) defines True Positive as "a patch classified correctly with its label" [PAGE 4] — a per-patch criterion. The paper's own framing is per-target association, so the metric and the objective are misaligned: a fragment of one person's track split across two IDs scores well per-patch in some frames and catastrophically on ID-switch rate, which is never measured. There is no IDF1, no MOTA, no track-fragmentation count anywhere in the six pages.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under stress (occlusion, as in 3dPes), the online baselines regress to their habitual failure: iteratively updating labelled points means the reference model migrates toward the occluder. The paper's numbers show the regression magnitude — TT NoUpd falls to 0.44 F and TT SpUpd to 0.64 F on 3dPes [PAGE 5], against GTG's 0.93. The regression signature is that *not updating* (TT NoUpd) and *updating* (TT SpUpd) both fail, in opposite directions: the former cannot follow appearance change, the latter follows it into the wrong person. Habit regression here is a false dilemma created by the online constraint, and the paper's off-line complete-graph formulation escapes it only by seeing all future frames at once — a luxury unavailable in the deployment it proposes.

**Counter-trap.** A counter-trap is an *adversarially wrong anchor*. Because labelled players are clamped ("they always play their already chosen kth pure strategy" [PAGE 2]), a mislabelled seed becomes an infinite-payoff attractor. Consider a single contaminated seed for identity \(1\) placed on identity \(2\): the bias term \(b_{i1}\) is positive across all of \(2\)'s patches, the flow converges, \(m_i \to 1\), and \(E\) records high precision on a confidently wrong labelling. **Counter-trap proof obligation:** the paper never injects label noise, never perturbs seed frames by ±1 frame, and never reports what fraction of contaminated seeds is survivable. Its robustness claim is one-sided (sparsity-only). The counter-trap prediction is therefore unfalsified *by the source itself* — a genuine evidentiary gap, not a settled robustness property.

**Clock.** The temporal resource is the labelled-frame budget. "These frames were randomly chosen and the results have been averaged on 20 different executions" [PAGE 4], swept over \(\{1,3,5,8,20\}\) frames [PAGE 6, Fig. 5]. The reported saturation — "almost saturate to satisfactory values when the number of frames is fixed to 5 demonstrating good reliability without requiring a large number of labelled data" [PAGE 4] — is the clock's key behaviour. The companion finding is variance collapse: "increasing the number of labelled frames also increase the stability of the solution while labelling only one or three frames, despite the precision and recall reach adequate levels, the standard deviation is large" [PAGE 4]. So the clock has two regimes: a **variance regime** (\(\le 3\) frames; mean adequate, dispersion unacceptable) and a **saturation regime** (\(\ge 5\) frames; mean flat, dispersion acceptable). Any deployment decision must be made on the dispersion channel, not the mean — and the dispersion channel is never tabulated, only described. Recursively, this means a reader cannot verify the clock behaviour from the tables alone; it exists only as a claim about Figure 5.

**Synthesis.** The system's theory of mind is shallow by construction: Detector has no model of Game, Game has no model of Detector, Evaluator models neither. The only genuine belief-state object is \(x_i \in \Delta^{c-1}\), and it is immediately collapsed to a point by \(\arg\max\) [PAGE 2, Eq. 1]. A tracker that reported \(H_i\) and \(m_i\) would expose its own uncertainty; this one cannot.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Each physical or numerical layer can independently veto the game layer. Veto means: no choice of \(W\), no seed set, and no equilibrium refinement can recover correctness.

**V1 — Photometric layer (illumination).** The design defence is stated: "The choice of using the HSV color space instead of the basic RGB is because of its invariance w.r.t. scale and shift variance of light intensity." [PAGE 3]. **Veto scope:** HSV is invariant to *scale and shift* of intensity, not to chromatic adaptation, white-balance drift across cameras, or hue wraparound at \(H=0/360\). 3dPes is explicitly multi-camera — "videos collected at different times of the day in an university campus from different surveillance cameras" [PAGE 4]. The layer veto: cross-camera appearance change is a chromaticity transform not in the invariance group, so \(\rho\) inflates, \(w_{ij}\to 0\) for the true match, and the graph disconnects *for that identity* — producing exactly the isolated-node singularity S4. **Verdict: provisional veto on any cross-camera generalization claim.** The 0.92 F on 3dPes is achieved with per-sequence processing; nothing in the paper demonstrates cross-camera identity linkage.

**V2 — Geometrical/occlusion layer (the "biomechanical" analogue).** Occlusion truncates the patch, changing \(n=w\times h\) and the feature cloud support. Table I's 3dPes entry (F = 0.92 across 5 targets, 280 frames) is the weakest of the three [PAGE 5], and the paper attributes this honestly: "The main challenge of this dataset, originally proposed for re-identification tasks, is the large number of occlusions of the targets, we thus use it to evaluate the behaviour of our method in critical situation." [PAGE 4]. **Veto scope:** truncation reduces the rank of \(C\) below \(d=9\); S3 fires; \(\rho\to\infty\); the pair is treated as maximally dissimilar even though it is the *same* person. Partial occlusion is therefore not a degradation but a *structural sign flip* in the similarity. **Verdict: hard veto on un-occluded covariance extraction.** The paper's own Fig. 4 [PAGE 4] shows bounding boxes during occlusion, but no per-occlusion-level breakdown is given, so the severity curve is unmeasured.

**V3 — Sensing/detection layer.** "we initially extracted those patches using a conventional people detector based on Histogram of Oriented Gradient" [PAGE 4]. HOG + linear SVM has a characteristic failure at low contrast and heavy truncation. **Veto scope:** a miss removes a node from \(V\); the corresponding true identity has zero mass in that frame, and the game's answer for that identity in that frame is undefined-by-absence, not wrong-by-estimate. This is a *veto of the evaluation*, not of the algorithm: recall is capped by detector recall, and precision is inflated because the hardest patches never enter the denominator. **Verdict: the reported numbers are conditional on detector recall, which is never measured.** No paper in this family can claim end-to-end tracking performance without a detector-only baseline; none is provided.

**V4 — Numerical/linear-algebra layer.** This is the strictest veto. The metric's validity sentence is explicitly conditional: "for positive definite symmetric matrices" [PAGE 3]. Empirically, \(C\) from a 9-channel patch is PD only if the \(n\) feature vectors span \(\mathbb{R}^9\) and \(n>9\). **Veto scope:** any patch with \(n\le 9\) pixels (rare) or with structural rank deficiency (common: flat regions, uniform clothing, near-zero Sobel response) produces \(\lambda_k\to 0\), \(\ln\lambda_k\to-\infty\), \(\rho\to\infty\). Numerically, implementations typically add a ridge \(\epsilon I\); the paper specifies none, so the reported results depend on an unstated regularization constant. **Verdict: veto on reproducibility.** Without a stated \(\epsilon\) and a stated PD-enforcement procedure, the distance matrix is implementation-defined.

**V5 — Topological layer.** Normalization requires \(d_{ii}=\sum_j w_{ij}>0\) for all \(i\) [PAGE 4]. **Veto scope:** if the bandwidth/thresholding implicit in \(w_{ij}\) (never specified — the paper gives \(\tilde W\) but not the kernel that produced \(W\)) leaves a node with all-zero row, \(D^{-1/2}\) is undefined and the row of \(\tilde W\) is NaN. Propagation then either crashes or silently zero-fills, and a zero row means uniform payoff ⇒ \(\arg\max\) is arbitrary. **Verdict: veto on un-thresholded complete graphs with sparse similarity kernels.** The paper's own choice of a *complete* graph [PAGE 3–4] mitigates this (all \(w_{ij}>0\) in exact arithmetic) but only by making the graph dense and thereby making the "similarity" numerically dominated by tiny residual values — a different failure, and the reason the normalization was adopted in the first place: "since it has been demonstrated that the normalization yield to better performance than the original similarity" [PAGE 4].

**V6 — Computational layer.** Polymatrix Nash is PPAD-complete [25],[26] [PAGE 6]. The complete graph over \(n\) patches costs \(O(n^2)\) distances and \(O(n^2 c)\) payoff storage. **Veto scope:** at 430 frames × 5 targets the node count is in the low thousands; beyond that, wall-clock and memory grow quadratically with no reported constant. Since "future work includes the extension to longer video sequence in order to test the robustness of the system also for long term tracking" [PAGE 5], this layer vetoes the stated roadmap unless a sparsification is introduced. **Verdict: soft veto — surmountable, but unaddressed.**

**Adjudication rule.** Layer precedence is V4 > V3 > V2 > V5 > V1 > V6. V4 is absolute because it invalidates the mathematical object the entire method is defined on; V3 is nearly absolute because it voids the measurement; the remainder are degradation terms that the game layer can partially absorb. **No result in Tables I or II is reported with any of these vetoes controlled.**
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario is stated as a falsifiable intervention on the source's own pipeline, with a prediction and a pass/fail criterion. All are checkable using only what the paper specifies.

**RLVR-1 — Positive-definiteness enforcement ablation.**
*Intervention:* replace the generalized-eigenvalue distance \(\rho^2=\sum_k \ln^2\lambda_k(C_i,C_j)\) [PAGE 3, Eq. 5] with a metric that is defined on PSD matrices (e.g., the Bures–Wasserstein or the Affine-Invariant metric restricted to the PSD cone), holding \(W\), the seeds, the HOG patches, and the 20-execution averaging protocol [PAGE 4] fixed. Also report the fraction of patch pairs with \(\min_k\lambda_k \le 10^{-8}\).
*Prediction:* the singular-pair fraction on 3dPes will exceed 10%, driven by occluded and low-texture patches; the PSD-safe metric will raise 3dPes F from 0.92 [PAGE 5, Table I] toward 0.95 but leave THIS essentially unchanged (0.99, near ceiling). On the single-target recast, the CAVIAR gap to TLT (0.91 vs 0.95) will narrow but not close.
*Falsifier:* if the singular-pair fraction is <1% on all three datasets, V4 is demoted from absolute veto to a non-issue, and the paper's unqualified use of Eq. 5 is vindicated.

**RLVR-2 — Label-contamination dose–response.**
*Intervention:* hold the labelled-frame budget at the paper's saturation point of 5 frames [PAGE 4], and systematically corrupt a fraction \(f\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.4\}\) of the seed labels by permuting IDs among the \(C\) targets. Report F-measure and, additionally, the equilibrium margin \(m_i\) and entropy \(H_i\) per node.
*Prediction:* because labelled players are clamped pure strategies — "they always play their already chosen kth pure strategy" [PAGE 2] — F will degrade *super-linearly* in \(f\) with a knee near \(f \approx 0.2\), and the margin distribution will become *bimodal and overconfident*: the corrupted identity's margin will rise toward 1.0 even as its F falls. That is, the system reports maximum confidence precisely on its most wrong labels.
*Falsifier:* if F decays only linearly and \(m_i\) remains calibrated (i.e., low margin on wrong labels), the clamped-bias design is self-correcting and the counter-trap in the previous section is dissolved. This is the single highest-value experiment absent from the source.

**RLVR-3 — Graph-density / detector-coupling ablation.**
*Intervention:* break the complete graph into a \(k\)-nearest-neighbour graph at \(k\in\{5,20,100,\infty\}\) on 3dPes, and simultaneously replace the HOG detector with a perfect oracle detector on a hand-annotated subset.
*Prediction (two parts, separately falsifiable):* (a) **Density:** F will be non-monotonic in \(k\), peaking at intermediate density — because the paper's own explanation for its gains is topological: "working with graphs with more nodes increase the possible paths among nodes representing the target patches in different frames" [PAGE 5]. If true, dense is better; but density also admits more spurious long-range paths, so the peak should sit below \(k=\infty\). (b) **Detector coupling:** with an oracle detector, 3dPes F should rise by ~5–8 points, and the *variance* across the 20 executions should collapse — proving that most of the residual error is detector-side, not game-side, and that the paper's "critical situation" framing [PAGE 4] conflates the two.
*Falsifier:* if F is monotone increasing to \(k=\infty\) with no interior peak, the "more paths" explanation is insufficient and a spectral/global-optimum account is needed instead; if the oracle-detector gain is <2 points, the detector-coupling veto V3 is refuted and the game layer genuinely dominates the error budget.

**Meta-prediction across all three.** Effect sizes will be ordered 3dPes > CAVIAR > THIS, because THIS is at ceiling (P = 1.00, R = 0.85 [PAGE 5, Table II]) and 3dPes carries the occlusion load. Any result that reverses this ordering — largest effects on THIS — falsifies the occlusion-driven account of the residual error and would force the conclusion that the reported dataset ranking is an artifact of sequence length rather than scene difficulty.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
