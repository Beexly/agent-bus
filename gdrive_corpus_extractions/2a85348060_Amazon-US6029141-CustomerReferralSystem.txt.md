<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The system contains several agents with nested beliefs. The associate believes that a copied or constructed URL will be parsed as intended. The merchant believes that associates will follow the prescribed format and that the parser will reject malformed strings. The customer believes that browsing is anonymous until checkout. The adversary believes that the parser can be manipulated by changing the associate ID, commission ID, or item ID. The enrollment agent models the applicant by scanning for “pre-specified terms” and flagging the application for human review [PAGE 9]. Formally, the associate’s belief about merchant parsing can be written \(B_a(B_m(\text{parse}(R)=\langle p,a,c\rangle))\). If the associate’s belief is wrong, the result is a link that looks valid to the associate but returns \(\bot\) at the merchant parser.

Crisis habit regression appears when time pressure or a failed link causes the associate to regress to a familiar but wrong habit: copy the browser address bar, paste the whole URL, leave the 19-character shopping-cart ID, use lowercase “a,” or include spaces or dashes in the ISBN. The patent’s counter-trap is explicit: “You \must\ use a capital A at the end of this link, not a lower-case a” and “be sure to remove the 19-character shopping cart ID that appears at the end of the bookmarked or copied URL” [PAGE 17]. The merchant’s parser is the trap detector; the associate’s habit is the failure mode. The clock creates a second regression: after a week of no sales, the associate may alter links, change product selection, or switch dissemination methods. The cart clock purges after one week [PAGE 14], the report clock is weekly [PAGE 18], and the payment clock is quarterly [PAGE 1]. The recursive layer is that the associate’s counter-trap must model the merchant’s parser, the merchant’s report must model the associate’s behavior, and the customer’s privacy expectation must model the merchant’s checkout boundary. The patent preserves that boundary by keeping PII away from associates before order submission [PAGE 13].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
For a referral commission to exist, define binary events: \(C\) = customer click, \(N\) = HTTP request delivered, \(P\) = parse success, \(A\) = valid and authenticated associate, \(S\) = order paid and shipped. The commission indicator is
\[
F=1 \iff C\land N\land P\land A\land S.
\]
Physical layer: the patent’s architecture requires a customer computer, an associate Web site, a merchant Web site, and the Internet [PAGE 5: “The system includes a customer computer 108, an associate Web site 100, and a merchant Web site 106, all of which are linked together by the Internet 104”]. Biomechanical layer: the customer must select or click the hyperlink; the patent states that when the customer selects the hyperlink, the customer is connected to the merchant site and presented with purchase options [PAGE 6: “When a customer selects (e.g., clicks on) the hyperlink associated with a particular product”]. Weather has no direct causal edge in the claim graph. Weather can veto only if it disrupts power or network transport, which would set \(N=0\). Conditional on \(C,N,P,A,S\), weather is independent of \(F\):
\[
P(F=1\mid W,C,N,P,A,S)=P(F=1\mid C,N,P,A,S).
\]
Thus a weather event cannot directly alter the URL parser, the associate database, the shopping-cart record, or the commission calculation. It can only act through infrastructure failure. Syntax and legal layers are stronger vetoes: malformed link format can set \(P=0\); unauthenticated or unqualified associate status can set \(A=0\) [PAGE 11: “referral credit may be withheld if the merchant has not yet authenticated and qualified the associate Web site for business”]. The cross-layer adjudication is therefore: no click means no request; no request means no parse; no parse means no cart entry; no valid associate means no attribution; no paid shipment means no fee. The physical/biomechanical/weather veto proof is complete because the only allowed weather path is through \(N\), and the only biomechanical path is through \(C\).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
