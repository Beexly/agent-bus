The paper states the boundary rule directly: if team \(i\) beats team \(j\), the rate from \(j\)-voters to \(i\)-voters is proportional to \(p\), while the reverse rate is proportional to \(1-p\) [PAGE 4]. The second hop is aggregation: because the walkers are independent, the expected populations \(v_i(t)\) obey a homogeneous linear ODE \(v'=Dv\), with \(D_{ii}=-p l_i-(1-p)w_i\) and, for \(i\ne j\), \(D_{ij}=\frac12 N_{ij}+\frac{2p-1}{2}A_{ij}\) [PAGE 5]. The multi-week traps are structural, not rhetorical. First is the date-blindness trap: the model ignores game dates, so an early loss and a late loss have the same edge effect; the source says “For simplicity, we ignore the dates of games” [PAGE 4] and “does not distinguish games based on the date played” [PAGE 12]. This matters in real seasons because a final-week loss can be treated as if it occurred in the opening week. Second is the connectivity-clock trap: the uniqueness theorem requires one connected component, but the graph “becomes a single connected component by the third or fourth week of the season” [PAGE 16]. Before that point, the theorem’s hypothesis is not licensed. Third is the interconference-reversal trap: the source’s edge-reversal experiment shows that “these first couple hundred edges removed are predominantly interconference games, whereas those that follow are intraconference games” [PAGE 19]. Reversing an early interconference edge has a systematically larger global effect than reversing a later intraconference edge. Fourth is the \(p\)-regime trap: near \(p=1/2\), the first correction is a schedule-strength Poisson potential [PAGE 8]; near \(p=1\), undefeated teams and undefeated subgraphs dominate [PAGE 9]. A multi-week analysis that fixes \(p\) without auditing that regime shift can therefore “retrocausally” reinterpret the same outcomes as strength-of-schedule evidence at one \(p\) and as undefeated-subgraph evidence at another. Fifth is the middle-rank crossing trap: because \(p_c\) can be arbitrarily near \(1/2\), a ranking can cross from win-loss-consistent to inconsistent with only a tiny change in \(p\) [PAGE 11]. The source’s first-last correction is a partial response, not a universal cure [PAGE 22].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The source licenses only a thin recursive psychology: each walker has a preference distribution over teams, but because walkers are independent, there is no walker-to-walker theory of mind in the base model.
