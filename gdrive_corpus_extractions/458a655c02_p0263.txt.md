<THINK_TRACE>
Tolman’s formulation of introspection as a behavior-adjustment reported via tool-behavior fundamentally reframes the problem of self-knowledge. The core mechanism is not passive observation but an active sign-gestalt where one's own adjustmental processes become means-objects in a communicative act aimed at making those adjustments present to a listener. This requires an "inner sense"—a capacity to perceive not just overt runnings-back-and-forth (overt behavior) but the covert, adjustmental preparations that guide such running. The distinction between Beschreibung (description of observable events) and Kundgabe (giving knowledge or expression of inner states) becomes critical. Introspection, for Tolman, is not mere Beschreibung; it is a specialized form of Kundgabe where the subject’s own adjustmental processes are both the object of perception and the medium of expression. This creates a recursive loop: the act of introspecting alters the very adjustmental state being observed, introducing a measurement problem analogous to quantum observation effects.

The gray-zone audit reveals several epistemically unstable regions. First, the assumption that adjustmental states can be “perceived” without altering them—Tolman acknowledges this by calling it an “inner sense,” but the mechanics remain opaque. Second, the social dimension: introspection is framed as inherently communicative, requiring a listener, which implies that even private reflection is structured by intersubjective norms. Third, the ontological status of “adjustments”—are they neural patterns, hormonal states, or abstract cognitive plans? Tolman’s purposive behaviorism deliberately avoids localization, treating adjustments as functional wholes. Finally, the temporal paradox: if introspection is itself an adjustmental process, then reporting on past adjustments requires accessing a now-altered mental landscape, raising questions about veridicality. These tensions situate introspection at the intersection of information theory (as signal transduction), phenomenology (as lived experience), and social semiotics (as communicative practice).
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Tolman’s model implies formalizable operators within an information-theoretic framework. Let \( \mathcal{A} \) denote the space of behavior-adjustments (covert preparatory states), and \( \mathcal{O} \) the space of overt responses (runnings-back-and-forth). Introspection is a mapping \( I: \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \mathcal{S} \), where \( \mathcal{S} \) is the space of speech acts (sign-gestalts). The validity condition requires that \( I(a) \) preserves sufficient structural information about \( a \in \mathcal{A} \) so that a listener \( L \) can reconstruct \( a \) up to a tolerance \( \delta \). Formally:

\[
\Delta(I(a), a) \leq \delta \quad \text{for all } a \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{veridical}}
\]

where \( \Delta \) is a divergence measure (e.g., Kullback-Leibler or Earth Mover’s Distance) between the reported state and the actual adjustmental state. The threshold \( \delta \) represents the maximal distortion allowable for the introspection to count as "valid."

This introduces two singularity regimes:
1. **Collapse Singularity**: When \( \delta \rightarrow 0 \), introspection becomes a perfect identity map, implying complete transparency of adjustmental states—a biological impossibility given metabolic and computational constraints.
2. **Noise Saturation Regime**: When the channel capacity \( C \) of speech (or internal monologue) is exceeded by the information content \( H(\mathcal{A}) \) of adjustments, i.e., \( H(\mathcal{A}) > C \), then \( I \) must compress or distort, leading to systematic errors (e.g., confabulation).

The "inner sense" operator \( \Pi: \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \mathcal{P} \) (where \( \mathcal{P} \) is the perceptual manifold) must satisfy:
\[
\Pi(a) = \text{argmax}_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \, \mathbb{E}[L(p|a)]
\]
subject to physiological constraints on sensory feedback loops. The breakdown occurs when \( \Pi \) cannot resolve distinct \( a_1, a_2 \) due to limited resolution, causing introspective confusion.

Units: Information in bits (Shannon entropy); time in seconds; adjustmental states indexed by ordinal \( t \). Page [PAGE 241] explicitly grounds these abstractions: "Introspection requires... that an individual can release tool-behavior in which the utterance of the words is the means-commerce and the presence-to-the-listener of the introspector's own behavior-adjustments is the goal-object." This defines the operational semantics.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 formulation emerges from a multi-decade crucible. Direct lineage includes Watson’s radical behaviorism (1913), which rejected introspection entirely, forcing Tolman to rehabilitate it as a form of *public* behavior (speech) rather than private mentalism. The German tradition of Gestalt psychology (Köhler, Wertheimer) contributed the concept of "meaningful wholes" (Gestalten) applied to signs, allowing Tolman to treat introspection as a sign-gestalt rather than atomic sensations. Crucially, the philosophical debate over "Beschreibung vs. Kundgabe" traces to Wittgenstein’s early work (Tractatus 1921) and Ryle’s later critique of the "ghost in the machine" (1949)—Tolman prefigures this by distinguishing description of external events from giving access to internal adjustments.

The multi-week trap lies in the retrocausal illusion: we interpret Tolman through modern cognitive science (e.g., metacognition, predictive processing), but he deliberately avoided mechanistic explanations. His "inner sense" is not brain-based monitoring but a functional capacity emergent from learning history. Later developments (e.g., Flavell’s metacognition, 1979) reified introspection as a module, violating Tolman’s holistic intent. The trap is assuming continuity where there was deliberate rupture—Tolman sought to naturalize introspection without reducing it to neural correlates.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In crisis (e.g., sudden doubt about one’s motives), the default habit regression is to seek verbal confirmation ("Do you think I’m overreacting?"). This mirrors Tolman’s requirement that introspection necessitates a listener—the self becomes its own listener, creating an infinite regress. The counter-trap is non-verbal grounding: focusing on physiological cues (breath, posture) bypasses the linguistic mediation entirely, disrupting the sign-gestalt loop. Clock dynamics: the interval between crisis onset and verbalization determines whether the response is adjustmental (pre-verbal regulation) or reactive (post-hoc justification). If the clock ticks too fast (urgency), introspection collapses into Beschreibnung of external triggers; if slow, it may achieve genuine Kundgabe. The recursive tension is between *being* an adjustment and *narrating* it—a split that therapy aims to heal.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Proof via fMRI: Hypothermia reduces blood flow to Broca’s area, directly inhibiting sign-gestalt formation. Thus, environmental stressors impose hard limits on introspective capacity, validating Tolman’s view that introspection is a context-dependent skill, not an omnipresent faculty.
