<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: A 95-yard return covers 86.864 m. At elite sprint speed (~9 m/s) this requires roughly 9.65 s of near-maximal effort; human anaerobic power decay imposes a velocity ceiling after approximately 6–8 s, so the returner must possess exceptional speed endurance or the defense must pursue suboptimally.

Biomechanical layer: Cuts and direction changes raise metabolic cost and reduce mean velocity. The tackle hazard \lambda(y) increases with defender density and is non-stationary near the goal line.

Weather layer: A fifty-plus-yard drop kick [PAGE 1] is sensitive to wind vector \vec{w} and air density \rho. Crosswind displacement scales approximately as \Delta x \approx (w_x/v_0)d; if |\Delta x| exceeds the crossbar lateral tolerance, the kick misses. Rain-softened turf can muff the plant foot of a drop kick, producing a low trajectory or turnover.

Veto proof: if surface degradation or wind exceeds the kicker's correction envelope, the midfield drop-kick attempt is physically vetoed; if defender convergence rate v_{def} exceeds ball-carrier speed plus separation, the run is tackled before the goal. These are hard physical constraints, not probabilistic wagering statements.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
