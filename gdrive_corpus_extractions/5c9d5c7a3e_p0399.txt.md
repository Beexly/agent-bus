Verifiable by comparing deposition rates into egg-containing versus experimentally emptied nests.

Scenario 2 — Broody-hen isolation: A broody hen (\(B=1\)) is placed in an environment with no eggs (\(S=0\)). Veto condition: bodily disposition without perceptual stimulus. Prediction: she will sit on the empty nest, confirming James's observation that broody hens sit where there are no eggs [PAGE 399]. This vetoes the hypothesis that egg perception alone causes sitting; it confirms that \(B\) is sufficient for sitting behavior but not for egg-directed incubation.

Veto condition: the term is left undefined so that all goal-directed behavior is classified as instinct. Prediction: the category's informational specificity collapses to zero bits; it becomes co-extensive with behavior itself, and James's distinction between blind instinct and reason becomes unusable, rendering the human instinct debate "fruitless" [PAGE 399]. Verifiable by behavioral coding: if 100% of acts receive the instinct label, precision for identifying non-reflexive, non-purposive action is zero.
