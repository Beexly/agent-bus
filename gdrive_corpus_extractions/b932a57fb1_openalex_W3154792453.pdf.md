Biomechanical veto: striking players sustain higher rotational acceleration for back-of-head impacts than front-of-head impacts, explained by forward head motion contacting an opponent’s body without helmet engagement; the accelerometer registers an anterior peak that is mapped to a posterior impact location [PAGE 7, citing reference 30]. Sensor placement (right of crown cushion) and the helmet-shell-to-centre-of-mass correction algorithm further intervene between raw measurement and reported kinematics [PAGE 2, 7]. This vetoes naive shell-level interpretation of rotational loading.

Environmental veto: the games were outdoor Fall contests in Ontario; temperature, humidity, precipitation, and helmet fit under varying thermal conditions were not modeled as covariates, and the GFT has mixed non-laboratory validation results [PAGE 8]. This vetoes transportability to indoor stadiums, different climates, or later-season conditions without recalibration.

Adjudication: only the interaction of player involvement × play type survives all three veto layers. The pooled main effect of involvement (striking vs struck) is null and does not survive biomechanical or structural scrutiny. Consequently, any safety recommendation must target kickoff and kick return play structure, not the striking/struck dichotomy in isolation.
