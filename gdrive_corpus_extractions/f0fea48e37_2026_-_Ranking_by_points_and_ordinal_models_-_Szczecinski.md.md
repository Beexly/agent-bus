<THINK_TRACE>
Let us trace the multi-hop derivation of Szczecinski's theorem and its gray-zone implications for frontier ranking. The starting axiom is the sufficiency of accumulated points $S$. In statistical decision theory, a statistic $T(X)$ is sufficient for a parameter $\theta$ if the conditional distribution $P(X \mid T(X), \theta) = P(X \mid T(X))$, independent of $\theta$. Applied to sports leagues, $X$ is the high-dimensional vector of match outcomes (e.g., win/loss/draw matrix), and $S = \sum_{i} w_k I(\text{outcome}_i = k)$ is the scalar league point total. The source [PAGE 1] states: "we find the score to be a sufficient statistic if and only if the points rule is constant-sum... and the outcomes follow the adjacent categories model whose slope parameters are the score-points themselves." This necessitates a rigorous mapping between the discrete outcome space and the continuous latent skill space $\Theta$.

The first derivation hop is identifying the probability manifold. Standard Bradley-Terry or Plackett-Luce models utilize logistic functions on skill differences. However, the adjacent categories logit model operates on ordered categorical outcomes (Win > Draw > Loss). If the odds of achieving at least category $k$ versus less than $k$ are governed by $\log \frac{P(Y \ge k)}{P(Y < k)} = \alpha_k + \beta (\theta_H - \theta_A)$, then the maximum likelihood estimates align with the observed scores only if the coefficients $\beta$ map perfectly to the awarded points $w_k$. The "constant-sum" constraint ($w_W + w_L = C$) arises because the sufficient statistic for an exponential family must aggregate probabilities linearly without cross-terms that depend on unobserved scheduling asymmetries. If $w_W = 3, w_D = 1, w_L = 0$, the sum is 3. But for sufficiency under the adjacent categories model, the transition slopes require a symmetrical base unit, effectively demanding $w_W = 2, w_D = 1, w_L = 0$ (sum = 2). Thus, modern football's 3-point rule structurally breaks the sufficiency condition.

Second hop: Schedule balance and Elo invariance. The paper notes: "when two players meet every other opponent equally often, and they are ordered identically by the score and by the estimated skills... The Elo ranking then produces the same order." We must analyze why. Elo updates rely on expected scores derived from a symmetric sigmoid function of skill differences. If schedules are perfectly balanced (venue-balanced round-robin), the noise introduced by home-field advantage and strength-of-schedule cancels out over the season. The cumulative observed results become an unbiased estimator of the underlying latent skill vector. Therefore, sorting teams by the simple sum of points (a zeroth-order estimator) yields the identical permutation as sorting by the converged Elo ratings (a higher-order iterative estimator).

Third hop: Gray-zone audit. Where does this theoretical perfection collapse? The "uniform rule awarding one point per outcome level" implies $w_W=3, w_D=2, w_L=1$ or similar uniform spacing. Actually, if levels are 1,2,3, points might just map directly to ordinal ranks. The text says: "the uniform rule... agrees with the estimated skills more closely than each league's own rule." This exposes a massive blind spot in league governance. Leagues prioritize marketing narratives (e.g., incentivizing wins via the 3-point rule) over mathematical coherence. The gray zone lies in the failure of the schedule condition. Modern leagues have asymmetric schedules (e.g., NHL divisional play, NFL conference biases). When Team A plays weak opponents at home and Team B plays strong opponents away, the sufficiency condition shatters. Points no longer isolate skill; they conflate skill with schedule topology. The "constant-sum" property becomes mathematically irrelevant if the exposure matrix is singular or highly ill-conditioned. The theoretical framework acts as a veto against trusting raw standings in unbalanced leagues, forcing a reliance on expected points metrics (like DVORAK or RAPTOR) which attempt to reconstruct the latent skill vector that raw points failed to capture sufficiently.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
We formalize the adjacent categories logit model for a match between Home ($H$) and Away ($A$) with latent skills $\theta_H, \theta_A$. Let outcomes $Y \in \{0, 1, ..., K\}$ representing strictly ordered levels (e.g., Loss, Draw, Win).
The probability mass function adheres to an exponential family form:
$$ P(Y = k \mid \theta_H, \theta_A) = \frac{\exp\left( \sum_{j=1}^k (\alpha_j + \beta_j (\theta_H - \theta_A)) \right)}{1 + \sum_{m=1}^K \exp\left( \sum_{j=1}^m (\alpha_j + \beta_j (\theta_H - \theta_A)) \right)} $$
[PAGE 1] explicitly requires the score $S_i$ for team $i$ to be a *sufficient statistic* for $\theta_i$. By the Factorization Theorem, the joint likelihood of all matches involving team $i$ must factor into $g(S_i, \theta_i) h(\mathbf{x}_{-i})$.
This necessitates the strict structural constraint on outcome weights $w_k$:
$$ \sum_{j \in \text{match}} w_{Y_j} = C \quad (\text{Constant-Sum Condition}) $$
Furthermore, the slope parameters in the logit link must correspond exactly to these weights:
$$ \beta_j = w_j - w_{j-1} $$
Under standard modern football rules ($W=3, D=1, L=0$), the sum is 3. However, the adjacent categories logit naturally assumes a baseline scaling where a unit increase in log-odds corresponds to the marginal point value. For $S$ to remain sufficient without requiring external covariates (like schedule strength), the differential utility of a Win vs Draw must equal the differential utility of a Draw vs Loss. Hence $3-1 \neq 1-0$, causing a breakdown. 
Theoretical Sufficiency Breakdown Regime: Occurs when $\exists k$ such that $w_k - w_{k-1} \neq w_{k+1} - w_k$ OR $\sum w_{Y_j} \neq \text{const}$.
Elo Convergence Operator: Let $\hat{\theta}$ be the skill estimate. If schedule adjacency matrix $M$ satisfies $M_{ij} = M_{ji}$ for all pairs $(i,j)$, then:
$$ \text{sgn}(S_i - S_j) = \text{sgn}(\hat{\theta}_i - \hat{\theta}_j) $$
Units: $\theta$ in logits (information units), $w$ in arbitrary discrete point integers, $C$ in integer points. Singularity occurs when $M$ is sparse or highly imbalanced, rendering $S$ statistically biased.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage of this paper traces back to the 1952 Bradley-Terry model, which established pairwise comparison probabilities using logistic functions of latent abilities. It was later expanded by David Cox’s 1958 work on predictive theory and the development of Item Response Theory (IRT) in psychometrics, specifically the adjacent categories logit model used for Likert scales. Sports analytics adopted these frameworks in the late 1990s with the advent of dynamic rating systems like Elo (introduced by Arpad Elo for chess in 1978 and adapted for soccer by Mike Magee in the early 2000s).
However, a multi-week trap emerged in the mid-1990s when major football federations universally adopted the 3-point-for-a-win rule to combat defensive stagnation. This was a purely heuristic, sociological intervention designed to alter player incentives. As Szczecinski [PAGE 1] highlights, this arbitrary change inadvertently shattered the "constant-sum" and "slope-parameter alignment" required for raw points to act as a sufficient statistic. For decades, analysts assumed the standings table was a pristine summary of team quality. The retrocausal realization is that the 1990s administrative tweak corrupted the information density of the standings table itself, creating a persistent illusion that a team with 30 points (mostly wins) is strictly comparable to a team with 30 points (mixed bag) in terms of underlying probabilistic skill. The uniform point system (e.g., 2-1-0 or even 3-2-1) preserves the geometric progression of probabilities required by the adjacent categories model, a fact ignored by modern sporting bureaucracy.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

They operate under the false premise that points are merely extrinsic levers, completely disconnected from the internal bookkeeping of the league's statistical validity.

They recognize the "schedule condition" failures and the violation of sufficiency. Instead of accepting the raw ordinal data provided by the league, they recursively infer the latent skill parameters $\theta$ through Maximum Likelihood Estimation or Bayesian hierarchical models, essentially discarding the league's official rank ordering in favor of an estimated one.
The clock on this cognitive divergence ticks louder as leagues introduce further complexities—salary caps, geographic divisions, and pandemic-shortened seasons. The raw scoreboard becomes increasingly divorced from true talent estimation. A recursive Theory of Mind analysis suggests that fans and bettors will continue to trust the "sufficiency" of the standings table until a catastrophic miscalibration event (a massively overrated team collapsing in playoffs due to weak schedule strength) forces a paradigm shift toward fully probabilistic public rankings.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The abstract mathematical elegance of Szczecinski’s sufficiency theorem faces immediate physical and biomechanical vetoes. The primary assumption of the adjacent categories model is stationarity: a team's latent skill $\theta$ remains fixed during the observation window. Biomechanically, this is impossible. Fatigue accumulation, injury cascades, and the compounding wear-and-tear of travel destroy the i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed) nature of match outcomes.
Consider the National Hockey League (NHL), cited in [PAGE 1] as failing the constant-sum condition (due to overtime/shootout rules distributing fractional points). Beyond the points rule, the physical layer imposes a veto on the very concept of a static "skill" parameter. Back-to-back games reduce reaction times and biomechanical output efficiency by measurable percentages. If Team A has a heavily loaded schedule while Team B rests, the estimated skill $\hat{\theta}_A$ derived from their points will be severely downwardly biased. No amount of mathematical adjustment to the $w_k$ weights can suffice if the underlying physical capacity to generate those outcomes is fluctuating daily.
Weather introduces another veto. Outdoor sports (football, baseball) suffer from environmental variance. A heavy rainstorm alters the physics of ball flight and traction, effectively changing the probability distribution of outcomes $P(Y \mid \theta)$ independent of skill. Thus, the raw points accumulated in such conditions are contaminated data. The mathematical sufficiency of points strictly holds only in a frictionless, vacuum-sealed environment with immortal, fatigue-free agents playing a perfectly balanced schedule—a regime fundamentally incompatible with biological reality.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

*Verifiable Reward Function:* Maximize Expected Points (xP) adjusted for opponent DVORAP.
*Veto:* Despite both teams finishing with 65 raw points (violating the pure sufficiency of $S$), the RLVR agent penalizes Team X's standing by -12% expected playoff win probability. The counterfactual prediction is that Team X collapses in the first playoff round against average competition, validating the schedule imbalance veto.

**Scenario 2: The Late-Season Attrition Veto**
*Condition:* In the final 5 games of an NHL season, a core forward suffers a lower-body soft tissue injury, reducing biomechanical skating velocity by 0.4 mph.
*Verifiable Reward Function:* Marginal Ice Time Quality (MITQ) and Corsi For%.
*Veto:* The team drops from 1st to 4th in raw points, drawing criticism that their "skill" degraded. However, tracking data shows their underlying xG generation remained top-3. The RLVR veto asserts that the drop in raw points was a biomechanical/weather veto, not a skill issue. Prediction: The team rebounds to top-2 performance in the following pre-season once full health is restored, exposing the raw points as a transient, insufficient statistic.

**Scenario 3: The Strategic Point-Maximization Paradox (Football)**
*Condition:* A team is mathematically guaranteed 2nd place but needs to win a specific margin of victory to secure home-ice/turf advantage for the next round. They adopt an overly aggressive formation (+15% risk of conceding, +20% chance of scoring 3+ goals).
*Verifiable Reward Function:* Probability of winning the match * Weighted importance of tie-breaker.
*Veto:* The team loses the match, dropping to 3rd place in raw points. While the 3-point rule incentivized the attack, the strategic context rendered the pursuit of the 'Win' outcome suboptimal for the actual tournament objective. The RLVR model predicts that playing for a Draw (securing 1 point and preserving tie-breakers) would have maximized overall tournament success probability. This confirms that optimizing for the locally awarded points ($w_W=3$) violates global strategic sufficiency.
