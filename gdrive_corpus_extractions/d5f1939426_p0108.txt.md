A later analyst, distrusting the mid-parent reduction, might model mother and father separately, add interaction terms, sex-of-parent effects, and maternal covariates. With N = 525 and unobserved Table 10 cell counts [PAGE 108], such a saturated model overfits noise, mistakes sampling fluctuation for biological mechanism, and throws away the very parsimony that Galton's Q test supports. The counter-trap is therefore not the corrective; it is the mirror error of under-regularization.

The clock layer arbitrates between the two. Galton's premise that stature is "nearly constant in the same adult" [PAGE 108] is true only at the terminal node t = adulthood. If the analyst's clock runs longitudinally—birth, childhood, puberty, adult—the mid-parent sufficiency claim is a boundary-condition statement, not a process statement. The recursive theory of mind thus nests four levels: (1) observed stature data, (2) the mid-parent deviation model, (3) the meta-model that Q equality implies sufficiency, and (4) the world of growth trajectories, secular trends, and environmental forcing. Under crisis (missing Table 10, unknown n_a/n_b, dependent siblings), the mind regresses to level 2; under reflection, it must check whether the clock of development and the environment of the world have vetoed level 3. The stable fixed point is not "mid-parent is always sufficient" but "mid-parent is sufficient only for adult stature within an exchangeable, environmentally homogeneous cohort."
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three physical layers can veto Galton's mid-parent sufficiency claim even when Q_a = Q_b on page [PAGE 108].

Physical layer (genetic–environmental constitution). Stature S is determined by additive genetic variance V_A, dominance/epistatic variance V_D, shared environment V_C, and individual environment V_E, with phenotypic variance V_P = V_A + V_D + V_C + V_E. Mid-parent captures only the additive transmission channel. If V_E or V_C is large relative to V_A—e.g., childhood disease, famine, or pollutant exposure—then two children with the same mid-parent can diverge substantially. Veto condition: if V_E/V_P > 0.5 in a population, mid-parent loses predictive dominance and the Q equality test is environmentally confounded. The claim "need not care whether the Parents are or are not unlike one another" [PAGE 108] fails whenever environmental variance differs systematically between group a and group b.

Biomechanical layer (skeletal growth and maturation). Stature is produced by chondrocyte proliferation at growth plates under GH/IGF-1, thyroid, and sex-steroid control. Mechanical loading and pubertal tempo impose bounds on final height. Group a (like parents) and group b (unlike parents) may differ in age at peak height velocity even if adult means coincide. Because Galton's D is computed around a fraternity mean M_i without an age covariate, a group with later maturation but equal adult stature will show inflated within-group D at intermediate ages. Veto condition: if mean age at measurement differs between groups or if pubertal tempo is heterogeneous, Q captures growth-tempo variance rather than hereditary dispersion. This layer vetoes any cross-sectional adult-only generalization unless all subjects are confirmed post-maturation.

Weather/environmental layer (seasonal and geographic forcing). Sunlight-driven vitamin D synthesis regulates calcium absorption and bone mineralization; temperature, humidity, and pathogen load modulate energy allocation to growth. If unlike-parent unions (group b) are geographically or seasonally distinct from like-parent unions (group a)—for example, due to migration, marriage selection across parishes, or occupational exposure—then the deviation scheme \mathcal{D}_b absorbs weather-correlated variance. Veto condition: if the seasonal or geographic variance component in stature exceeds the inter-group Q difference detectable at N = 525, the null result Q_a = Q_b is an artifact of uncontrolled environmental blocking, not evidence of hereditary irrelevance.

Adjudication rule. Galton's conclusion holds only if (i) environment is exchangeable across groups, (ii) all individuals are adult so the growth clock has stopped, (iii) measurement error \delta_{\mathrm{meas}} \ll Q, and (iv) the deviation distribution is approximately Normal so Q tracks \sigma [PAGE 108]. Failure of any condition invokes a layer-specific veto and invalidates the blanket sufficiency claim.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies an intervention, the verifiable observable, the prediction under Galton's null [PAGE 108], and the veto outcome that falsifies mid-parent sufficiency.

Veto Scenario 1 — Nutrition equalization. Intervention: Randomly assign children from like-parent (group a) and unlike-parent (group b) families to either a high-nutrition arm or a control arm for the full growth period. Observable: Adult stature variance within each parental group and nutrition arm; compute Q_a and Q_b separately per arm. Galton prediction: Q_a = Q_b within each arm; nutrition shifts the mean M but not the dispersion Q. Veto prediction: If Q_like \neq Q_unlike in the high-nutrition arm while Q remains equal in control, environmental variance was masking genetic dispersion differences; mid-parent sufficiency is environment-dependent and vetoed by the physical layer.

Veto Scenario 2 — Longitudinal growth tempo. Intervention: Measure the same 525 children (or a comparable cohort) at ages 6, 10, 14, and adulthood; compute Q based on deviation from age-specific means at each wave. Galton prediction: Because adult stature is nearly constant [PAGE 108], adult-based Q should equal the mid-parent-referenced Q; childhood Q values need not match adult Q if tempo varies. Veto prediction: If Q at age 10 significantly predicts group membership (like vs unlike parents) but adult Q does not, the cross-sectional adult test is a terminal-effect artifact; the clock layer vetoes static sufficiency because dispersion is developmentally dynamic.

Veto Scenario 3 — Maternal-effect stratification at constant mid-parent. Intervention: Hold mid-parent stature constant (e.g., within ±1 cm) and stratify children by maternal stature tertile (tall mother/short father vs short mother/tall father). Observable: Q of child deviation D within each maternal stratum. Galton prediction: Since only Mid-Parent matters and parental unlikeness is irrelevant [PAGE 108], Q should not vary by maternal stratum at fixed mid-parent. Veto prediction: If Q_tall-mother \neq Q_short-mother at identical mid-parent, then parental dissimilarity, maternal effects, or sex-of-parent transmission contribute to dispersion; the unlike-parent null is falsified at the mechanism level and mid-parent is not sufficient.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
