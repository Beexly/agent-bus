<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under stress—long occlusion, detector dropout, or a 34.7 ms frame budget from 28.8 FPS [PAGE 4]—the system’s safest habit is to fall back to the Kalman filter or a constant-velocity prior. That is not a moral failure; it is a risk-minimizing response when the learned hidden state is stale or uncalibrated. The paper’s own contrast is that Kalman filters are effective for “regular and linear movements” but struggle with “diverse and irregular motions” [PAGE 1]. A recursive tracker should therefore model its own belief about the motion regime: if the recent residual \( \|Y_t-B_t^{det}\| \) is low and the tracklet length is within the trained horizon, use Mamba; if residuals spike or detections vanish for more than \(n\) frames, regress to a conservative prior and widen association gates.

Counter-trap: the benchmark can reward the wrong habit. If HOTA/IDF1 improve mainly through tracklet merging, authors are tempted to add more post-hoc clustering; reviewers are tempted to accept the headline; deployers are tempted to ship the composite system without ablating the motion head. The recursive theory-of-mind move is to model the reviewer’s counterfactual: “Would this gain survive same-detector, same-association, no-merging controls?” The paper already gives a partial control by using ByteTrack detections on DanceTrack [PAGE 4], but MambaMOT+ changes the association pipeline via merging [PAGE 3]. A counter-trap-aware evaluation must report the ablation hierarchy: motion head only, motion head plus embedding, embedding plus clustering, and clustering-only.

Clock: the real-time claim is a scheduling constraint, not a proof. At 28.8 FPS, the nominal period is 34.7 ms [PAGE 4]; if detector inference, Mamba recurrence, Hungarian assignment, and clustering exceed that, the system will regress to cheaper habits. The 50-frame merging threshold [PAGE 4] corresponds to 1.67 s at 30 FPS, so a tracklet can be “same identity” across a long gap; that is a clock-dependent prior, not a physical law. The recursive policy should separate fast association (KF or short-horizon SSM) from slow trajectory embedding and clustering, and should expose a latency budget to the user rather than hiding it inside a single FPS number.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: after camera homography, let \(\mathbf{c}_t=(x_t+w_t/2,\;y_t+h_t/2)\) in metres. A predicted box is physically inadmissible if
\[
\|\mathbf{c}_t-\mathbf{c}_{t-1}\|_2 > v_{\max}\Delta t+\epsilon_v
\]
or
\[
\|\mathbf{c}_t-2\mathbf{c}_{t-1}+\mathbf{c}_{t-2}\|_2 > a_{\max}\Delta t^2+\epsilon_a,
\]
with \(v_{\max}\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(a_{\max}\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), \(\Delta t\) in seconds, and \(\epsilon_v,\epsilon_a\) as measurement tolerances. For human sports, illustrative conservative priors are \(v_{\max}\approx12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) and \(a_{\max}\approx12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\); for dance, lower values apply. The paper does not provide homography or physical calibration, so the veto is only implementable after external calibration; without it, the “50 pixels” threshold [PAGE 4] is not a physical proof. Also require \(w_t,h_t>0\) and a plausible human aspect ratio; zero-area or inverted boxes veto IoU/GIoU.

Biomechanical veto: bounding boxes do not contain joint angles, limb lengths, or ground reaction forces. The source uses \([x,y,w,h]\) tracklets [PAGE 2] and has no pose keypoints or biomechanical variables. Therefore any claim that MambaMOT validates biomechanical feasibility is unidentifiable from the source. A bbox-only audit can enforce continuity, size, and acceleration bounds, but it cannot prove gait, balance, or joint-torque plausibility. Veto strong biomechanical wording; allow only “bbox-motion plausible.”

Weather veto: the paper evaluates DanceTrack and SportsMOT [PAGE 3–4] and reports no rain, snow, glare, fog, or thermal covariates. Weather affects detector recall and blur, which in turn corrupt the input boxes \(x_t\); the SSM cannot recover information that is absent from its input. Therefore a weather-robustness claim is vetoed unless a new experiment reports detector-miss rates and tracking metrics under stratified weather conditions. The physical veto also applies: if weather causes a large box-center jump beyond \(v_{\max}\Delta t\), the prediction should be rejected rather than merged. In short, the source proves a benchmark-level motion-model improvement, not a cross-layer physical, biomechanical, or weather guarantee.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
