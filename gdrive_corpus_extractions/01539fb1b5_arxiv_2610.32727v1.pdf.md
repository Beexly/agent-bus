Crisis habit regression: when a human player is under anti-cheat scrutiny or competitive pressure, motor entropy regresses to stereotyped, low-variance habits (repeated hook angles, fixed jump rhythms), which shrinks intra-player embedding variance and helps nearest-centroid separation but simultaneously increases cross-player collision risk when multiple stressed players converge on similar optimal routes. Counter-trap: smurfing [6] and cheating are deliberate attempts to break fingerprint consistency—smurfs play below rated skill, cheaters use aim assist or macro inputs—producing the out-of-manifold clusters 0 and 3 detected by UMAP/HDBSCAN [PAGE 6-7]. A sophisticated adversary would not merely cluster separately but would actively mimic the population distribution (input jitter, randomized timing, client-version rotation) to force centroids to overlap, turning the mean-pooling assumption of Proxy Anchor Loss into a liability. Clock: the physical simulation runs at 50 Hz [PAGE 2], imposing a hard Nyquist-like bound on resolvable input changes; the 1,000-timestep window (20 s) is the integration clock for the LSTM, and M∈{1,3,6} extends the decision clock to 20 s, 60 s, 120 s [PAGE 4]. Longer averaging smooths short-term counter-adaptations but assumes player identity and client configuration are stationary over 120 s; if a cheater toggles assistance mid-sequence, the aggregated query vector becomes a convex blend of human and non-human sub-trajectories, degrading identification and creating false centroids. The recursion closes at deployment: any operational system must model the adversary’s theory of mind (will the cheater regress to human-like noise under pressure?) and the physical clock (can 20 s of 50 Hz input disambiguate a macro from a human?).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto: DDNet is a physics-based side-scrolling platformer with grappling-hook mechanics at 50 discrete timesteps per second [PAGE 2]. Human motor output is bounded by reaction latency and bounded cursor/angular velocity; cluster 0 exhibits “input precision and speed far beyond human capability” [PAGE 6], a direct physical-layer violation. Any embedding claiming to represent a human player that falls in the cluster-0 region is vetoed as non-human and routed to cheat-client adjudication rather than player identification. Biomechanical-layer veto: cursor position is encoded as continuous polar angle and distance [PAGE 2]; human hand trajectories exhibit tremor and continuity, whereas cluster 3 shows cursor movements that “snap to positions,” indicating aim assistance [PAGE 6-7]. Snapping violates the continuity prior of the 8-dimensional input manifold, so embeddings from cluster 3 are biomechanically inadmissible as regular player fingerprints. Weather/environmental layer: no meteorological variable exists in a deterministic game client; however, client modification acts as an environmental confounder. Because UMAP shows known and unknown players overlapping in one large smooth cluster while four small dense clusters map to specific client modifications [PAGE 6-7], client version is a latent conditioning variable. Veto rule: before nearest-centroid assignment, run a client-integrity check; if the query embedding falls in a separated modification cluster, veto player-ID output and flag client anomaly. Adjudication proof: the embedding space is mostly smooth and uniform for regular players [PAGE 6], but the existence of well-separated cheat clusters means an unconditional centroid classifier risks assigning multiple humans to one cheat centroid or splitting one human across client versions; therefore the veto must precede classification, not follow it.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with quantitative predictions. Each scenario is constructed so that, if the prediction holds, the corresponding architectural or data assumption in the paper is falsified.

Veto scenario 1 (temporal-context ablation): Reduce sequence length from 1,000 timesteps (20 s) to 250 timesteps (5 s) while keeping M = 1 and n = 1000. Veto condition: if unknown-player balanced accuracy KU→U falls below the uniform-chance bound for 1,000 classes plus a small margin, i.e., < 0.002, the claim that 20 s is near-minimal for behavioral fingerprinting is invalidated. Prediction: with only 5 s of grappling-hook and movement dynamics, the single-layer LSTM cannot integrate sufficient cross-feature temporal structure; KU→U M=1 balanced accuracy collapses to approximately 0.0015 ± 0.0003 (at or below chance), while K→K M=1 also drops well below 0.7899. This veto targets the assumption that short raw-input windows contain discriminative individual patterns [PAGE 2-3].

Veto scenario 2 (normalization leakage): Replace train-only Z-score statistics with pooled train+test mean and standard deviation before encoding, thereby leaking test-distribution information into every embedding [PAGE 2]. Veto condition: if known-player K→K M=6 balanced accuracy exceeds 0.9995 while unknown-player KU→U M=6 falls below 0.50, the train-only standardization assumption is violated and reported transfer is an artifact. Prediction: pooled statistics align query embeddings to test-set reference geometry, inflating K→K M=6 to ≈ 0.9998 ± 0.0001 (essentially perfect memorization of the test split), while KU→U M=6 collapses to ≈ 0.45 ± 0.02 because unknown-player centroids are pulled toward known-player test statistics. This would falsify the claimed generalization gap between known and unknown players at n = 1000 [PAGE 5, Table II].

Veto scenario 3 (gradient-clipping removal): Remove LSTM gradient clipping (max norm → ∞ instead of 10) on the 1,000-timestep unidirectional LSTM [PAGE 4]. Veto condition: if training loss diverges to NaN within fewer than 50 epochs, or if the early-stopped model yields unknown-player KU→U M=6 balanced accuracy below 0.30, the necessity of clipping for convergence is confirmed by counterfactual failure. Prediction: backpropagation through time over 1,000 steps with Proxy Anchor Loss and learning rate 1e-3 produces exploding gradients; validation loss fails to improve by ≥1% over 20 consecutive epochs, triggering early halt, and the resulting model achieves KU→U M=6 ≈ 0.28 ± 0.04, far below the reported 0.8786 [PAGE 5]. This veto isolates gradient clipping as a load-bearing component rather than a incidental hyperparameter.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
