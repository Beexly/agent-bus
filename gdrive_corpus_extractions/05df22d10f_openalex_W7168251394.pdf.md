**O1 — Multi-view posterior operator (the baseline).**
[PAGE 4]: `ˆa = MLLM(S), S = [ q; ⟨V 1⟩X1; . . . ; ⟨V N⟩XN ], (1)` and `ˆa = arg maxak∈A log pθ(ak | S)`.

Formally, with score function ℓ(a) ≡ log[p(a|S)/(1−p(a|S))] in **nats**, and X_i the uniformly sampled frame set from view V_i at ρ = 2 fps:

ℓ(a | X_1..X_N, q) = ℓ_0(a|q) + Σ_{i=1}^{N} [ℓ_i(a|X_i,q) − ℓ_0(a|q)]

*Assumption A1:* conditional independence of views given a (naive-Bayes fusion). *Units:* nats per decision. *Breakdown regime:* let ρ̄ be mean pairwise view redundancy after DINOv2 agglomerative clustering [PAGE 12]; effective independent view count N_eff = N/[1 + (N−1)ρ̄]. This is the formal statement of the observed +0.91-point result.

**O2 — View-selection operator.**
σ*: 2^V → 2^V, σ*(V) = argmax_{U ⊆ V, |U| ≤ k, tok(U) ≤ B} I(a*; X_U | q), **units: nats**.

*Assumption A2:* submodularity of I in U. **This assumption fails.** Marginally, Δ_j = I(a*; X_j | X_U, q) can be negative when V_j is occluded or misleading, so greedy selection's (1 − 1/e) guarantee does not apply and the argmax may be empty or non-unique. **Singularity:** when max_U I(a*; X_U|q) is attained at |U| = 0, the view operator is undefined and control must transfer to tool execution — exactly the branch structure of Alg. 1 [PAGE 5]. The benchmark's causal ancestry runs backwards through officiating technology, not through computer vision. Three inherited biases travel down that genealogy. First, **review-selection bias**: only contested plays are reviewed, so the corpus over-represents overturn-eligible events and the prior over decision classes is not the match-flow prior. Second, **consensus-label bias**: the gold answer is filtered by LLM proposal, GPT-5 audit repeated three times with shuffled view order [PAGE 3], and five annotators with a 13.81% discard rate [PAGE 13] — three correlated filters whose errors are not independent. Third, **broadcast-aesthetic bias**: [PAGE 12] retains only shots classified *game* and discards *close-up* and *graphic*; but tight close-ups are frequently where contact detail is actually legible, so the filter that removes scoreboard noise also removes the highest-resolution evidence.

Multi-week traps. (a) *The taxonomy trap*: because tools are closed-set to SoccerNet/FineSports classes [PAGE 10], any model tuned on this benchmark learns a decision boundary aligned to those taxonomies and will silently mis-label at deployment on sports or leagues outside the ten. (b) *The asynchrony trap*: the paper asserts asynchrony "further increases task difficulty" [PAGE 12] but never ablates synchronization. A team that spends weeks building a cross-view time-alignment module may find the gain is zero — the claim is currently untested and therefore unfalsified rather than supported. (c) *The guided-prompt trap*: M-View (Guided) at 49.04 already exceeds the full agent's PAR-minus-tools pathway for some categories; a team could ship a prompt and capture a large fraction of the headline gain with none of the loop infrastructure.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
*Habit regression.* The orchestrator must hold a second-order belief about its own perceptual state — not "what happened" but "whether I can see what happened." Under distribution shift (dense occlusion, [PAGE 1]: "dense occlusion, rapid motion, and complex interactions that are difficult to resolve from a single viewpoint"), the model regresses to its pretraining habit: narrate a single salient frame and commit. The measured signature is unambiguous — COT drops Qwen3-VL-30B from 43.26 to 39.32, and PAR from 42.63 to 39.82 [PAGE 4]. Self-reflection without new visual evidence amplifies the habit rather than correcting it. Because Alg. 1 gates termination on conf(H_t), the loop inherits exactly this miscalibrated confidence: a model that is confidently wrong in one pass will be confidently wrong at step one and exit immediately.

*Counter-trap.* The reflex response — add capacity — is measured and mostly fails. Sports knowledge adds 45.43 vs 43.26 = +2.17 points [PAGE 4], and the paper's own lesson is "Domain knowledge is not the primary bottleneck" [PAGE 5]. A stronger perception tool adds +0.41 [PAGE 7]. Only the *action* of looking elsewhere pays, and it pays 7.72 points. The counter-trap for a practitioner is to interpret the 15.61% headline as validating the agent architecture when the telescope says 83.6% of it is the view-switch primitive.

*Clock.* Three clocks bind simultaneously. The step clock t_max is unstated, so loop cost is unbounded in the published description. The sampling clock is 2 fps [PAGE 6], which is the aliasing constraint derived in O5. The corpus clock is the duration window — "15–45 s" and "15–90 s" [PAGE 12] — which caps how much temporal context any single view can contribute. A theory-of-mind that ignores the sampling clock will over-attribute failures to reasoning and under-attribute them to the shutter.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer 1 — projective geometry (hard veto).** Occlusion is not a model property; it is a set-membership property. Player j occludes contact point p from camera c iff p ∈ cone(c, B_j). Joint visibility requires ∃c ∈ C with p ∉ cone(c, B_j) ∀j. Since σ(V) ⊆ V, view selection cannot enlarge the camera ring: I(a*; X_{σ(V)}) ≤ I(a*; X_V). If the ring subtends < 180° (typical: one wide plus two or three same-side close-ups), P_irr = P(no camera sees p) > 0 and, with |A| = 4, Acc ≤ 1 − 0.75·P_irr. **Veto proof:** this bound is independent of N, of orchestrator capability, and of tool quality. Any claimed agentic gain must be shown to lie inside this ceiling; the paper reports no such stratification.

**Layer 2 — optics and sampling (hard veto).** At 2 fps the integration window is 0.5 s while a clip/trip contact is ~0.05–0.15 s. The perception channel is a low-pass filter with cutoff far below the event bandwidth; the information is destroyed before the model sees it. Orchestrator scaling (+10.99) cannot recover aliased signal. **Veto:** stratify accuracy by measured motion blur (Laplacian variance) and by within-clip optical-flow magnitude; if the orchestrator-scaling slope flattens to zero in the high-blur decile, the +10.99 does not generalize to the physically hard subset.

**Layer 3 — biomechanics (soft veto).** Contact detection T_con emits a binary [PAGE 6]. Sustained-force fouls (holding, pushing) persist 0.2–1.0 s and are over-sampled; impulsive fouls (clip, trip) are under-sampled. The tool's recall is therefore systematically biased toward sustained contact. **Veto:** if recall(holding/pushing) − recall(clip/trip) ≥ 0.10 on a stratified probe, the binary contact operator is not a neutral evidence source and downstream ADR accuracy is confounded by foul-type prevalence.

**Layer 4 — weather (confounder, not veto).** Wet pitch raises required shutter speed, lowers exposure, raises gain and noise, and simultaneously changes sliding friction and ball roll — coupling image quality to the *semantics* of the label (slip vs tackle). The benchmark does not stratify by weather, so this remains uncontrolled.

**Adjudication rule:** a claim survives only if it holds under the worst-case layer. The 15.61% headline survives Layers 3 and 4 as a corpus-average effect; it does **not** survive Layer 1 or Layer 2 as a statement about capability, because both impose ceilings that are invariant to the intervention being credited.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
