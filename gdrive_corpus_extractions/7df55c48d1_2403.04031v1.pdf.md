<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and the game clock.

Under high information density (NBA), models exhibit crisis habit regression: instead of maintaining a symbolic running total, they regress to associative retrieval of team names, memorized scores, or plausible-sounding aggregates. This is visible in the hallucinated response in Figure 6, where Llama-2-70b invents player point totals and a final Cavaliers-Pistons score that never appears in the input [PAGE 12], and in Figure 7, where masked teams are hallucinated by GPT-3.5, Claude-2.1, and Llama-2-70b [PAGE 12]. The theory-of-mind failure is that the model simulates a sports commentator rather than a scorekeeper: it generates narrative consistency (team names, leads, final scores) without enforcing the conservation constraint that quarter points equal the sum of scoring plays.

The counter-trap is divide-and-conquer with n = 3. By forcing the LLM to act as a local executor on short clips, the method temporarily restores the scorekeeper's mental state: initialize team scores, iterate plays, add points on made shots, print final scores [PAGE 4, Figure 3]. However, this reintroduces a serial dependency: each clip begins with no persistent memory of prior clips, so the model must re-derive context or trust the aggregation program. The recursion breaks down when per-clip error \epsilon_k is non-zero; aggregation cannot recover lost points, and over-counting in one clip cascades into the quarter total, explaining MAPE > 100% for Llama-2-70b and Gemini-Pro [PAGE 6].

The clock is a causal constraint. The source emphasizes that the "Time" column displays times in descending order because "the game clock counts down" [PAGE 4]. A model that reorders plays or attributes a score to a time before possession occurs has violated the scorekeeper's theory of mind. Temporal order is therefore not decorative; it is a hard constraint on the admissible state-transition graph of the scoring process, and violations are most likely at small step sizes where system messages "overshadow[ ] the critical play-by-play data" [PAGE 8].
