The footnote explicitly invokes "Kóhlers experument on usng a box to reach a stick h dden in the rof in order to use the st ck to rake in food" and cites "Mentahty of Apes i925 pp 18ff" [PAGE 190]. This is a means-end hierarchy par excellence: the box is a means to reach the stick; the stick is a means to rake the food; the food is the terminal goal. Tolman imports this Gestalt-insight structure into his purposive-behavior framework, replacing Köhler's sudden "insight" with a more gradual expectation-chaining over path divisions.

Tolman's position sits between strict behaviorism and cognitive representationalism. By asserting that rats form "correct expectations dependent upon" hierarchical principles [PAGE 190], he anticipates later cognitive-map theories, yet he remains committed to the idea that logic—not psychology—should settle the formal count of independent means-end relations [PAGE 190]. This creates a retrocausal temptation: reading modern unified cognitive architectures (e.g., predictive processing or hierarchical reinforcement learning) back onto 1932 as if Tolman had already demonstrated the "one capacity" he only speculates about [PAGE 190].

The multi-week trap is specific to this genealogy. Köhler's ape experiments were not one-trial insight events; they involved days or weeks of manipulation, failed attempts, and restructuring. Tolman's rat work (cross-referenced at p. 128 f [PAGE 190]) similarly depended on extended maze training. A synchronic reading of page 190 treats the "field" as if it were fully formed at the moment of observation, ignoring the diachronic consolidation over weeks that makes hierarchical expectations possible. The trap is to mistake the mature, post-training field for the initial state of the organism, thereby projecting the endpoint of learning backward onto the start.

Furthermore, the footnote's OCR degradation ("i925" instead of 1925, etc.) introduces a source-chain trap: secondary citations to Tolman may propagate the OCR errors, compounding uncertainty about the exact Köhler page range and experimental details. Verification requires returning to the 1925 German/English original, not the 1932 secondary report.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The theory-of-mind recursion on page 190 operates across three layers. Layer 1 is the organism: the rat, cat, ape, or man forms expectations about means-end relations [PAGE 190]. Layer 2 is the observer: Tolman attributes to the organism a capacity for "correct expectations dependent upon" hierarchicalness [PAGE 190]. Layer 3 is the meta-observer: Tolman proposes that the logician should formalize these relations [PAGE 190]. Each layer risks projecting structure that belongs to the analyst rather than the subject.

Crisis habit regression describes what happens when the expectation mechanism is stressed. If the joining operator \(\bowtie\) fails—because path divisions cannot be concatenated—the organism may regress from hierarchical expectation to simpler associative chaining. Tolman's rat, confronted with a disrupted total path, may revert to local cue-response habits rather than maintaining a representation of the whole path. This regression is not described on page 190, but it is the necessary counterpart to the claim that higher animals "to a much larger degree" use hierarchical expectations [PAGE 190].

The counter-trap is the inverse error: the analyst imposes a logical, hierarchical grammar on behavior that the animal does not actually use. Tolman is aware of this risk; he refuses to "usurp" the logician's function [PAGE 190], implicitly acknowledging that his psychological vocabulary may outrun the formal structure available to describe animal behavior. The counter-trap is to treat Tolman's "forms" [PAGE 190] as if they were internal representations in the rat's mind, when they may be merely convenient descriptions by the human observer.

The clock enters through direction-distance correlations [PAGE 190]. Expectations are not static; they are temporally indexed by leading-on-ness events and by the metric relation between direction and distance. If the reward is delayed beyond the expected latency, or if the spatial direction is rotated, the clock of the expectation is violated. Under clock violation, the probability of correct expectation decays, potentially triggering crisis habit regression. The recursive loop closes when the experimenter, observing the regression, attributes a failed theory of mind to the animal rather than recognizing a violation of the experimental clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Tolman's cognitive account operates only above a physical veto layer. Köhler's box-stick-food experiment [PAGE 190 footnote] requires biomechanical preconditions: the ape's arm length \(l_a \in \mathbb{R}^{+}\) (meters), grip force \(F_g \in \mathbb{R}^{+}\) (newtons), and box mass \(m_b \in \mathbb{R}^{+}\) (kilograms) must satisfy roof height \(h_r \le l_a + h_b\), where \(h_b\) is box height. Formal veto: \(V_{bio} = 1\) iff \(\exists c \in \mathcal{C}_{bio}\) such that \(c(s) > c_{crit}\). When \(V_{bio} = 1\), \(P(\text{rake food} \mid \text{expectation}, V_{bio}=1) = 0\), regardless of the strength of the cognitive expectation [PAGE 190].

For the rat in a maze, the locomotor layer imposes \(v_{max} \in \mathbb{R}^{+}\) (meters per second). If path divisions require traversal speed exceeding \(v_{max}\) within the expectation window, the joining of divisions fails physically, and the cognitive expectation is vetoed. Tolman does not specify \(v_{max}\) on page 190, so this constraint is external to the text but necessary for any complete model.

Environmental layer: Köhler's experiment is indoor and thus insulated from weather, but Tolman's rat work implicitly depends on ambient temperature \(T_a \in \mathbb{R}\) (degrees Celsius) and illumination \(E \in \mathbb{R}^{+}\) (lux). These variables are absent from page 190, representing an underspecification: if \(T_a\) or \(E\) falls outside the rat's operative range, expectation formation may be vetoed before any means-end computation occurs.

Adjudication rule: Tolman's field-theoretic claims hold only in the veto-free regime \(V = V_{phys} \lor V_{bio} \lor V_{env} = 0\). In Köhler's setup, if the stick is hidden behind a roof aperture too small for the arm to pass, the physical layer vetoes the means-end chain regardless of the ape's expectation. The cognitive layer cannot override biomechanical impossibility. Therefore, any inference from observed success to "correct expectation" must first condition on \(V=0\); otherwise the attribution of purposive behavior is confounded by unmeasured physical constraints.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR counterfactuals here are verifiable interventions designed to falsify Tolman's claim that expectations depend on joining path divisions and hierarchical means-end relations [PAGE 190].

Scenario 1 (Tool removal). In Köhler's paradigm [PAGE 190 footnote], after the ape has formed an expectation of food, remove the stick but leave the box and food in place. Veto: no tool exists to bridge the distance to food. Prediction: \(P(\text{food obtained}) = 0\) despite \(P(\text{expectation of food}) > 0\). Verifiable by video scoring: the ape may reach for the box but cannot execute the rake. Breakdown if \(P(\text{food obtained}) > 0\) without the stick, which would imply an alternative means not captured by the box-stick-food hierarchy.

Scenario 2 (Path discontinuity). In a rat maze based on Tolman's p. 128 f reference [PAGE 190], insert a physical barrier between path divisions \(\gamma_i\) and \(\gamma_{i+1}\) such that the joining operator \(\bowtie\) is undefined in state space. Veto: the total path cannot be traversed. Prediction: the rat exhibits local search or circling at the barrier rather than goal-directed traversal of the total path; \(P(\text{goal reached})\) drops to baseline chance. Verifiable by trajectory logs with positional resolution in meters and timestamps in seconds. Breakdown if the rat detours around the barrier and still reaches the goal, indicating that the expectation was not dependent on the specific path division joining.

Scenario 3 (Direction-distance decoupling). Rotate the goal location relative to the start while keeping all local olfactory and visual cues constant, thereby violating the direction-distance correlation \(d : \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{S} \to \mathbb{R}^{+} \times \mathbb{R}^{+}\) [PAGE 190]. Veto: the metric relation between direction and distance no longer predicts the goal. Prediction: correctness of choice falls to chance level (\(p = 0.5\) for a binary maze choice), measurable by binomial test at \(\alpha = 0.05\) over \(N \ge 100\) trials. Verifiable by choice-frequency counts. Breakdown if correctness remains significantly above chance (\(p > 0.5, p\text{-value} < 0.05\)), which would falsify the necessity of direction-distance correlation for the expectation.

Collectively, these vetoes establish the boundary conditions under which Tolman's means-end formalism holds: only when tools are present, paths are joinable, and direction-distance correlations are intact [PAGE 190]. Outside these regimes, the cognitive attribution collapses to biomechanical or environmental determinism.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
