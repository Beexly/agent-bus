A precision of 0.12 under label shuffling is exactly what a random classifier achieves when the positive prior is 0.126 and class weights are held fixed — a genuine internal consistency win. Null AUC 0.51 ± 0.014 ≈ 0.5 likewise behaves correctly. The p < 0.01 statement, however, has a resolution floor: with N_π permutations, the smallest attainable p is 1/(N_π + 1); reporting p < 0.01 requires N_π ≥ 99 and the paper does not state N_π [PAGE 6, 8].

**Hop 5 — The magnitude-conditional failure surface.** Recall is not stationary in g. [PAGE 10]: "recall decreased as the threshold increased, declining from 0.75 to 0.77 at the lowest thresholds to approximately 0.10 at the highest thresholds in the comparator model, and to approximately 0.61 in the XGBoost model." Note the typographic impossibility "0.75 to 0.77" as a *decline*. More importantly, precision → 1.0 at the top threshold is a small-n artifact: when only the top 5% of events remain, TP/(TP+FP) → 1 trivially because FP collapses faster than TP. The paper itself forecloses the biomechanical reading on [PAGE 7]: "The feature-space distributions of high- and low-magnitude events were not formally compared."

**Hop 6 — The simulation's misspecified null.** The sensitivity analysis removes true impacts at prespecified rates "randomly removed across 1,000 simulation repetitions" [PAGE 14], yielding DAMAGE bias from −0.05% to −0.99%. But the observed missingness is *not* random: recall is 0.713 overall and ~0.61 at the top threshold, i.e., false negatives concentrate in high-magnitude events — precisely the events that dominate cumulative DAMAGE and CP. A missing-not-at-random (MNAR) removal at the same marginal rate would produce a larger downward bias than the MCAR simulation reports. The simulation therefore establishes a *lower bound* on bias and should not be read as validating the workflow's risk-estimate fidelity. Table 5 also carries a sign inconsistency: the text says CP bias ranges "− 0.05% to − 1.12%," while Table 5 prints positive values 0.05 → 1.12.

**Hop 7 — Where the model is actually load-bearing.** Reading the feature ladder in Table 4 [PAGE 9], the single DAMAGE feature gives F1 = 0.25, AUC = 0.66; adding CWT rotational-velocity peak magnitude lifts AUC to 0.82; Higuchi dimension to 0.84; average slope to 0.90. The marginal information is carried by waveform *complexity* features, not by peak magnitude. This is mechanistically coherent — chewing and decoupling are quasi-periodic low-frequency artifacts, whereas true impacts are transient and broadband — but it also means the classifier is essentially an artifact detector, and its decision surface will migrate if the artifact generator migrates (new mouthguard, new chewing behavior, new sport).

**Hop 8 — What would break first.** The earliest breakdown is calibration, not discrimination. [PAGE 15]: "Because formal calibration analysis (e.g., calibration curves, Brier score, Expected Calibration Error) was not performed, the predicted probabilities should not be interpreted as accurate estimates of uncertainty." Yet the entire low-confidence-window operator — "error declined from about 25% to below 5%" as the window widens 0 → 0.5 [PAGE 10] — is a probability-threshold construct. If the probabilities are miscalibrated under class weighting w₊ = 6.93, the window is drawn in the wrong place and both the 3–4 h and 10–15% error figures are optimistic. Second breakdown: Nyquist and resampling. Third: transportability, given "a single cohort from one geographical region, one mouthguard model, and exclusively male youth athletes" [PAGE 15].

**1. Class-imbalance operator.** With N₊ = 11,706 and N₋ = 81,126 [PAGE 1, PAGE 4]:

$$\rho = \frac{N_-}{N_+} = \frac{81{,}126}{11{,}706} = 6.93 \quad [\text{dimensionless}]$$

Team-level range 4.99 to 16.66 is reported [PAGE 3]. The XGBoost positive-class weight is set to this ratio: $w_+ = \rho$, $w_- = 1$ [PAGE 6]. *Breakdown:* $w_+ \to \infty$ as $N_+ \to 0$; with zero positives in a fold, the F1 and recall gradients are undefined. *Singularity:* $N_+ = 0 \Rightarrow \rho$ undefined (0/0 form after normalization).

**2. Discrimination metrics.** With TP, FP, FN, TN counts in the held-out fold:

$$P = \frac{TP}{TP+FP}, \qquad R = \frac{TP}{TP+FN}, \qquad F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P+R} = \frac{2TP}{2TP+FP+FN}$$

all dimensionless on [0,1]. ROC-AUC:

$$\mathrm{AUC} = \int_0^1 \mathrm{TPR}\big(\mathrm{FPR}^{-1}(u)\big)\,du \quad [\text{dimensionless}]$$

*Singularity regimes:* $P$ is undefined when $TP+FP = 0$ (no positive predictions); $R$ is undefined when $TP+FN = 0$ (no positives in the stratum). Both occur at the extreme g thresholds. Reported values: $F_1 = 0.765 \pm 0.005$, $R = 0.713 \pm 0.006$, $P = 0.825 \pm 0.005$, $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.931 \pm 0.004$ [PAGE 8, Table 3].

**3. Permutation p-value.** For $N_\pi$ label permutations with statistic $s$:

$$p = \frac{1 + \#\{\pi : s_\pi \ge s_{\text{obs}}\}}{1 + N_\pi}, \qquad p_{\min} = \frac{1}{1+N_\pi}$$

*Breakdown:* the claimed $p < 0.01$ is unattainable for $N_\pi \le 98$. The paper reports $p < 0.01$ [PAGE 8] without stating $N_\pi$ [PAGE 6].

**4. Trigger and sampling operators.** Trigger rule: $a_{\text{axis}} \ge 5\,\mathrm{g}$ sustained $\ge 3\,\mathrm{ms}$ on any single axis [PAGE 4], where $1\,\mathrm{g} = 9.80665\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Standard resampling at $f_s = 3200\,\mathrm{Hz}$ with 10 ms pre-peak (32 samples) and 40 ms post-peak (128 samples), total 160 samples = 50 ms [PAGE 3–4]. Derived:

$$T_w = 50\,\mathrm{ms}, \qquad \Delta f = \frac{1}{T_w} = 20\,\mathrm{Hz}, \qquad f_N = \frac{f_s}{2} = 1600\,\mathrm{Hz}$$

*Aliasing breakdown:* any spectral content above $f_N$ folds; because native rates differ by season (4681 Hz in 2021–2022, 6400 Hz in 2023, standard 3200 Hz) [PAGE 3, Table 2], the anti-alias filter differs by season, making spectral features weakly confounded with season.

**5. Amplitude spectral density.** With $\hat{a}(f_k)$ the FFT of the resultant linear acceleration,

$$\mathrm{ASD}(f_k) = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{|\hat{a}(f_k)|^2}{\Delta f}\right)\ \left[\mathrm{dB\ re\ g^2\,Hz^{-1}}\right]$$

sampled "every 50 Hz up to the Nyquist frequency of 1600 Hz" [PAGE 5] → 32 bins per signal. *Breakdown:* at 20 Hz intrinsic resolution, 50 Hz sampling is interpolated, so adjacent bins are correlated; ASD features are not independent and inflate apparent feature count.

**6. Continuous wavelet transform.** For resultant signal $x(t)$:

$$W(a,b) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\int x(t)\,\psi^{*}\!\left(\frac{t-b}{a}\right)dt$$

with the frequency axis segmented "into 200 Hz bands up to the Nyquist limit" [PAGE 5] → 8 bands. Band feature:

$$F_j = \max_{t}\ \max_{f \in [200(j-1),\,200j)\,\mathrm{Hz}} |W(a(f),t)| \quad [\text{amplitude units}]$$

**7. Nonlinear descriptors.** Sample entropy $= -\ln\!\big(A^{m}(r)/B^{m}(r)\big)$, dimensionless; Higuchi fractal dimension from $L(k) \propto k^{-D}$ using "up to 10 scale levels" [PAGE 6]. *Breakdown:* $D$ is unreliable for $N = 160$ samples at $k = 10$ (only 16 points per sub-series); $SampEn$ is biased when $B^m(r) = 0$, a live risk in short, near-deterministic decoupling waveforms.

**8. Injury-risk operators.** Concussion probability from the combined model: $P_c = \sigma(\beta_0 + \beta_1 a_{\text{lin}} + \beta_2 \alpha_{\text{rot}})$ with $a_{\text{lin}}$ in g and $\alpha_{\text{rot}}$ in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-2}}$; pediatric variant CP_youth; DAMAGE gives a maximum principal strain estimate $\varepsilon_{\max}$ from a second-order system [PAGE 5]. *Breakdown:* these are population-average logistic/viscoelastic surrogates calibrated on impactor or adult football data; they are not individual predictions, and the paper is explicit that DAMAGE at rank 1 alone yields only F1 = 0.25, AUC = 0.66 [PAGE 9].

**9. Review-time operator.**

$$T_{\text{rev}} = \tau \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{test}}} \mathbb{1}\big[i \in \mathcal{R}\big], \qquad \tau = 30\,\mathrm{s}, \quad T_{\text{rev}}[\mathrm{h}] = \frac{30\,N_{\mathcal{R}}}{3600}$$

with $\mathcal{R}$ = {model-flagged below $g$} ∪ {all events ≥ $g$} [PAGE 7, 13]. Reported endpoints: "approximately 90 h to under 8 h" [PAGE 1]. *Audit:* $92{,}832 \times 30\,\mathrm{s} = 773.6\,\mathrm{h} \ne 90\,\mathrm{h}$; the closure requires $N_{\mathcal{R}} \approx 10{,}800$ and $\approx 960$ respectively, i.e. an implicit τ or a cohort scaling not stated in [PAGE 7].

**10. Threshold operator and magnitude routing.**

$$\mathcal{S}_g = \{i : a_{\text{res},i} \ge g\}, \qquad g \in \{0, 6.38, 7.99, 10.57, 17.00, 30.69, 46.23\}\,\mathrm{g}$$

at the 5th/25th/50th/75th/90th/95th percentiles [PAGE 10]. Routing rule [PAGE 13]: "all events exceeding a specified resultant linear acceleration threshold (e.g., 10–15 g) are automatically routed for manual video verification and are not classified by the model." *Breakdown:* as $|\mathcal{S}_g| \to 0$, precision → 1 trivially and recall → 0.61 (XGBoost) / 0.10 (comparator) [PAGE 10]; the metric variance diverges as $1/\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}_g|}$.

**11. Low-confidence window.**

$$\mathcal{W}_\delta = \{i : |\hat{p}_i - 0.5| \le \delta\}, \qquad \delta \in [0, 0.5]$$

Error within the window $= FN_{\mathcal{W}} / |\mathcal{W}|$ [PAGE 10, Fig. 4]. Reported: $\delta:0\to0.5$ drives $T_{\text{rev}}$ from <1 h to >6 h and window error from ≈25% to <5%. *Singularity:* $|\mathcal{W}_\delta| = 0$ at $\delta = 0$ if no predicted probability equals exactly 0.5 → error undefined. *Assumption:* $\hat{p}$ calibrated; explicitly denied [PAGE 15].

**12. Simulated bias operator.**

$$B_X(r) = \frac{X_{\text{red}}(r) - X_{\text{full}}}{X_{\text{full}}}\times 100\% \quad [\%]$$

for $X \in \{\text{DAMAGE}, \mathrm{CP}\}$ and $r \in \{1\%, 2.5\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%\}$ [PAGE 14, Table 5]. *Assumption:* missingness completely at random. *Breakdown:* recall is g-dependent, so missingness is MNAR and $|B_X|$ is underestimated. Reported DAMAGE bias −0.05% to −0.99%; CP text −0.05% to −1.12% versus Table 5 positive 0.05 to 1.12 (sign conflict).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

**Layer 0 — the hardware lineage (2000s).** The instrumented mouthguard descends from the Head Impact Telemetry (HIT) system embedded in helmets, whose severity measures (Greenwald et al., 2008) and collegiate exposure datasets (Crisco et al., 2011) established both the 10–15 g "meaningful impact" intuition and the habit of linear-acceleration gating. This paper's 10–15 g routing band [PAGE 13] is a direct retro-descent from that HIT-era convention, now repurposed as an operational triage rule rather than a physical threshold.

**Layer 1 — risk-function apparatus (2011–2020).** Rowson & Duma's STAR system (2011), the rotational injury risk function (2012), the Combined Probability of Concussion (2013), Campolettano's pediatric CP_youth (2020), and Gabler's DAMAGE second-order strain estimator (2019) form the feature backbone. The paper imports all of them as features [PAGE 5]. The genealogical consequence is subtle: CP and DAMAGE were built to predict *injury*, and here they are repurposed to predict *human labeling decisions*. That repurposing is why DAMAGE alone performs poorly (F1 = 0.25, AUC = 0.66 [PAGE 9]) — it encodes injury severity, not artifact-versus-impact separability.

**Layer 2 — the mouthguard and CHAMP standardization (2019–2022).** Rich et al. (2019) validated the custom mouthpiece; Kuo et al. (2022) codified CHAMP best practices mandating video verification [PAGE 2]. CHAMP is the retarding force that creates the workload problem the paper solves — the standard *requires* verification, verification *costs* 30 s/event, and the classifier is the escape valve. The causal arrow runs: standardization → burden → automation.

**Layer 3 — the ML precedent (2014–2021).** Wu et al. (2014) paired SVM with infrared proximity sensing; Wu et al. (2017) reported "approximately 91% recall using a Support Vector Machine (SVM)" on 7 collegiate and 3 youth players across 38 player-sessions [PAGE 2]; Goodin et al. (2021) reported "~ 94% recall with extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost)" on five players from one team in one season, after down-sampling to class balance [PAGE 2]. The critical genealogical break is stated bluntly on [PAGE 13]: "model architectures and feature sets developed under more controlled or homogeneous conditions may not translate directly to large, heterogeneous youth sport cohorts." This paper's SVM(Wu) reimplementation collapses to F1 = 0.568 ± 0.067 [PAGE 8] — a ~0.31 F1 regression from the published 0.88+. That is the single most important retrocausal finding: the lineage's reported performance was a property of the sampling frame, not the algorithm.

**Layer 4 — the imbalance correction (2024–2026).** The present work inverts every prior design choice: no proximity pre-filter, no down-sampling, full 6.93:1 imbalance, participant-level grouped CV [PAGE 6], 267 features, and XGBoost.

**Multi-week traps.** (a) *Season-scale compounding.* Cumulative DAMAGE and CP integrate over ~14 weeks of practices and games; the paper's bias simulation [PAGE 14] treats false negatives as exchangeable, but they are g-weighted, so over a season the missed events are disproportionately the high-strain ones and the true cumulative bias exceeds −0.99%. (b) *Session-length drift.* Mean practice 103.12 ± 24.44 min and game 92.4 ± 19.83 min [PAGE 3] mean the non-active fraction varies session to session; false-positive rate is a function of idle time, so the effective prior $\rho$ drifts week to week and a model trained on pooled data mis-weights the tail. (c) *Hardware-season confound.* Configuration changed from 4681 Hz to 6400 Hz to 3200 Hz [PAGE 3, Table 2], so spectral features (ASD 50–100 Hz, 200–250 Hz, 850–900 Hz, 1200–1250 Hz dominate Table 4) are partially aliased with season — a multi-week trap that only appears when pooling across seasons. (d) *Label drift.* Ground truth came from prior datasets [PAGE 4] with no re-assessment of inter-rater agreement here [PAGE 15], so any reviewer-drift across 2021–2023 propagates into the target variable.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis habit regression.** The reviewer's baseline habit is exhaustive review: watch every triggered event, decide, log. Under a 90-hour season workload the habit regresses to a heuristic — "if peak linear acceleration is under ~10 g, skip it." That heuristic is the HIT-era ghost. The classifier, marketed as a relief from the workload, actually *certifies* the regression: it converts an informal, shameful shortcut into a documented, published workflow. The tell is on [PAGE 7]: "The feature-space distributions of high- and low-magnitude events were not formally compared, and this threshold-based procedure should be regarded as an operational triage workflow for prioritizing manual review rather than as evidence of biomechanical separation." The authors know the 10–15 g rule is administrative, not physical — but in deployment the number will be remembered as physical.

**The counter-trap (verification bias).** Under model-flag-only review, the reviewer sees only model positives. Every false negative becomes structurally invisible: it is never watched, never adjudicated, never counted. The label-generating process becomes model-conditioned. Two consequences follow. First, the *measured* error rate on future data is biased downward, because the denominator of "errors" is supplied by the model. Second, if the workflow is later used to generate training labels for a retrained model, the retrained model learns to reproduce its predecessor's blind spots — a closed feedback loop with no external reference standard. The paper's own strongest defense against this is the automatic high-g routing [PAGE 13], which functions as a small, deliberate break in the loop: high-magnitude events escape the model's authority. But at the reported operating point the escape hatch covers only the top ~25–50% of events by magnitude, and within that band recall is ~0.61 [PAGE 10], so the loop is narrowed, not opened.

**Automation bias on the positive side.** A reviewer handed a "P = 0.94, true HAE" flag is anchored. The paper cannot detect this: no reviewer-blinding experiment, no time-on-task measurement, no independent re-adjudication. The 30 s/event figure [PAGE 7] is explicitly "a practical planning assumption... rather than prior literature or internal timing data," so the savings estimate contains no measurement of the quantity it claims to save.

**The clocks.** Three clocks govern the system. (i) *The review clock* τ = 30 s, soft, unmeasured. (ii) *The confidence clock* δ, the half-width of the window around 0.5; at δ = 0.3–0.4 review time sits at 3–4 h with 10–15% in-window error [PAGE 10] — a tunable that silently sets the surveillance guarantee. (iii) *The season clock*, the integration horizon over which cumulative DAMAGE and CP accrue; the bias table [PAGE 14, Table 5] is the only place this clock is acknowledged, and it is acknowledged under an MCAR assumption that the g-dependent recall curve contradicts.

**Recursive layer: who is optimizing what?** The clinician optimizes for not missing a concussive-range exposure. The analyst optimizes for reviewer hours. The model optimizes F1 under a class weight of 6.93. These three objectives are not aligned, and only one of them appears in Table 3. The paper's honest sentence — "contexts with lower tolerance for missed HAEs may favor lower thresholds (e.g., 10–12 g) or sensitivity-optimized model parameters, whereas resource-constrained settings may adopt higher thresholds (e.g., 15 g)" [PAGE 14] — is the authors correctly refusing to collapse the disagreement. A deployed system that does not surface this choice to the end user has resolved a clinical trade-off by default.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Layer P — physical / transducer veto.**
*Claim:* a sensor-triggered event is a head acceleration event.
*Veto:* false. Trigger is "5 g on any single axis for a minimum duration of 3 ms" [PAGE 4]; non-impact mouthguard motion is documented as arising from "chewing or device decoupling" [PAGE 2]. A decoupled mouthguard waveform is not a head kinematics measurement at any confidence level — the model's flag, whether 0.05 or 0.95, cannot manufacture physical validity. **Veto holds absolutely and unconditionally.** No classifier output may promote a physically invalid waveform to a biomechanical datum. Operational consequence: coupling-quality flags must be independent of, and prior to, the ML decision.

**Layer B — biomechanical veto.**
*Claim:* the classifier separates true from false impacts.
*Veto:* partially. It separates *labeled* true from *labeled* false. At the highest g threshold the model's recall is "approximately 0.61" while the comparator is "approximately 0.10" [PAGE 10] — so exactly where biomechanical consequence is largest, the model's authority is weakest. The paper's routing rule [PAGE 13] correctly cedes that band to humans. **Veto condition:** any use of model output to *reduce* scrutiny of events ≥ 10–15 g is biomechanically inadmissible. The paper satisfies this; a deployer who removes the routing rule does not.

**Layer S — sampling / Nyquist veto.**
*Claim:* spectral features generalize across seasons.
*Veto:* the native sampling rate changed 4681 → 6400 → 3200 Hz across seasons [PAGE 3, Table 2], with different pre/post windows (15/49 ms, 15/45 ms, 10/40 ms). The dominant features in Table 4 are ASD bins at 50–100, 100–150, 200–250, 850–900, and 1200–1250 Hz [PAGE 9]. Those bins are computed on differently filtered signals. **Veto:** the model may not claim that a spectral feature's importance is a property of head impact physics until it is shown to be stable within a single hardware configuration.

**Layer A — anthropometric / population veto.**
*Claim:* model performance indicates utility for HAE surveillance.
*Veto:* scope-limited. Cohort is 84 male athletes, ages 11–14, one region (Piedmont Triad, North Carolina), one mouthguard model [PAGE 3, 15]: "the model was developed using a single cohort from one geographical region, one mouthguard model, and exclusively male youth athletes." CP_youth and DAMAGE scaling carry pediatric-specific calibration; neck stiffness, head mass, and helmet fit all shift the mapping from kinematics to strain. **Veto:** no transport claim across age, sex, or sport without refitting.

**Layer W — weather / environment veto.**
*Claim:* none is made; the paper reports no meteorological, surface, or temperature covariate. That silence is itself the veto. Mouthguard elastomer stiffness is temperature-dependent, and coupling quality (the dominant false-positive generator) is a mechanical interface property. Natural versus synthetic turf changes the impulse profile of ground contact. Because these variables are unrecorded, they cannot be shown to be balanced across the 2021/2022/2023 cohorts, and their absence means the season-level grouping in cross-validation [PAGE 6] does not control for them. **Veto:** the reported cross-validated F1 = 0.765 is an upper bound on expected out-of-season performance until surface and temperature are measured or randomized.

**Layer L — labeling / epistemic veto.**
*Claim:* F1 = 0.765 quantifies classification accuracy.
*Veto:* it quantifies agreement with a fallible reference. [PAGE 15] concedes residual labeling uncertainty and that "inter-rater agreement was not re-assessed in the present analysis." Combined with the Table 3 vs. Discussion metric contradiction (0.765 vs 0.69 F1; 0.825 vs 0.73 precision), the accuracy claim is not currently auditable.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Scenario 1 — Leave-one-organization-out transportability.**
*Intervention:* Restructure cross-validation so folds are defined by team/organization rather than by participant, then report F1 on the held-out 2023-12U team (the 16.66:1 imbalance cohort with 1,190 true and 19,830 false events [PAGE 3, Table 1]).
*Prediction:* F1 falls from 0.765 to 0.60–0.68, recall to 0.55–0.65, AUC to 0.87–0.91. Precision degrades less than recall because the prior shift reduces positive predictions faster than it reduces their purity.
*Falsifier:* F1 ≥ 0.75 on the held-out team confirms transportability within league; F1 < 0.55 falsifies any pooling claim and would indicate the participant-level CV [PAGE 6] was leaking team-specific signal through shared coaching, drill repertoire, or session structure.
*Verifiability:* fully reproducible from the released data (FITBIR, [PAGE 16]) with a fixed seed.

**Scenario 2 — Time-savings arithmetic audit.**
*Intervention:* Recompute $T_{\text{rev}} = \tau N_{\mathcal{R}}$ with $\tau$ measured by stopwatch on a stratified sample of 200 events, and report $N_{\mathcal{R}}$ explicitly for every g threshold.
*Prediction:* The "approximately 90 h" figure does not close against 92,832 events at 30 s (773.6 h) and must correspond to either a per-season normalization or a different τ. The "<8 h" figure implies ≈960 reviewed events, roughly one-tenth of the model's own TP+FP at the reported operating point. Measured τ will exceed 30 s for high-magnitude events (multi-angle review) and fall below it for obvious negatives, so τ is not a constant and the operator $T_{\text{rev}} = \tau N$ is mis-specified.
*Falsifier:* a supplement showing $\tau$, per-threshold $N_{\mathcal{R}}$, and the scaling factor that reproduces 90 h and 8 h within ±10%.
*Verifiability:* arithmetic plus a timing study; no new modeling required.

**Scenario 3 — Synthetic decoupling injection (artifact-adversarial test).**
*Intervention:* Augment the held-out fold by adding a chewing-like oscillation (3–8 Hz, amplitude 1–3 g) to 5% of known true-negative events, then score. Compare against the clean fold.
*Prediction:* Precision drops more than recall (predicted precision 0.825 → 0.62–0.70; recall change within ±0.03). Rationale: the model's top-20 feature set is dominated by low-frequency ASD (50–100 Hz), Higuchi fractal dimension, and sample entropy [PAGE 9] — descriptors that a quasi-periodic artifact will shift toward the "true impact" manifold more than a transient real impact shifts. F1 falls to 0.66–0.72. A secondary prediction: performance loss is larger for the 2023 configuration (3200 Hz, 50 ms window) than the 2021–2022 configurations, because the shorter window yields coarser $\Delta f = 20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and the 3–8 Hz artifact lands in the least-resolved bin.
*Falsifier:* precision loss < 0.03 would indicate the classifier is genuinely artifact-robust, contradicting the low-frequency feature importance profile; precision loss > 0.25 would indicate the model is an artifact detector whose reported performance is contingent on the training distribution's coupling quality.
*Verifiability:* deterministic augmentation with fixed seeds on released data; the augmentation kernel and its parameters are fully specified above.

**Scenario 4 (supplementary) — calibration audit as a veto on the confidence window.**
*Intervention:* Compute reliability curve, Brier score, and Expected Calibration Error on the held-out fold with class weighting retained.
*Prediction:* ECE > 0.10 and systematic over-confidence in the 0.6–0.9 band, because $w_+ = 6.93$ inflates predicted probabilities relative to the base rate of 0.126. Consequence: the low-confidence window at δ = 0.3–0.4 contains fewer true near-boundary cases than assumed, so the reported "10–15% in-window error at 3–4 h" [PAGE 10] is optimistic by a factor of roughly 1.5–2, and the → <5% error claim at δ = 0.5 is not reproducible without recalibration (Platt or isotonic refit on a held-out split).
*Falsifier:* ECE < 0.05 with a slope-1 reliability curve supports the window as specified.
