Scenario 1 (certain-wage floor). Impose a binding wage floor w_floor such that w_floor > E[W_u | a] for all professional trainees, verifiable from administrative wage and enrollment data. Prediction: entry into uncertain professions falls measurably within one application cycle; the negative premium is eliminated at the entry margin because the fixed outside option binds; incumbents remain due to sunk costs, generating hysteresis in the profession's composition.

Scenario 2 (probability disclosure). Require publication of the exact probability p of conspicuous success and the full income distribution F(w | a), verifiable via a randomized disclosure experiment across labor markets. Prediction: when p is explicit and small, probability-neglect ends; the "lure" weakens; entry falls unless non-pecuniary ν compensates; the negative premium may reverse into a positive compensating differential for bearing measurable risk, because the choice is no longer between a vague dream and a known wage.

Scenario 3 (credit default trigger). Exogenously verify that the default rate q exceeds the break-even threshold q_critical = i_c / (1 + i_c), so that expected repayment falls short of principal plus contractual interest. Prediction: lenders ration credit or require full collateral; observed contract rates rise not because a pure risk premium over an identified pure rate has been revealed, but because the quantity of loans contracts; Knight's identification problem [PAGE 367] remains unresolved, but market thickness collapses to a verifiable zero or near-zero in affected segments. All predictions are checkable against loan-volume, interest-rate, and enrollment time series without reference to spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds.
