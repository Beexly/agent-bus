The "law" is not just about human psychology; it is a fundamental constraint on any system that compresses high-dimensional reality into low-dimensional symbolic meanings over extended temporal scales.

Tolman's critical insight introduces Level 3: the organism anticipates that it will eventually need to recall or reproduce this sign. Knowing that memory decays, the brain preemptively optimizes the storage of the sign by fusing it with the significate. It engages in a counter-trap against its own biological limitations. Instead of storing the messy, high-entropy original data, it stores a compressed, high-significance version. However, this creates a new crisis habit: the "Crisis Habit Regression." If the environment suddenly demands exact replication of the original messiness (e.g., forensic reconstruction of a crime scene sketch), the system fails because it has already aggressively simplified the data to fit the "common sense object" archetype.

This recursive loop establishes a psychological clock. The "clock" ticks down the fidelity of the raw sign as the pull of the significate increases. The longer the interval between apprehension and reproduction, the more completely the "sign object... tends always to approach more and more to the character of its own original significate." The mind, in trying to be efficient, fundamentally alters the nature of the truth it is meant to preserve, trapping itself in a web of its own constructed meanings.

Let the presented drawing be represented by a vector $s_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$. According to Tolman ([PAGE 412], "what really happens is the establishing of complete sign gestalts"), the original apprehension instantiates a conditional dependency, forming a joint state $P(s_0, r)$, where $r \in \mathcal{R}$ is the 'significate' (e.g., a nameable geometric figure). 

The dynamic process of forgetting is modeled as a continuous-time Markov process driven by a minimization of cognitive complexity (related to the Minimum Description Length principle). We define the **Sign-Significate Divergence Operator** $\mathcal{D}(s_t, r)$ measuring the deviation of the current memory trace $s_t$ from the idealized prototype $r$:
$$ \mathcal{D}(s_t, r) = \| s_t - \mu_r \|_{\Sigma_r^{-1}}^2 $$
where $\mu_r$ is the centroid of the significate manifold and $\Sigma_r$ is its covariance matrix.

Tolman states: "[with the passage of time] the sign object in an acquired sign gestalt tends... always to approach more and more to the character of its own original significate" ([PAGE 412]). We model this as a stochastic differential equation (SDE) describing the evolution of the sensory trace $s_t$:
$$ ds_t = -\nabla_{s_t} \mathcal{D}(s_t, r) dt + \sigma dW_t - \lambda u(t) (s_t - s_0) dt $$
Here, the first term represents the deterministic pull toward the significate prototype (the "law of forgetting"). The second term, $\sigma dW_t$, represents standard synaptic noise (Brownian motion) which typically degrades fine details. The third term is the intervention factor: $u(t)$ represents the strength of external retrieval cues or the "re-presentation of the original stimulus conditions," and $\lambda$ is the coupling constant. If $u(t) > 0$, the trajectory is anchored back toward $s_0$, preventing total absorption into the significate.

**Units and Parameters:**

- $t$: Time in seconds (biological timescale of memory consolidation).
- $\mathcal{D}$: Dimensionless KL-divergence proxy (measured in nats or bits of semantic loss).
- $\sigma$: Synaptic noise amplitude (standard SI units adjusted for neural firing rates, e.g., spikes/s).

**Singularity/Breakdown Regimes:**

The unique identity of the original drawing is lost entirely, replaced by a generic instance of the category. The Fisher Information Matrix regarding the specific features of $s_0$ approaches zero.
2. **Apprehension Lock:** If the initial categorization is ambiguous (i.e., $s_0$ lies equidistant between two manifolds $\mathcal{R}_1$ and $\mathcal{R}_2$), the gradient $\nabla_{s_t} \mathcal{D}$ becomes unstable, leading to bistable perceptual flips rather than smooth convergence.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The Cross-Layer Veto Adjudication requires us to test the psychological and informational laws against immutable physical and biomechanical constraints. Tolman’s Law of Significance describes a top-down, conceptual overwrite of sensory data. However, can this purely cognitive drift be vetoed by lower-level systems?

First, consider the **Biomechanical Veto**. When Gibson’s subjects attempted to reproduce the figures, their motor execution was governed by proprioceptive feedback and muscle fatigue. Even if the cognitive sign $s_t$ had fully converged to an abstract prototype $r$ (e.g., a perfect circle), the biomechanical limits of the hand and arm might introduce specific, non-random tremors or deviations. These physical artifacts can act as a veto against the pure "significance" law, injecting high-frequency noise that prevents the drawn reproduction from ever perfectly matching the smoothed cognitive mean $\mu_r$. The physical medium (paper, pencil friction) imposes a hard floor on the fidelity of expression, creating a measurable divergence between the mental image and the physical output.

Second, the **Metabolic and Neurochemical Veto**. The SDE introduced above assumes a steady-state cognitive resource allocation. However, the brain operates under strict metabolic constraints. Glucose depletion or fluctuations in neurotransmitter levels (e.g., acetylcholine during sleep/wake cycles) can alter the coefficient $\lambda$ or the noise term $\sigma$. A severe metabolic stress acts as a veto on the complex process of "gestalt integration." If resources are too low to maintain the higher-order association between $s$ and $r$, the system may default to a purely rote, stimulus-bound repetition of $s_0$ (a primitive mimicry) or fail entirely, bypassing the semantic smoothing mechanism altogether. 

Finally, the **Environmental/Weather Veto** applies to the "re-presentation of the original stimulus conditions." Tolman notes that the approach to the significate is prevented if the original conditions are re-presented. An abrupt change in environmental lighting or the introduction of a novel, highly salient distractor acts as a massive exogenous input that resets the attentional focus. This external shock effectively sets $u(t)$ to a maximum value momentarily, freezing the memory trace at its current stage of abstraction and preventing further drift. Thus, the "law" is not absolute but is continuously adjudicated by the body's physical state and the immediate environment.
