Capitalization is the valuation of future service flows; if the future is not probabilistically knowable (Knightian uncertainty), the expected-present-value operator E[·] is undefined, and no actuarial price can be formed.

Multi-week traps: In the weeks following engagement with this argument, the primary trap is reification—treating short-run fixities (land as fixed, capital as mobile) as eternal ontological categories rather than as horizon-dependent supply conditions. A second trap is to accept Knight's repudiation at face value and then smuggle the tripartite schema back through "expository convenience," confusing heuristic shorthand with causal structure. A third trap is to ignore the temporal boundary Δt = τ_adapt and apply static marginal productivity to dynamic capitalization problems, misattributing distributive outcomes to policy when Knight locates the controversy in the failure to separate long- and short-period problems, "and not to questions of propriety or policy" [PAGE 1].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under analytical or market crisis, decision-makers compress Δt and regress to low-entropy classification priors. The tripartite schema "land, labor, capital" is a cognitive attractor because it reduces classification entropy to H = log₂3 ≈ 1.585 bits and carries "social and ethical significance" [PAGE 1]. Knight himself notes the terms are retained for exposition, not classification [PAGE 1]; crisis habit regression mistakes this heuristic for ontology.

Counter-trap: The opposite error is over-correction—denying any stable classification whatsoever, inducing analytic paralysis and preventing capitalization. Knight steers between over-simplification and undifferentiated holism by tying classification to long-run supply conditions [PAGE 1].

Clock: The operative clock is Δt relative to τ_adapt. If crisis shortens Δt below τ_adapt, agents appear specific and S^S governs; the mind regresses to tripartite fixities. If Δt exceeds τ_adapt, agents appear malleable and long-run supply conditions dominate. Formally, cognitive load L = H(C) + λ||Δt − τ_adapt||. As urgency weight λ → ∞ during crisis, L is minimized by collapsing to the tripartite prior regardless of Δt, producing a systematic classification error whenever the true regime is long-run malleable. The clock therefore determines whether the analyst sees fixed factors or adaptable supply conditions.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Productive agents are embodied. A machine cannot transmute into land, and land cannot be relocated at zero cost. Veto condition: if physical transformation cost Φ(x→y) exceeds capitalized service value V(x), substitution is physically vetoed.

Biomechanical layer: Organizations have retooling and retraining kinetics; human agents require training time and machinery requires installation lead time. If organizational adaptation time τ_org > Δt, use-specific equivalence ∼_u is biomechanically infeasible and the agent is effectively specific, regardless of market quotation.

Weather layer: Market climate—demand shocks, technological change—is exogenous stochastic forcing. Under Knightian uncertainty, the probability distribution over future states is not estimable, so no actuarial "weather forecast" can price the adaptation option. Any classification assuming insurable risk is vetoed by this layer.

Veto proof: A classification scheme C passes adjudication iff V(C) = 0, where V(C) = 1 iff ∃ layer ℓ ∈ {physical, biomechanical, weather} such that constraint C_ℓ is violated. Tripartite classification fails the veto when applied cross-temporally: it ignores biomechanical adaptation time (treating capital as instantly malleable) and weather-layer Knightian uncertainty (treating future uses as probabilistically known). Only a horizon-conditioned, supply-correspondence classification survives all three layers [PAGE 1].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: A firm treats a specialized machine as homogeneous "capital" and attempts instantaneous reallocation to a new product line after a demand shock. Veto: biomechanical layer; τ_retool > Δt_crisis. Prediction: capitalization value is written down, the output frontier shifts inward by ΔY > 0, and the realized loss is Knightian (uninsurable) because the shock lay outside the modeled probability space.

Scenario 2: An economist models land and capital as distinct ontological categories in a long-run equilibrium (Δt → ∞). Veto: physical/long-run supply layer; land can be improved (capitalized) and capital can be sunk or immobile (land-like), so supply conditions converge rather than remain categorically separate. Prediction: the model overestimates land-supply rigidity and underestimates capital mobility; land-tax incidence predictions misallocate deadweight loss by a measurable wedge in informational surplus (ΔH > 0 bits).

Scenario 3: A trader relies on "Things quoted under the same name and identically priced may be taken as identical" [PAGE 1] to arbitrage two apparently identical productive assets under one name. Veto: use-specific non-transitivity; assets equivalent in use u diverge under use v when demand shifts. Prediction: arbitrage collapses with information-theoretic surprise ΔH = H(posterior use-specific value | shock) − H(prior market price) exceeding 1 bit, revealing that market identity was a latent singularity, not robust equivalence [PAGE 1].
