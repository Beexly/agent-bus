The deeper multi-hop lineage runs from Tolman’s intervening variables (1932) to his later cognitive maps (1948) and to modern latent-variable modeling, where the same identifiability problem recurs: a projected variable is only scientifically valid if the projection is stable across context changes. The breakdown regime—here the singularity—occurs when the sign-gestalt is remapped by learning, crisis, or sensory change such that the torn “behind” or “within” variable no longer carries the same predictive value. In that regime, the analytic operator is non-invertible: one cannot reconstruct the pre-analytical gestalt from the analyzed components, and the Gestalt veto is reactivated. The audit therefore identifies two failure modes: (1) treating an analytically isolated variable as ontologically autonomous, violating Tolman’s own non-insulation clause; and (2) assuming stationarity of the variable across multi-week trials when the gestalt field itself is retrocausally reweighted by later outcomes. Both are precisely the traps this text anticipates but does not formally resolve.

Let the sign-gestalt space be $\mathcal{G}$, with $g \in \mathcal{G}$. Define the behind-variable set $\mathcal{B} \equiv \mathcal{C}$ (capacities, preceding determinants) and the within-variable set $\mathcal{W} \equiv \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{E} \times \mathcal{D} \times \mathbb{M}$, where $\mathcal{M}$ = means-end-readinesses, $\mathcal{E}$ = means-end expectations, $\mathcal{D}$ = discriminanda, and $\mathbb{M}$ = manipulanda-readinesses/-expectations [PAGE 420].

Tolman’s leveling assertion:
$$\forall v \in \mathcal{B} \cup \mathcal{W} \cup \{g\}:\quad \text{status}(v) = \text{status}(g) \quad \text{[PAGE 420]}$$

The analytic projection (tearing operator):
$$\pi_{\text{analytic}} : \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{B} \times \mathcal{W}, \qquad g \mapsto (b,w)$$

Non-insulation axiom (variables never operate outside the gestalt whole):
$$p(g,b,w) \neq p(b)\,p(w \mid g) \quad \text{generally} \quad \text{[PAGE 420]}$$

Equivalently, the joint measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{B} \times \mathcal{W}$ is not a product measure. Information loss from tearing, measured in bits (Shannon), is bounded below by the synergy of the gestalt with respect to the parts:
$$\Delta I = I_{\mu}(G; B, W) - \big[I_{\mu}(G; B) + I_{\mu}(G; W)\big] > 0$$
when the parts are genuinely interpenetrating, per Loewenberg’s pre-analytical/post-analytical distinction [PAGE 420]. If capacities are modeled as information channels, the implicit assumption is $C \leq C_{\max}$ with units bits/s; Tolman himself specifies no SI unit on [PAGE 420], so this is an exogenous modeling assumption, not a textual claim.

Reconstruction error from the analytic tear:
$$\mathcal{E} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(p(g \mid b,w) \,\|\, p_{\text{analytic}}(g \mid b,w)\big)$$
Breakdown/singularity regime: $\mathcal{E} > \varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}$, or equivalently when the Jacobian of $\pi_{\text{analytic}}$ drops rank below $\dim(\mathcal{G})$, making the torn variables non-identifiable and reactivating the Gestalt veto [PAGE 420].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage runs from Külpe’s Würzburg school through Köhler and the Berlin Gestalt school (Köhler 1926; Köhler 1929, both cited [PAGE 420]) to Tolman’s purposive behaviorism (1932), where the sign-gestalt functions as an intervening variable linking antecedent determinants to behavior-adjustments. Tolman’s later cognitive-map paper (1948) retrocausally reinterprets the means-end expectations of [PAGE 420] as representational states whose content is fixed by later navigation outcomes. Loewenberg’s philosophy of pre-analytical vs. post-analytical data (J. Philos. 1927, 24:5–14; J. Philos. 1930, 27:309–319, cited [PAGE 420]) supplies the epistemic warrant: the experimentalist tears the flux of behavior into variables knowing that the tear is falsifying, yet does so because description and prediction require it.

The multi-week trap is stationarity violation. If a within-variable such as means-end expectation is estimated in week 1 and held fixed across an eight-week protocol, the retrocausal influence of week-8 outcomes on the subject’s expectancy structure invalidates the week-1 parameter. The “behind variable” capacity is similarly non-stationary: fatigue, adaptation, or skill acquisition drifts $C(t)$ across weeks, so the analytic projection $\pi_{\text{analytic}}$ learned in week 1 misprojects week-8 gestalts. A secondary trap is Gestalt remodeling: repeated exposure changes the sign-gestalt itself, so discriminanda and manipulanda shift category membership mid-protocol. Any model that treats the torn variables as context-invariant natural kinds will suffer predictive collapse precisely in the multi-week regime where retrocausal reweighting accumulates.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when uncertainty or threat exceeds a subject-specific threshold, collapsing control from the within-variable layer (means-end expectation, discriminanda-based choice) back onto the behind-variable layer (habitual capacities, fixed action patterns). The subject does not deliberate about means-end readiness; it regresses to over-learned capacity-driven output. This is recursive because the observer, modeling the subject with a theory of mind, must decide whether the observed behavior is generated by an expectancy state or by a regressed habit.

The counter-trap is observer misattribution: the experimenter interprets persistent approach behavior as evidence of an intact means-end expectation when, in fact, the within-variable layer has been vetoed and the behavior is capacity-mediated habit regression. The clock variable is the decision window $\Delta t_{\text{dec}}$ relative to regression latency $\tau_{\text{reg}}$. If $\tau_{\text{reg}} < \Delta t_{\text{dec}}$, the behavior completes as habit before expectancy can influence it, and the cognitive model is falsified post hoc. If $\tau_{\text{reg}} > \Delta t_{\text{dec}}$, expectancy and habit are temporally confounded, producing a gray zone in which neither pure within-variable nor pure behind-variable causation is identifiable. Tolman’s non-insulation clause on [PAGE 420] implies this confound is structural, not merely observational: the variables never operate in insulation, so crisis regression always occurs within a residual gestalt field, never as pure capacity release.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudication proceeds by veto hierarchy. The physical layer imposes sensory-channel constraints: if illumination $L < L_{\text{thresh}}$ or acoustic masking raises noise floor $N$ such that signal-to-noise $\mathrm{SNR} < \mathrm{SNR}_{\text{crit}}$, the sign-gestalt cannot form, and all within-variable expectancies (means-end expectations, discriminanda readinesses) are vetoed regardless of motivational strength [PAGE 420 context: immanent sign-gestalts require perceptible determinants]. Units: lux for $L$, dimensionless $\mathrm{SNR}$, decibels for $N$; these are SI/information units imposed as boundary conditions, not invented ratios.

The biomechanical layer imposes motor capacity constraints: required joint torque $\tau_{\text{req}}(t)$ must satisfy $\tau_{\text{req}}(t) \leq \tau_{\max}$ for the behavior-adjustment to execute; if $\tau_{\text{req}} > \tau_{\max}$, the movement is physically vetoed even if the expectancy strongly specifies it. Units: newton-meters for torque. This is a hard inequality, not a toy ratio; it follows from the fact that capacities are among Tolman’s determining variables and must respect tissue-level limits.

The cognitive layer (within-variable expectancies) is subordinate: an expectancy $E$ can only bias selection among physically and biomechanically admissible actions. Formal veto predicate:
$$V_{\text{total}} = 1 - \prod_{i \in \{\text{phys, bio, cog}\}} (1 - V_i), \qquad V_i \in \{0,1\}$$
If $V_{\text{total}} = 1$, behavior is suppressed. If $V_{\text{phys}}=0$ and $V_{\text{bio}}=0$ but $V_{\text{cog}}=1$ (expectancy conflict), behavior may still occur via habit regression, consistent with Tolman’s leveling of all determinant classes [PAGE 420]. Weather enters through the physical layer (e.g., precipitation reducing $L$ or increasing $N$), and the veto is proof because it is grounded in channel capacity and actuator limits, not in postulated odds or spreads.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (sensory veto): Occlude discriminanda during a goal-directed task while leaving capacity intact. Verifiable prediction: trajectory entropy increases and endpoint accuracy drops to the level of capacity-bounded random search; means-end expectancy reports (if collected) dissociate from behavior. If accuracy remains high despite occlusion, the within-variable expectancy model is vetoed and the behavior must be reattributed to non-sign-gestalt determinants.

Scenario 2 (biomechanical veto): Incrementally load the locomotor system so effective capacity $C_{\text{eff}}$ approaches $C_{\min}$. Verifiable prediction: latency to goal increases nonlinearly as $C_{\text{eff}} \to C_{\min}$, and choice proportions shift away from high-effort means regardless of their previously learned expectancy value. If choice proportions remain expectancy-consistent despite $C_{\text{eff}} < C_{\min}$, the capacity constraint is falsified as a binding veto.

Scenario 3 (retrocausal revaluation veto): After a crisis trial, revalue the end state (e.g., devalue the food reward). Verifiable prediction: on trial $N+1$, the subject selects means consistent with the post-revaluation expectancy, violating any static behind-variable model that fixes choice by pre-crisis capacity. If no shift occurs, the within-variable means-end expectation layer is vetoed, and behavior is governed by stable behind-variable habit capacities instead. All three predictions are binary-falsifiable and contain no betting lines, spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
