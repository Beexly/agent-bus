We formalize Knight’s dichotomy using information theory and Bayesian statistics. Let $E$ denote an event (e.g., a fire). The classical actuarial approach assumes $P(E)$ is derivable from large numbers.

**1. Objective Probability (Life Insurance Regime)**
For a homogeneous class of individuals, let $X_i \in \{0,1\}$ represent death within a year for individual $i$. Following Knight [PAGE 249], these follow the binomial law:
$$ P(X_i = 1) = p, \quad \text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p) $$
As $N \to \infty$, the Law of Large Numbers dictates:
$$ \bar{X}_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N X_i \xrightarrow{a.s.} p $$
Here, the entropy $H(p) = -p \log p - (1-p) \log (1-p)$ is fixed and known. There is no margin for entrepreneurial profit because the expected value is perfectly calculable.

**2. The Uncertainty Margin (Fire Insurance Regime)**
In fire insurance, the population lacks strict homogeneity. We introduce a latent variable $\theta_j$ representing the unique environment/moral hazard of firm $j$. The observed probability becomes:
$$ P(Fire_j | \theta_j) $$
Since $\theta_j$ cannot be perfectly classified, the insurer relies on a prior distribution $\pi(\theta)$. The predictive probability is:
$$ P(Fire_j) = \int P(Fire_j | \theta) d\pi(\theta) $$
Knight notes [PAGE 249]: *"insurance does not take care of the whole risk... there are usually large indirect losses... entirely unprovided for."*
Let $L_{dir}$ be direct damage and $L_{indir}$ be interruption costs. The total loss is $L_{total} = L_{dir} + L_{indir}$. Insurance pays $I(L_{dir})$.
The residual uncertainty variance is:
$$ \sigma^2_{uncertainty} = \text{Var}(L_{indir}) + \text{Var}(L_{dir} - I(L_{dir})) $$
This variance represents the "margin of uncertainty" where profit/loss occurs. It cannot be eliminated by the binomial law because $L_{indir}$ depends on highly idiosyncratic, unrepeatable strategic interactions.

**Breakdown Regime:**
The actuarial model breaks down when the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the true underlying distribution of $\theta$ and the insurer's assumed parametric model exceeds a critical threshold $\epsilon$. At this singularity, "judgment" replaces mathematical formula.
