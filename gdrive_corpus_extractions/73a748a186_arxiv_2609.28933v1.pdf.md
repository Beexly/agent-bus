<THINK_TRACE>
The paper’s causal chain must be audited from geodetic input to chassis command, because the stated contribution is not a new estimator or a single control law but a reproducible contract among sensing, localization validity, waypoint geometry, control, arbitration, and actuation. The first multi-hop dependency is geographic: WGS–84 latitude, longitude, and ellipsoidal height are converted into a local East–North–Up state only after a session datum is fixed by the first accepted RTK-fixed position. The authors explicitly state that “The first accepted RTK-fixed position initializes the session datum” [PAGE 3] and that “A process restart creates a new session datum” [PAGE 3]. Therefore, any downstream cross-track error is conditioned on a datum that is session-consistent but not necessarily millimeter-repeatable across restarts. This is a gray-zone: the same field route can produce different local coordinates after a restart even if the physical path is identical, so run-to-run comparisons must not merge logs from different sessions without a stored surveyed datum.

The second hop is localization-state construction. The single-GNSS–IMU branch accepts RTK-fixed GGA position and the latest valid IMU yaw only when the heading timestamp differs from the position timestamp by no more than 0.25 s. The authors call this an “asynchronous, latest-valid-sample assembly, not a tightly coupled GNSS–inertial filter” [PAGE 3]. Thus the branch deliberately withholds state during outages rather than propagating an unobserved state. The dual-antenna branch has stricter gates: RTK fixed, differential age at most 3 s, heading status SOL_COMPUTED/NARROW_INT, measured baseline within 1.18±0.05 m, heading standard deviation at most 1°, at least six satellites supporting heading, heading age at most 0.3 s, and position–heading skew at most 0.2 s. The paper states that “These gates distinguish a parsed message from a navigation-valid state” [PAGE 4]. This is the central gray-zone audit rule: a parsed message is not a valid state, and a valid state is not an independent ground-truth measurement.

The third hop is geometry. Waypoints are projected once in the datum-centered transverse Mercator frame, and every accepted receiver sample is transformed into that fixed frame. The active directed segment defines projection coordinate, signed cross-track error, and wrapped heading error. The sign convention is explicit: e_y > 0 denotes the left side of travel. The fourth hop is control. All modes share the unicycle model, nominal 1.0 m/s speed, angular limit 0.65 rad/s, 0.30 m goal radius, 0.10 rad alignment threshold, 1.0 m cross-track abort, and 10 Hz control period. The row-hybrid dispatcher selects NMPC for segment length at most 3 m, pure pursuit for 3 m to 15 m, and PID for at least 15 m. The fifth hop is safety: validity and freshness are enforced outside the controller, odometry older than 0.25 s is rejected, invalid localization triggers a refreshed zero-velocity command, and the serial bridge clamps commands to 2.0 m/s and 1.5 rad/s with a 0.6 s zero-output watchdog.

The empirical claim is narrow. The paper reports that “The row-hybrid mode yields the lowest run-averaged post-acquisition mean absolute cross-track error (MAE) in the evaluated dataset: 0.00952 m with single GNSS+IMU and 0.00846 m with dual GNSS” [PAGE 1]. Table III gives 100 runs per condition, 800 included runs, and 100% completion. However, the authors warn that “The reported errors characterize the recorded trajectories relative to the reference route; they should not be equated with independently validated absolute positioning or vehicle-tracking accuracy” [PAGE 9]. The planar unicycle model “neglects skid-steer slip and actuator dynamics” [PAGE 8], and dense canopy, prolonged GNSS loss, severe wheel slip, and unmodeled obstacles “requires further validation” [PAGE 9]. The gray-zone conclusion is that the reported centimeter-level MAE is a trajectory-relative tracking statistic under valid-state gating, not a universal absolute-accuracy or safety certificate.

\[
D=(\phi_0,\lambda_0,h_0),
\]
with \(\phi,\lambda\) in angular units and \(h\) in metres. The datum is immutable for the process, but a restart creates a new session datum [PAGE 3]. The projection is
\[
\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}

\]
with \(x,y\) in metres. This is a local transverse Mercator approximation, not a global Cartesian truth.

For single GNSS+IMU, the yaw is
\[
\psi=\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(m_\psi\psi_{\mathrm{imu}}+\psi_{\mathrm{off}}),
\]
with configured \(m_\psi=1\) and \(\psi_{\mathrm{off}}=90^\circ=\pi/2\) rad. The acceptance condition is that the heading timestamp differs from the position timestamp by at most 0.25 s [PAGE 3]. There is no inertial-bias estimation and no position propagation through GNSS loss. For dual GNSS, the receiver bearing and yaw are
\[
\chi=\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(\chi_b-\alpha_b+\delta\chi),\qquad
\psi=\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\chi\right),
\]
with calibrated offset \(\alpha_b=-90^\circ\) for ANT2 mounted left of ANT1 [PAGE 4]. The midpoint reconstruction uses
\[
r_h=\frac{b}{2}\cos\theta_p,\qquad r_z=\frac{b}{2}\sin\theta_p\quad[\mathrm{m}],
\]
with baseline \(b=1.18\) m and receiver pitch \(\theta_p\) in radians. The midpoint altitude is \(h_m=h_1+r_z\). The dual state is accepted only under the full gate set quoted above, including baseline \(1.18\pm0.05\) m, heading standard deviation at most \(1^\circ\), heading age at most 0.3 s, and skew at most 0.2 s [PAGE 4]. Breakdown regimes include baseline outside tolerance, heading status not NARROW_INT, insufficient satellites, and any timestamp skew beyond the gate.

For a directed segment \(S_i=(p_i,p_{i+1})\), the source defines
\[
d_i=p_{i+1}-p_i,\quad L_i=\|d_i\|,\quad \psi_i=\operatorname{atan2}(d_{i,y},d_{i,x}),
\]
\[
s_i=\frac{(p-p_i)^{\mathsf T}d_i}{d_i^{\mathsf T}d_i},\qquad
\hat p_i=p_i+s_i d_i,
\]
\[
e_y=\frac{d_{i,x}(y-\hat y_i)-d_{i,y}(x-\hat x_i)}{L_i},\qquad
e_\psi=\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(\psi_i-\psi).
\]
Units are metres for \(L_i,e_y\) and radians for \(\psi_i,e_\psi\). \(L_i=0\) is a formal breakdown because the cross-track denominator vanishes. The unicycle model is
\[
\dot x=v\cos\psi,\qquad \dot y=v\sin\psi,\qquad \dot\psi=\omega,
\]
with \(v\) in m/s and \(\omega\) in rad/s; it formally assumes no skid-steer slip and no actuator dynamics, which the authors identify as a limitation [PAGE 8].

Pure pursuit uses
\[
L_d=\operatorname{sat}_{[1.5,6.0]}(2.5v_d)\quad[\mathrm{m}],
\]
where \(v_d\) is the scheduled forward-speed reference in m/s and the gain has units of seconds. Curvature and commands are
\[
\kappa=\frac{2\sin\alpha}{\max(e_{Ld},\epsilon_L)},\qquad
v=v_d,\qquad
\omega=\operatorname{sat}_{[-0.65,0.65]}(v\kappa),
\]
with \(\kappa\) in \(1/\mathrm{m}\) and \(\omega\) in rad/s. The denominator guard \(\max(e_{Ld},\epsilon_L)\) is the exact near-zero look-ahead breakdown regime. The PID virtual-point law uses
\[
I_k=I_{k-1}+e_{y,k}\Delta t_c,\qquad
\dot e_{y,k}=\frac{e_{y,k}-e_{y,k-1}}{\Delta t_c},
\]
\[
v_{\perp,k}=\operatorname{sat}_{[-0.4,0.4]}\left(-K_p e_{y,k}-K_i I_k-K_d\dot e_{y,k}\right)\quad[\mathrm{m/s}],
\]
with configured \((K_p,K_i,K_d)=(0.45,0.05,0.20)\). Dimensional consistency under the stated variables requires \(K_p\) in \(1/\mathrm{s}\), \(K_i\) in \(1/\mathrm{s}^2\), and \(K_d\) dimensionless. The path-frame rotation and inverse kinematics are
\[
\begin{bmatrix}v_x^d\\v_y^d\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi_i & -\sin\psi_i\\ \sin\psi_i & \cos\psi_i\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}v_\parallel\\v_\perp\end{bmatrix},
\]
\[
v=v_x^d\cos\psi+v_y^d\sin\psi,\qquad
\omega=\frac{-v_x^d\sin\psi+v_y^d\cos\psi}{\epsilon}+K_\psi e_\psi,
\]
with \(\epsilon=0.5\) m and \(K_\psi=1.35\). The exact singularity is \(\epsilon\to0\); the implementation fixes \(\epsilon=0.5\) m, so the inverse-kinematic term is bounded but not singular-free in theory.

NMPC uses forward-Euler prediction
\[
q_{j+1}=
\begin{bmatrix}
x_j+\Delta t_p v_j\cos\psi_j\\
y_j+\Delta t_p v_j\sin\psi_j\\
\operatorname{wrap}_{[-\pi,\pi)}(\psi_j+\Delta t_p\omega_j)
\end{bmatrix},
\]
with \(N=8\) and \(\Delta t_p=0.25\) s, giving a 2.0 s prediction horizon distinct from the 0.1 s control period. The objective is
\[
J(U)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left[
q_y e_{y,j+1}^2+q_\psi e_{\psi,j+1}^2+q_g\bar d_{g,j+1}^2
+r_v(v_j-v_d)^2+r_\omega\omega_j^2
+r_{\Delta v}(v_j-v_{j-1})^2+r_{\Delta\omega}(\omega_j-\omega_{j-1})^2
-q_s s_{j+1}
\right]
+q_{y,f}e_{y,N}^2+q_{\psi,f}e_{\psi,N}^2+q_{g,f}\bar d_{g,N}^2,
\]
with dimensionless implementation weights and input bounds \(0\le v_j\le1.0\) m/s and \(|\omega_j|\le0.65\) rad/s [PAGE 5]. SLSQP is used with at most 40 iterations and tolerance \(10^{-3}\); the paper does not report solver worst-case time, so real-time breakdown is not bounded by the stated equations. The hybrid dispatcher is
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{hyb}}(L_i)=
\begin{cases}
\pi_{\mathrm{NMPC}}, & L_i\le3\ \mathrm{m},\\
\pi_{\mathrm{PP}}, & 3\ \mathrm{m}<L_i<15\ \mathrm{m},\\
\pi_{\mathrm{PID}}, & L_i\ge15\ \mathrm{m}.
\end{cases}
\]
Run-level metrics are
\[
\mathrm{MAE}_r=\frac{1}{n_r}\sum_{k\in K_r}|e_{y,r,k}|,\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}_r=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_r}\sum_{k\in K_r}e_{y,r,k}^2},
\]
with metres as units; P95 is an empirical percentile, not a probability guarantee [PAGE 6]. The stated condition-level values are MAE 0.0125/0.0112 m for pure pursuit single/dual, 0.0110/0.0098 m for PID, 0.0098/0.0088 m for NMPC, and 0.00952/0.00846 m for hybrid [PAGE 7]. The overall evaluated maximum satisfies \(E_{\max}<0.20\) m, but this is a dataset statement, not a safety certification.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s lineage is explicitly built from agricultural autoguidance, geometric path following, NMPC, dual-antenna GNSS attitude, GNSS–inertial integration, ROS 2, transverse Mercator accuracy, and sequential quadratic programming. The references trace pure pursuit to Coulter’s 1992 implementation, Stanley to Thrun et al., NMPC to Rawlings, Mayne, and Diehl, dual-antenna heading and baseline constraints to Ding et al., GNSS–inertial principles to Groves, ROS 2 architecture to Macenski et al., transverse Mercator to Karney, SLSQP to Kraft, and SciPy to Virtanen et al. [PAGE 9–10]. The historical trajectory is therefore not “new autonomy” but a systems-integration retrofit: agricultural guidance already relied on satellite positioning, while modern field robots combine RTK with modular compute and electronically controlled bases [PAGE 1].

The retrocausal trap is that future field failures are often traceable to early session-level choices rather than to the controller. First, first-fix datum initialization is convenient but means a process restart creates a new local frame; any multi-week dataset that silently concatenates sessions can inherit a datum translation that looks like tracking error. Second, the dual-antenna branch depends on rigid surveyed mounts, two RF feeds, calibrated 1.18 m baseline, and antenna phase-center alignment; the paper warns that omitting either antenna, feed, or baseline calibration makes the setup incomplete [PAGE 2]. Over weeks, cable strain, connector wear, mount flex, or antenna swap can silently violate the acceptance gate. Third, the single branch depends on a separate IMU yaw and timestamp alignment; magnetic sensitivity and yaw-offset drift are not solved by the RTK receiver. Fourth, the software stop is not an emergency stop, and the authors explicitly say “A physical emergency-stop circuit remains mandatory for personnel protection” [PAGE 6]. Fifth, the evaluation has no independent external ground truth; the reported errors are relative to the reference route, so repeated deployment success does not prove absolute positioning accuracy [PAGE 9]. These are multi-week traps because they are invisible in a single successful run and only surface after re-datuming, hardware rework, canopy change, or a new operator.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under field stress, three failure modes are predictable. Crisis habit regression is the operator’s fallback to the most transparent controller or to manual teleoperation when anomalies appear. The paper retains pure pursuit as “the simplest geometric baseline” [PAGE 4] and provides keyboard teleoperation at priority 255, software safety stop at 200, default command at 20, and autonomous navigation at 10 [PAGE 6]. The recursive trap is that a team may believe pure pursuit is safer because it is simple, yet the paper states that availability of pure pursuit and PID modules alone does not establish an automatic fallback policy [PAGE 8]. Counter-trap reasoning is the belief that dual GNSS is ground truth or that the software stop is an emergency stop. The source directly contradicts both: dual GNSS supplies a navigation-valid state only under ambiguity, baseline, satellite, and timestamp gates, and “The software stop is not an emergency stop” [PAGE 6]. Clock reasoning is the third mode: the architecture is multi-rate, with 10 Hz control, 0.1 s control period, 0.25 s NMPC prediction step, 2.0 s horizon, 0.25 s odometry freshness, 0.2 s dual skew, 0.3 s heading age, 0.5–1.0 s source timeout, 0.1 s watchdog evaluation, and 0.6 s bridge zero-output condition [PAGE 3–6]. A stressed operator can misread “10 Hz” as a safety guarantee, but the actual validity contract is a set of independent timestamp and status gates. The correct theory of mind is mutually recursive: the operator must know that the maintainer knows that the safety monitor knows that autonomous commands are only priority 10 and can be superseded by a human at 255. That is a supervised-recovery design, not an emergency-protection design.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: the control stack assumes the planar unicycle model, but the source admits it “neglects skid-steer slip and actuator dynamics” [PAGE 8]. If severe wheel slip or actuator lag exceeds the modeled regime, the predicted \(\dot x,\dot y,\dot\psi\) are not the realized chassis motion, and the cross-track abort at 1.0 m is the final backstop. The veto proof is conditional: the reported MAE is valid only while the vehicle remains within the valid-state and low-slip regime; otherwise the model itself is falsified. Mechanical and biomechanical-analogue veto: the dual-antenna baseline must be rigid, level, and within 1.18±0.05 m; swapping ANT1 and ANT2 “changes the heading by approximately \(\pi\)” [PAGE 7]. The IMU must be rigidly mounted and its yaw offset checked at the deployment site. If mounting geometry is wrong, the state constructor may either reject the state or, worse, produce a geometrically inconsistent state that is numerically plausible. That is exactly the warning that “A controller can receive a numerically plausible but geometrically inconsistent state” [PAGE 1]. Weather veto: dense canopy, prolonged loss of GNSS corrections, severe slip, and unmodeled obstacles require further validation [PAGE 9]; dual GNSS also depends on antenna visibility and successful carrier-phase ambiguity resolution [PAGE 8]. Therefore no claim of all-weather or canopy-robust operation is admitted by the source. Actuation veto: the serial bridge clamps to 2.0 m/s and 1.5 rad/s and writes zero if no valid output arrives for 0.6 s [PAGE 6]; these are protective interface limits, not nominal controller settings. The cross-layer proof is by contradiction: if any validity gate is false, the localization front end withholds state; if state is withheld, the controller cannot receive a navigation-valid \(q=[x,y,\psi]^{\mathsf T}\); therefore any tracking statistic computed during that interval is outside the paper’s stated contract. The empirical results are conditioned on valid-state gating, not on invalid or stale intervals.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
1. Datum-reset counterfactual. Scenario: restart the navigation process mid-mission and continue on the same physical route without loading a surveyed stored datum. Source gate: “A process restart creates a new session datum” [PAGE 3]. Prediction: local \((x,y)\) coordinates shift by the projection of the new first-fix datum relative to the old datum; waypoints from the old log no longer overlay the new local positions unless reprojected. Verifiable veto: compare the old and new datum tuples and the projected waypoint residuals. Any run-level MAE that pools the two sessions without datum alignment is invalid under the paper’s own session-datum contract. The correct prediction is not a numeric error magnitude but a structural one: cross-session concatenation is vetoed unless a stored surveyed datum is used.

2. Timestamp-skew counterfactual. Scenario: inject a 0.30 s position–heading skew in the single-GNSS–IMU branch, or a 0.25 s skew in the dual branch. Source gate: single branch allows at most 0.25 s; dual branch allows at most 0.2 s skew and 0.3 s heading age [PAGE 3–4]. Prediction: the state constructor withholds the navigation-valid state; the monitor refreshes a zero-velocity command rather than allowing a one-shot stop to age out [PAGE 6]. Verifiable veto: check the emitted odometry topic and the validity flag; the expected result is no accepted state during the skew interval, not a degraded MAE sample. Any tracking statistic computed across that interval is vetoed because the paper’s contract requires timestamp coherence, not merely parsed messages.

3. Baseline or antenna-swap counterfactual. Scenario: change the dual-antenna baseline outside 1.18±0.05 m, or swap ANT1 and ANT2 without recalibrating \(\alpha_b\). Source gate: measured baseline must lie within 1.18±0.05 m, and swapping antennas changes heading by approximately \(\pi\) [PAGE 4, PAGE 7]. Prediction: if baseline is outside tolerance, the dual state is rejected. If the swap is not corrected, \(\chi\) is offset by approximately \(\pi\) rad; the heading error \(e_\psi\) and cross-track error \(e_y\) become geometrically inconsistent, and the 1.0 m cross-track abort is likely to trigger. Verifiable veto: inspect the baseline gate flag, the heading residual against a known physical heading, and the abort log. This scenario vetoes any claim that dual GNSS is automatically more accurate regardless of installation geometry; the paper explicitly requires calibrated baseline geometry and warns that omitting either antenna, feed, or calibration makes the setup incomplete [PAGE 2].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
