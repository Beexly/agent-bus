**1. The Binocular Projection Operator ($\Pi_{binoc}$)**
We model the visual system as a dual-pinhole camera setup. Let the distance between the centers of rotation of the left and right eyes be the interpupillary distance (IPD), denoted as $I$ (typically $0.064$ m). The visual axes converge at a fixation distance $d_f$ (m). The vergence angle $\alpha$ (rad) is given by:
$$ \alpha = 2 \arctan\left(\frac{I}{2 d_f}\right) $$
A point in 3D space $P = (X, Y, Z)$ is projected onto the left and right retinas, yielding retinotopic coordinates $u_L, v_L$ and $u_R, v_R$ (measured in meters relative to the fovea). The projection operator $\Pi_{binoc}$ is defined as:
$$ \Pi_{binoc}: \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2, \quad P \mapsto \left( (u_L, v_L), (u_R, v_R) \right) $$

**2. The Geometric Similarity and Disparity Metric ($\Delta_{ret}$)**
James's concept of "geometrically similar" points refers to points that perceive the same spatial location when fused. The locus of such points in 3D space forms the *Horopter*. For small angles, the horizontal disparity $\delta_u$ (linear distance on the retina in meters) is approximated by:
$$ \delta_u = u_L - (-u_R) = u_L + u_R $$
Assuming the fovea is at $u=0$ for both eyes. If $\delta_u = 0$, the points are geometrically similar (on the horopter). The formal Disparity Operator $\mathcal{D}$ outputs the magnitude of geometric disparity:
$$ \mathcal{D}(P) = \| \Pi_L(P) - \Pi_R^{sym}(P) \|_2 $$
where $\Pi_R^{sym}$ denotes the coordinates after mirroring the right retinal frame to align with the left. The output unit is meters (m) on the retinal surface.

**3. The Perceptual Direction Mapping ($f_{dir}$)**
James states that disparate points are projected in disparate directions. We formalize the mapping from a retinal coordinate $u$ to a perceived visual direction $\theta$ (radians relative to the primary line of sight):
$$ \theta = f_{dir}(u) \approx k \cdot u $$
where $k$ is a scaling factor dependent on the nodal point distance to the retina (approx. $0.017$ m). 
For an object generating homonymous diplopia (hitting both nasal retinas, so $u_L < 0$ and $u_R > 0$ in standard Cartesian coordinates centered on fovea, assuming nasal is toward the nose), the perceived directions become:
$$ \theta_L = k \cdot u_L \quad (\text{Leftward}) $$
$$ \theta_R = k \cdot u_R \quad (\text{Rightward}) $$
The angular separation (Diplopic Angle) $\Theta_{dip}$ is:
$$ \Theta_{dip} = | \theta_L - \theta_R | = k \cdot | u_L - u_R | = k \cdot \delta_u $$

The genealogical lineage extends backward to Thomas Young and even further to Kepler’s initial understanding of inverted retinal images. <CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
While James presents the geometric projection of disparate points as an ironclad rule, the biological reality of vision is governed by a hierarchical series of "vetoes" capable of overriding pure optics. We can categorize these vetoes across physical, neurological, and environmental layers.

**1. The Physiological/Biomechanical Veto: Panum’s Fusional Area**
This is the primary veto against James's absolute dichotomy. The human visual system possesses a narrow region in front of and behind the horopter called Panum’s area (typically extending $\pm 5$ to $10$ arcminutes horizontally). Within this zone, even though the retinal images fall on technically "disparate" points (violating strict geometric similarity), the brain’s fusion mechanisms forcefully veto the diplopic projection. The disparate directions are collapsed into a single percept, with the remaining disparity converted purely into a sensation of relative depth. James’s rule is thus adjudicated as "conditionally true": it only manifests once the disparity metric $\mathcal{D}(P)$ crosses the Panum threshold.

**2. The Neurological Veto: Anomalous Retinal Correspondence (ARC)**
In cases of long-standing strabismus (e.g., esotropia), the brain employs a radical veto over anatomical geometry. Through neuroplasticity, the sensory decussation is altered. A point on the deviated eye's retina (say, far out in the temporal field) is surgically "reassigned" to correspond to the fovea of the fixing eye. When an object falls on these newly aligned points, the geometric reality dictates massive disparate projection, yet the patient reports a single, fused image (though often displaced in space). Here, the neurological layer entirely overrides the optical/geometric layer.

**3. The Contextual/Monocular Veto**
Powerful monocular depth cues possess veto authority over binocular disparity. Consider a forced-perspective illusion (like the Ames Room) or a magic trick utilizing mirrors. The binocular inputs might inherently contain slight disparities suggesting one layout, but if occlusion, lighting, and perspective overwhelmingly suggest another, the cortex issues a top-down veto, suppressing any nascent diplopia and locking the percept to the monocular narrative.

**4. The Environmental/Weather Veto (Scattering and Luminance)**
Physical atmospheric conditions can veto the formation of clear disparate images. In heavy fog, rain, or snow, the scattering of light (Mie and Rayleigh scattering) drastically reduces contrast and introduces veiling luminance. To maintain visibility, the pupil dilates, increasing optical aberrations. More importantly, under low-contrast conditions, the precision of retinal feature detection degrades. If the edges of the stimulus are blurred beyond the resolution required to compute fine disparity, the system defaults to coarse, monocular processing. The sharp, distinct "double star" James describes dissolves into a hazy, singular blob. The weather physically destroys the prerequisite signal integrity needed for the disparate projection mechanism to engage.

**Proof of Veto Dominance:**
If James's geometric law were absolute, every person with even a micro-strabismus would experience constant, chaotic double vision. They do not. Furthermore, if geometry were supreme, astronauts undergoing microgravity fluid shifts (which alter the shape of the eyeball and thus the focal length) would suffer unmitigated diplopia; instead, their visual cortex rapidly adapts (vetoing the geometric shift) within days.
