No source-supplied equation appears in this review; the following are audit-grade modeling operators, stated with assumptions, SI or information units, and exact breakdown domains. They are not presented as author claims.

1. Whole-body heat balance. Let \(\dot S\) be the body heat storage rate in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), \(M\) metabolic heat production in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), \(W\) external work in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), \(\dot E_{\mathrm{resp}}\) respiratory evaporative loss in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), \(\dot E_{\mathrm{sw}}\) sweat evaporation in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\), and \(C\), \(R\), \(K\) convective, radiative, and conductive exchange in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\). A lumped operator is
\[
\dot S=M-W-\dot E_{\mathrm{resp}}-\dot E_{\mathrm{sw}}-C-R-K .
\]
With body heat capacity \(C_b\) in \(\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), body mass \(m_b\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\), Du Bois area \(A_D\) in \(\mathrm{m^2}\), and mean body temperature \(T_b\) in \(\mathrm{K}\),
\[
\dot S=\frac{C_b m_b}{A_D}\frac{dT_b}{dt}.
\]
Assumptions: whole-body lump, dry exchange signs defined as heat gain positive, and no storage in clothing. Mathematical domain edge: \(A_D=0\) or \(m_b=0\) is inadmissible. Physiological breakdown: when \(\dot E_{\mathrm{sw}}\) is near its maximum and \(C+R+K\) cannot offset \(M-W-\dot E_{\mathrm{resp}}\), then \(\dot S\) remains positive and \(T_b\) rises. This is the formal version of [PAGE 6] “difficultés de dissipation de chaleur corporelle pouvant augmenter la température centrale” and [PAGE 12] “l’augmentation de la température centrale induite par la TE et/ou l’exercice”.

2. Hydration deficit. With \(m_{b,0}\) the pre-exercise body mass in \(\mathrm{kg}\) and \(m_b(t)\) the current mass in \(\mathrm{kg}\),
\[
D_w(t)=\frac{m_{b,0}-m_b(t)}{m_{b,0}},
\]
dimensionless and usually reported as percent. Assumptions: mass change dominated by sweat and respiratory water loss, with fluid intake and urine accounted for in \(m_b(t)\). Domain edge: \(m_b(t)=0\) is physically irrelevant; \(D_w\) is bounded in practice. Performance and safety breakdown are not a mathematical singularity but a threshold region: [PAGE 11] cites a 2% body-mass-loss ceiling but also notes that “jusqu’à 3% de perte de masse corporelle” did not influence a 20 km cycling time trial in heat. [PAGE 12] recommends beginning fluid intake “avant d’atteindre 1 à 2% de la perte de masse corporelle” because cognition can be affected before marked dehydration. The exact adjudication is context-dependent: \(D_w\ge 0.02\) is a conservative safety flag, while \(D_w\approx 0.03\) is not a universal performance cliff in every task.

3. Acclimation kinetics. For an adaptation magnitude \(A(t)\) with saturation \(A_\infty\) and time constant \(\tau\) in days,
\[
A(t)=A_\infty\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{t}{\tau}\right)\right],\quad t\ge 0.
\]
Units of \(A\) may be \(\mathrm{L}\) for plasma volume, \(\mathrm{L\,h^{-1}}\) for sweat rate, or \(\mathrm{bpm}\) for heart-rate change, depending on the measured adaptation. Assumptions: constant heat dose, adequate recovery, and no injury or overtraining. There is no finite-time mathematical singularity; the model is saturating. The breakdown regime is biological: when cumulative environmental and training load exceeds recovery capacity, the monotone model fails. The source warns exactly against this: [PAGE 10] states athletes must not “basculer dans le surentrainement par un excès de charge, dont celle environnementale, trop important” and must not have “excès de confiance”. The recommended clock is [PAGE 9] “au moins 14 jours avant le début des épreuves”.

4. Cooling energy and central-temperature change. For a cooling intervention active from \(t_0\) to \(t_1\),
\[
Q_{\mathrm{cool}}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\dot Q_{\mathrm{cool}}(t)\,dt \quad [\mathrm{J}].
\]
A first-order estimate of central-temperature reduction, assuming well-mixed body and negligible simultaneous metabolic heat production, is
\[
\Delta T_c=-\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cool}}}{C_b m_b},\quad C_b\approx 3.47\times 10^3\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}.
\]
For ice slurry or cold water, the absorbed heat can be approximated as
\[
Q_{\mathrm{cool}}=m_{\mathrm{ice}}\left[c_{p,w}(T_{\mathrm{body}}-T_0)+L_f\right],
\]
with \(c_{p,w}\approx 4.18\times 10^3\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), \(L_f\approx 3.34\times 10^5\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}\), \(T_0\) in \(\mathrm{K}\), and \(m_{\mathrm{ice}}\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\). Assumptions: complete melting and absorption, no shivering, and no cold-induced vasoconstriction severe enough to negate heat loss. Domain edge: \(m_b=0\) is inadmissible. Safety breakdown: excessive or poorly tolerated cooling can produce “inconfort, des troubles gastriques, des allergies ou des céphalées” [PAGE 13], and external devices may be banned by “les règlements des compétitions” [PAGE 14]. Thus \(Q_{\mathrm{cool}}\) is not a free variable; it is constrained by tolerance and rules.

5. Menthol dose. The source gives a beverage mass fraction range:
\[
\phi_m\in[0.01\%,0.1\%]=[10^{-4},10^{-3}]\quad \mathrm{w/w}.
\]
[PAGE 13] states: “Le menthol doit cependant être prudemment et précisément dosé (de 0,01% à 0,1%) dans les boissons”. Assumption: uniform beverage concentration. Breakdown: outside this range, the thermal information via TRPM8 may bias central analysis; the same page notes that cold-receptor information “est susceptible de biaiser l’analyse des situations par le système nerveux central [69, 75].” This is a perceptual operator, not a proof of reduced core temperature.

6. Information-resource reserve. Let \(C_a(t)\) be available cognitive channel capacity in \(\mathrm{bit\,s^{-1}}\) and \(D(t)\) the task demand in \(\mathrm{bit\,s^{-1}}\). A reserve operator is
\[
R_a(t)=C_a(t)-D(t)\quad [\mathrm{bit\,s^{-1}}].
\]
Stable task performance requires \(R_a(t)\ge 0\). Assumptions: additive task demand, no parallel resource conflict, and constant encoding efficiency. Breakdown: \(R_a(t)\) negative means overload, attentional narrowing, and errors; heat can lower \(C_a\) and raise \(D\) simultaneously. This is consistent with [PAGE 15] “on dispose de ressources cognitives limitées” and [PAGE 17] “l'écoute de la musique empêche le sportif d’être attentif simultanément aux sensations de fatigue dues à l’effort”. \[
P(H_1\mid D)=\frac{P(D\mid H_1)P(H_1)}{P(D\mid H_1)P(H_1)+P(D\mid H_0)P(H_0)}.
\]
All probabilities are dimensionless in \([0,1]\). Assumptions: \(H_0\) and \(H_1\) are exhaustive and \(P(D)>0\). Breakdown: if \(P(D)=0\), the conditional probability is undefined. This operator governs the RLVR vetoes below: a strategy is not admitted merely because a narrative review cites it; it must survive a pre-registered measurable prediction.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under heat, the athlete’s crisis habit regression is predictable: the athlete reverts to pre-trained pacing, ignores thirst, and interprets discomfort as a character test. The source gives direct support: [PAGE 11] states “la sensation de soif peut ne pas être un bon indicateur du statut d'hydratation”, and [PAGE 16] states that motivation, willingness to continue, and thermal perceptions can be “négativement impactés avant tout changement physiologique mesurable”. That is a recursive trap. The athlete’s internal model says “I feel capable, therefore I am safe.” The coach’s model says “the athlete has trained, therefore the athlete is adapted.” The athlete then models the coach’s confidence and suppresses the warning signal. This is not a failure of willpower; it is a failure of shared state estimation.

Positive self-talk can be a counter-trap, but only if it is trained and bounded. [PAGE 17] reports that positive self-talk improved “la durée maximale de pédalage” and cognitive-task speed and precision. That is a top-down regulation effect, not a thermodynamic one. The recursive theory-of-mind requirement is that the athlete and coach share a third model: the physiological state model. The athlete must not infer safety from the coach’s calm; the coach must not infer safety from the athlete’s compliance. Music creates a similar recursion. [PAGE 17] explains that music prevents the athlete from being simultaneously attentive to fatigue; [PAGE 18] reports the large synchronous-music effect but also warns of “dépassement des seuils physiologiques”. The athlete can mistake distraction for reserve, and the coach can mistake improved tolerance for improved safety.

The clock is the corrective. Acute perceptual strategies operate on minutes: cold suggestion was “limitée à une dizaine de minutes” [PAGE 19]. Hydration decisions operate on the 1-2% body-mass-loss window [PAGE 12]. Acclimation operates on 1-2 weeks and a 14-day arrival threshold [PAGE 9]. Self-talk training operates on two weeks [PAGE 16-17]. Mindfulness operates on seven weeks [PAGE 20]. The recursive counter-trap is therefore a clock-aware protocol: the athlete uses self-talk or music only when a separate clock—core temperature, sweat rate, body mass, RPE trend, and competition rules—is being monitored externally. The athlete’s model of the coach’s model should include the sentence “my perceived relief is not evidence of my physiological reserve.” That is the only way to keep a psychological strategy from becoming a recursive permission structure for overrun.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The physical layer has veto power because heat balance is not negotiable. If \(\dot S>0\) persists and evaporative capacity is saturated, central temperature rises regardless of motivation. The hydration layer adds a second veto: severe dehydration can cause “crampes [52]” and “malaises pouvant entraîner une hyperthermie maligne d’exercice (e.g., coup de chaleur d’exercice) et causer la mort [53]” [PAGE 11]. A music or self-talk intervention cannot prevent neuromuscular failure in repeated-sprint work; it can only change pacing, attention, or tolerance. The weather layer vetoes planning that ignores the actual environment. A strategy that is physiologically sound but rule-illegal is not admissible.

Adjudication order should be: first, safety and medical veto; second, competition-rule veto; third, physiological-reserve veto; fourth, psychological-strategy admission as adjunct. The proof is that the source repeatedly frames mental strategies as modifiers of perception and top-down control, not as replacements for cooling or acclimation. [PAGE 21-22] calls them a “complément potentiellement intéressant” and warns of “contre performance”. [PAGE 18] explicitly warns that music can cause “non prises en compte des signaux d’alertes”. Thus the cross-layer veto is asymmetric: a psychological strategy may be admitted only if it does not increase the probability that the athlete ignores a physical alarm. If it does, the physical layer wins.

Scenario 1: Acclimation-duration veto. Test 7-day versus 14-day active heat acclimation in a tropical or hot-humid environment.
