**Operator 1 — Convolutional activation geometry (Eq. 1, [PAGE 6]).**

$$V_2 = \frac{V_1 - F + 2P_z}{S} + 1 \quad [\text{px}]$$

where $V_1, V_2$ are input/output spatial extents in pixels, $F$ the filter extent in px, $P_z$ zero-padding in px, $S$ the stride in px·px⁻¹ (dimensionless integer). **Breakdown regime:** $V_2 \to 0$ when $F > V_1 + 2P_z$; the layer becomes degenerate and no activation survives. For a 1–2 px ball entering a 3×3 filter at $P_z=1$, $V_2$ saturates at the input size but the *effective* signal-to-noise of the response is near zero because the filter integrates 9 cells of which at most 2 carry ball energy.

**Operator 2 — Local Response Normalization (Eq. 2, [PAGE 6]).**

$$b^{i}_{x,y} = \frac{a^{i}_{x,y}}{\left(k + \alpha \sum_{j=\max(0,\,i-n/2)}^{\min(N-1,\,i+n/2)} \left(a^{j}_{x,y}\right)^{2}\right)^{\beta}}$$

Units: $a, b$ are dimensionless activations; $k$ dimensionless offset; $\alpha$ in (activation)⁻²; $\beta$ dimensionless exponent. **Breakdown:** as $\alpha \sum (a^j)^2 \gg k$, the denominator grows superlinearly and suppresses an isolated high-contrast blob (the ball) adjacent to a low-contrast region (grass) — the opposite of the desired effect for small targets.

**Operator 3 — Cross-entropy classification loss (Eq. 4, [PAGE 6]).**

$$\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{i}\sum_{j} t_{ij}\,\ln P(t_j = 1 \mid x_i; \Theta) \quad [\text{nats}]$$

$t_{ij} \in \{0,1\}$ dimensionless one-hot target; $P \in (0,1]$ dimensionless. Answer in nats; divide by $\ln 2$ for bits. **Singularity:** $\mathcal{L} \to +\infty$ as $P \to 0$ for a true class. This is the exact regime the 0.35 gate avoids by refusing to commit — the authors are buying finite loss at the price of the 67 ball false negatives [PAGE 20].

**Operator 4 — Softmax output (Eq. 5, [PAGE 6]).**

$$y_j = \frac{e^{z_j}}{\sum_{k} e^{z_k}}, \qquad \sum_j y_j = 1$$

**Breakdown:** numerical overflow when $\max_k z_k$ exceeds float range; standard shift-invariance correction $z_j \leftarrow z_j - \max_k z_k$ is required and unstated in the paper.

**Operator 5 — Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (Eq. 6, [PAGE 7]).**

$$\Theta_{l+1} = \Theta_{l} - \alpha \nabla E(\Theta_l) + \gamma\left(\Theta_l - \Theta_{l-1}\right)$$

$\alpha$ learning rate in (parameter)⁻¹·(loss-unit)⁻¹; $\gamma \in [0,1)$ dimensionless; $\nabla E$ in loss-units·(parameter)⁻¹. **Breakdown:** $\gamma \to 1$ gives an undamped integrator whose effective step is $\alpha/(1-\gamma)$ — divergence when $\alpha/(1-\gamma)$ exceeds the curvature bound $2/\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2 E)$.

**Operator 6 — YOLO bounding-box decode (Eq. 9, [PAGE 13]).**

$$b_x = \sigma(t_x) + c_x,\quad b_y = \sigma(t_y) + c_y,\quad b_w = p_w e^{t_w},\quad b_h = p_h e^{t_h}$$

$b_x,b_y,c_x,c_y \in [0, W]\times[0,H]$ in px; $b_w,b_h,p_w,p_h$ in px; $t_\bullet$ dimensionless logits. **Singularity:** $\sigma'(t) = \sigma(t)(1-\sigma(t)) \to 0$ as $|t| \to \infty$ — the vanishing-gradient regime. For a target whose center sits near a cell boundary, $t_x$ must be large in magnitude, so the very detections that need the most correction receive the least gradient. Widened $b_w = p_w e^{t_w}$ is unbounded above: a single mis-scaled $t_w$ produces boxes larger than the frame, which the paper's own "high chance of getting false-positives" note [PAGE 7] does not cover.

**Operator 7 — Anchor-prior k-means distance (Eq. 10, [PAGE 14]).**

$$d(\text{box}, \text{centroid}) = 1 - \mathrm{IOU}(\text{box}, \text{centroid}) \quad [\text{dimensionless}]$$

**Singularity:** $d \to 0$ as $\mathrm{IOU} \to 1$. The metric is scale-free by construction, which is why the authors adopt it — but it is also *empty* for IOU = 0 pairs (all $d=1$), so a cluster of non-overlapping priors has zero gradient and the centroid never moves. Priors for a 1–2 px ball therefore cannot be recovered by this objective; they are inherited from COCO's 80 classes.

**Operator 8 — SORT state vector and linear motion (Eq. 12, [PAGE 16]).**

$$\mathbf{x} = \left[u,\; v,\; s,\; r,\; \dot{u},\; \dot{v},\; \dot{s}\right]^{\!\top},\qquad \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{w}_k$$

$u,v$ in px; $s$ (box area) in px²; $r$ (aspect ratio) dimensionless; $\dot{u},\dot{v}$ in px·frame⁻¹; $\dot{s}$ in px²·frame⁻¹; $\mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{Q})$ with $\mathbf{Q}$ in the corresponding squared units. **Note the paper's notation collision:** it writes "'s' represents the area and 'v' represents the aspect ratio" [PAGE 16] while $v$ is already the vertical center — the standard SORT symbol is $r$, and the paper's own text is internally ambiguous. **Breakdown regime:** the constant-velocity assumption fails whenever the second derivative is non-negligible over the innovation window. For a ball struck at ~30 m·s⁻¹ at 25 fps, inter-frame displacement is 1.2 m; any curvature in the trajectory over that interval produces an innovation that exceeds the gate and forces a new identity.

**Operator 9 — Hungarian assignment cost (Eq. 11, [PAGE 15]).**

$$C_{b,k} = \left\| \mathbf{C}_b - \mathbf{C}'_{k+1} \right\|_2 + \lambda\left| P_b - P_{k-1} \right|$$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage runs backward through four distinct scientific strata, and each stratum deposits a trap that this paper inherits without discharging.

**Stratum I — Static-background subtraction (Sophie et al. [2], cited [PAGE 4]).** The paper records their method fairly: "they used a background learning process" and "a simple and time efficient variance based function is used and noise is removed using a morphological function" — but also records the fatal limit verbatim: "their algorithm works only when the ball is separated from the player and cannot detect the ball when it is completely or partially occluded by the player" [PAGE 4]. *Multi-week trap:* the variance-threshold regime is silently re-imported whenever occlusion is dense. The present paper's ball recall of 75% is the same trap resurfacing under a different name — occlusion is still the dominant false-negative mechanism, as the authors admit: "Missed detections occur occasionally when the ball is severely occluded by the players" [PAGE 20].

**Stratum II — Geometric field priors (Wayne et al. [3], [PAGE 4]).** Hough-transform boundary lines, corners, vanishing points, median-filter background subtraction. *Trap:* field-line geometry is a strong prior in a fixed camera and a false anchor in a panning one. The present paper inherits the problem but not the solution — it never estimates a homography, despite claiming to exploit field color conventions ("Teams are not allowed to wear green on the field since the grass itself is similar in color" [PAGE 3]) purely as a classifier cue, never as a geometry cue.

**Stratum III — Blob decomposition and joint interaction modeling ([4], [5], [PAGE 4]).** Choi et al.'s player-blob subtraction; Maksai et al.'s Mixed Integer Program with "second order motion of the ball, player and ball interaction" [PAGE 4]. *Trap:* the MIP formulation encodes physical constraints that the SORT/Kalman pipeline discards entirely. The descent from physically-constrained interaction modeling to a constant-velocity per-target filter is a genuine capability regression, and the paper does not acknowledge it.

**Stratum IV — Deep association (DeepSORT, Wojke et al. [9], [PAGE 5]) and trajectory-first detection (Yu et al. [7], [PAGE 4]).** The paper cites both but adopts neither: it uses appearance-free SORT rather than DeepSORT's re-identification embedding, and it uses frame-first detection rather than Yu's candidate-trajectory-first logic. *Trap:* the paper's own "future scope" section proposes jersey-color analysis [PAGE 22] — which is precisely DeepSORT's appearance metric, rediscovered a decade late. The genealogy is therefore circular: the authors route around a published solution and list that solution as future work.

The **retrocausal** structure is the important part. The final metric — ball $F_1 = 0.85$ against player $F_1 = 0.95$ [PAGE 20] — is not produced by the architecture; it is produced by a decision made at the very end of the pipeline, the 0.35 confidence gate, which "leads to a slight increase in the number of false negatives as the ball was distorted due to its high speed or was very small due to its distance from the camera" [PAGE 21]. Read backward, the entire upstream design (three-scale FPN, Darknet-53 depth, anchor priors from COCO) is retro-justified by a downstream scalar. And the GOTURN comparison [PAGE 22] is retrocausally weak: it demonstrates that a *different* tracker misidentifies the goalkeeper's glove as the ball, but it does not isolate whether YOLOv3 avoids that error because of feature learning or because of the 0.35 gate.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model the system as three nested belief states. **B_annotator** is the human who produced ground truth by "manual inspection" [PAGE 21]. **B_detector** is YOLOv3's implicit posterior over "is this a ball." **B_tracker** is SORT's posterior over "is this the same entity."

**B_annotator vs. B_detector — the frame-denominator recursion.** The annotator decided the ball was absent in 89 of 400 frames [PAGE 21]. The detector never sees that decision. So every ball false negative counted in the 67 figure is scored against a belief state the model was never given. The recursion is: the model believes a ball is present, the annotator believes it is not, and the metric silently sides with the annotator. This is not a bug in the detector; it is an unmodeled prior.

**Crisis habit regression.** When the ball's evidence collapses — motion blur at 30 m·s⁻¹, sub-pixel scale in the top half of the field [PAGE 21] — the network reverts to its strongest training-time associative habit: *white, round, near a hand*. That is exactly the failure the authors document in the GOTURN baseline, where the model "identifies the glove of the goal keeper as the ball because of similarity in color and shape with the ball" [PAGE 22]. The present paper presents this as a GOTURN-specific pathology, but the mechanism — color-and-shape prior overriding motion evidence — is architecture-agnostic. YOLOv3 has the same prior; it is merely gated at 0.35. Consequently the paper's 97% ball precision [PAGE 20] is a statement about the gate, not about the representation.

**Counter-trap.** The deliberate defenses against habit regression are: (i) the 0.35 confidence gate [PAGE 18]; (ii) the `IOU_min` rejection of low-overlap assignments [PAGE 16]; (iii) the probation period before a new track is trusted [PAGE 16]; (iv) the `T_lost` termination rule [PAGE 16]. Each is a hand-set scalar standing in for a belief. None is learned from data, and none is cross-calibrated with the others. A genuine counter-trap would couple them — e.g. raise the confidence gate *only while* `IOU_min` is satisfied, so that the two independent evidence channels must agree. The paper does not do this, which is why the reported failure mode persists: "Tracking errors mainly occur when 2 or more players occlude each other and this leads the model to detecting them as the same player" [PAGE 21].

**Clock.** The temporal lattice is 25 fps [PAGE 16], and every belief update is indexed to it. The Kalman prediction is one frame ahead; the probation period is a fixed number of frames; `T_lost` is a frame count. This makes the system's notion of "same player" purely an elapsed-time notion. The paper's own recovery observation — "once the players get separated from one another, the tracking algorithm is able to distinguish them as they were before the occlusion" [PAGE 21] — is a clock statement, not an identity statement: identity was never lost, only the measurement was. At 25 fps a 1-second occlusion is 25 frames; whether that is inside or outside `T_lost` determines whether the identity survives, and the paper never reports the value of `T_lost`. This is the deepest recursion gap: the reader cannot reconstruct the system's memory horizon.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Four physical layers can veto a detection. The question is which one actually dominates, and the paper's own numbers adjudicate.

**Veto L1 — Optical resolution (dominant).** Stated as the first challenge: "The size of the ball is very small when compared to the size of each frame size. This is a major issue in BSV in which the camera shots are far-view shots" [PAGE 7]. Quantify: at 640×360 training resolution [PAGE 20], a ball subtending ~1–2 px in the far half of the pitch occupies ≤0.5 cells of the stride-8 finest feature map. *Proof of dominance:* the paper's own spatial asymmetry — "good results when the ball is in the bottom half of the field (closer to the camera), but the results deteriorated when it was in the top half of the field i.e. farther away from the camera" [PAGE 21] — is a pure projected-size gradient. No other veto mechanism produces a monotone dependence on image-plane row. Verdict: **L1 dominates**, accounting for the 75% → 97% recall gap between ball and player [PAGE 20].

**Veto L2 — Occlusion geometry (secondary, identity-destroying).** "Frequent occlusion of the ball by the players takes place which makes detection difficult" [PAGE 7]; "the ball may appear to have merged with the lines on the field or the players in few frames" [PAGE 7]. *Proof of non-dominance for detection but dominance for tracking:* occlusion reduces recall (already counted under L1's 67 false negatives) but its distinct signature is the identity merge — "2 or more players occlude each other and this leads the model to detecting them as the same player" [PAGE 21]. Verdict: **L2 vetoes identity, not existence.**

**Veto L3 — Biomechanical/pose non-stationarity.** "soccer players contort their bodies in unique poses that don't occur in pose estimation datasets" [PAGE 3], compounded by "changes in player's clothes, position, pose, and velocity" [PAGE 3]. *Proof of bounded effect:* if L3 were dominant, player precision would fall below player recall, because pose novelty creates false positives (misclassified body parts). Observed: player precision 97%, recall 93% [PAGE 20] — precision *exceeds* recall, meaning the system errs conservative. L3 is therefore vetoed as a first-order cause. Verdict: **L3 is real but sub-threshold.**

**Veto L4 — Environmental/weather.** "shadowing, shape distortions caused by high velocity, the ball kicked out of frame, and environmental conditions" [PAGE 3]. *Proof of under-determination:* the dataset comprises four tournaments — "UEFA EURO 2016, FIFA World Cup 2014, Premier League 2016/2017 and AFC Asian Cup 2015" [PAGE 16] — with no reported stratification by weather, illumination, or stadium. Without per-condition breakdown, L4 cannot be adjudicated from this paper at all; it is an unquantified residual. Verdict: **L4 is unfalsifiable as reported.**

**Adjudication.** L1 ≻ L2 (for identity) ≻ L3 ≻ L4 (unmeasured). The practical consequence: improving the anchor-prior set for sub-grid objects, or upsampling the finest prediction scale from stride 8 to stride 4, targets the dominant veto. Tightening the confidence gate targets L1's symptom while paying in false negatives — which is exactly what the authors did [PAGE 21].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario is falsifiable against Table 2 [PAGE 20] or against a stated mechanism in the text.

**Veto 1 — Confidence-gate counterfactual.** *Intervention:* lower the detection threshold from 0.35 [PAGE 18] to 0.15. *Mechanism:* the paper states "There is a high chance of getting false-positives due to the presence of several ball-like objects in the given frame" [PAGE 7], and the 0.35 value exists specifically "to avoid false positives" [PAGE 21]. *Prediction:* ball precision falls below 0.90 while ball recall rises above 0.85 on the same 311 frames; player precision degrades far less because the "person" class has no confusable co-located distractor of comparable scale. *Falsifier:* ball precision holding at ≥0.95 under the 0.15 threshold would refute the claim that ball precision is gate-driven rather than representation-driven.

**Veto 2 — Camera-motion-compensation counterfactual.** *Intervention:* estimate the inter-frame global homography from static field lines (the Hough/vanishing-point machinery the paper cites but never implements [PAGE 4]) and warp the Kalman prediction before the IOU association step. *Mechanism:* the paper concedes the motion model is "independent of other objects in the frame and motion of the camera" [PAGE 16], while preprocessing explicitly removes zoom-ins and replays [PAGE 2] — meaning residual pan survives inside the retained far-view shots. *Prediction:* identity switches during pan segments drop substantially, and the "2 or more players occlude each other … same player" failure [PAGE 21] decreases in frequency because the innovation residual is no longer inflated by ego-motion. *Falsifier:* unchanged switch counts would prove the mapping is dominated by target–target occlusion rather than camera motion, shifting the remedy to appearance re-identification (DeepSORT).

**Veto 3 — Motion-model-order counterfactual.** *Intervention:* replace the constant-velocity state with constant-acceleration (augment $\mathbf{x}$ with $\ddot{u},\ddot{v}$) or an IMM mixture. *Mechanism:* Eq. (12) [PAGE 16] carries three velocity terms and no acceleration term; a ball struck at ~30 m·s⁻¹ displaces ~1.2 m per 25 fps frame, and any curvature over that interval breaks the model outright. *Prediction:* ball track lifetime (frames per identity) increases measurably while player track lifetime is essentially unchanged, because player acceleration is bounded by biomechanics and ball acceleration is bounded only by aerodynamics and impact. *Falsifier:* if ball track lifetime is invariant to model order, then the loss is entirely in the detector's frame-level misses (the 67 false negatives), not in the filter — which would redirect effort to Operator 6's sub-grid decode rather than to SORT.

All three vetoes are stated in terms the paper's own reported quantities can adjudicate, and none requires equipment or annotation beyond what Section 4.3 [PAGE 16] already describes.
