An observer modeling the organism must therefore distinguish between (a) a genuine failure of spontaneous recovery and (b) habit regression under changed motivational conditions. The trap is counterfactual: the observer attributes post-break gains to a latent learning process that never occurred. A recursive model must represent the observer's false belief ("practice during rest") versus the organism's actual state (reduced inhibition, unchanged habit strength). If the observer then schedules more breaks expecting further gains, they are operating inside the counter-trap.

Clock: Two temporal clocks govern the corollaries. The external clock is the inter-reinforcement interval \( \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \) and the no-practice lag \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} \); Corollary XVIII requires \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} \) to be several times \( \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \). The internal clock is the organism's decay of inhibition over that lag. An observer modeling another agent must track both clocks. If the observer misestimates \( \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \) or assumes \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} \gg \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \) when it is only marginally larger, the observer will predict reminiscence where the organism shows none. Recursive theory of mind therefore requires the observer to model the organism's schedule-sensitive inhibitory decay, not merely its habit strength.

Crisis regression interacts with the clock: under crisis conditions, the organism's internal motivational clock desynchronizes from the external reinforcement schedule, invalidating the synchrony assumption embedded in XVIII–XXI.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto adjudication examines whether observed "reminiscence" can survive independent physical, biomechanical, and environmental challenges. Any one layer failure vetoes the psychological inference regardless of Hullian fit.

Physical layer: The stimulus must be physically delivered and detected. If the stimulus transducer fails, latency exceeds the organism's sensory integration window, or the receptor organ is impaired, the re-delivered stimulus on [PAGE 296] never registers as the conditioned stimulus. In that case, sER cannot be evoked, and an observed absence of response is a physical veto, not a psychological one. A claimed reminiscence effect must be rejected unless stimulus delivery is verified independently of the organism's response.

Biomechanical layer: Motor output constraints can masquerade as inhibitory potential. Fatigue, neuromuscular refractory periods, glycogen depletion, or tendon strain can suppress reaction emission after massed practice. If the organism's reaction apparatus is temporarily fatigued, a post-break increase in responses may reflect biomechanical recovery rather than increased sER. Conversely, a failure to show reminiscence may reflect persistent fatigue rather than failed spontaneous recovery. The veto condition: muscle and neuromuscular state must be equated or monitored across pre-break and post-break tests; otherwise the measured \( R_{10} \) conflates \( s_{ER} \) with peripheral execution capacity.

Weather / environmental layer: Hull's constant-motivation assumption is environmentally embedded. Temperature, humidity, barometric pressure, and ambient context can alter drive \( M \), sensory thresholds, and the effective stimulus intensity. If the no-practice period occurs in a different thermal or contextual environment than the acquisition phase, motivation is not constant and the stimulus may not be perceptually equivalent. A 24-hour no-practice period spanning day-night transitions or weather changes introduces uncontrolled environmental variance. Veto condition: the testing environment must match the acquisition environment, or environmental covariates must be measured and shown not to covary with \( M \) or stimulus detection.

Veto proof: A reminiscence claim on [PAGE 296] is provisionally acceptable only if (1) stimulus delivery is physically verified, (2) biomechanical execution capacity is shown not to limit response emission, and (3) environmental context and motivation are held constant or measured as invariant. Absent any one of these, the observed difference between \( s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}} + \Delta t_{\text{NP}}) \) and \( s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}}) \) is not attributable to Hullian reminiscence.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a condition, a verifiable measurement, and a prediction under Hull's framework. RLVR here denotes reinforcement-learning verification regimes in which counterfactual outcomes are checked against observable metrics.

Veto Scenario 1 — Motivation-drift veto:
Condition: Motivation \( M \) is not constant across the no-practice period; e.g., food deprivation changes such that \( |M_{\text{post}} - M_{\text{pre}}| > \epsilon_M \), where \( \epsilon_M \) is a pre-registered drive-difference threshold verified by independent deprivation-hour or response-rate measures.
Verification metric: Pre- and post-break drive indices (e.g., hours since last feeding, baseline free-response rate) must differ beyond threshold.
Prediction: Even if \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} \gg \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \), \( s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}} + \Delta t_{\text{NP}}) \ngtr s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}}) \). If drive drops, post-break response rate may fall below termination level despite massed practice. Any observed increase would be attributed to drive change, not reminiscence; a null or negative result confirms the veto.

Veto Scenario 2 — Insufficient no-practice lag veto:
Condition: The no-practice period is not "several times as long as the interval between reinforcements"; formally \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} \leq 2 \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \).
Verification metric: Session logs timestamped to confirm \( \Delta t_{\text{NP}} / \Delta t_{\text{IRI}} \leq 2 \).
Prediction: Spontaneous recovery is incomplete; aggregate inhibitory potential remains elevated; \( s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}} + \Delta t_{\text{NP}}) \approx s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}}) \) or lower. The reminiscence gain \( \mathcal{R} \) should be statistically indistinguishable from zero. A significant positive \( \mathcal{R} \) under this condition would falsify Corollary XVIII's boundary specification.

Veto Scenario 3 — Long-interval inversion veto (rote series):
Condition: Rote series learned by massed practice; no-practice lag \( \tau \) exceeds the inflection \( \tau^{*} \) where Corollary XX's rise with negative acceleration is replaced by a fall.
Verification metric: Multiple post-break test points across lags (e.g., 1 hour, 24 hours, 1 week, 4 weeks) to locate \( \tau^{*} \) empirically.
Prediction: \( \mathcal{R}(\tau) \) increases initially, peaks near \( \tau^{*} \), then declines; for \( \tau \gg \tau^{*} \), performance falls below \( s_{ER}(t_{\text{end}}) \) because forgetting dominates over spontaneous recovery. A monotonic simple growth function (Corollary XIX) extrapolated beyond \( \tau^{*} \) would be falsified by a downturn. Verification requires observing at least one post-peak lag with \( \mathcal{R} < 0 \) relative to termination.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
