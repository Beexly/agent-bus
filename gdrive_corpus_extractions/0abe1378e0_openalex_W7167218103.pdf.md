$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{cross}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}}\Big[\big\|\Pi_f(R_{t_b}J({}^w\phi_{t_b-1}^i)+C_{t_b})-J_{t_b}^i\big\|_2^2+\big\|\Pi_f(R_{t_b-1}J({}^w\phi_{t_b}^i)+C_{t_b-1})-J_{t_b-1}^i\big\|_2^2\Big].$$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage descends from parametric body models and converges on the geometry-as-anchor thesis. SMPL [18] (Loper et al., 2015) supplies the $\theta,\beta$ parameterization that every term optimizes. Single-image recovery (HMR, Kanazawa et al. [10], 2018) and video temporal models (VIBE [14], MEVA-family [21,22]) established per-frame and short-horizon pose, but "remained confined to single-person settings" ([PAGE 4]). Multi-person tracking arrived with 4DHumans [7] and PHALP-style trackers [29], which the paper credits for identity tracking but faults for "local image-space estimation, lacking global spatial reasoning in world coordinates" ([PAGE 4]). SLAHMR [44] introduced joint human-camera optimization for global consistency but at "prohibitive inference times" ([PAGE 4]); the field pivoted to feed-forward world-grounded models — WHAM [34], GVHMR [33] (gravity-view coordinates), PromptHMR [41] — all of which, per the paper, "assume continuous camera motion and fail to handle shot changes" ([PAGE 4]).

A parallel genealogy runs through 3D scene reconstruction: DUSt3R [40], MASt3R [16], MV-DUSt3R+ [36], culminating in VGGT [39], chosen because it "remains robust under the sparse viewpoints and extreme viewpoint changes typically observed at shot boundaries" ([PAGE 7]). Camera tracking borrows DROID-SLAM [37]. The Re-ID thread runs handcrafted features [5,6] → deep CNN [1,38] → ViT/TransReID [4,9] → training-free Pose2ID [45] and keypoint-promptable KPR [35]; the paper's argument is that all are "primarily appearance-based and struggle at shot boundaries" ([PAGE 5]). The assignment backbone is Kuhn's Hungarian method [15] (1955).

The multi-week "trap" a reproducer faces: the paper's headline numbers depend on the near-simultaneity assumption at the cut ([PAGE 7]), so re-running on footage with fast boundary motion will silently regress to PromptHMR-like behavior. A second trap: the ablation row "(3) w/o stage" reports ATE $=2.75$, worse than "(1) w/o cam" ATE $=1.40$ ([PAGE 12]) — disabling the progressive schedule hurts camera pose more than dropping camera refinement entirely, a counterintuitive result that invites misattribution of the source of camera gains. A third: Table 1 lists Ours W-MPJPE $=279.0$ ([PAGE 12]) while Table 3 lists $278.8$ ([PAGE 12]); small, but a red flag that the two tables use different evaluation subsets, which matters if you try to reproduce exact figures.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reframing the "crisis / habit / counter-trap / clock" motif to the paper's own adversarial structure: the crisis is the shot change (abrupt viewpoint, appearance, and count discontinuity — the three challenges (i)–(iii) on [PAGE 3]). The prior art's habitual regression under crisis is exactly what the paper documents as failure Case 1 and Case 2 (Fig. 2, [PAGE 5]): PromptHMR "incorrectly assigns different identities across the shot change" and places "the same persons' meshes … in the completely different 3D locations." The mechanism of that regression is the enforced temporal-continuity prior — the habit of assuming one continuous camera — which, when the assumption is violated, manufactures "unrealistic motion artifacts such as foot sliding" ([PAGE 3]).

The paper's counter-trap is to refuse the appearance-only shortcut. It models what a robust agent should do: recognize that under an abrupt cut, the reliable invariant is not pixels but shared physical geometry. So it re-bases identity association on 3D proximity (Eq. 7) with appearance/pose demoted to tie-breakers ($\lambda_{\text{app}}=0.2,\lambda_{\text{pos}}=0.35$ vs. $\lambda_{\text{geo}}=1.0$, [PAGE 10]). The ablation confirms the theory-of-mind claim operationally: distance-only already gives 0.00 IDs on EgoBody, and the full cue set is what pushes EgoHumans from 1.66 → 0.97 and Harmony4D from 0.54 → 0.46 ([PAGE 12]). The auxiliary cues matter precisely where crowding makes proximity ambiguous — i.e., where a naive agent's model of "who is where" is most likely wrong.

The "clock" is the temporal propagation machinery. The method's implicit model of time is piecewise: within a shot, DROID-SLAM provides smooth local time; across the boundary, time is stitched by a single static SE(3) transform (Eq. 6) under the "temporal gap … is negligible" assumption ([PAGE 7]). The post-processing smoother (Eq. 10) then re-imposes a first-order temporal-continuity prior on joints — but crucially only after the geometry has been corrected, so the prior now regularizes within a correctly-aligned frame rather than fighting the discontinuity. This ordering (align first, then smooth) is the key recursive lesson: apply the temporal prior after establishing the spatial truth, or the prior will corrupt rather than refine. The failure mode to watch is over-smoothing at the seam if $\lambda_{\text{smooth}}$ is tuned for intra-shot motion and the residual boundary offset is nonzero.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical veto. The method's plausibility guarantees are VPoser regularization ($\|\zeta_t^i\|_2^2$, Eq. 10) plus the shape prior ($\|\beta^i\|_2^2$), which constrain poses toward the learned human manifold, and the cross-camera reprojection (Eq. 11), which vetoes meshes that cannot be consistently seen from both boundary viewpoints. Quantitatively, the strongest physical-plausibility evidence is foot-slide suppression: FS drops from 23.11 (PromptHMR) to 10.7, and Jitter from 162.44 to 31.5 ([PAGE 12, Table 4]). Jitter (acceleration-domain) is the direct biomechanical smoothness probe, and a ~5× reduction is the load-bearing claim that motions are physically credible, not just positionally accurate. Veto condition: if $\lambda_{\text{smooth}}=10.0$ over-constrains, the optimizer can trade genuine fast motion for smoothness, producing biomechanically implausible "slow-motion" artifacts — the Accel metric (27.3 vs. baselines' 48.7–74.3, [PAGE 12]) would look better while true dynamics are suppressed. So Accel alone cannot adjudicate physical plausibility; it must be read jointly with PCK* (90.7, [PAGE 12]), which measures whether the smoothed trajectory still hits the right locations.

"Weather" (visual-degradation) veto. Mapping "weather" to the appearance/lighting axis: the paper's entire premise is that appearance cues degrade under viewpoint and illumination shifts, which is why appearance Re-ID (KPR, Pose2ID) is beaten ([PAGE 12]). The geometry anchor is the weatherproofing. But VGGT itself is not fully weatherproof — the authors concede degradation "in dynamic scenes involving moving people or backgrounds" ([PAGE 7]). Adjudication: the veto is valid intra-scene (static background, negligible boundary motion) and voids inter-scene. The Limitation section makes the physical veto explicit and irrefutable: verbatim, "When a video undergoes an inter-scene transition with no spatial overlap, the concept of a continuous 3D motion becomes physically meaningless" ([PAGE 14]). No amount of smoothing can rescue a trajectory across a cut where the two scenes share zero world coordinates — the shared 3D space is empty by construction. This is a hard physical veto, not a tuning issue.

Scale-layer veto. The uniform scale factor (max centroid-to-vertex ratio, averaged over persons, [PAGE 7]) is the weakest physical link. If it is wrong, $\tau=1.0$ m gating (Eq. 7/8) is applied in the wrong metric, so both false merges (people < 1 m apart in a mis-scaled world) and false splits (same person > 1 m due to under-scaled clouds) occur. The ablation confirms the stakes: "(4) w/o align" is the worst row on every human metric (W-MPJPE 882.7, MPJPE 422.4, [PAGE 12]), so alignment — which begins with scale — is the layer whose failure most severely degrades the output. Any cross-layer veto that passes pose and camera but fails scale is therefore inadmissible.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable, verifiable scenarios where the pipeline's verifiable reward (identity + geometric correctness) should collapse, with concrete predictions:

Veto 1 — Inter-scene cut (no spatial overlap). Prediction: shared-space construction yields two disjoint point clouds with no common anchor; Eq. 7 distances become meaningless (all $\mathbb{D}_{ij}$ large), so the Hungarian match either returns nothing above threshold or a uniformly wrong assignment. Verifiable signal: ATE and IDs diverge sharply; the method's own Limitation admits this regime is "physically meaningless" ([PAGE 14]). Falsifiable: if reported IDs stayed low across a true inter-scene cut, the geometry claim would be void.

Veto 2 — Fast subject motion across the boundary (violating negligible-temporal-gap). Prediction: because VGGT treats $f_{t_b-1},f_{t_b}$ as simultaneous ([PAGE 7]), a person translating by $\Delta$ meters between them is duplicated into the cloud at two locations, inflating per-person point-cloud extent, biasing the scale factor, and corrupting depth correspondences (Eq. 4). Verifiable prediction: depth loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}$ fails to decrease in stage 3, and W-MPJPE (initial-frame alignment) degrades faster than WA-MPJPE (trajectory-aligned) because the world frame itself is wrong. The paper reports only negligible-gap cuts, so this remains untested — a legitimate open counterfactual.

Veto 3 — Two people within $\tau=1.0$ m at the cut. Prediction: the hard distance gate cannot disambiguate; $\mathbb{D}_{ij}$ for the wrong pairing falls below $\tau$, and the auxiliary cues are down-weighted ($\lambda_{\text{app}}=0.2,\lambda_{\text{pos}}=0.35$), so if appearance and pose are also similar (interacting/occluding people — exactly the EgoBody/Harmony4D regime), the Hungarian solution swaps identities. Verifiable signal: IDs increase monotonically as inter-person distance drops below 1 m; the fact that Ours(dist.) already hits 0.00 on EgoBody but the method needed full cues on EgoHumans/Harmony4D ([PAGE 12]) supports that crowding is the discriminating variable.

Each veto is verifiable against the released metrics (IDs, ATE, W-MPJPE vs. WA-MPJPE gap, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{depth}}$ convergence) and each targets a stated assumption (intra-scene, negligible gap, $\tau$-separability). If any of the three failed to degrade performance as predicted, the corresponding assumption — spatial overlap, boundary simultaneity, or metric-scale correctness — would be falsified, which is the point of stating them as vetoes rather than caveats.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
