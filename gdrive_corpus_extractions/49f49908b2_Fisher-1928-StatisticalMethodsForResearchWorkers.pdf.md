<THINK_TRACE>
The logical spine of Fisher's program is a five-stage deductive pipeline: hypothetical infinite population → random sample → statistic → sampling distribution of that statistic → significance test. Every "operator" in the book is an instance of this pipeline, and every "breakdown regime" is a place where one link fails. Let me derive the unification multi-hop.

Start with the normal law [PAGE 64]: $df = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-(x-m)^2/2\sigma^2}dx$. From a sample, the mean $\bar{x}=S(x)/n$ and $s^2=S(x-\bar{x})^2/(n-1)$ [PAGE 66]. The sampling standard error of the mean is $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ [PAGE 70]. When $\sigma$ is *known*, $(\bar{x}-m)/(\sigma/\sqrt n)$ is a normal deviate and Table I applies. When $\sigma$ is *estimated* by $s$, Fisher (following "Student," 1908) shows the ratio $t=\bar{x}\sqrt n/s$ is no longer normal; the departure is governed *solely* by $n$ (degrees of freedom), because $s^2/\sigma^2$ follows $\chi^2/n$ [PAGE 124]. This is the first multi-hop bridge: the $\chi^2$ distribution (variance sampling) and the $t$ distribution (mean sampling with estimated variance) are the *same* object viewed through the ratio of a normal deviate to the root of an independent scaled $\chi^2$.

The second bridge is Pearson's $\chi^2=\sum x^2/m$ [PAGE 95], which Fisher notes is "in reality equivalent to the distribution of the variance as estimated from normal samples" [PAGE 36]. So goodness-of-fit, variance estimation, and the $t$-test are one family. The third bridge is the $z$ distribution: $z=\tfrac12\log(s_1^2/s_2^2)$, the difference of half-log variances, with $\sigma_z=\sqrt{\tfrac12(1/n_1+1/n_2)}$ [PAGE 194]; $t$ and $\chi^2$ are special cases ($n_1=1$, or $n_2\to\infty$). Gray-zone audit: the normal approximation to the mean's distribution holds "except in the exceptional class of distributions for which the distribution of the mean does not tend to normality" [PAGE 99] — a genuine singularity (heavy-tailed/Cauchy-type populations) that Fisher flags but does not treat. A subtler gray zone is the correlation coefficient: its standard error $(1-r^2)/\sqrt{n-1}$ is valid only for "large samples, and moderate or small correlations" [PAGE 158]; near $\rho=\pm1$ and for small $n$ the distribution of $r$ is badly skew, so the naive SE is "without utility" [PAGE 161] and one must transform to $z=\operatorname{artanh}(r)$ with $\sigma_z=1/\sqrt{n-3}$ [PAGE 184]. The deepest structural insight is the ANOVA identity $S(x-\bar x)^2=kS(\bar{x}_p-\bar x)^2+S(x-\bar{x}_p)^2$ [PAGE 210], which makes the intraclass correlation $r$ merely "the fraction of the total variance due to that cause which observations in the same class have in common" — i.e., $kS(\bar x_p-\bar x)^2=n's^2\{1+(k-1)r\}$ [PAGE 200]. Correlation, regression, and variance-partition collapse into a single accounting of sums of squares.
