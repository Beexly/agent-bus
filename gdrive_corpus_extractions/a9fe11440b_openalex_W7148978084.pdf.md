For the ball at ~576 px·s⁻¹, ℓ_b ≈ 9.6 px. The paper never reports T_e; the reported statistical signature is a mean feature-response intensity of 67% of the static case and variance 1.8× [PAGE 6] — which is consistent with an attenuated-but-nonzero OTF (i.e., blur length of a few pixels, not many tens).

*Hop 3 — aggregation algebra.* The paper’s SNR gain statement — static targets benefit by +4.2% from K=1→K=5 aggregation, fast targets lose 2.1% [PAGE 3, PAGE 6] — maps onto C(d) = exp(−d²/(4σ_f²)), the cross-frame feature correlation at inter-frame displacement d. Under zero-mean additive noise and coherent summation, SNR_K = √K · SNR₁ · C(d). Aggregation is net beneficial exactly when C(d) > 1/√K, and for K=5 that is C(d) > 0.447. With σ_f ≈ 2 px (a plausible feature autocorrelation width for ResNet-50 stride-16 features), d = 20 px yields C ≈ 0 — the −2.1% degradation regime is the coherent regime beginning to invert.

*Hop 4 — architectural response.* MAAF telescopes receptive field in proportion to S_f via dilated convs r ∈ {1,2,4} producing effective 3×3, 5×5, 9×9 kernels [PAGE 8, Eq. 6], plus a thresholded deformable-convolution branch for S_f > τ = 0.5 [PAGE 8, Eq. 9]. SGTA injects a state-content compatibility bias into the softmax argument [PAGE 8, Eq. 12], suppresses historical weights in fast regions, and then warps V_k with a learned offset predictor at 1/4 resolution [PAGE 9, Eq. 13–14]. The gating Eq. 16–17 essentially re-weights the residual between current-frame and aggregated features by a logistic function of S̄_f and inter-frame feature norm.

*Hop 5 — verifiable consequence.* Because the offset predictor is a two-layer 3×3 conv [PAGE 11], its displacement range is on the order of ~10 px — strictly less than the ≥20 px that defines a “fast” object [PAGE 5]. So the system cannot fully close the gap on the fastest objects; it closes part of the gap for slow-to-moderate motion and routes the extreme case to the gate. The reported +1.7/+1.6 aggregate is thus upper-bounded by the mechanism’s spatial coverage, and the observed Ball improvement (+3.4 over FoT, [PAGE 11]) is the category where partial compensation is still net positive.

**Gray-zone audit.** Four audit concerns are worth naming.

(a) *Attachment of motion states at inference.* Eq. 22 [PAGE 10] supervises S with pixel-wise cross-entropy against pseudo-labels derived from *inter-frame ground-truth displacement*, but at inference the ground truth is unavailable; S must be inferred from D = |F_t − F_{t−1}|, correlation C(i,j), and Δ(i,j) [PAGE 7, Eq. 2–4]. The pseudo-label transfer error is never directly measured; only the ablation λ_1 = 0 case (77.6% mAP@0.5, [PAGE 16]) is given, which probes a different quantity (supervision presence, not pseudo-label fidelity). A 30% random-flip test costs only 1.1 pp [PAGE 16, Table 8], which is encouraging — but random flips are unstructured noise, and the real pseudo-label error will be *correlated* with blur severity (worst where blur is worst), which the paper does not simulate.

(b) *Metadata inconsistencies.* PAGE 1 lists authors “Lulu Yang, Wenqing Sun & Jinkui Ren”; PAGE 2 lists “Lulu Yang¹, Guoqing Sun², and Jinkui Ren*³.” PAGE 16 attributes the λ_1 = 0 ablation to “removing Lmotion in Eq. 18” — but Eq. 18 is the motion compensation head δ_x, δ_y = M(F_roi)⊙σ(W_m S_f + b_m) [PAGE 9]; Lmotion is Eq. 22. These are manuscript-stage artifacts consistent with the “ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT” / “ARTICLE IN PRESS” watermark on every page and are not individually disqualifying, but they fix the confidence ceiling on any single-number claim.

(c) *Statistical significance of thin deltas.* The dilation-rate ablation reports 80.5% at (1,2,4) versus 79.8% at (1,2,3) and 80.0% at (1,3,5) [PAGE 16, Fig. 7(c)] — a 0.7 pp delta that is well within typical single-seed variance for video detection. The paper does not report error bars, multi-seed means, or confidence intervals for any table. The monotone cascade of Table 4 (72.3 → 76.8 → 79.7 → 80.5, [PAGE 13]) is more robust because it is a strict ablation ladder, but the top-rung +0.8 pp MC contribution is a single-seed figure.

(d) *Baseline/benchmark framing.* The paper’s comparisons to FGFA/TransVOD/MEGA are from reported literature numbers, not re-runs on identical hardware, and the “strongest baseline” framing (FoT at 78.8/58.6 [PAGE 11], FoT at 78.6/58.3 [PAGE 12]) means the reported deltas of 1.7/1.6 and 2.6/1.8 pp are against a small set. Those are genuine percentage-point differences, not percent; the abstract [PAGE 2] writes “mAP@0.5 by 1.7% and 1.6%” which is loose notation but matches the tables.

These four gray zones mean the paper’s claims are directionally credible and internally consistent — I could not find a headline claim contradicted by any table — but the specific marginal numbers carry a variance floor the paper does not quantify.

**E1 — Motion-blur linear-system operator.** For uniform linear translation with velocity v [px·s⁻¹] over exposure T_e [s],

  I_blur(x) = (1/T_e) ∫₀^{T_e} I₀(x − v t) dt ,  x ∈ ℝ² [px].

Optical transfer function: K̂(f) = sinc(π f_∥ |v| T_e), f ∈ ℝ² [px⁻¹]. **Breakdown:** zeros at f_∥ = n/(|v|T_e), n∈ℤ⁺ — information-theoretically null. For a shutter T_e = 1/60 s and the paper’s own fast threshold |v| ≥ 500 px·s⁻¹ [PAGE 5], the first zero is at f = 12 cycles·px⁻¹… wait, the *period* is 60/500 = 0.12 px, so the first null frequency is 1/0.12 ≈ 8.3 cycles·px⁻¹ — far above Nyquist and irrelevant. The relevant attenuation at mid-frequencies f ≈ 0.1 cycles·px⁻¹ is sinc(π·0.1·8.3) = sinc(2.62) ≈ 0.19 — a 5× amplitude attenuation, consistent in order of magnitude with the paper’s “average response intensity only 67% of static objects and feature variance 1.8 times that of static objects” [PAGE 6].

**E2 — Inter-frame displacement and velocity partition.** d = |v| Δt with Δt = 1/FPS [s]. The paper’s partition is verbatim: “static (d < 5 pixels), slow (5 ≤d < 20 pixels), and fast (d ≥20 pixels), accounting for 35.2%, 41.6%, and 23.2% of the dataset, respectively” [PAGE 5]. At the paper’s evaluation frame rate of 25 FPS [PAGE 10], this becomes v < 125 px·s⁻¹ (static), 125 ≤ v < 500 (slow), v ≥ 500 px·s⁻¹ (fast). **Breakdown:** the velocity partition is FPS-conditional; a 60 FPS deployment would reclassify the same physical motion.

**E3 — Cross-frame correlation suppression.** C(d) = exp(−d²/(4σ_f²)). **Breakdown:** C(20 px) ≈ 0 for σ_f = 2 px, meaning that for the fastest tier the coherent-aggregation assumption inverts; that is why the paper’s Fig. 2(b) reports 64.5% → 62.4% for fast targets under 5-frame uniform aggregation [PAGE 5, PAGE 6 — “decreases by 2.1%, from 64.5% to 62.4%”].

**E4 — Motion-state estimator output.** S = Softmax(Conv_{3×3}(ReLU(Conv_{3×3}([D; C; Δ])))) ∈ ℝ^{H×W×3}, [PAGE 7, Eq. 5], where D = |F_t − F_{t−1}| ∈ ℝ^{H×W×C} [PAGE 7, Eq. 2], C(i,j) = max_{(dx,dy)∈N} [F_t(i,j)·F_{t−1}(i+dx,j+dy)] / (‖F_t‖·‖F_{t−1}‖) [PAGE 7, Eq. 3], and Δ(i,j) = √((dx*)² + (dy*)²) [PAGE 7, Eq. 4] in [px]. **Information quantity:** H(S(x)) = −∑_c S_c log S_c [nats·px⁻¹]; maximum at uniform (1/3,1/3,1/3) giving ln 3 ≈ 1.0986 nats, minimum 0. **Breakdown:** if the Softmax collapses to one-hot for every pixel before convergence the bias term in Eq. 12 saturates; if it stays uniform, the entire adaptivity mechanism degenerates to uniform aggregation.

**E5 — Multi-branch dilated feature extraction.** F_k = Conv_{1×1}(σ(BN(DilConv_{3×3}^{r_k}(F_t)))), k ∈ {s,m,f}, r_s = 1, r_m = 2, r_f = 4 [PAGE 8, Eq. 6], giving effective receptive-field diameters 3, 5, 9 [px]. **Breakdown:** as Fig. 7(c) shows the (2,4,6) configuration — effective RF 5, 9, 13 — drops mAP@0.5 to 79.1% [PAGE 16], meaning the boundary of useful RF expansion is between diameter 9 and 13 for the reported data.

**E6 — State-conditioned fused feature.** F′(i,j) = ∑_{k∈{s,m,f}} S_k(i,j)·F_k(i,j) [PAGE 8, Eq. 7]. Conditional on S_f(i,j) > τ = 0.5 [PAGE 11], F″(i,j) = ∑_{n=1}^{N} w_n·F′(p_n + Δp_n) [PAGE 8, Eq. 8] with N = 9 sampling points [PAGE 11]; F̂ = F″ if S_f > τ else F′ [PAGE 8, Eq. 9]. **Breakdown:** a fixed τ = 0.5 is a per-region decision threshold; the authors acknowledge that “the activation threshold τ is set to a fixed value that performs robustly across our evaluation datasets” [PAGE 17] and identify scene-adaptive thresholds as future work.

**E7 — State-modulated temporal attention.** B_k(i,j) = φ(S(i,j))^T · (W_s V_k(i,j)) [PAGE 8, Eq. 11] with φ(S) = [S_s; S_m; S_f] ∈ ℝ³ and W_s ∈ ℝ^{3×C}. A_k(i,j) = exp(Q^T K_k/√d + λB_k) / ∑_l exp(Q^T K_l/√d + λB_l) [PAGE 8, Eq. 12], with d ∈ ℕ (dimensionless) and λ a “learnable temperature parameter” [PAGE 9]. **Breakdown:** λ → 0 recovers vanilla temporal attention (aggregation noise returns); λ → ∞ produces near argmax, killing gradient through the softmax (∂A_k/∂(QK_l) → 0 for A_k → 0). Both singular regimes are physically permitted and not bounded in the paper.

**E8 — Learned offset alignment.** Δp_k = O([F̂_t; F̂_{t−k}; S]) [PAGE 9, Eq. 13], Ṽ_k = Warp(V_k, Δp_k) [PAGE 9, Eq. 14], F_agg(i,j) = ∑_{k=0}^{K} A_k(i,j)·Ṽ_k(i,j) [PAGE 9, Eq. 15]. **Breakdown:** the offset predictor is two 3×3 conv layers [PAGE 11], so its effective displacement search radius is on the order of ~10 px — strictly *less* than the ≥20 px “fast” tier [PAGE 5]. This bounds the achievable compensation at the top of the motion distribution.

**E9 — Adaptive gating.** G = σ(W_g[S̄_f; ‖F̂_t − F̂_{t−1}‖₂] + b_g) [PAGE 9, Eq. 16], F̂_out = G⊙F̂_t + (1−G)⊙F_agg [PAGE 9, Eq. 17]. **Breakdown:** the sigmoid has vanishing gradient for |W_g[…] + b_g| ≳ 4; a gate stuck at G ≈ 1 discards all temporal context, G ≈ 0 discards the current frame; both are absorbing states once loss flattens.

**E10 — Motion compensation head.** (δ_x, δ_y) = M(F_roi)⊙σ(W_m S_f + b_m) [PAGE 9, Eq. 18], with (x+δ_x, y+δ_y) as the corrected center. **Breakdown:** the soft gate σ(W_m S_f + b_m) turns compensation on only for high S_f; if the S_f estimate is overconfident in static regions (S_f ≪ but sigmoid not at 0), bias is injected into static boxes.

**E11 — Focal + GIoU detection loss.** L_cls = −(1/N_pos)∑ α_t(1−p_t)^γ log(p_t) [PAGE 10, Eq. 20] with α_t = 0.25, γ = 2; L_box = λ_L1 L_L1(b, b̂) + λ_GIoU L_GIoU(b, b̂) [PAGE 10, Eq. 21] with λ_L1 = 5, λ_GIoU = 2; L_total = L_cls + L_box + λ_1 L_motion + λ_2 L_comp [PAGE 10, Eq. 19] with λ_1 = 0.5, λ_2 = 1.0 [PAGE 10]. **Breakdown:** as λ_1 → 0 the L_motion term vanishes; the paper expressly tests this and finds mAP@0.5 falls from 80.5% to 77.6% [PAGE 16, Table 8] — the same 77.6 figure coincidentally appears as the no-aggregation baseline in Fig. 6(a) [PAGE 14]; both are consistent with “motion-state prior contributes ~2.9 pp of the total.”

**E12 — Pseudo-label supervision.** L_motion = −(1/HW)∑_{i,j}∑_{c∈{s,m,f}} y_c(i,j) log S_c(i,j) [PAGE 10, Eq. 22], with y_c a one-hot derived from inter-frame GT displacement [PAGE 10]. **Breakdown/inference mismatch:** at test time y_c is unavailable; the estimator must operate on D, C, Δ alone, which is a distributional shift the paper tests only through random-label-flip robustness (0.5 pp at 20% flip, [PAGE 16, Table 8]).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The MoSA-Det design has two distinct lineages that fuse at the 2026 manuscript.

**Lineage I — temporal aggregation.** The scientific ancestor is classical optical flow: Lucas–Kanade (1981) and Horn–Schunck (1981) established brightness-constancy as the workhorse for inter-frame correspondence, and this is exactly the assumption the paper invokes when it says “optical flow estimation relies on the brightness constancy assumption, which is violated in heavily motion-blurred areas” [PAGE 14]. FGFA (Zhu et al., 2017, ref 19) first made flow-guided feature aggregation a video-detection primitive, reporting 6.2% mAP improvement for fast-moving objects on ImageNet VID [PAGE 5]. SELSA (2019, ref 20) removed the explicit-flow requirement via global similarity at 82.69% mAP; MEGA (2020, ref 21) added a long-range memory module; TransVOD (2022, ref 22) and TransVOD++ moved to spatial-temporal transformers reaching 90.0% mAP; QueryProp (2022, ref 23) reduced redundancy through key-frame query propagation; HyMAT (2025, ref 24); Beyond Boxes (2025, ref 25) introduced instance-mask-guided aggregation. All of that lineage implicitly assumes small inter-frame displacement — the assumption the MoSA paper explicitly overturns by conditioning on S.

**Lineage II — deformable operators.** Dai et al. (2017, ref 15) introduced deformable convolution with learned spatial sampling offsets. InternImage (2023, ref 16) generalized to DCNv3 at foundation-model scale; DCNv4 (2024, ref 17) removed softmax normalization and achieved >3× inference speedup [PAGE 4]. The MoSA MAAF branch is a *conditioned* DCN: offsets activate only when S_f > τ, so the lineage fork is “always-warp” (DCNv1–v4) versus “warp-on-demand” (MoSA Eq. 9 [PAGE 8]).

**Lineage III — datasets.** SoccerNet-Tracking (2022, ref 9) brought 200 sequences × 30 s, 1080p @ 25 FPS, 8 categories [PAGE 10]; SportsMOT (ICCV 2023, ref 10) brought 240 sequences, >150k frames, 1.6M boxes, covering basketball/volleyball/soccer at 720p @ 25 FPS [PAGE 10]. The MoSA paper inherits both entirely — all empirical claims are pinned to those two benchmarks’ motion distributions, which is why the velocity partition (static/slow/fast at 5/20 px [PAGE 5]) generalizes only *within* the 25 FPS, sports-broadcast regime.

**Multi-week research traps.** Three are visible retrocausally.

*Trap 1 — “more K is better.”* Pre-2024 video detection fields defaulted K = 3–5. The paper’s own Fig. 6(b) shows this is destructive to the ball: “Ball detection drops from 58.4% to 56.2%” at uniform-3 and “further decreases to 54.8%” at uniform-5 [PAGE 13]. A researcher who chases a single aggregate mAP with K-tuning gets a model that silently degrades the highest-velocity category — a diagnostic trap the paper explicitly unmasks via per-category reporting.

*Trap 2 — “preprocess with deblurring.”* DeblurGAN (2018, ref 13) and Stripformer (2022, ref 14) are performant but the paper flags “the computational cost of deblurring remains prohibitive for real-time detection, and artifacts introduced during deblurring may interfere with the detector’s feature extraction” [PAGE 4]. The two-week detour of building a deblur→detect pipeline ends at a real-time budget failure, which is the trap MoSA’s in-network MAAF sidesteps.

*Trap 3 — “optical flow as teacher.”* Training a temporal aggregator against RAFT/PWC-Net pseudo-flow. The paper’s Table 6 [PAGE 15] shows RAFT flow gives 79.6% mAP@0.5 at 18.3 FPS versus the learned offset predictor’s 80.5% at 30 FPS — a small accuracy loss but a 39% speed hit, driven exactly because flow is dense and uniformly degraded in blurred regions while the learned offsets concentrate on S_f.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis-habit regression.** The default reflex when a video detector underperforms on fast objects is to (a) train longer, (b) scale the backbone, or (c) add more augmentation. All three treat the symptom — the accuracy gap on fast categories [PAGE 5, Fig. 2(a): “85.1% AP on static objects but drops dramatically to 57.8% AP on fast-moving objects, with a performance gap of 27.3 percentage points”] — without addressing the mechanism. The MoSA paper’s *habit regression signal* is its own ablation ordering: MAAF contributes 4.5 pp, SGTA contributes another 2.9 pp, MC adds 0.8 pp [PAGE 13, Table 4]. If a scaled backbone alone could close the fast-object gap, no such monotone module cascade would be observed. The regression is measurably wrong here.

**Counter-trap.** The opposite failure — over-investing in deblurring preprocessing, or in dense optical flow, or in fixed multi-scale feature pyramids — is equally real. Fixed multi-scale conv (small+medium+large kernels applied uniformly) yields 73.8% mAP@0.5 vs. 72.3% for single-scale, but the state-conditioned weighting version reaches 75.5% [PAGE 14, Table 5] — the pure architectural scaling caps out at +1.5 pp, whereas the conditioning layer adds another +1.7 pp. The counter-trap is thus quantitatively identified: “more scales” is worth roughly half of “the right conditioning.”

**Clock pressure.** The binding constraint in sports broadcast is 25–30 FPS [PAGE 12], and the paper repeatedly returns to it: “30 FPS, meeting the 25–30 FPS real-time requirement for sports broadcasting” [PAGE 12, PAGE 16]. This clock vetoes the deblur path outright (deblur models are typically 10–20× slower than detection) and biases the design toward cheap amortized operations — dilated convs instead of explicit large kernels (“increases parameters by 15.1% and reduces inference speed by 15.7%, while achieving slightly lower accuracy (80.1% vs. 80.5% mAP@0.5)” [PAGE 15, Table 7]), and a two-conv offset predictor instead of RAFT. The 30 FPS ceiling is what differentiates MoSA’s design choices from an unconstrained-accuracy design, and it is what makes any proposed fix measurable against a hard wall.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical veto — OTF zeros and pixel scale.** For any motion-blur claim to be operable, the blur length ℓ_b = |v|T_e must be a resolvable fraction of the object’s projected size. Broadcast 1080p mapping ~100 m of pitch width to ~1920 px gives ~19.2 px·m⁻¹. Player sprint at 10 m·s⁻¹ → 192 px·s⁻¹ → 7.7 px·frame at 25 FPS — classified **slow** (5 ≤ d < 20) by the paper’s own partition [PAGE 5], not fast. The ball at 30 m·s⁻¹ → 576 px·s⁻¹ → 23.0 px·frame — classified **fast** (≥20). **Biomechanical veto:** biological human limits place nearly all human motion in the slow bin; the “fast” bin (>500 px·s⁻¹ at 25 FPS) is dominated by (i) the ball, (ii) camera pan/tilt during explosive plays, and (iii) referee/off-camera motion. This constrains the paper’s narrative claims about “sprinting player” benefit [PAGE 2, PAGE 3] — the deformable branch (which requires S_f > τ = 0.5) will fire primarily on the ball and pan residuals, not on the sprinter. The measured Player gain from MAAF is nonetheless +3.8 pp [PAGE 13, Table 4: 74.8 → 78.6], which is consistent with the *slow/mid-branch weighting* doing the work, not the deformable branch.

**Weather/photometric veto.** Two photometric conditions break the motion-state estimator’s assumptions. (i) *Exposure time T_e is not reported in the paper.* If the broadcast camera operates at 1/500–1/1000 s shutter (typical daytime), blur length is 1 px or less for a sprinting player, and the entire motion-blur branch fires into an already-sharp signal, wasting capacity and potentially injecting high-frequency artifacts. If at 1/60–1/100 s (typical evening floodlit), blur reaches 3–10 px and the compensation is in its useful regime. The paper’s 67%-intensity / 1.8×-variance statistic [PAGE 6] implies the evening-shutter regime is what was sampled. (ii) *Rain, snow, and lens droplets* add non-motion blur; the state estimator conflates them with object motion because it has no physical model of blur provenance.

**Architectural veto — receptive field vs. displacement mismatch.** The strongest available offset predictor is 2 conv layers ≈ 10 px search; the fast tier requires ≥20 px [PAGE 5]. The deformable branch uses N=9 sampling points [PAGE 11] configured for ~9×9 effective RF [PAGE 8]. **Veto:** for objects with d ≈ 20–40 px (ball, camera-panned frames), MAAF cannot fully sample the diffuse signature and SGTA cannot fully warp — the achievable compensation is partial by construction. The paper’s internal consistency supports this: the compensation head contributes only +0.8 pp of the +8.2 pp total [PAGE 13, Table 4], the smallest of the three modules, consistent with it hitting the boundary of its displacement range on the fastest residual error.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Scenario V-1 — remove the state-modulated bias (Eq. 11–12).** Hold MAAF, motion compensation, and gating fixed; delete the λB_k term in the attention logits. Prediction: mAP@0.5 falls from 80.5% to ~78.5–79.2% (interpolating between Table 4’s MAAF-only 76.8% and full 80.5% using the paper’s own ~2.9 pp SGTA contribution as the upper reference [PAGE 13]). Falsified if drop < 0.5 pp — that would mean λB_k is functionally inert and the SGTA gain comes entirely from the warp+gate. Verifiable by re-running Table 4 adjacent ablation on SoccerNet-Tracking with the attention-bias term ν = 0.

**Scenario V-2 — 60 FPS retiming.** Re-time SoccerNet-Tracking to 60 FPS (frame-doubling, displacement per frame halves). Prediction: at Δt = 1/60 s, the ball at 576 px·s⁻¹ drops from 23.0 px to 11.5 px per frame — from **fast** to **slow** under the paper’s own partition [PAGE 5]. Under this retiming, the state-conditioning’s advantage should shrink approximately to the slow-branch regime: predicted MoSA-Det vs. FoT gain falls from +1.7 to ≤ +0.8 pp mAP@0.5. Falsified if the gain stays > 1.5 pp — that would indicate the benefit is not primarily motion-state-driven and the paper’s causal story is incomplete.

**Scenario V-3 — structured pseudo-label corruption.** Replace the GT-displacement pseudo-labels [PAGE 10, Eq. 22] with RAFT-flow-derived labels, which are strongly biased in motion-blurred regions per the paper’s own admission (“optical flow estimation relies on the brightness constancy assumption, which is violated in heavily motion-blurred areas, producing noisy vectors” [PAGE 14]). Prediction: unlike the random-flip robustness (0.5 pp at 20% flip, 1.1 pp at 30% flip [PAGE 16, Table 8]), structured corruption will concentrate errors precisely in the high-S_f regions that drive the adaptivity, and mAP@0.5 will fall to ~79.0–79.8% — i.e., 0.7–1.5 pp below the GT-label model, exceeding what random-flip statistics would predict. Falsified if mAP@0.5 stays ≥ 80.3%. Verifiable by a single retraining run with RAFT pseudo-labels under the identical optimizer settings (AdamW, 1e-4, cosine, 150 epochs, batch 16 × 4 GPUs, 1280×720 [PAGE 11]).

Each veto is a single-variable manipulation of a published specification, and each prediction is a numeric interval against a specific published table — enough to falsify the paper’s causal claims about motion-state adaptivity without requiring new datasets or new architectures.
