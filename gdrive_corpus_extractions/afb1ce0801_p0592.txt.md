Third, "consciousness abolished" is an observational report, not a measured neural state; the gray zone is whether subcortical arousal could persist without reportable consciousness. Fourth, the stopping condition "parts were exhausted" is a black box: exhaustion could be metabolic (ATP depletion), electrochemical (ion-gradient run-down), or structural (contractile damage), and James does not specify which. These gray zones bound any formal model: the equations below are minimal closure assumptions, not physiological proofs.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
State variables and units. I_ext(t): stimulus intensity, dimensionless input [0,1]. s(t): activation of sensory cell S, dimensionless [0,1], with s(t) = σ(I_ext(t)) and σ a sigmoid. M(t): motor-cell activation, dimensionless [0,1]. F(t): isometric muscle force, newtons [N], F(t) = α M(t), α [N] force per unit activation. K(t): activation of secondary sensory cell K, dimensionless [0,1]. τ_M, τ_K: membrane time constants, seconds [s]. w_SM, w_KM, w_MK: synaptic weights, dimensionless. E(t): available metabolic energy in the motor pool, joules [J], with Ė = −β F(t) − γ M(t), β [J/N], γ [J/s]. Exhaustion occurs when E(t) ≤ E_crit [J]. I_inh(t): inhibition from secondary/experience-formed paths, dimensionless [0,1].

Governing equations:
τ_M dM/dt = −M(t) + w_SM s(t) + w_KM K(t) − I_inh(t)
τ_K dK/dt = −K(t) + w_MK F(t) = −K(t) + w_MK α M(t)

Breakdown regime. With I_inh = 0 and abundant E, the Jacobian of the (M,K) subsystem has trace T = −(1/τ_M + 1/τ_K) and determinant D = (1 − w_KM w_MK α)/(τ_M τ_K). If w_KM w_MK α ≥ 1, then D ≤ 0 and one eigenvalue is non-negative: marginal stability or runaway until E(t) reaches E_crit. Under approximately constant activation M̄, finite-time exhaustion occurs at t* = E(0)/[(β α + γ) M̄]. This is the singularity: self-maintenance persists only until the metabolic reservoir is drained.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. James's Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, p. 582 [PAGE 582], reduces habit and reflex to a schematic cell assembly and cross-references Vol. I, p. 81, note. The catalepsy application invokes M. Pierre Janet's dissociation framework [PAGE 582], in which consciousness is abolished while motor automatism persists. Charcot's Salpêtrière school supplies the forensic test: holding a strained, unnatural posture is offered as evidence against hypnotic subjects shamming, because voluntary simulation would collapse under fatigue [PAGE 582]. Golgi's neuron doctrine—axis-cylinder processes emerging from the cell base, each cell having one such process forming a nervous network—is explicitly bracketed out by James's footnote [PAGE 582], indicating that James knew the diagram was not a wiring diagram. Modern descendants include spinal reflex arcs, recurrent inhibition (Renshaw cells), central pattern generators, catatonia with waxy flexibility, and functional neurological disorders in which motor output is decoupled from voluntary report.

Multi-week traps. First, in clinical catatonia or neuroleptic malignant syndrome, maintained postures over days to weeks produce pressure injuries, rhabdomyolysis, dehydration, and thrombosis; James's "stop only when the parts were exhausted" [PAGE 582] then becomes a lethal boundary unless medical intervention (nutrition, intravenous fluids, benzodiazepines, electroconvulsive therapy) breaks the loop. Second, in pathological habit formation, experience-formed secondary paths can become compulsive; over weeks the same recurrent architecture supports cognitive rituals rather than muscular movement, with exhaustion shifting from metabolic to psychological. Third is the reification trap: treating Fig. 87 as literal neuroanatomy, which James expressly forbids [PAGE 582], leads to false localization of catalepsy in a single "motor cell" rather than distributed network dynamics.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. The observer's theory of mind, as operationalized by Charcot, infers non-shamming because a simulator under crisis—fatigue, time pressure, or discomfort—would regress to the comfortable, overlearned habit: the limb would drop [PAGE 582]. The cataleptic subject does not regress, implying that motor execution is dissociated from conscious volition and held by the afferent-efferent loop rather than by choice.

Crisis habit regression. In normal subjects, stress or fatigue collapses secondary-path modulation and behavior reverts to prepotent reflexes or rest. Catalepsy blocks this regression: the limb retains the externally impressed position because K excites M continuously, not because the patient actively resists [PAGE 582].

Counter-trap. The examiner may misread waxy flexibility as active resistance or feigned compliance. The counter-trap is that the posture is a passive mechanical fixation sustained by the K→M feedback loop; active resistance would require electromyographic co-contraction that fatigues, whereas passive fixation can be maintained by low-level tonic activation driven by sensory feedback.

Clock. Time-to-exhaustion is the endogenous clock. Under normothermia and hydration, an unnatural isometric posture fails in minutes to hours; catalepsy extends this by suppressing the inhibitory off-switch. If the observed duration exceeds the metabolically predicted t* derived above, the assumption that no secondary-path inhibition or metabolic revision occurs is falsified. The clock therefore adjudicates between the open-loop model and hidden modulatory processes.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto (energy conservation). Maintaining isometric force F [N] over time consumes chemical energy; no physical system sustains arbitrary strain without an energy flux. Any claim that catalepsy can persist indefinitely without metabolic input violates the first law of thermodynamics. Veto condition: E(t) > 0 is required; if E(t) ≤ E_crit, then M(t) = 0. This makes James's stopping condition—"the movement ... would stop only when the parts were exhausted" [PAGE 582]—a physical necessity, not a psychological choice.

Biomechanical veto (joint mechanics). A strained, unnatural position generates gravitational torque τ_g = m g L sin(θ) [N·m] that active muscle force must balance. Passive tissue creep and spindle feedback imply that perfect rigidity is impossible without micro-adjustments. Veto condition: without afferent K input or external support, posture drifts; observation of perfectly fixed limbs over long intervals implies either external support or continuous sensory-motor correction, not a static "motor cell" lock.

Weather veto (thermal context). Ambient temperature T_amb [kelvin] affects muscle contraction kinetics and nerve conduction velocity through a Q₁₀ of approximately 2–3. Higher T_amb accelerates metabolic exhaustion (shorter t*) while lowering viscosity; lower T_amb slows conduction and increases stiffness, potentially prolonging fixation while raising cooling-injury risk. Veto condition: the duration of cataleptic posture cannot be predicted without T_amb and hydration state; any universal time bound is invalid.

Adjudication. These three vetoes do not refute the James–Janet–Charcot observation [PAGE 582]; they circumscribe it. Cataleptic maintenance is a bounded, metastable phenomenon contingent on metabolic reserve, joint mechanics, and thermal context.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each mapped to terms in the governing equations.

Scenario 1: Afferent deafferentation. Block K's input (e.g., local anesthetic to the muscle tendon or dorsal-root afferents) while holding the limb in a strained position. Veto condition: w_MK = 0. Prediction: the K→M loop opens; without external support the limb collapses and cannot maintain cataleptic rigidity; tonic motor output falls to baseline. Verifiable measurement: electromyography shows no sustained motor-unit firing, and range of motion drops to passive zero within seconds.

Scenario 2: Secondary-path reinstatement. Introduce strong inhibition (voluntary counter-effort in a non-catatonic subject, or a GABAergic agonist) while the loop is active. Veto condition: I_inh(t) > w_SM s(t) + w_KM K(t). Prediction: loop gain falls below one; M(t) decays exponentially with time constant τ_M; force F(t) → 0 and the position releases. Verifiable measurement: a force transducer records F(t) collapsing with the predicted time constant.

Scenario 3: Metabolic clamp. Sustain E(t) well above E_crit (e.g., isolated muscle perfusion or glucose/ATP support) while preventing voluntary inhibition. Veto condition: E(t) ≫ E_crit for a duration T ≫ t*_normal. Prediction: the self-maintaining contraction persists beyond the normal exhaustion time, confirming that stopping is resource-depletion limited rather than a central decision. Verifiable measurement: time-to-force-failure is extended by a factor of two or more relative to an unclamped control.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
