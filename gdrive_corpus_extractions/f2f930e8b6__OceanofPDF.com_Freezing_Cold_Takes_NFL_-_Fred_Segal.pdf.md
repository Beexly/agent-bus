<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

I state only well-defined operators with units and explicit breakdown regimes; the book supplies **no** quantitative model, so these are analytic lenses, not fitted results.

**1. Proper scoring (Brier).** For a probabilistic forecast $f_t\in[0,1]$ and realized outcome $o_t\in\{0,1\}$,
$$\mathrm{BS}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}(f_t-o_t)^2,\qquad \mathrm{BS}\in[0,1]\ \text{(dimensionless)}.$$
Murphy's decomposition $\mathrm{BS}=\overline{\text{reliability}}-\overline{\text{resolution}}+\overline{\text{uncertainty}}$ shows the media's failure mode: their takes are pushed to $f\approx0/1$ (high resolution *if right*, catastrophic reliability *if wrong*). Breakdown regime: for a deterministic call, $\mathrm{BS}\in\{0,1\}$; a single wrong "done forever" (O'Connor on Belichick, "should be done as a head coach in the NFL, now and forever," PAGE 19) yields $\mathrm{BS}\approx1$. The Bayes-minimizing forecast equals the base rate, not the salient sample.

**2. Surprisal.** $I(o)=-\log_2 p(o)$ bits. Improbable results carry high information; the book's "biggest upsets in NFL history" (PAGE 13, PAGE 89) are high-surprisal events that, once realized, force maximal belief updating.

**3. Small-sample standard error.** For a completion proportion $\hat p$ over $n$ attempts, $\mathrm{SE}(\hat p)=\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$. Scott Mitchell: $44/62=0.710$, so $\mathrm{SE}=\sqrt{0.710\cdot0.290/62}\approx0.058$ ($\approx5.8$ pp); a 95% interval is roughly $60\%\text{–}82\%$. The realized 119.2 rating (PAGE 28) therefore cannot separate "elite" from "average." Breakdown: $\mathrm{SE}\sim n^{-1/2}$ diverges as $n\to0$; at $n\approx60$ the preseason estimator is nearly uninformative about true skill. This is the formal core of Chapter 5 ("It was just preseason, I should have known better," PAGE 74).

**4. Regression to the mean.** Model $X_i=\mu+\varepsilon_i$, $\varepsilon_i\sim(0,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d. Then $E[X_2\mid X_1=x]=\mu$: extreme first measurements revert. If serial correlation $\rho<1$ (AR(1)), $E[X_2\mid X_1=x]=\mu+\rho(x-\mu)$ — shrinkage. Breakdown: no regression iff $\rho\to1$ (pure persistent skill). Preseason traps require $\rho\ll1$, i.e., performance dominated by opponent quality, garbage time, and noise.

**5. Bayes / base-rate neglect.** $P(H\mid E)=\dfrac{P(E\mid H)P(H)}{P(E)}$. The systematic error is inflating $P(E\mid H)$ for a flattering small sample while setting the prior $P(H)$ ("this 2.5-game backup is a franchise QB") far too high relative to the true base rate of such conversions.

**6. Peter Principle (named in-text, PAGE 21).** Let competence in role $k$ be $C_k$; promote iff $C_k>\tau$. If $\{C_k\}$ are effectively independent across levels, promotion from $k$ carries zero information about $C_{k+1}$, guaranteeing nonzero stationary mass at "the level of incompetence" — Pompei's exact charge against Belichick.

**7. Signal detection under hidden state (Mandarich).** Hidden $S\in\{\text{clean},\text{using}\}$; observed physique/acne/weight as signal. Detection probability $\Pr(\text{detect}\mid\text{using})\to0$ as concealment improves and test sensitivity $\to0$; then posterior $\approx$ prior regardless of the signal. This formalizes "we only catch the dumb users" (NCAA's Schultz, PAGE 126) and the Packers' hollow due diligence (PAGE 127).
