Second, the annotation methodology is itself a trap. Figure 3 ([PAGE 4]) shows the "diameter approach" systematically underestimates true diameter, while "center+projection" achieves "<1 pixel" ([PAGE 4]); a team naively labeling diameters will bake a bias into ground truth and never converge to the paper's 1.6 px. Third, the training/eval split is arena-disjoint ([PAGE 4] "each fold contains images from basketball arenas exclusive to that fold"), so leakage-free reproduction requires respecting fold A as unseen; violating this inflates metrics. Fourth, APIDIS is a known poisoned-well: unsynchronized streams cause "dozen of pixels differences" ([PAGE 3]) and the dataset "does not provide any information about ball visibility." A reproducer who benchmarks on APIDIS without these caveats will misjudge their own model.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Framed for this vision task, the "theory of mind" is the two-stage detector↔CNN interaction. The detector BallSeg has a belief over ball locations (Ψ); the CNN holds a second-order belief about whether each proposed patch is truly a ball, formalized by M^c_θ. The paper's own reasoning is explicit: "when k > 1, it is likely that most candidates are false positives" ([PAGE 2]) — the CNN is designed to model the detector's error distribution and override its top-1 choice via Eq. 10.

Crisis-habit regression: under occlusion or poor contrast (the "crisis" conditions the paper targets), BallSeg's confidence collapses and its top-1 is unreliable. The CNN's counter-move is to re-rank the top-k, which the paper shows "recovers balls missed by BallSeg-top1 but who ranked in the topk with k ≥ 2" ([PAGE 6]). This is a learned adversarial correction to the detector's habitual failure mode.

Counter-trap: the trap would be trusting the detector's most-confident candidate. The CNN avoids it by decoupling detection (stage 1) from verification (stage 2) and by training with an explicit ball/not-ball target that masks L_d on negatives (Eq. 3). The result is a strict improvement in TP-rate at low FP-rate (Figure 5, [PAGE 6]).

The clock: the paper deliberately removes the temporal dimension — single image, no time consistency, no filtering. It defers temporal regularization: "our image-based approach provides raw estimations that could be improved based on temporal filtering when needed" ([PAGE 8]). So the recursive loop here is memoryless by design; the model reasons per-frame with no belief propagation across time. That is the paper's principal self-imposed limitation and the obvious extension axis.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto. Eq. 6 requires b_y ± d/2 to land on genuine ball edges. This fails under (i) occlusion — a defender covering the vertical diameter biases both rays; (ii) partial visibility — the paper markets tolerance to partly visible balls ([PAGE 1]) but the mechanism only holds if the vertical extent is intact; (iii) motion blur — indoor lighting produces blur ([PAGE 3]), smearing edges and inflating d̂. Proof of veto: if the true edge y-position is perturbed by ε px, then e_cy⁺ − e_cy⁻ shifts by ~ε, and from ΔZ/Z ≈ Δd/d the depth error grows without bound as the true diameter shrinks — there is no bounded-error regime once edges are corrupted on small balls.

Biomechanical/annotator layer veto. Ground truth depends on a human clicking center then ground projection (Figure 1, [PAGE 3]). The "projection approach" reaches "<1 pixel" ([PAGE 4]) but the "diameter approach" has a systematic under-estimation bias (Figure 3, [PAGE 4]). Veto: any pipeline that trains on diameter-annotated truth inherits a directional bias, not zero-mean noise, so averaging cannot remove it.

Weather/lighting layer veto. APIDIS is captured under "a rather weak lighting setup, resulting in poorly contrasted images" ([PAGE 3]). The authors attribute its distribution shift to this: "We conjecture that the distribution shift observed on the APIDIS dataset is caused by the poorly contrasted images" ([PAGE 7]). Veto proof: low contrast reduces the effective gradient SNR at the ball boundary, which is exactly the signal a diameter regressor consumes; below a contrast threshold the diameter estimate is dominated by noise and Eq. 7 output is meaningless. This is corroborated by APIDIS having the worst MAE in Figures 4 and 8.

Net adjudication: the method is valid in a bounded envelope — high resolution, frontal or mildly occluded balls, adequate contrast, accurate calibration. Outside it, at least one layer vetoes the metric claim.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto 1 — small/far ball depth blow-up. Counterfactual: force d below ~7 px (or place the ball far). Prediction: MAE[px] stays roughly constant (~1–2 px) but MAE[m] and MAE[%] diverge as d⁻¹ and d⁻² respectively (my ΔZ/Z ≈ Δd/d identity). Verifiable against Figure 6 ([PAGE 7]), where APIDIS balls "<7 pixels were ignored," and against the authors restricting training to 14–37 px. Falsification test: synthetically shrink DeepSport test balls to 5 px and measure MAE[m]; the theory predicts a super-linear rise.

Veto 2 — occlusion of the vertical diameter. Counterfactual: occlude the top or bottom edge while keeping the center visible. Prediction: e_cy⁺ − e_cy⁻ is corrupted, so Eq. 7 yields a large, biased depth error even though the CNN's classification output may still fire. Verifiable via a controlled occlusion sweep — which the paper does not run; its robustness claim ([PAGE 6]) rests only on matching aggregate MAE across DeepSport and the clean set, a proxy that cannot exonerate the mechanism. Prediction: MAE[m] grows monotonically with occluded fraction of the vertical extent.

Veto 3 — training-set-size dependence of the baseline. Counterfactual: remove the extended DeepSport data ([PAGE 4], NDA set). Prediction: HCT baseline stays near ~5 px, whereas with the larger set it drops "more than 50% of the mean absolute diameter error on all datasets" (Figure 8, [PAGE 7]) because "ball is much better segmented." Verifiable directly by comparing Figure 4 (dimmed) vs Figure 8. This veto isolates the claim that CNN superiority over HCT is partly a data-efficiency artifact: with abundant data the classical baseline nearly closes the gap (down to "one pixel on our evaluation set," [PAGE 7]).

All three vetoes are testable with the released code and datasets and would either confirm the bounded-validity envelope or falsify a specific published claim.
