If the crisis imposes a deadline shorter than this sum—for example, a withdrawal required within 50 ms of threat onset—the three-node chain is too slow and is vetoed by the clock. The organism then regresses to the direct reflex. The clock therefore adjudicates between mediated control (available when time permits) and reflexive regression (imposed when time collapses).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The efferent limb of $R_L$ travels through the facial nerve (CN VII) to the orbicularis oculi; the afferent limb of the hammer tap uses trigeminal pathways. $R_F$ is executed via spinal reflex circuits (C6–T1) and corticospinal modulation. A veto at this layer—facial nerve palsy, cervical spinal block, or peripheral neuropathy—sets $P(R_L\mid S_L)=0$ or $P(R_F\mid S_P)=0$, collapsing the mediated product to zero even when perception and motivation are intact.

Biomechanical layer: Lid closure occurs in roughly 30–50 ms; proprioceptive reafference adds conduction time; finger flexor activation requires additional milliseconds. The complete mediated chain likely exceeds 100 ms. If the behavioral deadline imposed by the environment is shorter than this biomechanical minimum, the chain cannot finish before the opportunity window closes. The veto is mechanical, not motivational: the subject “wants” to respond but the anatomy cannot deliver $R_F$ in time after $S_L$.

Weather/environmental layer: High background illumination can reduce the contrast and salience of $S_L$, raising the threshold for $P(R_L\mid S_L)$. Cold ambient temperature lowers nerve conduction velocity, lengthening $\Delta t_{LP}$ and potentially pushing it beyond $\tau_{\text{assoc}}$. Humidity and barometric pressure have no direct pathway in this excerpt and are therefore null vetoes.

Adjudication rule: The mediated response occurs only if the logical conjunction of layer gates is true:
\[
G = G_{\text{physical}} \land G_{\text{biomechanical}} \land G_{\text{environmental}} = 1
\]
If any gate is false, the prediction “light evokes finger retraction” is vetoed. The source text implies all gates were open in the Shipley–Lumsdaine setup, because photographic records show lid closure accompanying finger retraction [PAGE 192].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
