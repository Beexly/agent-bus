Theory-of-mind recursion in the 1914 tactical game:

Level 0 (object habit): Close formations and line-plunging. Under pressure or confusion, teams regress to this crisis habit because it is the historically familiar, low-cognitive-load mode.

Level 1 (Stagg's counter): Open football via forward pass and kick. Stagg models the defense as expecting line-plunging and selects pass/kick actions that punish that expectation, compressing the field before the line smash [PAGE 1].

Level 2 (opponent best response): Rivals adopt open football to "meet them at their own game" [PAGE 1]. The opponent now models Stagg as an open-play user and shifts defense accordingly. Both sides operate in the same expanded action alphabet, raising Shannon entropy of play-calling for both.

Level 3 (meta-instability): "The present style is more hazardous and ... mistaken play may cause an entire reversal of the possibilities of the game being won or lost" [PAGE 1]. At Level 3, second-order reasoning collapses into execution noise: a bad pass, a missed tackle, or a mis-shifted defense produces non-linear outcome swings. The stronger team can be put "completely on the defensive" by one unexpected pass or kick [PAGE 1].

Clock layering. Two clocks interact: the within-game clock and the weekly preparation clock. Crisis habit regression operates on the short clock—late in a close game, coaches and players may revert to line-plunging because it feels safer under pressure. The counter-trap operates on the long clock: if a team spends the week training to defend the pass but the opponent surprises with line-plunging, the defensive preparation is inverted. The text's emphasis on "variety of attack" [PAGE 1] implies this inversion is available, though not executed in the narrative.

Regression prediction. Veteran-heavy rosters [PAGE 1] sustain Level 2/3 reasoning longer and resist Level 0 regression. Inexperienced or fatigued teams regress to close formations under stress, forfeiting the open-play variance advantage and becoming vulnerable to the "entire reversal" mechanism.
