<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogy of this scientific episode traces back to the early 20th-century mechanistic philosophy rooted in Comtean positivism and Pavlovian physiology. Edward Tolman, trained under William James and John Watson, inherited a dual mandate: maintain behaviorism's empirical rigor (observables only) while accommodating the obvious *purposefulness* of animal behavior—a phenomenon pure mechanism could not explain without invoking covert mentalism.

His solution was **intervening variables**, which retrocausally shaped how we define the subject matter of psychology. Before Tolman, "learning" was often defined operationally as the gradual strengthening of S-R bonds. Tolman redefined it as the formation of cognitive maps, thereby making *anticipation* a central, measurable entity. This created a generative lineage: his maze experiments directly inspired the discovery of **place cells** by John O'Keefe in the 1970s, providing a neurobiological substrate for the cognitive map. Thus, Tolman's 1932 theoretical construct retroactively determined the kind of neural code scientists would search for decades later.

The **"multi-week trap"** identified in the source [PAGE 1] is the **Simplicity Prior Trap**. Mid-century experimentalists, influenced by logical positivism, equated theoretical virtue with parsimony of entities. Molecular theories (like Hull's) postulated fewer unobservables. This trap persisted for ~25 years because:
1.  **Experimental Convenience**: Measuring discrete S-R strengths (reaction time, error counts) was technically easier than probing representational content.
2.  **Philosophical Dogma**: Radical behaviorism held that referencing internal states was metaphysical speculation.
3.  **Slow Technological Change**: Computers capable of simulating map-based planning did not exist until the 1950s, delaying the appreciation of the computational advantages of internal models.

The trap was broken when **information theory** (Shannon, 1948) and **cybernetics** (Wiener, 1948) provided a new ontology: *signals, codes, and feedback*. In this framework, an internal model was not a mysterious "mind" but a statistically efficient compressor. Decision theory further showed that rational choice requires representing contingencies beyond the present moment. These external fields supplied the mathematical language that validated Tolman's intuitions, demonstrating that his "molar" entities were not heuristic fluff but **necessary components of any system optimizing long-term reward under uncertainty**.
