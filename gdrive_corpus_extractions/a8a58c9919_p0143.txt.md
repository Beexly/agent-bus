The lineage of these equations stretches back to Ernst Heinrich Weber and Gustav Fechner’s psychophysical laws (1830s-1860s), which posited that perception grows logarithmically with physical stimulus intensity. Hull’s 1943 work retroactively validates this by showing that *motor output* (salivation, running speed) obeys the same scaling as *perception*, implying a unified central processing bottleneck for incentive motivation.
Historically, these log-curves were used in industrial psychology (Hawthorne studies era) to optimize worker incentives—a trap where managers assumed increasing pay would indefinitely increase productivity, ignoring the logarithmic saturation demonstrated by Fletcher’s chimpanzee data [REF 1]. A multi-week trap emerges in operant conditioning schedules: if an experimenter relies solely on these 1943 static curves to predict behavior under variable-ratio schedules, they will fail because the "spontaneous activity" baseline ($I_0$, $E_{spont}$) is highly plastic. Over weeks, extinction procedures alter the intercept, turning a high-drive animal into one behaving as if $I_0 \approx 0$. The genealogy thus points toward Rescorla-Wagner models (1972), which introduced associative strength limits to explain why these early log-models eventually failed to predict blocking and overshadowing effects.

If the environmental temperature drops significantly, the viscosity of synovial fluid in the chicken's joints increases, and muscle contraction velocity decreases due to slowed enzymatic kinetics. This physical layer veto adds a massive constant delay to $t$, rendering the logarithmic savings from increased rice grains negligible.
Furthermore, humidity acts as a veto on Gantt's salivary measurements. Saliva is primarily water. In extremely dry air, evaporation rates exceed glandular secretion rates. The measured $S$ (amount of secretion) would plummet regardless of the calculated log-value based on $w$, because the fluid is lost to the atmosphere before collection. 
A proof of this veto mechanism: If $w$ is doubled (theoretically increasing $S$ by $25 \times \log_{10}(2) \approx 7.5$ units), but ambient humidity is reduced from 50% to 10%, the actual collected saliva might decrease by 50% due to evaporative loss.
