RLVR veto condition: despite tissue-distention state sufficient to activate nociceptors in the intact animal [PAGE 1], the model predicts \(\nu = 0\) and absence of aversive behavior (e.g., no vocalization, escape, or guarding). Verification: pressure distention via barostat with concurrent behavioral scoring; blockade confirmed by loss of afferent firing; behavior resumes after washout. Counterfactual: without blockade, identical \(P,\tau\) yields \(\nu > 0\) and aversive behavior. Falsification: if aversive behavior persists under complete nociceptor blockade, then either non-nociceptive afferents carry the injury signal or the “persistent distention” description on [PAGE 1] omits a necessary parallel pathway.
