The behavior cycle \( B \) consists of a sequence of micro-actions \( A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n \) with durations \( t_i \), forming short cycles repeated until need termination or external interruption.

The frequency distribution of actions \( f(A) \) follows a multinomial distribution where:
\[
f(A_i) = \frac{N(A_i)}{\sum_j N(A_j)} 
\]
and \( N(A_i) \) is the count of occurrence of action \( A_i \).

The modal action \( A_{mode} \) satisfies:
\[
A_{mode} = \arg\max_A f(A) 
\]
and is characterized by highest probability, earliest onset, and greatest remedial effect under typical conditions.

The success probability \( S \) of a behavior cycle terminating the need is modeled as:
\[
S(B) = P(\text{Need terminated} | B) = \int_{\mathcal{E}} p(\mathcal{E}) \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{Need resolved in } \mathcal{E}] d\mathcal{E}
\]
where \( \mathcal{E} \) denotes environmental states (defense alignment, weather).

When environment shifts (\( \mathcal{E}' \neq \mathcal{E} \)), the system samples alternative cycles with adjusted probabilities:
\[
P(B'|\mathcal{E}',D) \propto P(B'|D) \cdot \exp(-\beta \Delta C)
\]
where \( \Delta C \) is the cost differential between cycles and \( \beta \) reflects urgency.
