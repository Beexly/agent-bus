Level 2 (other model): The pipeline implicitly assumes players remain within or near \mathcal{R}_k between capture and processing; it does not model the player as an agent who may deliberately exit the ROI. Level 3 (crisis regression): Under stress—motion blur, occlusion, or rapid player acceleration—the system regresses to a crisis habit: the simplest deterministic selection, typically a fixed rectangular box with a predetermined margin, because outline-following and adaptive resizing are computationally expensive. Level 4 (counter-trap): An opposing dynamic element exploits this regression by moving faster than the box can adapt or by positioning at the border so that clamping truncates the target, forcing the system into full-frame fallback exactly when latency is most critical. Level 5 (clock): The hard real-time clock of S3006 imposes a ceiling on total processing. If the recursive reasoning (adaptive sizing + margin + storage lookup + comparison) exceeds the frame budget, the clock veto forces a downgrade to fixed-box full-frame processing, trading the promised efficiency for determinism. The counter-trap is therefore not merely geometric but temporal: the clock converts a selective advantage into a liability precisely when the crisis habit is engaged.

If \sigma_{\text{PSF}} exceeds the smallest feature scale of o_k, localization uncertainty \sigma_k grows such that \epsilon_k < \delta_{\text{loc}}. Over one frame interval \Delta t, displacement is \Delta x = v_{\text{max}}\Delta t. If \Delta x > \epsilon_k, the player leaves \mathcal{R}_k between detection and crop generation. Veto: unless predictive extrapolation \ell_k(t+1) = \ell_k(t) + \mathbf{v}_k\Delta t is applied and verified, reject the ROI. Weather layer veto. Rain, fog, or glare reduce scene contrast C. Veto: disable object-vs-complement selection and default to full-image processing. Adjudication hierarchy. Physical veto outranks biomechanical veto outranks weather veto: an optically blurred image cannot be rescued by motion models or contrast enhancement. If any layer triggers, the system abandons the selected portion and uses \mathcal{I} rather than \mathcal{I}', sacrificing the |\mathcal{I}'| < |\mathcal{I}| benefit for guaranteed object completeness.
