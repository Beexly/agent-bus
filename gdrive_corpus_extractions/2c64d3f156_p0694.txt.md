This aligns with modern quantitative genetics, where the heritability ($h^2$) of complex behaviors is often low, and environmental variance ($V_E$) dominates. James's skepticism regarding the "physical refinement" of aristocratic families anticipates modern critiques of eugenicist assumptions, recognizing that observed phenotypic traits may reflect shared environment rather than additive genetic effects. The case of greyhounds in Mexico further illustrates his commitment to proximate physiological mechanisms (thoracic adaptation) over ultimate genetic ones.

His insistence on "discriminating treatment" implies a demand for rigorous control of confounding variables, anticipating the need for twin and adoption studies to separate $V_G$ from $V_E$.

Crucially, James identifies a systematic cognitive trap: the "hasty conclusion" driven by temporal co-occurrence. When a parent’s deterioration (acquired state) coincides with offspring pathology, the intuitive causal arrow points toward inheritance. However, this ignores the possibility that the same environmental stressors affect both generations, or that the parent’s acquired condition alters prenatal/early-life environments (maternal effects), creating a spurious correlation. This recursive error is foundational to the persistence of Lamarckian intuitions despite overwhelming evidence for Mendelian/epigenetic complexity.

Furthermore, the text implicitly defines a boundary regime for Lamarckian claims: only traits that are universally expressed across diverse environments and resist explanation via selection or learning warrant serious consideration. Thus, James establishes a decision-theoretic threshold for accepting soft inheritance, one that remains relevant in evaluating contemporary epigenetic findings.

\[
P(I | D) = \frac{P(D | I) P(I)}{P(D | I) P(I) + P(D | \neg I) P(\neg I)}
\]
where \(P(I)\) is the prior belief in Lamarckian inheritance. The source text argues \(P(I)\) should be low due to lack of corroborating evidence [PAGE 684].

**Operator 2: Selection Bias Correction**
The observed frequency of a wary phenotype after predation pressure (\(f_w'\)) is not equal to the true inherited frequency (\(f_w\)). It is corrected by the survival differential:
\[
f_w' = \frac{f_w S_w}{f_w S_w + f_b S_b}
\]
where \(S_w\) and \(S_b\) are survival rates of wary and bold types, respectively. James notes "the bolder families have been killed off," implying \(S_b \ll S_w\), thus inflating \(f_w'\) independently of inheritance [PAGE 684].

**Operator 3: Educational Transmission Model**
Cultural learning can mimic inheritance. Let \(T_{gen}\) be the trait prevalence in generation \(t\), influenced by parental demonstration (\(E\)):
\[
T_{gen+1} = \alpha T_{gen} + \beta E + \epsilon
\]
where \(\alpha\) represents genetic contribution, \(\beta\) represents social learning efficiency, and \(\epsilon\) is noise. James proposes "new educational tradition" as a sufficient explanation, setting \(\alpha \approx 0\) for certain traits [PAGE 684].

- **Singularity 1**: If \(S_b = 0\) (complete elimination of bold types), \(f_w' = 1\) regardless of \(f_w\), creating an apparent 100% inheritance rate—a breakdown of inference.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
William James’s skeptical stance emerges from a historical triangulation: (1) Darwin’s *Expression of the Emotions* (1872) provided cross-species examples of expressive habits, which James interprets as spontaneous variations rather than inherited modifications; (2) Romanes’s *Mental Evolution in Animals* (1883) championed Lamarckian continuity, prompting James to seek "corroboration"; (3) Galton’s statistical work on heredity (1889) introduced quantitative rigor that James adopts implicitly. The "Yankee type" reference reflects contemporary debates about American exceptionalism being genetically fixed versus environmentally induced.

A multi-week trap arises when interpreting "accumulated" neuroses: observers see worsening parental health and offspring decline, concluding genetic load increases. Historically, this fueled eugenic policies. James warns against this "hasty conclusion," identifying a retrocausal illusion where present observation (parental sickness) distorts attribution of past causality (offspring constitution). The trap persists today in misinterpreting transgenerational trauma without controlling for shared adversity.

The genealogical insight is that James positioned psychology as a filter between biology and culture, refusing to let either domain monopolize explanatory power. He recognized that what appears as inherited refinement might merely be sustained cultural capital—a dynamic still unresolved in behavioral genetics.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
James demonstrates a second-order theory of mind by modeling how naive observers commit specific errors. The crisis habit regression occurs when faced with correlated generational suffering (e.g., "irregular habits of life" leading to neurosis): the observer defaults to a linear inheritance narrative because it simplifies complexity and assigns blame. This regression is reinforced by emotional salience—seeing a sickly child evokes a search for familial cause.

The counter-trap James employs is deliberate parsimony reversal: instead of seeking the most dramatic explanation (inheritance of acquired weakness), he actively constructs mundane alternatives (environmental exposure, poor nutrition, learned helplessness). This requires overriding the intuitive "family curse" schema.

His method operates on a clock of evidentiary sufficiency: each claim (e.g., "better breathing of greyhounds") is subjected to a waiting period during which simpler mechanisms (post-natal thoracic adaptation) are exhaustively tested before invoking soft inheritance. The "spontaneous variation" hypothesis for unique examples like the begging pup functions as a temporal placeholder—it delays final causal assignment until recurrence patterns emerge. This prevents premature closure on inheritance narratives, embodying a meta-cognitive safeguard against confirmation bias.

James’s arguments function as veto mechanisms across explanatory layers:

**Biomechanical Veto**: For greyhounds in Mexico, he rejects inherited respiratory enhancement in favor of direct mechanical adaptation—"post-natal adaptation of the pups' thorax to the rarer air." This bypasses genetic explanation entirely, attributing change to structural plasticity within a single lifetime. The veto proof is developmental: if pups raised at sea level later moved to altitude show the same improvement, inheritance is falsified.

**Environmental/Climatic Veto**: The "physical refinement" of European descendants in America could result from improved diet, reduced pathogen load, or different lifestyle—not accumulated nervousness. James demands controls for these variables before asserting generational neurological change.

**Behavioral Ecology Veto**: Wariness in island birds may stem from heightened predator density or novel hunting methods, selecting against boldness rather than educating caution. Without tracking family lines, one cannot distinguish selection from learning.

These vetoes establish a hierarchy: physiological adaptation > cultural transmission > selective mortality > genetic inheritance. Only when lower-level explanations are exhausted does inheritance gain credibility. This preemptively blocks conflations between phenotype and genotype.

The adjudication logic is proof-by-elimination: each proposed inherited trait must survive sequential challenges from more immediate causal domains. Failure at any layer invalidates the Lamarckian claim.
