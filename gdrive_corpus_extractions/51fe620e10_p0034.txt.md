<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
To formalize Knight's conceptual boundary, we define the economic state of a competitive agent (e.g., a player or franchise) over a discrete temporal interval $t$. Let $V_t$ represent the perceived market value (or competitive equity) and $C_t$ represent the resource expenditure (training load, roster salary).

In a state of perfect, theoretical equilibrium, the relationship is deterministic:
$$ V_t = C_t $$

However, as Knight notes, this equality is "only by an occasional accident" [PAGE 1]. We introduce the profit/loss margin $\Pi_t$, resulting in the operational equation:
$$ \Pi_t = V_t - C_t $$

Knight bifurcates the underlying stochastic processes driving these variables. For a domain of **Risk ($R$)**, outcomes are governed by a known probability distribution function $P(x)$, allowing for the calculation of an expected value $E[X]$ and a finite variance $\sigma^2$. The "cost" of this risk can be hedged or insured, meaning its impact on $\Pi_t$ is bounded and predictable.

Conversely, for a domain of **Uncertainty ($U$)**, as Knight states it must be taken in a "sense radically distinct" [PAGE 1], there exists no valid $P(x)$. The event space is non-ergodic and potentially singular. We can formally express the breakdown regime of risk-based modeling as:
$$ \lim_{\text{complexity} \to \infty} \sigma^2_R \to U_{unknowable} $$

In this singularity, traditional actuarial adjustments fail. The "bearers" of $U_{unknowable}$ (the entrepreneurs, the clutch performers) operate outside standard deviation. Their success is not a function of optimizing $E[X]$ under constraints, but of creating new value $V_t$ through acts of judgment that cannot be reduced to equations. Thus, $\Pi_t$ in the face of Knightian Uncertainty is inherently binary and discontinuous—it represents either a massive capture of premium value or total ruin, with no reliable statistical interpolation between the two states.
