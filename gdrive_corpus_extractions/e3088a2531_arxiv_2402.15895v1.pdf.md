<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Perceptual-physical veto proof. The method is "pure appearance-based" [PAGE 5]. Define inter-instance appearance distance Δ_app = E‖f(O_a) − f(O_b)‖ over the fused CSC-token. The compositional level adds discriminative power only when part statistics differ; the flamingo case works because "by focusing on the distinguishable parts... such as the shape of the wing red mark, we can easily spot the individual" [PAGE 1]. Adversarial regime: DanceTrack's "similar appearances" and "uniform appearance" [ref 34, PAGE 4]. When costumes are near-identical, Δ_app → 0 for the compositional/semantic channels; the compositional 2×2 bins become statistically indistinguishable, and the contextual negative term (Eq 4) is the only surviving discriminator — but it too collapses when targets overlap heavily (F^bg_U loses contrast). Formally, identity resolution requires the association entropy H(M^t) to be minimizable below the log-cardinality of confusable neighbors; when Δ_app → 0 and motion is disowned (appearance-only), no fusion order can lower H(M^t). That is a hard information barrier, not an architectural deficiency. The second veto is optical: full occlusion yields zero foreground pixels, so f(O) is undefined and the tracker must fall back on trajectory priors — which CSC-Tracker caps at H=8, producing exactly the MOT20 shortfall the authors concede [PAGE 4]. The robustness result (Table IX: CSC-Tracker ↓1.1 HOTA under injected shift/resize noise versus GTR ↓2.2 and OC-SORT ↓2.6) proves the compositional level defends against box jitter, but it cannot defend against appearance collapse or total occlusion. Verdict: no cross-layer veto overturns the claimed gains inside their tested regime; the veto binds only at Δ_app → 0 and full occlusion.
