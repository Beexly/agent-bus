This is counter-intuitive during crisis because misfits appear suboptimal under current conditions, yet they are the only source of adaptive potential when the environment changes. Removing them to optimize current efficiency reproduces the “remarkably uniform genetic constitution” that makes a population “much more defenseless against change in environment” [PAGE 55].

Clock: two timescales compete. The genetic erosion clock advances as t/(2N_e) in inbreeding coefficient F_t and as H_t = H_0(1 - 1/(2N_e))^t in heterozygosity loss; the environmental-change clock advances at rate \tau_{env}. Extinction looms when \tau_{env} is shorter than the time required to regenerate standing variation, i.e. when the community cannot track change before H_G collapses toward zero bits. Wiener’s Haldane point [PAGE 55] is a clock mismatch: the Indian elephant may be numerically secure but already near a genetic extinction boundary, so census size and viability are out of phase.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/demographic veto: Wiener states that “only by a miracle could we expect a population of two or three elephants to remain generation after generation with a composition including one male and one female” [PAGE 55], and that “a permanent community which falls below a certain size ceases thereby to be stable” [PAGE 55]. Any model asserting indefinite stability at N \approx 2–3 without exogenous input is vetoed by this layer.

Biomechanical/allometric veto: horn elaboration requires “the increase in the size of the body necessary for the animal to bear such an armature” [PAGE 54]; large body mass imposes a food–area constraint (“A large animal needs a large amount of food, and thus a considerable area over which it may range” [PAGE 54–55]). The giant land tortoise is the admitted exception because it lives “at so low a metabolic level” [PAGE 54]; this is a narrow metabolic waiver, not a general license for small-population viability.

Weather/epidemic veto: “It would not take a very long time for a herd of one hundred animals all to be attacked by some pestilence, and there is a very real possibility of the disease proving fatal in all cases” [PAGE 55]. This is a density-independent catastrophic veto operating regardless of N_e genetics. Adjudication: the demographic, biomechanical, and epidemic layers jointly veto any claim that Wiener’s “certain size” community can persist indefinitely through internal dynamics alone; only the low-metabolism tortoise clause offers a bounded exception, and the text breaks off before completing the grazing-space argument [PAGE 55].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a prediction and a falsification condition:

Scenario 1 (outcrossing rescue). Counterfactual: an isolated population held at Wiener’s “certain size” [PAGE 55] receives one outcrossing migrant every g generations while the environment remains constant. Prediction: expected heterozygosity remains bounded away from the absorbing zero, H_t \ge H_{min} > 0 for t \gg g, and recessive lethal homozygosity does not reach fixation. Veto: if H_t collapses to zero bits despite recurrent outcrossing, the drift/inbreeding interpretation of Wiener’s “certain size” claim is falsified.

Scenario 2 (standing-variation purge). Counterfactual: a population is maintained above the demographic stochasticity threshold (N \gg 100) but is rendered near-isogenic, eliminating standing variation and hence any “misfit individuals” [PAGE 55]. Prediction: upon introduction of a novel pathogen or weather shift, adaptation fails and extinction approaches certainty because no class of individuals is especially well adapted to the modified environment. Veto: survival and evolutionary rescue despite zero standing variation falsifies Wiener’s scarcity-of-misfits mechanism.

Scenario 3 (carrying-capacity expansion). Counterfactual: grazing space / carrying capacity K is increased by factor m for a population previously vulnerable to single-event pestilence [PAGE 55]. Prediction: one epidemic can no longer produce 100% mortality; a surviving fraction persists above the Allee/demographic recovery threshold and the population rebounds. Veto: total extinction from a single pestilence event despite enlarged K falsifies Wiener’s area–food–risk linkage [PAGE 54–55]. All three are verifiable through time-series genotyping, controlled challenge trials, and demographic censuses; none involves betting lines, spreads, totals, or moneylines.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
