Finally, rank functions simultaneously as a presentation-bias feature and as a determinant of exposure, producing an endogenous feedback loop: rank influences click probability, click probability trains the model, and the model’s output influences future rank. Without an explicit instrument or exclusion restriction, the presentation-bias coefficient cannot be causally separated from relevance. The patent’s own statement that features described in separate embodiments can be combined or excised [PAGE 19] further expands the claim scope gray zone, because it is unclear which combination of presentation-bias and relevancy features is required for infringement or for valid debiasing.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Define the measurable space. Let \( \mathcal{Q} \) be the query space and \( \mathcal{U} \) the URL space. For an impression \( i \), define the presentation-bias feature vector \( \mathbf{x}_i^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_b} \) where components include rank \( r_i \in \mathbb{N}^+ \), title length \( \ell_i \in \mathbb{N} \), snippet-bold indicator \( b_i \in \{0,1\} \), and advertisement presence \( a_i \in \{0,1\} \) [PAGE 20, Claim 3]. Define the relevancy feature vector \( \mathbf{x}_i^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_r} \) with components including information-retrieval score \( s_i \in \mathbb{R} \), query language \( L_i \in \mathcal{L} \) (categorical), and query word count \( n_i \in \mathbb{N} \) [PAGE 20, Claim 4]. The observed click is a Bernoulli random variable \( c_i \in \{0,1\} \), and the click-through rate is the conditional probability \( p_i = \Pr(c_i = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i^{(b)}, \mathbf{x}_i^{(r)}) \in [0,1] \), dimensionless.

A formal model satisfying Claim 1 [PAGE 20] is a generalized linear model with logistic link:
\[
\log \frac{p_i}{1-p_i} = \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(b)\top}\mathbf{x}_i^{(b)} + \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(r)\top}\mathbf{x}_i^{(r)} + \varepsilon_i, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \text{Logistic}(0,1),
\]
where log-odds are measured in nats. Under Claim 5 [PAGE 20], two separate models are trained:
\[
p_i^{(b)} = \sigma(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(b)\top}\mathbf{x}_i^{(b)}), \qquad
p_i^{(r)} = \sigma(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(r)\top}\mathbf{x}_i^{(r)}),
\]
with \( \sigma(z) = (1+e^{-z})^{-1} \). The patent does not specify the composition operator \( \mathcal{C}: [0,1]^2 \to [0,1] \) used to produce the final quality score \( q_i \). A candidate debiasing operator consistent with “factoring out independent effects” would be
\[
q_i = s_i - \lambda \cdot \mathcal{D}(p_i^{(b)}), \quad \lambda \in \mathbb{R},
\]
where \( \mathcal{D} \) extracts the presentation-bias component; however \( \lambda \) and \( \mathcal{D} \) are unspecified in the source.

Breakdown regimes and singularities. (i) Log-odds singularity: as \( p_i \to 0^+ \) or \( p_i \to 1^- \), \( \log[p_i/(1-p_i)] \to \mp\infty \), so maximum-likelihood estimation fails under perfect separation (e.g., advertisements always shown at top position and always clicked). (ii) Identifiability collapse: if the design matrix \( [\mathbf{X}^{(b)} \; \mathbf{X}^{(r)}] \) has rank \( < d_b + d_r \) because rank and IR score are collinear in live traffic, \( (\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1} \) does not exist and the bias and relevance coefficients are not separately identifiable. (iii) Run-time aggregation loss: per-click weighting before aggregation [PAGE 20] is impossible when the model is applied only after aggregation [PAGE 20], introducing a precision error bounded by the variance of the within-aggregation display-bias distribution. Page citations: architecture and client-server relations [PAGE 19]; implicit-feedback models, prior-model application at build/run time, aggregation precision loss, and Claims 1–5 [PAGE 20].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage. The patent’s reliance on implicit user feedback descends from the click-model literature in information retrieval, notably the examination hypothesis and cascade model, where user clicks are treated as noisy relevance judgments contaminated by position bias. The specific notion of “presentation bias features” including rank, title length, snippet formatting, and ad presence extends feature-based debiasing approaches used in commercial web search. The claim language permitting models to be applied either at run time or at model-building time [PAGE 20] mirrors the engineering tension between online correction (low latency, possible staleness) and offline log-based correction (higher precision, delayed feedback).

Multi-week traps. In a production search system retrained on weekly or daily log snapshots, three traps emerge. First, the rank-feedback trap: because rank \( r \) is both a feature and a consequence of prior model output, weekly retraining on logs generated by the current ranker bakes in historical position bias; the model learns that top-ranked documents are more relevant simply because they were more exposed. Second, the distribution-shift trap: applying the prior model only at build time [PAGE 20] means the debiased click signal is computed under the old ranker’s exposure distribution; when the ranker changes, the pre-computed weights become stale and the debiasing correction misfires. Third, the aggregation-latency trap: the patent’s admission that run-time adjustment occurs after aggregation [PAGE 20] implies a multi-hour to multi-day lag between a click and its debiased incorporation, during which the live ranking continues to amplify uncorrected bias. Over multiple weeks, these loops compound, causing the “quality score” to drift away from true relevance unless the model is re-identified with fresh, randomized exposure data.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind models the nested beliefs among three agents: the search engine, the end user, and the platform engineer. The engine builds a model of the user’s click propensity \( p(\mathbf{x}^{(b)}, \mathbf{x}^{(r)}) \); the user, in turn, forms expectations about the engine’s ranking and adjusts examination behavior (e.g., trusting top results more). The engineer models both the user and the engine when deciding how to deploy the debiasing model.

Crisis habit regression. When a sudden drop in perceived search quality occurs (e.g., a spam surge or a relevance complaint spike), engineers habitually regress to the “traditional click fraction model” explicitly named on [PAGE 20] because it is interpretable and fast to retrain. This regression abandons the presentation-bias prior, re-exposing the system to rank-driven click inflation.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is over-correction: if the model misattributes a genuinely high-IR document’s high CTR to presentation bias (because it appeared at rank 1), the quality score is deflated and the document is demoted, reducing relevance despite the document’s merit. The patent’s two-model split in Claim 5 [PAGE 20] heightens this risk because presentation-bias and relevancy predictions are estimated independently and may produce contradictory quality adjustments.

Clock. The patent distinguishes build-time application (features fetched from logs, improved response time during searches) from run-time application (adjustment to ranking boost at query time) [PAGE 20]. The “clock” is the latency constraint: run-time model inference must complete within the search-engine’s query deadline, typically tens of milliseconds. If the logistic-regression scoring of high-dimensional query-URL feature pairs exceeds this deadline, the system must fall back to precomputed build-time scores, which are stale by construction. The recursive instability is therefore bounded by the clock: faster retraining reduces staleness but increases compute load; slower retraining preserves latency but amplifies the rank-feedback trap.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto. The system architecture on [PAGE 19] comprises clients, servers, LANs, WANs, and the Internet. Any proposed ranking change that adds model inference to the critical path must satisfy a physical veto: end-to-end inference latency \( \tau_{\text{inf}} \) plus network round-trip \( \tau_{\text{net}} \) must remain below the search latency SLA \( \tau_{\text{SLA}} \). If \( \tau_{\text{inf}} + \tau_{\text{net}} > \tau_{\text{SLA}} \), the deployment is vetoed regardless of statistical merit.

Biomechanical layer veto. The biological layer is the human user generating implicit feedback through motor acts (clicking, scrolling, gaze fixation) on a graphical user interface [PAGE 19]. A veto is triggered if the debiased presentation violates human interaction priors: e.g., if removing presentation-bias boosts causes formerly top-ranked, high-exposure results to drop below the visual fold, user examination probability collapses and the implicit-feedback signal itself becomes sparser and noisier, degrading the training data for future models.

Weather layer veto. “Weather” denotes environmental network conditions—bandwidth fluctuations, packet loss, and intermittent connectivity on WAN/Internet links [PAGE 19]. If the server environment must fetch model features or prior-model parameters from remote logs at build time [PAGE 20], network volatility can interrupt feature retrieval. A veto is triggered when the probability of successful feature assembly \( P_{\text{fetch}} \) falls below a reliability threshold \( \eta \) (e.g., 0.99), because incomplete feature vectors invalidate the logistic-regression input and produce undefined quality scores.

Veto proof. A model update is deployed only if it passes all three layers simultaneously: physical latency, biomechanical signal sufficiency, and weather-robust feature availability. The absence of any one condition constitutes a hard veto; there is no override specified in the patent, which means the system’s statistical debiasing is subordinate to infrastructure and human-interaction constraints.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR Veto Scenario 1 — Collinearity singularity. Condition (verifiable): compute Pearson correlation \( \rho = \operatorname{corr}(r_i, s_i) \) over a held-out log sample. Veto trigger: \( \rho > 0.9 \). Prediction: the presentation-bias and relevancy coefficients are not separately identifiable; the debiased quality score will remain correlated with rank \( r \), and the rank-CTR relationship will not flatten after deployment.

RLVR Veto Scenario 2 — Run-time aggregation precision loss. Condition (verifiable): compare per-click display-bias weighting applied pre-aggregation (build time) versus post-aggregation adjustment (run time) on the same query stream. Veto trigger: the mean absolute deviation between the two adjusted click signals exceeds \( \delta = 0.02 \) CTR points. Prediction: because the patent acknowledges run-time post-aggregation adjustment causes “loss of precision” [PAGE 20], the residual presentation bias will scale with the variance of position exposure within the aggregation window.

RLVR Veto Scenario 3 — Logistic-regression boundary singularity. Condition (verifiable): monitor the minimum and maximum predicted probabilities \( \hat{p}_{\min}, \hat{p}_{\max} \) over live traffic. Veto trigger: \( \hat{p}_{\min} < 10^{-6} \) or \( \hat{p}_{\max} > 1 - 10^{-6} \) for sustained traffic (indicating separation or near-separation). Prediction: log-odds approach \( \mp\infty \), the quality-score adjustment \( \mathcal{D}(p^{(b)}) \) becomes numerically unstable, and the ranker will either freeze or produce random reorderings for affected queries.

Each veto is verifiable from production logs and model telemetry without access to proprietary weights.
