<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let the match-level outcome be \(Y_i\in\{0,1\}\), with \(Y_i=1\) when get_points is yes, and let \(X_i\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\) be the engineered match-level predictors. The source frames the task as supervised binary classification: "estimates the probability of securing at least one point by match end (90 minutes plus stoppage time) using match data" [PAGE 1]. The model operator is
\[
\hat p_i=f_\theta(X_i),\qquad \hat p_i\in[0,1],
\]
where \(\theta\) denotes algorithm-specific parameters and tuning hyperparameters. A thresholded decision is
\[
\hat Y_i(\tau)=\mathbf{1}\{\hat p_i\ge\tau\}.
\]
The paper does not fix \(\tau\); it reports accuracy and ROC AUC "without specifying an operational threshold or cost-sensitive objective" [PAGE 14]. Accuracy is dimensionless:
\[
\mathrm{Acc}(\tau)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{\hat Y_i(\tau)=Y_i\}.
\]
The Brier score is a mean squared probability error:
\[
\mathrm{Brier}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat p_i-Y_i)^2,
\]
with units of squared probability, hence dimensionless on the probability scale. The reported MLP values are ROC AUC \(0.8045\) and Brier \(0.2072\) [PAGE 13]. A constant base-rate predictor with \(\bar y=39/82\approx0.4756\) gives \(B_0=\bar y(1-\bar y)\approx0.2494\), so the MLP's Brier improvement is about \(0.0422\) squared-probability units. This is a legitimate benchmark, not a toy ratio.

The ROC AUC operator for a randomly selected positive and negative pair is
\[
\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr(\hat p_+>\hat p_-)+\frac{1}{2}\Pr(\hat p_+=\hat p_-),
\]
dimensionless. With \(K=5\) stratified cross-validation folds, the tuning rule is
\[
\overline{\mathrm{AUC}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathrm{AUC}_k(\theta),
\qquad
\theta^*=\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\overline{\mathrm{AUC}}(\theta).
\]
The source states that "the best combination of hyperparameters was chosen based on the highest ROC AUC averaged along the cross-validation training folds" [PAGE 6]. Grid sizes are 200 candidate configurations for logistic regression, random forest, SVM, and MLP, and 600 for extreme gradient boosting [PAGE 6]. Hyperparameters are dimensionless except counts such as trees, epochs, hidden units, and tree depth.

The named preprocessing operators are: "k-NN imputation for missing values; (ii) dummy encoding for nominal predictors, and (iii) removal of zero-variance predictors" [PAGE 5]. A standard k-NN imputation operator is
\[
\hat x_{ij}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\ell\in N_k(i)}x_{\ell j},
\]
with units of the imputed feature; however, the source does not report the neighborhood size \(k\), distance metric, or scaling, so the exact imputation operator is under-specified. For logistic regression, the paper names the glmnet engine and tunes penalty and mixture [PAGE 6]. The standard link is
\[
p_i=\sigma(\eta_i)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\eta_i}},\qquad
\eta_i=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jx_{ij},
\]
with elastic-net penalty \(P_\alpha(\beta)=\lambda[\alpha\|\beta\|_1+(1-\alpha)\|\beta\|_2^2/2]\), where \(\lambda\) and \(\alpha\) are dimensionless tuning parameters and the penalty scale depends on preprocessing. For the RBF SVM, the standard kernel is
\[
K(x,x')=\exp(-\gamma\|x-x'\|^2),
\]
with \(\gamma\) the rbf_sigma hyperparameter. The reported SVM values include cost \(8.209957655\), margin \(0.102854643\), and rbf_sigma \(1.27436\times10^{-6}\) [PAGE 12]. That very small kernel width is a breakdown risk: as \(\gamma\to0\), \(K(x,x')\to1\) and the kernel approaches a constant, producing an underfit near-intercept model; as \(\gamma\to\infty\), the kernel becomes nearly diagonal and can overfit. The source does not report feature scaling, so the numerical interpretation of this \(\gamma\) is unresolved.

Breakdown and singularity regimes are explicit. ROC AUC is undefined if the evaluation set contains only one outcome class; with a 21-match test set this risk is small but not zero. Accuracy is threshold-dependent and therefore not a unique model score without \(\tau\). Logistic regression can break down under complete separation or rank deficiency; the paper reports that "The absolute values of the coefficients do not exceed 0.53" [PAGE 11], which reduces linear-collinearity concern but does not eliminate separation or nonlinear dependence. The MLP reports hidden_units \(7\) and epochs \(707\) with penalty \(5.34812\times10^{-7}\) [PAGE 12]; with a nonconvex objective and low penalty, overfit and initialization variance are breakdown regimes. XGBoost fixes 1000 trees and tunes tree_depth \(14\), min_n \(5\), learn_rate \(0.004430591\), sample_size \(0.753788572\), mtry \(3\), and loss_reduction \(1.90523\times10^{-6}\) [PAGE 12]; its test ROC AUC \(0.5318\) is close to random [PAGE 13], so boosting is a documented weak regime here. Feature importance is not specified by an exact equation in the source; the vip package is named [PAGE 6], but the mathematical importance metric is algorithm-specific. Therefore only the ranking and the interpretive caution are source-supported: importance is "predictive contribution rather than causal effects" [PAGE 14].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive decision loop has at least four minds: the analyst who engineers features, the coaching staff who select lineups and formations, the players whose minutes are realized, and the model that learns from those decisions. The model output \(\hat p_i\) is not read in a vacuum; staff interpret it through expectations about the team's crisis state, and their subsequent selection behavior changes future \(X_i\). This creates a recursive theory-of-mind problem: the model is trained on behavior that is itself a response to prior beliefs. The source's near-balanced outcome distribution, 52 percent no and 48 percent yes [PAGE 6], reduces simple majority-class habit, but the seasonal deterioration [PAGE 7] means the local habit can regress toward "no points" within a losing regime. That is crisis habit regression: the model may learn a season-specific losing prior and then mistake it for a stable squad property.

The counter-trap is to avoid converting predictive importance into a causal instruction. If minutes_top_3 is important, the naive counter-trap is "play the top-ranked player more." But the source says key-player minutes may reflect "selection and match-management decisions (e.g., stronger line-ups remaining on the pitch longer)" [PAGE 14]. The correct counter-trap is a decision-theoretic audit: separate availability, selection, match status, injury, and opponent quality; use the model as a diagnostic ranking within the observed feature space, not as a causal prescription. The paper also warns that importance can be influenced by correlated predictors and missingness mechanisms [PAGE 5] and that interpretation is "predictive contribution rather than causal effects" [PAGE 14]. A staff member who treats the model as an oracle would close the loop badly; a staff member who treats it as a hypothesis generator preserves the loop for testing.

The clock is the third component. The target includes "90 minutes plus stoppage time" [PAGE 1], but the features include full-match minutes and formation durations [PAGE 3]. If the model is consulted before kickoff, the clock is violated because the features contain future within-match information. If it is consulted during the match, the model must be timestamped and re-estimated at the decision time; the source does not provide a time-resolved hazard or state-space formulation. The recursive theory-of-mind verdict is therefore: use the model to structure retrospective review and to generate testable hypotheses; do not let crisis habit regression harden into a fixed selection rule; do not fall into the counter-trap of causal overreach; and respect the clock by matching the prediction timestamp to the feature timestamp.
