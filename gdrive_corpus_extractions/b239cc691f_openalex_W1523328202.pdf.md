The market forecast is valid only while the square-root expressions for \(t_b\) remain real and while the “heavy” market acceleration remains within the golden-section style bound. Any claim that the model “proves” a real-world innovation outcome would overreach: it proves saddle and sensitivity properties inside its own assumptions. Externally, there is no physical, biomechanical, or weather layer in the equations; those layers can only appear as calibration vetoes or as model misspecification. The recursion is that the innovator observes its own stock, estimates the market’s possible commercialization interval, chooses a scenario, receives feedback, and then re-evaluates; dynamic programming guarantees time consistency along the optimal trajectory. The most dangerous multi-week trap is to treat the point estimate \(t_b^0\) as the actual market time and ignore \([t_b^2,t_b^1]\). A second trap is to fit \(\alpha\) near 1 and then use the closed-form plan, which is singular there. A third trap is to use acceleration levels beyond the inertness bound and still expect the one-switch and robustness results. The source’s own conclusion is modest: it constructs a feedback policy and studies sensitivity and robustness, not empirical validation.

The source does not assign SI units. The innovator’s light dynamics is [PAGE 6]:
\[
\dot x(t)=-\sigma x(t)+r_a^{\gamma}(t),\qquad 0\lt\gamma\lt1,\quad \sigma\gt0.
\]
With \(u=r_a^{\gamma}\) and \(\alpha=1/\gamma\gt1\), the control problem is [PAGE 8]:
\[
\dot x(t)=-\sigma x(t)+u(t),\qquad
J=\int_{t_0}^{t_a}e^{-\lambda s}u^{\alpha}(s)\,ds,\qquad \alpha=\frac1\gamma\gt1.
\]
The Hamiltonian and first-order condition are [PAGE 9]:
\[
H=-\psi_1\sigma x+\psi_1u-\psi_2e^{-\lambda s}u^{\alpha},\qquad
\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}=\psi_1-\alpha\psi_2e^{-\lambda s}u^{\alpha-1}=0.
\]
The resulting optimal plan is [PAGE 10]:
\[
u^0(s)=K e^{\frac{\lambda+\sigma}{\alpha-1}s},
\]
and the closed-form plan is [PAGE 10]:
\[
u^0(s,t_0,x_0,t_a,x_a,\alpha,\lambda,\sigma)
=\frac{(x_a e^{(t_a-s)\sigma}-x_0e^{-(s-t_0)\sigma})\rho}
{e^{(t_a-s)\rho}-e^{-(s-t_0)\rho}},
\qquad
\rho=\frac{\alpha\sigma+\lambda}{\alpha-1}.
\]
The value function is [PAGE 17]:
\[
w(t,x,t_a,x_a,\alpha,\lambda,\sigma)
=\rho^{(\alpha-1)}
\frac{e^{-\lambda t_a}(x_a-xe^{-\sigma(t_a-t)})^{\alpha}}
{(1-e^{-\rho(t_a-t)})^{(\alpha-1)}}.
\]
The optimal feedback is [PAGE 18]:
\[
u^0(t,x,t_a,x_a,\alpha,\lambda,\sigma)
=\frac{\rho(e^{\sigma(t_a-t)}x_a-x)}
{e^{\rho(t_a-t)}-1}.
\]
The benefit and profit functions are [PAGE 19]:
\[
d=\frac{S_a}{\lambda-\mu}e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t_a}
+\max\left\{0,\frac{S_b}{\lambda-\mu}
\left(e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t_a}-e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t_b}\right)\right\},
\]
\[
R(t,x,t_a,x_a,t_b,S_a,S_b,\alpha,\lambda,\mu,\sigma)
=d(t_a,t_b,S_a,S_b,\lambda,\mu)
-w(t,x,t_a,x_a,\alpha,\lambda,\sigma).
\]
The market heavy dynamics is [PAGE 28-29]:
\[
\dot y(s)=-\sigma y(s)+z(s)y(s),\qquad
\dot z(s)=v(s),\qquad |v(s)|\le v_0.
\]
Under zero acceleration the market commercialization time is [PAGE 30]:
\[
t_b^0-t=\left(\frac{\dot y}{y}\right)^{-1}(\ln y_b-\ln y).
\]
For general bounded acceleration [PAGE 30]:
\[
t_b-t=
\frac{-\dot y/y+\left((\dot y/y)^2+2v(\ln y_b-\ln y)\right)^{1/2}}{v},
\]
with the analogous formula for \(v=-v_0\) and \(v=+v_0\). The sensitivity bounds are [PAGE 31]:
\[
0\le t_b^1-t_b^0\le 2v_0\left(\frac{\dot y}{y}\right)^{-3}(\ln y_b-\ln y)^2,
\]
\[
0\le t_b^0-t_b^2\le \frac12 v_0\left(\frac{\dot y}{y}\right)^{-3}(\ln y_b-\ln y)^2.
\]
The threshold time satisfies [PAGE 33]:
\[
R^m=RC_1^m=RC_2^m-r(t_b^*),
\]
and the saddle value and strategy are [PAGE 34]:
\[
V=V^*=V_*=
\min_{t_b\in[t_b^2,t_b^1]}\max_{t_a\gt t}R(t_a,t_b)
=\max_{t_a\gt t}\min_{t_b\in[t_b^2,t_b^1]}R(t_a,t_b),
\]
\[
t_a^e=
\begin{cases}
t_1^m, & t\lt t_b^2\lt t_b^*,\\
t_2^m, & t_b^2\ge t_b^*,
\end{cases}
\qquad t_b^e=t_b^2.
\]
The robustness condition uses the golden-section constant [PAGE 38]:
\[
0\le v_0=v_1\le v_2\le
\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\frac{R^2}{\ln y_b-\ln y},
\]
where \(R=\min_{s\ge t_0}|\dot y(s)/y(s)|\). The robustness estimate is [PAGE 38]:
\[
|R(v_2)-R^m(v_1)|\le
\frac12 S_b e^{-(\lambda-\mu)t}
\left(\frac{\dot y}{y}\right)^{-3}
(\ln y_b-\ln y)^2(v_2-v_1).
\]

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The model’s lineage is retrocausally visible in its final structures. The static optimal-timing root is Barzel’s “Optimal Timing of Innovations” [PAGE 43], which the paper generalizes into a dynamic benefit stream. The optimal-control root is Pontryagin’s maximum principle and Bellman’s dynamic programming [PAGE 5, PAGE 43], which produce the exponential investment plan and the value-function recursion. The differential-game root is Isaacs and Krasovskii-Subbotin [PAGE 29, PAGE 43], visible in the “crocodile” heavy dynamics and the “boy” light dynamics. The R&D-investment root is Griliches and Watanabe [PAGE 4, PAGE 43], visible in the obsolescence and time-delay structure. The saddle result is not an arbitrary add-on: it is the retrocausal consequence of treating the market as an adversarial but inert player with bounded acceleration.

Multi-week traps follow from that genealogy. First, a team may spend weeks fitting a single market commercialization date \(t_b^0\), forgetting the interval \([t_b^2,t_b^1]\); the model’s sensitivity bounds show that the interval width scales with \(v_0(\dot y/y)^{-3}(\ln y_b-\ln y)^2\) [PAGE 31]. Second, a team may calibrate \(\alpha\) near 1 and then use the closed-form exponential plan, which is precisely where \(\rho\) and the final-time control diverge [PAGE 10]. Third, a team may treat the profit function as concave and use a single local search; the source instead has two scenarios and a threshold, with \(t^m_2\lt t^m_1\) [PAGE 22]. Fourth, a team may ignore the golden-section bound and still trust the robustness estimate; the proof of the one-switch and robustness properties uses that inertness condition [PAGE 38-41]. Fifth, a team may interpret the “heavy” market as a physical object; the source’s heavy dynamics is a phenomenological exponential stock with bounded acceleration, not a weather or biomechanical model [PAGE 28-29].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory of mind here is not psychological speculation; it is the innovator’s nested estimation of the market’s possible behavior. Level 1: the innovator observes its own \(x(t)\) and the market’s \(y(t),\dot y(t)\). Level 2: it estimates the market first-passage interval \([t_b^2,t_b^1]\) from bounded acceleration. Level 3: it computes the threshold \(t_b^*\) that equalizes slow-scenario profit \(RC_1^m\) and fast-scenario profit \(RC_2^m-r(t_b^*)\) [PAGE 33]. The “crisis habit regression” is the default to the slow scenario when \(t_b^2\lt t_b^*\); this is the local-profit habit that avoids the bonus-sales gamble. The “clock” is the dynamic-programming and threshold clock: along the optimal trajectory the threshold moves monotonically, and the source proves that optimal strategies switch scenarios at most one time [PAGE 36]. Thus the recursive model is stable only if the innovator’s mental model of the market’s acceleration remains inside the inertness band; outside it, the recursion can oscillate or mis-time the switch.
