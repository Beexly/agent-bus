The paper derives a hierarchical Bayesian Bradley-Terry model by first stating the base paired-comparison likelihood on page 1: P{team i defeats team j} = π_i / (π_i + π_j), where π_i ∈ ℝ⁺ is a team strength. The full-season likelihood factors over head-to-head matchups as a product of binomial terms (Eq. 2, page 1). The core statistical difficulty is that maximum likelihood estimation of π has no closed form and admits pathologies: strength ratios can collapse to zero, diverge to infinity, or become undetermined, which forces win probabilities to 0, 1, or undefined values (page 1, Section 1.2). The authors note MLB is "practically guaranteed immunity" from these issues because of its balanced 162-game schedule, yet they argue a Bayesian approach "fully guards against them" (page 1).

The derivation then migrates to log-strengths λ_i = log π_i. Two prior families satisfy Whelan's four desiderata (team interchange, win-loss interchange, team elimination invariance, and propriety): an isotropic Gaussian IN with λ_i ∼ N(0, σ²), and a Beta-induced Iβ prior on ζ_i = π_i/(1+π_i) (page 2, Eqs. 3-4). The authors select IN by a maximum-entropy argument: fixing prior mean 0 and variance σ² makes the Gaussian the maximum differential-entropy density on ℝ, so IN "carries less information about λ_i" (page 4, Eq. 13 and surrounding text). The Iβ prior maps to a generalized logistic of the third kind in λ-space (page 3, Eq. 8), with E[λ_i | Iβ] = 0 and V[λ_i | Iβ] = 2ψ'(η) (page 3, Eq. 11).

The hierarchical layer places a hyperprior on σ derived from the previous season via a Laplace/MAP expansion (page 5, Eqs. 14-18). The log-posterior (Eq. 14, page 5) combines the Bradley-Terry likelihood in λ-space with a Gaussian prior on λ and a Jacobian term −N log σ. The MAP point solves coupled equations (Eq. 16, page 5), but because the likelihood determines λ only up to an additive constant, the authors impose the gauge Σ_i λ_i = 0 (page 5). They then replace the coupled MAP solution with MLE estimates under the assumption that total wins V_i are large compared to the shrinkage term bλ_i/bσ² (page 5), yielding bσ ≈ sqrt((1/N) Σ_i (bλ^MLE_i)²) (Eq. 18, page 5). The hyperprior variance bς is obtained from the inverse Hessian (Eqs. 19-21, page 6), approximated as block-diagonal because the {n_ij} terms dominate with a full season's data, giving bς ≈ bσ²/(2N) and a Gamma hyperprior Γ(2N, 2N bσ²) (page 6, Eq. 21). Crucially, the hyperprior for a target season is built from the previous season's data to avoid using the target season's own observations (page 6).

Gray-zone audit: (1) The MLE-to-MAP substitution (Eq. 18) is only asymptotically justified; early in the season V_i is small and the approximation can misstate shrinkage. (2) The block-diagonal Hessian (Eq. 21, page 6) neglects off-diagonal coupling through shared opponents; it is accurate only when within-team diagonal terms dominate. (3) The gauge fixing Σ λ_i = 0 is a modeling convention, not an empirical constraint; different gauges shift all λ_i but leave pairwise differences and predictions invariant. (4) The hyperprior uses a one-season lag, so abrupt mid-decade parity shifts are not captured until the following year. (5) Proper priors are required for MCMC stability; improper priors are explicitly rejected (page 5). These gray zones define the exact regimes where the model's predictive advantage over MLE is largest (early season, Table 5, page 10) and where it converges (late season, after July 1).

The recursion is that each team's latent strength is inferred conditionally on the league-wide hyperparameter σ, which is itself inferred from the previous season's realized outcomes, creating a lagged second-order belief about league parity that updates annually rather than intra-season.

Physical layer: the likelihood p(V | λ) depends exclusively on head-to-head win counts V_ij and game counts n_ij (Eq. 12, page 4). Pitching biomechanics, pitcher fatigue, injury status, bullpen usage, and player substitutions are not represented in λ or in the likelihood. Any such physical shock enters the model only ex post through the realized win/loss matrix V; there is no intra-season biomechanical state variable that can veto or modify a predicted game probability. Biomechanical veto: structurally absent; the model is record-only by design (page 4, Section 2.1: "teams be evaluated only based on their performance against one another... relies solely on the head-to-head records").

Weather layer: temperature, wind, precipitation, and park effects are absent. A game played in extreme heat or high wind has the same likelihood contribution as a game in neutral conditions, conditional on the same n_ij and V_ij. Weather veto: absent from the generative model; weather can only alter outcomes insofar as it changes V, after which the likelihood updates at the next inference pass.

Cross-layer adjudication: because the model's sole sufficient statistic is the win-loss record (Eq. 2, page 1), all non-record physical, biomechanical, and weather variables are exogenous hard vetoes. They cannot invalidate the likelihood internally, but they also cannot be corrected for prospectively. If a starting pitcher is scratched or a game is delayed by weather, π_i does not change until the outcome is recorded. The model's proof of veto immunity is therefore also its limitation: it is robust to unmodeled physical noise because it ignores it entirely, but it cannot anticipate a physical disruption before it is reflected in V. No betting lines, spreads, totals, or moneylines are invoked anywhere in this adjudication; all quantities are probabilities, strengths in log-space, and game-count errors.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-via-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios, with formal predictions grounded in the source:

Scenario 1 — Early-season small-sample veto (partition Apr 15). Suppose a team begins the season 10–2 against a balanced subset of opponents. The MLE fixed-point iteration (Eq. 17, page 5) drives bλ^MLE_i toward an extreme positive value because V_i is large relative to losses, producing an overconfident point prediction for remaining games. The posterior predictive (Eq. 23, page 9) integrates over λ ∼ N(0, σ²I) with hyperprior σ ∼ Γ(60, 60 bσ²) from 2016 (bσ = 0.262, Table 1, page 6), shrinking E[λ_i | V] toward zero. Verifiable prediction: |error^Bayes_i| ≤ 8.82 games and |error^MLE_i| ≥ 24.65 games at the Apr 15 partition (Table 5, page 10); specifically, the Bayesian absolute error is at most roughly 36% of the MLE error, and sd^Bayes (6.58 games) < sd^MLE (17.34 games). A run of Ford's iteration vs. Stan HMC on held-out wins will confirm MLE over-dispersion.

Scenario 2 — Mid-season convergence veto (partition Jul 1). By July 1 each team has roughly 85–90 games, so V_i is large compared to bλ_i/bσ², validating the MLE–MAP substitution of Eq. 18 (page 5). The likelihood dominates the prior; posterior variance contracts. Verifiable prediction: error^Bayes = 4.04 games vs error^MLE = 4.17 games (Table 5, page 10), a difference < 0.2 games, and sd^Bayes ≈ sd^MLE (3.46 vs 3.43 games). Any predictive pipeline that still shows a large Bayesian advantage at Jul 1 is mis-specified relative to this paper's empirical regime.

Scenario 3 — Degenerate-record singularity veto. Suppose a team goes 0–10 against a divisional opponent, so n_ij = 10, V_ij = 0, V_ji = 10. Under MLE the implied strength ratio π_i/π_j → 0, so P{i defeats j} → 0 exactly (pathology described verbatim on page 1: "probabilities... 0, 1, or undetermined"). The likelihood becomes degenerate and Ford's iteration may fail to converge or produce infinite/NaN strengths (page 1, Section 1.2). Under the Bayesian model, the proper Gaussian prior (Eq. 3, page 2) and proper hyperprior (Eq. 22, page 6) keep the posterior proper and integrable. Verifiable prediction: MCMC yields finite E[λ_i | V] with posterior variance bounded below by the prior variance σ², and no infinite or undefined game probabilities are emitted; MLE yields an undefined or infinite strength ratio. This can be verified by simulating a winless subset and comparing optimizer exit status to posterior sample diagnostics.
