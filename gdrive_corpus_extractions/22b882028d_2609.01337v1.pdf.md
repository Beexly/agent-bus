The sports-specific veto layers (biomechanical/weather) do not exist for a CPU-side software estimator, so I state that plainly and substitute the real hard constraints — the numerical/algorithmic breakdown regimes of the estimator.

<THINK_TRACE>
The paper's claim reduces to one move: replace a holistic map from evidence bundle to answer with a factorized map. In Monolithic Prediction the LLM computes f directly from (T, E); in LEAP the LLM emits local parameters, and a closed-form Bayesian update computes f. Multi-hop derivation: (1) The generative model [PAGE 4] posits θ ∼ P0(θ) and ei | θ ∼ind Pi(ei | θ), i.e., a latent answer with conditionally independent per-item likelihoods. (2) Bayes' rule over a retained subset R gives P(θ | E) ∝ P0(θ) ∏_{i∈R} Pi(ei | θ) [PAGE 5, Eq. 4]. (3) Conjugacy is chosen so Eq. 4 is closed form [PAGE 5]: "We instantiate P0 and {Pi} as conjugate pairs so Equation (4) is closed form." (4) Each output type τ selects a conjugate family — Gaussian, categorical-multinomial, or independent Bernoulli — with a tempered update (temperature η, role weights wi) that reduces to Eq. 4 at η=1, wi=1 [PAGE 5]. (5) The forecast f is a deterministic readout: quantiles, softmax-normalized option masses, or Bernoulli marginals. (6) Auditability is ∆j, the leave-one-out change recomputed on R∖{j} [PAGE 5].

Gray-zone audit. The load-bearing assumption is ei ⊥ ej | θ. The authors concede its fragility: [PAGE 4–5] "Because duplicated or common-source evidence can violate this, LEAP later clusters dependent evidence before aggregation." Residual correlation after imperfect clustering is the deepest gray zone — the product in Eq. 4 double-counts shared-source items, inflating effective sample size and sharpening the posterior past what the evidence supports [PAGE 8]: "overcounting common source evidence and making the posterior sharper than the evidence warrants." Empirically this is not hypothetical: Table 13 shows removing clustering raises ECE from 0.0876 to 0.1578.

A second gray zone is the prior. Ablation [PAGE 8] shows it is the single most important component — removing it drops FutureX below Monolithic (0.6427 vs 0.6512), "making it the most important component on this subset." The safeguard design is entangled with the prior: outlier rejection only fires when the prior is data-derived [PAGE 14], so a blind prior silently disables the 4σ guard.

A third gray zone is calibration vs sharpness. LEAP trades peak sharpness for calibration: [PAGE 7] "Monolithic concentrates probability on one option, lowering Brier when it is right but paying with sharp overconfidence on the rest." So Brier is the least uniform metric (LEAP wins 3/5 models) while ECE/overconfidence improve uniformly — a resolution-of-the-forecast vs honesty-of-the-forecast distinction that a naive single-metric read would miss.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Task and evidence (SI/units: q text; tfreeze a timestamp; τ∈{continuous, single, multi}; O a set of K options):
$$T=(q, t_{freeze}, \tau, O), \qquad E=\{e_1,\dots,e_n\}.$$

Generative model [PAGE 4, Eq. 3] with conditional independence ei ⊥ ej | θ:
$$\theta \sim P_0(\theta), \qquad e_i \mid \theta \stackrel{indep}{\sim} P_i(e_i \mid \theta).$$

Posterior over retained set R ⊆ {1,…,n} [PAGE 5, Eq. 4]:
$$P(\theta \mid E) \propto P_0(\theta)\prod_{i\in R} P_i(e_i \mid \theta).$$

Continuous (Gaussian conjugate) [PAGE 12–13, Eq. 5–7]. Units: µ in target units (e.g., USD), σ in target units, precision τₓ = 1/σₓ² in (target units)⁻². With tempered η:
$$\tau_{post}=\tau_0+\eta\sum_{i\in R}\tau_i, \qquad \mu_{post}=\frac{1}{\tau_{post}}\Big(\mu_0\tau_0+\eta\sum_{i\in R}\mu_i\tau_i\Big), \qquad \Delta_j=\mu_{post}-\mu_{post}^{(-j)}.$$
Worked instance [PAGE 20]: prior N(250.74, 7.52222) → τ₀=0.017672; τ_post=0.017672+0.013069=0.030741; posterior N(250.157, 5.70312); ∆₍E5₎=−0.6727, ∆₍E1₎=+0.1520, ∆₍E8₎=+0.0680 (USD). Prior precision share τ₀/τ_post = 57.5% verifies.

Single-choice (categorical–multinomial) [PAGE 13, Eq. 8–9]. π₀∈Δ^{K−1}; Lᵢ(k)=P(eᵢ|θ=k)∈[0,1]; α prior power; wᵢ∈[0.05,1.5]:
$$\log\pi_{post}(k)\ \propto\ \alpha\log\pi_0(k)+\eta\sum_{i\in R} w_i\log L_i(k), \qquad \Delta_j=\pi_{post}(\hat k)-\pi_{post}^{(-j)}(\hat k).$$

Multi-choice (independent Bernoulli) [PAGE 13, Eq. 10–11]. π₀(k)∈(0,1); log-LR in nats:
$$\ell_i(k)=\log\frac{p^{true}_{i,k}}{p^{false}_{i,k}}, \qquad \operatorname{logit}\pi_{post}(k)=\operatorname{logit}\pi_0(k)+\eta\sum_{i\in R} w_i\,\ell_i(k), \qquad \Delta_j=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big|\pi_{post}(k)-\pi_{post}^{(-j)}(k)\big|.$$
Anchor check: strong support ptrue≈0.85, pfalse≈0.15 ⇒ ℓ=ln(5.667)=1.735 nats [PAGE 15]; verifier cap ln(0.995/0.005)=5.30 nats.

Metrics [PAGE 16–17]: $\text{Brier}=\frac1N\sum_i\frac1{K_i}\sum_k(p_{ik}-y_{ik})^2$; Spherical $=\frac1N\sum_i\frac{\sum_k p_{ik}y_{ik}}{\sqrt{\sum_k p_{ik}^2}\sqrt{\sum_k y_{ik}^2}}$; $\text{NCRPS}_i=\min(1,\text{CRPS}_i/\max(|v_i|,1))$.

Exact breakdown/singularity regimes:
1. Flat-prior Gaussian collapse: σ₀→∞ ⇒ τ₀→0; if all σᵢ→∞, τ_post→0⁺ ⇒ Var=1/τ_post→∞; at τ_post=0 the mean μ_post is 0/0 (improper, non-normalizable).
2. Zero-likelihood absorbing barrier: any retained Lᵢ(k\*)=0 (or pfalse→0 ⇒ ℓᵢ→−∞) drives log-posterior(k\*)→−∞ ⇒ π_post(k\*)=0; multiplicative updating is absorbing (0·x=0), unrecoverable. Table 8 floors (min 0.05, neutral 0.35) and clips (±5.30, ±3.00 nats) prevent exact zeros.
3. Logit boundary: logit requires argument strictly in (0,1); π₀(k)∈{0,1} ⇒ ±∞ (hence the paper's π₀(k)∈(0,1)).
4. Spherical 0/0: empty ground-truth set (multi-choice) gives Σyᵢₖ²=0; all-zero forecast gives Σpᵢₖ²=0 — no min/max guard, unlike NCRPS.
5. Underflow of ∏Pᵢ: mitigated by log-space [PAGE 13]: "The posterior is computed in log space for numerical stability."
6. Outlier guard disabled: [PAGE 14] "This rule is not applied when the prior is itself blind to evidence" — z=|µᵢ−µ₀|/σ₀>4 fires only for data-derived priors (case: z=|190.00−250.74|/7.5222=8.07).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage is deep and explicit. The engine is Bayesian updating (Bayes/Laplace) instantiated with conjugate pairs, so the Gaussian/normal–normal, categorical–Dirichlet-multinomial, and Bernoulli closed forms are direct descendants of 18th–19th-century inverse probability. Evaluation descends from Brier (1950), "Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability," and from Gneiting & Raftery (2007) on strictly proper scoring rules, which supply Spherical and CRPS. Calibration methodology descends from Guo et al. (2017); note the paper's temperature η scales likelihood contributions inside the generative model and is conceptually distinct from Guo-style post-hoc logit temperature scaling — a genealogy trap if one conflates the two.

The methodological spine — "LLM as local interpreter, deterministic solver as judge" — descends from faithful-explanation work: ERASER (DeYoung et al., 2020) and faithful chain-of-thought (Lyu et al., 2023). The LLM-in-Bayes lineage is BIRD (Feng et al., 2025), Nafar et al. (2026), and the Bayesian Linguistic Forecaster (Murphy, 2026), all cited as precedents [PAGE 2, PAGE 3]. Motivation descends from long-context failure literature: "lost in the middle" (Liu et al., 2024a) and hallucination surveys (Ji et al., 2023; Niu et al., 2024; Belém et al., 2025).

Multi-week traps are the operational hazards of long-horizon evaluation. (a) Look-ahead leakage: the temporal leakage audit [PAGE 15] verifies every page timestamp against tfreeze and reports "no cutoff violations and no suspected leakage cases were detected." (b) Knowledge-cutoff contamination: base models are chosen so cutoffs precede evaluated timestamps [PAGE 6]. (c) Horizon conflation: Figure 3 shows gains widening monotonically with horizon (FutureX 6.8→11.9, overconfidence gap 13.3→23.3 over 7/30/60 days), so any study that samples only short horizons systematically understates LEAP and biases the genealogy of "where the gain came from."
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the two systems as strategic predictors with distinct stress policies. Monolithic's habit: holistic synthesis → peaked commitment. Under stress (long horizon, sparse/noisy evidence) this habit regresses to overconfident commitment on the wrong option — Case 1 (Monolithic Christ Church p=0.85, wrong; LEAP St. Canice's p=0.54, right) and Case 3 (Monolithic Jan-2-1974 p=0.63, wrong; LEAP uniform p=0.25) [PAGE 19]. LEAP's counter-trap is architectural: with |R|=0 after clustering it "returns the uniform prior rather than committing to a guess" — a designed honest-uncertainty reflex, not a learned one.

The "clock" is the forecast horizon. The two policies diverge monotonically as the clock lengthens [PAGE 7]: "As evidence becomes more indirect, Monolithic continues to commit firmly to a single option, while LEAP recognises weaker support in E and produces wider posteriors—degrading by becoming conservative rather than confidently wrong."

Counter-trap audit — does LEAP merely win via the prior or via more tokens? Table 4 answers with matched baselines: "Monolithic + same prior" reaches FutureX 0.6742 and the two-sample-mean budget match (11,753 tokens) reaches 0.6736, both below LEAP at 0.7284 on 11,508 tokens. The counter-trap fails to close the gap, isolating the factorization itself as the causal source. This is the strongest available theory-of-mind falsification: if the effect were prior or budget, the matched baselines would erase it.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Honest adjudication: physical/biomechanical/weather veto layers do not apply. There are no athletes, venues, surfaces, or meteorological variables in a CPU-side Python estimator; the authors state [PAGE 16] "the experiments used zero local GPU hours," and inference is closed-form arithmetic. Importing a biomechanics veto would be a fabricated constraint, so I reject it and substitute the estimator's genuine hard constraints — the invariants that cannot be argued away, only satisfied or violated.

Veto A (zero-likelihood absorbing barrier, proof by contradiction): assume a retained item maps the true option k\* to Lᵢ(k\*)=0. Then log π_post(k\*) = α log π₀(k\*) + η Σ wᵢ log Lᵢ(k) contains log 0 = −∞ ⇒ π_post(k\*)=0, and no later item can restore it (0·x=0). QED — the veto holds unless a likelihood floor >0 intervenes (Table 8's floors are therefore load-bearing, not cosmetic).

Veto B (Spherical 0/0): if Σpᵢₖ²=0 (all-zero forecast) or Σyᵢₖ²=0 (empty truth set in multi-choice), the Eq. (14) denominator is 0 ⇒ estimator undefined. No min/max guard exists on Spherical (the max(|vᵢ|,1) guard is on NCRPS only). QED.

Veto C (improper flat posterior): as τ_post→0⁺, variance 1/τ_post→∞; at τ_post=0, μ_post is 0/0. The estimator is only well-defined on τ_post>0. QED.

These three are the "physical law" substitutes: they gate whether LEAP's arithmetic is even defined, independent of any empirical claim.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
The paper is RLVR-adjacent in verifiability, not in training: [PAGE 16] "We did not train or fine-tune any language model," updates are deterministic, the repo is public (github.com/layingfish/LEAP), and results are N=5 seeds with median σ≈0.010. So the following counterfactuals are checkable and each carries a falsifiable prediction.

Veto 1 — prior necessity. Counterfactual: ablate the prior. Verified in Table 3: "LEAP w/o prior" FutureX 0.6427 < Monolithic 0.6512. Prediction: on any re-run of the diagnostic subset, prior ablation inverts the sign of the FutureX delta (LEAP falls below Monolithic). Falsifier: w/o-prior ≥ Monolithic ⇒ veto fails.

Veto 2 — zero-likelihood load-bearing floor. Counterfactual: disable Table 8's likelihood floors and inject a spurious exact-zero likelihood on the true option in a single-choice task. Prediction: π_post(true)→0 and stays 0 regardless of remaining evidence (Veto A mechanism). Falsifier: truth retains nonzero posterior ⇒ some floor silently saved it, contradicting the absorbing-barrier claim.

Veto 3 — determinism/audit reproducibility. Counterfactual: recompute the continuous posterior from Table 15 items and remove one item. Prediction: the update reproduces N(250.157, 5.70312) within rounding; dropping E5 shifts μ_post by +0.6727 (its ∆₍E5₎=−0.6727), and E1 by −0.1520. This ∆j audit signal is "reproducible by running the update on R∖{j}" [PAGE 5], unlike a free-text rationale. Falsifier: recomputation diverges beyond float tolerance ⇒ determinism claim fails.

Ordering robustness cross-check (verifiable): [PAGE 16] "The ordering of LEAP versus Monolithic … is preserved under every individual seed in 48 out of 50 cells," the two exceptions being DeerFlow Accuracy (tie within ±0.01) and DeepSeek-V3.2 Brier (LEAP behind by 0.0066, <1σ). Prediction: re-seeding cannot flip any cell whose mean gap exceeds 2σ; only these two near-ties are flippable.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
