No objective cardiorespiratory fitness measure (VO\(_2\) max, heart-rate reserve, actigraphy) was recorded; intensity relied on the subjective Borg scale \(11\text{–}13\) [PAGE 2 of 6], explicitly acknowledged as subjective [PAGE 2 of 6]. Veto: without objective dose quantification, any claim that “sports participation” per se caused the EBA change is physically underdetermined.

Biomechanical layer: Basketball involves jumping, cutting, acceleration, deceleration, and hand-eye coordination. Schizophrenia is associated with motor retardation and impaired motor coordination, which are general psychopathology items [PAGE 5 of 6]. If patients could not fully execute basketball skills, the observed fMRI effect during observation of basketball-related motions might reflect compensatory visual-attentional strategies rather than action-execution resonance. Veto: no motion-capture or skill-acquisition data were collected during the actual intervention, so the biomechanical fidelity of exposure is unverified.

Weather layer: The protocol states program duration “was determined by weather condition (temperature and humidity) and the participants’ physical fatigue” because it was conducted in summer [PAGE 2 of 6]. High heat and humidity can truncate sessions, lower intensity, or increase perceived exertion independently of therapeutic intent. This introduces session-level dose variance that is correlated with calendar time and potentially with symptom fluctuation. Veto: no weather logs, session duration records, or temperature/humidity covariates are provided, so weather cannot be regressed out.

Adjudication: The weather and subjective-intensity vetoes are not refuted by the text; they remain active confounders. The physical layer (BMI, antipsychotic load) is described but not modeled as a moderator of fMRI change. Regression analyses showed gender, age, illness duration, and antipsychotic dosage were unrelated to EBA signal change (\(P>0.05\)) [PAGE 4 of 6], yet with \(n=13\) those null tests are underpowered and cannot exonerate pharmacological confounding. Therefore the cross-layer veto stands: the study demonstrates an association within a specific, weather-varied, subjectively dosed, non-randomized cohort, but does not prove a biomechanically specific, weather-independent causal pathway from basketball to EBA to psychiatric symptom change.

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios and falsifiable predictions.

Scenario 1 — Active-control social contact veto.
Counterfactual: Randomize schizophrenia patients to (a) the original sports program or (b) an equal-duration, equal-contact non-sports day-hospital activity (e.g., supervised art or discussion groups) for 3 months, with identical assessor blinding and identical fMRI BRM/BUM paradigm.
Verifiable prediction: If the control activity produces equivalent reduction in PANSS general psychopathology but no selective right EBA increase during BRM vs BUM, the sports-specific neurobiological claim is vetoed; if only the sports arm shows both EBA increase and symptom improvement, the sports-specific pathway is supported. Falsifiable via group × time interaction on EBA beta values and PANSS general subscale in an adequately powered RCT (\(N\ge 60\) to detect interaction \(f=0.25\) at \(\alpha=0.05, 1-\beta=0.80\)).

Scenario 2 — Medication-dose stability veto.
Counterfactual: Hold the sports intervention constant across two strata but randomize antipsychotic management: (a) dosage held constant (as in source) versus (b) dosage optimized/clinically adjusted per routine care, with serum or PET D2 occupancy monitoring to quantify exposure.
Verifiable prediction: If the \(\Delta\mathrm{EBA}\)–\(\Delta\mathrm{PANSS}_{\mathrm{gen}}\) correlation (\(r=-0.78\)) persists in the constant-dose stratum but attenuates to null in the variable-dose stratum, antipsychotic fluctuation is a causal confounder and the original neurobiological mediation claim is vetoed; if correlation remains significant in both strata, medication confounding is rejected. Requires measuring chlorpromazine-equivalent dose at baseline and endpoint and testing moderation by \(\Delta\)dose.

Scenario 3 — Objective dose / climate-controlled veto.
Counterfactual: Move all exercise sessions indoors under temperature- and humidity-controlled conditions, standardize workload via heart-rate reserve or power output (watts/kg) rather than Borg \(11\text{–}13\), and record session-by-session duration and intensity for all participants over 3 months.
Verifiable prediction: If EBA activation change scales linearly with objectively recorded mechanical work (\(\int \mathrm{HRR}\,dt\) or \(\sum \text{watts/kg}\)) and not with calendar days, the weather/Borg subjective-dose confound is resolved; if EBA change is unchanged despite large variation in objective workload, weather and subjective intensity are not causal drivers and the original “sports participation” effect is non-specific. Predictive model: \(\Delta\mathrm{EBA} = \beta_0 + \beta_1(\text{objective work}) + \beta_2(\text{weather index}) + \varepsilon\); veto occurs if \(\beta_1\approx 0\) while \(\beta_2\neq 0\).
