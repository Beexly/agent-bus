<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage begins with James’s Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, p. 489 [PAGE 489], where the “sense of attitude” is treated as a resident kinesthetic feeling most probably arising from articular surfaces, per Chapter XX of the same work. The immediate experimental source is Professor A. Strümpell’s case report in Deutsches Archiv f. Klin. Medicin, vol. xxii, p. 821 [PAGE 489], a German clinical anatomy/neurology venue of the late 19th century. Scientifically, this descends from the Bell–Magendie distinction of sensory and motor roots (early 1800s), through the identification of muscle spindle and Golgi tendon organ end-organs in the 1880s, to Sherrington’s coining of “proprioception” (1906) and later kinesthetic-illusion paradigms (e.g., tendon-vibration illusions, 1970s onward). The case also anticipates modern deafferentation literature in which patients lacking large-fiber proprioception can still move using vision and efference copy but collapse when vision is removed.

Multi-week traps: In proprioceptive rehabilitation, week-one gains may reflect compensatory reliance on vision and hearing rather than true afferent recovery; discharging a patient because he “feels” his limb under masked conditions is a trap because the compensatory channel will fail under load or darkness. A second trap is equating absence of reported fatigue or pain with tissue safety; as the boy was placed in “fantastic and apparently the most inconvenient attitudes” without suspicion [PAGE 489], mechanical strain can accumulate silently while conscious report remains null. A third temporal trap is the one-to-two-minute open-loop hold: the subject’s ability to sustain an arm “without trouble” [PAGE 489] is easily mistaken for endurance, but it actually reflects an open-loop motor program drifting toward an unperceived mechanical or metabolic limit. Any protocol that benchmarks “recovery” at under two minutes, or that trusts verbal fatigue reports in a deafferented limb, commits this clock-based error.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under near-total anesthetic deafferentation, the boy regresses to residual exteroceptive channels—right eye and left ear [PAGE 489]. When vision is withdrawn by bandaging, he substitutes auditory inference for missing proprioception; after manipulations he “sometimes inferred from the sounds connected with the manipulation that something special was being done with him” [PAGE 489]. This is a crisis habit: the subject builds a theory of the experimenter’s actions from sound because the body schema has gone offline.

Counter-trap: the experimenter believes proprioception is abolished, yet auditory cues leak into the subject’s inference loop. The counter-trap is that the subject’s report of “something special” is not evidence of residual kinesthesia but of intact hearing and causal attribution. A valid test must mask sound or treat the subject’s post-hoc verbal inference as contaminated by the experimenter’s visible and audible behavior.

Clock: the recursive temporal structure runs as follows. At \(t = 0\) the subject receives an efferent command to raise and hold the arm; he reports no trouble [PAGE 489]. Because afferent error is null, the internal model cannot correct endpoint drift. For \(\Delta t \approx 60\text{--}120\) s the open-loop program runs; the subject’s first-order theory of his own arm is that it remains where he placed it. The crisis is reached not when he feels fatigue but when an unperceived mechanical or metabolic boundary is crossed—precisely the truncated moment “the arm began to” [PAGE 489]. Recursively, the subject models the experimenter as manipulating a body he cannot feel; the experimenter models the subject as unaware; surprise is triggered only when the visual channel is restored and the handkerchief is removed [PAGE 489]. The head-down dizziness without an assigned ground [PAGE 489] further shows that a vestibular signal can be present while its interpretation remains unavailable, splitting sensation from metacognitive access.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: joint-capsule and ligament nociceptors account for the dull, vaguely localized strain during violent forced hyperextension, especially at the knees [PAGE 489]. Veto: this is nociception, not kinesthesia; it confirms that extreme mechanical load recruits a separate channel even when the articular position sense is abolished.

Biomechanical layer: passive manipulation into “fantastic and apparently the most inconvenient attitudes” [PAGE 489] imposes real tissue and joint loads despite the subject’s null suspicion. A load cell or goniometer would register force and angle even though the subject reports nothing. Veto: absence of phenomenal report does not veto absence of mechanical stress; tissue damage can proceed under zero conscious proprioceptive signal.

Weather/environmental layer: the manipulations occur indoors with bandaged eyes and controlled examiner handling [PAGE 489]. No meteorological variable (barometric pressure, temperature, humidity, wind) is present or varied. The dizziness on head-hanging is vestibular (head position relative to gravity), not atmospheric. Veto: weather is a non-confounder across conditions; indoor thermal stability means any temperature effect would be constant and cannot explain the dissociation between movement and sensation.

Integrated veto proof: the only layers surviving cross-examination are (i) thresholded articular/nociceptive afference, (ii) intact vestibular response to head position, and (iii) intact auditory exteroception used for post-hoc inference. All other channels—deep muscular cramp pain, cutaneous touch, conscious fatigue reporting, and weather—are vetoed as insufficient to explain the pattern [PAGE 489].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Auditory masking during passive movement. Veto condition: broadband white noise at >70 dB SPL is presented to the left ear while the eyes remain bandaged and passive joint movements are performed. Verifiable prediction: the subject must not later infer that “something special is being done” from sound; any post-manipulation claim of inference falls to chance, and surprise upon unveiling remains maximal. If he still reports inference, the auditory channel was not fully masked or the claim is confounded; the kinesthetic-null hypothesis is vetoed for that trial.

Scenario 2 — Sub-threshold joint positioning. Veto condition: all passive movements are restricted to angles \(\theta \le \theta_0 - \delta\), with \(\theta_0\) the hyperextension threshold for strain and \(\delta = 5^\circ\) (\(\approx 0.087\) rad), so that \(S(\theta) = 0\) by the strain law above. Verifiable prediction: the subject reports no sensation whatsoever in those ranges. Any report of strain localizes to skin or is a confounder, vetoing the articular-source claim for non-hyperextended ranges and confirming that the “dull vague feeling” [PAGE 489] is threshold-gated.

Scenario 3 — Efference-only hold with deafferented limb. Veto condition: the subject attempts to maintain arm abduction with eyes shut while afferent feedback is blocked (as in the anesthetic state); endpoint angle \(\theta(t)\) is recorded at 100 Hz. Verifiable prediction: without afferent position or fatigue error, the hold cannot be stabilized beyond open-loop drift; angular error grows as \(\langle |\Delta\theta(t)| \rangle \approx \sigma_w \sqrt{t}\) with \(\sigma_w > 0\), exceeding \(10^\circ\) within 60–120 s despite the subject’s report of “no trouble” [PAGE 489]. Measured drift beyond this bound vetoes the claim that voluntary position holding is stable without kinesthetic feedback.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
