Crisis habit regression describes the tendency of analysts, under deadline or performance pressure, to collapse sophisticated models back into familiar, low-dimensional defaults. In this corpus, the crisis habit would be to abandon the bivariate conditional Poisson goal model’s negative home/away dependence [PAGE 1] and revert to independent Poisson or Dixon-Coles with a binary home flag, because those are computationally simpler and easier to explain to stakeholders. Likewise, the rugby sevens continuous proximity measure [PAGE 1] is cognitively harder to defend than a binary home/away dummy, so under crisis an analyst may regress to $H \in \{0,1\}$, throwing away the geographic/temporal/cultural gradient.

The counter-trap is the opposite over-correction: adopting the continuous proximity score without an identification strategy, and treating the regional differential as causal when it may reflect correlated travel schedules, shared time zones, or diaspora effects [PAGE 1]. Graph-to-Grid faces a related counter-trap: because the pass-surface loss trains the player encoder end to end [PAGE 1], an analyst may attribute all predictive power to player skill when the grid painting itself (coordinate resolution, kernel bandwidth) is doing the causal work. Similarly, the empirical-Bayes shrinkage in the T20 model [PAGE 1] can be misread as removing luck when it merely borrows strength across sparse observations.

The recursive theory of mind nests these layers: the analyst models the team, the team models the opponent, the opponent models the analyst’s model. In a crisis, the analyst regresses to a habit (independent Poisson, binary home flag). The opponent, anticipating this regression, exploits the predictable simplification (e.g., by scheduling travel to maximize unmodeled proximity effects). The analyst’s counter-trap (over-fitting continuous proximity) is itself predictable and exploitable. Physical layer veto: Graph-to-Grid requires measured player coordinates $(x_p, y_p)$ in meters [PAGE 1]. The pitch has finite dimensions; if the grid cell width exceeds the typical passing interaction radius (on the order of meters), the bilinear scatter over-smooths the pass surface and the loss cannot resolve fine-grained defensive pressure. Veto condition: if tracking coordinates are missing for more than a threshold $\epsilon$ of pass events, the gradient $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{pass}}/\partial \mathbf{f}_p$ is masked and the encoder cannot be trained end to end. No betting implications are drawn.

Biomechanical layer veto: pass success depends on ball release velocity (m/s), receiver acceleration (m/s²), and defender closing speed (m/s). None of these biomechanical quantities appear in the abstract descriptions [PAGE 1]; if the player encoder receives only position-scattered features without velocity context, the pass-surface loss is misspecified because identical grid paintings can correspond to physically distinct pass outcomes. Veto condition: if the feature vector $\mathbf{f}_p$ omits kinematic state, the pass-surface model conflates position with intent.

Weather and environmental layer veto: EPL soccer and T20 cricket are outdoor sports subject to rain, wind, and pitch conditions that alter ball behavior. The source abstracts mention match covariates generally [PAGE 1] but do not confirm weather inclusion. Omitted weather variables induce omitted-variable bias in both the goal-dependence model and the T20 runs-above-expected scale. Veto condition: if rain or wind affects scoring intensity but is not in $\mathbf{x}_{ij}$, the Poisson conditional mean is biased and the empirical-Bayes shrinkage targets a contaminated estimand.

Cross-layer adjudication rule: a model layer is vetoed if any downstream dependency lacks the required physical or stochastic input. Specifically, if the G2G grid lacks sub-meter coordinate resolution, the downstream pass-surface loss is vetoed; if the EPL bivariate Poisson lacks overdispersion parameters, the standard errors are vetoed; if the NBA longitudinal Bayesian network lacks injury/trade indicators, the latent transition is vetoed; if the rugby proximity score lacks instruments for cultural confounding, the causal claim is vetoed. These vetoes are deterministic logic checks on model identifiability, not probabilistic forecasts of match outcomes.
