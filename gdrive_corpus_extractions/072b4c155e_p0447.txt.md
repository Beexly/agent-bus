Verifiable measure: retention percentage at 7 and 14 days post-surgery. A statistically significant difference falsifies molar-only sufficiency and activates the physical veto.

Scenario 2 — Occipital sensory channel veto.
Setup: Visually guided habit in rats, with visual stimulus $s_{\text{vis}} \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{vis}}$ driving approach/avoidance behavior under appetite $a$ and aversion $v$.
Counterfactual: Molar theory predicts behavior from $(s_{\text{vis}}, a, v)$ independent of occipital integrity.
Veto prediction: Occipital destruction reduces the mutual information between behavior and visual stimulus below a critical threshold:
\[
I(\mathcal{B}; \mathcal{S}_{\text{vis}} \mid n_{\text{occ-les}}) < I_{\text{crit}} \quad \text{bits},
\]
while $I(\mathcal{B}; \mathcal{A})$ and $I(\mathcal{B}; \mathcal{V})$ remain intact. Visually guided habits fail even when the animal is hungry or threatened.
Verifiable measure: choice accuracy in a visual discrimination task pre- and post-occipital lesion. Failure at above-chance visual stimuli confirms the neurological veto.

Scenario 3 — Developmental sequence veto (Coghill).
Setup: *Amblystoma* larvae observed across developmental time; molar behavior $b(t)$ is recorded alongside neuroanatomical maturation state $D(t)$ (per Coghill, 1929, cited [PAGE 416]).
Counterfactual: Molar behavior can be predicted from concurrent $(s,a,v)$ alone at all ages.
Veto prediction: There exists no function $\hat{b} = \Psi(s,a,v)$ that tracks adult-typical molar behavior across development. Instead,
\[
\frac{d b}{d t} = G(b, D), \qquad D(t) = D_0 + \kappa t \quad \text{(maturation clock)},
\]
and arresting $D$ (developmental veto) freezes $b$ at a juvenile molar form despite exposure to adult-level $(s,a,v)$.
Verifiable measure: Compare behavior of age-matched larvae with experimentally delayed neural development versus controls under identical stimulus-reward regimes. Divergent molar trajectories confirm that behavior facts are not sufficient unto themselves without the developmental-neurological state variable.
