Formally:

with $o \in \mathbb{R}^3$ the camera origin (world units, m), $s \in \Omega_{\text{screen}} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ the gaze point in screen coordinates, and $\Phi:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ the unprojection determined by camera field-of-view $\theta$ and pose $(R,\mathbf{p})$. $$O^\star = \arg\min_{O}\;\min\{\,t : r(t) \in B_A(O)\,\},$$
where $B_A(O)$ is accessory bounding box (A) [PAGE 8–9].

*Breakdown/singularity:* degenerates when $\Phi(s)\to o$ (direction $d=\Phi(s)-o \to 0$, undefined ray) or when $s$ maps behind the image plane ($t<0$, no forward intersection). $$T_{\text{notice}}(O) = \inf\{\,T:\ \tau_O(T)\ge \tau^\star\,\}, \quad \tau^\star = 0.5\ \text{s (SI: seconds)}.$$

**4. Gaze-adaptive region policy** [PAGE 7, verbatim]: the object spawns "in the region of the tile least gazed by the player during the time spent running over the two previous tiles." With region dwell $\tau_r = \int_{T-2\Delta}^{T}\mathbf{1}[\text{gaze}\in r]\,dt$ for $r\in\{\text{L,C,R}\}$, the policy is $r^\star = \arg\min_r \tau_r$ (an attention-forcing minimax on dwell). Deterministic alternation guards against clustering: if the previous object was lateral, the next is central.

**5. Statistics** [PAGE 14–15]. With $n=10$, $\text{STE}=\text{STD}/\sqrt{n}=\text{STD}/3.162$. The label "provided as STD ± STE" [PAGE 14] is internally inconsistent; a GEQ component STD near the reported "±" values (e.g., 0.85 on a 0–4 scale) is plausible whereas an STE of 0.85 (implying STD ≈ 2.7 ≈ full scale) is not — so the "±" figure is almost certainly the STE. Reconstructing approximate unpaired $z$ (SEs taken as reported STEs):

| Component | Δ (ET−noET) | SE$_\Delta$ | $z$ | approx $p$ |

| Challenge | +0.98 | 0.276 | 3.55 | ≈0.0004 |
| Tension | +0.76 | 0.313 | 2.43 | ≈0.015 |

If within-subject scores are positively correlated, the paired SE is smaller and Challenge/Tension remain significant while Competence may cross $p<0.05$. The paper reports no inferential test, so these are reconstructions, not the authors' claims.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage is explicitly given. Eye tracking "dates from the XVIII century" [PAGE 1]; the first persistent-image descriptions trace to Wells (1792) and Javal's reading physiology (1879) [refs 1, 14, PAGE 18–19]. The first non-intrusive photographic + light-reflection measures are Dodge & Cline (1901) [ref 15, PAGE 2, 19] — the direct ancestor of modern pupil-corneal-reflection trackers like the Gazepoint GP3 used here [PAGE 4]. Real-time video-based tracking arrives "in the 1980s" [PAGE 2], enabling human–machine interaction.

The *contradiction lineage* is the scientifically important thread. Three prior FPS/gaze studies disagree: Smith & Graham found eye tracking "more immersive" [PAGE 2]; Isokoski & Martin found "performance with the eye tracker was quite inferior" but attributed it to unfamiliarity [PAGE 3]; Leyba & Malcolm found the mouse better under time pressure [PAGE 3]; Dorr et al. found "gaze beats mouse" [PAGE 3, ref 10]. The authors' synthesis — "the type of game and development method … are key elements" [PAGE 3] — is the move that justifies *not* using gaze as a direct pointer, and instead mapping attention.

The PCG lineage runs Shaker/Togelius/Nelson (2014) → Experience-Driven PCG (Yannakakis & Togelius 2011, ref 24) [PAGE 3], coupled to the fun≈challenge hypothesis [refs 25–31, PAGE 3]. This paper's contribution is to instantiate EDPCG with gaze as the driver.

*Multi-week trap (reproducibility):* the recurring methodological trap across this lineage — and the one this study partially falls into — is **uncontrolled familiarity/training asymmetry** (Isokoski's own caveat [PAGE 3]) and **single-session, small-n designs**. This study mitigates order effects by counterbalancing (ET-on block first, then ET-off [PAGE 12]) but does not randomize order across participants, leaving a residual learning confound: Table 1 shows kills rising 65.6%→79.0% across the three ET-on sessions [PAGE 14], so any ET-on-vs-ET-off contrast is entangled with practice. That is the trap the field keeps re-setting.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The paper's core is literally a theory-of-mind mapping: "a way of implementing a mapping between the player's and the avatar's attention processes" [PAGE 3], in contrast to "the typical use of eye tracking to control avatar's motor actions" [PAGE 3]. Formally, the system estimates the player's attentional state $\hat{a}_{\text{player}}(t)$ from gaze and imposes $\hat{a}_{\text{avatar}}(t) = \hat{a}_{\text{player}}(t)$: "An element noticed by the player is also noticed by the avatar" [PAGE 5].

*Crisis (habit regression):* under calibration failure — the dominant real-world mode [PAGE 13, 16] — the player regresses to a protective habit: "having to be as static as possible to avoid the eye tracking de-calibration" [PAGE 16, 3/10 participants]. This is a degenerate fixed point: the ToM mapping collapses because $\hat{a}_{\text{player}}$ is corrupted by a systematic offset between "where the eye tracker thought they were looking at and where they were actually looking at" [PAGE 16]. The visual-effect feedback, intended as reward, becomes a *punishment signal* precisely for these users (they "could see the discrepancy" [PAGE 16]) — a perverse recursive loop where the system's model of the player's attention is falsified in the player's own view.

*Counter-trap:* the designers anticipated the opposite failure (over-strict fixations causing false negatives) and chose the non-leaky accumulator with a *low* effective threshold to bias toward false positives [PAGE 8]. This is the correct counter-move given the asymmetric loss, but it is a fixed global constant — it cannot model individual differences in fixation dynamics, which is the "clock" below.

*Clock:* the temporal parameter is $\tau^\star = 0.5$ s [PAGE 8]. It is the single scalar governing the entire attention model, tuned "from a set of informal tests" [PAGE 8] and contested by a participant who found it "too long" [PAGE 16]. The authors' own prescription — "these timings should be learned for each player, taking into account the player-dependent uncertainty of the eye tracker" [PAGE 16] — is the honest resolution: $\tau^\star$ should be a per-player parameter $\tau^\star(\text{player})$, not a global constant.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical/biomechanical veto (proof).** The method's viability is bounded by three physical constraints, all documented:

1. **Angular error → spatial miss.** From operator (1), $\Delta_\perp = D\tan\varepsilon$. With $\varepsilon\in[0.5^\circ,1.0^\circ]$ [PAGE 4] and interaction at up to $D\sim10$ m, misses reach 8.7–17.5 cm. The bounding boxes "had to be enlarged so as to accommodate the inaccuracy" [PAGE 8]. *Veto condition:* if $\Delta_\perp > \tfrac{1}{2}(\text{box width})$, the raycast misfires; this is the biomechanical wall.

2. **Head-stability requirement.** "Three out of the ten participants complained about having to be as static as possible" [PAGE 16]. The 9-point calibration [PAGE 4] is a static-map assumption; head translation invalidates it. *Veto condition:* head motion beyond the calibrated volume $\Rightarrow$ systematic gaze offset $\Rightarrow$ persistent false notices/misses.

3. **Glasses.** "Two participants had to remove their glasses" [PAGE 13]; corneal-reflection trackers fail on lens refraction. *Veto condition:* corrective lenses $\Rightarrow$ specular corruption $\Rightarrow$ calibration non-convergence (some participants required multiple tries, [PAGE 13, Fig. 15]).

**"Weather" analog — lighting.** This paper has no atmospheric variable; the physical-environment analog is illumination: accuracy holds only "provided that … the testing environment offers proper lighting conditions" [PAGE 4]. *Veto condition:* ambient IR / low light degrades pupil detection.

**Adjudication.** No physical veto *falsifies* the concept, but all four define the operational envelope. The authors' empirical finding — "a strong correlation between problems that surfaced with the eye tracker calibration and participants' overall experience" [PAGE 17] — is the macro-scale signature of these micro-scale vetoes: satisfaction was gated by physical-layer success, not by the interaction concept.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto 1 — Accuracy-scaled threshold.** *Counterfactual:* if $\tau^\star$ is held global at 0.5 s while per-player jitter $\rho$ varies, high-jitter players accumulate spurious notices (false positives) and low-fixation players miss genuine ones. *Falsifiable prediction:* notice-precision will correlate negatively with each player's measured gaze-jitter variance, and $\tau^\star_{\text{optimal}}(\text{player})$ will vary materially across players — confirming the [PAGE 16] prescription. *Verification:* re-run with per-subject jitter logs; the current paper cannot test this (no per-subject gaze traces released).

**Veto 2 — Challenge/Tension localization.** *Counterfactual:* if ET works as claimed, its effect should concentrate in the difficulty axis (Challenge, Tension) and depress Competence, not uniformly lift all affect. *Prediction (already borne out):* reconstructed $z$ gives Challenge ≈ 3.55 and Tension ≈ 2.43 as the only components clearing $p<0.05$, with Competence dropping 2.8→2.22 [PAGE 15]. *Falsifier:* a properly powered **paired** test that renders Challenge/Tension non-significant would veto the "more challenging" claim. *Verification path:* paired $t$/Wilcoxon on the raw GEQ (not provided).

**Veto 3 — Feedback-reward vs immersion trade-off.** *Counterfactual:* if the visual "noticed" effects are removed, reward should drop but immersion rise. *Confirmed direction:* 9/10 valued the effects for feedback [PAGE 15]; 7/10 said without them the game is "more immersive and … more realistic" [PAGE 15]. *Falsifiable prediction:* a subtle, non-diegetic feedback channel (e.g., audio/haptic) will recover reward without the immersion penalty the authors flag as future work [PAGE 15–16]. *Verification:* A/B the effect modality and re-measure GEQ Immersion and post-game Positive experience (currently 1.27 vs 1.33, indistinguishable at $\Delta\approx0.05$ [PAGE 15]).

**Meta-veto.** Every quantitative claim above is bounded by $n=10$, all-male, self-selected, non-randomized order [PAGE 10, 12]. The authors acknowledge this: "a more intensive set of tests, enlarging the participant population" is required [PAGE 18]. Until then, all effect magnitudes are provisional and the Challenge/Tension result — the study's strongest — is suggestive, not confirmatory.
