The page sits at a crossroads in the history of distribution theory. Its immediate genealogy runs backward to John Bates Clark’s marginal productivity framework (*The Distribution of Wealth*, p. 374 note, cited on [PAGE 139]), to Frank Carver (*The Distribution of Wealth*, p. 85, cited on [PAGE 139]), and to Chester Davenport (*Economics of Enterprise*, chaps. XI and XXI, cited on [PAGE 139]). Clark’s two-factor analysis treats land and labor as distinct “factors” whose shares can be read from their marginal physical products; Knight rejects this because it presumes a measurable contribution where only a market price exists. Carver’s ditch-digger/steam-shopper/bookkeeper analogy supplies the equivalence intuition: economically, what matters is not physiology but substitutability in the production of value.

Deeper lineage. Ricardo and Mill posed the distribution problem as the division of output among land, labor, and capital; the marginalists (Jevons, Menger, Walras, Clark) attempted to solve it via marginal conditions. Knight’s page is a pre-emptive strike against the entire factor-imputation program: if agencies form a homogeneous fund within substitution limits, then the “factor” is an analytical fiction imposed by the theorist, not an observable boundary in production.

Forward lineage. The argument anticipates the capital-theory controversies of the 1950s–60s (Sraffa 1960; Robinson 1953–54), which showed that physical measures of capital cannot be ranked independently of distribution, and Arrow–Debreu (1954), which proves competitive equilibrium existence only under fixed endowments and complete markets—precisely the conditions Knight interrogates here. It also points toward the property-rights and incomplete-contracts tradition (Coase 1937; Grossman–Hart–Moore), where the boundaries of the firm and the allocation of residual control are determined by bargaining when no complete set of contingent markets exists.

Multi-week traps. A short-run, weekly planning model typically freezes factor proportions and treats each agency as a fixed input. Over a single week, the ditch-digger, steam shovel, and bookkeeper may indeed appear non-substitutable because contracts, shifts, and equipment are locked in. Over multiple weeks, however, the substitution margin reopens: firms can hire more steam shovels, retrain clerks, or subcontract excavation. A model that calibrates substitutability on weekly data and then projects it over months or years commits a temporal aggregation error: the “limits” of substitution \(\bar{\Delta}_{ij}\) are horizon-dependent. Conversely, a long-run model that assumes costless substitution ignores adjustment costs and the Knightian uncertainty about whether future demand will make the ditch-digger or the steam shovel the scarce agency. The trap is therefore recursive: short-run rigidity is mistaken for permanent indispensability; long-run fluidity is mistaken for costless reversibility.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory of mind here concerns how economic agents reason about one another’s bargaining behavior under the conditions Knight describes, and how that reasoning regresses under crisis.

Crisis habit regression. Under ordinary competitive conditions, agents treat agencies as substitutable and bid for them separately, forming beliefs about rivals’ bid functions \(b_k(\cdot)\). Under Knightian uncertainty—when the probability distribution over future demand or productivity is itself unknown—agents cannot compute expected marginal contributions \(\mathbb{E}[\partial F/\partial x_k]\). They therefore regress to habit: they treat currently employed agencies as if they were indispensable, freeze existing contracts, and refuse to re-bid. The mind reverts from strategic bidding to status-quo preservation because the computational problem of valuing a homogeneous fund under unknown limits is intractable.

Counter-trap. The opposite error is the neoclassical counter-trap: assuming that because agencies *can* substitute within limits, they *will* substitute smoothly in real time. This treats \(\bar{\Delta}_{ij}\) as a known constant and ignores that discovering the limit requires costly experimentation. A manager who assumes the ditch-digger can be replaced by a steam shovel at a moment’s notice, without accounting for site access, crew retraining, or capital lead times, will mis-price the agency and mis-estimate distributive shares. The counter-trap is to confuse the *possibility* of competitive organization (a theoretical condition on [PAGE 139]) with its *instantaneous realizability*.

Clock variable. Let \(T_{\text{dec}}\) be the decision horizon for hiring or reallocating an agency, measured in days, and let \(\tau_{\text{sub}}\) be the physical-institutional lag required to effect substitution (equipment delivery, retraining, contract renegotiation), also in days. The homogeneous-fund assumption is decision-relevant only if
\[
I_{\text{fund}} = \mathbf{1}\{T_{\text{dec}} \ge \tau_{\text{sub}}\}\times \mathbf{1}\{\sigma_{ij}>0\}\times \mathbf{1}\{\text{market for }a_k\text{ exists}\} = 1,
\]
where \(\sigma_{ij}\) is the elasticity of substitution. If \(T_{\text{dec}}<\tau_{\text{sub}}\), the agency is effectively indispensable for that decision, competitive bidding cannot operate in time, and distributive shares are determined by incumbent rights rather than by market equivalence. Under crisis, \(T_{\text{dec}}\) collapses (decisions must be made immediately), while \(\tau_{\text{sub}}\) may lengthen (supply-chain disruption), so \(I_{\text{fund}}\) flips to zero precisely when agents most need a coherent distribution rule.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The economic equivalence claimed on [PAGE 139] can be vetoed by physical, biomechanical, and weather constraints. If any layer vetoes equivalence, the homogeneous-fund assumption fails for that service bundle and distributive shares cannot be derived from competitive bidding alone.

Physical layer (energy/power). SI unit: watt (W) for power, joule (J) for work. A human ditch-digger’s sustained metabolic power is on the order of \(10^{2}\) W (roughly 75–100 W over an eight-hour shift); a steam shovel’s mechanical power is \(10^{4}\)–\(10^{5}\) W. They are not physically equivalent in work rate. The equivalence is purely economic: price per cubic metre of earth moved. Veto condition: if the required earth-moving rate \(P_{\text{req}}\) in W exceeds the sustainable human output \(P_{\text{human}}\approx10^{2}\) W, the human cannot substitute for the machine regardless of wage. Singularity: \(P_{\text{req}}/P_{\text{human}} > 1\) with no crew replication possible within site constraints.

Biomechanical layer. Human labor is bounded by the force–velocity curve and by fatigue. Let \(F_{\max}\) be maximal sustainable force (newtons) and \(v_{\max}\) maximal sustainable velocity (metres per second). Mechanical power \(P=Fv\) cannot exceed the aerobic ceiling \(P_{\text{aer}}\approx 300\) W for prolonged effort; beyond it, lactate accumulation forces recovery time \(t_{\text{rec}}\) in seconds, during which \(x_{\text{human}}\) drops to zero. Veto condition: continuous substitution hour-for-hour violates \(P\le P_{\text{aer}}\), producing a productivity collapse singularity at the metabolic limit.

Weather layer. Earthwork is exposed to precipitation and temperature; bookkeeping is not. Let \(\omega\in\Omega\) denote weather state. The productivity of ditch digging \(x_{\text{ditch}}(\omega)\) falls sharply under rain (\(\omega=\omega_{\text{rain}}\)) due to soil cohesion loss and equipment bogging, while bookkeeping \(x_{\text{book}}(\omega)\) is invariant. Economic equivalence \(x_{\text{ditch}}\sim x_{\text{book}}\) holds only in \(\Omega_{\text{clear}}\); it breaks down in \(\Omega_{\text{rain}}\). Veto condition: if the probability mass \(\Pr(\Omega_{\text{rain}})\) is non-negligible over the contract horizon, the expected service flows are not equivalent and no single price can clear both services across states without state-contingent contracts, which the page does not presuppose.

Adjudication proof. Define equivalence \(a_i\sim a_j\) iff all layers pass:
\[
a_i\sim a_j \iff \left(P_{\text{req}}\le P_i\land P_{\text{req}}\le P_j\right)\land \left(P_i,P_j\le P_{\text{aer}}\text{ or mechanical substitution}\right)\land \left(\forall\omega,\; x_i(\omega)=x_j(\omega)\right).
\]
On [PAGE 139], Knight’s abstraction suppresses the layer index \(\omega\), the power constraint \(P\), and the fatigue ceiling \(P_{\text{aer}}\). Therefore the homogeneous fund is a high-abstraction equilibrium object, not a physical primitive. When any veto fires, distributive shares must be renegotiated under the binding constraint rather than imputed from a contribution that does not exist at that layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
