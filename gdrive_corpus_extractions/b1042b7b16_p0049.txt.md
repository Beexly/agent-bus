<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage of this intensity-score architecture runs through several distinct historical layers. First is the heart-rate-reserve formalism itself: normalizing HR by \(HR_{\max}\) and \(HR_{\rest}\) descends from the Karvonen method (1957) for prescribing exercise intensity as a percentage of HRR, which in turn underlies the percentage-of-MHR heart-rate zones mentioned on [PAGE 26]. Second is the impulse-response training-load tradition, exemplified by Banister’s TRIMP model, in which training impulse accumulates exponentially weighted stimuli with separate fast and slow fatigue/fitness decay constants; the patent’s iterative, path-dependent weighting is a simplified, non-convolutional analog of that state-space logic. Third is the session-RPE method of Foster et al. (1998), which multiplies duration by perceived exertion; here the patent inverts part of that logic by using perceived exertion as a training label to learn HRR weights via logistic regression rather than as a direct multiplier.

The biochemical layer traces to classical exercise physiology: aerobic oxidative phosphorylation yielding on the order of 30–32 ATP per glucose in modern accounting (the patent cites the older textbook figure of 36 [PAGE 25]), anaerobic glycolysis yielding 2 ATP per glucose at the anaerobic threshold, and the immediate phosphocreatine/CP system at higher intensities—referred to in the source as “creatine triphosphate (CTP),” likely a nomenclature slip for creatine phosphate. The multi-week trap embedded in this genealogy is threshold drift: \(AT\) and \(CPT\) are not fixed physiological constants; they shift with training status, detraining, heat acclimation, and illness. A static weighting scheme calibrated in week 1 becomes systematically miscalibrated by week 6 if thresholds are not re-estimated, causing the intensity score to over- or under-report cardiovascular load relative to actual metabolic strain. A second multi-week trap is the delayed soreness feedback loop: because delayed-onset muscle soreness peaks 24–72 hours post-exercise, an “automatically adjusted” regimen that reacts to next-day soreness is controlling on stale data, risking either excessive accumulation if the response is too weak or unnecessary detraining if the response is too aggressive.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The user–system interaction forms a recursive loop with three identifiable regimes: crisis habit regression, counter-trap overcorrection, and clock-scale mismatch. In crisis habit regression, the system detects consistently high intensity scores and, per [PAGE 25], may recommend that “the exercise regimen of the user should be automatically adjusted (e.g., made easier).” A user with a high-intensity identity regresses to the crisis habit of ignoring the prescription and maintaining or increasing load. The system then ingests perceived-exertion labels that no longer match the recommended intensity, corrupting the logistic-regression training labels and causing coefficient drift: the model learns from behavior it did not cause but now indirectly reinforces.

The counter-trap is the opposite regression: the user over-complies, systematically selecting easier sessions than warranted, causing intensity scores to collapse. The adaptive weighting scheme, interpreting low scores as low capacity, further lowers prescribed intensity or threshold assumptions, producing a detraining spiral in which fitness declines because the load prescription chases the user downward rather than leading upward.

The clock variable is the mismatch among three time scales: real-time HRR display (seconds), next-day soreness feedback (≈24–48 hours), and chronic fitness adaptation (weeks). A controller with high proportional gain on the delayed soreness signal but no model of the week-scale adaptation delay will oscillate: it prescribes easier workouts after soreness reports, then harder workouts when scores drop, producing a sawtooth load pattern rather than periodized progression. Because the user observes the displayed intensity score and modifies exertion in response, the system is also performative: the distribution of HRR that the model was trained on shifts as users game or suppress effort to manage the displayed number, invalidating the stationarity assumption of the logistic weighting layer.
