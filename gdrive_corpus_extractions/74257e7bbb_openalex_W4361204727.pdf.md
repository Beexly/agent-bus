<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three recursive layers govern system behavior under stress.

Layer 1 — Crisis habit regression. Under deadline pressure or rare edge cases, a computer vision system regresses from robust deep inference to brittle local shortcuts. The source explicitly concedes that CV "cannot be expected to replicate the human eye perfectly" [PAGE 1] and that performance is limited by parameter sensitivity and algorithm strength [PAGE 2]. Because end-to-end learning removes explicit feature engineering [PAGE 3], there is no interpretable symbolic fallback when the CNN encounters an out-of-distribution input; the network instead amplifies whichever spurious texture, color, or background correlation dominated training, producing a confident but wrong classification.

Layer 2 — Counter-trap. An adaptive environment or adversary exploits the regressed habit. In sports analytics [PAGE 3], if a tracking system has learned to rely on jersey color or court markings, an opponent changing uniforms, lighting, or camera angle triggers systematic identity switches. In surveillance or autonomous driving, adversarial illumination, camouflage, or occlusion converts the system’s own prior into an exploit surface. The counter-trap is recursive: regression creates a predictable failure mode; the environment discovers and amplifies it; the system, lacking a meta-cognitive layer [PAGE 4 open problem], cannot detect that it is being gamed.

Layer 3 — Clock. Real-time processing imposes a hard deadline [PAGE 4]. Let frame period be T_f = 1/fps and inference latency be T_inf(R,C), a superlinear function of resolution R and model capacity C. Stability requires T_inf < T_f. As accuracy demands rise, R and C grow, T_inf increases nonlinearly, and the clock forces either frame drops (temporal aliasing) or resolution reduction (feature loss). This creates the recursion: pressure → habit regression → counter-trap exposure → clock violation → total collapse. The source mentions real-time processing and edge hardware [PAGE 4] but provides no latency budget, no frame-rate/accuracy trade-off curve, and no interrupt policy for when T_inf exceeds T_f, leaving the clock layer formally unspecified.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three physical veto layers operate independently of algorithmic sophistication and are entirely absent from the source.

Physical (sensor/radiometric) layer. Image formation is photon-limited. Photoelectron count N_pe = η L A t_e, where η is quantum efficiency, L is scene radiance, A is aperture area, and t_e is exposure time. Shot noise σ_shot = √N_pe imposes SNR = √N_pe. If SNR falls below a detection threshold τ_det, no CNN depth [PAGE 3] can recover the object. Weather attenuation follows Beer–Lambert: I(d) = I_0 exp(−βd), with extinction coefficient β in m⁻¹ and range d in m. Heavy fog (β ≈ 0.05–0.2 m⁻¹) or rain reduces contrast exponentially; beyond d_max = ln(C_min)/β the target is below the minimum usable contrast C_min. The source mentions only generic "cameras, sensors or other devices" [PAGE 2] with no radiometric parameters, so all-weather or outdoor claims are vetoed absent a photon budget.

Biomechanical layer. Moving objects induce motion blur b = v · t_e pixels, where v is angular velocity projected to the image plane. In sports footage [PAGE 3], limb velocities can exceed 100°/s; at t_e = 1 ms this produces blur larger than SIFT/SURF keypoint localization tolerance [PAGE 2], breaking feature matching. Self-occlusion in articulated poses violates the segmentation disjointness assumption ∪ R_i = Ω, R_i ∩ R_j = ∅ [PAGE 2], causing region mergers that no post-processing can undo without a 3D body prior.

Weather veto. Precipitation adds forward-scatter noise n_precip, reducing effective contrast-to-noise ratio CNR = |I_obj − I_bg| / √(σ_n² + σ_precip²). Veto condition: CNR < 3 ⇒ detection recall collapses regardless of architecture [PAGE 3 deep-learning claims notwithstanding].

Source gap: The paper discusses self-driving cars [PAGE 3], robotics [PAGE 3], and surveillance [PAGE 3] without modeling weather, biomechanics, or sensor physics. Any conclusion about reliable operation in uncontrolled physical environments is therefore formally unsupported and vetoed at the cross-layer level unless β, t_e, v, η, and CNR are specified and shown to exceed detection thresholds.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
