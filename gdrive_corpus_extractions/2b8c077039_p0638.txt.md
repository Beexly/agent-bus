Validity requires S to be a complete mediator: M \perp E \mid S. Units: dimensionless probability. Breakdown: if occipital or frontal lobe removal severs S (e.g., visual or affective channels), then P(S=s \mid do(E)) = \delta(s-s_0) and \mathcal{F} is singular [PAGE 628].

Back-door operator \mathcal{B}: agents affecting the brain directly [PAGE 628],
\[
P(M \mid do(E)) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}} P(M \mid B=b, do(E))\,P(B=b \mid do(E))\,db.
\]
Here E may bypass S entirely. If B is manipulated surgically (bilateral occipital ablation) or pharmacologically (alcohol), P(B \mid do(E)) shifts independently of P(S \mid do(E)).

Causal graph and conditional independence test: front-door universality asserts E \rightarrow S \rightarrow M with no direct E \rightarrow B edge. The Goltz–Loeb dog footnote on [PAGE 628] establishes E_{surg} \rightarrow B \rightarrow M without an S-mediated kindness history. The drunkenness footnote establishes an ancestral confounder U (evolutionary history) such that U \rightarrow B and U \rightarrow M (susceptibility to alcohol) while E_{alcohol} \rightarrow B \rightarrow M via molecular receptors; the "outer relation" of alcohol is not the cause of the susceptibility itself [PAGE 628].

Exact verbatim citations [PAGE 628]:
- "The way of 'experience' proper is the front door, the door of the five senses."
- "restrict the word 'experience' to processes which influence the mind by the front-door-way of simple habits and association."
- "dogs become mild in character when their occipital, and fierce when their frontal, brain-lobes are cut off."
- "Each one of them made him more good-natured."
- "The love of drunkenness is a purely accidental susceptibility of a brain, evolved for entirely different uses, and its causes are to be sought in the molecular realm, rather than in any possible order of 'outer relations.'"

Breakdown regimes:
1. Sensory occlusion singularity: occipital ablation sets P(S_{visual} \mid do(E)) = 0; front-door identification of visual experience on behavior is undefined.
2. Back-door clamp saturation: B fixed by surgery or intoxication renders \mathcal{B} insensitive to variations in E that do not alter B.
3. Convergence regime: \mathcal{F}(E_{train}) \approx \mathcal{B}(E_{surg}) in M-space, producing observational equivalence that vetoes causal inference from outcome alone.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The front-door/back-door distinction sits at the intersection of two historical lineages. The front door descends from British associationism—Locke's sensory empiricism, Hartley's vibrations and associations, and James Mill's analysis of the mind as a product of experience—where mental contents are built from simple ideas entering through the senses and binding by contiguity and repetition. James inherits this architecture but explicitly restricts the term "experience" to that sensory-associative channel [PAGE 628], thereby excluding innate aptitude and constitutional brain state from the definition.

The back door descends from experimental cerebral localization: Flourens's ablation method, Broca's clinicopathological correlations, and, as James cites on [PAGE 628], Goltz and Loeb's dog lobectomy studies. These researchers demonstrated that character (mildness vs. fierceness) could be altered by cutting brain lobes without any corresponding change in the animal's sensory experience of outer relations. James treats this as a challenge to associationist monism: if removing occipital tissue makes a dog trustful, then "experience" cannot be the sole educator of good-nature, because the cause was structural, not associative [PAGE 628].

James also anticipates a modern evolutionary mismatch argument. Alcohol, he notes, "came into the environment but yesterday," yet the cash-value of its sales in the United States rivals that of meat and bread combined [PAGE 628]. He rejects the idea that an ancestral "outer relation" produced the love of drunkenness; instead, the susceptibility is accidental, exapted, and molecular. This is a retro-causal genealogy: the present mental effect (drunkenness craving) is explained not by a history of outer relations but by the absence of ancestral selection against a novel chemical agonist acting on a brain evolved for other uses.

Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy:
1. The outcome-equivalence trap: a dog made docile by weeks of kindness (front-door, \tau_H \sim weeks) and a dog made docile by occipital surgery (back-door, \tau_B \sim hours) are behaviorally indistinguishable at week four. An observer who records only the endpoint cannot recover the intervention time or causal class without an experimental log.
2. The sobriety trap: weeks of alcohol abstinence may extinguish front-door associative cues (bars, drinking companions, ritual contexts), but James's molecular susceptibility remains latent. Reintroduction of alcohol can trigger immediate relapse, proving that the back-door cause outlasts the front-door cue window.
3. The skill-crystallization trap: the natural genius's drawing ability may reflect maturational back-door cycles, while the plodder's reflects slow front-door habit accumulation. At the moment both display equal skill, the causal histories are wholly disparate [PAGE 628], but a cross-sectional observer cannot separate them without longitudinal intervention data.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
A recursive theory of mind must model not only what another agent believes, but which causal layer the agent is using to generate behavior. Crisis habit regression occurs when acute stress or resource depletion compromises higher-order, back-door executive control, causing the agent to regress to front-door habits—simple associations built through repeated S \rightarrow H pairings [PAGE 628]. Formally, habit strength H follows a first-order consolidation dynamics,
\[
\frac{dH}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau_H}\bigl(H - H_{\infty}(S)\bigr) + \xi(t),
\]
where H is dimensionless, \tau_H is measured in seconds (typically O(10^5\ \mathrm{s}) for durable habits), H_{\infty}(S) is the sensory-driven asymptote, and \xi(t) is a crisis noise term with units of bits\cdot s^{-1}. During a crisis, |\xi| dominates and \tau_H is effectively stretched; H regresses toward baseline associations rather than reflecting deliberate, back-door model-based control.

The counter-trap is the false inference that equal behavioral output implies equal mental history. James explicitly blocks this: two draftsmen of equal skill do not "alike owe their skill to their 'experience'" because their causes lie in "wholly disparate natural cycles of causation" [PAGE 628]. In recursive terms, observer O forms a belief B_O(C_A = C_B \mid M_A = M_B). James asserts this belief is generally false: P(C_A \mid M) \neq P(C_B \mid M) when C_A is natural/back-door and C_B is plodding/front-door. The trap is to model the other mind as a single-layer experiential system when it is in fact a mixed causal graph.

The clock variable imposes timescale separation. If the observation window \Delta t \ll \tau_H, then front-door habit formation cannot explain a rapid behavioral shift; a back-door cause (lesion, intoxication, acute neuromodulator shift) must be invoked. Conversely, if \Delta t \gg \tau_B but \Delta t \sim \tau_H, slow associative drift becomes visible. A recursive agent that ignores this clock will misattribute acute back-door changes to long-term character and chronic front-door habits to situational states.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto proof requires showing that a claim—here, that mental change is explained by front-door "experience"—fails under manipulation at any one of three independent layers: physical, biomechanical, or environmental (weather/context).

Physical layer veto: On [PAGE 628], James describes bilateral occipital lobe operations that made a fierce dog "perfectly trustful and harmless," with each operation increasing good-nature. This is a direct physical manipulation of B (brain structure) producing M (docility) without any front-door sensory-associative history of kindness. If the front-door model were complete, P(M \mid do(E_{kindness})) should track P(M \mid do(E_{surg})) only through S; but S is not the mediator here. The physical layer vetoes the sufficiency of experience: a change in B alone changes M.

Biomechanical layer veto: Equal drawing skill between natural genius and plodder [PAGE 628] masks biomechanical divergence. Let K denote the kinematic output (line trajectory, pressure, timing). K_{genius} = K_{plodder} is possible while the motor-command generators G differ: G_{genius} may rely on innate predictive forward models, while G_{plodder} relies on accumulated sensory-error correction. Because K alone does not identify G, any theory that explains skill solely as front-door habit association is biomechanically underdetermined and therefore vetoed unless it can dissociate the generators under perturbation (e.g., delayed visual feedback).

Weather/environmental layer veto: James notes the massive cash-value of alcohol sales in the United States [PAGE 628], indicating a strong environmental expression of drunkenness. Yet he locates the cause of the susceptibility in the molecular realm, not outer relations. An environmental veto—such as removing alcohol availability (prohibition, weather-driven scarcity, or social context change)—can suppress the expression of M (drunkenness) without eliminating the underlying B susceptibility. Re-exposure after a weather-imposed or policy-imposed gap restores M immediately, demonstrating that the back-door vulnerability persists independently of the front-door associative context. If M vanished permanently after environmental removal, the front-door model would be supported; persistence under re-exposure vetoes it.

Composite veto rule: the universal claim "mental change is experience" is falsified if (i) physical manipulation of B changes M holding S constant; (ii) biomechanical equivalence K(A)=K(B) coexists with causal-generator divergence G(A)\neq G(B); or (iii) environmental removal suppresses M expression while latent B susceptibility remains, with rapid recovery upon re-exposure. James's [PAGE 628] cases satisfy all three.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a condition, an intervention, the vetoed front-door-universal prediction, the Jamesian back-door alternative, and the verification metric. No betting lines, spreads, totals, or odds are referenced.

Scenario 1: Sensory-channel occlusion in matched drawing skill.
Condition: Two artists, A (natural genius, minimal training) and B (plodder, years of studio habit), matched on baseline drawing output K. Intervention: impose reversible binocular occlusion or a fixed 500 ms visual-feedback delay during drawing for both. Vetoed prediction (front-door universalism): because all skill is experience/association, both artists degrade identically; mutual information I(gaze; hand) drops by the same amount. Jamesian alternative: B's performance collapses because his skill depends on the front-door S \rightarrow H loop; A's degrades less or along different error dimensions because natural cycles support predictive motor control less dependent on continuous sensory association. Verification: measure line-error variance \sigma^2_{line} (units: mm^2) and I(gaze; hand) (bits) before and after occlusion. If the drop in I(gaze; hand) is statistically identical for A and B across 200 trials, James's distinction is vetoed for this domain; if B's drop exceeds A's by a large effect size, the front-door restriction is supported.

Scenario 2: Ablation versus training in canine docility.
Condition: Two cohorts of dogs matched for baseline aggression. Cohort S receives bilateral occipital lobectomy (per the Loeb protocol cited on [PAGE 628]); Cohort T receives intensive kind training with intact brains. Intervention: after behavior stabilizes, introduce a standardized frustration probe (removal of a guarded resource) daily for 30 days. Vetoed prediction: both cohorts show identical docility curves because "experience" educates good-nature equally regardless of cause. Jamesian alternative: Cohort S shows immediate, context-invariant docility (back-door structural change); Cohort T shows context-dependent docility with rising aggression under repeated frustration probes (front-door habit extinction). Verification: record latency-to-growl and bite probability per probe; fit a Cox proportional-hazards model for aggression onset. If the hazard functions are indistinguishable across all 30 probes (hazard ratio \approx 1 with narrow confidence interval), the back-door/front-door distinction is vetoed; if Cohort S maintains significantly lower hazard, the physical veto stands.

Scenario 3: Molecular susceptibility after enforced abstinence.
Condition: A human population with high self-reported susceptibility to drunkenness, matched on age, sex, and baseline consumption. Intervention: enforce four weeks (2.4 \times 10^6 s) of zero alcohol exposure, removing front-door contextual cues (bars, drinking rituals, purchase environments). Then re-expose participants to an alcohol odor/taste cue without ingestion. Vetoed prediction: after four weeks, front-door associative cues are extinguished; cue exposure elicits no craving and no autonomic response. Jamesian alternative: the susceptibility is molecular and ancestral, not relational; cue exposure after abstinence still elicits craving (visual analog scale, 0–100 mm) and autonomic response (heart-rate variability change, ms^2; cortisol, nmol\cdot L^{-1}) because the cause lies in the brain's molecular reactivity, not in recent outer relations. Verification: compare pre-abstinence and post-abstinence cue reactivity. If post-abstinence cue response falls to baseline zero and remains zero, the molecular claim is weakened; if craving and autonomic response persist at >50% of pre-abstinence magnitude, the front-door habit model is vetoed and the back-door susceptibility confirmed.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
