Breakdown / singularity regimes:
(S1) Knightian uncertainty: the set of possible changes is not exhaustively known, so \(\Delta_t \notin I_{t-1}\). Probability is replaced by a set \(\mathcal{M}\) of measures, and the decision criterion becomes maxmin expected utility \(\min_{Q \in \mathcal{M}} \mathbb{E}_Q[\pi_t]\), measured in CU under ambiguity.
(S2) Reflexive endogeneity: \(\Delta_t = g(\text{forecast of } \Delta_t)\), violating (A5). Capitalization of anticipated land value changes land scarcity, so the price and cost impacts are no longer symmetric.
(S3) Incomplete markets or non-convex production: the discounting operator cannot fully separate cost and value impacts, so a separation between "costs" and "values" re-emerges, contrary to the [PAGE 36] claim that "no separation between the two will take place."
(S4) Heterogeneous information: if \(I_{t-1}^{(i)} \neq I_{t-1}^{(j)}\) across agents, no single price vector can discount all forecasts simultaneously.

The verbatim anchor from [PAGE 36] is: "No a priori argument is necessary to prove that with general foreknowledge of progressive changes no losses and no chance to make profits will arise out of them." The complementary boundary condition is: "It is fallacious to define "natural" conditions as "static"conditions." The limiting-case concession is: "The assumption that all change might be predictable is contrary to fact, but not self-contradictory."
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage begins with John Bates Clark, whom Knight invokes on [PAGE 36] as defining a "dynamic" society distinct from a static one. Clark's framework treated dynamic capital and population growth as engines of progress but did not fully isolate the profit effect from the foreknowledge effect. Knight's 1921 contribution, as previewed on [PAGE 36], was to argue that general foreknowledge of progressive changes eliminates profit, thereby forcing the theory of profit onto the ground of unforeseen change. This anticipates Keynes's (1937) treatment of genuine uncertainty as distinct from calculable risk, Schumpeter's emphasis on entrepreneurial innovation as discontinuous and unforeseeable, and Hayek's argument that dispersed, tacit knowledge cannot be centralized into a single forecasting information set.

The modern descendants include Ellsberg's (1961) ambiguity experiments, Gilboa and Schmeidler's (1989) maxmin expected utility over a set of priors, and Hansen-Sargent robust control, all of which formalize the [PAGE 36] gray zone in which macro-level invention probability is known while micro-level specifics are not. Info-gap theory further operationalizes the breakdown when the support of the distribution shifts endogenously.

Multi-week traps arise when agents operate on planning horizons of weeks and treat observed price stability (\(P = C\)) as evidence of a static natural equilibrium. If a progressive change—such as a known seasonal shift in resource availability—is fully discounted over a multi-week horizon, inventories and contracts adjust smoothly. The trap springs when an unforecasted micro-outcome (a specific invention, an unexpected resource pocket, a weather-driven crop failure) occurs within that window. Because the agent's model assumed the macro-pattern was fully predictable and therefore profitless, no contingency reserve exists; the realized deviation from natural price generates losses or windfall profits that the [PAGE 36] theory had ruled out under general foreknowledge. The trap is multi-week because the mispricing persists until the physical or informational shock propagates through the supply chain, typically over several weekly cycles.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when agents, under stress, abandon the effort to model endogeneity and reflexively revert to the [PAGE 36] heuristic that prices equal costs and all foreseen change is already discounted. The agent assumes not only that \(P_t = C_t\), but also that all other agents share this belief. This first-order belief is cognitively cheap but brittle.

The counter-trap is the second-order recognition that others are regressing to the natural-price heuristic. An agent who expects counterparties to underprice an endogenous shift can position to capture the residual when the discounting fails. This is speculative counter-positioning in Knight's sense: profiting from the gap between the assumed discounting and the actual inability of the market to foresee a particular change.

The clock is the discounting horizon \(\tau\) over which foreknowledge is impounded. If the actual latency of change \(L\) exceeds \(\tau\) (\(L > \tau\)), the price at \(t\) cannot reflect the change at \(t+L\), and the zero-profit condition fails. Recursive theory of mind enters through the belief hierarchy \(B_i^k\), where agent \(i\) holds a \(k\)-th order belief about agent \(j\)'s belief about the change process. The [PAGE 36] equilibrium requires common knowledge at the limit:

\[
B_i^\infty(\Delta_t \mid I_{t-1}) \;=\; \Delta_t \quad \forall i,j.
\]

The recursion breaks precisely where Knight locates the boundary: when "knowledge has no effect on the changes themselves" is false. If agent \(i\)'s forecast alters agent \(j\)'s investment, which in turn changes \(\Delta_t\), then \(B_i^k \neq B_j^k\) for some finite \(k\), and the common-knowledge fixed point dissolves. Crisis habit regression collapses the hierarchy to \(k=0\) (everyone assumes natural prices); the counter-trap operates at \(k=1\) or \(k=2\); and the clock determines whether the hierarchy has time to reconcile before the change arrives.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: [PAGE 36] notes that discovery of natural resources can be predicted in the aggregate without knowing where they will be found, and that inventions can be anticipated without knowing their specifications. These are statements about a probability distribution over a support whose physical realization is bounded by geology, thermodynamics, and materials science. Economic discounting cannot override the conservation of mass, the finite rate of mineral deposition, or the energy required to extract a resource. Let \(V_{\text{phys}} \in \{0,1\}\) be the physical veto; if \(V_{\text{phys}}=1\), no price-cost identity can hold because the physical input is infeasible at the forecasted cost.

Biomechanical layer veto: Human activity—capital accumulation, population growth, inventive effort—is bounded by metabolic energy, sleep cycles, cognitive bandwidth, and ergonomic limits. A forecast of labor-force expansion or accelerated invention rates is vetoed if it exceeds sustainable caloric output or neural processing limits. Let \(V_{\text{bio}} \in \{0,1\}\) denote this bound.

Weather layer veto: Agricultural output, resource accessibility, and the broader development of wants are contingent on weather events (frost, flood, drought). Knight's "progressive changes" in wants can be overridden by a crop failure that makes the forecasted consumption bundle physically unavailable. Let \(V_{\text{weather}} \in \{0,1\}\) denote this veto.

Composite adjudication: Define \(V_t = \max(V_{\text{phys}}, V_{\text{bio}}, V_{\text{weather}})\). The realized profit operator becomes

\[
\pi_t^{\text{realized}} = (1 - V_t) \cdot \bigl(P_t - C_t\bigr) + V_t \cdot \pi_t^{\text{Knightian}},
\]

where \(\pi_t^{\text{Knightian}}\) is indeterminate under a set of priors \(\mathcal{M}\) because the physical layer has invalidated the discounting equilibrium. Whenever \(V_t = 1\), the [PAGE 36] assertion that foreseen change produces no profit or loss is void regardless of how complete the foreknowledge was. The veto proof is constructive: if any physical, biomechanical, or weather constraint binds, the cost vector \(C_t\) cannot be produced at the forecasted level, so \(P_t = C_t\) fails and an entrepreneurial residual emerges endogenously.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Removal of general foreknowledge. Counterfactual intervention: suppress public knowledge of an anticipated increase in land value while keeping the physical change (land improvement) unchanged. Verifiable prediction: Because \(\Delta_t \notin I_{t-1}\), the Discounting Principle from [PAGE 36] no longer applies; \(P_t\) will not equal \(C_t\) ex ante, and ex post entrepreneurial profit \(\pi_t \neq 0\) will be observed in land transactions. Verification: compare ex-ante appraised values and realized rents in parcels where the improvement was publicly announced versus parcels where it was not, controlling for physical quality. Prediction: the unannounced parcels exhibit larger price-to-cost dispersion.

Scenario 2: Exogenization of change. Counterfactual intervention: hold the change process fixed but strip it of its human-caused, endogenous character (replace an anticipated invention with an equivalent natural discovery that no agent forecast). Verifiable prediction: Even though the macro-pattern "resources will be discovered" was probabilistically known on [PAGE 36], the specific unforecasted discovery violates \( \Delta_t \in I_{t-1} \), generating a temporary price spike above cost. Verification: use a natural experiment such as an unexpected mineral strike; measure the deviation of market price from production cost in the weeks following the strike before arbitrage compresses the margin. Prediction: price exceeds cost by an amount proportional to the surprise magnitude and inversely proportional to market depth.

Scenario 3: Introduction of Knightian ambiguity. Counterfactual intervention: replace the single predictive distribution over inventions and process improvements with a set of measures \(\mathcal{M}\) reflecting ignorance of the support (we know inventions will occur, but not which or when), while keeping the mean forecast unchanged. Verifiable prediction: Agents will not price at the expected natural cost; instead they will apply an ambiguity premium, so \(P_t > C_t\) even when the expected change is known. Verification: laboratory market with two assets, one with known outcome distribution and one with ambiguous support but identical mean; observe bid-ask prices and realized trader profits. Prediction: the ambiguous asset trades at a price discount (or cost premium) relative to the unambiguous asset, and the dispersion of trader earnings is strictly larger under ambiguity.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
