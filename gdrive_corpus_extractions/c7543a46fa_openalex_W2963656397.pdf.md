**Notation and units.** Image coordinates $(u,v)$ in pixels; focal length $f$ in pixels; principal point $(c_x,c_y)$ in pixels; MegaDepth output $MD(u,v)$ dimensionless (relative); scale factor $s$ in length-units per relative-depth-unit (so $z$ is in length units, e.g. metres, if $s$ is m/unit); areas $a, a_i, a'$ in px² (or any consistent area unit); perimeters $p_j$ in px; RANSAC tolerance $\tau$ in the same length units as the point cloud; $\alpha \in (0,1)$ dimensionless.

**1. Segmentation Quality Score** [PAGE 5, Eqn. 1]:
$$SQS = S_{cp}\,S_{cl}\,S_{sp}, \qquad SQS_{\min} = 1.$$

*Component score* [PAGE 6, Eqn. 2]:
$$S_{cp} = \frac{a}{\max_i a_i} \ \ge 1,$$
with $a$ the total crowd-region area and $a_i$ the area of the $i$-th connected component. Equality iff the region is a single component. Verbatim: "This value will always be larger than or equal to 1 (which will be the case when there is only one major component)" [PAGE 6].

*Completeness score* [PAGE 6, Eqn. 3]:
$$S_{cl} = \frac{a'}{a} \ \ge 1,$$
$a'$ = area with all holes filled. Equality iff hole-free.

*Shape score* [PAGE 6, Eqn. 4]:
$$S_{sp} = \frac{p_j}{2\sqrt{\pi\, a_j}} \ \ge 1,$$
where $p_j, a_j$ are perimeter and area of the largest connected component. The floor is the isoperimetric inequality $p^2 \ge 4\pi a$, with equality iff the region is a disc — matching the paper's own gloss: "a factor of $2\sqrt{\pi}$ so that the minimum value of $S_{sp}$ is 1 (when the shape is circular)" [PAGE 6]. $S_{sp}$ is the reciprocal of the normalized circularity.

*Scaling invariance.* Under a dilation of the binary mask by factor $k$: $p \to kp$, $a \to k^2 a$, hence $S_{sp} \to kp/(2\sqrt{\pi k^2 a}) = S_{sp}$; $S_{cp}$ and $S_{cl}$ are area ratios and trivially invariant. **SQS is exactly scale-invariant in the mask.** It is *not* invariant across the segmentation network that produces the mask — see the source's own admission: "Accurate segmentation of our target texture, crowd, is sensitive to scale and lighting" [PAGE 14].

*Elasticities.* $\mathrm{d}SQS/SQS = \mathrm{d}S_{cp}/S_{cp} + \mathrm{d}S_{cl}/S_{cl} + \mathrm{d}S_{sp}/S_{sp}$; all three elasticities equal 1. Since $S_{cp},S_{cl} \to 1$ while $S_{sp}$ is unbounded, the log-variance of SQS is dominated by shape.

*Selection rule* [PAGE 6]:
$$F^{*} = \arg\min_{f \in \mathcal{F}} \; SQS_f \quad \text{subject to} \quad a_f \ \ge\ \tfrac{1}{2}\max_{g\in\mathcal{F}} a_g .$$
Breakdown regime: $\mathcal{F}$ collapses to a single frame when all sampled areas are within a factor 2; the 1 Hz sampling rate [PAGE 5] makes this likely for short clips.

**2. Pinhole back-projection** [PAGE 8, Eqns. 5–7]:
$$x = c_x + (u - c_x)\frac{z}{f},\qquad y = c_y + (v - c_y)\frac{z}{f},\qquad z = s\,MD(u,v).$$
> "To convert the relative depth values ($MD(u, v)$) estimated by MegaDepth to absolute depth values ($z$), we multiply by an empirically determined scale factor $s$." [PAGE 8]

Transcription defect: the printed Eqn. 6 carries $(u - c_y)$ in the ordinate; the correct form requires $(v - c_y)$. The convention is stated verbatim: "We assume that the optical center of the camera $(c_x, c_y)$ is at the center of the image and let the world coordinate system origin $(0, 0, 0)$ be located at the image coordinates origin $(0, 0)$" [PAGE 8]. Under this convention the optical centre sits at $(c_x, c_y, 0)$ in world coordinates.

*Gauge symmetry.* The map $s \mapsto \lambda s$ induces $X \mapsto (c_x,c_y,0) + \lambda\,[X - (c_x,c_y,0)]$, a homothety. Homotheties preserve incidence, angles, and coplanarity, so **the final homography and hence the composited image are invariant under $s \to \lambda s$ provided the inferred inlier set is unchanged.** The RANSAC test $|n^\top X + d| < \tau$ breaks this: the effective tolerance is $\tau_{\text{eff}} = \tau/\lambda$. With $\tau = 10^{4}$ and $s = 10^{6}$ [PAGE 13, Fig. 8 caption], $\tau_{\text{eff}} = 10^{-2}$; at $s = 10^{3}$, $\tau_{\text{eff}} = 10^{1}$ — a $10^{3}$-fold change in geometric stringency. **Breakdown regime:** the pipeline is gauge-invariant only while $\tau/s$ is small compared with the crowd surface's out-of-plane relief $\delta$.

**3. Focal length from orthogonal vanishing points** [PAGE 7–8, citing Li et al. [17]]:
$$f^{2} = -\,(v_1 - c)\cdot(v_2 - c), \qquad c = (c_x,c_y).$$
Breakdown regime, stated in-source: "The focal length estimation method (detailed in Sec. 3.2) was designed for images that contain clear orthogonal axes, such as present in a Manhattan world" [PAGE 12]. Singular when $v_1 \to v_2$ (parallel line families) or when the detected lines are dominated by the curved bowl rather than a rectilinear structure.

**4. Alignment-plane construction** [PAGE 9–10]. Plane normal from three back-projected points:
$$n = (X_2 - X_1)\times(X_3 - X_1).$$
The source sets $z_1 = z_2 = 0$: "We assume that points $P_1$ and $P_2$ in our image have depths of 0, and point $P_3$ has an arbitrary (non-zero) depth of $z$" [PAGE 10]. Substituting into Eqns. 5–6 gives $X_1 = X_2 = (c_x, c_y, 0)$, hence $\|n\| = 0$ and the normal direction is undefined. **Exact singularity:** the construction is well-posed only when $z_1 \ne z_2$, i.e.
$$\big\|(X_2 - X_1)\times(X_3 - X_1)\big\| \ >\ \varepsilon .$$
The alignment vector is $v = \Pi_{\text{crowd}} \cap \Pi_{\text{align}}$, so the singularity propagates directly into the asset's in-plane roll angle.

**5. Plane-induced homography** [PAGE 10, citing Hartley & Zisserman [15]]:
$$H = K\Big(R + \tfrac{1}{d}\,t\,n_{\Pi}^{\top}\Big)K^{-1}, \qquad K = \begin{pmatrix} f & 0 & c_x \\ 0 & f & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}.$$
Breakdown regimes: (a) $d \to 0$ (plane passes through the optical centre) — $H$ diverges; (b) the surface is non-planar. For a crowd point offset by $\delta$ from $\Pi$ along $n_\Pi$, the residual between true projection and homography-warped projection is, to first order,
$$\varepsilon_{p} \ \approx\ \frac{f\,\|t_{\parallel}\|\,\delta}{Z^{2}},$$
with $t_{\parallel}$ the translation component parallel to $\Pi$ and $Z$ the mean depth. A tiered grandstand has $\delta$ of order the row height (≈0.5–0.8 m) across the extent of the crowd; with $f \approx 10^{3}$ px, $Z \approx 40$ m, and a pan of $t_{\parallel} \approx 1$ m between frames, $\varepsilon_p \approx 0.3$–$0.5$ px per row-step — tolerable for the plane fit, but accumulating across a 20-row stand into a visible shear of several pixels at the hull boundary.

**6. Corner-velocity blending** [PAGE 11, Eqn. 8]:
$$v_{\text{corner}_i} = \alpha\, v_i + (1-\alpha)\,\frac{v_1 + v_2 + v_3 + v_4}{4}, \qquad \alpha \in (0,1).$$
Verbatim: "We found that $\alpha \ge 0.8$ works best, because the motion of the features in the group centered at a particular corner are more indicative of that corner's movement than the motion of features from the other corner groups" [PAGE 11]. Bias bound:
$$\big\|v_{\text{corner}_i} - v_i\big\| = (1-\alpha)\,\big\|\bar v - v_i\big\| \ \le\ 0.2\,\big\|\bar v - v_i\big\|.$$
**Breakdown regime:** when $(1-\alpha)\|\bar v - v_i\|$ exceeds the per-frame corner-matching residual, i.e. under rotation-plus-zoom camera moves where $\bar v - v_i$ is structurally non-zero. Feature groups are defined by "a circle with radius $r = 50$ px centered at each corner" [PAGE 11].

**7. Kalman corner predictor** [PAGE 11, citing Kalman [16]]. State $\mathbf{x} = [u, v, \dot u, \dot v]^\top$, transition $F = \begin{pmatrix} I_2 & \Delta t\, I_2 \\ 0 & I_2\end{pmatrix}$, process noise $Q$, measurement noise $R$. Under measurement dropout (corner out of frame) the covariance propagates as $P_{k+1} = F P_k F^\top + Q$, growing as $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})$ per unobserved step; the predicted corner drifts quadratically in time. The Eqn. 8 velocity is applied as a pseudo-measurement to "update the error estimation" [PAGE 11], which bounds the drift only while the quad's centroid velocity remains a good predictor.

**8. Shot-change handling** [PAGE 10–11, citing Yildrim [30]]: suspend tracking, archive features, re-match on new frames, restart on "excessive" lapse. **No equation and no bound is given**; verbatim: "We leave the definition of excessive up to the preference of the user" [PAGE 11]. This is an unbounded latency in the failure-recovery path.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The pipeline's ancestry is a chain of 1960–2018 recoveries, each one a fix for a failure of its predecessor, and each one importing its own domain assumption.

**1960 — Kalman [16].** Linear-Gaussian state estimation enters as the corner predictor. Its retrocausal cost: the model is *constant-velocity*, so it encodes the belief that quads translate. Perspective warps under pan+zoom violate that belief continuously, not occasionally.

**1972 — Hough [9].** The alignment line is a Hough maximum. The transform assumes the target is a straight segment with a well-defined orientation histogram peak; on a curved bowl the accumulator has no dominant peak, and "the longest line segment" [PAGE 9] becomes an arbitrary tie-break.

**1981 — Lucas–Kanade [22] and RANSAC [13] on the same shelf.** LK supplies the flow field; RANSAC supplies the dominant plane. RANSAC's entire epistemology is "assume the inlier fraction exceeds the breakdown point." The paper never reports the inlier fraction of the crowd-plane fit — a missing diagnostic that would have exposed the $\tau/s$ coupling described above.

**1986 — Canny [4].** Edge detection on the *binary segmentation mask*, not the photometric image [PAGE 9, Fig. 6a–b]. This is a subtle inheritance: the "edge" being detected is the boundary of a learned classifier's decision region, so Canny's gradient operator is being applied to a semantic quantity. Any segmentation wobble becomes a geometric line.

**2004/2008/2012 — SIFT [21], SURF [3], KAZE [2].** Three detector-descriptor families are fused with no ablation: "We use a combination of of SIFT, SURF and KAZE feature descriptors" [PAGE 10]. Each was designed for a different invariance profile (SIFT scale+rotation, SURF speed, KAZE nonlinear diffusion for blur). Fusing them without reporting per-detector contribution means the tracker's failure envelope is the *union* of three failure envelopes, undocumented.

**2010 — Li et al. [17] vanishing-point calibration.** This is the recovery from the "intrinsics unknown" premise [PAGE 1, PAGE 4]. But it is a *relocation*, not a removal, of the calibration assumption: instead of needing the camera's $f$, it needs the scene's orthogonality. The paper concedes the swap: the method "was designed for images that contain clear orthogonal axes, such as present in a Manhattan world" [PAGE 12].

**2017–2018 — PSPNet/ADE20K [31,33,32] and MegaDepth [19].** Both are trained on internet-scale natural-image corpora. The domain shift to broadcast sport is the deepest genealogy trap: MegaDepth's priors were formed on tourist photography, where crowds are *not* the dominant plane and floodlit night stadia are rare. The source names the consequence precisely: "Night-time scenes are challenging for MegaDepth [19] due to the bright overhead lighting often used by sporting arenas. Scenes in which the crowd is covered by an awning are also challenging for MegaDepth as problematic shadows are cast" [PAGE 12].

**The ancestral antagonist.** The paper defines itself against field-marking systems — tennis [5,6], soccer [29], baseball [18] — and against manual artist workflows, promising: "We aim to be independent of such assumptions while focusing on the crowds and the surrounding areas at sporting events" [PAGE 3]. But the crowd is the *worst* geometric substrate in the scene: the field is flat, rectilinear, and painted with fiducials; the grandstand is curved, tiered, and filled with moving non-rigid objects. The genealogy runs backwards from "easy geometry, intrusive placement" to "hard geometry, non-intrusive placement," trading a solved problem for an unsolved one.

**Multi-week traps.** (1) *The $s$ trap*: "an empirically determined scale factor $s$" [PAGE 8] with the only published value buried in a figure caption [PAGE 13]. A reader reimplementing this will pick a different $s$, and — because of the $\tau/s$ coupling — will get a different asset size and orientation, then attribute the discrepancy to a bug. (2) *The "markerless" trap*: markerless with respect to fiducials, but not with respect to geometry; the calibration burden reappears as vanishing-point detection, which fails on curved stands. (3) *The evaluation trap*: "With the exception of focal length estimation (Sec. 3.2) and the SQS score (Sec. 3.1), few steps in the pipeline can be assessed quantitatively" [PAGE 14] — so a reproduction that differs in the qualitative output has no referee. (4) *The occlusion trap*: segmentation is discontinued after seed selection, so a reproduced system that adds re-segmentation will look "wrong" against the paper's own outputs (it will suppress the asset where the original drew it over a player).

Therefore rung 3 is *structurally unavailable* in any live setting, and the pipeline's stated purpose — "The aim is to augment the scene such that there is no longer a need for commercial breaks" [PAGE 1] — is contradicted by its own timing table. The tracking rung, which is the only live-capable path, has no timing row in Table 1 at all. So the system's real operating regime is post-production, and the clock, not the algorithm, decides which rung of the habit ladder is even reachable.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto is a condition under which no amount of downstream tuning rescues the output. Define the composite veto predicate over a frame $t$:
$$V(t) = V_{\text{geo}} \vee V_{\text{occ}} \vee V_{\text{photo}} \vee V_{\text{motion}} \vee V_{\text{clock}}.$$
The pipeline evaluates only $V_{\text{geo}}$, and only at $t = t_{\text{seed}}$.

**$V_{\text{geo}}$ — the non-planarity veto (physical geometry).** The dominant-plane model asserts the crowd is a plane. A grandstand is a stepped surface with row pitch $\delta$ and rake angle; the homography residual derived above is $\varepsilon_p \approx f\|t_\parallel\|\delta/Z^2$. For a 20-row stand the accumulated shear at the hull boundary is of order several pixels — perceptible on a static overlay. *Verdict: graded veto, surviving on shallow, distant, small-$\delta$ stands; fatal on close, steep, or curved bowls.* This is exactly the class of scene the paper's own Manhattan-world caveat excludes [PAGE 12].

**$V_{\text{occ}}$ — the occlusion veto (physical/optical).** The depth map is computed and is used **only** to fit the crowd plane; it is never used as a depth test against the composite. Meanwhile segmentation is abandoned after the seed frame: "Once we have identified a seed frame, we discontinue segmentation until tracking fails" [PAGE 5]. Therefore, from frame 2 onward, a player, umpire, foul pole, or camera operator entering the corridor between the camera and the crowd plane is *not* detected, and the asset renders **in front of them**. This is a categorical failure, not a graded one: an advertisement drawn over a player's body is instantly legible as an error and destroys the "seamless" claim [PAGE 1]. It is also cheap to fix (z-test the warped asset against $z = s\,MD$), which makes its absence a design omission rather than a limitation. *Verdict: dominant veto.*

**$V_{\text{photo}}$ — the photometric veto (lighting/weather).** Night games and awning shadows are named explicitly as failure modes for the depth network [PAGE 12]. Rain, snow, and lens flare are unaddressed. Because the asset is composited by homography warp with no color-harmonization stage (unlike [5,6], cited but unused), the asset carries its own white balance and gamma. Under floodlights with strong color temperature and specular bloom on the crowd, the composited rectangle has a step discontinuity at its border that no geometric accuracy can hide. *Verdict: graded veto, monotone in the mismatch between asset and scene illuminant.*

**$V_{\text{motion}}$ — the biomechanical veto (crowd non-rigidity).** The "texture of interest" is a crowd of people [PAGE 4], but the tracked entity is the *quadrilateral*, anchored on structural features at the corners. Individual humans are non-rigid and their apparent motion is uncorrelated with the stand's. This is why the four-anchor design works — "tracking to be far more accurate if we use four anchor quadrilateral tracks around the asset rather than track the points within the asset boundary" [PAGE 10] — but it also means the *interior* of the quad is not stabilized at all: the ad is pinned to the concrete, not to the people, and the people slide underneath it. For a static image asset that is acceptable; for the video asset the paper demonstrates, it is a persistent low-frequency incoherence. *Verdict: graded veto, independent of geometry.*

**$V_{\text{clock}}$ — the temporal veto.** 41.45 s per seed frame [PAGE 12, Table 1] versus a 33.3 ms frame interval. *Verdict: absolute veto on live use; null on post-production.*

**Adjudication order.** Dominance is decided by *recoverability*: a veto that no downstream stage can reverse outranks one that tuning can reduce relative to signal. That yields
$$V_{\text{occ}} \succ V_{\text{clock}} \succ V_{\text{geo}} \succ V_{\text{photo}} \succ V_{\text{motion}}.$$
$V_{\text{occ}}$ dominates because it is categorical and because the information needed to fix it is already in hand (the depth map) and unused. $V_{\text{clock}}$ is second because it is binary and determines whether the other four even matter in a given deployment. $V_{\text{geo}}$ is third because it is systematic but bounded and partially correctable by hull-shrinking. $V_{\text{photo}}$ and $V_{\text{motion}}$ are last because both are graded and both are addressable by well-understood post-processes (harmonization; per-frame re-warping from a denser plane set).

**The cross-layer proof.** Note that the ranking is not a matter of taste — it follows from a structural asymmetry in the pipeline's information flow. Segmentation and depth are *both* computed at the seed frame and *both* discarded thereafter (segmentation explicitly [PAGE 5]; depth implicitly, since tracking never re-consults it). Therefore every veto that depends on scene state — occlusion, lighting, crowd configuration — is frozen at $t_{\text{seed}}$ and cannot be re-evaluated. The only veto the system can express after frame 1 is geometric drift of the quad. This is the precise sense in which the pipeline is a *single-frame* method with a tracker glued on, and why its physical and photometric vetoes are unadjudicated by construction.
