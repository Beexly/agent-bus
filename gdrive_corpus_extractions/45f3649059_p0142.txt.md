Historically, the "stiff arm" became prominent as players moved from simply grappling with opponents to using extended limbs to create space—a technique refined by players like Amos Alonzo Stagg's athletes at Chicago. The lineage also connects to military drill manuals of the era, which stressed low stances for stability under fire (e.g., bayonet charges). Football borrowed these physical principles. Moreover, the instruction to read the opposition reflects the growing analytical sophistication of coaches who began keeping detailed records of opponents' habits—a precursor to modern film study.

This philosophy directly influenced later defensive schematics, including the Notre Dame Box and eventually zone defenses, where linebackers (evolved from tackles) were trained to read keys rather than match up manually.
