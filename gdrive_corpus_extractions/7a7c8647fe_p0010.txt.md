The patent demonstrates reliability over Bout 1 and Bout 2 (n=8 each) but discloses no autocorrelation function, no exponential decay for chronic load, and no normalization by player mass or maturational status. Band zones fixed in arbitrary units (Band 1 \(=[0.0,0.3]\), Band 4 \(=[1.81,3.0]\)) will drift in physiological meaning across weeks as players adapt, accumulate fatigue, or change body composition. A second trap is maneuver-specificity: slap-shot shows high within-bout \(R^2\) but low between-bout agreement for Zone 5 [PAGE 11], so a model trained on one week’s slap-shot profiles may not transfer to the next week’s ice conditions or player fatigue states. Finally, the absence of environmental covariates (ice temperature, rink humidity) means sensor drift from cold-induced MEMS bias is unmodeled, creating a hidden confounder that compounds across a multi-week schedule.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the coaching or front-office decision node as a recursive Bayesian agent updating availability beliefs \(A_t\in\{0,1\}\) given observed load \(L_t\):

\[
P(A_t=1\mid L_{1:t},S_t) \propto P(L_t\mid A_t=1)\,P(A_t=1\mid S_{1:t}),
\]

where \(S_t\) is the schedule clock (games remaining, travel density). Three recursive dynamics apply.

Crisis habit regression: after a loss or injury, the decision-maker reverts to a habitual high-load practice profile because past victories are attributed to mechanical load exposure. This ignores the patent’s own finding that Zone 5 slap-shot models explain variance within bouts but show low between-bout agreement [PAGE 11]; thus the “crisis” load prescription may be fitting bout-specific noise rather than a stable injury-risk signal.

Counter-trap: stratified PBZ is used to deliberately prescribe low-zone recovery days. However, if Band Zone 1 for coasting/bench sitting is a degenerate identity map (\(\hat\beta_1=1.00,\hat\beta_0=0.00,R^2=0.99\)) [PAGE 12], then “low zone” may simply be a rescaled version of Total Load and not a distinct physiological state. The counter-trap therefore collapses into the original load metric, offering no additional protection.

Clock: the game schedule imposes a hard deadline on the availability decision. Because measurement bias is session-dependent (intercept 95% CL suggested bias in cross-bout models but not in repeat testing) [PAGE 11], the decision-maker must act before bias can be characterized. The recursive belief update is therefore forced to condition on a potentially biased likelihood \(P(L_t\mid A_t)\), causing systematic miscalibration of availability probability as the clock approaches game time.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The claim specifies “player worn torso data loggers including accelerometers and gyroscopes … affixed to an upper torso region on a respective player's back” [PAGE 12]. This placement captures trunk kinematics but not lower-limb impact forces, stick-contact loads, or cervical loading. Placement variance across players introduces inter-subject scaling noise that is not corrected because band zones are in arbitrary units rather than mass-normalized SI units.

Biomechanical layer: Ice skating has a low-friction interface; ice coasting produces near-zero trunk acceleration, which explains why Band Zone 1 maps with slope 1.00 and intercept 0.00 to Total Load (\(R^2=0.99\)) [PAGE 12]. This is a kinematic artifact, not evidence that coasting is mechanically uninformative. Conversely, slap-shot loads are transient high-acceleration events captured well within a bout (high \(R^2\)) but poorly across bouts (low agreement) [PAGE 11], indicating that the band-zone signal is task-contingent and not a stable trait of the player.

Weather layer: Ice temperature alters the blade–ice friction coefficient \(\mu\), changing the acceleration spectra measured by the torso logger. Cold temperatures also induce MEMS accelerometer bias drift. The patent discloses no temperature-compensation model, so unmeasured rink conditions act as an omitted-variable confounder on both \(x_i\) (Total Load) and \(y_i\) (Band-zone load), violating the RMA error-in-variables assumption.

Adjudication rule: A veto is issued if any layer yields statistical evidence of bias (\(0\notin CI_{95\%}(\hat\beta_0)\) or \(1\notin CI_{95\%}(\hat\beta_1)\) at \(p<0.05\)) or if the physical layer exhibits missingness (e.g., logger detachment) exceeding a predefined threshold. In Table 4, the combined repeated-shift Zone 4 intercept (\(CI_{95\%}=(-4.61,-2.17)\)) excludes zero [PAGE 12], triggering a fixed-bias veto at the combined level even though individual bouts appeared unbiased.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Fixed measurement bias veto. Verifiable condition: for any maneuver–band pair, the combined-bout intercept 95% confidence limits exclude zero (\(0\notin CI_{95\%}(\hat\beta_0)\)) while within-bout intercepts individually contain zero. Verification: re-fit RMA on held-out bouts and recompute \(CI_{95\%}(\hat\beta_0)\). Prediction: availability recommendations will systematically under- or over-estimate mechanical load by an additive offset \(\hat\beta_0\); injury risk is miscalibrated by approximately \(\exp(|\hat\beta_0|/\sigma_y)\) in log-odds space. The veto triggers mandatory recalibration with temperature-controlled bench testing before the next game cycle.

Scenario 2 — Between-bout agreement collapse veto. Verifiable condition: within-bout \(R^2>0.90\) but between-bout agreement \(R^2<0.70\) for the same band zone and maneuver (empirically suggested for Zone 5 slap-shot: “Nearly the entire variance … explained” yet “between bout agreement appeared low” [PAGE 11]). Verification: split-sample RMA on Bout 1 versus Bout 2; compute agreement \(R^2\) from the agreement row of Table 4. Prediction: the model memorizes bout-specific ice-condition or fatigue signatures; week-to-week availability prescriptions flip discontinuously despite stable player physiology. The veto locks the prior week’s load prescription until inter-bout agreement exceeds 0.80 on two consecutive validation sets.

Scenario 3 — Degenerate identity-mapping veto. Verifiable condition: slope \(\hat\beta_1=1.00\), intercept \(\hat\beta_0=0.00\), and \(R^2\ge0.99\) simultaneously (observed for Zone 1 ice coasting and bench sitting [PAGE 12]), combined with a band-zone range that is a strict subset of the Total Load scale. Verification: compute the condition number of the design matrix \([1,x]\); if slope confidence-limit width is \(<0.01\) around 1.00 and \(R^2>0.98\), declare rank deficiency. Prediction: stratified PBZ adds zero incremental information beyond univariate Total Load; availability outputs are identical to an unstratified model. The veto forces inclusion of multivariate gyroscope features, higher-order jerk derivatives, or player-mass normalization before the system may issue stratified recommendations.
