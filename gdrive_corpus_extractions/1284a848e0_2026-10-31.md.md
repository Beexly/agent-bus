The trap here is the assumption that richer inputs (pose, higher-dimensional tracking, multimodal commentary) automatically improve valuation; the evidence says feature addition can degrade held-out value when the objective is misspecified. The recursive move is therefore to fix the loss function (pinball quantile at the correct \(\tau\)) before adding pose or broadcast modalities.

The clock layer is the release calendar: sportsdataverse-py 0.2.0b1 on 2026-10-26 precedes the 0.2.0 final ([PAGE 2]); Polars 2.1.0 on 2026-10-25 follows the 2.0 major ([PAGE 2]); DuckDB 2.0.0 on 2026-10-21 is unreviewed ([PAGE 2]). Each date is a potential forcing function. If the team waits for the sportsdataverse final before writing the TTA adapter, they lose integration time; if they integrate against the beta without pinning Polars `<3` and DuckDB pre-2.0 behavior, they absorb silent schema breaks. The theory-of-mind read is that the team must simultaneously (a) avoid regressing to physics baselines, (b) avoid the feature-richness trap, and (c) ship against the clock with pinned, tested dependencies.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: TTA labels require tracking sampled above the Nyquist rate for player maneuvers; if \(\Delta t_s\) violates \(1/(2f_{\max})\), arrival-time targets are aliased and the model is vetoed on physical grounds. Pitch dimensions and player radius define the minimum resolvable arrival distance; sub-centimeter tracking noise is irrelevant, but meter-scale registration error between tracking and event streams invalidates the quantile target.

Biomechanical layer: the pose paper shows 3D pose added little value (helped 3/50, hurt 12/50) and its data are not public ([PAGE 1]); therefore a biomechanical pose module cannot be assumed to improve defensive-coverage or arrival-time estimates, and any such module must pass a null test before inclusion. The existing repo ranking note that "the control group and the null test are worth more than the model" applies here ([PAGE 2]).

Weather layer: the TTA paper reports no wind, precipitation, temperature, or humidity covariates ([PAGE 1]); outdoor generalization to match-day weather is unproven. A veto is required if the model is deployed in conditions outside the 2022 World Cup envelope without recalibration or an explicit weather-robustness ablation.

Data-governance layer: PFF World Cup tracking must remain out of cloud sessions ([PAGE 2]); this is a hard veto independent of model performance.

Adjudication: the TTA module may proceed only if (i) tracking sampling satisfies the Nyquist constraint, (ii) no pose or weather covariates are added without a null-test win, (iii) PFF data stays on-prem or in approved local storage, and (iv) sportsdataverse-py, Polars, and DuckDB versions are pinned with regression tests against empty-frame and error-raising semantics ([PAGE 2]).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
