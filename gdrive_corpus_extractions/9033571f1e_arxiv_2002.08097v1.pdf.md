Physical/biomechanical layer. A rally is "the duration when the ball is in play on the table" ([PAGE 3]); table-tennis ball trajectories reach high speed but the *rally duration* (serve-to-point) is short (order $1$–$6$ s), which is why the "$\pm4$ seconds" window and "$\pm3$ seconds" tolerance are physically sized to a single rally. Veto check 1 (temporal sampling): span $=205$ frames; at $25$ FPS this is $8.2$ s $>$ one rally, adequate; at the lower bound $10$ FPS it is $20.5$ s, spanning multiple rallies and smearing the confidence region — a physical veto of the segmentation claim at low FPS. The paper's "$10\sim25$ FPS" real-time claim is therefore only self-consistent near the top of that band. Veto check 2 (serve biomechanics): the discriminative cue is the serve action, and "let" serves replicate that biomechanics without a valid rally — a biomechanical veto of precision that no amount of pose augmentation removes (it is a label-semantics conflict, not an appearance gap). Veto check 3 (occlusion geometry): a $(0.4H,0.2H)$ occluder removes $8\%$ of frame area; since player reID relied on part-pooling [25], occluding torso/leg parts degrades $g_{person}$ but the reported qualitative robustness holds because clustering was a *pre-processing* step done on clean frames, not on occluded test frames — so the retrieval stage is not physically stressed by the occlusion test, only the classifier is. This is a partial-scope caveat, not a refutation.

Weather layer. Table tennis is an indoor, climate-controlled sport; the corpus contains no wind, precipitation, or illumination-weather variation. The weather veto is therefore *vacuously satisfied / inapplicable* — there is no environmental channel through which weather could invalidate the results, and equally none through which the method's weather-robustness could be claimed. Adjudicated as a null veto in both directions.

Lighting-as-weather proxy. The only environmental-adjacent covariate is illumination ("similar jerseys, lighting conditions" cause players to merge into one cluster, [PAGE 4]). This is the fitness-measure's entire reason for existing, and it is a real physical failure mode: under low-contrast lighting the two-player GMM collapses to one dominant component, $S_{fitness}$ drops below $t_l$, and the loop must re-cluster. This is the one place where a physical (photometric) condition can veto the retrieval stage — and the paper's own mitigation (fitness-driven re-clustering) is the acknowledgment of that veto.

Verdict: no physical/biomechanical veto defeats the *retrieval* claims; two partial vetoes bind the *segmentation* claims (low-FPS temporal aliasing; let-event semantic mimicry); weather is inapplicable.
