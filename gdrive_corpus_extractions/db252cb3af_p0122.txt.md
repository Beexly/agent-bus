The transition is governed by a connectivity matrix \( \mathbf{W} \), shaped by Hebbian learning (habit):
\[ \Delta \mathbf{W} = \eta \cdot \mathbf{s}_{this} \mathbf{s}_A^T \]
where \( \eta \) is the learning rate.
The probability flux (irradiation) from 'this' to A is modeled as a Markov transition kernel \( K(\mathbf{s}_A | \mathbf{s}_{this}) \). Direct paths maximize the transition probability:
\[ K_{direct} \gg K_{roundabout}(\mathbf{s}_{this} \to \mathbf{s}_{those} \to \mathbf{s}_A) \]

**3. Information-Theoretic Entropy of Illusion**
Referencing the footnote: "[When] M is 'undistributed' the conclusion is liable to error... Illusions would thus be logical fallacies" [PAGE 122].
Let \( H(M) \) be the Shannon entropy of the middle term class. If \( M \) is undistributed, \( H(M) \) is high, meaning the mapping \( M \rightarrow A \) is many-to-one or ambiguous.
The Mutual Information between the sign 'this' and the target A is:
\[ I(\text{'this'}; A) = H(A) - H(A | \text{'this'}) \]
An illusion occurs when the system defaults to a high-entropy prior \( P(M) \) rather than the precise context, maximizing the conditional entropy \( H(A | \text{'this'}) \). The error rate \( \epsilon \) correlates with the divergence between the true distribution and the inferred syllogistic conclusion.
