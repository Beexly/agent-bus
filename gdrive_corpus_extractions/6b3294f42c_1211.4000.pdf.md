I will call the source’s central market variable \(M\), the posted consensus margin in points, because the no-lines invariant forbids reproducing wagering vocabulary. The first hop is conceptual: the paper argues that collective intelligence can synthesize dispersed information, quoting that “Collective intelligence is a way of synthesizing information from a group of people that no one person would have known on their own” [PAGE 1], and that “Under the right circumstances, groups are remarkably intelligent, and are often smarter than the smartest people in them” [PAGE 2]. The second hop is measurement: the paper computes Margin of Victory, \(MOV = WinnerScore - LoserScore\), and Line Difference, \(LD = (FavoriteScore - UnderdogScore) - |M_{close}|\). The units are points for scores, \(MOV\), \(LD\), and \(M\); \(LD\) has mean \(-0.009\) and standard deviation \(13.588\) points [PAGE 7]. The third hop is distributional: the paper reports that “A chi-squared goodness of fit test comparing the LD curve to a Gaussian distribution with a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 13.588 indicates that the distribution of LD is not statistically different” [PAGE 8]. From \(LD \sim N(0,\sigma^2)\) and \(D = FavoriteScore - UnderdogScore\), the event \(D>0\) is equivalent to \(LD > -|M|\), so \(\Pr(D>0 \mid M=m)=\Phi(m/\sigma)\). With \(\sigma=13.588\), \(m=7\) gives \(\Phi(7/13.588)=0.697\), matching the source’s 69.7% approximation [PAGE 8]. The fourth hop is aggregation over a season: the paper multiplies or sums sequence probabilities and simulates each season 1,000 times [PAGE 9]. The fifth hop is retrospective validation: division-winner reconstruction reached 67/80 correct, i.e. 83.75%, with minimum annual accuracy 75% [PAGE 11]. The gray-zone audit is essential: the full-season \(M\) values are used retrospectively, so this is not a prospective forecasting proof; the source itself notes the retrospective character [PAGE 10]. Pushes, tie rules, non-Gaussian tails, changing variance across eras, dependence among games, and data-source heterogeneity are all unmodeled or only partially modeled. Equation (1)’s cost-recovery threshold has no mathematical singularity unless the stake-plus-payout denominator is zero: \(WR = S/(S+W)\); with \(S=110\), \(W=100\), \(WR=110/210=0.5238\). The physical-layer veto is separate: if injury, weather, travel, rest, or venue shifts the conditional mean or variance, the market-only model is misspecified even if its retrospective average was good. That is the correct gray-zone conclusion: \(M\) is a useful feature, not a causal guarantee.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind here means forecasters model other forecasters’ models. The sportsbook adjusts to balance beliefs; agents infer what others know from movement; the paper’s feedback description implies nested beliefs rather than independent guesses [PAGE 2-3]. Crisis habit regression: when injury, weather, or venue news arrives, agents often regress to the last stable consensus \(M\) and over-weight prior reputation instead of updating the conditional distribution. Counter-trap: if everyone expects a correction after a public shock, the correction is already embedded in the posted margin; the counter-trap is to ask whether the physical information is genuinely new relative to the timestamp of \(M\). Clock: the relevant clock is not the game clock but the information-arrival clock—opening, midweek injury reports, late weather, and game-day confirmation. The source says late bettors use accurate weather and current injury reports, and that late action can move the posted margin [PAGE 13]. The recursive failure mode is therefore double-counting: treating \(M\) as independent of the same injury/weather information that the model also uses. The counter-trap is to condition on information timestamp: if \(M\) already incorporates the shock, do not add it again; if \(M\) predates the shock, the physical layer should veto the stale market-only forecast. Crisis habit regression also appears in retrospective division-winner reconstruction: a model that has seen the whole season’s \(M\) can “predict” winners by memory rather than by prospective inference. The clock-aware rule is to evaluate weekly, with only pre-game information, and to segment week 1 and week 17 as separate regimes.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather layers can veto a market-only forecast. Formal setup: let \(D\) be the favorite-minus-underdog score difference in points, \(M\) the posted consensus margin in points, and \(X\) a vector of physical covariates—wind speed, precipitation, temperature, injury status, rest days, travel load, venue neutrality. Baseline model: \(D\mid M \sim N(M,\sigma^2)\), so \(\Pr(D>0\mid M)=\Phi(M/\sigma)\). Physical model: \(D\mid M,X \sim N(M+\beta^{T}X,\sigma^2)\). Null \(H_0:\beta=0\) is the market-only model. The veto statistic is \(T=\hat\beta/\mathrm{se}(\hat\beta)\); reject \(H_0\) when \(|T|>z_{1-\alpha/2}\). Equivalently, a physical layer vetoes the market-only forecast when the physical effect size satisfies \(|\beta^{T}X|>z_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt{n_{\text{eff}}}\). Units: \(\beta^{T}X\) is points, \(\sigma\) is points, \(n_{\text{eff}}\) is dimensionless. Breakdown regimes: \(n_{\text{eff}}=0\); \(\sigma=0\); \(X\) collinear with \(M\); \(X\) measured after \(M\) but before the game (time leakage); and neutral-site or venue-change games where home-field terms are undefined. The source supports the physical layer: home advantage is attributed to “familiarity, travel requirements, and factors related to the crowd [13]” [PAGE 4]; “injury reports are released, weather reports become more accurate” [PAGE 11]; and “Unless a team has a major injury or venue change” [PAGE 13], the posted margin is expected to be stable. Proof sketch: if \(\beta^{T}X\) shifts the conditional mean by more than the sampling threshold, then the market-only probability \(\Phi(M/\sigma)\) is miscalibrated; the correct probability is \(\Phi((M+\beta^{T}X)/\sigma)\). If \(X\) shifts variance rather than mean, the market-only CDF is still vetoed because \(\sigma\) is no longer constant. Therefore physical/biomechanical/weather veto is not a post-hoc excuse; it is a likelihood-ratio rejection of the restricted model.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR means verifiable-reward reinforcement learning: the model’s forecast is rewarded only when an external, checkable outcome confirms it. Three counterfactual veto scenarios:
1. Injury/venue shock veto. Counterfactual: a starting player is ruled out or the venue changes after \(M\) was posted. Verifiable evidence: timestamped inactive list, official venue record, and the model’s information timestamp. Prediction: the stale market-only model has higher log loss and Brier score than a model that conditions on the shock; the recalculated probability should move toward 0.5 or reverse sign if the shock is large. Veto rule: reject the stale forecast when the injury/venue indicator is newer than \(M\) and the effect size exceeds the threshold in the cross-layer equation.
2. Weather-regime veto. Counterfactual: wind speed, precipitation, or temperature crosses a pre-registered threshold. Verifiable evidence: weather-station readings at the venue and forecast-issue time. Prediction: scoring variance increases, the normal-CDF confidence shrinks toward 0.5, and calibration slope moves away from 1; the model should widen its interval and lower extreme probabilities. Veto rule: reject the constant-variance model when residual variance in the weather stratum is significantly larger than the baseline variance.
3. Week-17 rest/incentive veto. Counterfactual: a team has clinched a playoff position or is eliminated and rests starters. Verifiable evidence: playoff-clinch status, inactive lists, and snap counts. Prediction: division-winner reconstruction accuracy in week 17 falls below the paper’s 75% annual floor, and full-season retrospective \(M\) overstates prospective accuracy. Veto rule: reject the retrospective whole-season model for week-17 inference; evaluate only on pre-game information and segment week 17 as a separate regime. Each scenario is verifiable because the intervention, timestamp, and outcome are externally auditable, and each prediction can be scored with calibration and proper scoring rules.
