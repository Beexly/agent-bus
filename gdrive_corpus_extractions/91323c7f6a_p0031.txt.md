\Phi = \{ \mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{p}_2, d, \vartheta, L_{\text{virt}}, d_{GL}, T_{\text{shot}}, B_{\text{pose}}, n_{\text{shot}}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{a}, S_{\text{court}}, C_{\text{game}}, \tau_{\text{shot}}, T_{\text{temp}}, \mathbf{W}_{\text{wind}}, t_{\text{day}}, \mathbf{A}_{\text{player}} \}
\]
SI units: positions and distances in m; velocity m/s; acceleration m/s²; temperature K or °C; wind vector m/s; time s; probability dimensionless.

Virtual line (soccer goal center or near/far post; tennis baseline analogue):
\[
L_{\text{virt}}(t) = \mathbf{p}_1 + t(\mathbf{c}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{p}_1), \quad t \in [0,1] \ [\text{PAGE p0031}]
\]

Goalkeeper off-line distance:
\[
d_{GL} = \min_{q \in L_{\text{virt}}} \| \mathbf{p}_{GK} - q \|_2 \quad [\text{m}] \ [\text{PAGE p0031}]
\]

Clarity / screening count:
\[
N_{\text{screen}}(r) = \left| \{ \mathbf{p}_i : \operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{p}_i, L_{\text{virt}}) \leq r \} \right|, \quad r \in \{0.3048, 0.9144, 1.524, 3.048\}\text{ m} \ [\text{PAGE p0031}]
\]

Save-probability permutation over shot angles from virtual line:
\[
P_{\text{save}}(\phi) = \text{permutation over shot locations at angles } \phi \text{ from } L_{\text{virt}} \ [\text{PAGE p0031}]
\]

Step 208 update:
\[
P_{\text{upd}} = M'_{\theta}(P_0, \Phi) \in [0,1] \ [\text{PAGE p0031}]
\]

Monotonic constraint (GAN, weight w_mon from undisclosed FIG. 4A):
\[
\frac{\partial P_{\text{score}}}{\partial d_{GL}} \geq 0 \quad \text{(goalkeeper farther from line of sight } \Rightarrow \text{ higher scoring likelihood)}
\]

Assumptions: L_virt is straight; shot speed and spin are captured in T_shot; screening within r degrades goalkeeper vision or causes deflection [PAGE p0031].

Breakdown / singularity regimes:
1. Open-net singularity: as \(\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{ball}} - \mathbf{c}_{\text{target}}\| \to 0\) with \(d_{GL} > 0\), \(P_{\text{score}} \to 1\).
2. Screening non-identifiability: \(N_{\text{screen}} \ge 1\) within \(r = 0.9144\) m creates occlusion/deflection ambiguity not resolvable from geometry alone.
3. Edge-case sparsity: drop-shot and deep-baseline entries in \(D_{\text{hist}}\) may be rare, making \(r_{\text{drop}}, r_{\text{deep}}\) high-variance.
4. Unquantified constraint strength: \(w_{\text{mon}}\) is not numerically specified on [PAGE p0031], so the monotonic regularization magnitude is unknown.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The method’s lineage descends from three historical strands. First, notational and spatial analysis in invasion sports: Reep’s early soccer tallies evolved into expected-goal frameworks and modern optical tracking (Second Spectrum, Stats Perform), which encode precisely the \(\mathbf{p}_1,\mathbf{p}_2,d,\vartheta\) geometry used here [PAGE p0031]. Second, racket-sport telemetry: Hawk-Eye line calling and IBM Watson/SAP tennis analytics established shot-trajectory, court-surface, and shot-type taxonomies (serve, return, forehand, backhand, smash, volley, tweener) that map directly onto \(\tau_{\text{shot}}\), \(T_{\text{shot}}\), and \(S_{\text{court}}\) [PAGE p0031]. Third, constrained generative modeling: monotonic GANs and physics-informed neural networks impose inequality constraints on outputs, corresponding to the patent’s GAN with per-constraint weights [PAGE p0031].

Multi-week traps arise from non-stationarity across a tournament. Surface transitions (clay to grass to hard) alter the ball bounce coefficient and court friction \(\mu\), invalidating a static \(S_{\text{court}}\) embedding if the model trains on a single surface. Accumulated load over two weeks depresses player acceleration bounds \(a_{\max}\) and reaction latency \(\tau_{\text{react}}\), shifting the feasible \(B_{\text{pose}}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) regime. Serve and strike velocity regress after marathon matches, so \(T_{\text{shot}}\) speed priors calibrated in week one overstate week-three capability. Goalkeeper positioning drifts with cumulative fatigue, changing the distribution of \(d_{GL}\). Diurnal temperature swings between day and night sessions change ball internal pressure and air density \(\rho\), violating a fixed \(T_{\text{temp}}\) assumption [PAGE p0031]. These are retrocausal in the sense that the current feature vector is conditioned on a opponent’s prior-week load profile, which itself was shaped by earlier match outcomes.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under high game-contest pressure \(C_{\text{game}}\) (match point, break point, score deficit), operators regress to their dominant shot archetype \(\tau_{\text{shot}}\) — for example an inside-out forehand or a preferred penalty side — collapsing the entropy of the shot-type distribution. Because step 206 includes previous action type and shot archetype [PAGE p0031], the model must condition \(P_{\text{upd}}\) jointly on \(C_{\text{game}}\) and \(\tau_{\text{shot}}\); otherwise it treats a regressed, predictable shot as if drawn from the player’s baseline repertoire.

Counter-trap: the defense anticipates the regressed habit and stations \(p_{GK}\) off \(L_{\text{virt}}\) (\(d_{GL} > 0\)) to cut the apparent angle, as illustrated by the goalkeeper away from the striker line of sight in FIG. 4C [PAGE p0031]. This narrows the near side but opens the far post. The offense can counter the counter-trap by varying archetype (central vs. lateral portion, contested vs. uncontested) precisely when \(C_{\text{game}}\) signals crisis, forcing the defender to choose between honoring the regression and guarding the opened angle.

Clock: \(t_{\text{day}}\), rally count, and shot count in a volley impose a non-stationary clock on the update map \(M'_{\theta}\). As fatigue accumulates, \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{a}\) bounds tighten and \(\tau_{\text{react}}\) lengthens, so the same \(d_{GL}\) and \(N_{\text{screen}}\) configuration yields a different \(P_{\text{upd}}\) at minute 75 than at minute 15. The clock therefore modulates the weight of the spatial feature set relative to the historical prior \(P_0\).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: if the predicted shot trajectory implies a speed \(\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{shot}}\|\) above the biomechanical maximum (elite tennis serve \(\approx 58\) m/s; elite soccer strike \(\approx 30\)–35 m/s), \(P_{\text{upd}}\) is vetoed because no human actuator can realize the required \(T_{\text{shot}}\).

Biomechanical layer veto: \(B_{\text{pose}}\) joint angles must remain within anatomical limits, and racket location must be reachable given \(|\mathbf{a}| \le a_{\max}\) and \(\tau_{\text{react}} \approx 0.3\)–0.5 s. A predicted return requiring a reach beyond \(v_{\max}\tau_{\text{react}}\) triggers veto.

Weather layer veto: \(\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{wind}}\|\) above roughly 8 m/s crosswind invalidates the straight \(L_{\text{virt}}\) assumption because drag and Magnus effects curve the trajectory; \(T_{\text{temp}}\) outside the training envelope changes air density \(\rho\) and court friction \(\mu\), altering bounce. When either weather variable exits the calibrated range, trajectory-dependent outputs are vetoed.

Adjudication: vetoes form a lattice. If any physical, biomechanical, or weather layer fires, the system falls back to \(P_0\) or flags epistemic uncertainty rather than emitting \(P_{\text{upd}}\). This prevents the feature set from overriding hard anatomical and fluid-dynamic bounds [PAGE p0031].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Screened central shot. \(N_{\text{screen}}(0.9144\text{ m}) \ge 1\), \(d_{GL} > 2\) m, \(\tau_{\text{shot}}\) = close-range kick from central portion [PAGE p0031]. Veto condition: the model assigns \(P_{\text{save}} > 0.5\) despite an intervening player inside the screening radius. Counterfactual prediction: because the screen removes the goalkeeper’s pre-shot visual set and adds deflection risk, \(P_{\text{upd}}\) collapses toward the unconditioned prior or is flagged low-confidence; the realized scoring distribution shifts upward relative to the unscreened baseline.

Scenario 2 — Tennis drop-shot edge case. \(D_{\text{hist}}\) contains high \(r_{\text{FH}}\) but sparse \(r_{\text{drop}}\); the player is at the baseline and the opponent is deep; ball flight time is below \(\tau_{\text{react}}\) [PAGE p0031]. Veto condition: \(P_{\text{upd}}\) remains moderate because \(M_{\theta}\) over-weights historical forehand completion rather than the edge-case drop-shot archetype. Counterfactual prediction: \(P_{\text{score}}\) for the drop shot spikes above 0.8; if the model fails to update, the veto triggers and the system defaults to the archetype-conditional prior for sparse edge cases.

Scenario 3 — Crosswind event. Outdoor hard court, \(\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{wind}}\| = 10\) m/s perpendicular to \(L_{\text{virt}}\) [PAGE p0031]. Veto condition: the virtual-line geometry is treated as straight while wind forces a curved trajectory. Counterfactual prediction: by the time the ball reaches the target plane, the impact location deviates by more than 0.5 m from the \(L_{\text{virt}}\) intercept; \(P_{\text{upd}}\) based on the unperturbed line is invalid, and the system falls back to a surface-specific historical baseline rather than a geometry-conditioned estimate.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
