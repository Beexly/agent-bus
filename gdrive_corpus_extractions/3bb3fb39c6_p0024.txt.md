A multi-week trap is embedded in treating James’s acute laboratory contrast as temporally invariant. Adaptation set-points A(x,t) recalibrate over days to weeks under chronic luminance or thermal backgrounds; thus a surround that produces simultaneous contrast on day one may produce little effect by week three as A shifts toward the new mean L. The “contrast constancy trap” assumes the acute effect size λ or Weber fraction k measured in a single session generalizes to multi-week environmental exposure. In thermal environments, seasonal acclimatization shifts skin temperature baselines and vasomotor tone, so Preyer’s lukewarm-water reversal may weaken or reverse after weeks of heat or cold acclimation. Any application that does not re-measure λ, k, and δT_thal after each multi-week adaptation epoch commits a retrospective causal error: it projects an acute contrast operator onto a chronically adapted subject whose A(x,t) has already been rewritten.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under high-stakes or time-pressured perception, an observer regresses to the immediately preceding sensory frame because successive contrast weights t−Δt heavily when cognitive resources for re-fixation are depleted. James’s claim that each sensation is “modified more or less by that just previously experienced” ([PAGE 14]) becomes a decision liability: the current stimulus is misread through the contrast shadow of the prior one.

Counter-trap: an adversarial actor can deliberately insert a priming stimulus (bright, dark, warm, or cold) to bias the subsequent judgment, exploiting the temporal gate Δt ∈ (τ_sacc, τ_ad). The trap is sprung when the target follows the prime within the adaptation window but outside conscious re-evaluation.

Clock: the decay of the bias follows E(t) = E₀ exp(−t/τ_ad). If the decision interval exceeds roughly 5τ_ad (≈ 0.5–50 s depending on modality and luminance), the successive-contrast coefficient α → 0 and the crisis regression effect vanishes. The clock therefore defines a “contaminated window”; outside it, the recursive theory of mind predicts no carry-over bias, while inside it the observer’s model of the current state is recursively contaminated by the previous state.
