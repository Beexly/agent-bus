James’ observation that changing attention between senses “only alters its direction between the sense-organs, leaving the rest of the head free from strain” [PAGE 138] implies \(\nabla \cdot \mathcal{A}_{ext} \approx 0\) in \(\mathcal{M} \setminus \partial \mathcal{M}_{ext}\) and \(\nabla \cdot \mathcal{A}_{ext} > 0\) at the attended organ.

Internal attention operator (memory/fancy):
\[
\mathcal{A}_{int}(t) = -\beta(t) \, \nabla \phi(\mathbf{x}), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{M}_{brain},
\]
where \(\beta(t)\) is the retraction gain (dimensionless) and \(\phi\) is a scalar potential centered in the brain parenchyma. James’ claim that vividness arises “in proportion as I retract it backwards” [PAGE 138] is modeled as
\[
V(I \mid t) = \sigma\!\left( \int_{\mathcal{M}_{brain}} \beta(t) \, dV - \lambda \int_{\partial \mathcal{M}_{ext}} \alpha_o(t) \, dS \right),
\]
with \(V \in [0,1]\) a dimensionless vividness probability, \(\sigma\) a sigmoid, \(dV\) volume element (\(m^3\)), \(dS\) surface element (\(m^2\)), and \(\lambda\) a coupling constant with units \(m^{-1}\). Breakdown: the equation is singular at \(\beta \to \infty\) or when \(\nabla \phi\) vanishes identically (no internal gradient), yielding \(V\) undefined.

Semi-circular canal operator (Fechner’s hypothesis, [PAGE 138]): Let \(\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\) be angular velocity in radians per second (rad/s) and \(\mathbf{c}(t)\) cupula displacement in meters. The canal signal \(S_{canal} \in \mathbb{R}^3\) encodes \(\dot{\mathbf{c}} = f(\boldsymbol{\omega})\). When changes are not perceived externally, the signal projects to “a vague internal space located within the head” [PAGE 138]. Formally this is a map \(\Psi: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathcal{V}_{int}\), where \(\mathcal{V}_{int}\) is an unmetricated internal volume. Because Fechner was ignorant of canal function at the time of his original explanation [PAGE 138], the operator is historically anachronistic and its domain is ill-posed.

Scalp-muscle operator (Fechner’s later description, [PAGE 138]): Let \(u_{scalp}(t)\) be normalized scalp muscle activation (\(0 \le u \le 1\), from EMG) and \(P_{cranium}\) be pressure in Pascals (Pa). Fechner’s “pressure from outwards in over the whole cranium” is modeled as
\[
P_{cranium}(t) = \kappa \int_{\partial \mathcal{M}_{scalp}} u_{scalp}(\mathbf{x},t) \, dA,
\]
with \(\kappa\) in Pa. James notes actual eye movements play a considerable part, adding an extraocular term \(u_{EOM}(t)\) in Newtons (N) of muscle tension.

Assumptions: (1) Introspective report is a noisy linear readout of efference copy and proprioceptive afference. (2) No cross-modal sensory substitution occurs during the reported interval. (3) Units are SI unless dimensionless. (4) The page contains no meteorological data; therefore no barometric or thermal operator can be derived from [PAGE 138].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of this page traces backward through several disciplinary strata. The immediate source is Fechner’s *Elemente der Psychophysik* (1860), cited at pages 475–6 and 490–91 in the footnotes of [PAGE 138], where Fechner attributes organic strain in attention to muscular contraction in the scalp and to a pressure “from outwards in over the whole cranium” [PAGE 138]. James inserts a critical historical caveat: Fechner was “ignorant of the but lately discovered function of the semi-circular canals” [PAGE 138], placing Fechner’s theorizing before the consolidation of vestibular physiology associated with Mach, Breuer, and Crum Brown in the 1870s. The physiological anchor is Foster’s *Text-book of Physiology*, book III, chapter V, section 2 [PAGE 138], which James invokes to validate the claim that semi-circular canals convey “the most delicate perception of change in direction” [PAGE 138]. The idiomatic residue “sich den Kopf zerbrechen, den Kopf zusammennehmen” [PAGE 138] shows that the phenomenology is linguistically embedded in German experiential grammar before it is translated into Anglo-American experimental psychology.

Multi-week trap: The page embeds a longitudinal risk trajectory. Fechner reports that during a former illness, when he “could not endure the slightest effort after continuous thought,” the muscles of the scalp, “especially those of the back-head, assumed a fairly morbid degree of sensibility whenever I tried to think” [PAGE 138]. This describes a positive feedback loop over days to weeks: cognitive effort -> scalp muscle contraction -> increased tenderness -> aversion to thought -> reduced cognitive endurance -> compensatory effort -> greater contraction. James’ own endorsement that “actual movements in the eyes play a considerable part” [PAGE 138] extends the loop to extraocular fatigue. The trap is not a single acute event but a recursive sensitization: repeated attempts to force memory retrieval via forward attention (which James shows is ineffective for vividness [PAGE 138]) recruit scalp and ocular musculature, producing a craniocentric pressure state that becomes the dominant cue for “effort,” thereby misdirecting future attentional strategy away from the backward retraction that actually produces vividness.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression: Under high cognitive load, the subject’s theory of mind about their own attention regresses to a peripheral somatic model. Instead of modeling memory retrieval as an internal retraction \(\mathcal{A}_{int}\), the subject attributes difficulty to “strain” localized in the scalp or eyes, mirroring Fechner’s illness narrative [PAGE 138]. The regression is self-fulfilling: monitoring for scalp tension increases tonic activation of the occipitofrontalis and extraocular muscles, which then generates afferent signals interpreted as evidence of effort, even though James reports vividness covaries with backward retraction, not forward strain [PAGE 138].

Counter-trap: The inverse error is to assume that because the feeling “withdraws entirely from the external sense-organs” [PAGE 138], no peripheral motor component exists. James explicitly interrupts this inference by noting that actual eye movements “play a considerable part” [PAGE 138]. A recursive theory of mind must therefore represent the subject’s meta-belief (“my attention is purely internal”) as potentially false while still being phenomenologically compelling. The counter-trap is to dismiss somatic correlates entirely and treat attention as a disembodied central process, which would falsely predict that paralyzing extraocular or scalp musculature leaves mnemonic vividness untouched.

Clock: There is an implicit temporal operator. Vividness of a recalled place or person is not instantaneous; it accrues “in proportion as” retraction is sustained [PAGE 138]. Define a dwell-time constant \(\tau\) (seconds) over which \(\beta(t)\) must remain above threshold for \(V(I)\) to approach asymptote. The clock veto operates if the subject aborts retraction before \(\tau\) because the felt “strain” is mislocalized to the scalp and interpreted as fatigue. In that case, the subjective report “I cannot recall it” reflects clock expiry, not memory absence. The recursive layer is that the subject then attributes the failure to insufficient forward effort, intensifying the very scalp/ocular tension that James shows is orthogonal to vividness [PAGE 138].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and climatological veto proof.

Physical layer (vestibular): The semi-circular canal signal encodes angular velocity \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) via endolymph displacement in the membranous labyrinth, with dynamics bounded by the cupula time constant (on the order of seconds). James’ text locates the feeling of attentional strain “within the head” and attributes it partly to “imaginary semi-circular canal sensations” [PAGE 138]. Veto condition: if attentional shifts between eye and ear occur with \(\boldsymbol{\omega} = 0\) (no head movement), canal afference cannot carry the directional information. Therefore the canal hypothesis alone cannot explain the inter-organ vibration of attention described in the first sentence of [PAGE 138]. The physical layer is vetoed as a sufficient explanation; it may at most contribute to the “vague internal space” during imagined rotations.

Biomechanical layer (somatic): Two motor systems are named. (i) Extraocular muscles: James states actual eye movements “play a considerable part” in his feelings of attention [PAGE 138]. Veto condition: if extraocular muscles are pharmacologically paralyzed and forward strain to real objects is abolished, James’ motor account is supported; if forward strain persists intact, the motor account is vetoed as necessary. (ii) Scalp musculature: Fechner describes craniocentric pressure from scalp contraction [PAGE 138]. Veto condition: if occipital nerve blockade abolishes the “pressure from outwards in” but the subject still reports backward retraction and vividness modulation, Fechner’s scalp theory is vetoed as sufficient for the mnemonic attentional feel. James’ own illness anecdote confirms scalp hypersensitivity can co-occur with impaired continuous thought [PAGE 138], but co-occurrence is not identity.

Weather/climatological layer: The source text [PAGE 138] contains no meteorological variables, barometric pressure readings, temperature, or humidity. Therefore no weather-derived veto can be extracted from this page. Any claim that barometric shifts alter semi-circular canal endolymph pressure and thereby modulate attentional localization would require external data not present in the drive source and is inadmissible under the page-bound constraint.

Adjudication: The text supports a composite, layered model rather than a unitary organ. External attention recruits exteroceptive anchoring plus extraocular efference; internal attention recruits central retraction plus, pathologically, scalp tension. No single layer can be vetoed in isolation without violating James’ explicit claim that the feelings are “highly composite” [PAGE 138]. The exact breakdown regime is simultaneous maximal loading of all three layers, at which point introspective localization becomes non-identifiable.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions. Each scenario specifies an intervention, the measurable variable, and the falsifying outcome. All are constrained to the phenomenology and physiology named on [PAGE 138].

Veto Scenario 1 — Extraocular paralysis during real-object attention.
Intervention: Transient reversible blockade of extraocular motor nerves (e.g., retrobulbar anesthetics) eliminating measurable eye movement (unit: degrees per second, deg/s; baseline > 0, post-blockade ≈ 0 deg/s).
Verifiable reward signal: Subjective report of “forward strain” to real objects on a 0–10 dimensionless scale, and objective fixation stability.
Prediction under James’ account [PAGE 138]: Forward strain is abolished or markedly reduced because the motor component is removed.
Veto condition (falsification): If forward strain remains at baseline intensity despite zero eye movement, the claim that actual eye movements “play a considerable part” is vetoed, and attention must be modeled as non-motoric at the exteroceptive layer.

Veto Scenario 2 — Vestibular ablation during imagined directional shifts.
Intervention: Bilateral semicircular canal dysfunction (e.g., gentamicin ablation) verified by absence of nystagmic response to caloric stimulation (unit: degrees per second, deg/s; post-ablation ≈ 0 deg/s).
Verifiable reward signal: Subjective report of “vague internal space” localization during attention shifts between senses, and report of directional strain “forwards” versus “backwards.”
Prediction under Fechner’s canal hypothesis [PAGE 138]: The vague internal space and directional quality of attentional strain collapse or become diffuse because the organ conveying “delicate perception of change in direction” is removed.
Veto condition (falsification): If the subject still reports clear forward/backward localization and inter-organ vibration despite zero canal signal, the canal hypothesis is vetoed as necessary for the spatial quality of attention.

Veto Scenario 3 — Scalp muscle anesthesia during effortful memory recall.
Intervention: Occipital and frontal scalp nerve block (verified by loss of pinprick perception, unit: von Frey force in millinewtons, mN; post-blockade threshold elevated) during attempts to recall a place or person.
Verifiable reward signal: Vividness of recalled image \(V \in [0,1]\) (dimensionless confidence rating), and subjective “pressure from outwards in” on a 0–10 scale [PAGE 138].
Prediction under Fechner’s scalp theory [PAGE 138]: Pressure report falls to near zero; if vividness is dependent on scalp contraction, \(V\) should fall correspondingly.
Veto condition (falsification): If pressure is abolished but vividness remains modulated by backward retraction (as James describes [PAGE 138]), then scalp muscle contraction is vetoed as the causal seat of mnemonic attentional feel, confirming James’ retraction model over Fechner’s organic seat model.

Each veto is verifiable within a single experimental session and maps directly onto clauses in [PAGE 138]; none invokes betting lines, spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
