- Breakdown: (i) undefined when $\mathbf{p}_i(t)=\mathbf{b}(t)$ or $\mathbf{p}_i(t)=\mathbf{g}$ (division by zero, $\arccos$ undefined); (ii) meaningless when tracking dropouts exist; (iii) non-invariant under pitch rotation.

**Operator 7 — Affordance landscape** (Withagen et al., 2012, p251, p254; cited [PAGE 17]–[PAGE 18]). Verbatim: "'manifold of action possibilities' (Withagen et al., 2012, p251)" [PAGE 17]; "'inviting potential' (Withagen et al., 2012, p254)" [PAGE 18]. No scalar metric is given; the only honest statement is the set predicate $\mathcal{A}_{\text{eff}}(t)=\{\,a:\ a\in\text{affordances}\ \wedge\ a\ \text{is realisable under effectivities of performer }i\,\}$. Breakdown: no falsifiable magnitude — treat as a qualitative design heuristic only.

**Synthesis.** Six of the seven operators reduce to measurable quantities (triad input, mutual information, search manifold, effective dimension, degeneracy count, angle). The seventh (affordance landscape) is preserved solely as a design predicate and must not be dressed up as a metric.

**Multi-week traps** the source itself enumerates:

1. **The 'pick & mix' trap** [PAGE 3]–[PAGE 4]: "adopting a 'pick & mix' approach could be a recipe for confusion, misconceptions and poor understanding" [PAGE 4]. The historical trap is multi-decade: motor-learning concepts from incompatible meta-theories get fused into a "smorgasbord of principles" that is neither Gibsonian nor cognitivist.
2. **The rote-repetition trap** [PAGE 12]: "This 'copy and paste' approach to practice and training is founded on historic ideas of rote learning which will only take a player a little way on their learning journey." It survives across seasons because it is legible to administrators (technical manuals, playbooks).
3. **The 'cheat codes and hacks' trap** [PAGE 13]: "Beware of (mainly online opinion) articles proclaiming the existence of 'cheat codes' and 'hacks' to bypass the lengthy learning process." This is a recurring multi-week lure whenever a new training gadget appears.
4. **The technique-optimisation trap** [PAGE 14]: "optimisation … infers that there is a unique movement execution of a technique that each learner is seeking to attain and comply with." The counter-position, "self-organising optimality" (Newell, 1986), has been available for decades yet re-emerges each cycle as "biomechanical perfection."

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Three stakeholder minds — coach, player, analyst — each with a documented regression-under-pressure pattern, a counter-trap, and a governing clock.

**Coach — habit regression under pressure.** The traditional verbs "[instructing, drilling, telling, informing, directing and prescribing solutions" are the regressions (verbatim [PAGE 15]: "coaches could consider re-imagining their main role, away from traditional methods of instructing, drilling, telling, informing, directing and prescribing solutions"). The trigger is loss of control over outcomes (defeats, injuries, board pressure). The counter-trap is the NLP design role: the practitioner as "a 'change agent channelling the dynamics that arise from the interaction of the learner with the environment in the realisation of a given task goal'" (Newell & Rovegno, 1990, p186, quoted [PAGE 15]) — "a learning designer in a nonlinear pedagogical methodology" [PAGE 15]. The clock is the multi-week / multi-season horizon on which search is realised; the source warns that "search activities of a learner should be ongoing and continuous … over the medium to long term" [PAGE 13].

**Player — habit regression under pressure.** Regression is reversion to memorised "plays recorded in a playbook manual" [PAGE 12] rather than searching. The counter-trap is "repetition without repetition" (Bernstein, 1967, referenced [PAGE 22]) and the design of "neutral (open, generative)" tasks so that affordances "appear and disappear depending on the ebb and flow of dynamics" [PAGE 17]. The clock is the developmental timescale — Thelen & Smith's (1993) instability/stability cycles [PAGE 22], Wormhoudt et al.'s (2018) post-Peak-Height-Velocity "re-learning" window [PAGE 22].

**Analyst — habit regression under pressure.** Regression is the racing-to-a-dashboard reflex: tracking metrics "such as the stretch index of an attacking team, logging the team centroid … using heatmaps, or surface area measures" [PAGE 10] get deployed without an ecological interpretation. The counter-trap is the eco-physical variable route — "player-ball-goal angles" [PAGE 27] — which is theoretically motivated and geometrically well-posed (Operator 6 above). The clock is the match timescale: [PAGE 27] notes "instability was identified … when the speed of the ball passing preceded a sudden change in the team configuration," i.e., analysts must resolve to the sub-minute scale.

**Recursion closure.** The three clocks interlock: coaching decisions on days-weeks modify the player's affordance landscape; the player's search outcome on weeks-months modifies which ecological variables the analyst can even measure; the analyst's report modifies the coach's next design. No single loop can be optimised in isolation — this is precisely the "transactional" claim of [PAGE 6] restated at the practitioner level.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A cross-layer veto is any physical, biomechanical, or environmental condition that (i) invalidates the stationarity of the constraint triad $O(t),T(t),E(t)$ or (ii) severs the perception-action loop. Each veto must be proven from source text, not assumed.

**Physical veto — maturation.** [PAGE 22] verbatim: "rapid growth in adolescence leading to fatigue, joint pains, disruption to sleep patterns, appearance of clumsiness due to changes in limb lengths and lack of strength." Proof: this is a simultaneous, multi-week perturbation of $O(t)$ that is neither controllable by the coach nor reversible. The veto consequence: any preset $\mathcal{F}$ trained on the pre-spurt organism is out of calibration; the source itself prescribes the response verbatim: "In periods of instability … coaches could temporarily focus on consolidating skills a little more and not challenge learners as much" [PAGE 22]. Multi-week; the Wormhoudt et al. (2018) "re-learn" prescription [PAGE 22] extends the veto to months.

**Biomechanical veto — efficiency floor.** [PAGE 14] verbatim on Sparrow (2000): "a major constraint on self-organising optimality is efficiency of movement." Proof: a search that ignores metabolic/variable cost produces solutions that are locally functional but globally non-viable over 90 minutes. The veto consequence: the search manifold $\mathcal{S}$ must be intersected with a metabolic-cost feasibility region. The source does not supply the cost function; the veto is therefore open at the numeric level and closed at the feasibility-direction level.

**Biomechanical veto — injury/chronic change.** [PAGE 14] verbatim: "as players age, or have to adapt to chronic physical changes as a result of conditions and injuries." Same structure as maturation but with a different clock — this can be permanent — so the veto must be modelled as a coordinate change in $O$ that invalidates any previous static map.

**Environmental/weather-equivalent veto.** [PAGE 14] verbatim: "events like rule changes, equipment re-design, as well as strategical and tactical innovations." Proof: $E(t)$ exhibits discrete jumps, so "prevailing constraints" [PAGE 14] no longer denote a stable reference. The veto consequence: no equilibrium coordination $\mathcal{C}^*$ exists across the jump; re-search on a multi-week window is mandatory. The open-systems claim [PAGE 9] (von Bertalanffy, 1950) authorises reading rule changes as environmental energy/information flux — a defensible extension of the source's own vocabulary.

**Composite adjudication.** When two vetoes coincide (e.g., maturation × tactical innovation), the safe posture is the source's own: reduce $T$-complexity [PAGE 22], preserve variability [PAGE 23], and hold the affordance landscape "neutral" [PAGE 17] until $O$ and $E$ stabilise. No numeric threshold is licensed by the source — this is an explicit gray zone.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three counterfactual vetoes, each stated as a testable prediction with a falsification criterion. All are grounded in source-verbatim claims; none invoke a ratio fabricated by the analyst.

**Veto 1 — Technique-optimisation veto.** *Claim on trial:* a coach implements a "one correct technique" program for a discrete skill (e.g., the chip; Chow et al., 2006, cited [PAGE 9]) — the [PAGE 13]–[PAGE 14] "unique movement execution" reading. *Source prediction:* the intervention maximally violates the [PAGE 20] claim "there is no ideal movement technique towards which all learners should aspire." *Counterfactual:* run pre- and post-intervention perturbation tests under a novel constraint (e.g., an altered goal height and a second defender). The optimised group should show narrower coordination variance and degraded transfer, because Bernstein's (1967, p. 228) dexterity criterion — "ability to find a motor solution for any external situation" [PAGE 21] — is *not* what a single-technique drill trains. *Falsification criterion:* if the single-technique group outperforms the search-designed group on the novel-constraint perturbation with $p<0.05$ and a non-trivial effect size, the Ecological Dynamics prediction is falsified. *Direction of failure if the prediction holds:* narrow $\mathcal{S}$, low $\mathcal{D}$, high transfer deficit.

**Veto 2 — Augmented-feedback-delay veto.** *Claim on trial:* immediate, every-trial augmented feedback after each action. *Source prediction:* [PAGE 19] verbatim — research has shown that augmented feedback "(i) does not need to be given after every practice trial, (ii) can be used to summarise learning … after every 5 or 10 trials or more, and most importantly, (iii) should be delayed." The theoretical mechanism is [PAGE 19]: "players need some time to perceive and think about the intrinsic feedback they have experienced." *Counterfactual:* compare retention at 24 h and 7 days for immediate-every-trial versus delayed-summary feedback groups. *Prediction:* the immediate group shows higher acquisition but lower retention; the delayed group shows the reverse (classic guidance hypothesis replicated inside the Ecological Dynamics framing). *Falsification:* if the immediate group's retention matches or exceeds the delayed group at 7 days, [PAGE 19] is defeated. *Cross-operator check:* this is the operator $\mathcal{I}_{I\!M}$ (Operator 2); immediate augmented feedback is predicted to *crowd out* the intrinsic channel, reducing $\mathcal{I}_{I\!M}$.

**Veto 3 — Passive-defender veto (representativeness).** *Claim on trial:* substitution of unopposed (11v3 style, [PAGE 12]) or passive-defender contexts for a representative game. *Source prediction:* [PAGE 25] verbatim — "Rather than using unopposed or passive contexts, incorporating more or less active defenders to create pressure could challenge a team to keep possession." The prediction follows from the affordance-landscape claim ([PAGE 8]: affordances of "player numbers … playing area dimensions … gaps … field locations … the goals") and the qualitative operator sum. *Counterfactual:* measure $\theta_i(t)$ (Operator 6, Carrilho et al., 2020) and shot-on-target distribution (or passing-lane utilisation, per Passos et al. 2020 [PAGE 8]) across three conditions: 11v3, 11v8 (passive defender), 11v11 representative. *Prediction:* passive/unopposed conditions compress the variance of $\theta_i$ and inflate the frequency of low-value actions (unopposed shots, uncontested dribbles). *Falsification:* if the angular variance and action-selection entropy are statistically indistinguishable across the three conditions, [PAGE 25] is defeated. *Breakdown guard:* if tracking quality is poor or players are pre-Peak-Height-Velocity adolescents whose $O(t)$ is non-stationary [PAGE 22], the test is uninformative and must be rescheduled.

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
