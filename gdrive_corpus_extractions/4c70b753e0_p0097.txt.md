Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week experimental traps.

The “usual rule” James describes on [PAGE 1] descends from Helmholtz’s doctrine of unconscious inference (1860s), in which perception is an inference from proximal stimuli to distal causes guided by prior experience. Wheatstone’s invention of the stereoscope (1838) and pseudoscope provided the empirical apparatus: by presenting disparate pictures to each eye, he isolated binocular disparity as a sufficient cue for solidity, and by swapping the eyes, he demonstrated that the sign of disparity is interpreted through a learned, probabilistic mapping rather than a direct optical readout. James’s 1890 Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, p. 87 [PAGE 1] synthesizes these optical findings with tactile illusions, arguing that both modalities share a common perceptive law: react to the sensation in a determinate, figured, and maximally probable fashion when possible.

The lineage continues through Gibson’s ecological optics (1950s–1979), which rejected the poverty-of-the-stimulus inference model in favor of direct pickup of invariant structure, and through Marr’s computational theory of vision (1982), which formalized stereopsis as a representation problem requiring a correspondence algorithm over disparity. Modern predictive coding (Rao & Ballard, 1999; Friston, 2005) recovers James’s structure under precision-weighted prediction error: perception minimizes free energy F = D_KL[Q(ψ|s) || P(ψ,s)], where the prior P(ψ) encodes the “usual rule” and the likelihood P(s|ψ) encodes the sensory signal. James’s claim that faces never appear hollow under the pseudoscope is exactly the prediction that a strong generative prior over facial convexity suppresses the disparity-driven likelihood.

Multi-week traps. Short-term laboratory exposures to pseudoscopes or crossed-finger tactile paradigms often produce immediate reversals, but longitudinal studies reveal consolidation traps. Repeated pseudoscope viewing over days to weeks can induce perceptual adaptation in which the hollow interpretation strengthens, then decays upon return to normal viewing—a hysteresis loop that violates the assumption of trial independence. Tactile recalibration after prism or crossed-finger exposure likewise remaps φ over sessions; a subject tested on day 1 and day 14 may show opposite double-touch reports despite identical stimulation, because the body-schema operator φ has been retrained. A multi-week experimental trap is therefore to treat the perceptive law as static when, in fact, the priors P(N) and P(shape | C) are themselves slowly varying latent states. Any protocol that ignores inter-session drift conflates state-dependent recalibration with moment-to-moment inference.

μ_post = ( γ_prior μ_prior + γ_likelihood μ_likelihood ) / ( γ_prior + γ_likelihood ).
During a crisis, γ_likelihood collapses because sensory evidence is under-sampled, so μ_post → μ_prior. For binocular vision, this means the face remains convex under the pseudoscope even when disparity strongly specifies hollow; for touch, the subject regresses to the uncrossed habit of reporting one object between pressed fingers. The regression is “habitual” because it reflects the most overlearned prior, not the current stimulus structure.

Counter-trap. The experimenter who presents a pseudoscope and expects a “hollow” report creates a second-order recursive loop. At level k=0, the subject reports the percept. At k=1, the subject models the experimenter’s expectation (“they want me to say hollow”). At k=2, the experimenter models the subject’s modeling and may discount enthusiastic “hollow” reports as demand characteristics. James’s caveat that the pseudoscope fails for faces “if it be an object which might probably be hollow, but not otherwise” [PAGE 1] is itself a counter-trap: the object class modulates whether the recursive inference terminates at depth inversion or at shape constancy. The stable fixed point of this recursion is not the raw percept but the percept filtered through mutually modeled incentives.

Clock. Perceptual decisions unfold on a temporal clock modeled by drift diffusion:
dX_t = v dt + σ dW_t, X_0 = 0, decision at X_t = ±a,
where v is evidence strength, σ is noise, and a is the threshold. For the pseudoscope face, v ≈ 0 because disparity (hollow) and prior (convex) oppose each other, so the mean decision time E[T] = a/v + T_0 diverges or produces switching. For tactile simultaneity, the clock is the binding window τ_bind: if two touches arrive within Δt < τ_bind (≈ 50–100 ms), they are bound into one event; if Δt > τ_bind, the clock resets and they are parsed as separate. Crisis load increases T_0 (non-decision time) and reduces v, prolonging the regression to prior-dominated habits.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

A veto predicate V ∈ {0,1} gates whether an illusion prediction is permitted. The illusion holds only if V = 1; if V = 0, the percept defaults to a unitary or flat interpretation.

Physical layer (optics). Stereopsis requires a non-zero baseline and resolvable disparity:
V_stereo = 1[ B > B_min ∧ |δ| > δ_min ],
with B_min set by interocular anatomy (≈ 0.06 m) and δ_min ≈ 5.8×10⁻⁴ rad (2 arcmin). If B → 0 (cyclopean viewing) or |δ| < δ_min, z = fB/δ diverges or is undefined, and the solid percept is vetoed. Units: B [m], δ [rad], z [m].

Physical layer (tactile topology). Two-point discrimination on the fingertip has threshold θ_2pt ≈ 2–3 mm. If ||p_a − p_b|| < θ_2pt, the nervous system cannot resolve two distinct contact locations, so the double-touch percept is vetoed regardless of finger crossing:
V_touch_topology = 1[ ||p_a − p_b|| > θ_2pt ].

Biomechanical layer. Mechanoreceptor adaptation time constants differ: slowly adapting SA1 (τ ≈ 100 ms) and rapidly adapting RA/Pacinian (τ ≈ 10–30 ms). If tactile events are separated by Δt > max(τ_SA1, τ_bind) ≈ 100 ms, the simultaneity operator fails:
V_touch_temporal = 1[ Δt < τ_bind ], τ_bind [s] ≈ 0.05–0.10 s.
Rapid sequential taps fall outside the binding window, collapsing “double” into sequential singles.

Environmental/weather layer. Temperature T and humidity H alter skin hydration and stiffness, scaling detection thresholds by κ(T,H) ≥ 1. The effective two-point threshold becomes θ_2pt·κ(T,H). In cold, dry conditions, κ rises, so:
V_environment = 1[ ||p_a − p_b|| > θ_2pt·κ(T,H) ].
If low humidity stiffens the skin sufficiently, two contacts that would feel double at 22 °C/50 % RH feel single at 5 °C/20 % RH.

Adjudication. The composite veto is V = V_stereo · V_touch_topology · V_touch_temporal · V_environment. Only when all factors equal 1 does James’s law (“react on the sensation in a determinate and figured fashion” [PAGE 1]) generate the predicted illusion; any zero factor forces a default percept (single touch, convex face, flat plane). This is a veto proof because it identifies necessary physical preconditions independent of subjective report.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) veto scenarios with counterfactual predictions.

Scenario 1: Crossed-finger temporal veto.
Independent variable: inter-stimulus interval Δt between contacts at somatotopically corresponding points a and b on crossed fingers. Dependent variable: proportion of “two touches” reports P_2(Δt). Verifiable reward signal: correctness relative to ground-truth object count.
Veto condition: Δt > τ_bind = 0.10 s.
Counterfactual prediction: For Δt ≤ 0.05 s, P_2 ≈ 0.6–0.8 (James’s “doubtful doubleness” [PAGE 1]); for Δt ≥ 0.15 s, P_2 ≤ 0.2 because the temporal binding veto fires and the contacts are parsed as successive single events. A verifiable experiment would show a sharp drop in P_2 near Δt = 0.10 s, rejecting the hypothesis that spatial crossing alone determines doubleness.

Scenario 2: Pseudoscope object-class prior veto.
Independent variable: object class C ∈ {face, cup, sphere}. Dependent variable: depth-sign report D ∈ {convex, hollow, flat/rivalry}. Verifiable reward: accuracy in matching the physical object’s sign under normal viewing.
Veto condition: P(shape = hollow | C) > 1 − ε, ε = 0.05.
Counterfactual prediction: Under the pseudoscope (δ′ = −δ), D_face = convex with P > 0.9 (prior veto on [PAGE 1]); D_cup = hollow with P > 0.8; D_sphere exhibits bistability or flat reports with P_flat ≈ 0.5 because P(hollow | sphere) ≈ 0.5 and neither prior nor likelihood dominates. The veto is verifiable by measuring the class-dependent report distribution and confirming that the face violates the disparity-sign prediction while the cup obeys it.

Scenario 3: Stereo baseline degradation veto.
Independent variable: effective interocular baseline B ∈ {0.02 m, 0.063 m, 0.10 m} via optical prism or camera rig. Dependent variable: relative depth threshold Δz/z at fixed viewing distance z = 1.0 m. Verifiable reward: depth-discrimination accuracy.
Veto condition: B < B_crit such that δ = fB/z < δ_min ≈ 5.8×10⁻⁴ rad.
Counterfactual prediction: At B = 0.02 m, δ falls below δ_min for z ≳ 0.7 m, so depth judgments are at chance (50 % correct) and the solid-percept prediction is vetoed; at B = 0.063 m, thresholds are ≈ 1 % of z (Δz ≈ 1 cm); at B = 0.10 m, thresholds improve to ≈ 0.6 % of z. The verifiable signature is a discontinuity in performance at the critical baseline where δ crosses δ_min, formalizing the singularity z = fB/δ as δ → 0.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
