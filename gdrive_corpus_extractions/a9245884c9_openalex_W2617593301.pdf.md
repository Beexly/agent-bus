Lineage begins with Nagumo (1942) [PAGE 5, Ref. 19], whose single-valued, time-independent contingent-cone condition is generalized here to time-dependent tubes \(P(t)\). The paper explicitly builds on Aubin’s Viability Theory (1991) [Ref. 2] and Aubin–Frankowska Set-Valued Analysis (1990) [Ref. 3], cited for tangent cones [PAGE 9], epigraph/hypograph identities [PAGE 14], and selection measurability [PAGE 18]. Vinter–Wolenski (1990) [Ref. 20] is the direct antecedent for Hamilton–Jacobi theory with data measurable in time [PAGE 6–7]. The viscosity branch traces Crandall–Lions (1983) [Ref. 8], Barron–Jensen (1987, 1990) [Refs. 4–5], Ishii, and Frankowska’s contingent Hamilton–Jacobi equation (1987) [Ref. 9]; this paper deliberately defines sub/superdifferentials without \(L^1\) test functions, contrasting with those approaches [PAGE 6]. Ledyaev (1989) [Ref. 17] provided earlier viability criteria via reachable sets, which the authors replace by pointwise Aumann-integral tangency conditions [PAGE 11–12]. Multi-week trap: in a rolling weekly re-solve, the full-measure set \(A\) may exclude a different null set each week; over multiple weeks the union of excluded impulse dates can have positive measure, so a trajectory viable week-by-week can still exit \(P(t)\) at a previously neglected discontinuity of \(P\) or \(F\). Another trap is importing time-independent Nagumo intuition into a nonautonomous setting: [PAGE 5] shows Nagumo’s condition is only sufficient unless \(P\) is absolutely continuous and \(F\) meets the measurable-time regularity. If the constraint tube is re-sampled weekly without verifying left absolute continuity between snapshots, the equivalence in Theorem 3.2 collapses and “viable this week” does not imply viable across the re-sampling boundary.
