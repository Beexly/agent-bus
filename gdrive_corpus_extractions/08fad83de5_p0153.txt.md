$$ \lambda^k = \alpha \implies \lambda = \alpha^{1/k} $$

$$ E = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \beta^i = \frac{\beta}{1-\beta} $$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s 1889 formulation sits at a precarious intersection of pre-Mendelian blending inheritance and the nascent field of biometrics. Its lineage traces back to Francis Bacon’s empiricism but is directly inspired by Darwin’s *variation* concepts. Galton sought to quantify "reversion," observing that grandchildren of eminent individuals were less eminent than their parents, who were less eminent than their grandparents—a pattern he mistook for a fundamental law of biology rather than a statistical artifact of selection and measurement error.

The "retrocausal" trap here is profound: Galton inferred the mechanism of inheritance (blending with a tax) from the outcome (regression to the mean). He assumed the effect dictated the cause. This led to the "Mid-Ancestor" fallacy—the idea that every ancestor contributes equally to the offspring, which would imply that ancient ancestors have just as much influence as recent ones if one sums an infinite series. Galton’s own wheat experiment was designed to test this. The fact that "pedigree wheat" reverted to the norm after a few generations without selective breeding proved that the "ancestral reservoir" was not a persistent force. This falsified the equal-contribution hypothesis and forced him toward the geometric decay model, where influence drops exponentially with generational distance.

For multi-week traps in modern contexts, this history warns against confusing **correlation structure** with **causal mechanism**. In financial time series or epidemiological models, observing a decay in autocorrelation over lags (like Galton’s generations) does not imply a specific physical "tax" or leakage process; it could simply reflect the mixing of many independent micro-processes (Central Limit Theorem effects). Galton’s error was reifying a statistical summary statistic into a biological tax law.
