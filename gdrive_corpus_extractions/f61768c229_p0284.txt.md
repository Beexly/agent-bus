Counter-trap: To escape regression, the agent must insert a model-based veto that interrupts M_regress(S) when interoceptive forerunners fail to match the expected quiescence or disturbance. Formally, if ||v(S) − v_expected(π_regress)|| > σ_v, the recurrent motor command is suppressed and a low-entropy search over Π⁺(S) or Π⁻(S) is re-initiated. The counter-trap succeeds only if the visceral feedback loop τ_v is faster than the disturbance onset τ_dist; otherwise the agent is caught in the regression before the veto can be computed.

Clock regime: The recursive theory of mind must resolve second-order beliefs within Δt_dec = τ_dist − τ_sign. If B’s sign S is ambiguous and A’s model of B’s 𝒰_B(S) has high entropy, A’s own decision latency increases, potentially violating the clock and forcing a default veto (freezing). This creates a vulnerability: a socially sophisticated agent can exploit A’s recursive uncertainty by presenting signs with P(Q|S) ≈ P(D|S), stretching Δt_dec beyond the physiological deadline.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Tolman’s doctrine requires “some sort of a positive engaging with the immediate sign” [PAGE 260]. This engagement is physically instantiated through incipient movement M(S) and visceral activity v(S), both of which are subject to hard constraints across three layers. A veto V ∈ {0,1} is issued if any layer blocks the commerce.

Layer 1 — Physical/energetic. The organism has an available metabolic energy budget E_available [J] at decision time. Executing incipient movement M(S) over duration Δt incurs cost E_cost(M(S), Δt) [J]. Veto V_E = 1 iff E_cost > E_available. Breakdown: sustained approach toward a pleasant sign under energy depletion produces approach-avoidance conflict because ℙ(S)=1 but V_E=1.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical. The musculoskeletal system has joint torque limits τ_limit,j [N·m] and velocity limits ω_limit,j [rad/s]. The required joint torque τ_j(M(S)) must satisfy |τ_j| ≤ τ_limit,j for all joints j. Veto V_B = 1 iff ∃j : |τ_j(M(S))| > τ_limit,j. Breakdown: a “slight movement of approach” [PAGE 260] toward a high sign may nonetheless require joint torques beyond limit if the sign is located behind an obstacle or at an extreme reach, converting affective approach into biomechanical impossibility.

Layer 3 — Weather/environmental signal degradation. The sign S is detected with signal-to-noise ratio SNR = μ_sign / σ_env [dimensionless], where σ_env includes weather-induced noise (rain, wind, thermal distortion) degrading the reliability of the “immediate sign” [PAGE 260]. Veto V_W = 1 iff SNR < SNR_min. Breakdown: when weather noise raises σ_env, the sign gestalt becomes unreliable; H(S|observation) increases, and the organism cannot discriminate ℙ(S) from 𝒰(S), forcing withholding of commerce even when both E and B layers permit action.

Adjudication proof: Total veto V = max(V_E, V_B, V_W). If V = 1, Tolman’s commerce C cannot be physically executed or the sign cannot be detected with sufficient fidelity; therefore the affective predicate ℙ(S) or 𝒰(S), though present at the cognitive level, is behaviorally inert. The system defaults to a null commerce (freezing) rather than risk an ungrounded “positive engaging.” This veto architecture respects the source’s under-specification: it does not prescribe *which* commerce to substitute, only that the asserted commerce is blocked when any layer constraint is violated, leaving the organism in the gray zone Tolman describes—where pleasantness asserts a good quiescence is reachable but neither the precise commerce nor its physical feasibility is guaranteed [PAGE 260].
