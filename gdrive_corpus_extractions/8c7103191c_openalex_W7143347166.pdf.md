### 1. Detection stage (Haar cascade and MTCNN)

The source names the detector family on [PAGE 3]: *"Player faces are detected and aligned using MTCNN or Haar-based classifiers."*

**Haar / integral-image operator.** For image intensity $i(x,y)$,
$$II(x,y)=\sum_{x'\le x}\sum_{y'\le y} i(x',y'),\qquad \text{units: intensity}\times\text{pixel}^2$$
Stage response $f_k=\sum_{\text{rect}} \sigma_{\text{rect}}\, II(\cdot)$, with $\sigma_{\text{rect}}\in\{+1,-1\}$ (dimensionless). Cascade rejection rule: reject if $f_k<\theta_k$.

Under the **assumption of stage independence** (false in practice, correlated stages share features):
$$P_d=\prod_{k=1}^{L}(1-\alpha_k),\qquad P_f=\prod_{k=1}^{L}\beta_k$$
where $\alpha_k$ = per-stage miss rate, $\beta_k$ = per-stage false-accept rate, both dimensionless. **Breakdown regime:** as $L$ grows, both products decay geometrically; correlated stages make the realized ROC strictly worse than the product model predicts, so the cascade's advertised operating point is unattainable. **Physical breakdown:** Haar features have no pose model — detection collapses for $|\psi|\gtrsim 45^\circ$ yaw and for faces below roughly $24\times24$ px.

**MTCNN** adds a Proposal–Refine–Output cascade with Non-Maximum Suppression:
$$\mathrm{IoU}(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|}\in[0,1],\qquad \text{keep if } \mathrm{IoU}<\tau_{nms}$$

> **Gray-zone flag:** the paper presents MTCNN (2016, multi-task CNN) and Haar (2001, boosted stumps) as interchangeable alternatives. They differ by ~15 years of representational capacity and by an order of magnitude in profile-face recall. Treating them as a single "or" hides the entire robustness question.

### 2. Embedding operator and triplet objective

[PAGE 3]: *"A DeepFace or FaceNet-based CNN model extracts 128-dimensional embeddings for facial representation."* Formally, with $\theta$ the network parameters:
$$f_\theta:\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times3}\to \mathcal{S}^{127}\subset\mathbb{R}^{128},\qquad \|f_\theta(x)\|_2=1$$
Embedding components are **dimensionless**; the constraint places the output on the unit 127-sphere.

Triplet loss with margin $\alpha$ (dimensionless, a squared-distance margin):
$$\mathcal{L}_{tri}=\sum_{i}\Big[\big\|f(x_i^a)-f(x_i^p)\big\|_2^2-\big\|f(x_i^a)-f(x_i^n)\big\|_2^2+\alpha\Big]_+$$

**Assumption:** identity is a well-defined equivalence class and intra-class variation forms a connected region of the face manifold.
**Singularity/breakdown:** (i) *triplet collapse* — if the miner supplies no semi-hard negatives, all triplets satisfy the margin and $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{tri}\to 0$ while the embedding remains discriminatively useless; (ii) *norm collapse* under unnormalized variants, where $\|f\|$ grows without bound and cosine similarity becomes numerically degenerate.

> **Gray-zone flag:** "DeepFace or FaceNet-based" conflates a *framework* (DeepFace wraps several backbones, some 4096-D) with a *specific architecture* (FaceNet = Inception-ResNet-v1, 128-D). Fixing "128-dimensional" while leaving the backbone open is not a specification.

### 3. Capsule routing operator

[PAGE 2]: *"a Capsule Network–based transfer learning model that preserved spatial hierarchies more effectively than traditional CNNs, improving facial identification accuracy under varying angles and occlusions."*

$$\hat{u}_{j|i}=W_{ij}u_i,\qquad s_j=\sum_i c_{ij}\hat{u}_{j|i},\qquad v_j=\frac{\|s_j\|^2}{1+\|s_j\|^2}\cdot\frac{s_j}{\|s_j\|}$$
$$c_{ij}=\frac{\exp(b_{ij})}{\sum_k \exp(b_{ik})},\qquad b_{ij}\leftarrow b_{ij}+\hat{u}_{j|i}\cdot v_j$$

All $u_i,\hat u_{j|i},v_j$ are **dimensionless** activation vectors; $b_{ij}$ are dimensionless log-prior couplings.

**Breakdown regimes (exact):**
- **Squash saturation.** $\dfrac{d}{ds}\left[\dfrac{s^2}{1+s^2}\right]=\dfrac{2s}{(1+s^2)^2}\to 0$ as $s\to\infty$. When a single parent capsule dominates, $\|s_j\|\gg1$ and gradients vanish — routing freezes at an arbitrary assignment.
- **Routing collapse.** With $N$ primary capsules and $r$ iterations, cost is $\mathcal{O}(Nr)$; when one $b_{ij}$ saturates, the softmax puts $c_{ij}\to1$ and the remaining parents receive no signal.
- **Small-$N$ degeneracy.** Capsule routing was designed for many primaries; in a tight face ROI with few high-level parts, agreement is dominated by noise.

> The "more effective than traditional CNNs" claim [PAGE 2] is a 2017-era assertion. The subsequent empirical record has not established capsule superiority at scale on large face benchmarks; the review adopts the ancestor's claim without the descendant's counter-evidence.

### 4. Similarity operator and its calibration surface

[PAGE 3]: *"embeddings are compared using cosine similarity to identify players with similar skills or styles."*

$$S_{\cos}(e_i,e_j)=\frac{\langle e_i,e_j\rangle}{\|e_i\|_2\|e_j\|_2}\in[-1,1]\quad(\text{dimensionless})$$

For unit-norm embeddings the metric is exactly Euclidean on the sphere:
$$\|e_i-e_j\|_2^2=2-2S_{\cos}(e_i,e_j)$$
*Consequence:* any claim that "cosine is more robust than Euclidean" is **vacuous** after $\ell_2$ normalization. The source implicitly normalizes and then implicitly claims a distinction it does not have.

Decision rule and error rates at threshold $\tau$:
$$\mathrm{FAR}(\tau)=\int_{\tau}^{1}p_{\text{imp}}(s)\,ds,\qquad \mathrm{FRR}(\tau)=\int_{-1}^{\tau}p_{\text{gen}}(s)\,ds,\qquad \mathrm{EER}:\ \mathrm{FAR}(\tau^*)=\mathrm{FRR}(\tau^*)$$

**Singularity/breakdown:** $\tau$ calibrated on a source distribution has **unbounded** FAR on a target distribution. If the target impostor mean shifts by $\delta$ along the embedding direction, then $\mathbb{E}[S_{\text{imp}}]$ increases by $\approx\delta$, and $\mathrm{FAR}(\tau)\to 1$ as $\delta\to 1-\tau$. There is no fixed threshold that is safe under broadcaster change, lighting change, or roster churn — which is precisely the [PAGE 4] challenge.

### 5. Multimodal fusion operator

[PAGE 2]: *"compares players using visual embeddings and statistical vectors"*; [PAGE 3] fuses "speech and visual data."

Distance-level fusion with weights $\mathbf{w}$:
$$d_{\text{fuse}}^2=\sum_{k} w_k d_k^2,\qquad \sum_k w_k=1,\ w_k\ge0$$
**Units problem:** $d_{\text{face}}$ is a cosine-derived chord on the unit sphere (dimensionless, range $[0,2]$); $d_{\text{stat}}$ is a distance in runs/wickets/strike-rate (units of cricket score). A linear combination is **dimensionally illegal** unless both are standardized or a learned metric $M\succeq0$ is fitted.

Score-level fusion under the conditional-independence assumption:
$$\log\frac{P(y\mid x_1,\dots,x_K)}{P(\neg y\mid x_1,\dots,x_K)}=\sum_{k}\log\frac{P(x_k\mid y)}{P(x_k\mid\neg y)}+\log\frac{P(y)}{P(\neg y)}$$
**Breakdown:** face appearance is *correlated* with era, team kit, and age — i.e., with the statistical features. When modalities are conditionally **dependent**, naive log-odds summation double-counts evidence and the fused posterior is systematically **overconfident**. The paper reports no independence test.

### 6. Ranking operator and the Bradley–Terry separation singularity

[PAGE 2]: *"PlayerRank, a model that leverages AI ranking algorithms to evaluate player positioning and performance within cricket games."*

Pairwise (RankNet-style) objective with $P_{ij}=\sigma(s_i-s_j)$:
$$\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{(i,j):\,y_i\succ y_j}\Big[\bar{y}_{ij}\log P_{ij}+(1-\bar{y}_{ij})\log(1-P_{ij})\Big]$$
Evaluation:
$$\mathrm{DCG@}k=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{2^{rel_r}-1}{\log_2(r+1)},\qquad \mathrm{NDCG@}k=\frac{\mathrm{DCG@}k}{\mathrm{IDCG@}k}\in[0,1]$$

**Exact singularity:** in the Bradley–Terry form $P(i\succ j)=\dfrac{e^{\beta_i}}{e^{\beta_i}+e^{\beta_j}}$, if some player $i^\*$ wins every comparison in the window, the likelihood is maximized only as $\beta_{i^\*}\to\infty$. The MLE **does not exist in the interior** — a textbook separation event. The reported ranking for that player is numerically arbitrary and monotonically compresses all other scores. **Remedy absent from the source:** a Gaussian prior (MAP) or a fixed $\ell_2$ penalty on $\beta$.

**Second breakdown:** non-transitive preferences. If head-to-head results are cyclic ($A\succ B\succ C\succ A$), no total order exists and any ranker's output is a projection artifact.

### 7. Forecasting / calibration

[PAGE 2]: *"Bhatia and Bala (2020) [8] presented a predictive model for assessing player performance using parameters such as recent form, venue conditions, and opposition."*

$$\text{Brier}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(p_i-o_i)^2\ \in[0,1]\ (\text{units: probability}^2)$$
$$\text{ECE}=\sum_{b}\frac{n_b}{N}\big|\mathrm{acc}(b)-\mathrm{conf}(b)\big|\ \in[0,1]\ (\text{dimensionless})$$
**Breakdown:** ECE is binning-dependent, non-differentiable, and not a proper scoring rule; it can be reduced without improving the forecast. Brier is proper but scale-dependent on base rates — a rare-event class (e.g., a once-per-tournament performance) makes Brier look excellent for a constant predictor.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Veto proof structure.** Model the pipeline as a composition $g=h_K\circ\cdots\circ h_1$ mapping scene radiance $X$ to decision $Y$. Any layer $h_i$ that is **information-destroying** induces a Markov chain $X\to Z_i\to Y$ with $Z_i=h_i(\cdots)$. By the **data processing inequality**:
$$I(X;Y)\ \ge\ I(Z_i;Y)$$
Therefore, if layer $i$ alone satisfies
$$I(Z_i;Y)\ <\ \frac{H(Y)-1}{\log_2(K-1)}\quad\text{(Fano floor, bits)}$$
then **no downstream operator of any capacity can recover the required error rate.** The veto is absolute and capacity-independent. This is the formal adjudication rule: *a cross-layer veto is valid iff the layer's output mutual information sits below the Fano floor for the required $K$ and $P_e$.* Model scaling, transformer upgrades [PAGE 4], and federated learning cannot override it.

**Layer A — sensor/photometric veto (illumination).** Shot-noise limited SNR in photons:
$$\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{\Phi\, A\, t_e\, \eta}{\sqrt{\Phi\, A\, t_e\, \eta}}=\sqrt{\Phi A t_e\eta}$$
with $\Phi$ = photon flux (photons·m⁻²·s⁻¹), $A$ = aperture area (m²), $t_e$ = exposure (s), $\eta$ = quantum efficiency (dimensionless). **Veto:** when $\mathrm{SNR}<20$ dB at the face ROI, the embedding's within-identity covariance inflates and $J$ collapses. Day/night transitions in cricket (floodlit ~10³ lux vertical vs. overcast daylight ~10⁴ lux) sit directly on this boundary. **Adjudication: sensor-layer veto, non-recoverable.**

**Layer B — biomechanical/geometric veto (motion blur).** Angular rate $\omega$ (rad·s⁻¹), focal length $f$ (m), pixel pitch $p$ (m·px⁻¹), exposure $t_e$ (s):
$$L_{\text{blur}}=\frac{f\,\omega\, t_e}{p}\quad\text{(pixels)}$$
**Veto criterion:** the point-spread function must not exceed half the projected inter-ocular distance,
$$L_{\text{blur}}\ \ge\ \tfrac12\, d_{\text{IOD}}^{\text{px}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{veto}$$
with adult inter-ocular distance $\approx 32$ mm projected through the camera model. At broadcast focal lengths a fast head turn during a bouncer delivery routinely violates this. **Adjudication: geometric veto — the identity cue is convolved away before any network sees it.**

**Layer C — biomechanical/occlusion veto (helmet grille).** The periocular region carries a disproportionate share of identity information. Define occlusion fraction $\eta_{\text{occ}} = A_{\text{occluded}}/A_{\text{ROI}}$ (dimensionless). **Veto:** $\eta_{\text{occ}}\gtrsim0.35$ over the eye band. *(Flagged honestly: 0.35 is an engineering threshold from face-recognition practice, not a value reported in this source; the source reports no occlusion numbers at all despite claiming capsule nets improve accuracy "under varying angles and occlusions" [PAGE 2].)* **Adjudication: valid veto, and the paper's own claim is untested against it.**

**Layer D — weather veto (rain, fog, glare).** Koschmieder contrast attenuation over path length $d$ (m) with extinction coefficient $\beta$ (m⁻¹):
$$C(d)=C_0 e^{-\beta d}+L_f\left(1-e^{-\beta d}\right)$$
**Veto:** $C(d)/C_0 \lesssim 0.2$ at subject distance. Rain droplets on the lens add a multiplicative speckle field; sun glare saturates the sensor locally ($C_0\to$ clipping) and destroys texture in exactly the region the embedding needs. **Adjudication: photometric veto, non-recoverable — DPI applies.**

**Layer E — codec veto (compression).** Broadcast delivery at constrained bitrate ($\sim$8–20 Mbit/s) places blocking artifacts preferentially in high-motion macroblocks. **Veto:** when quantization step $Q$ exceeds the local texture contrast in the eye band, the high-frequency identity features are quantized to a constant. **Adjudication: valid, and entirely absent from the source's [PAGE 4] challenge list.**

Because the DPI bound is capacity-independent, the review's proposed remedy — *"Developing transformer-based visual recognition, multimodal fusion models, and federated learning approaches"* [PAGE 4] — **cannot** discharge these vetoes. The correct intervention is upstream: capture geometry, exposure policy, ROI pixel budget, and an explicit reject option. That is the cross-layer adjudication.
