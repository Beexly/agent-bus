Counterfactual: Two spatially separated observers, A and B, each perform a counting operation on a distinct set of units and then communicate results to a third observer C who applies Grassmann’s axiom (a+b)+1 = a+(b+1) [PAGE 654] assuming a unique temporal order of the two countings.
Veto: If the two counting events are spacelike separated, no invariant temporal order exists; the expression “operated on in the same way” [PAGE 654] lacks a frame-independent referent.
Verifiable prediction: Place atomic clocks at A and B and synchronize via Einstein convention; spacelike-separated counting events will show order reversal under Lorentz boost. A prediction that depends on a global “same way” temporal sequence (e.g., a distributed ledger incrementing a counter without a consensus protocol) will produce frame-dependent final values, observable as divergent counter readings after Lorentz transformation. The veto is verifiable by GPS satellite clock desynchronization, which already requires relativistic correction because simultaneous counting is not absolute.

Scenario 3: Continuum/singularity veto.
Counterfactual: A physical quantity (e.g., volume of liquid in a vessel) is assumed to obey James’s axiom that the matter forms a series “which can be cut into by us wherever we like without the matter changing” [PAGE 654], and that integer counting units combine invariantly.
Veto: On a continuous domain with Lebesgue measure, the counting measure is singular; arbitrary subdivision produces non-measurable decompositions, and additive integer units do not exist for the continuum.
Verifiable prediction: Attempt to divide one liter of water into exactly three equal countable units by iterative halving or marking; at some subdivision scale, surface tension, molecular discreteness, and thermal fluctuations break the invariant unit assumption. More formally, the Banach–Tarski decomposition shows that a solid ball can be partitioned into a finite number of non-measurable pieces and reassembled into two balls of the same radius, violating the intuition that combinations of units preserve quantity. Experimental verification: measure the volume after such decomposition with an interferometric displacement method; the measured value will not equal the integer sum of the sub-unit counts because the sub-units are not measurable in the sense required by James’s axiom. This verifies that the singularity regime of continuous measure vetoes the discrete counting axiom.

Each scenario is falsifiable with standard laboratory equipment (interferometer, atomic clocks, precision volumetrics), and each identifies an exact breakdown regime rather than a speculative ratio.
