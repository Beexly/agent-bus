$$t^\star = \arg\max_{t < t_{\text{lock}}} \mathbb{E}\big[V(\mathcal{I}_t)\big] - c(t_{\text{lock}} - t)$$

where $\mathcal{I}_t$ is the information set at time $t$ and $c(\cdot)$ is the cost of a missed deadline. **Recursive field modelling.** In rank-order contests the payoff depends on other entrants' lineups, which depend on their models, which consume the same public statistics. The correct object is a fixed point over the field distribution $Q$:

$$U(x)=\sum_k \pi_k \Pr\big(\text{rank}(x)=k \,\big|\, x, Q\big),\qquad Q = \Phi\big(\{x^{(j)}\}_{j\neq i}\big)$$

Maximizing $\sum S_i x_i$ is optimal only if $\pi_k$ is linear in points — which it is not in convex payout structures. A lineup that maximizes expected points is systematically over-owned and therefore under-paid. The paper reports no uniqueness term and no field model at all.
