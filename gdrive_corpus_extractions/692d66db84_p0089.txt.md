<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Two recursive models operate simultaneously. The experimenter models the rat as possessing an expectancy E(g | x_{1:t}) about goal location g given path x_{1:t}; the rat, in a purposive-behavior account, models the experimenter’s reward contingency r_t. The formal belief update is
\[
p(g \mid x_{1:t}) = \frac{p(x_{1:t} \mid g) p(g)}{\sum_{g'} p(x_{1:t} \mid g') p(g')},
\]

The decisive discriminator is transfer to a novel start-blind with the same goal location: model-based expectancy predicts immediate reorientation; pure habit predicts path dependence on the training start.

The clock variable is explicit in Watson’s delayed feeding [PAGE 71] and Warden & Haas’s short feeding-delay intervals [PAGE 71]. Let Δ be the delay between response and food (s). Biomechanical layer P_bio: running speed v (m/s) must remain in a viable interval [v_min, v_max]; deprivation d must be below critical d_c; weight, sex, and pigmentation covariates [PAGE 70] included as regressors because they correlate with individual maze differences. Environmental/weather layer P_env: for laboratory work this leaf reports no weather, but for field generalization ambient temperature T_a (°C), relative humidity (RH %), and barometric pressure must be within pre-set tolerances ε_T and ε_RH; absence of weather controls in the source is itself an external-validity limitation.

Veto predicate:
\[
V = \mathbb{1}\!\left[ \neg P_{\mathrm{phys}} \lor \neg P_{\mathrm{bio}} \lor \neg P_{\mathrm{env}} \right],
\]
where V = 1 vetoes the cognitive inference and V = 0 permits it only after covariate adjustment. Singularity regimes: v → 0 (immobility) makes all speed- and latency-based maze measures undefined; d → d_c makes physiological distress dominate motive variables, invalidating the single-drive operator; obstruction removal latency τ → ∞ indicates motor failure rather than choice. If any veto fires, the claim “rats learned a cognitive map” is rejected pending a layer-specific control experiment.

Scenario 1: Reward removal versus non-reward [PAGE 70]. Counterfactual: rats trained on rewarded short versus long blinds are shifted to non-reward. Prediction under model-based expectancy: choice proportions shift immediately toward the previously reinforced goal location without additional training, because the map was already latent; performance decline should not equal extinction of a single habit. Verifiable veto: if choice proportions do not change within the first N non-reward trials, or if short- and long-blind running speeds change by the same multiplicative factor, the motor/deprivation confound veto fires and the cognitive-map claim is rejected. Reliability gate: ρ ≥ 0.80 for blind-order counterbalancing; blinded scoring required.

Scenario 2: Degree of hunger manipulation [PAGE 70]. Counterfactual: deprivation d is set below, near, and above d_{1/2} for the same rats across sessions. Prediction: P(d) rises with d until d_{1/2}, then declines as d → d_c due to distress. Verifiable veto: if P(d) increases monotonically up to the point of session termination without the predicted turnover, or if sex/thirst controls [PAGE 71] are not matched for deprivation-equivalent motivation, the single-drive scalar is falsified and hunger effects cannot be isolated from general arousal.

Scenario 3: Delayed feeding credit assignment [PAGE 71]. Counterfactual: food reward is delivered after delays Δ = {0, 30, 60, 120} s following maze completion. Prediction: learning rate decreases approximately as exp(-Δ/τ_decay), with τ_decay in seconds; long delays produce acquisition curves indistinguishable from non-reward controls even when motivation is unchanged. Verifiable veto: if Δ has no effect on acquisition, the delayed-feeding operator is inactive for this apparatus; if the effect is explained by total session time rather than Δ, the temporal-credit-assignment claim is vetoed. Stopping rule: pre-register equivalence intervals for Δ effects; failure to fall inside them rejects the causal claim.
