<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The document's genuine "theory of mind" is the modeling of the **broadcast director's conditioned editorial habits**. The detection hypothesis only works because the director is *predictable*: on a score he cuts to a reaction-phase, suppresses the scoreboard to update it, and replays from multiple angles. On [PAGE 90]: "the programme director utilizes this crea[tion]-p[h]ase (RP) segment to capture the responses of players and/or crowds." The system is, in effect, a second-order belief model: "if a score occurred, the director will *show* reactions, therefore reaction-shot cues imply a score." The "clock" maps precisely to the reaction-phase seek window $\text{RPSW}=24\text{ s}$ ([PAGE 90]), a hard temporal prior bounding the director's habit.

"Counter-trap vs crisis-habit regression" maps to the **false-positive regression in slow-paced genres**. The director's *same* habit (cutting to close-ups/crowd/replays) fires constantly during ordinary stoppages in low-tempo games, so the model regresses to over-triggering. The author's own diagnosis: "the slower paced games tend to exhibit a relatively higher sporadic abundance of the features critical to the SUE model deployed, and as a consequence their genres are relatively more challenging" ([PAGE 194]). This is quantitatively confirmed: rugby (slowest) has the worst optimum $\delta_{opt}=32.8$, $C=0.4$; hurling/soccer (fastest) the best $\delta_{opt}=22.4/24.6$, $C=0.75/0.70$ (Table 7.3, [PAGE 193]). The lower optimum $C$ for rugby is the tell — a "softer margin" is needed because positive and negative points overlap more (director-habit collision), exactly as the author argues ([PAGE 195]).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three genuine cross-layer robustness ("veto") proofs, each with its stated regime.

**Physical / camera-geometry layer.** End-zone recognition is vetoed onto real geometry: global views are "cameras held in a fixed overhead position" ([PAGE 110]), so end-zone field lines "assume certain angles" within a narrow band. Proof obligation met empirically: measured end-zone line orientations fall in $[5^\circ,25^\circ]$ with "only a negligible number… mapped outside the interval" (Fig. 5.28, [PAGE 156]); verified extractions $\theta=12^\circ$ (rugby) and $\theta=17^\circ$ (hurling) (Figs. 5.24–5.25, [PAGE 149]–[PAGE 150]). **Breakdown:** placed kicks "assume an abnormal view (e.g. behind the target)" ([PAGE 94]), which is why end-zone coincidence is only 74.1% (Table 4.8, [PAGE 95]).

**Biomechanical layer.** Two proofs. (i) Skin-tone invariance: hue interval $[10^\circ,55^\circ]$ is claimed race/nationality-independent ([PAGE 100]), enabling CuC. (ii) Human-response bound: "the average human responds to a stimulus within 0.75s–1.0s" ([PAGE 130]) justifies the 0.5 s / 13-frame smoothing on SBAL — long enough to kill "fleeting noise spikes," short enough to track vocal dynamics. **Breakdown:** the close-up model assumes "an ideally centred player with little or no occlusion," so "sharp discrimination involving close-up views of a non-ideal nature cannot be expected" ([PAGE 124]).

**Weather layer.** RFPC luminance binarization explicitly vetoes weather: "a fixed threshold may be unreliable for varying image brightness/contrast, which is typically a consequence of varying weather conditions" ([PAGE 113]). The counter-measure is a self-calibrating threshold — "it is assumed the mode luminance of this set corresponds to the prevailing luminance of the grass… using this mode luminance value as a threshold" ([PAGE 113]). This makes grass/line separation invariant to first-order illumination changes; residual risk is only when field-line and grass modal luminances converge (low-contrast conditions), which the document does not quantify — an unproven edge.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Frame note: RLVR (verifiable-reward RL) is anachronistic to this 2006 thesis, so I supply the transferable part — three *verifiable, falsifiable* counterfactual vetoes with concrete predictions, each grounded in the author's own stated failure modes (not invented).

**Veto 1 — Off-screen scoreboard update.** The MVM feature only fires on *suppression*. Counterfactual: a broadcast that updates the tally on-screen produces $\mathrm{MVM}\approx$ present-level ($\sim0.42$–$0.58$), so VCC3 stays low and the SUE is not corroborated. Verification/prediction: the thesis states suppression occurs in "61%" of SUEs ([PAGE 94], Table 4.7), i.e. "for the remaining 39%… the aforementioned technique will be unsuccessful" ([PAGE 209]). Falsifiable prediction: on a broadcaster that never suppresses, recall for scoreboard-cued SUEs should drop to the crowd/audio/VAM-only floor; expect SUERR to fall materially below the 91.3% global figure for that source.

**Veto 2 — Commentator-silent, crowd-quiet goal.** CF3 assumes vocal/energy surges. Counterfactual: an away goal announced flatly with a muted crowd yields SBAL below the broadcast mean → VCC2 low. Verification: audio-energy peaks were present in only 85.4% of SUEs (Table 4.6, [PAGE 93]); the document concedes "the energy dynamic of commentator vocalisations" is the intended cue ([PAGE 103]). Falsifiable prediction: for the ~15% of SUEs lacking an audio peak, detection depends entirely on VCC1/VCC4/VCC5; ablating VCC2 should hurt those cases disproportionately while barely moving the 85% majority — an ablation the paper does not run, so it is a clean, testable prediction.

**Veto 3 — Resolution/bitrate recalibration of CF4.** The PSB threshold "AC-DCT count ≥ 10" and "48 blocks" are CIF/bitrate-specific. Counterfactual: at higher resolution or lower bitrate the coefficient statistics shift. Verification: Table 5.8 ([PAGE 159]) states recalibration is "possibly required for bitrate constraints lower/higher" and "required for scenarios with non-CIF video image resolution." Falsifiable prediction: applying the frozen constants to HD or heavily re-compressed FSV will raise MVM false-discrepancies and inflate CRR error without retraining; the effect is monotonic in the resolution/bitrate delta from the CIF/[1.5 Mbps] operating point ([PAGE 76]).
