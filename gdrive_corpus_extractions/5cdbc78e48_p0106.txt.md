Recursive theory of mind layer. The organism forms a model M_O(E) of the environment’s reinforcement contingency E. The environment, through repeated pairings, imposes a model M_E(O) on the organism’s expectancy. When the organism misattributes a secondary reinforcer (superstitious chain), it enters a self-confirming loop: M_O(E) predicts reward from S_k, behavior is emitted, and absence of primary devaluation temporarily preserves the illusion. The loop breaks only when a veto event—temporal gap, association inconsistency, or effector failure—reveals that M_O(E) was misaligned with M_E(O). Hull’s molar distinction that secondary reinforcement is “associated … with stimulation” while primary is “associated with the cessation of stimulation” [PAGE 106] captures the recursive sign flip: the organism chases the secondary stimulus as if it were the goal, forgetting it is merely a pointer to the primary clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A secondary reinforcement chain is behaviorally viable only if it passes three independent veto layers. Failure in any layer is absolute: V_k = 0 for that chain regardless of associative strength in the other layers.

Physical layer (neural signaling). Synaptic transmission, axonal conduction, and plasticity induction have finite latencies (milliseconds to seconds). Hull’s requirement of “consistent and repeated association” [PAGE 106] presupposes that the nervous system can bind S_k and S_{k+1} within an integration window. If the inter-stimulus interval exceeds the neural trace duration, or if the total chain duration outlasts the behavioral relevance of the need state, the physical layer vetoes the chain. Hard constraint: every ISI_i must fall within the primary gradient window τ_max ≈ 30 s [PAGE 106], or the primary signal cannot reach that link.

Biomechanical layer (effector plant). Hull asserts that “A receptor-effector conjunction involving any effector may be reinforced by any secondary reinforcing situation” [PAGE 106]. This is a molar claim that ignores effector constraints. Veto: if the required response topography is outside the organism’s motor repertoire, exceeds force-velocity or fatigue limits of the musculature, or requires anatomical degrees of freedom the organism lacks, the association cannot be expressed. Example: a secondary reinforcer cannot establish a lever-press in an organism without the digits to manipulate the lever, no matter how consistent the pairing. The effector must be physically able to close the receptor-effector loop within the temporal window.

Environmental/weather layer (context stationarity). “Weather” here denotes non-stationary ambient forcing—lighting shifts, odor changes, social disturbance, temperature drift—that alters the stimulus situation S between trials. Stable and consistent association [PAGE 106] requires S to be recognized as identical across repetitions. If context drift makes S_{t} and S_{t+1} discriminably different, the association operator T fails because c drops toward zero. This veto is independent of neural or effector integrity: even a perfect brain and a capable body cannot associate two stimuli the environment presents as categorically distinct.

Adjudication rule. A proposed chain passes iff Physical = PASS AND Biomechanical = PASS AND Environmental = PASS. Any single veto collapses V_k multiplicatively to zero. This is a strict conjunctive proof, not a compensatory trade-off.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with formal predictions. RLVR = Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Relations; each scenario is falsifiable via standard operant measures (response rate, latency, suppression).

Scenario 1: Contingency corruption veto.
Intervention: Randomly decouple S_k from S_{k+1} on 50% of trials, so association consistency c = 0.5 while preserving all other parameters.
Formal model: V_k = V_P^0 (η · 0.5)^k. With η = 0.9, after k = 4 links V_4 ≈ V_P^0 · 0.066, below typical response thresholds.
Prediction: Terminal stimulus response rate collapses to near-baseline within 50 trials; chain length observable behaviorally contracts to k ≤ 2. Verification: measure response rate to S_4 before and after corruption; predicted reduction >90% relative to control.
Breakdown regime: c < c_crit where η c falls below the geometric decay threshold for visible reinforcement.

Scenario 2: Temporal gap extension veto.
Intervention: Increase delay between the secondary stimulus and primary need reduction to Δt = 60 s (> τ_max = 30 s) [PAGE 106], while keeping pairings consistent (c = 1).
Formal model: G_P(60 s) = 0 by the hard singularity of the primary kernel; therefore no secondary value can be bootstrapped at that link.
Prediction: No conditioned response to S emerges after 20 pairings; response rate remains at pre-training baseline. Verification: compare acquisition curves for Δt = 15 s (control, acquisition expected) versus Δt = 60 s (veto, no acquisition).
Breakdown regime: τ > τ_max; primary gradient is identically null, making secondary transmission impossible at that node.

Scenario 3: Primary devaluation veto.
Intervention: Satiate the drive (e.g., food satiety) so need reduction no longer reduces homeostatic deficit; set V_P^0 → 0 while keeping stimulus associations intact (c = 1, η = 1).
Formal model: V_k = 0 · (1 · 1)^k = 0 for all k despite perfect associative structure.
Prediction: Previously established secondary reinforcers lose all reinforcing power; subjects cease responding to them within 10 non-primary-reinforced exposures. Verification: extinction of secondary-reinforced responding should be rapid and indistinguishable from never-trained controls.
Breakdown regime: V_P^0 = 0 annihilates the chain at the root; secondary reinforcement is parasitic on primary value [PAGE 106].

Each scenario yields a binary verification (response present/absent above threshold) and maps to a distinct singularity in the formal operators: R2 (temporal), R1 (associative consistency), and root devaluation (V_P^0).
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
