Recursive theory of mind here means modeling the theorist modeling the organism, with attention to what happens under explanatory crisis. When confronted with a complex instinct (hive-bee comb-building, ant caste behavior) [PAGE 683], the human mind faces a crisis of explanation: the trait is too intricate to have arisen by chance, yet too teleological to be easily derived from blind variation. In this crisis state, the theorist regresses to a “habit” explanatory schema—use it or lose it, practice makes perfect, acquired effects are transmitted—because that schema matches the intuitive psychology of skill acquisition within a single lifetime. This is crisis habit regression: the theorist’s own cognition reverts to a Lamarckian, within-lifetime learning model precisely when the ultimate evolutionary problem demands a cross-generational, population-level model.

The counter-trap is to assume that because habit regression is intuitive, it must be wrong in all cases. The correct position is domain-specific: habit acquisition operates on τ_behavior ≈ seconds-to-days and shapes individual skill; natural selection operates on τ_genetic ≈ generations and shapes species-typical instinct. The counter-trap would be to deny any role for plasticity in evolution (e.g., rejecting the Baldwin effect or gene-culture coevolution outright). James himself avoids this by acknowledging that under changed conditions “slight modifications of instinct might be profitable” [PAGE 683], leaving a narrow door for variation that selection can then act upon.

Clock analysis: define τ_acq as the time for an individual to acquire a behavior through practice (minutes to days), and τ_gen as the mean generation time (weeks to years). The evolutionary clock ticks in units of τ_gen. For an acquired habit to influence the evolutionary clock, it must alter the germline-encoded probability distribution over behaviors before the next tick. In neuter insects, τ_gen for the worker phenotype is infinite (no reproduction), so the clock never receives the worker’s acquired signal [PAGE 683]. In domestic breeds, τ_gen is short (cows, rabbits, ducks reproduce within years), but unless h² > 0, the acquired change is washed out each tick.

Recursive loop: The theorist observes the organism’s crisis behavior (e.g., a duck not flying), models it as habit-driven, then projects that model onto the origin of the species’ instinct. The recursive correction is to instantiate two models: (1) an intra-generational learning model L_t for the individual, and (2) an inter-generational selection model S_g for the population. Crisis habit regression occurs when the theorist substitutes L_t for S_g under cognitive load. The counter-trap is to insist S_g is the only valid model, thereby ignoring that L_t can expose standing genetic variation (via the Baldwin effect) which S_g then captures. The clock adjudicates: only changes that survive at least one tick of τ_gen and alter germline-inherited parameters count toward the origin of instinct.

Verification protocol: Cross-foster worker larvae of a eusocial species (e.g., honeybee or ant) so that experienced foragers raise naïve brood, and separately measure whether the offspring generation of workers shows improved foraging efficiency relative to a control brood raised by naïve workers. Genotype the queens and workers to confirm relatedness and sterility.
Prediction: Offspring worker foraging performance tracks the queen’s genotype and the colony’s genetic composition, not the practice history of the prior worker generation. If improved foraging appears in offspring without corresponding queen genotype change, the veto is falsified.
Counterfactual: In a world where worker practice were heritable, colonies with experienced foragers would produce superior daughter workers within one generation; the veto predicts no such transgenerational boost.

Scenario 2 — Domesticated duck wing-bone reduction ([PAGE 683]).
Veto condition: Reduced wing-bone size from ancestral disuse is a somatic/developmental phenotype; forcing domestic ducks to fly increases wing loading and bone strain phenotypically, but offspring bone size is determined by parental additive genetic variance, not parental flight history.
Verification protocol: Common-garden experiment with two lines of domestic ducks: Line A exercised to fly daily; Line B kept flight-restricted. After several months, collect eggs from both lines, incubate and raise all offspring identically without flight training. Measure humerus and sternum dimensions in offspring.
Prediction: Offspring wing-bone dimensions show no significant difference between Line A and Line B offspring; parent-offspring regression of bone size yields h² > 0 based on genetic pedigree, while the flight-history coefficient is statistically indistinguishable from zero. If exercised parents produce offspring with proportionally larger wing-bones under identical rearing, the veto is falsified.
Counterfactual: A Lamarckian disuse/inheritance model predicts exercised parents yield larger-boned offspring; the veto predicts environmental effects are erased in the common garden.

Scenario 3 — Cavefish eye degeneration and darkness ([PAGE 683]).
Veto condition: Obsolete eyes in cave animals are not produced by inherited disuse alone; they result from mutation accumulation and selection in darkness, or developmental plasticity that is reset each generation. Surface fish raised in darkness do not transmit eye reduction to offspring unless genetic eye-reduction alleles are present.
Verification protocol: Raise surface-dwelling fish (with functional eyes) in complete darkness for multiple generations. In parallel, maintain a light-reared control line. Every generation, outcross a subset of dark-reared fish to light-reared fish and quantify eye size, lens apoptosis, and visual behavior in F1 and F2 under common light rearing. Perform QTL mapping for eye size.
Prediction: Dark-reared fish show plastic eye reduction within a lifetime, but F1/F2 offspring raised in light recover normal eye development according to their genotype; QTLs for eye size segregate independently of parental rearing environment. If dark-reared parents produce blind offspring even when those offspring are raised in light and lack eye-reduction alleles, the veto is falsified.
Counterfactual: Pure disuse inheritance predicts progressively smaller eyes across dark-reared generations regardless of genotype; the veto predicts reversion to the genetic mean when the environmental darkness is removed.
