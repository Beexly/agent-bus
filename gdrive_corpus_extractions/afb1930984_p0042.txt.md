<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage plus multi-week traps.

The patent's sensing stack inherits from distinct historical lineages. Photoplethysmography (PPG) optical heart-rate sensing dates to Herz (1929) and was later miniaturized for wearables (Allen, 2007; Poh et al., camera/contact PPG). Accelerometer-based actigraphy for sleep/wake classification originates in the 1970s (Kripke et al., 1978), where wrist motion absence is used as a proxy for sleep. The thermoregulatory sleep-onset model (Krauchi & Dijk) posits that distal vasodilation and a drop in core-to-distal temperature gradient precede sleep; [PAGE 11] operationalizes this as "body temperature drops significantly when an individual is about to fall asleep." Galvanic skin response / electrodermal activity traces to the psycho-galvanic reflex (Jung, early 1900s) and modern EDA wearables. The modular hot-swappable battery architecture echoes earlier portable-electronics designs but is claimed here for "continuous" physiological monitoring without strap removal [PAGE 11–12].

Multi-week traps arise from underspecified temporal boundaries. [PAGE 11] quotes "around a week" for the removable battery and "around a day" for the built-in battery, but provides no Wh rating, discharge curve, or duty-cycle assumption. Over a multi-week observation window, a user who charges the removable battery externally and fails to re-dock the head portion will experience a ~24 h strap-only window followed by total data loss once the built-in battery depletes. Because sleep detection is nightly, a single missed night irrecoverably deletes sleep-architecture metrics (latency, continuity, HRV during sleep). Additionally, comparing aggregate activity to "previous recordings" and "other users" [PAGE 11] creates a reference-drift trap: as the user's fitness or health changes over weeks, μ_user and σ_user shift, and the population baseline may become demographically mismatched. The system presents these comparisons as stable norms, but they are non-stationary, so week-over-week trend interpretation is invalid without recalibration.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock structure.

Crisis habit regression: under physiological or psychological crisis (high stress, illness, elevated HRV depletion), users regress to low-device-engagement behaviors—removing the strap to charge, setting it aside because of discomfort, or neglecting to re-dock the modular head portion. [PAGE 11] asserts the system is designed for "continuous wear and usage," yet this assumption is exactly most likely to fail when continuous data are most valuable. The model of the user embedded in the patent assumes rational, consistent wear; the recursive theory of mind must instead predict compliance collapse precisely at high-stakes moments.

Counter-trap: the modular design contains an embedded counter-trap. Because the head portion can recharge the strap while docked [PAGE 12], the user is incentivized to reattach it. However, if the user charges the removable battery externally and leaves the head portion undocked, the strap continues heart-rate monitoring via its built-in battery "for around a day" [PAGE 11]. The user observes a functioning HR readout and infers the full system is operational, while GPS, display, and head-portion context are absent. Sleep detection may still run on the strap (it can sense temperature and GSR [PAGE 12]), but the loss of head-portion modalities degrades multimodal confidence. The trap is a partial-function illusion: the system appears alive but is semantically crippled.

Clock structure: the battery architecture imposes an asymmetric temporal clock—removable ≈ 7 days, built-in ≈ 1 day. This creates a 7:1 recharge duty ratio. Sleep episodes recur every ~24 h; therefore any undocking interval exceeding the built-in battery life coincides with at least one complete nocturnal sleep window. The singularity occurs at time t* when E_built(t*) = 0 during the user's habitual sleep interval: from t* onward until re-dock and recharge, HRV and sleep staging are irrecoverably missing. The patent's "continuous monitoring" claim is thus a piecewise-continuous function with scheduled discontinuities determined by user charging behavior, not by the hardware alone.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

Physical layer veto: optical PPG requires stable emitter-detector-skin coupling. If the strap loosens during doffing, high-impact sport, or tissue compression, the optical signal-to-noise ratio collapses. Accelerometer-based motion filtering [PAGE 11] cannot recover cardiac information when the optode is decoupled from the skin or when motion artifact occupies the same spectral band as the pulse (running cadence ≈ HR fundamental). Veto rule: if SNR_PPG < threshold or contact impedance indicates poor coupling, the HR output is vetoed and flagged as motion-corrupt or offline.

Biomechanical layer veto: sleep inference uses "lack of activity according to data collected by the accelerometer" [PAGE 11]. This cannot discriminate sleep from motionless wakefulness (reading, meditation, post-exercise rest, illness). Near-zero accelerometer variance combined with reduced HR and temperature drop is necessary but not sufficient for sleep. Veto rule: sleep onset is declared only if the multivariate predicate holds AND a temporal-context gate (e.g., user-set bedtime window or absence of wakeful interaction) is satisfied; otherwise output is "rest/indeterminate," not sleep.

Weather and environmental layer veto: [PAGE 11] uses a thermometer for body or skin temperature and GSR. Skin temperature is confounded by ambient temperature, humidity, radiative heat load, and peripheral vasomotion unrelated to sleep. GSR is confounded by thermoregulatory sweating, caffeine, and emotional arousal. Veto rule: if |T_skin − T_ambient| < δ_thermal or if ambient temperature shifts rapidly, the temperature-drop sleep cue is vetoed; if GSR rises concurrently with high ambient temperature, the GSR cue is attributed to thermoregulation, not psychogenic or sleep-state change. The combined sleep classifier must return indeterminate when environmental confounds cannot be excluded, rather than forcing a binary sleep/wake decision.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 (spectral-overlap HR veto): Counterfactual — the user runs at 180 steps/min (3.0 Hz) while true HR is 160 bpm (2.67 Hz), measured simultaneously by an ECG chest strap. Patent optical HR uses accelerometer filtering [PAGE 11]. Prediction: because cadence and cardiac fundamental overlap, the adaptive filter cannot separate them; optical HR error exceeds ±15 bpm or exhibits dropout. Verifiable veto: optical HR output is nulled during the overlap interval; ground-truth divergence >10 bpm confirms the veto.

Scenario 2 (sleep-vs-rest false positive veto): Counterfactual — the user lies motionless in a cool (18 °C), dark room for 120 minutes while awake and reading. Patent sleep rule uses temperature drop, low GSR, no accelerometer activity, reduced HR [PAGE 11]. Prediction: the system classifies this as sleep onset, overestimating total sleep time by ~120 min relative to self-report or EEG. Verifiable veto: without a bedtime-context gate, the sleep classifier is vetoed and output is relabeled "quiet wakefulness/rest."

Scenario 3 (battery continuity veto): Counterfactual — the removable battery is depleted and the head portion remains undocked for 30 hours. Patent claims continuous monitoring via combined battery sets [PAGE 12] and rates built-in power "for around a day" [PAGE 11]. Prediction: the strap expires at ~24 h, producing a data gap from hour 24 to hour 30 that includes one full nocturnal sleep period; HRV and sleep metrics for that night are missing. Verifiable veto: the claim of continuous 7-day monitoring is falsified; measured data completeness over the 7-day window falls below 100% (roughly <92% if one full day plus night is lost), vetoing the continuity guarantee.
