Physical layer veto: if intervals represent time windows or spatial extents measured by an apparatus with resolution \(\varepsilon > 0\) (SI unit: meter or second), then any interval with \(b - a < \varepsilon\) is physically indistinguishable from \(\varnothing\). The semiclosed-versus-closed distinction collapses below \(\varepsilon\), vetoing the exact-endpoint bookkeeping that Theorem A assumes.

Biomechanical layer veto: model a joint range of motion as \([a,b)\) in degrees. Proprioceptive thresholds mean \(b-a\) below the sensor’s just-noticeable difference is vetoed as null. Weather layer veto: precipitation intervals \([t_{\text{start}},t_{\text{end}})\) have radar- and gauge-resolution uncertainty. Adjacent weather events may merge or split within error bars, so the finite disjoint class \(\{E_i\}\) is not empirically verifiable.

\exists \text{ layer } L,\; \delta_L > \min\Bigl(\min_i (b_i-a_i),\; b_0-a_0\Bigr)
\Rightarrow \text{Theorem A is empirically unverifiable at layer } L.
