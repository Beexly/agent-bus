This lineage extends forward through Tolman and Honzik's latent-learning experiments, through O'Keefe and Nadel's (1978) hippocampal cognitive-map theory, and into modern working-memory and episodic-future-thinking research. Each link shifts the unit of analysis from associative strength to representational content: from "does the rat remember?" to "what format is the memory in, and what operations can it support over a gap?"

Multi-week traps. A systematic error in this research tradition is extrapolating second- to minute-scale laboratory delays to week-long retention intervals without modeling systems-level consolidation, reconsolidation, and sleep-dependent stabilization. A second trap is treating species differences (rats vs. apes vs. children) as quantitative rather than qualitative shifts in representational format.
