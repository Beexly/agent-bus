<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Theoretical lineage: Bass and Avolio’s transformational leadership (individual consideration, intellectual inspiration) and Schein’s (2010) conception of leaders as architects of culture and structure provide the organizational behavior foundation. Berri et al. (2009) established that sports outcomes are confounded by roster quality, motivating the residual approach. Fort et al. (2008) critiqued tenure-based evaluation; Brown et al. (2020) linked coaching experience to playoff efficiency; Zhou et al. (2024) examined player-turned-coaches. Methodologically, Breiman (2001) and Hastie et al. (2009) validate ensemble predictive modeling; Uhl-Bien et al. (2007) complexity leadership and Sirmon et al. (2007) resource orchestration justify treating coaching as emergent, system-level coordination rather than isolated tactics.

Multi-week traps: An NBA season spans roughly 30 weeks and 82 games, during which the preseason baseline decays. Opponent adaptation is a first-order multi-week trap: teams accumulate film on a coach’s tendencies, so a strategy effective in November may be neutralized by February; the model’s preseason features cannot capture this within-season coevolution. Injury accumulation is a second trap: per-minute prior-season statistics assume player durability, but tissue fatigue compounds non-linearly across weeks. Load management decisions interact with the schedule (back-to-backs, four-in-five nights), yet the model deliberately excludes in-season contextual factors, assigning their management to the coach. Third, roster turnover via trades and waiver-wire acquisitions after the preseason invalidates the fixed-roster assumption; the margin cannot distinguish a coach who successfully integrates a deadline acquisition from a general manager who made the acquisition. Finally, the post-2015 era shift toward three-point-centric play means the mapping from preseason features to outcomes is non-stationary across the sample; a coach effective in the 2000s may appear average in the 2020s not because leadership declined but because the tactical environment changed.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under sustained losing streaks or high-leverage games, coaches tend to regress to default play-calling habits rather than optimize situationally. This habit formation is predictable to opponents who have scouted the coach over prior weeks. Counter-trap: if the opponent correctly anticipates the coach’s regression, they can design a counter-scheme that exploits the default tendencies. A coach with higher-order theory of mind should recognize this and deviate from the habit, but the coaching margin metric aggregates outcomes over 82 games and cannot identify whether such recursive adaptation occurred in any specific week. Clock: two clocks operate. The game clock governs end-of-period substitution and play-call timing—micro-decisions invisible to preseason features. The season clock governs job security and trade-deadline urgency; a coach on the hot seat may alter rotation patterns or risk-taking in ways that change the margin without reflecting underlying leadership capability. Because the target variable is season win differential and inputs exclude in-game score differential, time remaining, and opponent adjustments, the model cannot separate strategic leadership from tactical end-game execution or from forced changes induced by proximity to season end. Recursive failure mode: p̂_itg treats opponent behavior as fixed by preseason statistics, but in reality opponent defensive schemes adapt to the coach’s known offensive system within the same season, violating the stable-unit-treatment-value assumption at the game level.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical layer: NBA games are indoors, so meteorological weather is a null veto; however, arena microclimate (humidity, altitude) and travel-induced circadian disruption are non-zero but unmodeled. The decisive physical layer is player load and injury biomechanics: minutes per game, back-to-back frequency, and acute versus chronic load determine tissue failure risk (e.g., Achilles, ACL, load-related muscle strain). The paper explicitly excludes in-season injuries and travel, arguing that a coach’s capacity to manage them is itself leadership. This creates a veto condition: if an exogenous physical shock S_it (e.g., a season-ending Achilles tear to a primary ball-handler unrelated to load management) occurs, then E[M_it | S_it] ≠ 0 and the residual is biased downward regardless of coaching quality. Conversely, a coach who reduces minutes to prevent injury may lower short-term win probability but preserve long-term health; the season-level margin conflates these opposing effects. Biomechanical veto proof requires partialling out injury-games-lost and travel-zone crossings before attributing residual variance to the coach. Without injury-report and load-monitoring covariates, the coaching margin cannot distinguish (a) coaching failure from (b) physical-layer collapse or (c) fortunate health. The model’s claim that excluding in-season factors isolates “implicit systems-thinking” is only valid if physical shocks are independent of coach identity, an assumption violated when certain coaches systematically manage older or injury-prone rosters.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards counterfactual veto scenarios:

Scenario 1 (Mid-season coaching replacement): Team retains roster but replaces head coach after game 41. Baseline Ŵ_it was computed for the full season under the predecessor’s system; the residual is assigned to the successor for the second half. Verifiable prediction: margin variance will spike at the replacement date, and coaches inheriting mid-season vacancies will show systematically wider margin distributions than full-season coaches even after controlling for roster strength. If verified, the team-season margin is not a stable coach-level attribute.

Scenario 2 (Exogenous star injury): A prior-season All-Star suffers a season-ending non-contact injury in game 3. Model expected wins assume a healthy star; actual wins drop. Verifiable prediction: M_it will correlate negatively with games lost to star-player injury even after including coach fixed effects and roster quality controls. A significant coefficient on injury-games-lost implies the margin measures roster health rather than leadership capability.

Scenario 3 (Trade-deadline roster turnover): Preseason features exclude players acquired after the trade deadline. Verifiable prediction: teams acquiring high-impact players at the deadline will exhibit positive margin spikes in the post-deadline segment uncorrelated with pre-deadline coach behavior. If the margin remains predictive after interacting with acquisition quality, the metric captures general-manager roster moves as much as head-coach leadership.

Each scenario is falsifiable using play-by-play, injury, and transaction logs; failure to reject the null in all three would be required before treating M_it as a causal leadership measure.
