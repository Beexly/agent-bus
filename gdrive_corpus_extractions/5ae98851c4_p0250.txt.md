Level 2 is the observer’s belief about the subject’s belief: if parents believe temper is fixed and prepotent, they may selectively report or even elicit temper-consistent behavior in children, creating a self-fulfilling family likeness that Galton attributes to biological prepotence but which is partly rater-constructed.

The counter-trap is the opposite overcorrection: attributing all sibling resemblance to prepotence or genes while ignoring Galton’s second deviation—the unnamed process in which “all the children are alike and yet their common likeness cannot be traced to their progenitors” [PAGE 1]. This counter-trap treats F_i as zero when it may be a shared rearing or de novo effect, thereby inflating heritability estimates. The clock is the timescale of observation: short windows (days to weeks) register prepotent clusters and apparent stability of a “new variety” [PAGE 1], whereas long windows (months to years) expose regression toward the population mean and the dissolution of environmentally induced fraternity clusters. A recursive trap occurs when the researcher assumes, as Galton does, that ancestors “married at haphazard so far as their tempers were concerned” [PAGE 1]; if subjects in fact use temper cues in mate choice, the baseline independence model is wrong, and every downstream inheritance estimate inherits that misspecification. The analyst must therefore model not only the transmission of temper but also the transmission of beliefs about temper, because the measurement instrument (human rater) is itself a recursive participant in the phenotype.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: temper in Galton’s text is a categorical, observer-inferred disposition; there are no biomechanical operands (force, lever arm, muscle activation, joint torque) and no meteorological variables. Consequently any claim that temper is physically analogous to strength, endurance, or weather-dependent performance is vetoed at the physical layer by absence of dimensionality: temper is not measured in SI units (meters, kilograms, seconds, newtons) and admits no force-balance equation. Weather veto: if temperament ratings were collected across seasons, ambient temperature, photoperiod, or barometric pressure could co-vary with mood-related ratings and with family-assessment batching, generating spurious “family likeness” because siblings assessed together in winter might all be rated “fretful” for meteorological rather than hereditary reasons. This vetoes a purely genetic reading of within-family temper correlation unless season is controlled. Biomechanical/observer layer: the rater is a physical instrument subject to fatigue, lighting, and time-of-day effects; long rating sessions can produce systematic categorical shifts that masquerade as ancestral “threads” [PAGE 1]. Cross-layer adjudication verdict: Galton’s inferences are inferential and pedigree-based, not instrumented; therefore the physical layer vetoes any strong claim of direct biomechanical determination of temper, and the weather/shared-assessment veto requires that familial temper patterns be replicated across independent assessment seasons and raters before prepotence or a fraternity-wide variety can be accepted as the source. Breakdown regime: if ambient covariates explain sibling temper covariance better than parental prepotence, the “family likeness” is environmental and the biological interpretation is invalidated.

Scenario 1 (Prepotence falsification). Formal model: fit a random-intercept temper model Y_{ij} = \mu + u_i + \varepsilon_{ij}, u_i \sim N(0,\sigma_f^2), \varepsilon_{ij} \sim N(0,\sigma_e^2), yielding ICC = \sigma_f^2/(\sigma_f^2+\sigma_e^2). Prediction under Galton’s prepotence: in families with one clearly dominant progenitor, ICC exceeds 0.40 and parent–offspring regression slopes differ by which parent is prepotent. Veto condition: if a likelihood-ratio test finds ICC indistinguishable from zero (p > 0.10) and the mother–offspring and father–offspring slopes are statistically equal at approximately 0.5 regardless of which parent shows stronger temper expression, then prepotence is vetoed; the data instead support independent assortment or blending with rapid regression. This is verifiable from family temper scores and does not rely on betting lines or odds.

Scenario 2 (Haphazard-marriage falsification). Formal model: contingency table C_{ab} for marriages between temper classes a and b; test of independence C_{ab} \propto n_a n_b using a log-linear model with association parameters \lambda^{TM}_{ab}. Prediction under Galton’s “married at haphazard” claim for the main good/bad divisions: \lambda^{TM}_{ab} \approx 0 after collapsing the five smaller classes [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: if the likelihood-ratio G^2 statistic rejects independence (p < 0.01) or the same-division versus cross-division association parameter deviates from unity by more than the sampling error implied by counts 6 and 13 [PAGE 1], then random mating with respect to temper is vetoed and assortative mating must be included as a confound in inheritance estimates.

Scenario 3 (Fraternity-wide variety falsification). Formal model: compare three correlations: siblings reared together r_{sib, together}, siblings reared apart r_{sib, apart}, and parent–offspring r_{po}. Prediction under Galton’s unnamed fraternity factor: r_{sib, together} > r_{po} and r_{sib, apart} \approx 0, because the likeness affects the whole fraternity but is not traceable to progenitors [PAGE 1]. Veto condition A: if r_{sib, apart} \approx r_{sib, together} > 0, the factor is transmitted vertically (genetic or parental), vetoing the de novo fraternity interpretation. Veto condition B: if r_{sib, together} \approx 0 while r_{po} > 0, the stable-family-variety claim is vetoed and transmission is strictly intergenerational. Either veto removes Galton’s “new variety … stable variety” [PAGE 1] as a standalone explanation and forces inclusion of measured shared environment or genotypic relatedness.
