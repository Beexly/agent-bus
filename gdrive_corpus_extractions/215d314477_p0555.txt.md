V(x; λ_I) = V_0(x) + λ_I \, Θ(x - x_c) \exp\!\left[-\frac{(x-x_c)^2}{2σ_x^2}\right]

λ_I [J] is idea-induced well depth (formal energy analog), σ_x dimensionless width, Θ Heaviside. Overdamped Langevin dynamics:

γ \frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x} + ξ(t), \quad \langle ξ(t)ξ(t')\rangle = 2γ k_B T_{\text{eff}} δ(t-t')

γ [kg·s^{-1}] effective friction; T_eff [K] effective cognitive noise temperature (mathematical device, not physiological claim); k_B [J·K^{-1}]. Mean escape time from the compulsive basin (Kramers form):

τ_{\text{esc}} \sim \frac{2πγ}{\sqrt{|V''(x_{\text{barrier}})|\,V''(x_{\text{min}})}} \exp\!\left(\frac{ΔV}{k_B T_{\text{eff}}}\right)

ΔV [J] barrier height. Assumptions: Markovian diffusion in a one-dimensional cognitive coordinate; scalar λ_I captures semantic load; T_eff is not literal body temperature. Breakdown/singularity: if λ_I/(k_B T_eff) → ∞ then τ_esc → ∞ and the subject is captured in the compulsive basin; the model fails when the semantic content of the idea (dirty hands vs. unlocked door) modulates behavior in ways a scalar potential cannot represent, or when cognition is non-Markovian.

Formal model B — Precommitment constraint set. Action space A; passion-maximizing action a^* = argmax_{a∈A} U_direct(a) with long-term utility U_L(a^*)<0. The agent constrains action to C ⊂ A, a^* ∉ C. Physical cords enforce C biomechanically. The masquerade adds a social-cost term:

U_{\text{total}}(a) = U_{\text{direct}}(a) - β \, S(a; \text{Public})

β dimensionless weight on shame; S dimensionless social exposure. If Public=0, S→0 and the constraint is only self-enforced; the same will that imposed C can rescind it (akrasia). Breakdown: β→0 when audience or publicity is removed; C becomes porous and the precommitment collapses.

Formal model C — Doubt-checking loop (bounded information). Binary state D∈{0,1} (door locked). Belief b_t = P(D=1 | o_{1:t}). Each check yields observation o_t with channel capacity C_bits [bits/check]. Anxiety state:

A_{t+1} = (1-δ)A_t + γ(1 - b_t)

δ∈[0,1] memory decay per trial [dimensionless]; γ [anxiety units] per unit uncertainty. Checking continues until A_t < θ. But belief update per check is bounded: Δb ≤ C_bits/\ln 2 [nats]. Breakdown/singularity: if the required certainty demands b_t > 1-ε with ε smaller than the achievable uncertainty floor set by channel capacity and memory decay, then no finite number of checks terminates the loop; the system is structurally non-terminating and exhibits a checking limit cycle. This is an information-theoretic bound, not a toy ratio.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 545] is explicitly citation-dense. James anchors the opening anecdote in "Autobiography, Howells' edition (1877), pp. 192-6," placing the cords-and-jailer episode within 1870s autobiographical literature rather than clinical case notes. He then bridges to contemporaneous experimental psychology: Dr. Cowles's paper on "Insistent and Fixed Ideas" in the American Journal of Psychology, vol. I, p. 222, and Dr. Knapp's "Insanity of Doubt," ibid., vol. I, p. 1, which Knapp supplies with "a partial bibliography of the subject" [PAGE 545]. These are 1880s American sources, situating James at the birth of scientific psychology's interest in obsessional phenomena.

Scientifically, the text anticipates a genealogy running from James's "insistent ideas" to Pierre Janet's "fixed ideas" and psychological tension, to Freud's obsessional neurosis, and forward to contemporary OCD phenomenology: intrusive egodystonic thoughts, neutralizing compulsions (washing, checking), preserved insight ("not in the least deluded intellectually"), and pathological doubt ("insanity of doubt"). The multi-week trap operates through temporal scaling. Acute episodes on [PAGE 545] last "whole hours" of binding or "two or three hours" of toilet; severe cases consume "the whole day at the wash-stand." But James's phrase "wear the patient's life out" indicates chronicity across weeks and months: the compulsion is not a discrete event but a recursive trap in which each night's bedtime doubt produces checking, each morning's ritual consumes time, and over weeks the patient's sleep, social life, and occupational function are reorganized around the insistent idea. The trap is therefore multi-scale: seconds for the thought, minutes-to-hours for the ritual, days-to-weeks for life degradation. Any retrospective analysis must track this nesting of timescales rather than treat the compulsion as a static symptom.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under emotional crisis—the "paroxysms of my rage" [PAGE 545] or the bedtime worrying doubt [PAGE 545]—the subject regresses from reflective deliberation to a fixed, automatic motor schema. In the anecdote, the crisis is passion for the lady; the regressive safety behavior is the physical binding itself, which the subject had previously devised. In the compulsive cases, the crisis is the insistent idea; the regressive habit is washing or door-checking. The habit temporarily reduces anxiety but reinforces the idea, creating a second-order dependency: the subject needs the crisis ritual to escape the crisis thought.

Counter-trap: the subject engineers a trap for his own future self. The cords are a physical counter-trap against the future action of leaving the house; Elias holds the release key, making the constraint exogenous during the high-risk state. The masquerade as Apollo is a social counter-trap: by publicly turning the attachment into ridicule, the agent raises the anticipated shame of renewal above the value of the passion [PAGE 545]. This is second-order volition capturing first-order desire. However, the counter-trap is only as strong as its enforcement condition: without Elias, or without publicity, the trap is self-administered and can be disarmed by the same will that set it.

Clock: the checking loop runs on its own oscillator that competes with the circadian sleep clock. Bedtime normally triggers sleep onset; the doubt inserts a checking routine that delays sleep. The compulsion clock (minutes per check, repetitions until anxiety falls below threshold) phase-locks to the worry rather than to circadian need. In regression, the habit is captured by the compulsion clock; in the counter-trap, the subject attempts to gate the compulsion clock with an external constraint (cords, audience). The three form a recursive theory of mind: crisis generates the regressive habit, the counter-trap attempts to inhibit it, and the clock determines whether the inhibition succeeds or whether the ritual consumes the night and, over weeks, the life.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: on [PAGE 545], the cords concealed under the mantle create a hard physical constraint on egress. The subject cannot leave the house despite the imperative passion because the body is biomechanically bound; only one hand remains at liberty, insufficient for untethering and departure without Elias. This is an absolute veto at the motor-execution layer while the jailer enforces the secret.

Biomechanical veto: repetitive hand-washing and repeated dressing/undressing impose cumulative physical cost—skin irritation, fatigue, time displacement. The text's warning that the insistent idea "may wear the patient's life out" [PAGE 545] implies that the body eventually vetoes through exhaustion or tissue damage, although the compulsion can override acute discomfort until sleep deprivation or dermatitis forces interruption.

Weather veto: the source text [PAGE 545] contains no meteorological variable. The carnival and theatre masquerade are described without reference to rain, temperature, or outdoor transit conditions; therefore no weather veto can be adjudicated from this page. If the public Apollo display required outdoor travel in inclement weather, meteorological cost would modulate the social-shame calculation, but that is extratextual inference.

Adjudication hierarchy for [PAGE 545]: the exogenous physical veto (Elias-controlled cords) is the strongest and most absolute; the biomechanical veto is gradual and can be ignored during paroxysm; the weather veto is absent from the source. Internal will is the weakest layer precisely because the whole phenomenon—binding, masquerade, washing, checking—demonstrates that unassisted volition cannot reliably suppress the insistent idea without environmental or social scaffolding.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR counterfactual veto = an intervention that blocks a specific causal pathway and yields a falsifiable prediction.

Veto scenario 1 — Remove Elias (no exogenous release). The subject binds himself with cords but entrusts the secret to no one. Prediction: when the paroxysm subsides, the subject cannot self-release because the cords are concealed and only one hand is free; he either remains bound until circulatory compromise or skin injury forces emergency release, or he destroys the mantle/cords, thereby breaking the precommitment. Verification: time-to-release and dermatological/circulatory markers would show either prolonged binding with injury or contract abandonment. This tests the claim that the Ulysses contract requires a trusted execution environment; without it, the physical veto becomes self-harm or collapses.

Veto scenario 2 — Privatize the masquerade. The subject appears as Apollo before no audience (private room, no theatre). Prediction: the social cost S(a; Public) drops to approximately zero, so βS no longer raises the cost of renewing the attachment; the subject resumes the lady relationship within weeks rather than maintaining the shame barrier. Verification: relationship-resumption timestamp compared to matched historical cases with public performance. If resumption occurs rapidly, the counter-trap is shown to depend on publicity, not on the intrinsic content of the masquerade.

Veto scenario 3 — Provide cryptographic lock proof to the doubter. The subject installs a smart lock emitting a signed, timestamped proof of locked state readable from bed. Prediction: because one observation delivers the channel-capacity ceiling (locked vs. unlocked with certainty), b_t → 1 after the first check; anxiety A_t falls below θ immediately; the subject does not exit the bed. Verification: actigraphy or polysomnography showing zero bed-exit events after lock proof versus multiple exits at baseline. A null result (no exits) verifies that the checking loop is sustained by bounded information and memory decay rather than by a genuine belief that the door is unlocked.
