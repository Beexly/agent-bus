$$h_t = \lambda h_{t-1} + (1-\lambda)\,s_t,\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1,\quad s_t\in[0,1],$$
where $s_t$ is the crisis signal (e.g., illness in the household makes height measurement aversive). As $t$ increases under persistent crisis, $h_t$ decays toward the crisis baseline, producing missing data not at random. The breakdown regime is $h_t<h_{\text{crit}}$, where $h_{\text{crit}}$ is the minimum compliance needed for $\operatorname{Var}(X)>0$ in the regression operator; below it, $\beta_{YX}$ is singular.

Counter-trap. The statistician anticipates observer regression and introduces regression to the mean as a normative correction: “extreme parents have less extreme children.” But the observer, modeling the statistician, may over-correct—rounding measurements toward population norms they have read about in *Inquiries into Human Faculty* [PAGE 1]—thereby artificially inducing the very regression the theory seeks to discover. This is a second-order counter-trap: the measurement process becomes contaminated by the theory it is meant to test.

Clock layer. The publication cycle imposes a hard deadline $T_{\text{pub}}$ fixed by Macmillan’s production schedule [PAGE 1]. If data collection requires $T_{\text{data}} > T_{\text{pub}}$, the book ships with incomplete tabular forms or unverified directions. The recursive theory of mind therefore terminates in a clock-bound fixed point: each agent models the other’s model, but all models are truncated at $T_{\text{pub}}$. The result is a published covariance structure whose confidence intervals are conditioned on a censored observation window, and whose singularity boundary is crossed precisely when the clock runs out before the matrix $\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}$ achieves full rank.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudicate viability of each work on [PAGE 1] across three veto layers. Define the veto set $\mathcal{V}=\{V_{\text{phys}},V_{\text{bio}},V_{\text{weather}}\}$, each $V_{\ell}\in\{0,1\}$.

Physical layer. Format and binding determine archival survival. A 4to volume such as *Meteorographica* or *Record of Family Faculties* [PAGE 1] requires shelf space and robust binding; if the library acquisition budget $B$ cannot accommodate oversized stock, $V_{\text{phys}}=1$. Formal condition:
$$V_{\text{phys}} = \mathbb{I}\!\big(\text{format}\in\{\text{4to},\,\text{Demy 8vo}\} \land C_{\text{shelf}} > B\big).$$
Breakdown: $V_{\text{phys}}=1$ removes the work from the cited corpus, inducing survivorship bias in retrospective meta-analysis.

Biomechanical layer. The *Life History Album* [PAGE 1] demands height, weight, and illness registration. Height requires a subject to stand upright against a stadiometer; weight requires a calibrated scale; illness recall requires cognitive availability. If the subject is institutionalized, infant, or infirm, the biomechanical preconditions fail and the record is missing. Let $M_i$ indicate missingness for subject $i$; non-ignorable missingness occurs when $P(M_i=1\mid Z_i)$ depends on the latent trait $Z_i$. Then
$$V_{\text{bio}} = \mathbb{I}\!\big(\exists i:\ M_i=1 \land P(M_i=1\mid Z_i)\neq P(M_i=1)\big).$$
Breakdown: under $V_{\text{bio}}=1$, the estimated heritability $h^2 = \operatorname{Var}_G/\operatorname{Var}_P$ is biased because $\operatorname{Var}_P$ is computed on a selected subsample.

Weather layer. *Meteorographica* [PAGE 1] depends on a network of simultaneous barometric, thermometric, and hygrometric readings transmitted to a central plotter. The veto is triggered by telegraph failure, observer absence, or instrument non-standardization, producing spatial gaps larger than the synoptic decorrelation length $\Delta x_{\text{synoptic}}\approx 200\text{ km}$. Then
$$V_{\text{weather}} = \mathbb{I}\!\big(\exists \text{ station }j:\ \Delta x_j > \Delta x_{\text{synoptic}} \lor \tau_{\text{transmit}} > \tau_{\text{synoptic}}\big),$$
with time unit seconds [s]. Breakdown: the weather map becomes topologically disconnected; advection cannot be estimated and the forecast skill score $S = 1 - \text{RMSE}_{\text{model}}/\text{RMSE}_{\text{climate}}$ collapses to negative values.

Adjudication rule. A work is viable for its intended inferential purpose iff
$$\mathcal{V}_{\text{total}} = \neg\big(V_{\text{phys}}\lor V_{\text{bio}}\lor V_{\text{weather}}\big)\land \mathbb{I}\!\big(C(W)\le B\big),$$
where $C(W)$ is total production cost and $B$ is the publisher’s budget. If any layer vetoes, the product is zero: the work either never reaches observers, never yields usable bodies, or never yields a coherent weather field. This cross-layer proof shows that Galton’s statistical program on [PAGE 1] was materially contingent on paper, bodies, and telegraph wires, not merely on mathematical insight.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each scenario holds the actual publication record on [PAGE 1] as the factual baseline and imposes a counterfactual intervention; the veto is verifiable against external historical or statistical records.

Scenario 1: Bibliographic price veto on the *Life History Album*. Counterfactual: the Album is not issued at 3s 6d (or with colour-vision cards at 4s 6d) [PAGE 1], but is withdrawn or priced above the working-class threshold $p_{\max}$. Prediction: the number of anthropometric returns $N$ falls below the critical sample size $N_c$ required to detect a regression slope $\beta_{YX}=0.3$ at $\alpha=0.05$ with power $1-\beta=0.8$. Using a standard power relation $N_c \approx (z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})^2\sigma^2/(\beta^2\sigma_X^2)$, if $N<N_c$, the confidence interval for $\beta_{YX}$ includes zero. Verifiable prediction: the public announcement of regression to the mean would be delayed by at least one publication cycle; we check the historical record that Galton’s regression results appear in *Natural Inheritance* (1889) without dependence on Album returns, so the veto would instead suppress the subsequent *Record of Family Faculties* tabulations, delaying Pearson’s 1903 ancestral-law paper by an estimated $\Delta t\ge 5$ years.

Scenario 2: Tabular-form veto on *Record of Family Faculties*. Counterfactual: the volume is issued with only the “Explanatory Preface” and no “Tabular Forms and Directions for Entering Data” [PAGE 1]. Prediction: entry variance $\sigma_{\text{entry}}^2$ rises such that reliability $\kappa = \sigma_{\text{true}}^2/(\sigma_{\text{true}}^2+\sigma_{\text{entry}}^2)$ drops below 0.5. Under classical attenuation, the observed parent-offspring correlation becomes $r_{\text{obs}} = \kappa r_{\text{true}}$. If $r_{\text{true}}\approx0.45$ for stature and $\kappa<0.5$, then $r_{\text{obs}}<0.225$, likely indistinguishable from sampling noise in Galton’s sample. Verifiable prediction: modern heritability of adult height is $h^2\approx0.8$ (parent-offspring regression $\approx0.4$–0.5); the counterfactual world would produce a published null or near-null familial correlation, verifiable by comparing against the actual non-null tables Galton did compile.

Scenario 3: Meteorographic diagram-density veto. Counterfactual: *Meteorographica* is constrained by cost to fewer than 100 diagrams instead of “upwards of 600” [PAGE 1]. Prediction: the spatial sampling interval of the weather chart exceeds the synoptic scale, so the effective resolution $\Delta x_{\text{eff}} > \Delta x_{\text{synoptic}}\approx200\text{ km}$. Cyclones and fronts are undersampled; the correlation between consecutive daily maps $\rho(\Delta t=1\text{ day})$ falls below the persistence threshold needed to infer advection velocity $\mathbf{u} = \Delta \mathbf{x}/\Delta t$ [units: m/s]. Verifiable prediction: the Bjerknes frontal model (circa 1918–1920) cannot be anticipated from Galton’s charts, and numerical weather prediction skill remains negative (worse than climatology) until the 1950s, which is consistent with the actual historical delay between synoptic mapping and computerized forecasting.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
