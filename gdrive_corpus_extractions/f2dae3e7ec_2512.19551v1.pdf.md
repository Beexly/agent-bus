Historical and scientific lineage. ES-MoE inherits the front-door adjustment and do-operator from Pearl (2018) [PAGE 3–4], the Mixture of Experts architecture from Jacobs et al. (1991) [PAGE 2, 4], and Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoding from Oord (2017) [PAGE 3]. The LoRA expert formulation builds on Hu et al. (2022) [PAGE 4], while lifelong learning baselines draw on parameter regularization (Kirkpatrick et al. 2016; Lopez-Paz & Ranzato 2017) [PAGE 2], model expansion via orthogonal subspace learning (Wang et al. 2023a, O-LoRA) [PAGE 2, 6], and shared-attention continual learning (Zhao et al. 2024, SAPT) [PAGE 2, 6]. MoE adapters for vision-language continual learning come from Yu et al. (2024a) [PAGE 2, 4], and the emotional motion precursor is EmotionalT2M (Yu et al. 2024b) [PAGE 5].

Multi-week traps. (1) Catastrophic forgetting is measured only terminally via FR and AF after scenario N [PAGE 6]; severe forgetting at intermediate steps i < N that is later recovered remains invisible, creating a deployment hazard for embodied agents that must operate online after each new scenario. (2) Negative transfer between emotionally congruent but kinematically disjoint scenarios: the paper’s own example contrasts “Shows” (exaggerated expression) with “Sports” (professional, skill-oriented movement) for the same “Sad” label [PAGE 1]. If the gating network over-weights emotional commonality, it may impose show-style exaggeration onto sports kinematics or vice versa. (3) Data drift from dataset construction: EmotionalT2M is small and expanded with Motion-X, whose texts and emotion labels are merged using ChatGLM [PAGE 5], introducing synthetic label noise and a distribution shift between tokenizer training data and continual tuning data. (4) Annotation reliability: Kappa = 0.85 [PAGE 5] implies roughly 15% scenario-label disagreement across annotators; over eight scenarios and multi-week collection, this disagreement compounds into systematic misrouting in the gating network.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. Treat ES-MoE as a recursive theory-of-mind agent: it must first infer the user’s emotional intent from text (first-order intent) and secondly infer the scenario-specific kinematic habit (second-order habit), then generate motion that satisfies both without collapse. Crisis habit regression occurs when training on scenario i causes the LoRA expert Δθi to regress to the dominant habit of scenario i−1—for example, applying a sports gait to a daily-life walking sequence—because the gating weight W_{i−1} remains dominant or because cross-sampling from the global dictionary injects previous-scenario motion statistics [PAGE 3–4].

Counter-trap: the paper’s random masking of trained experts during gating weight assignment [PAGE 4] is designed to prevent over-reliance on previous experts. This creates a counter-trap: if masking is too aggressive, previous experts are starved of gradient updates and the model underfits old scenarios; if masking is too lenient, the model overfits previous scenarios and cannot adapt to new ones. The optimal masking rate is not specified, representing an unstable fixed point in the recursion.

Clock: FR_N is a terminal clock read only after the final scenario N [PAGE 6]. It does not capture the trajectory of forgetting across sequential steps. A model could forget scenario 1 severely at step 2, partially relearn it by step N, and still post a low FR, while an embodied robot deployed after step 2 would already have failed. Singularity: at i = 1 there are no prior experts, so the MoE gate is trivial (W1 = 1) and the scenario-adapting mechanism provides no benefit over standard fine-tuning until i ≥ 2.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical and biomechanical veto proof. ES-MoE emits discrete motion tokens decoded by a VQ-VAE decoder into 3D pose sequences [PAGE 3, 6]. The training objective contains no explicit physics terms: no gravity, no joint-limit constraints, no contact or ground-reaction-force penalties, and no center-of-mass stability criterion. The reconstruction loss L_vq = L_re + L_embed + L_commit [PAGE 5] optimizes token-level reconstruction only. Consequently, any generated sequence exhibiting foot sliding, joint hyperextension, or dynamic imbalance is physically invalid regardless of its AF, AR, or AWF score. Because the paper reports no physical plausibility metrics [PAGE 6–8], the framework is not veto-proof for embodied deployment; a humanoid executing a high-AWF but kinematically impossible motion would fail at the physical layer.

Weather veto. The input conditioning is textual emotion description only [PAGE 1–2]; there is no weather, friction, temperature, or terrain modality. In outdoor humanoid deployment, weather alters motor dynamics (e.g., slippery ground changes foot contact), violating the i.i.d. assumption between training motion tokens and test conditions. ES-MoE has no mechanism to condition on or adapt to such exogenous physical context, so weather constitutes an out-of-distribution shift outside the model’s causal graph X → M → Y.

Singularity and breakdown. The VQ-VAE quantization arg min over codebook C = {c1, ..., ck} [PAGE 3] is singular when k is too small to cover the emotion-by-scenario manifold, causing distinct emotional motions to collapse to the same token. At this singularity, the front-door path X → M → Y is blocked because M cannot represent emotion independently of scenario.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reward verification via held-out causal/physical checks) veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 — Unseen emotion outside label set. Deploy ES-MoE on a ninth scenario whose target emotion (e.g., “awe”) is absent from EmotionalT2M and Motion-X label sets. Verifiable check: L_emo = CrossEntropy(ye, ŷye) is ill-posed because ye has no valid class index; AWF cannot be computed meaningfully. Prediction: AWF collapses to chance-level uniform prediction over known classes; AR drops because text-motion match fails for the unknown emotion; the gating network routes to the nearest semantic expert, causing negative transfer. Expected outcome: ES-MoE performs no better than SeqLoRA on AWF and AR, vetoing the claim of generalizable emotion transfer.

Veto scenario 2 — Biomechanically orthogonal scenario order. Train on Daily Life first, then place Acrobatics as final scenario N. Verifiable check: instrument the decoded motion for joint-angle violations and foot-ground penetration; measure AF. Prediction: low-rank LoRA updates Δθi = AiBi cannot capture the high-dimensional acrobatic kinematic manifold; AF increases (worse FID) because motions become physically incoherent; FR is positive and large because r_{N,acrobatics} is far below any historical baseline; cross-sampling from the global dictionary (K-means on prior scenarios) injects daily-life emotional commonality such as lowered head into acrobatic motions, producing biomechanically absurd combinations. Expected outcome: veto triggered when AF exceeds SAPT baseline or joint violations exceed a predefined kinematic threshold.

Veto scenario 3 — Symmetric emotion-label noise. Inject noise rate η into motion emotion labels ye during continual tuning. Verifiable check: sweep η from 0 to 0.5 and monitor AWF and AR on held-out clean scenarios. Prediction: because L_emo is minimized against noisy ye, the causal decoupling block highlights spurious features correlated with label noise rather than invariant emotional commonality; AWF declines monotonically with η. At η > 0.3, ES-MoE’s AWF drops below the w/o Lemo ablation (which already falls from 0.340 to 0.326 on the unseen dataset [PAGE 6]), and eventually below MTL. Expected outcome: veto triggered when AWF < MTL baseline, demonstrating that the front-door adjustment cannot compensate for corrupted emotional ground truth.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
