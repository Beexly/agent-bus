James reframes this: the "paralysis" or "hallucination" is not a lesion but a successful prediction enforced by a high-precision prior. This lineage passes through John B. Watson’s behaviorist reductionism—which would have dismissed internal states—and finds its true heir in the mid-20th century work of Francis Crick and Christof Koch on neural correlates of consciousness, particularly their emphasis on thalamo-cortical resonance and attentional gating.

In contemporary science, the retrocausal influence is evident in Karl Friston’s Free Energy Principle, which directly inherits the Bayesian logic implicit in James’s description of "impulse rising in him, he knows not why." The modern concept of "predictive coding" is essentially a mathematical formalization of James’s intuition that the mind continuously generates expectations; hypnosis simply allows an experimenter to plant a malicious expectation.

Furthermore, this genealogy extends into computer security. The structure of a post-hypnotic suggestion—an instruction implanted in one state, executed in another, often hidden from introspection—is isomorphic to a steganographic worm or a dormant malware payload. The "signal" James mentions ("marked by a certain signal") mirrors a polymorphic trigger condition in code. The historical trap lies in viewing these phenomena as either purely psychological (dismissing physical mechanisms) or purely neurological (ignoring the informational content). James bridges this by treating the suggestion as an *information object* that persists and exerts causal force across changes in physiological state, anticipating the symbol grounding problem and the challenge of maintaining identity across state transitions in artificial agents.
