There is no covariate for cumulative minutes played in the preceding club season, no minutes-restriction flag, and no squad-depth attrition model. Veto condition: if any projected starter has accumulated more than 4,500 competitive minutes in the 12 months prior to the tournament, the model's implicit freshness assumption fails and the prediction is vetoed for that match.

Biomechanical layer: The design matrix contains no GPS load, accelerometry, hamstring-injury probability, or recovery index [PAGE 5]. Attack parameter \alpha_i^{\text{att}} is estimated from past results, not from current player health. Veto condition: if a key attacker sustains a grade-2 hamstring strain in the six weeks before the tournament, \alpha_i^{\text{att}} is stale; the Poisson lambda for matches involving that team is biased upward, and the prediction is voided.

Weather layer: The paper lists no meteorological covariate—temperature, humidity, precipitation, or wind—despite outdoor stadiums [PAGE 5]. Wet surfaces alter ball roll and coefficient of restitution; extreme heat reduces high-intensity sprint frequency. Veto condition: if forecast wet-bulb temperature exceeds 28°C or precipitation probability exceeds 60% for a knockout match, the Poisson lambda calibrated on historical dry-weather data is no longer transportable, and the simulation output is inadmissible.

Adjudication proof: Let \mathcal{F}_t be the sigma-algebra generated by pre-tournament squad covariates and historical results. Let E be an in-tournament veto event (injury, red card, weather shock). By the tower property, \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \neq \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathcal{F}_t, E] whenever E \notin \mathcal{F}_t. Since E is physically possible and not represented in \mathbf{x}, the conditional probability estimate from the model is not the true conditional probability given the realized tournament state. Therefore any veto event at the physical, biomechanical, or weather layer structurally invalidates the model's output for the affected fixture.
