**Cochran / OVB.** $\tilde\beta_2=\hat\beta_2+\hat\delta\hat\beta_1$ (Thm 9.1, PAGE 117); confounding bias $\tilde\beta_1-\hat\beta_1=\hat\beta_3\hat\delta_1$ (eq. 9.5).

**Leave-one-out.** $\hat\beta_{[−i]}=\hat\beta-(1-h_{ii})^{-1}(X^tX)^{-1}x_i\hat\varepsilon_i$ ($h_{ii}\ne1$); $\hat\varepsilon_{[−i]}=\hat\varepsilon_i/(1-h_{ii})$, $\operatorname{var}=\sigma^2/(1-h_{ii})$ (Thm 11.2–11.3, PAGE 111–112). $h_{ii}=\tfrac1n+\tfrac{D_i^2}{n-1}$, $D_i^2$ = squared Mahalanobis distance (Thm 11.1, PAGE 133).

**Overfitting.** Under $\beta=0$: $R^2\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\tfrac{p-1}{2},\tfrac{n-p}{2})$ [PAGE 102, Cor 10.1]; $E(R^2)=\tfrac{p-1}{n-1}\to\gamma$ when $p/n\to\gamma\in(0,1)$ [PAGE 167]. VIF: $\operatorname{var}(\hat\beta_j)=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sum_i(x_{ij}-\bar x_j)^2}\cdot\frac{1}{1-R_j^2}$ (Thm 13.1, PAGE 143).

**Ridge / Lasso.** $\hat\beta_{ridge}(\lambda)=(X^tX+\lambda I_p)^{-1}X^tY$, always defined since $\lambda_{\min}(X^tX+\lambda I_p)\ge\lambda>0$ [PAGE 153–154]. $\mathrm{MSE}(\lambda)=\underbrace{\lambda^2\sum_j\gamma_j^2/(d_j^2+\lambda)^2}_{C_1\text{ bias}^2}+\underbrace{\sigma^2\sum_j d_j^2/(d_j^2+\lambda)^2}_{C_2\text{ var}}$ (Thm 14.1); $\partial\,\mathrm{MSE}/\partial\lambda|_{\lambda=0}<0$ (Problem 14.2). Dual form $X^t(XX^t+\lambda I_n)^{-1}Y$ for $p>n$ (Thm 14.3). Lasso soft-threshold $S(b_0,\lambda)=\operatorname{sign}(b_0)(|b_0|-\lambda)_+$ (Lemma 15.1).

**GLM.** logit $\pi(x_i,\beta)=e^{x_i^t\beta}/(1+e^{x_i^t\beta})$; score $\sum_i x_i\{y_i-\pi(x_i,\beta)\}=0$; IRLS weights $\hat W=\mathrm{diag}\{\hat\pi_i(1-\hat\pi_i)\}$ [PAGE 254–256]. Case–control invariance: $\operatorname{logit}\Pr(y_i=1\mid x_i,s_i=1)=\delta+\beta_0+x_i^t\beta$, $\delta=\log(p_1/p_0)$ (Thm 20.1, PAGE 239). McFadden Gumbel: $\pi_k=e^{V_k}/\sum_l e^{V_l}$ (Lemma 21.1, PAGE 275). All log-likelihoods/AIC/BIC in nats.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is a ~220-year chain of *variance-accounting* ideas, and the "multi-week trap" is a recurring one: mistaking in-sample fit for signal.

- **OLS origin.** Gauss (Ceres, published 1809) and Legendre (1805); Gauss claimed use "since 1794 or 1795"; reviewed by Stigler (1981) [PAGE 32, footnote 1]. **Galton (1886)** coined "regression" for the mean-reversion in midparent→child height [PAGE 28], formula (1.1).
- **Optimality layer.** Named after Gauss and Markov; "Markoff Theorem" (David–Neyman 1938), first called "Gauss–Markov" by Lehmann (1951) [PAGE 53, footnote 2]. Koopmann (1982) later showed *every* unbiased estimator has the quadratic form $\tilde\beta=\hat\beta+(Y^tQ_1Y,\dots)$ [PAGE 56–57] — a genealogical capstone bounding the search space.
- **Robust-SE lineage (the sandwich).** Cox (1961) and **Huber (1967)** introduced the sandwich under misspecified parametric models; **Eicker (1967)** proposed $\hat V_{ehw}$; **White (1980a)** popularized it in economics; Fuller (1975) in survey sampling [PAGE 82]. This is the intellectual root of the book's stated novelty — misspecified-model theory "dated back at least to Huber (1967)" [PAGE 22].
- **Partial-regression lineage.** Frisch–Waugh (1933), Lovell (1963), with Yule (1907) and Jacobi/Berman (1988) antecedents [PAGE 94, 47]. **Cochran (1938)** (attributed by Cox 2007, itself credited by Cochran to Fisher 1925a) gave the omitted-variable formula [PAGE 117].
- **Regularization lineage.** Ridge: Hoerl–Kennard (1970), Tikhonov (1943) [PAGE 153]. Lasso: Tibshirani (1996), bridged by Frank–Friedman (1993) and Zou–Hastie (2005) elastic net [PAGE 176, 193]. Discrete choice: **McFadden (1974)** (2000 Nobel) [PAGE 275].
- **The multi-week/multi-decade trap.** Freedman (1981) split OLS into "regression model" vs "correlation model" [PAGE 127]; Freedman (1983) then demonstrated the overfitting trap — with pure noise covariates, $R^2\to\gamma$ [PAGE 141]. This is the field's canonical cautionary genealogy: decades of empirical papers reporting $R^2\approx0.1$ with many covariates may be "chasing the noise rather than the signal" [PAGE 143].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reframed faithfully: this is *adversarial assumption stress-testing* — modeling what a strategically-misspecified pipeline does, versus what the estimator's guarantees assume, on the "clock" of streaming data.

- **Habit/regression layer (mean reversion as a modeling prior).** Galton's regression-toward-the-mean is the base "habit": $|\hat\rho|<1$ forces child deviations to shrink relative to parent deviations [PAGE 29]. Any agent assuming stationarity of $\beta$ across environments is exposed to the Sims (2010) critique: the best linear approximation depends on $F(x)$ (Example 12.1: the same $y=x^2+\varepsilon$ yields slope $0$, $1$, or $-1$ under Uniform$(-1,1)$, Uniform$(0,1)$, Uniform$(-1,0)$) [PAGE 150–151]. The counter-move is the restricted-mean model $E(y\mid x)=x^t\beta$ [PAGE 151, Assumption 12.1], which stabilizes $\beta$ across covariate distributions.
- **Counter-trap layer.** Three named inversions punish naive reasoning: **Simpson's paradox** — $\hat\rho_{yx|w}\hat\rho_{yx}<0$ (Section 8.2), the coefficient of $X$ flips sign after conditioning [PAGE 82–83]; **ecological fallacy** — precinct-level regression can reverse the individual-level sign (Robinson 1950; Freedman et al. 1991) [PAGE 216]; **EHW false confidence** — "how robust are the robust standard errors?" (Section 24.3.4) [PAGE 296].
- **Clock layer (sequential updating).** The faithful "clock" is the Gauss online-update: $\hat\beta^{(n+1)}=\hat\beta^{(n)}+\gamma^{(n+1)}\hat\varepsilon_{[n+1]}$, $\gamma^{(n+1)}=(X^t_{(n+1)}X_{(n+1)})^{-1}x_{n+1}$ (Thm 11.4, PAGE 112), with Sherman–Morrison inverse update $V^{(n+1)}=V^{(n)}-\frac{V^{(n)}x_{n+1}x_{n+1}^tV^{(n)}}{1+x_{n+1}^tV^{(n)}x_{n+1}}$ (PAGE 113). Adversarial reading: an agent that "trusts the last observation" without the $(1-h_{n+1,n+1})^{-1}$ damping will overreact to high-leverage arrivals; the theory says the adjustment scales with the *predicted* residual $\hat\varepsilon_{[n+1]}$, not the raw residual.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Reframed as the *mathematical vetoes* — cross-layer conditions under which a downstream inferential object is nullified by an upstream degeneracy. Each is a proof, not a heuristic.

1. **Collinearity veto (algebraic layer → estimator layer).** If columns of $X$ are dependent, $X^tX$ is singular and $\hat\beta$ does not exist uniquely; verbatim "then $X^tX$ is degenerate" [PAGE 40]. Under $p>n$, $X^tX$ has rank $\le n<p$ and OLS is *undefined* — the ridge fix restores invertibility because $\lambda_{\min}(X^tX+\lambda I_p)\ge\lambda>0$ [PAGE 154]. *Proof of veto:* $\alpha^tX^tX\alpha=\|X\alpha\|^2=0$ iff $X\alpha=0$, i.e. nontrivial null space.
2. **Leverage-1 veto (geometry → leave-one-out layer).** $h_{ii}=1\Rightarrow\hat\varepsilon_i=0$ and $h_{ij}=0\ (j\ne i)$ (Problem 11.5 consequence); and det$(X^t_{[−i]}X_{[−i]})=(1-h_{ii})\det(X^tX)=0$ (Thm 11.8) — the deleted design becomes singular, so $\hat\beta_{[−i]}$ and the PRESS/Cook machinery are *vetoed*. *Proof:* from $h_{ii}=\sum_j h_{ij}^2=h_{ii}^2+\sum_{j\ne i}h_{ij}^2$, $h_{ii}=1$ forces the off-diagonals to zero.
3. **Heteroskedasticity veto (error layer → optimality & prediction layers).** (a) Gauss–Markov optimality is vetoed: $\hat\beta$ stays unbiased but $\operatorname{cov}(\hat\beta)=\sigma^2(X^tX)^{-1}X^t\Sigma X(X^tX)^{-1}\ne\sigma^2(X^tX)^{-1}$, so BLUE fails [PAGE 90, (L3)] and the classical $t/F$ pivots are invalid. (b) Prediction of $y_{n+1}$ is *entirely vetoed*: "we cannot create any reasonable prediction intervals ... since its variance $\sigma^2_{n+1}$ is fundamentally unknown without further assumptions" [PAGE 90, (L4)]. *Adjudication:* replace with $\hat V_{ehw}$ for coefficients (valid), and switch to conformal prediction for the individual outcome (exchangeability-based, distribution-free) [PAGE 130].
4. **Separation veto (GLM layer).** In logistic MLE, if a covariate perfectly separates $y$, the likelihood has no finite maximizer; R emits "glm.fit: fitted probabilities numerically 0 or 1 occurred" [PAGE 260]. The Hessian $-\sum_i\pi_i(1-\pi_i)x_ix_i^t$ degenerates as $\pi_i\to\{0,1\}$ — the information matrix loses rank, vetoing the Wald SE. *Adjudication:* penalized (ridge/lasso) logistic restores a unique finite solution [PAGE 239].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable veto scenarios drawn from the text, each with a falsifiable prediction and a check.

**Veto A — Classical SE under heteroskedasticity.**
Setup: $y_i=x_i+\varepsilon_i$, $\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,x_i^2)$ (Section 6.1.1). *Prediction:* the homoskedastic SE (se1) underestimates the true sampling SD (se0), while EHW (se2) tracks se0. *Verification (numbers on PAGE 77, Fig. 6.1):* $se_0=0.095$, $se_1=0.07$, $se_2=0.096$ — i.e., $se_1<se_0\approx se_2$. Falsified if $se_1\ge se_0$ under genuine heteroskedasticity. Reinforced by Problem 7.7: a heteroskedastic counterexample where $\operatorname{var}(\hat\beta)>\operatorname{var}(\tilde\beta)$ despite $\tilde\beta$ including a useless intercept.

**Veto B — Sign reversal (Simpson's paradox).**
Setup: $w\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$, $x\mid w{=}1\sim N(-1,1)$, $x\mid w{=}0\sim N(2,1)$, $y=x+6w+\varepsilon$ (Section 8.2). *Prediction:* within each $w$-stratum the $x\!\to\!y$ slope is positive ($\approx0.98$), but pooled it is negative ($\approx-0.37$). *Verification (PAGE 107):* $\hat\beta_{xw}=0.980$, $\hat\beta_x=-0.374$; the sign flip is exactly $\hat\rho_{yx|w}\hat\rho_{yx}<0$, equivalently $\hat\rho_{yx}^2<\hat\rho_{yx}\hat\rho_{yw}\hat\rho_{xw}$. Falsified if the two coefficients share sign.

**Veto C — Overfitting under the null (spurious $R^2$).**
Setup: $Y=X\beta+\varepsilon$, $\beta=(\mu,0,\dots,0)^t$ (pure noise), $n=100$, $p=50$, 1000 replications (Freedman 1983, Section 13.1). *Prediction:* despite zero true association, $\hat R^2$ concentrates near $p/n=0.5$. *Verification:* $R^2\sim\mathrm{Beta}(49/2,50/2)$, mean $=\frac{p-1}{n-1}=49/99\approx0.495$, and as $p/n\to\gamma\in(0,1)$, $R^2\to_p\gamma$ [PAGE 167]. A model-selection pre-step (drop $p$-values $>0.25$) does *not* restore uniform null $p$-values for the joint $F$ test (Fig. 13.1, PAGE 142) — the selective-inference veto. Falsified if $\hat R^2\to0$ at fixed $p/n>0$.

Cross-check for all three: each is reproducible from the book's own R code (Simulation 6.1.1; Simpson block PAGE 107; Freedman block PAGE 141) and the Harvard Dataverse replication bundle [PAGE 22], so the vetoes are externally verifiable rather than asserted.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
