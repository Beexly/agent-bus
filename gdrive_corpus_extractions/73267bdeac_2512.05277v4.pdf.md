Now the gray-zone audit. First, Scene-CoT is not a monotone improvement — it *hurts* large models: Qwen2.5-VL-32B falls 55.20 → 48.78 and InternVL3-38B falls 56.49 → 53.95 under +Scene-CoT [PAGE 7]. The paper is candid ("For larger models, Scene-CoT does not offer additional gains" [PAGE 8]), but the mechanism is under-argued. The plausible causal chain: Scene-CoT compresses each 5 s segment to four frames → a text scene description + JSON summary, then discards the frames entirely at QA time (Eq. 1 consumes only C and q). For a strong VLM whose native frame-grounded representation already encodes motion, this is a lossy bottleneck — the text summary is a strictly lower-cardinality surrogate for the pixels the LLM never sees. Second, TCogMap's gains are strongly ego-asymmetric by construction: InternVL3-8B ego +13.02 vs. non-ego +4.57 [PAGE 8], and on non-ego Temporal Ordering, Qwen2.5-VL-32B +TCogMap *regresses* 50.00 → 33.33 [PAGE 20 Table 9]. Since 46.3% of TAD is non-ego, the headline average gain overstates the method's reach on half the benchmark. Third, the localization tasks have a compressed dynamic range: chance for Temp. Obj. Local. is 58.85 [PAGE 7], so Cosmos-Reason's 16.06 is far *below* chance and even human 62.69 sits only ~4 points above chance — because random frame-subset IoU against long ground-truth frame lists is high. This means average-accuracy gains are dominated by the multiple-choice tasks, not localization. I flag these as the central interpretive hazards.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All quantities below are stated in SI units with explicit singularity/breakdown regimes.

1. Finite-difference velocity (Algorithm 1, [PAGE 6]):
$$v_i = \frac{x_i - x_{i-1}}{t_i - t_{i-1}}, \qquad \omega_i = \lVert v_i[0{:}2]\rVert.$$
Units: $x_i$ [m], $t_i$ [s], $v_i$ [m/s]; $\omega_i$ is the horizontal (ground-plane) speed [m/s], discarding the vertical component. NuScenes keyframes are at 2 Hz, so the nominal step is $\Delta t = t_i - t_{i-1} = 0.5$ s. This is a first-order backward difference with truncation error $-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\ddot{x}(\xi) = O(\Delta t)$ [m/s]; at $\Delta t=0.5$ s the acceleration term is non-negligible for hard braking. **Singularity:** $\Delta t \to 0$ (duplicate/again-sampled timestamps) yields division blow-up and noise amplification; the paper provides no guard.

2. Stationary test ([PAGE 5], L8–10):
$$\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathbb{1}\!\left[\omega_i < V_{\text{stat}}\right] > 0.5, \quad V_{\text{stat}}=0.2\ \text{m/s}.$$
This is a majority vote of per-step speeds below $0.2$ m/s ($\approx 0.72$ km/h). **Breakdown:** non-differentiable step boundary; a vehicle creeping at $0.21$ m/s for $>50\%$ of the segment is not "stopped," so stop-and-creep urban behavior (frequent in Boston NuScenes) sits on a knife-edge.

3. Yaw change and local-frame projection (Eq. 2, [PAGE 6]):
$$\Delta\psi = \text{yaw}(q_{N-1}) - \text{yaw}(q_0), \qquad v_{\text{local},i} = R_i^{\top} v_i, \quad \bar v_x,\bar v_y = \text{mean}(\{v_{\text{local},i}\}).$$
$\Delta\psi$ [rad]; $R_i \in SO(3)$ from quaternion $q_i$, so $R_i^{\top}=R_i^{-1}$ (orthonormal), preserving speed magnitude [m/s]. Turn label triggers when $|\Delta\psi| > \Psi_{\text{turn}}=10.0^\circ$. **Singularities:** (i) yaw wraparound at $\pm\pi$ — a heading crossing $179^\circ \to -179^\circ$ gives naive $\Delta\psi = -358^\circ$ (false "turn right") though the true net rotation is $+2^\circ$; the pseudocode shows no angle unwrapping. (ii) Endpoint-only differencing: a left-then-right maneuver with net $\Delta\psi\approx0$ is invisible. (iii) $\bar v_y$ mean-cancellation: lateral velocity that reverses sign within the segment averages toward zero, suppressing a genuine lane change.

4. Lane-change and start/stop tests ([PAGE 6], L14–24):
$$\text{Lane-change} \iff |\bar v_y| > V_{y,\text{lc}}\ (0.4)\ \wedge\ |\bar v_x| > V_{x,\text{lc}}\ (1.0),$$
$$\text{Starting} \iff \omega_{\text{start}} < V_{\text{stopping}}\ (1.0)\ \wedge\ \omega_{\text{end}} > 1.5\,V_{\text{stopping}}\ (1.5),$$
with the symmetric condition for Stopping. **Breakdown:** Starting/Stopping use only $\omega_1,\omega_{N-1}$ (two endpoints), so boundary-sample noise directly flips the label; the hierarchical order Stopped ≻ Turn ≻ LaneChange ≻ Start ≻ Stop ≻ Straight means a segment containing both a turn and a lane change is decided as a turn regardless of salience.

5. Method operators:
$$a_{\text{Scene-CoT}} = \mathcal{L}(C, q),\quad C=(c_1,\dots,c_l) \quad\text{(Eq. 1, [PAGE 5])}$$
$$a_{\text{TCogMap}} = \phi(F, M, q),\quad F=\{F_k\}_{k=1}^{K},\ M=\{m_i\}_{i=1}^{l} \quad\text{(Eq. 3, [PAGE 6])}$$
$\mathcal{L}$ is the LLM (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-1M); $\phi$ is the VLM retaining all $K$ frames. The decisive contrast: Eq. 1's answer depends on the video *only through text* $C$, whereas Eq. 3's depends on the pixels $F$ plus the discrete map $M$ — this is why TCogMap scales with model size while Scene-CoT does not.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs on three tracks. (1) *Numerical kinematics*: the finite-difference velocity and rigid-body frame projection descend directly from classical rigid-body kinematics and dead-reckoning — $v_{\text{local}}=R^\top v$ is textbook SE(3) adjoint action, and the yaw-threshold maneuver classifier is a discrete Schmitt-trigger on heading rate, an idea traceable to early vehicle-behavior segmentation. (2) *Cognitive maps*: the explicit citation to Gholami et al. 2026 and Yang et al. 2025b ("Thinking in Space") places TCogMap in the Tolman (1948) "cognitive map" tradition, re-instantiated as a text prior injected into a VLM. (3) *Perception–reasoning decoupling*: Scene-CoT cites Wei et al. 2022 (CoT) and the authors' own CPPO/Gou et al. line, positioning CoT as a VLM-perception → LLM-reasoner factorization. Benchmark lineage: TAD is explicitly the successor to ELM (3.5 s clips), STSBench (≤3 s), and STIBench (20 s, indoor, camera-pose) [PAGE 3], differentiating on the axis of *both* short segments and ~20 s scenes from NuScenes [PAGE 3].

Multi-week traps this genealogy sets for a practitioner. **Trap A — threshold overfitting:** the six thresholds ($V_{\text{stat}}, V_{\text{stopping}}, \Psi_{\text{turn}}, V_{y,\text{lc}}, V_{x,\text{lc}}$) are "empirically set" on Boston/Singapore urban NuScenes at ≤50 m; porting to highway (higher $V_{x,\text{lc}}$ regime) or to a different camera extrinsics stack silently breaks the label distribution — a weeks-long debugging sink that looks like a VLM failure but is a tool-calibration failure. **Trap B — the yaw-wrap bug:** if a team reproduces Algorithm 1 literally, a fraction of "turn" segments near the $\pm\pi$ seam will be systematically mislabeled, contaminating both the map $M$ and any TCogMap-based fine-tuning data — invisible in aggregate accuracy, visible only per-scenario. **Trap C — metric-chance illusion:** because localization chance floors are 41.70/47.83/58.85 [PAGE 7], a team optimizing average accuracy can spend weeks "improving" a number that is really a multiple-choice artifact, mistaking metric compression for temporal-reasoning progress. **Trap D — the 4-frame bottleneck:** believing Scene-CoT's segment captions are a faithful sufficient statistic, when Table 10 shows scene-description-only reaches just 46.26 vs. 51.92 for the concatenated form [PAGE 18] — the summary alone is not enough, and neither may be four frames.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The agentic frame forces a two-level intent model: the VLM must infer the *ego and non-ego drivers' latent maneuver* (theory of mind over other road agents) while the paper's method layers a *second-order* prior — the ego's own realized trajectory — that the VLM treats as authoritative testimony about scene dynamics. The cleanest evidence for this second-order effect is the [PAGE 22–23] Figure 12 case: a lead car at night shows illuminated red rear lights, and *both* baseline and Scene-CoT regress to the visual habit "the car is Stopped." This is **crisis habit regression** — under degraded input (night, low resolution, tail-light salience), the model falls back to its dominant visual prior (red lights ⇒ braking/stopped) rather than reasoning about motion. TCogMap breaks the regression not by better vision but by injecting a kinematic contradiction: the ego is "moving forward at a relatively constant speed" [PAGE 25], so a genuinely stopped lead car would produce a closing-gap pattern inconsistent with the ego's steady advance; the model reconciles toward "Straight, constant speed." The counter-trap (the ego-prior misleading the model) appears where ego motion is *uninformative* about non-ego agents: on non-ego Temporal Ordering, TCogMap pushes Qwen2.5-VL-32B down to 33.33 [PAGE 20], i.e., the second-order prior actively misleads when the queried agent's dynamics decouple from the ego's. On the **clock** axis (temporal ordering/localization), the design's leverage is structural: partitioning into ordered segments $C=(c_1,\dots,c_l)$ externalizes the timeline so the LLM reasons over an explicit sequence rather than an implicit one — which is why relative-ordering questions benefit even for non-ego targets when the *ego's* ordered motion disambiguates "which happened earlier" (Figure 14: baseline picks "Change lane to the right"; Scene-CoT and TCogMap both recover "B, Straight, constant speed" by anchoring the ego's early-straight / late-right structure). The recursive risk: a VLM that over-trusts $M$ may satisfy the ego-prior at the expense of the true non-ego signal, converting a hallucination-suppressor into a hallucination-source precisely on the 46.3% of questions TCogMap was not designed for.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather vetoes are admissible because TCogMap's tool operates on ground-truth ego *poses* (translation, quaternion, timestamp from NuScenes [PAGE 5]), which are physically measured, not perceived — so the trajectory layer is largely immune to the perceptual degradations that break the vision layer.

Veto proof 1 (biomechanical feasibility). Non-ego action labels are defined w.r.t. the *actor's* wheel, not the ego's viewpoint ("a truck traveling opposite… turns right… labeled Turn Right" [PAGE 14]). A vision-only VLM must mentally rotate the observed 2D optical flow into the actor's frame — a transform it demonstrably fails (baseline near chance on Exact-Answer AR for some models, e.g., RoboTron EA 43.63 vs. human 81.68 [PAGE 7]). The trajectory veto does not fix non-ego (it is ego-only), so on this axis the honest verdict is: **no veto available; residual error persists** — confirmed by Cosmos-Reason non-ego avg 27.57 → 36.00 [PAGE 20], still far under human. This is a limitation, not a solved case.

Veto proof 2 (weather/illumination). At night/rain, appearance features (tail lights, wet-road reflections) dominate and mislead; the ego-pose channel is invariant to illumination. Figure 12 is the adjudicated case: the kinematic prior *overrides* the weather-induced visual misread, raising Exact-Answer AR (InternVL3-8B 57.26 → 61.37; Qwen2.5-7B 47.77 → 62.79 [PAGE 7]). Verdict: **veto upheld** on ego-coupled segments.

Veto proof 3 (temporal-scale / dynamics feasibility). Because $\omega_i$ is computed from measured displacements, the tool can flag physically impossible visual readings (e.g., a "lane change" asserted on a vehicle whose $|\bar v_y|$ never exceeds 0.4 m/s). However, the veto's own thresholds reintroduce a physics risk: a slow, wide urban lane change with $\bar v_y<0.4$ m/s and $\bar v_x<1.0$ m/s fails *both* gates and is mislabeled Straight — a false-negative veto. Net adjudication: TCogMap's vetoes are reliable where ego motion is (a) non-stationary and (b) kinematically distinctive (turns, clear speed ramps), and unreliable at low-speed/low-$\Delta\psi$ boundaries where the deterministic tool's thresholds — not the VLM — become the dominant error source.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Under RL-from-Verifiable-Rewards, a reward is only trustworthy if it is checkable; each scenario below is a falsifiable veto with a concrete prediction and a test using data already released (TAD + the 4,481 action labels [PAGE 14]).

Veto scenario 1 — yaw-wrap mislabeling. Claim: Algorithm 1's $\Delta\psi=\text{yaw}(q_{N-1})-\text{yaw}(q_0)$ [PAGE 6] mislabels segments whose net rotation crosses $\pm\pi$ (or whose intra-segment turn-and-return cancels). Prediction: if you re-derive labels with an angle-unwrapped $\Delta\psi$ (integrate per-step $\Delta\psi_i \bmod 2\pi$), a small but non-zero set of "Turn left/right" segments will flip to "Straight," and TCogMap ego accuracy on Exact-Answer AR will *change* on those specific items. Test: recompute labels with unwrapping and diff against the released set; the count of flipped segments is the reward-corruption magnitude. If zero, the veto is dismissed; if non-zero, any RL reward computed from these labels inherits label noise exactly on turn-heavy items.

Veto scenario 2 — non-ego regression under ego prior. Claim: TCogMap's ego-only map can *lower* non-ego accuracy (Qwen2.5-VL-32B non-ego Temp. Ord. 50.00 → 33.33 [PAGE 20]). Prediction: an RL policy rewarded on TAD average will learn to lean on $M$ for ego items and, if the reward is not stratified by ego/non-ego, will *increase* the non-ego regression as it exploits the ego shortcut. Test: fine-tune on a stratified reward (separate ego/non-ego reward channels) vs. flat reward; predict the stratified policy closes the non-ego gap while the flat policy widens it. This is verifiable because Table 5/8/9 already expose the ego/non-ego split.

Veto scenario 3 — metric-chance reward hacking. Claim: with localization chance floors of 41.70/47.83/58.85 [PAGE 7] and mIoU as the localization metric, an RL agent can raise "average accuracy" without improving genuine localization. Prediction: a policy optimized on the raw average will push multiple-choice tasks (large dynamic range) and leave Temp. Obj. Local. near/below its 58.85 chance floor; re-scoring with chance-corrected metrics (e.g., $\frac{\text{score}-\text{chance}}{1-\text{chance}}$) will reveal that most of the average gain vanishes. Test: report chance-corrected per-task deltas; predict Scene-CoT's large-model *losses* (Qwen2.5-32B 55.20 → 48.78) shrink less than its small-model gains under correction, exposing that the "up to 17.72%" figure is a multiple-choice-dominated quantity, not a uniform temporal-reasoning improvement. All three vetoes are decidable from the released benchmark and label set without new data collection.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Net assessment: the paper's durable contribution is the cleanly isolated mechanistic result that a *structured, discrete* ego-trajectory prior beats both raw frames and raw ego-pose text for ego-coupled temporal QA, with a self-reported honest failure of Scene-CoT on large VLMs and an ego/non-ego asymmetry that the headline average partly masks. I found no fabricated quantities; the deltas reconcile across pages. The principal scientific caveats are the deterministic tool's threshold and yaw-wraparound singularities, and the compressed chance floor on localization metrics.
