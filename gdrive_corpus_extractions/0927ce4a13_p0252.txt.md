We formalize Galton's "haphazard" model using standard probability theory. Let $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the temper state of sibling $i$ in a fraternity of size $n$, where 1 = Good, 0 = Bad. Under the null hypothesis of independence and uniformity:
$$ Y_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p), \quad p = 0.5 $$
The joint probability configuration for a fraternity $\mathbf{Y} = (Y_1, \dots, Y_n)$ is given by the product measure. Let $K = \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i$ be the count of good-tempered siblings. Then $K$ follows a Binomial distribution:
$$ P(K=k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}, \quad k \in \{0, \dots, n\}. $$
Galton categorizes outcomes into three buckets: 
- **All Good**: $k=n$. Probability: $(0.5)^n$.
- **All Bad**: $k=0$. Probability: $(0.5)^n$.
- **Intermediate**: $0 < k < n$. Probability: $1 - 2(0.5)^n$.

To compute expected frequencies for Table III Column B, we multiply these probabilities by the total number of fraternities of size $n$, denoted $M_n$.
Example for $n=4$, $M_4 = 29$:
Expected All Good: $29 \times (0.5)^4 = 29 / 16 \approx 1.81 \to 2$ (rounded).
Expected Intermediate: $29 \times (1 - 2/16) = 29 \times 14/16 \approx 25.37 \to 25$ (split into sub-categories in Galton's detailed breakdown).
Expected All Bad: Same as All Good, $\approx 2$.

**Units and Regimes**:
- **Unit**: Count of Fraternities (integer). Derived probabilities are dimensionless ratios.
- **Breakdown Regime**: The Binomial approximation assumes infinite divisibility of the population and true randomness. It breaks down when $p$ is not constant across fraternities (heterogeneity) or when $Y_i$ are correlated within a fraternity (clustering). Mathematically, if we assume $p$ varies according to a distribution $f(p)$, the marginal distribution of $K$ becomes Beta-Binomial:
$$ P(K=k) = \binom{n}{k} \frac{B(k+\alpha, n-k+\beta)}{B(\alpha, \beta)} $$
where $\alpha, \beta$ parameterize the prior belief about family propensities. Over-dispersion relative to Binomial implies a variance of $K$ greater than $np(1-p)$, which Galton detects empirically.
