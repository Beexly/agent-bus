Under this condition, "massed evocations" produce failure to respond rather than extinction, invalidating the premise of Corollary XI. Weather/environmental layer: Ambient temperature \(T_a\) outside the thermoneutral zone raises thermoregulatory drive \(D_{\text{thermo}}\). If \(D_{\text{thermo}} \gg D_{\text{hunger}}\), patterning gates temperature-appropriate responses (panting, shelter-seeking, shivering) and vetoes food-seeking even when the hunger stimulus is present. Sensory masking (wind, background noise) degrades the discriminability of \(S_r\) and \(S_t\) below the just-noticeable difference, vetoing differential choice at the maze.

Adjudication rule: the behavioral prediction is accepted only if \(P_{\text{continuous}} \le \eta E_{\text{food}}\), \(\Delta t_{\text{IEI}} \ge \tau_{\text{muscle}}\), \(D_{\text{thermo}} \le D_{\text{hunger}}\), and stimulus discriminability exceeds threshold. Any single veto nullifies the Hullian prediction for that trial.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios. RLVR is operationalized as binary reward signals derived from observable response metrics; each scenario specifies a veto condition and a prediction.

Scenario 1: Context-shift veto. Extinguish lever pressing for food under hunger drive \(D_1\). Immediately increase drive to \(D_2\). Present the original lever stimulus in a novel context (not the training chamber). Hull predicts response recovery; patterning predicts suppression because the full stimulus combination is unlicensed. Veto condition: response rate \(< 5\%\) of baseline for 5 minutes and latency to first press \(> 60\) s. Prediction: if veto triggers, zero presses; if not, immediate resumption at \(D_2\). Verifiable metric: cumulative press count and inter-press interval distribution.

Scenario 2: Competing-drive veto. After extinction under hunger \(D_1\), induce thirst \(D_{\text{thirst}}\) such that \(D_{\text{thirst}} > D_{\text{hunger}}\). Increase hunger drive and present the food cue. Hullian hierarchy predicts food-seeking; patterning plus drive competition predicts thirst vetoes food-seeking. Veto condition: first contact with water spout within 10 s and zero food-cup entries during the first 2 minutes. Prediction: water-seeking monopolizes behavior; food-directed responding is suppressed. Verifiable metric: time-to-first-contact and bout duration per consummatory response.

Scenario 3: Patterning-load veto. Train differential responses to \(2^n\) distinct stimulus combinations under food drive. Increment \(n\) until the information load \(I_{\text{pat}} = n \log_2(k)\) bits exceeds estimated working-memory capacity \(C_{\text{working}}\) (approximately 7 \(\pm\) 2 bits, or the species-specific channel capacity). Then test need-based choice. Veto condition: choice probability \(p_i \approx 1/m \pm 0.05\) across all \(m\) combinations despite intact drive hierarchy. Prediction: differential responding collapses to chance; no need-specific selection occurs. Verifiable metric: choice entropy \(H = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i\) approaching \(\log_2 m\), and response accuracy at or below chance.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
