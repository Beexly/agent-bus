Clock structure. The contract horizon for hiring labor and borrowing capital at fixed remuneration [PAGE 318] defines a decision window. Inside the window, $\Omega$ is treated as stable enough for factor pricing; outside it, $\Delta K$ may invalidate the entire valuation. Recursive agents therefore face a timing game: act too early, and fixed costs accrue before the discovery; act too late, and rivals have already expanded $\Omega$ and captured the profit stream. The equilibrium is not a Bayesian Nash equilibrium over a fixed $\Omega$, but a robust-selection outcome over a family of possible state spaces.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any forecast derived from [PAGE 318] must survive vetoes from three physical layers. Physical/geological layer: “the discovery of new natural resources” [PAGE 318] depends on subsurface and geographic states that cannot be anticipated ex ante. Veto condition: an economic plan assigning specific quantity, quality, and location to new natural productive capacity is voided because Knight states “we cannot … say what quantity and quality of new natural productive capacity will be developed and where” [PAGE 318]. Formal veto: $\exists$ a geological constraint map $C_g:\Omega\to\{0,1\}$ such that $C_g(\omega)=0$ eliminates state $\omega$ regardless of price expectations; exploration uncertainty makes $C_g$ unknowable before discovery.

Biomechanical/cognitive layer: “deliberate application of resources” [PAGE 318] requires human cognition and labor. The inventor’s mind is a bounded information channel with finite Shannon capacity, and labor supply is contractible only at fixed remuneration, not at guaranteed inventive output. Veto condition: no contract can specify the content of a future invention ex ante, because anticipation would equal performance [PAGE 318]. Formal veto: the biomechanical production function for knowledge, $\Delta K = f(L_{\text{cognitive}}, t)$, is non-invertible ex ante; $f^{-1}$ does not exist for novel $\Delta K$.

Weather/environmental layer: agricultural and natural productive capacity are weather-sensitive. Synoptic weather predictability decays beyond a Lyapunov horizon of order 10–14 days; beyond that, atmospheric states are effectively Knightian with respect to economic planning. Veto condition: a sales-market forecast contingent on crop yields or resource extraction rates within the weather predictability window is admissible; beyond it, any specific yield assumption is vetoed by atmospheric chaos.

Adjudication rule: an economic projection survives only if it is conditional on physical states that remain invariant over the decision horizon and does not require inverting the non-invertible knowledge-production function. If any layer returns $C=0$, the forecast is vetoed and the entrepreneur must hold residual uncertainty rather than insure it away.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Actuarial insurance of a specific R&D breakthrough. Counterfactual: an insurer offers a policy priced from historical innovation frequencies. Veto: the breakthrough event $I\notin\mathcal{F}_0$ and $\Omega_{t+1}\supsetneq\Omega_t$; no unique $P$ exists, so the premium integral $\int I(\omega)\,dP$ is undefined [PAGE 318]. Prediction: the insurer either withdraws coverage or charges an ambiguity premium; conditional on funding, the project outcome is binary—discontinuous profit or total loss—not mean-reverting around the historical rate.

Scenario 2: Central bank fixing the discount rate $r$ to stabilize capital values $V_K$. Counterfactual: monetary policy holds $r$ constant while treating $V_K=\int e^{-rt}\pi(t)\,dt$ as a deterministic function of expected product prices. Veto: capital values are the “fundamental exception” [PAGE 318]; $\pi(t)$ depends on unknowable $\Delta K$ and on rival supply/consumer wants [PAGE 318], so $V_K$ cannot be stabilized by fixing $r$ alone. Prediction: duration mismatch emerges; entrepreneurial profit/loss volatility exceeds model-implied variance, and credit rationing appears at the fixed rate because lenders cannot price the residual uncertainty.

Scenario 3: Regional development allocation based on forecasts of consumers’ wants $W$ and purchasing power $M$. Counterfactual: a planner allocates capital to regions using a multi-week forecast of $(W,M)$. Veto: wants co-evolve with innovation, and “to anticipate the advance would be to make it at once” [PAGE 318]; the forecast state space omits the very discoveries that will redefine demand. Prediction: allocated capital is stranded or misallocated until the discovery occurs; at discovery, valuations jump discontinuously and the pre-allocation plan is rendered obsolete, with surplus capacity in some regions and shortage in others.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
