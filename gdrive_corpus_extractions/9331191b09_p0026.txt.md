<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under liquidity stress, participants regress to the dominant fiat habit. A local business facing rent or payroll will convert received tokens to ETH/USD immediately rather than hold them for appreciation, precisely the behavior the text permits ("may convert loyalty points to Fiat in order to cover the cost-of-sale") [PAGE 19]. When regression becomes common, the local currency ceases to function as a store of value and collapses to a payment rail with negative merchant margin (the discount) and no offsetting capital gain.

Counter-trap: Merchants who hoard tokens expecting appreciation create a speculative overhang. If competitors convert to fiat to cover hard costs while one merchant hoards, the hoarder bears relative disadvantage: same discount cost, higher working-capital risk. The counter-trap is that holding is individually rational only if others also hold; if even a minority converts aggressively, the reserve does not grow, price stagnates per Eq. 1, and hoarders incur opportunity cost. The recursive dynamic is a stag-hunt: coordination on holding yields appreciation; unilateral deviation to fiat conversion yields safety but undermines the collective reserve.

Clock: The admin timelock on 7,500 retained tokens [PAGE 19] introduces a public, deterministic clock. Rational agents condition behavior on upcoming unlock blocks. Anticipating increased S_t, users may delay acquisition or accelerate exits before the release, because P_t = R_t / (S_t · κ) implies lower post-unlock price absent new demand. The clock converts opaque founder risk into scheduled supply risk, allowing market participants to price unlocks in advance. The OCR text does not state whether the smart contract enforces a vesting cliff, linear release, or market-based unlock mechanism; without such detail, the clock's exact cadence is unverifiable from [PAGE 19] alone, but its existence as a state variable in S_t is explicit.

Recursive equilibrium: The stable fixed point requires (a) merchants to hold a meaningful share of receipts, (b) users to buy tokens primarily for usage rather than speculation, and (c) reserve inflows to match or exceed fiat conversion outflows. Any one condition failing recursively undermines the others: lower price reduces user acquisition incentive, which reduces merchant volume, which increases fiat conversion pressure, which further reduces reserve and price.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The claimed method requires a blockchain network to receive a smart contract, validate it, and update/distribute a block [PAGE 19]. This presupposes physical infrastructure: nodes, electricity, telecommunications, and hardware security modules. A physical-layer veto—power outage, fiber cut, natural disaster, or hardware failure—prevents reception, validation, or block distribution. Because the text makes transaction execution conditional on network validation ("if the request is validated that performing the transaction, updating a certain block") [PAGE 19], no smart-contract term can override a physical-layer interruption. The physical layer holds final veto authority over ledger state transitions.

Biomechanical layer: Participation requires human users to manage private keys, sign transactions, and interact with wallets. Physical loss of keys, coercion, fatigue-induced input error, or device damage constitutes a biomechanical veto. The patent text does not describe key recovery, social recovery, or custodial abstraction; therefore a single biomechanical failure can irrevocably exclude a participant from the method of [PAGE 19].

Weather veto: Local-currency utility depends on physical foot traffic at local businesses offering discounts [PAGE 19]. Severe weather (storms, floods, extreme temperatures) reduces in-person purchases denominated in the local token, decreasing transaction volume, reserve inflows, and token velocity. Because the model's value proposition is tied to local commerce ("Local businesses can offer a discount for those who pay with the local currency") [PAGE 19], weather acts as an exogenous demand shock that the on-chain price function cannot absorb; P_t remains a function of R_t, S_t, and κ only, with no weather input parameter.

Adjudication: The cross-layer veto hierarchy is strictly ordered. Physical validation is the gatekeeper; if the secure ledger network cannot receive or validate the smart contract or the transaction request, the block is not updated [PAGE 19]. Biomechanical and weather layers operate upstream of the smart contract by shaping user and merchant behavior, but they cannot be encoded into Eq. 1 because the disclosed state parameters are limited to supply, reserve, and reserve-ratio constant [PAGE 19]. Thus the formal system is veto-proof only within the bounds of uninterrupted physical network operation; outside those bounds, the equations describe a state that cannot be transitioned.
