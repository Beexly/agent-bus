<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The conceptual foundations of inferential behavior trace back to European structuralist psychology, particularly the early work of the Berlin school. In 1913, Kurt Koffka demonstrated that rats could solve mazes by forming mental images of the environment, which he called “Ausbildung of the whole.”[Page citation needed] This marked the first empirical evidence for non-simple stimulus-response associations, paving the way for Tolman’s later theorizations on cognitive maps.

Edward Tolman built directly upon Koffka's ideas in his 1932 seminal work, introducing the term “cognitive map” to describe the mental structures that organisms use to navigate spatial environments. Central to his theory was the notion of “expectancy”—the organism formulates predictions about future events based on past experiences. Tolman argued that behavior is fundamentally purposeful, guided by goals and expectations rather than mere habit strength.

Hsiao's experimental design specifically tested whether rats could exhibit inferential reasoning by choosing indirect paths toward a goal when the direct route was blocked. This resonated with Tolman's broader hypothesis that animal intelligence involved more than passive association; it required active construction of spatial relationships within a “means-end-field.”

By the 1950s, Tolman's ideas had merged with cybernetics and information theory, influencing early artificial intelligence research. Norbert Wiener and others recognized that computational systems needed to simulate this kind of purposive behavior, involving predictive models of the environment. Today, the foundational concepts remain intact but have been augmented by neuroimaging techniques that allow scientists to visualize actual neural correlates of cognitive mapping.

Thus, the concept of inferential sign gestalt expectations crystallized from a historical trajectory beginning with structuralism, evolving through Tolman's purposive behaviorism, and continuing into modern cognitive neuroscience—a journey marked by both revolutionary breakthroughs and subtle detours in understanding how organisms reason about their world.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
