Mass and dash time therefore encode *the same* latent quantity — mass-specific power — which is exactly what $r(\text{Weight},\text{40Yard})=0.8592$ [PAGE 5] reports and what the significance pattern confirms: weight significant in 10/10 folds, height in 7/10, 40-yard time in **zero** folds ([PAGE 14]: "it is not deemed significant in a single one"). The physical veto is decisive: after conditioning on body mass, the dash time is a redundant proxy, so the network's third input contributes near-zero independent information and the reported advantage cannot plausibly be attributed to it. This same layer explains the sign of the dash coefficient: "faster players are less likely to be predicted as 'on the line'" [PAGE 14].

**Layer 3 — stature/lever-arm: ADMITTED, PARTIALLY.** Height is the residual channel through which limb length (blocking reach, leverage at the point of attack) enters. Its coefficient is positive — "taller players have a higher probability of playing a line position" [PAGE 14] — but its significance is fold-unstable at 7/10, consistent with $r(\text{Height},\text{Weight})=0.6943$ [PAGE 5] and $\mathrm{VIF}\ge3.82$. The biomechanical interpretation survives; the inferential one does not.

**Adjudication.** Physical plausibility is *necessary but not sufficient*. The biomechanical layer passes (mass dominates, dash is redundant, height is a weak lever proxy), the stature layer passes weakly, and the weather layer is inadmissible for absence of any operator. Crucially, passing the physical layer does **not** rescue the statistical claim: a physically well-motivated predictor set can still produce a statistically null model difference. The veto stands at the level of inference, not mechanism.
