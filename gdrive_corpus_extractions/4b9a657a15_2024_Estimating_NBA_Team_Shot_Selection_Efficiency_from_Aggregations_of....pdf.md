It supports location-conditional yield claims, including the observation that long 2PAs in the left baseline-extended region yielded “greater than 1.2 points per shot” for the 2021-22 Bucks [PAGE 3], and that deep-3 efficiency “fell off sharply beyond ~26 feet” [PAGE 3]. It also supports the historical line-move fact: the three-point line moved “from 23.75 feet to 22 feet before being restored” [PAGE 1]. It does not support extrapolation beyond the observed half-court, beyond the observed distance range, or to rule changes not present in the data. Veto any claim that the surface is physically invariant across line placements or arena geometries.

Biomechanical-layer adjudication: the paper models shot location and scoring outcome, not kinematics, release angle, jump load, fatigue, injury, defender distance, or player anthropometry. A GAM surface cannot identify a biomechanical mechanism. It can show that a region has high true yield; it cannot prove that a player’s biomechanics caused it. Veto causal claims about fatigue, altered landing mechanics, shooting form, or injury risk. The free-throw result is an outcome-accounting result, not a biomechanical proof: the paper says “failing to include shot-pursuant FT scoring places a substantial, and typically downward, bias on the estimated yield of shots near the basket” [PAGE 6]. That is an accounting bias, not a biomechanical law.

Weather-layer adjudication: the supplied source contains no weather covariate, no outdoor exposure, and no environmental measurement. NBA arenas are referenced in the SportVU adoption context [PAGE 1], but the model has no temperature, humidity, altitude, wind, or travel-weather layer. Veto any weather-based explanation of shot yield, free-throw rate, or three-point dispremium. The only admissible environmental statement is that the source does not estimate one.

Cross-layer verdict: approve descriptive and team-season associational claims about spatial true yield, Shot Selection Efficiency, payroll-conditional win association, and true-chart 3PA dispremium. Veto player-level causal biomechanics, weather effects, late-clock psychology, defensive-pressure causality, and any claim that the GAM surface is a structural physical law. The paper’s own strongest language is associational: “Shot Selection Efficiency is a ‘moneyball’ or supra-payroll source of wins” [PAGE 12], not a randomized causal identification.
