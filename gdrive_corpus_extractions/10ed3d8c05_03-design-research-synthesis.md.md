Environmental/weather layer: Solar glare reduces effective luminance contrast. Even if a design passes $$C \ge 4.5$$ in a lab, outdoor glare can push the effective ratio below threshold, constituting an environmental veto for Rating and Player Lab routes used in bright conditions. Similarly, cold or wet conditions reduce touch precision, increasing the effective D/W ratio in Fitts's Law and raising MT beyond the interaction budget. Network weather — congestion, handoff between cells, captive portals — inflates INP beyond 200 ms [PAGE 1] independent of code quality.

Veto proof: Define the composite veto $$\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{V}_{\text{phys}} \lor \mathcal{V}_{\text{bio}} \lor \mathcal{V}_{\text{env}}.$$ A route is deployable only if $$\mathcal{V} = \text{false}.$$ Because each layer has an independently measurable threshold (INP p75 = 200 ms [PAGE 1], C = 4.5, Fitts's MT budget, reduced-motion fallback presence), the system is veto-proof: no single layer can be optimized at the expense of another. For example, adding Awwwards-style craft motion [PAGE 1] might improve visual identity but trigger $$\mathcal{V}_{\text{phys}}$$ via INP > 200 ms; the motion must be deferred or removed regardless of its aesthetic merit. This cross-layer adjudication is what makes GSE a "command center" rather than generic SaaS: every visual primitive (signal, pressure, risk, freshness, movement, receipts) must survive all three layers simultaneously.
