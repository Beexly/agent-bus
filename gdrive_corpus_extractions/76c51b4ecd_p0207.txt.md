<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under fatigue or scoreboard pressure, operators regress to earliest-learned motor programs. The quarterback's instruction to "help carry the runner from the inside" [PAGE 1] may regress to a premature inside shove, destabilizing the fullback's forward lean or drawing a holding flag if contact persists after the defender is beaten. The fullback, expecting hip assistance on both sides [PAGE 1], may decelerate to wait for the left halfback's right-hip block, converting the "revolving mass" into a static maul and killing momentum. Linemen regress from the dynamic assignment to "block the man in front of me": the right tackle may continue blocking the guard to left [PAGE 1] even after that defender has looped outside, leaving the actual edge threat unblocked.

Counter-trap: the defense diagnoses "mass on right tackle" and crashes interior guards and tackles to clog the hole, expecting the fullback to bounce right behind the right end's seal [PAGE 1]. The counter-trap is a cutback left: because the center blocks left [PAGE 1] and the left guard goes through to cut off reinforcements [PAGE 1], the A-side is thinly defended if the defense over-commits to the right. Conversely, if the defense contains the edge by defeating the right end's "block opposing tackle out" [PAGE 1], the mass is funneled inside where unblocked linebackers wait.

Clock layer: the play clock compresses \tau_{lt}; a hurry-up offense reduces time for the left tackle to "come around quickly" [PAGE 1], pushing \tau_{lt} toward or past \tau_{crit}. Over a season, cumulative fatigue lowers peak acceleration a_{\max}, lengthening the release and path time for the pulling left tackle and the around-blocking left end. In the fourth quarter, the same assignment that succeeded in the first quarter becomes brittle precisely when clock pressure is highest.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: each block requires the no-slip condition |F_x| \le \mu_s m g \cos\theta. Assume an elite collegiate blocker of m = 110 kg on dry natural grass with \mu_s \approx 0.7; N \approx 1079 N, so F_{\max} \approx 755 N. To displace a 100 kg defender 0.5 m in 0.7 s from rest requires average velocity \approx 0.71 m/s and final velocity \approx 1.43 m/s; kinetic energy \approx 102 J, implying average force F \approx 204 N over 0.5 m. The required \mu = 204/1079 \approx 0.19, well below 0.7, so dry conditions do not veto the play. However, in standing water or mud, \mu_s can fall to \approx 0.2–0.3, placing F_{\max} near or below F_{\text{req}}. Veto regime: \mu_s < F_{\text{req}}/(m g \cos\theta).

Biomechanical layer: the left tackle must traverse a path s \approx 4–6 yards (3.7–5.5 m) from stance to the fullback's left hip [PAGE 1] before the defensive line collapses. If the available window is \Delta t \approx 1.2–1.5 s, required average speed v \approx 2.5–4.6 m/s. Peak acceleration from a three-point stance is bounded by human muscle force-velocity properties; sustained change-of-direction over several meters with direction reversal is typically \le 4–6 m/s^2. If s/\Delta t exceeds the blocker's sustainable velocity, or if the lateral load during the turn exceeds \mu_s N, the block arrives late or the blocker slips: physical veto.

Weather layer: precipitation introduces a lubricating film between cleat and turf, lowering \mu_s; snow obscures the visual cue for the left end to "follow around and pushes from the rear" [PAGE 1] and for the left tackle to locate the hip; wind degrades audible communication of any in-play adjustment. Because every assignment on [PAGE 1] depends on timely contact and sustained friction, adverse weather can veto the play independent of player intent: if \mu_s drops below the no-slip threshold or visibility prevents the rear push, the mass cannot revolve and the play collapses at the line of scrimmage.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — Left-tackle timing veto. Counterfactual: the defensive right end aligns wide and attacks the left tackle's outside shoulder at the snap, forcing the tackle to absorb contact rather than release. Verifiable condition: game film shows the left tackle's first step is lateral/backward to absorb the rush, and his arrival at the fullback's left hip occurs after the fullback has reached the line of scrimmage, i.e., \tau_{lt} > \tau_{crit}. Prediction: the mass does not coalesce; an unblocked middle linebacker meets the fullback at the point of attack; the play gains zero yards or loses yardage.

Scenario 2 — Edge-seal veto. Counterfactual: the defensive right tackle uses a rip/swim move to defeat the right end, who fails to "block opposing tackle out" [PAGE 1]. Verifiable condition: film shows the defensive tackle driven inside the frame of the offensive right tackle before the right halfback crosses the line of scrimmage; the right halfback blocks air. Prediction: the edge collapses inward; the fullback's path is constricted; the penetrating defensive tackle makes the tackle for a loss.

Scenario 3 — Weather/traction veto. Counterfactual: heavy rain in the second quarter reduces the static friction coefficient on natural grass below the no-slip threshold. Verifiable condition: broadcast footage shows the left guard's plant foot slide more than 0.3 m while attempting to "go through, cut off reinforcements" [PAGE 1], and the linebacker he was to block flows unimpeded to the hole. Prediction: reinforcements are not cut off; the linebacker tackles the fullback at or behind the line of scrimmage within 1.8 s of the snap.
