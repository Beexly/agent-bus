James and Bain ground social inference in outward display: “we apply the knowledge to infer other men’s feelings from their outward display” [PAGE 373]. This is a recursive theory-of-mind loop: Subject A feels $E$, produces display $\mathbf{M}$; Observer B observes $\mathbf{M}$ and infers $\hat E$. The mapping is compromised by the inhibitory arrest James describes. When a walking subject is suddenly arrested by a sound, sight, smell, or thought [PAGE 373], the observable motor output collapses while the felt emotion may spike; the inference channel $E\to\mathbf{M}$ becomes non-injective because high $E$ can yield low $\mathbf{M}$ (freezing).

Crisis habit regression: locomotion is a consolidated habit; under startle, the organism regresses to a fixed action pattern (arrest/freezing) rather than flexible deliberation. Counter-trap: the observer’s own startle response to the subject’s arrest creates social contagion, so B’s inference of A’s emotion is contaminated by B’s autonomic state within the same rebound window ($\tau_{rebound}\approx3\text{--}15\,\text{s}$ after vagal arrest). Clock: the sequence shock $\to$ momentary arrest $\to$ accelerated palpitations [PAGE 373] implies $\tau_{arrest}\approx0.5\text{--}2\,\text{s}$ (vagal) and $\tau_{rebound}\approx3\text{--}15\,\text{s}$ (sympathetic reinstatement). If the counter-trap (social observation) lands inside $\tau_{rebound}$, the inferred emotion reflects the observer’s physiology, not the subject’s.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto. Bilateral vagotomy or high-dose muscarinic blockade (atropine) eliminates the pneumogastric outgoing current described on [PAGE 373]. The subject still experiences the “sudden shock of feeling,” but the heart does not transiently stop or slow; James’s claim that every feeling produces a definite visceral wave is falsified at the efferent layer.

Biomechanical-layer veto. The walking-arrest example [PAGE 373] presumes central motor inhibition can override ongoing gait. In Parkinsonian gait, startle sometimes paradoxically improves locomotion (kinesia paradoxica) rather than arresting it, because the basal-ganglia–brainstem startle circuit interacts aberrantly with the locomotor pattern generator. The universal arrest law breaks down when motor-priority circuits veto the inhibitory siphon.

Weather/environmental-layer veto. Ambient temperature alters nerve-conduction velocity and baseline autonomic tone. In hypothermia, vagal effects are potentiated and the transient arrest may approach true asystole (singularity prolonged); in hyperthermia, sympathetic dominance masks vagal bradycardia, producing monotonic tachycardia. Thus James’s “seemingly universal” law [PAGE 373] is context-dependent: temperature, pharmacology, and lesion state each provide an independent veto that collapses the claimed exceptionlessness.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — Pharmacological vagal blockade. Intervention: intravenous atropine to block muscarinic vagal action at the sinoatrial node, then deliver a standardized auditory startle. Prediction: no transient HR arrest; $HR(t)$ shows monotonic sympathetic tachycardia rather than the stop–slow–palpitates sequence on [PAGE 373]. Verification: continuous ECG; RLVR reward = 1 if observed $\dot{HR}>0$ throughout the first 5 s post-startle, 0 otherwise. This vetoes the claim that pneumogastric arrest is a necessary component of emotional shock.

Scenario 2 — Central inhibitory lesion. Intervention: reversible inactivation of the vagal motor nucleus/nucleus ambiguus (or its homologous startle-inhibition node) while preserving somatic startle pathways. Prediction: startle produces somatic motor jump (flexion, head turn) with preserved or enhanced sympathetic tachycardia, but absence of the cardiac pause. Verification: simultaneous surface EMG and ECG; RLVR reward = 1 if EMG startle latency $<100\,\text{ms}$ and ECG shows no $HR$ dip below baseline for $>500\,\text{ms}$. This vetoes James’s conflation of central inhibitory arrest with peripheral vagal cardio-inhibition.

Scenario 3 — Sensory deafferentation of the trigger modality. Intervention: effective masking or local anesthesia of the sensory channel that normally delivers the “sudden shock” (e.g., intense broadband white noise to mask an auditory startle cue). Prediction: no subjective shock, no diffuse motor wave, no glandular/visceral discharge; the Bain–James emission $P(\mathbf{M}|E)$ collapses to baseline because $E\approx0$. Verification: self-report plus EMG/ECG; RLVR reward = 1 if all output channels remain within baseline variance for $10\,\text{s}$ post-cue. This vetoes the universality of the diffusion claim by showing the wave is gated by afferent arrival, not by feeling alone.
