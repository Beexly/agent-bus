1. DCNN feature operator. Let \(I_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}\) be a frame or frame patch. The convolutional feature extractor is
\[
z_t=\phi_{\theta}(I_t),
\]
where \(\phi_{\theta}\) denotes convolution, nonlinear activation, and pooling with parameters \(\theta\). The paper’s LeNet-5 description states: “The feature maps in the sub-sampling layer are subjected to spatial down-sampling with a ratio of 2” [PAGE 10]. Units: pixel intensities are dimensionless or device units; feature maps are dimensionless activations. Assumption: fixed camera calibration and sufficient resolution. Breakdown regime: occlusion, motion blur, severe illumination change, or unknown camera pose makes \(\phi_{\theta}\) non-injective and destabilizes downstream classification. The paper itself concedes susceptibility to “the quality of the images, lighting, camera angles and other environmental factors” [PAGE 29].

2. DELM forward model. A standard single-hidden-layer ELM/DELM with \(M\) hidden units is
\[
\hat{y}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\beta_i\,h(\mathbf{w}_i^{\top}\mathbf{x}+b_i),
\]
or in matrix form
\[
H\beta=T,\qquad H_{ji}=h(\mathbf{w}_j^{\top}\mathbf{x}_i+b_j),
\]
where \(\beta\) is the output-weight vector, \(T\) is the target matrix, and \(H\) is the hidden-layer output matrix. The manuscript says “Eq. (1) represents the activation feature” [PAGE 11], then gives a garbled display. Its matrix equation is reported as \(T=\xi B\) or equivalent in the corrupted text, and the source states: “The Moore-Penrose inverse of the matrix B is written as sign B⁺” [PAGE 11]. The intended solution is
\[
\hat{\beta}=H^{\dagger}T.
\]
Units: \(\mathbf{x}\) has feature units, \(\mathbf{w}_i\) has inverse feature units if the dot product is to be dimensionless before activation; \(\beta\) carries output units; \(T\) carries target units. Assumption: \(H\) has full column rank or is treated by pseudoinverse. Exact breakdown regime: if \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\to 0\), then \(\kappa(H)=\sigma_{\max}(H)/\sigma_{\min}(H)\to\infty\) and \(\hat{\beta}\) becomes numerically unstable. If \(H\) is rank-deficient, the pseudoinverse gives a minimum-norm solution but not a unique causal parameter estimate. A regularized form,
\[
\hat{\beta}=(H^{\top}H+\lambda I)^{-1}H^{\top}T,
\]
is required when \(H^{\top}H\) is singular; the manuscript does not state such regularization in the displayed DELM solution [PAGE 11]. This is a real singularity/breakdown regime, not a stylistic issue.

3. ChOA search operator. The source describes chimpanzee-inspired updates with coefficient vectors \(q\), \(s\), and \(y\): “Where k is the iteration number, xchimp is the ideal location of the chimpanzee, xprey is the best position (solution) found so far, , , and q s y are the vectors of coefficients” [PAGE 12]. \[
\mathbf{x}_i(k+1)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathbf{x}_j(k)-\mathbf{a}_j\odot \mathbf{d}_j,
\]
with
\[
\mathbf{d}_j=\left|\mathbf{c}_j\odot \mathbf{x}_j(k)-\mathbf{m}\odot \mathbf{x}_i(k)\right|,
\]
where the four \(\mathbf{x}_j\) are the best, second-best, third-best, and fourth-best agents, and \(\mathbf{m}\) is a chaotic or social vector. Units: positions carry the units of the optimized parameters; coefficients \(q,s,y\) are dimensionless. Assumption: the top-four leader set is meaningful and the chaotic map remains bounded. Breakdown regime: if all leaders collapse to the same point, then \(\mathbf{d}_j\to 0\) and the swarm loses exploration; if \(e\) is reduced too quickly, premature convergence occurs. The manuscript’s own claim that compact learning models “are susceptible to generating a sluggish or underdeveloped convergence rate” [PAGE 12] acknowledges this regime.

w_i=\frac{obj_{\max}-obj(x_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[obj_{\max}-obj(x_i)\right]}.
\]
Units: \(obj(x_i)\) has the objective-function units, \(obj_{\max}\) must have the same units, and \(w_i\) is dimensionless with \(\sum_i w_i=1\) if the denominator is positive. Assumption: \(obj_{\max}\) is the maximum objective value in the current population and \(obj(x_i)\leq obj_{\max}\) for every \(i\). Exact singularity regime: if \(obj(x_i)=obj_{\max}\) for all \(i\), the denominator is zero and \(w_i\) is undefined. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The tactical decision layer is a recursive belief problem. Let the coach’s belief about the opponent’s formation be \(B_c\), the opponent’s belief about the coach’s belief be \(B_o(B_c)\), and the coach’s action be \(a\). Under time pressure \(t\to T\), a crisis-habit regression model predicts that the coach increasingly samples from a prior policy \(\pi_0\), such as a familiar 4-4-2 or 3-5-2 shape. The paper presents exactly those formations as reference templates: “(a) 4-4-2 formation - involving four defenders, four midfielders and two forwards” and “(b) 3-5-2 formation leading to more control in the midfield” [PAGE 23]. A counter-trap arises when the opponent anticipates \(\pi_0\) and best-responds to the habitual shape. The clock term is the utility discount: as remaining time shrinks, the coach trades exploration for a known pattern. The ACHOA-DELM model could help only if it reports calibrated uncertainty and counterfactual options; instead, the manuscript emphasizes “Allowing real time tracking of player mistakes, in order to make tactical changes in real time” [PAGE 8] and “real-time tracking and error detection in matches” [PAGE 29]. That framing invites automation bias: a coach may treat a 95%+ classifier output as a certain tactical signal, even though the paper concedes it “does not have the ability to include contextual information, e.g., match dynamics, team strategy, player relationships, and situational awareness” [PAGE 30]. The correct counter-trap is not more confident classification; it is an uncertainty-aware policy that preserves optionality, randomizes predictable habits, and separates “clock pressure” from “model confidence.” Without calibration, recursive belief updating collapses into overtrust.
