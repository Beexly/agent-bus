The gray zone is precisely where the text is most vulnerable: the distinction between "legitimate phonetic formations showing the accepted laws" and "regrettable accidents such as nonce words, popular etymologies, and slang" [PAGE 98]. There is no stated operational criterion for when a nonce word graduates to legitimacy, nor for how internal grammatical constraints (the "strait-jacket manufactured for Latin and Greek") interact with social contact [PAGE 98-99]. Otto Jespersen is cited as the first to give philologists enough objectivity to describe language as actually spoken and written rather than as a prescriptive copybook [PAGE 99].

Step four extends the temporal analysis to classical languages. Latin, "slain by its own children," remained the universal language of clergy and learned men through the Middle Ages despite the rise of vulgar tongues [PAGE 99]. Sanskrit persisted in India alongside neo-Sanskritic languages; classical Arabic united the Moslem world despite dialectal divergence; Modern Hebrew survived two millennia without vernacular use [PAGE 99]. The multi-hop implication is that a language can be socially dead yet institutionally alive, provided the intercourse pattern shifts from oral vulgar transmission to written scholastic reproduction. The gray-zone audit therefore flags three under-specified boundaries: (1) the threshold at which social force overwhelms internal grammatical drift; (2) the observability window required to distinguish a nonce word from a sound law; and (3) the channel capacity below which a classical language ceases to reproduce itself faithfully across generations.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formalization. Let \mathcal{G} be a finite-dimensional manifold of grammatical and phonological parameters, and let \ell(t) \in \mathcal{G} denote the state of a language at time t. Let X_t be a discrete random variable over an alphabet \Sigma of utterances, with Shannon entropy H(X_t) measured in bits. The social intercourse pattern is represented by a time-varying contact intensity \mathcal{I}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}, dimensionless, derived from the interaction graph of speakers.

The language dynamics are modeled as
\frac{d\ell}{dt} = \underbrace{\mathbf{A}_{int}(\ell)}_{\text{internal grammatical drift}}\;[\text{parameter}\,\text{yr}^{-1}] + \underbrace{\mathbf{B}_{soc}(t;\mathcal{I})}_{\text{social intercourse operator}}\;[\text{parameter}\,\text{yr}^{-1}] + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)}_{\text{nonce/slang innovation noise}}\;[\text{bits}\,\text{utterance}^{-1}].
Assumptions: (A1) \mathbf{A}_{int} is Lipschitz continuous on compact subsets of \mathcal{G}; (A2) \mathcal{I}(t) is measurable and bounded; (A3) \boldsymbol{\xi}(t) is zero-mean with bounded variance \sigma^2.

Intergenerational mutual information, in bits, is
I(\ell_t; \ell_{t+\Delta t}) = H(\ell_t) + H(\ell_{t+\Delta t}) - H(\ell_t, \ell_{t+\Delta t}).
Channel capacity for transmission across a speech community is
C = \max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)\;[\text{bits per channel use}].
Breakdown/singularity regimes: (S1) Creolization singularity: if \|\mathbf{B}_{soc}\|_{\text{op}} > \lambda_c \|\mathbf{A}_{int}\|_{\text{op}} for a structural-stability constant \lambda_c, the attractor of internal grammar is destroyed and no single legitimate ancestor exists [PAGE 98]. (S2) Intelligibility collapse: if I(\ell_t; \ell_{t+\Delta t}) < \epsilon for an intelligibility threshold \epsilon > 0, the speech varieties are distinct languages. (S3) Classical-language extinction: if C < H(\ell_{\text{sacred}}) due to loss of native intercourse, the sacred variety fails to reproduce and diglossia emerges [PAGE 99].

Verbatim anchors: "languages were independent, quasi-biological entities, with their developments modified entirely by internal forces and needs" [PAGE 98]; "they are epiphenomena of human intercourse" [PAGE 98]; "Mischsprachen, of languages such as Lingua Franca, Swahili, Yiddish, Chinook Jargon" [PAGE 98]; "Otto Jespersen" and "copybook" [PAGE 99]; "slain by its own children" [PAGE 99].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from early nineteenth-century comparative philology to Wiener's cybernetic-inflected critique. Grimm and Rask established the Indo-European grouping and the Teutonic subfamily, positing a distant common parent [PAGE 98]. This evolutionism predated and initially outpaced biological Darwinism, but it carried a hidden metaphysical commitment: languages as independent, quasi-biological entities subject only to internal forces [PAGE 98]. Wiener reverses the vector of explanation: language is an epiphenomenon of intercourse, not an organism [PAGE 98].

The scientific genealogy continues through Otto Jespersen, whose descriptive turn made philology a representation of language as actually spoken rather than a prescriptive copybook for teaching Eskimos or Chinese [PAGE 99]. Yet the deeper historical layer is the persistence of classical languages without vernacular vitality: medieval Latin as the universal language of Western clerics and learned men [PAGE 99]; Sanskrit beside neo-Sanskritic languages in India; classical Arabic uniting the Moslem world despite dialectal fission [PAGE 99]; Modern Hebrew surviving two thousand years without use in the time of Christ [PAGE 99]. These cases show that institutional and liturgical intercourse can maintain a language long after vulgar communication has abandoned it—a retro-causal pattern in which the scholarly function preserves the form that the social function has discarded.

Multi-week traps: observing language over a window of only weeks captures nonce words, slang, and popular etymology but misses phonetic drift governed by \mathbf{A}_{int} [PAGE 98-99]. Conversely, observing over centuries without measuring \mathcal{I}(t) mistakes contact-induced creolization for internal decay. The trap is to infer \mathbf{B}_{soc} = 0 from a short time series, or to infer \mathbf{A}_{int} = 0 from a long one.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when social pressure forces speakers to abandon descriptive accuracy for prescriptive rigidity. Under colonization, trade collapse, or religious orthodoxy, a community regresses to the "strait-jacket manufactured for Latin and Greek" [PAGE 98-99], attempting to force all grammatical phenomena into an inherited paradigm rather than attending to actual intercourse [PAGE 99]. The regressed speaker models the listener as a prescriptive judge rather than a communicative partner, raising the stakes of correctness above mutual intelligibility.

The counter-trap is the descriptive overcorrection: if all linguistic forms are merely social epiphenomena [PAGE 98], then grammatical norms lose all constraining force, and the analyst cannot predict which nonce words will fossilize. This is the recursive trap of theory-of-mind modeling: each speaker models the other's model of "proper" language, creating a higher-order fixation that accelerates divergence from the intercourse patterns that originally generated the language [PAGE 98].

The clock operates on two timescales. The grammatical-change clock \tau_{gram} runs over centuries (internal drift \mathbf{A}_{int}); the social-intercourse clock \tau_{soc} runs over days to weeks in trade, conquest, or liturgical contact \mathcal{I}(t) [PAGE 98-99]. When \tau_{soc} \ll \tau_{gram}, Mischsprachen emerge because the social operator \mathbf{B}_{soc} overpowers internal grammar before the community can consolidate a legitimate ancestor [PAGE 98]. The recursive loop closes when speakers, aware of the clock mismatch, deliberately preserve a classical variety (Latin, Arabic, Hebrew) as a high-prestige attractor insulated from the fast social clock [PAGE 99].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Articulatory phonetics imposes biomechanical bounds on \mathcal{G}. No social prestige can sustain a phoneme requiring vocal-tract configurations outside the feasible motor repertoire; such phonemes are vetoed in loanword adaptation regardless of their status in the donor language [PAGE 98 implicit in "legitimate phonetic formations"]. Biomechanical layer veto: hearing thresholds and speech-motor repetition rates bound the channel's symbol rate and noise floor \sigma^2.

Weather/social layer veto: The contact intensity \mathcal{I}(t) is driven by exogenous conditions—trade routes, conquest, clerical networks—analogous to weather in its unpredictability [PAGE 98-99]. A sustained drop in \mathcal{I}(t), such as the decline of the medieval clerics' universal Latin network, vetoes the Ciceronian standard and permits Thomistic Latin to diverge until Latin is "slain by its own children" [PAGE 99]. Conversely, high-intensity trade contact vetoes grammatical purity and produces Lingua Franca or Chinook Jargon [PAGE 98].

Adjudication rule: Any proposed trajectory of language change that predicts a phonological or grammatical state violating the articulatory bound or the \|\mathbf{B}_{soc}\| > \lambda_c \|\mathbf{A}_{int}\| creolization threshold is physically and socially impossible; the higher-layer theory (e.g., monogenetic descent from a single legitimate ancestor) is falsified [PAGE 98].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
