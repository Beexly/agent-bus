$$ \forall \varepsilon > 0, \exists \delta > 0 \text{ such that } \mu(E) < \delta \implies |\nu(E)| < \varepsilon, \quad \forall E \in \mathcal{S}. $$
This is the classical $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition, ensuring no "jumps" in $\nu$ occur on sets of vanishingly small $\mu$-measure.

$$ \int_{\{x : |f(x)| > c\}} |f| \, d\mu < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
Now, split any set $E \in \mathcal{S}$ into $E_1 = E \cap \{ |f| \le c \}$ and $E_2 = E \cap \{ |f| > c \}$. Then:
$$ |\nu(E)| \le \int_{E_1} |f| \, d\mu + \int_{E_2} |f| \, d\mu \le c \cdot \mu(E_1) + \int_{\{ |f| > c \}} |f| \, d\mu. $$
Choosing $\delta = \min\left( \frac{\varepsilon}{2c}, \text{threshold for tail} \right)$ ensures that if $\mu(E) < \delta$, then $|\nu(E)| < \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/2 = \varepsilon$.
