Biomechanical layer (oculomotor plant). Spatial constancy depends on efference-copy signals \mathbf{e}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{muscles}} from extraocular muscles and head-on-body proprioception. If the oculomotor loop is open (e.g., paralysis or fixation paralysis), the constancy operator \mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{e}) loses its eye-position term and cannot stabilize \hat{\mathbf{x}} across saccades. Veto proof: models of spatial perception that omit \mathbf{e}(t) and claim constancy under free viewing are biomechanically vetoed. Breakdown regime: \|\dot{\mathbf{e}}\| > \dot{e}_{crit} [rad s^{-1}] exceeds the update rate of \mathcal{C}, producing spatial smearing.

Weather/environmental layer (illumination and atmosphere). Color and spatial contrast constancy require a stable illumination spectrum E(\lambda) [W m^{-2} nm^{-1}] and known atmospheric transmittance \tau(\lambda). Under fog, thunderstorm, or rapid cloud occlusion, E(\lambda) becomes time-varying and non-identifiable, making P(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{x}) ill-conditioned. Veto proof: constancy claims predicated on fixed daylight are vetoed under undefined or rapidly fluctuating illumination. Breakdown regime: spectral Cramer-Rao bound for illuminant estimation diverges when the sensor null space overlaps the illumination variation subspace.

Adjudication hierarchy. Physical veto is first-order: if the optical signal contains no metric information, no downstream operator can manufacture it. Biomechanical veto is second-order: if the oculomotor state is unavailable, spatial constancy cannot be computed even with good optics. Weather veto is third-order: if illumination is undefined, the likelihood itself is undefined.
