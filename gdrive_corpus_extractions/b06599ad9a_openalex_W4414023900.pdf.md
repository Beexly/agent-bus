\[
\mathbf{p}_t=\pi_{\mathrm{fish}}(\mathbf{X}_t;\mathbf{K},\mathbf{D})+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t,
\]
where \(\mathbf{K}\) is the intrinsic matrix in pixels, \(\mathbf{D}\) is the distortion-coefficient vector and is dimensionless, and \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\) is measurement error in pixels. Assumption: the camera is rigidly mounted, intrinsics are known, and the ball center is visible inside the calibrated field of view. The source requires a “fisheye lens with an exceptional 170 or 180-degree field of view” [PAGE 6] and gives the “2MP 1085P H.264 UVC fisheye video camera board” as a candidate [PAGE 6]. Breakdown regimes: near the 170–180 degree boundary the inverse projection becomes ill-conditioned; strong distortion, rolling shutter, vibration, or calibration drift make \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t\) non-zero-mean and spatially correlated. In those regimes, intersection in image space is not a reliable proxy for 3D passage.

\[
(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_t,\hat{c}_t,\hat{s}_t)=f_{\mathrm{YOLOv8}}(\mathbf{I}_t),
\]
where \(\mathbf{b}_t=(x_t,y_t,w_t,h_t)\) is a bounding box in pixels, \(\hat{c}_t\) is the class label, and \(\hat{s}_t\in[0,1]\) is a confidence probability. The source says YOLOv8 “employs a single convolutional neural network to simultaneously predict object classes and their associated bounding boxes in real-time” [PAGE 6]. Assumption: \(\mathbf{I}_t\) has been resized, filtered, and colour-balanced as described in the pseudo-algorithm [PAGE 8]. Breakdown regimes: the paper reports that “YOLOv8's performance in detecting the basketball across consecutive frames during image acquisition exhibits variability” [PAGE 7], and that YOLOv8 lacks “inherent mechanisms for recognizing scoring events” [PAGE 7]. \[
\mathbf{x}_{t+\Delta t|t}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{x}_{t|t},\qquad

with state \(\mathbf{x}_t=[\mathbf{p}_t,\mathbf{v}_t]^{\top}\), position in pixels, velocity in pixels per second, covariance \(\mathbf{P}_t\) in pixels squared and pixels squared per second, and process noise \(\mathbf{Q}\). The source’s pseudo-algorithm says “Define the scoring zone near the hoop using Intersection Detection” [PAGE 8] and then “IF projectedPath intersects with the scoring zone: Score tracking: scoreCount = scoreCount + 1.” [PAGE 8]. Formally,
\[
S_t=\mathbf{1}\left[\exists\tau\in[t,t+\Delta t]:\hat{\mathbf{p}}_\tau\in Z_t\right],
\]
where \(S_t\in\{0,1\}\). Assumption: \(Z_t\) is correctly calibrated and the projected path is accurate. Breakdown regimes: tangent intersection at the zone boundary, zone size mismatch, occlusion behind the rim, and false projection through the zone after a rim bounce. The source does not specify a downward-velocity gate or a 3D hoop-plane crossing in the pseudo-algorithm; that omission is a documented gray zone, not an inferred requirement.

\[
C_t^{+}=C_t+S_t,

followed by correction

C_{t+\Delta t}=C_t^{+}-\Delta C_t^{\mathrm{corr}},

where \(\Delta C_t^{\mathrm{corr}}\in\{0,1\}\) is applied when “any inconsistency detected” [PAGE 8]. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The system is embedded in a social adjudication loop: the vision model believes a score occurred, the referee may believe otherwise, the operator may believe the model, and the players may dispute the display. The pseudo-algorithm encodes this recursively: it increments `scoreCount`, then “Listen for referee announcements,” checks exclusion zones, and “adjust scoreCount if necessary” [PAGE 8]. That means the model’s belief is not final; it is a provisional belief about what the referee and RFID layer will later confirm. The recursive theory-of-mind failure is crisis habit regression: when a false score appears, an operator under time pressure may skip the referee cross-check, disable the validation branch, or manually increment the count. The source’s own “adjust scoreCount if necessary” [PAGE 8] creates a human-in-the-loop correction path that can become a habit of after-the-fact patching rather than pre-display veto.

The counter-trap is overcorrecting with more layers. A designer may add multiple cameras, image fusion, and more powerful processing, but the paper warns that multiple cameras “simultaneously causes a lot of technical intricacies and development costs” [PAGE 2]. The counter-trap is believing that additional sensors automatically resolve ambiguity, when in fact fusion introduces synchronization, calibration, and latency failure modes. A second counter-trap is over-trusting RFID: if the vision layer is treated as merely decorative because RFID is “used primarily for verification purposes” [PAGE 1], then vision false positives may be tolerated or ignored until the verification layer fails.

The clock is the third recursive constraint. Referee announcements are asynchronous, exclusion zones are time-indexed, and the score display is expected in real time. The system must align frames, trajectory timestamps, referee events, and exclusion intervals on a common clock. If the clock skews, a valid score can be excluded by a stale timeout zone, or an invalid score can be accepted because the referee announcement arrives after the display update. The paper’s real-time concern [PAGE 9] is therefore not only computation time; it is belief-update time. The correct recursive design is to display a provisional score only if the local vision evidence is strong, then finalize only after the referee/RFID layer has had a bounded time window to veto. That preserves the paper’s redundancy goal while preventing the operator from becoming the de facto scoring authority.

A score should be accepted only when no layer vetoes it. The physical layer vetoes when the ball does not actually pass through the hoop: a rim bounce, a ball passing behind the backboard, or a fisheye projection that maps a near-miss into the scoring zone. The source’s single-camera geometry makes this layer especially important because the camera is “directly above the basketball hoop” [PAGE 5] and uses a wide fisheye lens [PAGE 6]. The biomechanical layer vetoes when players occlude the ball or hoop: “Occlusions occur when players block each other, making it difficult to detect individual players accurately” [PAGE 3], and “Drastic interactions, like slam dunks or steals, can also hinder player detection algorithms” [PAGE 3]. The paper also notes “frequent and severe occlusions” in multi-player tracking [PAGE 4]. In a dunk, the hand and forearm may cover the ball exactly at the scoring-zone crossing; in a screen or steal, bodies may hide the ball for several frames. The environmental layer vetoes when lighting, glare, vibration, dust, or exposure changes corrupt detection; the paper explicitly names “fluctuating lighting situations” [PAGE 9]. Weather is not addressed in this indoor basketball source, so an outdoor weather veto would be an extrapolation, not a source claim; the defensible environmental proxy is lighting and mechanical vibration.

Formally, the adjudication is a conjunction of vetoes:
\[
V_t=D_t\land T_t\land\neg E_t\land\neg R_t\land L_t,
\]
where \(D_t\) is detection sufficiency, \(T_t\) is trajectory/zone evidence, \(E_t\) is an exclusion-zone hit, \(R_t\) is referee contradiction, and \(L_t\) is latency feasibility. All terms are Boolean. The score increment is \(\Delta C_t=\mathbf{1}[V_t]\). This is not a weighted ratio; it is a veto logic. A physical veto alone is sufficient: if \(D_t=0\) because the ball is occluded, no score is accepted even if the projected path crosses the zone. A biomechanical veto alone is sufficient: if the ball is hidden by a hand at the critical frame, the system must wait for post-occlusion evidence or referee/RFID confirmation. An environmental veto alone is sufficient: if glare destroys ball contrast, the confidence threshold must fail closed. A clock veto alone is sufficient: if \(L_t=0\), the display must flag the score as provisional rather than final. The source’s validation language supports this: “cross-referencing with referee announcements, timeout-based exclusion zones and error correction techniques” [PAGE 9]. The proof obligation is that every accepted score has at least one independent witness beyond the projected-path intersection, and every rejected score records which layer vetoed.
