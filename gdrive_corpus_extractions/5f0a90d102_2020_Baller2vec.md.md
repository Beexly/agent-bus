<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage runs from Vaswani et al. (2017) Transformers, through sports-specific MASM precursors, to baller2vec’s multi-entity attention tensor. Zheng et al. (2016) pioneered discretized trajectories but required “macro-goal” destination inputs; Felsen et al. (2018) introduced conditional variational autoencoders with role-alignment preprocessing that imposes an agent order and risks destroying identity information; Yeh et al. (2019) combined variational RNNs with graph neural networks, achieving permutation invariance via GNN message passing but limiting personalization because edge/type embeddings scale poorly with player count; Zhan et al. (2019) used programmatic weak supervision for multi-agent trajectory generation. Alcorn (2018) showed neural embeddings can encode player attributes in baseball (batter|pitcher2vec), a result baller2vec extends to basketball. In computer vision, Giuliari et al. (2020) applied Transformers to individual pedestrians without interaction modeling; Yu et al. (2020) used separate temporal and spatial Transformers for pedestrians, processing agents independently in each sub-network; Sanford et al. (2020) used Transformers for soccer event classification but restricted inputs to the \(K\)-nearest players and imposed a distance-based ordering that can change between windows.

Multi-week traps arise directly from this lineage. First, single-season training (2015–16 only) means identity embeddings overfit season-specific rosters and coaching schemes; the paper notes the Golden State Warriors’ record three-point offense as an example of a pattern recognizable from bird’s-eye view without IDs (Section 6). A model deployed in week 1 of a new season would face roster turnover, coaching changes, and rule emphasis shifts, causing identity embeddings to regress toward team-system prototypes rather than intrinsic player qualities. Second, the static fully connected attention mask cannot encode evolving defensive schemes or switching rotations, so multi-week tactical adaptation requires re-architecting the mask tensor rather than fine-tuning weights. Third, discretization into fixed 11 ft \(\times\) 11 ft and 19 ft cubes assumes NBA court dimensions and rim height; international or college courts, or altered arena geometries, shift the cell-label mapping and invalidate label semantics without re-discretization. Fourth, because baller2vec is trained by maximum likelihood on one data distribution, it lacks calibrated uncertainty for out-of-distribution game states (e.g., overtime, end-of-clock scenarios), making multi-week deployment sensitive to distribution shift.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears in baller2vec when historical context is impoverished. At \(t=0\), with zero seconds of observation, the model’s forecast distribution is diffuse, split between “stationary” and generic movement toward the opponent’s basket (Figure 8); it regresses to the prior rather than committing to a specific intent. Under increased ambiguity—such as when a ball handler drives past a defender at \(t=8\)—the distribution splits between committing to the handler or staying with the trailing man, revealing a regression to conservative, state-averaging behavior rather than decisive theory-of-mind inference.

The counter-trap is the model’s learned anticipation of others’ mental states. Figure 1 documents a self-attention head that, at \(t=3\), assigns near-zero weight to all players when the ball handler drives (predicting no pass), then at \(t=7\) assigns high weight to the teammate who receives the pass at \(t=12\). This is a recursive theory-of-mind operation: the model represents the ball handler’s likely intent, the defender’s positioning, and the teammate’s availability, then assigns credit to the future recipient before the pass occurs. Figure 10 extends this: head 2-7 tracks the ball handler’s teammates through time, while head 6-2 anticipates the pass recipient. These heads function as implicit “what is the other agent thinking?” modules.

The clock is the 4-second, 5 Hz observation window (20 steps). Within this clock, causal masking prevents future leakage, but the finite horizon imposes a recency bias: the model weights recent frames more heavily, so as the window progresses from \(t=0\) to \(t=8\), certainty initially rises, then falls again when novel defensive pressure arrives. The recursive tension is therefore: under clock pressure and limited history, does the model regress to myopic ball-handler tracking (crisis habit) or maintain the counter-trap of teammate anticipation? Figure 9 shows that generated trajectories become noisier with less historical context, confirming that the counter-trap depends on sufficient clock depth; when history is stripped, the model’s theory-of-mind degrades into undirected diffusion.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto. The player discretization uses \(1\text{ ft}\times1\text{ ft}\) cells over an \(11\text{ ft}\times11\text{ ft}\) region. NBA sprint speed exceeds \(30\text{ ft/s}\); at \(5\text{ Hz}\) (\(0.2\text{ s}\) per step), a player can traverse \(>6\text{ ft}\) in one step, skipping multiple cells. The classification label therefore forces a discontinuous jump across cells, producing high gradient noise and label ambiguity near court boundaries. A player near the sideline attempting an outward drive may have no valid cell outside the grid, creating a hard boundary veto: trajectories exiting the \(11\text{ ft}\) window are unrepresentable.

Biomechanical veto. Ball motion is discretized in \(19\text{ ft}\times19\text{ ft}\times19\text{ ft}\) cubes. Arena heights and lob passes can exceed \(19\text{ ft}\) in the \(z\)-dimension; high-arcing passes or alley-oops that leave the cube are clipped or mislabeled, vetoing accurate ball-forecasting for vertical plays. Player acceleration limits are not explicitly encoded, so the model may assign probability to physically impossible one-step displacements unless the data distribution itself suppresses them.

Weather veto. Basketball is played indoors; weather is not a direct input. However, camera calibration, lighting, and optical tracking noise are unmodeled measurement processes. If tracking jitter exceeds \(1\text{ ft}\), cell labels flip stochastically, vetoing the assumption that observed labels equal true labels.

Cross-layer interaction. Section 2.3 fixes the attention tensor to a static fully connected graph; Section 7 notes the mask can be adapted for dynamic graphs but was not. This vetoes modeling of time-varying defensive assignments (e.g., switching screens) unless the mask tensor is re-engineered per possession. Identity embeddings are vetoed by season entanglement (Section 6): because rosters and systems are static within the 2015–16 half-season, the model cannot separate intrinsic player traits from team strategy, so cross-layer transfer from identity embeddings to new lineups is invalid without additional seasons. No veto is triggered by weather because none exists in the domain, but measurement noise and grid quantization constitute the operative physical vetoes.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 (season-transfer identity collapse). Train baller2vec on 2015–16 data, then evaluate Task P on 2016–17 held-out games with unchanged architecture. Veto condition: if the 10-I versus 10-NI NLL gap falls below 1% or reverses, player identity embeddings are confirmed to encode season-specific roster/coach effects rather than transferable intrinsic qualities. Prediction: perplexity for the identity model on 2016–17 approaches the no-identity baseline, because 2015–16 lineups (e.g., Warriors spacing) do not recur.

Veto scenario 2 (spatial discretization boundary failure). Construct a counterfactual test set in which a player starts within 1 ft of the court edge and attempts a drive requiring \(>1\text{ ft}\) of lateral motion within a single 0.2 s step. Veto condition: if Test NLL spikes by \(>50\%\) relative to interior-court sequences, the 1-ft grid cannot represent high-velocity boundary motion. Prediction: perplexity for edge-start sequences collapses toward the uniform label-distribution baseline (16.90 for Task P) because the true destination lies outside the representable cell set.

Veto scenario 3 (causal-mask information leak ablation). Remove the square-subsequent temporal mask so attention at \(t_1\) may attend to \(t_2>t_1\), then evaluate on the standard test set and on a temporally permuted test set (time steps shuffled within each 4-second sequence). Veto condition: if standard-test perplexity improves artificially but permuted-test perplexity degrades to the training-distribution baseline (Task P: 16.90; Task B: 322.40), the model was exploiting future leakage rather than learning causal dynamics. Prediction: causal masking is necessary; the unmasked variant shows spurious gains on ordered data and catastrophic failure under permutation.
