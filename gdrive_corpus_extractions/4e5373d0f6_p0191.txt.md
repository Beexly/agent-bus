Model the interaction recursively. Level 0: the kicker chooses θ. Level 1: the returner and coverage unit anticipate θ based on down, distance, field position, and game clock. Level 2: the kicker anticipates that anticipation.

Crisis habit regression: under pressure (close score, late clock), a kicker tends to regress to the text’s baseline, θ = 0, because a straight kick is “quite sufficient” and carries less out-of-bounds risk than the sideline rule [PAGE 191]. This regression is predictable to an opponent who has film on the kicker’s high-leverage punts.

Counter-trap: if the opponent expects regression and shifts coverage toward the middle, a verified high-skill kicker (low σ_K) can instead execute θ_s and pin the ball near the sideline. The counter-trap only pays out when the kicker’s reliability exceeds the text’s threshold; for an unreliable kicker, the angled kick under pressure produces out-of-bounds or short kicks, worsening field position relative to the straight baseline.

Clock layer: as the game clock winds down, the value of field-position asymmetry changes. A touchback hands the opponent a known starting point (their own 20-yard line equivalent in modern spacing; in 1921, a dead ball behind the goal line conferred defined possession), whereas a ball pinned inside the 10-yard line [PAGE 191] forces the opponent to traverse nearly the full 91.44 m. The recursive equilibrium is a mixed strategy: the kicker plays θ_s with probability p and θ = 0 with probability 1−p; the coverage allocates fraction c to the sideline and 1−c to the middle. Expected field position is bilinear in (p, c). A saddle point exists only when the kicker’s σ_K is low enough that the sideline option remains a credible threat; otherwise p collapses to 0 and the opponent sets c = 0 (all coverage middle), confirming the text’s baseline supremacy for average operators.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three independent veto layers must all clear before a sideline kick is recommended; a single failure defaults to the straight kick per [PAGE 191].

1. Physical/geometric layer: field width W ≈ 48.77 m. A lateral kick is physically admissible only if |K sin θ| ≤ W/2 unless an intentional out-of-bounds is desired. Veto condition: P(|K sin θ| > W/2) exceeds the tolerance for dead-ball turnover. On a narrow field or with large θ, this probability rises sharply.

2. Biomechanical layer: punter consistency characterized by σ_K. Veto condition: σ_K / μ_K > 1/3 (high relative variability). This is the formal translation of “unless he is an unusually reliable kicker” [PAGE 191]; when relative variability is high, the expected value Δ̄(θ_s) is dominated by variance and OOB risk.

3. Weather layer: crosswind w_y (m/s) adds lateral stochasticity δ ~ 𝒩(0, σ_w²) to the ball’s trajectory. Veto condition: σ_w > 1.5 m/s or headwind component sufficient to reduce μ_K below the distance needed to reach the target zone. Precipitation further degrades drop consistency and footing, increasing σ_K.

Adjudication: the sideline kick is permitted only if all three layers pass. In the short-range regime (x₀ within 41.148 m of the goal [PAGE 191]), an additional precision veto applies: if the kicker cannot bound P(overshoot past the goal line | x₀, θ_s) below the tolerance for a touchback, the conservative straight kick is selected. The veto proof is conjunctive, not compensatory; biomechanical reliability cannot be traded against weather tolerance.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
