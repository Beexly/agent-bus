**Hop 2 — The information-theoretic ceiling.** The pipeline forms a Markov chain $y \to F_{1:T} \to z \to q(z)$, where $y$ is the agent label, $F$ the raw frame sequence, $z$ the latent, $q$ the k-means quantizer. **O1 — Frame tensor (units: dimensionless channel counts; feature values scaled to $[0,1]$).**
$$X_t \in [0,1]^{h\times w\times c_o}, \qquad A_t \in \{0,1\}^{h\times w\times c_a}, \qquad F_t = X_t \;\|\; A_t$$
Assumption: $c_o$ concatenates numeric channels (Hit Points, Resources, 2-D relative attack position) with one-hot categoricals; the paper's modification 2 states "We use a two-dimensional representation of relative attack position to preserve distances" [PAGE 2]. Breakdown regime: one-hot blow-up makes $c_o$ sparse; when the number of non-null classes per categorical is small, the frequency features of the baseline and the one-hot channels of the tensor carry nearly identical information, which is why handcrafted remains competitive.

**O2 — Frame encoder (time-agnostic CNN).**
$$e_t = \Phi_{\text{CNN}}(F_t) = \mathrm{GELU}\!\circ\mathrm{MaxPool}\circ\mathrm{Conv}\;\big(\mathrm{GELU}\circ\mathrm{MaxPool}\circ\mathrm{Conv}(F_t)\big)$$
Per [PAGE 2]: "two blocks of Convolution, Max Pooling, and GELU." Units: $e_t$ is dimensionless. Assumption: shared weights across $t$ — i.e., strict time-agnosticism. Singularity: because $\Phi_{\text{CNN}}$ is time-agnostic, all temporal ordering information must be carried by O3; if $|\partial e_t/\partial t|$ is small (static frames), the BiLSTM has nothing to integrate and $z$ degenerates to a bag-of-frames code.

**O3 — Bidirectional latent.**
$$\overrightarrow{h}_t=\mathrm{LSTM}(\overrightarrow{h}_{t-1},e_t),\quad \overleftarrow{h}_t=\mathrm{LSTM}(\overleftarrow{h}_{t+1},e_t),\quad z=\big[\overrightarrow{h}_{T};\overleftarrow{h}_{1}\big]\in\mathbb{R}^{1024}$$
Per [PAGE 2]: "the latent representation $z$ of size 1024 (512 from each LSTM direction)." Units: dimensionless, 1024 real scalars. **Breakdown/capacity singularity:** a 32-frame input ($T=32$) is compressed into 1024 dimensions. If $\dim(z) \ge \mathrm{rank}(\{F_{1:T}\})$ over the training set, the autoencoder admits a near-lossless identity solution, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}\to 0$, with $z$ an approximately invertible recoding of $F$. By DPI, $I(z;y)\to I(F;y)$ but no abstraction is gained, and any clustering is clustering the raw traces under a warped metric. A low reconstruction loss is therefore a *red flag*, not a success criterion — and the paper reports no reconstruction baseline to calibrate against.

**O4 — Decoder objective (units: nats for the categorical term if natural log; squared feature units for the numeric term).**
$$\mathcal{L}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big[\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{CE}\big(y_{t,c},\hat p_{t,c}\big)+\lambda\!\!\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\!\big(y_{t,n}-\hat y_{t,n}\big)^{2}\Big]$$
Justified by [PAGE 2]: "distribution probabilities for categorical features and predicted values for numerical features." **Unpinned operator:** $\lambda$ is never reported. Since $\mathcal{N}$ has 4 channels (HP, Resources, $x$, $y$) and $\mathcal{C}$ has 16 one-hot groups, the implicit weighting decides whether the latent is dominated by spatial or by scalar dynamics. This is an unreported degree of freedom with direct downstream consequences.

**O5 — Projection and partition.**
$$u = P_{64}^{\top}(z-\bar z),\quad P_{64}=\arg\max_{P^{\top}P=I_{64}}\mathrm{tr}(P^{\top}\Sigma_z P),\quad \{S_1..S_k\}=k\text{-means}(u)$$
**Singularity regimes, stated exactly:** (i) if the eigenvalue spectrum of $\Sigma_z$ is flat, $\lambda_{65}/\lambda_1 \approx 1$, PCA is a rotation and discards label information arbitrarily; (ii) if a few eigenvalues dominate, $u$'s leading coordinates encode game progression — the *time-axis confound* — and clusters are stages, not styles; (iii) PCA maximizes variance, not $I(u;y)$; there is no guarantee that discriminative directions survive truncation from 1024 → 64.

**O6 — Chance-adjusted recovery metrics (dimensionless, $\in[-1,1]$ for ARI, $\le 1$ for AMI; entropy in bits or nats).**
$$h=1-\frac{H(C\mid K)}{H(C)},\qquad c=1-\frac{H(K\mid C)}{H(K)}$$
$$\mathrm{ARI}=\frac{\sum_{ij}\binom{n_{ij}}{2}-\Big[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\Big]\Big/\binom{n}{2}}{\tfrac12\Big[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}+\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\Big]-\Big[\sum_i\binom{a_i}{2}\sum_j\binom{b_j}{2}\Big]\Big/\binom{n}{2}}$$
$$\mathrm{AMI}=\frac{I(C;K)-\mathbb{E}[I(C;K)]}{\mathrm{norm}\{H(C),H(K)\}-\mathbb{E}[I(C;K)]}$$
**Exact breakdown:** $h$ is undefined ($0/0$) when $H(C)=0$ — a single agent in the slice; $c$ is undefined when $H(K)=0$ — a single cluster; the ARI denominator vanishes when $k=1$ or when every trace is its own cluster. The paper's own guard — "We limit the clustering to sequences that are coherent both temporally (e.g., the starting subsequence) and spatially (same map and same side)" [PAGE 2] — bounds these degeneracies but does not eliminate them. The **AMI normalization variant is unpinned**: max-entropy, arithmetic-mean, and min-entropy normalizations give different numbers on identical partitions, and the paper does not state which. Reported AMI values are therefore not exactly comparable to other studies using a different convention.

**O7 — Windowing operator (units: frames).**
$$N_w(T)=\Big\lfloor \tfrac{T-32}{8}\Big\rfloor+1,\qquad \text{train stride}=8,\ \text{test/val stride}=32$$
Per [PAGE 2]: "subsequences of length 32 with a stride of 8 for training... and 32 for testing and validation." **Breakdown:** if $T_{\text{end}}<32$ — a game terminated by WorkerRush pre-emption — then $N_w=1$ and the single window is padding-dominated; the label $y$ is assigned to a trace that never expressed it, so $I(F;y)\to 0$ by construction on that subset.

The lineage runs 1996 → 2025 as a single argument about *what unit of behavior is the right unit of analysis*, and this paper is both its inheritor and, in one respect, its recidivist.

[5]) replaces types with factor analysis of survey plus usage data, moving from *a priori* categories to *extracted* dimensions; Tychsen & Canossa (2008, ref. [6]) impose the three-tier hierarchy — "play modes, play styles, and play personas" [PAGE 1] — which is the first move to separate the *level of the game* from the *habit of the player*. Bakkes, Spronck & van Lankveld (2012, ref. [7]) then split the field into "four approaches to player behavior modeling, distinguishing between models based purely on gameplay data and those grounded in psychology and sociology" [PAGE 1]. This paper declares its allegiance explicitly: "This research takes the quantitative approach from [7]" [PAGE 1].

The critical branch begins with Drachen, Canossa & Yannakakis (2009, ref. [8]) on Tomb Raider: Underworld — self-organizing maps over death statistics, time, and hint usage. The retrospective verdict on that branch is embedded in this paper's own related work: "A common aspect of these studies is their reliance on aggregate statistics, which removes the sequential structure of play traces" [PAGE 1]. Canossa et al. (2018, ref. [11]) is the correction — event subsequences in The Division, styles defined by subsequence frequency. Lin, Chiu & Wu (2021, ref. [12]) supply the metric-theoretic version: shared deep-encoded states plus response actions. Ingram et al. (2022, ref. [13]) supply the architecture this paper inherits — "embeddings from deep recurrent autoencoders to cluster play traces in Super Mario Bros" — but retain "handcrafted features such as the number of jumps in addition to action encodings" [PAGE 1]. Huang et al. (2021, ref. [14]) supply the tensor schema, which this paper modifies in three named ways [PAGE 2]. The 2025 contribution is precisely the removal of that last handcrafted residue.

**The retrocausal loop.** The paper's motivating critique — aggregate statistics destroy sequence — is the critique that *condemns the paper's own baseline*. The comparison is not learned-vs-sequence (the lineage's actual frontier) but learned-vs-aggregate, i.e., it wins against the 2009 paradigm while claiming to have advanced past 2022. Read retrocausally, the paper's delta over [13] is small and precise (drop the handcrafted jump counts, feed raw tensors), but its framing is large and generic ("removes the need for handcrafted features" [PAGE 1]).

**Multi-week traps.** (T1) *$k$-drift*: a follow-on study tuning $k$ for AMI will drift upward past 16, because homogeneity inflates mechanically with $k$ while completeness deflates — the pair is a confound, not a dial. (T2) *Map-family drift*: the A–K / L split is arbitrary within a single family, "12x12basesWorkers{i} where i ∈ {A..L}" [PAGE 2]; once more maps accumulate, the "L is easier" hypothesis collapses into ordinary sampling variance unless per-map SDs were captured from day one. (T3) *Label drift*: the moment one admits "agent name... is not a play style label" [PAGE 3], every Table I number becomes a proxy metric for a proxy label, and a re-labeling pass invalidates the entire comparison grid. (T4) *Augmentation drift*: mirroring is a symmetry assumption about map physics that the mayari pathing evidence contradicts; a later study that drops mirroring will find different clusters and attribute the difference to architecture.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Four nested belief states are in play, and each can be wrong about the level beneath it.

**Level 0 — the agent's objective.** Each of the 13 agents optimizes something (win, survive, explore, do nothing). It has no belief about being observed and no incentive to be legible. RandomAI and PassiveAI are, by construction, maximally illegible in style terms while maximally distinct in label terms.

**Level 1 — the labeler's belief.** The analyst believes agent name ≈ style. The paper explicitly denies this — "some agents may change their behaviour depending on the opponent, while some may be similar in play style" [PAGE 3] — yet the label is used as ground truth for all four metrics. This is a *belief held and disavowed simultaneously*, the classic recursive-theory-of-mind failure: the model of the agent's behavior is correct in prose and wrong in code.

**Level 2 — the encoder's belief.** The autoencoder has no belief about style; it minimizes O4. If $z$ is an invertible recoding of $F$ (O3 breakdown), the encoder "believes" the trace is its own best summary. Nothing in the objective says clusters should be interpretable.

**Level 3 — the analyst's belief about the visualization.** The paper anticipates and pre-empts this one correctly: "The 2D t-SNE representation can be deceiving: for example, one may think that there is one big cluster of the random variants and the NaiveMCTS traces, however, the k-means clustering separates the NaiveMCTS from the random variants quite successfully" [PAGE 3]. That is a textbook counter-trap, executed cleanly.

**Crisis habit regression.** The crisis habit for this research program is: when clustering looks messy, fall back to counting actions. That is precisely the 18-feature baseline — class frequencies plus two position sums [PAGE 2] — and it is the 2009 paradigm reappearing under stress. The regression signal to watch for in any follow-on work is the phrase "for interpretability we also report action frequencies"; once that appears, sequence has been quietly abandoned. The paper does not regress, but it keeps the aggregate baseline alive and, on A–K ARI at $k=16$, that baseline is within 0.005 of the joint model — the habit is one bad conference review away from returning.

**Counter-trap.** The mirror-image failure is over-reading the merged cluster. MixedBot and Tiamat merging looks like a clustering error and is in fact a *correct* detection: "MixedBot is an ensemble of agents, one of which is Tiamat" [PAGE 3]. Symmetrically, Droplet splitting between the WorkerRush and NaiveMCTS clusters is correct — Droplet is WorkerRush-plus-MCTS. The counter-trap is treating every off-diagonal cluster assignment as noise; here two of the three "anomalies" are signal. The third, the PassiveAI cluster, *is* noise — crashed traces: "these are play traces of agents that failed to run or crashed during the data collection process" [PAGE 3]. Distinguishing signal-anomaly from noise-anomaly requires reading the traces, not the metric. That is the paper's real methodological lesson and it is under-stated.

**The clock.** Two clocks bind the analysis. The *window clock*: 32 frames at stride 8 (O7); mayari's pathology is "later corrected outside of our chosen subsequence length" [PAGE 3], so the window is causally responsible for a false style attribution, and lengthening it dissolves the artifact. The *engagement clock*: the WorkerRush pre-emption ends games before style expression, so "they did not have the time to show their playstyle before being defeated" [PAGE 3]. In this domain, tempo is not a nuisance covariate — it is a *label-validity gate*. Any trace shorter than the expressive horizon should carry a missing label, not a true one.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three reinforcement-learning-with-verifiable-rewards counterfactuals, each with a mechanically checkable reward signal and a pre-registered prediction. Each is designed so that a *pass* confirms the paper and a *fail* vetoes a specific claim.

**RLVR-1 — Label-permutation null.**
*Intervention:* hold the trained encoder and the reported $z$ fixed; uniformly permute the 13 agent labels across the clustered points; recompute AMI and ARI over $N=1000$ permutations. *Verifiable reward:* $\mathrm{AMI}_{\text{perm}}$ must fall inside the permutation null band, $\lvert \mathrm{AMI}_{\text{perm}}\rvert < 0.02$ at 95% coverage. *Prediction:* **pass.** Reported map-L joint AMI of 0.715 at $k=13$ [PAGE 3] is far above any plausible null. If it fails — if permuted labels still yield AMI > 0.1 — the pipeline is leaking map index, seed, or POV identity into $z$, and *every* Table I number is vetoed as a style metric. Secondary prediction: the gap $\mathrm{AMI}_{\text{obs}}-\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{AMI}_{\text{perm}}]$ will be *largest* for the actions-only embedding at $k=16$ (0.677) and *smallest* for states-only (0.627), consistent with the authors' symmetry-breaking argument that states-only "uses a single feature (owner) to break the symmetry" [PAGE 3].

**RLVR-2 — POV-pair grouped holdout.**
*Intervention:* rebuild the train/test split with `GroupKFold` keyed on *game identity*, so that both POV traces of a game always land on the same side. Re-encode, re-cluster, recompute AMI on map L. *Verifiable reward:* report $\Delta = \mathrm{AMI}_{\text{grouped}} - \mathrm{AMI}_{\text{trace-split}}$. *Prediction:* **fail-with-magnitude.** Because the two POVs "swap the owner labels" [PAGE 2] and share the opponent trajectory entirely, grouped splitting will reduce AMI by a measurable margin. Prediction: $\Delta \in [-0.12, -0.03]$ on map L. If $\Delta > -0.03$, the encoder is genuinely POV-invariant and the pairing is harmless — the paper's numbers stand.
