Crisis habit regression posits that under acute pressure or novelty, behavior regresses to the most over-learned, dominant S–R habit in the individual’s hierarchy. In the Spearman/Tolman grammar, this means that under crisis the specific factor \(s_{i,r}\) for a recently overtrained response may dominate performance even if general factors \(g, c, \omega, o\) are higher in absolute allotment [PAGE 402]. The counter-trap is theorist projection: Tolman, in rendering Spearman’s normative principles as “mental events which … intervene between stimulus and responses” [PAGE 402], risks attributing to the organism a cognitive architecture (apprehension, eduction) that reflects the observer’s own theory of mind rather than the organism’s demonstrated contingency. The clock supplies the third term: heredity operates on a phylogenetic clock (generations), training on an ontogenetic clock (weeks to months), and immediate performance on a millisecond-to-second clock. Recursive theory of mind enters when the modeler models the individual as if the individual were modeling the environment through “eduction of relations and correlates”; if that recursive embedding is false—if the organism is not actually educing correlates but merely responding to proximal cues—the entire normative layer is a counter-trap projected by the theorist. Thus the crisis habit, the observer’s trap, and the clock interact: short-timescale stress collapses behavior onto low-level habits, invalidating any model that assumes the cognitive principles are online at all timescales.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Spearman’s normative chain begins with sentience as a “pre-condition” [PAGE 402]. Sentience requires that the physical stimulus exceed sensory transduction thresholds. If stimulus energy \(I_S\) falls below threshold \(\theta_{\text{sens}}\), then \(Se_i = 0\) and every downstream cognitive principle—apprehension, eduction of relations, eduction of correlates—is vetoed regardless of the individual’s allotments of \(g, c, \omega, o,\) or \(s\) [PAGE 402]. Biomechanical layer: the response \(R_1\) in S→R₁ [PAGE 402] presupposes intact effectors. Paralysis, effector fatigue, or neuromuscular failure sets \(\mathcal{P}_{i,r} = 0\) even when factor allotments are maximal. Weather/environmental layer: environmental noise degrades the stimulus–response channel; if \(\text{SNR}_{\text{env}} < \theta_{\text{env}}\), the reliable specification of S is compromised and the factor model’s input is undefined.

Composite veto predicate:
\[
\mathcal{V}_i = \mathbb{I}[I_S > \theta_{\text{sens}}] \times \mathbb{I}[\text{effector}_i = \text{intact}] \times \mathbb{I}[\text{SNR}_{\text{env}} > \theta_{\text{env}}].
\]
If \(\mathcal{V}_i = 0\), then \(\mathcal{P}_{i,r} = 0\) by physical necessity, and no psychometric factor can rescue performance. This yields a veto-proof boundary: the Spearman–Tolman model as stated on [PAGE 402] is valid only within the envelope where sensation is possible, effectors are functional, and environmental conditions permit stimulus detection. Outside that envelope, the “degree of success” is determined by physical exclusion, not by factor allotment.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Zero-factor counterfactual): Select participants whose psychometric allotments are empirically at the floor for \(g, c, \omega, o,\) and \(s_{i,r}\), with minimal training exposure. Present S→R₁. Veto condition: if observed \(\mathcal{P}_{i,r}\) exceeds chance by a pre-registered margin \(\delta > 0\). Prediction under the source model: because all requisite factors are near zero [PAGE 402], performance should remain at chance level. Verifiable via a standard psychometric battery plus a novel S→R task; a significant positive result falsifies the factor-allotment necessity claim.

Scenario 2 (Sentience ablation counterfactual): Pharmacologically or neurologically suppress the sentience precondition while leaving basic sensory transduction and motor output intact. Test performance on a task requiring “eduction of relations” [PAGE 402]. Veto condition: if \(\mathcal{P}_{i,\text{eduction}}\) remains at baseline despite verified sentience suppression. Prediction under the source model: because sentience is a precondition for the cognitive principles [PAGE 402], eduction performance should collapse. Verifiable via concurrent physiological monitoring of sentience correlates and a relational-learning task; persistence of eduction vetoes the normative hierarchy.

Scenario 3 (Training plasticity counterfactual): Conduct a randomized longitudinal intervention with a controlled training dose \(D = \int T_i\,dt\) exceeding a critical threshold \(T_{\text{crit}}\) over multiple weeks. Measure factor allotments pre- and post-intervention. Veto condition: if no statistically significant shift in any factor allotment occurs despite \(D > T_{\text{crit}}\). Prediction under the source model: because past training contributes to allotments [PAGE 402], sufficient training dose should shift allotments measurably. Verifiable via repeated factor-analytic measurement; a null result supports heredity-only determination and vetoes the joint heredity–training claim as stated.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
