\[
\mathbb{P}_F(B) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^{G \setminus F}} \mathbb{P}_G(B \times \mathbb{R}^{G \setminus F}) \, d\lambda,
\]

where \(\lambda\) is the Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb{R}^{G \setminus F}\). Then there exists a unique probability measure \(\mathbb{P}\) on the product σ-algebra such that \(\mathbb{P}_F\) is the pushforward of \(\mathbb{P}\) under the projection map.

This theorem is a cornerstone of modern probability theory and underpins the rigorous construction of Brownian motion, Gaussian processes, and Markov chains. Vitali-Hahn-Saks Convergence Theorem

Another critical operator is the Vitali-Hahn-Saks theorem, which concerns the pointwise limit of a sequence of signed measures. Let \((\Omega, \Sigma, \mu)\) be a measure space and suppose \(\{\nu_n\}\) is a sequence of signed measures on \(\Sigma\) that converge pointwise:

\[
\lim_{n \to \infty} \nu_n(A) = \nu(A) \quad (\forall A \in \Sigma),
\]

where \(\nu\) is a finite signed measure. If the \(\nu_n\) are absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu\), i.e., for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there exists \(\delta > 0\) such that \(|\mu(A)| < \delta\) implies \(|\nu_n(A)| < \varepsilon\) for all \(n\), then \(\nu\) is also absolutely continuous with respect to \(\mu\). Moreover, the Radon-Nikodym derivatives \(f_n = \frac{d\nu_n}{d\mu}\) converge in \(L^1(\mu)\) to \(f = \frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\).

This theorem is pivotal in proving the closedness of certain operator ranges in Banach spaces of measures and is essential for establishing the weak-* compactness of probability measures in Prokhorov’s theorem.

Haar Measure Uniqueness

For locally compact groups \(G\), the Haar measure \(\lambda\) is the unique (up to scaling) left-invariant regular Borel measure with \(\lambda(K) < \infty\) for every compact set \(K \subseteq G\). Its existence and uniqueness are established via the Riesz Representation Theorem applied to the positive linear functional on \(C_c(G)\) given by convolution with a fixed non-negative test function. Specifically, if \(\rho\) is a right-invariant measure and \(\phi \in C_c(G)\) is non-negative, then

\[
\Lambda(\phi) = \int_G \phi(x) \, d\rho(x)

defines a Radon integral, and there exists a unique Radon measure \(\mu\) such that \(\Lambda(\phi) = \int \phi \, d\mu\). The invariance of \(\rho\) forces \(\mu\) to be left-invariant, yielding the Haar measure.

The condition that \(\mu\) be finite on compact sets ensures that \(\mu\) is σ-finite, allowing the application of the Radon-Nikodym theorem to derive density functions for convolutions and character sums in harmonic analysis.
