<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(X\) be a set, \(\mathcal{S}\) a \(\sigma\)-algebra of subsets of \(X\), and \(\mu : \mathcal{S} \to [0, \infty]\) a countably additive measure. A simple function \(f: X \to \mathbb{R}\) is expressed as \(f = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i \chi_{E_i}\), where each \(\alpha_i \in \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\{E_i\}_{i=1}^{n}\) is a finite collection of disjoint sets in \(\mathcal{S}\).  
\[
\int_X f \, d\mu = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i \mu(E_i).
\tag{Halmos-1950}
\]
If \(\mu(E_i)<\infty\) for all \(i\), then \(f\) is integrable, and the integral is well-defined because \(\mu\) is \(\sigma\)-additive. For a measurable subset \(E \in \mathcal{S}\), the integral over \(E\) is defined by restriction:  
\[
\int_E f \, d\mu = \int_X (\chi_E f) \, d\mu.
\]
The characteristic function of a set \(F\) with finite measure yields \(\int_X \chi_F \, d\mu = \mu(F)\). This construction enables the extension of integration to non-negative measurable functions via monotone limits.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
