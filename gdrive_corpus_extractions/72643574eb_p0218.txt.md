Level 2 is the subject’s theory of the observer’s theory: blind people “talk in visual terms just like ourselves” [PAGE 208], which can be read as a Level-2 accommodation—using the sighted observer’s spatial vocabulary to be understood, regardless of whether the private format matches.

Crisis habit regression occurs at Level 1 when uncertainty is high: the sighted theorist regresses to the egocentric visual template, assuming that because space is given to them simultaneously and metrically, it must be so for the blind subject too. This is the default habit under information poverty. The counter-trap is Dunan’s strong reaction: because blind language and behavior differ, the private representation must be “absolutely naught in common” [PAGE 208]. This counter-trap is itself a habit—projecting radical alterity from linguistic difference. James’s task is to resist both regressions: deny total incompetence of touch without conceding total incommensurability of format.

The clock is the temporal-binding operator introduced by the imagery contrast on [PAGE 208]. Mental rotation “turn round mentally” implements U(Δθ) on the imaginal buffer; after rotation by Δθ, the front surface falls outside the attentional window and “vanishes.” Simultaneous six-surface imagery requires maintaining all six faces within Δt (seconds). If rotation time T_rot > Δt, the successive acts become mutually exclusive, exactly as James describes. In recursive theory of mind, the clock bounds recursion depth: I can model that you model that the blind subject thinks in time, but each level consumes working-memory refresh cycles. When the recursion depth exceeds approximately two levels, or when the temporal binding window collapses (aging, distraction, sensory deprivation), the model flattens: the theorist collapses Level 2 back into Level 0, treating the blind subject’s public spatial speech as direct evidence of private spatial format. Breakdown occurs precisely at this collapse, where the clock runs out before the recursion completes.

Physical layer veto: The receptor arrays of the two modalities do not support a common fine-grained metric. Foveal cone center-to-center spacing is roughly 2–3 µm, corresponding to ≈ 0.5–1 arcmin of visual angle; fingertip Merkel-disc and Meissner-corpuscle arrays yield active two-point thresholds on the order of 1–2 mm. If representational identity required an isometry between M_v and M_h, the spatial resolution sigma_h of touch would have to match sigma_v of vision after distance scaling; it does not (sigma_h >> sigma_v by roughly an order of magnitude at comparable object scales). Veto condition: sigma_h > k·sigma_v with k ≫ 1 falsifies strong cross-modal metric identity at the physical layer. Units: mm (haptic) versus arcmin converted to meters at viewing distance.

Biomechanical layer veto: Haptic exploration of a room is active and sequential. Scanning time T_scan = L / v_hand, with L [m] the path length and v_hand [m/s] typical hand velocity (≈ 0.2–0.5 m/s). For room-scale exploration, L is several meters, so T_scan ranges from seconds to tens of seconds, exceeding the attentional dwell Δt ≈ 2–3 s. Consequently the entire room cannot be bound into a single haptic gestalt; the percept is a temporal montage. Veto condition: T_scan > Δt invalidates any claim that haptic “space” is experienced as an all-at-once spatial array equivalent to a visual glance. The same clock applies to James’s “mentally turn round”: if the imagined rotation time exceeds working-memory refresh, the front surface is lost, producing the mutually exclusive acts described on [PAGE 208].

Weather layer: NULL. The scene on [PAGE 208] is an imagined indoor room; no meteorological variable (temperature, humidity, barometric pressure, precipitation) enters Platner’s quotation, Dunan’s testimony, or James’s analysis. The weather veto set is W_weather = ∅. Any invocation of weather effects on skin sensitivity or exploration speed would require evidence outside the supplied page and is excluded from this adjudication.

Adjudication: The physical and biomechanical vetoes jointly falsify Dunan’s weak isomorphism (common metric) and Platner’s total-incompetence claim only partially—they rule out fine-grained identity and simultaneity, but they do not by themselves establish whether a coarser, amodal topological structure is shared. James’s implicit middle position—that the formats differ while the object may be the same—is the only position not directly vetoed by the physical and biomechanical layers. The weather layer contributes no veto because it is absent from [PAGE 208].

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a condition, the theory it vetoes, the prediction, and the verification metric.

Veto Scenario 1 — Post-surgical cross-modal transfer (Molyneux modern). Condition: A congenitally blind adult undergoes sight restoration (e.g., cataract removal) and is immediately tested on haptically familiar geometric forms using vision only, with no visuo-motor training interval. Theory vetoed: strong cross-modal/amodal geometric identity (the assumption that haptic and visual form spaces share the same sufficient statistics). Prediction: Forced-choice shape matching accuracy at day 0–3 post-surgery is not significantly above chance (≈ 25% for a 4-alternative task; 95% CI includes chance), and rises above baseline only after weeks of correlated visuo-motor experience. Verification: percent correct, binomial test vs. chance, N ≥ 20, alpha = 0.05. Breakdown regime: invalid if the subject had any residual vision or adventitious sight; valid only for congenital, total, pre-operative blindness.

Veto Scenario 2 — Simultaneity probe in mental imagery. Condition: Sighted participants who report being able to “think of all six surfaces at once” are probed for the occluded rear surface after establishing an imagined heading, then compared with front-surface probes under identical encoding. Theory vetoed: the strong simultaneous-imagery hypothesis that all six surfaces are co-active in working memory (James’s first group on [PAGE 208]). Prediction: Response time for the rear surface exceeds that for the front surface by RT_rear − RT_front = k·Δθ with k > 0 ms/deg and a statistically significant positive slope; the simultaneous model (k = 0) is rejected. Verification: within-subjects reaction time [ms], linear mixed model, N ≥ 30, alpha = 0.01. Breakdown regime: invalid if participants adopt verbal label strategies rather than spatial imagery; process-tracing (eye tracking or verbal protocols) required to confirm imaginal mode.

Veto Scenario 3 — Temporal vs. spatial framing in blind distance judgment. Condition: Congenitally blind and sighted control participants estimate haptic distances under two instruction frames: spatial (“the object is farther”) versus temporal (“it takes longer to reach”). Theory vetoed: Dunan’s inference that shared visual spatial vocabulary implies shared private spatial representation (the “talk in visual terms just like ourselves” deception on [PAGE 208]). Prediction: For congenital blind participants, temporal framing reduces absolute distance estimation error by > 30% relative to spatial framing (error in meters), whereas sighted controls show no such temporal advantage or a significantly smaller one. Verification: mean absolute error [m], two-way ANOVA (group × frame), N ≥ 24 per group, alpha = 0.05. Breakdown regime: invalid if blind participants have been explicitly trained to translate temporal idioms into visual concepts; the effect must be spontaneous, not strategically instructed.

Each scenario produces a binary veto outcome: if the prediction is confirmed, the target theory is vetoed for the specified population and breakdown regime; if the null is observed, the theory survives this counterfactual test but remains subject to the page-boundary limitations of [PAGE 208].
