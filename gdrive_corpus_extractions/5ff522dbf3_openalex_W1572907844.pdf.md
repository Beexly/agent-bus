**Sampling and commensurability [PAGE 11, PAGE 26]**  
P-channel sample rate f_s = 15.374 Msamples/s; C/A sample rate = 1.5374 Msamples/s (exactly f_s/10). Fundamental P chip rate f_c = 10.23 MHz. The "commensurability ratio" is reduced to lowest terms:

$$
\frac{f_s}{f_c} = \frac{15\,374\,000}{10\,230\,000} = \frac{7687}{5115},
$$

so the placement of sample points relative to chip edges recurs after 7687 p-samples = 0.5 ms (source: "after 7687 samples (0.5 ms), the placement sequence of sample points relative to nearest chip edges begins to repeat"). The associated worst-case delay quantization is

$$
\Delta\tau_{\min} = \frac{1}{7687}\cdot T_c^{(P)} = \frac{1}{7687}\cdot\frac{1}{10.23\,\mathrm{MHz}} \approx 1.27\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{s},
$$

which equals $c\,\Delta\tau_{\min}\approx 3.8\,\mathrm{mm}\approx 0.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ for P — matching the source verbatim: "delay could not be measured any more accurately than 1/7687 chip (0.4 cm for P)." Units: seconds (delay), meters (range-equivalent).

**Doppler smearing of the quantization floor [PAGE 26–27]**  
The source states that with RF Doppler rate $\dot f_D = 0.7\,\mathrm{Hz/s}$, the quantization error of 1/7687 chip "will be traversed in only 0.24 s." Using the carrier cycles-per-chip identity (L1: 1575.42 MHz ÷ 1.023 MHz = 1540 cycles/chip), the traversal time is

$$
T_{\text{traverse}} = \frac{1/7687\ \mathrm{chip}}{\dot f_D / 1540\ \mathrm{Hz/chip}} \approx \frac{1.30\times10^{-4}}{4.55\times10^{-4}}\ \mathrm{s}\approx 0.29\,\mathrm{s},
$$

close to the source's 0.24 s given rounding of the Doppler/chip ratio. This is a **Doppler-averaging operator** that breaks the quantization floor by spreading phase relative to chip edges — the source notes it "will be made much smaller by the smearing caused by changing Doppler."

**Three-level phasor and quadrature-loss budget [PAGE 11, PAGE 12]**  
Three-level (±1, 0) phasor quantization gives 4% voltage-SNR loss; two-level baseband sampling gives 20% voltage-SNR loss. Total loss is multiplicative in voltage:

$$
\eta_{\text{total}} = (1-0.04)(1-0.20) = 0.768 \;\Rightarrow\; 23.2\% \text{ voltage-SNR loss}.
$$

With 24-bit phase/rate registers, the maximum frequency mismatch from rate rounding is "about 30 nanocycles/sample." The resulting amplitude loss over an 18-ms interval is bounded by

\frac{\Delta A}{A}\lesssim \tfrac12 \left(2\pi \cdot 30\times 10^{-9}\cdot N_{\text{samp}}^{18\,\mathrm{ms}}\right)^2,

where $N_{\text{samp}}^{18\,\mathrm{ms}} = 1.5374\times10^{6}\cdot 0.018 \approx 27\,673$ for C/A. Source bound: 0.0001% for C/A and 0.01% for P. <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The Rogue receiver descends from a specific lineage of JPL radio science and radio interferometry practice. The acknowledgments [PAGE 8] name L. E. Young, G. H. Purcell, T. K. Meehan, and T. P. Yunck as readers, and explicitly state: "The three-level approach for counterrotation sinusoids has been adopted from correlator designs used in radio interferometry." This is not cosmetic — it puts the receiver in a direct line from the VLBI correlator tradition (Mark I/II/III heritage at Haystack and JPL's Goldstone correlators) where ±1/0 three-level multiplication was standard for packing correlation into cheap gate arrays. The chip advancer's use of integer overflow to trigger a code-generator advance was contributed by Meehan and Srinivasan, per the same paragraph — this is the technique that decouples the sample rate from the chip rate and thereby reduces commensurability error [PAGE 45].

Retrocausally, this design choice is what made the later Rogue-family evolution possible. The Rogue was built for geodetic and Earth-dynamics work: Section 2.1 [PAGE 10] specifies "point positioning solutions should be carried out by the receiver to measure receiver location and velocity and to synchronize the receiver clock in offset and rate with GPS time" with 0.1–3 μs clock accuracy. That is a geodetic-GPS requirement, not a navigation requirement — the receiver is a scientific instrument masquerading as a navigation receiver. The multipath and antenna errors are explicitly excluded from the accuracy budget [PAGE 10], which is the exact refinement that later geodesists (Dong, Bock, and the GIPSY/OASIS lineage) exploited.

The "carrier-aided averaging" technique [PAGE 40–41] has a genealogy that predates GPS: the technique of using carrier phase to smooth pseudorange is present in TRANSIT/NNSS Doppler integration work, but its formalization here — reversing the sign of the ionospheric term so the synthetic delay has the correct group-delay sign for all components — is a specific 1988 construction that propagates forward into the "phase-connected pseudorange" algorithms of precise point positioning. The 1.5× noise-reduction factor relative to quadratic fit is the document's own quantitative claim, and it became a design target for subsequent geodetic GPS software.

The multi-week trap: any analyst who mistakes [PAGE 10]'s "1 cm or less for P group delays" as an observed performance would be wrong. Section 7.13 [PAGE 48] restates it as theory-plus-to-date-tests, contingent on 5-min averaging and the stated system parameters. A second trap: the "42" OCR artifacts, if taken literally, would imply non-quadrature P channels are 42× noisier in voltage — which would render the P lock reliability claim [PAGE 12: "the P channels should not lose lock if the C/A channel maintains lock"] impossible, since the entire P-lock argument rests on C/A-driven fast feedback tolerating a modestly noisier P front end. The internal consistency check forces the reading √2.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/biomechanical/weather vetoes operate at multiple layers and the audit must prove which layer is load-bearing for each claim.

**Front-end thermal veto** [PAGE 17]: "GaAs FET with a noise temperature of about 80 K" and "125-K system temperature" [PAGE 10]. The 125 K is the antenna-plus-receiver aggregate; at L-band with a good sky, 80 K LNA plus ~30–40 K spillover/antenna is physically coherent. No layer above (digital processor, tracking) can *improve* on this — the 5-min integration SNR is bounded by the 125 K. This is a veto on any claim of sub-centimeter P delay at low elevation without averaging.

**Bandwidth veto** [PAGE 17]: BPF 1140–1640 MHz, LPF 720 kHz (C/A) and 7.2 MHz (P). The 7.2 MHz P-channel bandwidth is wider than the 10.23 MHz chip main lobe — deliberately so, to avoid P-code edge distortion. Any claim of P delay precision much better than the inverse bandwidth (~1/7.2 MHz ≈ 139 ns) is vetoed unless averaging over many chips (which the source does via 18-ms and 1-s sums).

**Sample-rate hardware veto** [PAGE 11]: "hardware restrictions at the time limited the P sample rate to less than 16 Msamples/s, even though a rate close to 20 Msamples/s would have been preferable." This is a physical veto — the chip technology couldn't support 20 Msps. Every downstream precision claim inherits this: the 1/7687-chip quantization floor exists *because* the rate is 15.374 rather than ~20 Msps at a more incommensurate ratio.

**Baseband-offset veto** [PAGE 17]: "the baseband carrier frequency should be about 5 kHz or greater so that carrier down-conversion in the baseband processor will work with high accuracy." The chosen offsets (12.32 kHz L1, 9.6 kHz L2) put the sum-note error below a millicycle over 18 ms and push harmonics of the three-level phasor to average out. Weather/atmosphere veto: the document explicitly excludes multipath and antenna errors from the intersatellite budget [PAGE 10], so ionospheric and tropospheric variability are handled *post-hoc* — the carrier-aided averaging [PAGE 40–41] is the layer that lets the receiver survive ionospheric delay-rate changes without losing P delay lock, because synthetic group delays track phase-delay variations with "phase-delay accuracy."

**Biomechanical/oscillator veto**: none in the strict sense, but the 5-MHz reference [PAGE 17] sets phase-noise floor; the source implicitly treats oscillator instability as one of the "all errors" that must keep feedback phase accurate to better than 0.25 cycle for the two-quadrant arctangent to work [PAGE 32]. This is the tightest cross-layer constraint: an unstable reference vetoes the arctangent extractor's cycle-ambiguity assumption.
