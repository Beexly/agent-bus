<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 348] traces back to Thorndike's law of effect (1898, 1911), in which responses followed by satisfaction are "stamped in" and those followed by annoyance are "stamped out," with strength assumed to grow through frequency and contiguity. Hull's 1943 habit-strength formulation (\(sEr \propto\) number of reinforced trials) formalized this same frequency logic. Tolman's 1932 purposive behaviorism, exemplified by this page, breaks the chain by arguing that behavior is guided by expectancies and cognitive maps rather than by blind S-R stamping. The direct scientific descendants include Tolman & Honzik's (1930) latent-learning experiments, in which unrewarded rats built cognitive maps that were only expressed when reward was introduced; O'Keefe & Nadel's (1978) cognitive-map and place-cell neurophysiology; and Rescorla & Wagner's (1972) expectancy-violation model, \(\Delta V = \alpha\beta(\lambda - \sum V)\), in which learning depends on the discrepancy between expected and obtained outcome rather than on simple repetition. Modern temporal-difference reinforcement learning, \(\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)\), inherits Tolman's core insight: the update signal is a prediction error about the goal relation, not a frequency count.

Multi-week traps: Single-session frequency null results on [PAGE 348] are often mis-extrapolated to multi-week habit formation. A rat may show no frequency effect in a one-session reversal (case c) yet develop a stubborn motor habit after weeks of overtraining; the timescale of habit crystallization differs from the timescale of expectancy-based reversal learning. Another trap is assuming that asymptotic performance reveals the learning mechanism; Tolman's latent-learning tradition shows that learning can be present behaviorally latent for days or weeks and then expressed abruptly when the contingency is made salient. Finally, treating frequency and recency as interchangeable ignores their distinct temporal signatures: recency decays exponentially with \(\lambda\), while frequency accumulates linearly, so multi-week interventions that manipulate one but not the other will produce dissociable patterns that the crude law of effect cannot predict.
