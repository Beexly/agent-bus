**1. Extinction Decay Operator**  
From "[PAGE 274]: 'the reaction tendency diminishes as a negative growth function of the number of closely successive unreinforced evocations.'" Define decay rate \(\lambda(t)\) with units trials\(^{-1}\). The habit strength evolution during extinction is governed by:
\[
\frac{dD}{dt} = -\lambda(t) D, \quad \lambda(t) = \lambda_0 e^{-\alpha N(t)},
\]
where \(N(t)\) is the cumulative count of unreinforced evocations up to time \(t\), and \(\alpha > 0\) controls acceleration of forgetting. Solution: \(D(t) = D_0 \exp\left(-\int_0^t \lambda(\tau) d\tau\right)\).  
*Assumptions:* Markovian decay; no interference from other habits.  
*Breakdown Regime:* When \(N(t)\) becomes large, \(\lambda(t) \to 0\), leading to incomplete extinction (asymptote \(D_\infty > 0\)). Empirical violation occurs if spaced retrieval induces reacquisition (\(dD/dt > 0\)).

**2. Generalization Gradient Interaction**  
From "[PAGE 274]: 'if the incremental factor (F) in the two cases were the same...'" Let \(K(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{s}') = F \exp\left(-\|\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{s}'\|^2 / 2\sigma^2\right)\) be a Gaussian kernel representing primary stimulus generalization. The effective habit field after extinction at \(\mathbf{s}_e\) is:
\[
D_{\text{eff}}(\mathbf{s}) = D_{\text{prim}}(\mathbf{s}) - K(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{s}_e) \cdot \Delta D,
\]
where \(\Delta D\) is the magnitude of extinction effect. For \(\mathbf{s}_e = \mathbf{s}_{\text{reinforce}}\), \(D_{\text{eff}}(\mathbf{s}) \equiv 0\) if \(F_{\text{ext}} = F_{\text{prim}}\).  
*Assumptions:* Linear superposition; isotropic generalization.  
*Singularity:* At \(\mathbf{s} = \mathbf{s}_e\), \(D_{\text{eff}}\) exhibits a cusp if \(F_{\text{ext}} \neq F_{\text{prim}}\), leading to discontinuous derivatives.

**3. Reaction Generalization Diffusion**  
From "[PAGE 274]: 'extinction effects manifest reaction generalization.'" Model response space as a graph with adjacency matrix \(A_{rr'}\) encoding similarity. Extinction spreads via:
\[
\frac{\partial D_r}{\partial t} = \gamma \sum_{r'} A_{rr'} (D_{r'} - D_r),
\]
with \(\gamma\) having units time\(^{-1}\). In spectral domain, \(\mathbf{D}(t) = e^{-\gamma L t} \mathbf{D}(0)\), where \(L\) is the graph Laplacian.  
*Assumptions:* Undirected, weighted graph; homogeneous coupling.  
*Breakdown:* If \(A\) has negative eigenvalues (non-Euclidean geometry), oscillatory solutions may arise, violating monotonic decay.

**4. Spontaneous Recovery Dynamics**  
From "[PAGE 274]: 'takes place approximately as a positive growth function of time elapsing since the termination of the extinction process.'" Define recovery function \(S(t)\) with maximum \(S_{\max}\). A phenomenological fit is:
\[
S(t) = S_{\max} \left(1 - e^{-(t/\tau)^\nu}\right), \quad \nu \in (0,1],
\]
where \(\tau\) is a characteristic time scale (~3 hours empirically).  
*Assumptions:* Static conditioned stimulus post-extinction; no new learning.  
*Sensitivity:* For \(\nu < 1\), initial recovery is sublinear, implying distributed memory traces with varying accessibility.

**5. Steepened Discrimination Gradient**  
When extinction occurs at \(\mathbf{s}_e \neq \mathbf{s}_{\text{reinforce}}\), the effective gradient becomes:
\[
\nabla D_{\text{eff}}(\mathbf{s}) = \nabla D_{\text{prim}}(\mathbf{s}) - \nabla K(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{s}_e) \cdot \Delta D.
\]
Between \(\mathbf{s}_e\) and \(\mathbf{s}_{\text{reinforce}}\), \(\nabla D_{\text{eff}}\) magnitude increases, enhancing discriminability. Quantitatively, Fisher information \(I(\mathbf{s}) \propto |\nabla D|^2 / \text{Var}(\text{noise})\) rises locally.
