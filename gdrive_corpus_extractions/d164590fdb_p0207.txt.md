A physical veto is issued when a shared boundary condition—say, thermal expansion of the sensor mount—induces a deterministic constraint $h(f,g)=0$ with $h:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ smooth and $\nabla h\neq 0$. The joint law is then supported on a 1-dimensional submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^2$, hence $(f,g)_*\mu\perp\mu_f\otimes\mu_g$ (the product measure assigns zero mass to the curve). Veto condition: the Radon–Nikodym derivative $d(f,g)_*\mu/d(\mu_f\otimes\mu_g)$ does not exist as an $L^1$ density because the measures are mutually singular.

Biomechanical layer. Let $f_1,f_2:X\to\mathbb{R}$ be two joint angles in radians. The musculoskeletal system imposes holonomic constraints $q_1=f_1(\omega)$, $q_2=f_2(\omega)$ lying on a configuration manifold $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ of dimension $<2$. The induced probability measure on $\mathcal{M}$ is carried by the Hausdorff measure $\mathcal{H}^1$ restricted to $\mathcal{M}$, not by 2-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The product measure $\mu_{f_1}\otimes\mu_{f_2}$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\lambda^2$ if the marginals have densities, so it is mutually singular with the constraint-supported law. Breakdown regime: at kinematic singularities where the Jacobian of the constraint map drops rank, the tangent space of $\mathcal{M}$ collapses and any local product-measure approximation fails with unbounded error in the total-variation norm.

Weather layer. Let $T:X\to\mathbb{R}$ (temperature, K) and $P:X\to\mathbb{R}$ (pressure, Pa). Atmospheric dynamics couple $T$ and $P$ through the ideal-gas law and hydrostatic balance, generating a joint law on $\mathbb{R}^2$ whose support excludes open rectangles where the equation of state is violated. Independence would imply $\mu_{T,P}(A\times B)=\mu_T(A)\mu_P(B)$ for all Borel $A,B$; the physical veto triggers when the conditional law $\mu_{P|T=t}$ varies with $t$ in total variation distance $\|\mu_{P|T=t}-\mu_P\|_{\text{TV}}>\delta$ for a fixed $\delta>0$. Because SI units (K, Pa) are preserved under the measurable maps, the singularity is geometric, not a unit-conversion artifact.

Cross-layer adjudication rule: A proposed product-measure or independence assumption is admissible only if the pushforward $T_*\mu$ is equivalent to $\otimes_i\mu_{f_i}$ (mutually absolutely continuous) with a Radon–Nikodym derivative bounded above and below on the relevant support. If mutual singularity holds on any layer, the assumption is vetoed outright; no recalibration of marginals can repair a support mismatch.

RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards) is instantiated here as a measurable reward function $r:(X,S,\mu)\to\mathbb{R}$ for which the audit event $\{r>\rho\}\in S$ has a verifiable probability $\mu(r>\rho)$. Three counterfactual veto scenarios:

Scenario 1 — Common-drift sensor veto. Two observation channels $f_1,f_2:X\to\mathbb{R}$ are assumed independent inputs to the reward model. Counterfactual: if the channels shared an independent time base, the joint law would satisfy $(f_1,f_2)_*\mu=\mu_{f_1}\otimes\mu_{f_2}$. In reality a common clock drift couples them, so the true law is supported on a graph $\{(u,v):v=u+\epsilon(\omega)\}$. Verification: compute the empirical mutual information $\hat I(f_1;f_2)$ from a hold-out set; if $\hat I>\epsilon_{\text{MI}}$, trigger veto. Prediction: the verifiable reward $\mathbb{E}_\mu[r]$ will be overestimated by the independent model by an amount bounded below by the total-variation distance between the true joint law and the product approximation, and the error diverges as the drift variance grows.

Scenario 2 — Biomechanical constraint veto. A policy issues torque commands $\tau_1,\tau_2\in\mathbb{R}$ (Newton-meters) assumed independent. Counterfactual: if the actuator model were truly decoupled, the action-space measure would factorize on $\mathbb{R}^2$. Actual shared muscle groups impose a constraint manifold $\mathcal{C}=\{(\tau_1,\tau_2):a\tau_1+b\tau_2=c\}$. Verification: test whether sampled actions fall off $\mathcal{C}$ with frequency exceeding $\eta$; if so, trigger veto. Prediction: the policy-gradient variance explodes proportionally to the condition number of the constraint Jacobian, and the verifiable reward achieved on-policy falls below the off-policy independent estimate by a gap equal to the probability mass of the constraint-violating region under the product measure.

Scenario 3 — Seasonal weather-mode veto. A control feature $f:X\to\mathbb{R}$ is assumed independent of a weather state $W:X\to\mathbb{R}$ (K). Counterfactual: if $W$ were independent of $f$, the conditional reward law $\mu(r\in\cdot\,|\,f)$ would not vary with $W$. In reality a seasonal cycle couples $f$ and $W$ through a common driver, so the disintegration $\mu=\int\mu(\cdot\,|\,W=w)\,d\mu_W(w)$ yields conditional laws varying in total variation. Verification: estimate $\sup_{w,w'}\|\mu(r\in\cdot\,|\,W=w)-\mu(r\in\cdot\,|\,W=w')\|_{\text{TV}}$; if above $\delta_{\text{TV}}$, trigger veto. Prediction: out-of-distribution degradation occurs precisely in the extremal quantiles of $W$, where the conditional reward mean shifts by more than the independent-model confidence interval, causing systematic miscalibration of the verifiable reward target.
