**Veto proof, stated as necessary conditions.** A cross-layer claim survives only if all four hold: **(C1) construct alignment** — the same construct is measured at both layers; **(C2) temporal alignment** — same trial or same analysis window; **(C3) reported units** — the source states the measurement units; **(C4) non-circularity** — the upper-layer measure is not a rescaling of the lower-layer outcome.

*Physical layer.* The editorial reports **no force, no torque, no velocity, no acceleration, no EMG, no ground-reaction force, and no metabolic datum**. It reports only behavioral outcomes (reaction time, accuracy, test duration, saccade error in degrees, fixation counts). C1 and C3 fail outright. **Veto: no physical-layer claim can be adjudicated from this source.**

*Biomechanical layer.* Biomechanical tasks appear — drop jump (Çiftçi & Yilmaz [PAGE 2]), concurrent balance (Amara et al. [PAGE 2]), three-ball juggling cascade (Geller et al. [PAGE 2]), climbing handholds (Luis del-Campo et al. [PAGE 2]), ergometer rowing and Taekwondo (Monz et al. [PAGE 1]), goal-kicking (Müller et al. [PAGE 2]). But for every one of these, the reported dependent variable is behavioral or perceptual, never kinematic or kinetic. C1 fails: the "balance" condition is never quantified as a postural-sway magnitude (e.g., centre-of-pressure path length in mm), and the "drop jump" outcome is described only as "physical performance" [PAGE 2]. **Veto: biomechanical moderation is unadjudicable; a mechanical account of Amara et al.'s facilitation [PAGE 2] cannot be confirmed or excluded here.**

*Weather / environmental layer.* **The source contains zero meteorological, thermal, humidity, altitude, or surface-condition variables.** Not one sentence on [PAGE 1] or [PAGE 2] references an environmental parameter. C1–C3 all fail vacuously. **Veto: any weather-modulated inference is fabricated. This is a hard refusal, not a soft caveat.**

*What the source does license.* A purely **descriptive, cross-sectional, behavioral** claim set: that interference is the modal dual-task outcome [PAGE 1]; that it has at least two documented exceptions [PAGE 2]; that expertise moderates it non-monotonically across samples and skill classes [PAGE 2]; and that variability measures (saccade endpoint SD) and gaze-distribution measures discriminate skill groups [PAGE 2]. Anything stronger — mechanism, causation, multi-week forecast, cross-layer moderation — is vetoed by C1–C4.

*Corollary veto on event-level inference.* The editorial contains no fixture, no date-stamped competitive outcome, no team-level result, and no individual performance record. It therefore cannot support any prediction about a specific contest, and no such inference is offered.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario specifies a **machine-verifiable reward signal**, a **directional prediction**, and a **veto condition** that would falsify the account. All three are within-subject or within-sample designs and require no data the source already contains.

**Veto 1 — Load monotonicity of dual-task cost.** *Claim under test:* interference is resource-competition-driven (D2), which implies monotonicity in cognitive demand. *Design:* within-participant, three load levels (1-back, 2-back, 3-back) crossed with a fixed motor task, mirroring Knöbel & Lautenbach's 3-back-with-shooting design [PAGE 1]. *Verifiable reward:* the sign and magnitude of the paired difference \(\Delta = \mathrm{DTC}^{\log}_{X}(3\text{-back})-\mathrm{DTC}^{\log}_{X}(1\text{-back})\), computed per participant from D1's log form. *Prediction:* \(\Delta>0\) with a 95% CI excluding zero, and monotone ordering across the three levels. *Veto condition:* if the CI for \(\Delta\) excludes the positive direction (flat or reversed), the single-pool capacity account is falsified for that task pair, and the Amara-style facilitation [PAGE 2] must be promoted from anomaly to primary phenomenon.

**Veto 2 — Stroboscopic occlusion-duty threshold.** *Claim under test:* interrupting the perception-action cycle degrades a closed-skill accuracy task (Müller et al.'s manipulation [PAGE 2]) — but only below a critical duty \(\delta^{*}\) (D11). *Design:* within-subject sweep of \(\delta\in\{0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.9\}\) on a goal-kicking task with accuracy as the outcome. *Verifiable reward:* the estimated change-point \(\hat\delta^{*}\) from a segmented regression of accuracy on \(\delta\), plus the sign of the slope below it. *Prediction:* accuracy is flat for \(\delta>\hat\delta^{*}\) and declines monotonically for \(\delta<\hat\delta^{*}\), with \(\hat\delta^{*}\) estimated in the 0.3–0.5 band. *Veto condition:* if accuracy is flat across the entire \(\delta\in[0.1,0.9]\) range (slope CI spanning zero everywhere), the coupling-interruption hypothesis is rejected outright and Müller et al.'s null becomes a genuine refutation rather than a boundary condition.

**Veto 3 — Range restriction as the explanation of the Brinkbäumer null.** *Claim under test:* the reported absence of a DTC–performance-level relationship "For athletes from team sports and ice hockey" [PAGE 2] is an artefact of elite range restriction, not a true null. *Design:* recompute \(r_{obs}(\mathrm{DTC},L)\) in the 322-athlete elite sample [PAGE 2], estimate \(s_L\) and the unrestricted \(S_L\) from a comparable sub-elite cohort, and apply the Case-II correction (D3). *Verifiable reward:* the corrected value \(r_c\) and the inequality \(r_c>r_{obs}\). *Prediction:* \(r_{obs}\approx0\) but \(r_c\) materially negative (higher performers show smaller costs), with the correction factor \(\left[1-s_L^{2}/S_L^{2}\right]^{-1/2}\ge1.5\). *Veto condition:* if the correction factor is near unity (near-degenerate restriction, \(s_L/S_L\to0\) making \(r_c\) undefined) **or** if \(r_c\approx r_{obs}\approx0\), the restriction hypothesis is dead and the automatization account of expertise must be abandoned for elite team-sport samples — leaving closed-vs-open skill class [PAGE 2] as the only surviving moderator.

**Cross-veto consistency note.** All three vetoes are falsifiable with quantities the primary studies could already have collected. None requires a fabricated index, none requires cross-study pooling of incommensurable scales, and none presupposes any mechanism the source does not at least name. That is the standard the invariants demand: a veto must cost something to state, and must be able to lose.
