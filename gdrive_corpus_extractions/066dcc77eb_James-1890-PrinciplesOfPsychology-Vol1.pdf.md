Under a GSE/GSI audit, James’s text cannot be treated as a closed axiomatic system. He states explicitly that “The reader will in vain seek for any closed system in the book. It is mainly a mass of descriptive details, running out into queries which only a metaphysics alive to the weight of her task can hope successfully to deal with” [PAGE 13 of 716]. The correct multi-hop derivation is therefore conditional: extract operators as mechanized descriptions, state their units and breakdown regimes, and then veto any later inference that exceeds the source’s evidence.

The first hop is reaction-time. James decomposes the interval from stimulus to movement into receptor, afferent, central, efferent, and muscle stages: “It is evident that some time is lost in each of the following stages” [PAGE 106 of 716]. From this decomposition it follows immediately that a simple reaction-time is not a direct clock on central thought. James makes the veto explicit: “These reaction-time experiments are then in no sense measurements of the swiftness of thought” [PAGE 112 of 716]. A gray-zone error arises when later interpreters collapse \(t_{\mathrm{central}}\) into the whole observed interval and infer mental speed from a reflex-like response. The same error recurs when discrimination or choice is added: Wundt’s discrimination-time and Cattell’s word/letter recognition latencies show that central processing can be isolated only by subtraction and task control, not by naming simple reaction-time “thought speed” [PAGE 544 of 716].

The second hop is summation of stimuli. James’s law is that a stimulus “which would be inadequate by itself to excite a nerve-centre to effective discharge may, by acting with one or more other stimuli (equally ineffectual by themselves alone) bring the discharge about” [PAGE 100 of 716]. This is a threshold-integration operator, not a hidden mental arithmetic. Its breakdown is at the submaximal boundary: if one assumes that only the final explosive discharge has a conscious correlate, one loses James’s own point that submaximal tensions may contribute to the “fringe” of consciousness [PAGE 100 of 716].

The third hop is habit. James says: “So nothing is easier than to imagine how, when a current once has traversed a path, it should traverse it more readily still a second time. But what made it ever traverse it the first time?” [PAGE 127 of 716]. This is the key singularity. Habit can explain lowered resistance after a first traversal, but it cannot explain the first traversal without an additional chance, reflex, instinct, or novel-path condition. Any GSI model that uses habit as a complete causal closure fails at \(t=0\). Habit also creates a long-horizon trap: James calls it “the enormous fly-wheel of society, its most precious conservative agent” [PAGE 139 of 716], and he warns that “Nothing we ever do is, in strict scientific literalness, wiped out” [PAGE 145 of 716]. The gray-zone audit must therefore distinguish a path that is merely repeated from a path that has become structurally consolidated.

The fourth hop is attention as preperception. James writes: “The image in the mind is the attention; the preperception, as Mr. Lewes calls it, is half of the perception of the looked-for thing” [PAGE 457 of 716]. This is a predictive-prior operator, but it is bounded by conflict: “Effort is felt only where there is a conflict of interests in the mind” [PAGE 469 of 716]. The breakdown regime is overconfident prior capture: attention can sharpen a weak signal, but it can also make an ambiguous figure or an awaited sound appear before its physical date. James reports that “it happens most frequently that the sound appears earlier than its real date—far less often coincident with it, or later” [PAGE 430 of 716]. Thus the attention operator cannot be treated as a neutral amplifier.

The fifth hop is the self and its splits. James’s hysteric and hypnotic cases force a veto on naive testimony: “we must never take a person’s testimony, however sincere, that he has felt nothing, as proof positive that no feeling has been there” [PAGE 229 of 716]. The gray-zone issue is that the primary consciousness may be anesthetic while a secondary consciousness reports through automatic writing or hand movements [PAGE 223 of 716]. This is not permission to multiply souls; it is a demand for convergent external probes. The psychologist’s fallacy is the corresponding error: “The great snare of the psychologist is the confusion of his own standpoint with that of the mental fact about which he is making his report” [PAGE 214 of 716]. Therefore the admissible source operators are conditional mechanisms with explicit physical and introspective vetoes, not a metaphysical system.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
1. Reaction-time decomposition. James’s five-stage account [PAGE 106 of 716] can be written formally as
\[
T_{\mathrm{RT}}=t_{\mathrm{rec}}+t_{\mathrm{aff}}+t_{\mathrm{cen}}+t_{\mathrm{eff}}+t_{\mathrm{mus}},
\]
with all terms in seconds. Assumptions: one known signal, one known movement, and stable attention. The source says “The time usually elapsing between stimulus and movement lies between one and three tenths of a second” [PAGE 106 of 716]. Breakdown: if discrimination, association, or choice is introduced, add a separate \(t_{\mathrm{disc}}\) term; James denies that simple reaction-time measures thought speed [PAGE 112 of 716]. Discrimination-time examples include “0.119 and 0.116” s for letter discrimination and “0.118 sec. to 0.158 sec.” for words [PAGE 544 of 716].

2. Summation of stimuli. Formal reconstruction:
\[
S_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)=\sum_i \int_0^t w_i(\tau)\,s_i(t-\tau)\,d\tau,
\qquad \text{discharge if } S_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\ge \Theta .
\]
Here \(s_i\) is stimulus intensity in arbitrary sensorimotor units, \(w_i\) is a dimensionless weighting, and \(\Theta\) is the discharge threshold in the same units as \(S_{\mathrm{eff}}\). Assumption: separate submaximal stimuli can sum to a discharge. James’s statement is exact: “a stimulus which would be inadequate by itself to excite a nerve-centre to effective discharge may, by acting with one or more other stimuli (equally ineffectual by themselves alone) bring the discharge about” [PAGE 100 of 716]. Breakdown: inhibitory inputs, refractory periods, or independent parallel channels invalidate the simple additive sum; also, the claim that submaximal tensions contribute to consciousness is not a quantitative identity.

3. Habit as path conductance. Formal reconstruction:
\[
g_{ab}(t+1)=g_{ab}(t)+\eta\,r_{ab}(t)\,[g_{\max}-g_{ab}(t)]-\lambda g_{ab}(t),
\]
where \(g_{ab}\in[0,g_{\max}]\) is dimensionless path permeability, \(r_{ab}(t)\) is the traversal event indicator, \(\eta\) is a per-traversal learning rate, and \(\lambda\) is decay. Assumption: repeated traversal lowers resistance. James states the operator as “when a current once has traversed a path, it should traverse it more readily still a second time” [PAGE 127 of 716]. Breakdown/singularity: the first traversal is not generated by \(g_{ab}\) alone; James immediately asks, “But what made it ever traverse it the first time?” [PAGE 127 of 716]. Saturation gives habit lock-in, consistent with his warning that “the character has set like plaster” by thirty [PAGE 139 of 716].

4. Attention as preperception. Formal reconstruction:
\[
P(x\mid s)\propto P(s\mid x)P(x),
\]
with probabilities dimensionless. \(P(x)\) is the anticipatory image or preperception. James says, “The image in the mind is the attention; the preperception ... is half of the perception of the looked-for thing” [PAGE 457 of 716]. Assumption: past association supplies a prior for the expected signal. Breakdown: an excessively strong prior produces hallucination-like capture; ambiguous figures and weak signals require the preperception, but conflict produces effort and error [PAGE 469 of 716]. The timing illusion in which a sound “appears earlier than its real date” is a direct breakdown of neutral timing [PAGE 430 of 716].

5. Weber-Fechner formalization. The just-noticeable-difference relation is
\[
\frac{\Delta I}{I}=k,
\]
with \(I\) in physical intensity units and \(k\) dimensionless. Fechner’s integration gives
\[
S=k\ln\!\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right),
\]
where \(S\) is in jnd or logarithmic units, not SI. The source reports limited validity: Merkel’s experiments show “between 200 and 2000 grams a constant fractional increase,” while “Above and below these limits the discriminative power grew less” [PAGE 562 of 716]. Breakdown: James rejects exact universality: “Fechner’s assumption that all equally perceptible additions are equally great additions is entirely arbitrary” [PAGE 565 of 716].

6. Self-esteem. James gives the source-native relation: self-feeling “is determined by the ratio of our actualities to our supposed potentialities; a fraction of which our pretensions are the denominator and the numerator our success” [PAGE 328 of 716]. Formally,
\[
E_{\mathrm{self}}=\frac{S_{\mathrm{success}}}{P_{\mathrm{pretension}}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{pretension}}>0.
\]
It is dimensionless. Singularity at \(P_{\mathrm{pretension}}=0\): “with no attempt there can be no failure; with no failure no humiliation” [PAGE 328 of 716]. Breakdown: the ratio is a descriptive self-feeling model, not a universal psychometric law; it fails when the “pure Ego” is treated as an object rather than as the vehicle of estimation.

7. Association as conditional probability and information. Formal reconstruction from James’s association problem [PAGE 568 of 716]:
\[
P(B\mid A)=\frac{P(A,B)}{P(A)},\qquad
I(A;B)=\sum_{a,b}P(a,b)\log_2\frac{P(a,b)}{P(a)P(b)}.
\]
Probabilities are dimensionless; mutual information is in bits. Assumption: co-occurrence and contiguity shape recall. Breakdown: James rejects atomistic “simple ideas” and the “brickbat plan of construction” [PAGE 214 of 716]; association is not a compounding of immutable psychic atoms.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from Descartes’s self-sufficing nervous machine, through Locke and Hume’s associationism, Herbart’s and Mill’s “ideas,” Bain’s habit physiology, and the Mills’ mental chemistry, into Wundt, Helmholtz, and Fechner’s psychophysics. James then bends this lineage toward Janet and Binet on dissociation, Broca and Ferrier and Munk and Goltz on localization, and Hodgson and Huxley on the automaton theory. The retrocausal point is that later cognitive psychology and predictive processing re-derive James’s fringe, preperception, and attention while often forgetting his vetoes.

Multi-week traps are the long-horizon misreadings that stabilize after repeated exposure:
1. Week-one reaction-time trap: simple reaction-time is renamed “speed of thought,” then practice shortens it to an individual minimum [PAGE 112 of 716], creating a false appearance of a stable central trait.
2. Week-two habit trap: repetition is treated as explanation, but the first traversal remains unexplained [PAGE 127 of 716]; by week three the “fly-wheel” [PAGE 139 of 716] is mistaken for a closed causal law.
3. Week-three dissociation trap: hysteric anesthesia is dismissed as malingering, even though James insists that sincere denial is not proof of no feeling [PAGE 229 of 716].
4. Week-four localization trap: transient post-lesion inhibition is read as permanent loss; James’s rule is that “The loss of the function does not necessarily show that it is dependent on the part cut out; but its preservation does show that it is not dependent” [PAGE 61 of 716].
5. Week-five psychophysics trap: Fechner’s logarithmic law is universalized despite James’s objection that its equal-addition assumption is “entirely arbitrary” [PAGE 565 of 716].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind here has three adjudication modes. First, crisis habit regression: as conflict and load rise, voluntary attention narrows and behavior regresses to habitual or reflex pathways. James says, “Consciousness ... is only intense when nerve-processes are hesitant. In rapid, automatic, habitual action it sinks to a minimum” [PAGE 160 of 716]. A recursive agent under crisis will therefore model other minds less flexibly and rely on cached preperceptions. Prediction: under high load, perspective-taking accuracy drops and response latencies move toward the simple-reaction minimum.

Second, counter-trap: introspective or recursive observation changes the state being observed. James’s image is exact: “As a snow-flake crystal caught in the warm hand is no longer a crystal but a drop” [PAGE 262 of 716]. The psychologist’s fallacy is the same error in recursive form: the observer confuses his own standpoint with the mental fact he reports [PAGE 214 of 716]. Prediction: asking an agent to explain an ongoing cognitive strategy will alter the strategy and reduce veridical self-report; external probes are required.

Third, clock: the timing apparatus is physical and attentional, not mental. Simple reaction-time is a reflex-like interval [PAGE 112 of 716], and attention can displace perceived order so that a sound “appears earlier than its real date” [PAGE 430 of 716]. In recursive ToM, the “clock” is therefore a measured latency plus an attention-dependent binding error. Prediction: two observers with different preperceptions will disagree about the order of near-simultaneous events even when the physical order is fixed.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical veto is neuroanatomical. James postulates that the brain is “the one immediate bodily condition of the mental operations” [PAGE 22 of 716]. Therefore any psychological operator must survive lesion evidence. The adjudication rule is: preservation after ablation vetoes necessary localization; transient loss does not prove localization because “remote effects of the wound on distant parts, inhibitions, extensions of inflammation” may explain it [PAGE 61 of 716]. This is a cross-layer veto against purely introspective localization claims.

The biomechanical veto is timing. Reaction-time includes receptor, nerve, centre, efferent nerve, and muscle contraction [PAGE 106 of 716]. A model that infers central thought speed from a hand-key response without subtracting efferent and muscular latency is vetoed by [PAGE 112 of 716]. James also notes that assent involves glottal and cephalic movements [PAGE 319 of 716], so “pure thought” cannot be measured while ignoring bodily expression.

The weather/thermal veto is experimental control. James reports that reaction-time may be “dredths of a second shorter on cold winter days” [PAGE 115 of 716], and that intellectual effort or emotion can cause “a general rise of temperature, which rarely exceeded a degree Fahrenheit” [PAGE 118 of 716]. Therefore any claim that a cognitive operation has an invariant latency or metabolic signature must control season, temperature, and arousal. Veto proof: if the effect disappears when thermal and biomechanical covariates are controlled, the psychological interpretation is not admissible.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
