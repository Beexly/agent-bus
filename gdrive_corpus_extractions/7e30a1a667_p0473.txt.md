Historical/scientific lineage and multi-week traps.

The text on [PAGE 463] belongs to the James-Lange peripheralist tradition: James argues that the feeling of bodily changes, including expressive movements, constitutes part of the emotion itself rather than a mere consequence. He explicitly inherits from Professor Bain's Emotions and Will (pp. 861–2, quoted verbatim on [PAGE 463]), who describes the suspension of an "emotional wave" when outward expression is arrested and its summoning by "action ab extra." This runs parallel to, but is distinct from, Darwin's 1872 account in The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals, where expression is largely an evolved discharge or serviceable habit; James adds the reflexive claim that re-enacting expression can retro-causally generate the feeling ("we are sometimes able to assume a cheerful tone of mind by forcing a hilarious expression" [PAGE 463]).

In the 20th century this lineage splits into (a) affect-script and facial-feedback programs (Tomkins; Ekman & Friesen; Izard), which treat facial afference as a modulator of emotional experience, and (b) self-perception theory (Laird, 1974), where individuals infer their emotional state from their own behavior—"Whistling to keep up courage is no mere figure of speech" [PAGE 463] becomes an operant self-signaling act. The 21st-century formulation is interoceptive predictive coding (Seth & Friston; Critchley & Garfinkel): the brain minimizes prediction error between expected and received afferent signals from expressive posture, so "smooth the brow, brighten the eye" [PAGE 463] alters the interoceptive inference and thereby the felt emotion.

Multi-week traps. James's imperative is "assiduously" repeated [PAGE 463], which implies a distributed practice schedule. A single forced smile is a transient perturbation δx ~ βu₀τ; lasting reweighting of the contrary disposition requires repetition over a habit-acquisition horizon T_habit measured in weeks (roughly 10⁶ s). If practice is interrupted, spontaneous recovery of the original sullenness or depression is expected because the original attractor basin has not been erased, only inhibited. The phrase "infallibly" [PAGE 463] therefore fails across the multi-week clock: compliance decay, sleep loss, and interoceptive variability all introduce relapse risk. A counter-trap is that deliberate expression under high cognitive load may be experienced as inauthentic, generating a prediction error that strengthens, rather than weakens, the original negative disposition.

Veto condition: continuous electrodermal activity (EDA) and corrugator EMG recorded throughout. Prediction: in condition A, x(t) rises monotonically toward a climax ("work ourselves up to a climax" [PAGE 463]); in condition B, if T_d > τ (τ estimated from individual decay baselines), x(t) decays toward pre-provocation baseline and the post-delay expression fails to re-ignite the climax; if T_d < τ, expression resumes while x(t) is still elevated and the climax proceeds. Verifiable by comparing EDA/corrugator area-under-curve between conditions.
