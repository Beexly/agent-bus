<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Tweets are not passive sensors. Let \(B_f\) be a fan’s beliefs about rival fans, the model, and the match; \(E_f\) the fan’s emotion; and \(C\) the match context. The tweet distribution is \(P(t\mid B_f,E_f,C)\). Recursive theory of mind enters because \(B_f\) contains beliefs about how other agents interpret the tweet, including rivals, journalists, and possibly predictive models. The classifier learns \(P(y\mid t)\), but the generative process is strategic and audience-dependent. Three regimes matter.

First, crisis habit regression: under relegation threat, a losing run, or a manager crisis, \(P(t\mid C_{\mathrm{crisis}})\) concentrates on habitual tokens such as club hashtags, booing, blame, and manager-out chants. Volume rises while conditional information \(I(T;Y\mid H)\) may fall, where \(H\) is the habitual phrase set. The model sees many tweets but few discriminative ones.

Second, counter-trap: if the model treats negative sentiment as evidence for a loss, rival fans or ironic home fans can inject counter-signals. A naive valence model can flip sign. The recursive belief “the model reads sentiment” creates incentives for bravado, jinxing, and infiltration. The paper’s hashtag filtering removes multi-team tweets [PAGE 4], but it does not remove single-team irony or rival fans using a single team’s hashtag.

Third, clock: match time \(\tau\) separates pre-match expectation, in-match reaction, and post-match evaluation. Pre-match tweets \(\tau<\tau_0\) may encode expectations and injury news; in-match tweets \(\tau_0\le\tau\le\tau_1\) encode reactions; post-match tweets \(\tau>\tau_1\) encode the outcome. The paper does not specify the tweet window [PAGE 3], and it explicitly leaves open whether the signal is “emotion of the fans, discussions about the outcome or news stories, such as injuries” [PAGE 10]. If post-match tweets are included, the model reads the clock backward. A counter-trap-aware design would use only pre-match tweets, separate venue-specific fan bases, and test for irony and rival injection. Without that, the recursive social layer can dominate the football layer.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Let \(q=(q_{\mathrm{phys}},q_{\mathrm{biomech}},q_{\mathrm{weather}})\) be the omitted physical, biomechanical, and weather state. Physical variables include injury count, rest days, travel, and squad availability; biomechanical variables include sprint load, shooting accuracy under fatigue, and repeated-sprint exposure; weather variables include temperature in K, wind speed in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), precipitation in \(\mathrm{mm\,h^{-1}}\), and pitch condition. The model’s implicit assumption is \(P(Y\mid X,Q)=P(Y\mid X)\). The veto condition is:
\[
I(Y;Q\mid X)>0\quad\text{and}\quad P(Y\mid X,Q)\neq P(Y\mid X),
\]
with \(I\) in bits. Table 1 lists only average goals, corners, shots on target, fouls, yellow cards, red cards, market value, average squad age, international players, EPL wins, EPL runners-up, and double winners [PAGE 5-6]. There is no explicit weather variable, no rest/travel variable, no biomechanical load variable, and no structured injury variable. The paper says Twitter can carry “players’ injuries, sacked coaches, and the average sentiment amongst the fan base” [PAGE 2], but those are unstructured and filtered. The historical features are averages updated before each game [PAGE 5], which reduces temporal leakage but does not add omitted physical layers.

The veto proof is a support argument: any model trained only on Table 1 cannot condition on \(Q\). If weather changes crossing difficulty, if a key player’s biomechanical load changes sprint capacity, or if an injury removes a central defender, the conditional distribution of \(Y\) given \(X\) shifts. The Twitter bag-of-words may partially capture injury news, but it is not a calibrated physical measurement. The paper’s own discussion leaves the information source unresolved [PAGE 10]. Therefore physical, biomechanical, and weather claims are not adjudicated by the reported experiments. The model can be predictive within the observed three-month window, but it is not a cross-layer causal model. A valid cross-layer study would require timestamped weather, rest, travel, injury, and load variables, plus tests of whether Twitter adds information after conditioning on those layers.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Define a verifiable reward for each counterfactual as \(R=1\) if the stated metric condition is met, else \(R=0\). These are falsification checks, not new empirical claims.

1. Temporal-block counterfactual. Re-evaluate the same Twitter-only and combined models with a blocked temporal split: train on matches before date \(d\), test on matches after \(d\), and compare with the reported leave-one-out or OOB estimates. Prediction: LOOCV accuracy exceeds blocked-test accuracy by more than \(3\) percentage points, and kappa exceeds blocked-test kappa by more than \(0.05\). If this prediction fails, the temporal-leakage veto is not triggered. This is verifiable from match dates and model outputs.

2. Pre-match tweet-window counterfactual. Rebuild the bag-of-words using only tweets with timestamp \(\tau<\tau_0-\Delta\), where \(\tau_0\) is kickoff and \(\Delta\) is a fixed pre-match window, and compare with the all-tweet model. Prediction: the all-tweet model’s accuracy is more than \(5\) percentage points higher than the pre-match-only model, and kappa is more than \(0.05\) higher. If post-match tweets add no such advantage, the retrocausal-leakage veto is not triggered. The paper does not specify this window [PAGE 3], so the test is necessary.

3. Nickname-collision stress counterfactual. Reintroduce the filtered cross-sport nickname tweets—Southampton “Saints” colliding with NFL New Orleans Saints, and Tottenham “Spurs” colliding with NBA San Antonio Spurs [PAGE 4]—or inject synthetic cross-sport tweets bearing the same tokens. Prediction: kappa for the affected teams falls by more than \(0.05\), and overall kappa falls by more than \(0.02\), relative to the filtered condition. If the model is robust, the collision veto is not triggered. This is verifiable by controlled re-inclusion and per-team confusion matrices.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
