James then generalizes to brutes: a person turning on a once-traversed road, a cow locating its stanchions, and a horse stopping at a familiar house are all guided by “a sum of impressions, not one of which is emphatic or distinguished from the rest, not one of which is essential, not one of which is conceived, but all of which together drive him to a conclusion to which nothing but that sum-total leads.” [PAGE 351] The final hop identifies the analytic vulnerability: when a person makes one impression characteristic and essential, the remaining impressions are suppressed; “if the (for him) essential feature be forgotten ur changed [sic], he is too apt to be thrown off altogether, and then the brute or the trance-subfect [sic] may seem to outstrip him in sagacity.” [PAGE 351] The page ends mid-sentence at “Dr. Romanes’s already quoted distinction between ‘receptual’ and,” so the opposing concept is unavailable on this page.

Gray-zone audit. The evidence is anecdotal and under-specified: “infallible correctness” is reported without the number of lines N, exposure duration, interstimulus interval, response timeout, false-alarm rate, or subject-hypnotizability screening. OCR noise introduces “hns,” “pmper,” “strect,” “subfect,” and “ur,” so verbatim quotations must be treated with [sic] where necessary. There is a serious experimenter-cueing confound: the operator keeps fixation on the target or counts its position, which could leak gaze or timing cues to the subject. The claim that trance is “like a relapse into the state of mind of brutes” is metaphorical, not a measured capacity; it conflates performance under a particular deadline with a fixed trait. The analytic-holistic contrast is therefore best read as an interaction between strategy, deadline, redundancy of cues, and fragility of the selected essential feature, not as proof that animals reason or that trance confers sagacity. Any quantitative use of this page must first bound N, viewing distance, contrast, fixation/saccade latency, serial counting time, and motor response time; otherwise the derivation remains at the level of qualitative psychology.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators and units, tied to [PAGE 351]. Let the target line index be K ∈ {1,…,N}; let the encoding stimulus be x^(e) ∈ R^m, where m is the number of spatial samples and positions are measured in pixels; let viewing distance be d metres and exposure time T_e seconds. A holistic “general look” operator is a normalized template match:
\hat{K} = argmin_{j∈{1,…,N}} || x^(t) − μ_j ||_2 ,
accept the match only if min_j || x^(t) − μ_j ||_2 ≤ δ, otherwise abstain. Distances are in normalized pixel-intensity units; δ is dimensionless. The information ceiling for identifying one line among N is I(K;\hat{K}) ≤ H(K) = log_2 N bits, and uniform guessing yields p_0 = 1/N, a dimensionless probability in [0,1].

For James’s “summation of stimuli,” define an impulsive drive S = Σ_i w_i x_i + η, where x_i are binary or contrast-coded stimulus components, w_i ∈ R are weights, η ~ Normal(0,σ_η^2) is dimensionless neural noise, and the decision operator is D = 1 if S ≥ θ, else D = 0; θ is a dimensionless criterion. This is discontinuous at S = θ. The analytic counting operator has required time T_count = n(τ_fix + τ_sac) + τ_dec + τ_motor seconds, with n the number of items to count, τ_fix the per-item fixation dwell, τ_sac the saccadic latency, τ_dec the decision latency, and τ_motor the reach/touch movement time. Counting is feasible only when T_available ≥ T_count; when T_available < T_count the analytic output is censored and accuracy collapses toward p_0 unless a holistic estimate is used.

Breakdown and singularity regimes. If one feature f* is over-weighted so that |w_f*| / Σ_j |w_j| → 1 and f* is later forgotten or changed, analytic accuracy conditioned on f* tends to p_0: catastrophic fragility, matching James’s claim that the analytic subject is thrown off when the essential feature changes [PAGE 351]. If weights are homogeneous, max_i |w_i| / Σ_j |w_j| ≤ ε, no single cue is essential and the redundant sum S can support discrimination when serial counting is impossible. Attention dispersion introduces an effectiveness factor α ∈ (0,1], giving SNR_eff = α Σ_i w_i^2 x_i^2 / σ_η^2; if α → 0 through over-dispersion, or σ_η → ∞, or d′ falls below the adopted decision threshold, even holistic discrimination breaks down. As N → ∞ at fixed total stimulus energy, per-cue SNR decays approximately as 1/N while T_count grows linearly in n, so the analytic advantage exists only above a deadline boundary and vanishes below it.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. James’s page sits at the intersection of associationist psychology, psychophysics, hypnotism, and early animal cognition. The “summation of stimuli” appeal to Chapter III continues Hartley’s and Bain’s associationist mechanics and James’s own principles of habit and stream of thought. The hypnotic line-identification experiment descends from Charcot, Bernheim, and Liébeault’s salience experiments, while the “total effect” anticipates Gestalt principles of Prägnanz and later preattentive texture segmentation. The contrast between a summed, non-emphatic impression and one essential, characteristic feature prefigures feature-integration theory and signal-detection separations of sensitivity and criterion; the page stops at Romanes’s “receptual” versus conceptual distinction, a lineage running from Romanes through comparative cognition to modern debates about perceptual categories versus conceptual rules.

Multi-week traps. Applying this page across weeks creates several failure modes. First, a holistic advantage measured in a brief trance session does not imply stable expertise; consolidation, sleep, and attentional fatigue can shift the weighting w_i over days, so a feature that was redundant in week one may become essential by week three. Second, analytic training that selects one essential feature produces overfitting to that feature; if the environment drifts and the feature is occluded or changed, performance can regress abruptly to p_0, whereas a subject who maintained distributed cue weighting degrades more gracefully. Third, strategy switching is context-dependent: an observer who counts successfully under long deadlines may reflexively count under short deadlines, miss the switch point T_available = T_count, and perform worse than a naïve holistic responder. Fourth, experimenter gaze or counting cues can produce spurious “infallibility” in early sessions that disappears when the operator is masked, creating a false positive that persists across weeks unless controlled. Fifth, repeated exposure can convert a holistic total effect into a serial analytic routine, eliminating the very redundancy that made the brute or trance subject robust under time pressure.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. Under deadline pressure, an agent with an established analytic habit regresses to a habitual or holistic policy when it models that serial counting will not complete before the response clock expires. Formally, let T_deadline be the time from target re-presentation to required response. If T_deadline − T_percept < T_count, the recursive best response is to switch to the holistic template operator; if an observer incorrectly believes the opponent will remain analytic, the observer may over-count and lose time, which is the crisis habit regression trap. The counter-trap is to feign regression or to impose a short deadline so that an analytic opponent is forced into censored counting while the feigner uses a precompiled holistic template.

The clock is the hard constraint: the switch boundary occurs at T_switch = T_count − τ_safety, where τ_safety is a motor and decision buffer. If T_available ≥ T_switch, analytic counting can dominate because it isolates the essential feature; if T_available < T_switch, holistic guidance dominates because it integrates the sum of impressions [PAGE 351]. In a recursive theory-of-mind loop, each agent models the other’s switch point. If both agents switch to holistic simultaneously, individual line identification can fall to p_0 unless the total-effect template has sufficient separation min_{i≠j} || μ_i − μ_j ||_2 relative to noise. If one agent over-weights an essential feature and that feature is changed between encoding and test, that agent’s accuracy tends to p_0 while the holistic agent remains robust until redundancy is lost. The model breaks down when the deadline is so short that even template encoding cannot complete, or when the feature set is so homogeneous that no holistic template carries more than p_0 information.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof. The psychological claim on [PAGE 351] is void whenever any independent physical layer vetoes the stimulus. Visual acuity imposes Δθ ≥ Δθ_min ≈ 2.91×10^−4 radians (one arcminute); at viewing distance d metres, line spacing s must satisfy s/d ≥ Δθ_min. If s/d < Δθ_min, neither analytic nor holistic operators can resolve the target, so “infallible correctness” is physically impossible and the claim is vetoed. Luminance contrast C = (L_max − L_min)/(L_max + L_min) is dimensionless; photopic vision requires adequate illuminance E in lux, and below mesopic/scotopic transition the Weber fraction rises, acuity falls, and rod intrusion slows temporal resolution. Under atmospheric extinction, C(d) = C_0 exp(−β_ext d), with β_ext in m^−1 and d in metres; using Koschmieder β_ext ≈ 3/V where V is visibility in metres, the contrast cue is vetoed when d > β_ext^−1 ln(C_0/C_W), with C_W the Weber threshold. Fog, rain, or wind that displaces the paper by more than one line width w between encoding and test invalidates the template match.

Biomechanical veto. Saccadic latency τ_sac ≈ 0.20 s and per-item fixation plus movement make T_count = n(τ_fix + τ_sac) + τ_dec + τ_motor. Reach-to-touch movement time follows MT = a + b log_2(1 + D/W) seconds, with D movement distance and W target width in metres. If the permitted response interval is below T_count, or if MT exceeds the available movement window, counting and pointing cannot be executed, regardless of cognitive strategy. Any one of these vetoes—acuity, contrast/extinction, illuminance, serial-count time, or motor window—renders the source observation inapplicable for that condition; it does not supply a probability estimate, only a “not evidence” ruling.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Rewards are verifiable correctness A ∈ [0,1] and latency in seconds; no shaping beyond measured accuracy and RT.

Scenario A, deadline-switch veto. Use N ruled lines, cue one line for T_e = 100 ms, remove the paper, then re-present it. Condition analytic: unlimited re-exposure and counting before response. Condition holistic: forced response within 500 ms. Prediction: analytic accuracy exceeds holistic when T_allowed ≥ N(τ_fix+τ_sac)+τ_dec+τ_motor; holistic accuracy exceeds analytic when T_allowed < N(τ_fix+τ_sac)+τ_dec+τ_motor. Veto condition: if holistic does not exceed analytic under the short deadline, or analytic does not exceed holistic when given full counting time, the Jamesian deadline interaction is falsified for this apparatus.

Scenario B, essential-feature corruption veto. Train observers to identify the target by one salient mark f* while holding whole-sheet texture constant. At test, change or remove f* but preserve all other local cues. Prediction: analytic group accuracy falls to p_0 = 1/N; holistic group accuracy remains above p_0 if redundant cues carry template information. Veto condition: if analytic accuracy remains high after f* corruption, f* was not essential; if holistic accuracy falls to p_0, there is no usable total-effect template. The information test is I(K;\hat{K}) > 0 bits for the holistic group and I(K;\hat{K}|f* corrupted) ≈ 0 bits for the analytic group.

Scenario C, sensory-leakage and biomechanical veto. Reduce illuminance or increase line density until either C(d) < C_W or s/d < Δθ_min, and independently mask the operator’s gaze so no fixation or counting cue can leak. Prediction: both conditions collapse to A ≈ p_0 and RTs saturate or increase; any above-chance accuracy below the computed acuity/contrast limit implies uncued sensory cues or experimenter leakage. Veto condition: if above-chance performance persists with masked operator and below-threshold stimulus parameters, the result is attributable to cueing rather than the psychological mechanism described on [PAGE 351].
