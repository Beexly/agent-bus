<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The crisis habit regression is the analyst’s default habit: “the device triggered, so \(t=0\) is the hit, and the recorded peak is the injury-relevant peak.” The source breaks that habit in three ways. First, the trigger is incisor linear acceleration, not impact onset; high angular velocity can occur without an impact [PAGE7]. Second, brain strain is history-dependent and viscoelastic [PAGE5, PAGE8], so a peak value without its preceding window is not a complete state variable. Third, multiple spikes mean the largest angular acceleration need not be the largest strain-producing event [PAGE8]. The counter-trap is equally dangerous: “if 70 ms is good, 150 ms must be better.” The source shows that late-peak numerical error can invalidate long-history simulations [PAGE7], and the device reset gap means adjacent events cannot be assumed continuous [PAGE8]. The clock is the actual object of inference: peak time relative to trigger, not trigger time relative to impact. The paper’s own clock result is that 95% MPS peak times are earlier than 95% MPSR peak times, and both can occur “much earlier or later than the triggering” in a few cases [PAGE7]. The recursive theory of mind is therefore: the device designer believes \(t=0\) is a well-defined event; the analyst believes the window contains the event; the brain’s viscoelastic response does not share that clock. The correct counter-trap is to retain a wider window when possible, but to define the minimum tested window as \([-20,70]\) ms and to compute the impact-specific required range from Eq.3 rather than from a fixed habit. The largest-brain cases are the adversarial test: the paper says “larger head models need longer histories of kinematics for calculation of both 95% MPS peak and 95% MPSR peak” [PAGE9], and the most significant changes at \(-10\) ms pre-trigger appeared in “the largest male, 50th percentile male, and original head models” [PAGE9]. Thus the crisis habit “short window is enough” fails precisely where the brain is largest, and the counter-trap “infinite window” fails where numerical drift and reset gaps dominate.
