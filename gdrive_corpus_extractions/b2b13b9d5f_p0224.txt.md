Biomechanical/developmental layer: the sample is restricted to "adult children" and families with at least six adult children [PAGE 1, footnote 1]. This truncates the fertility distribution and correlates family size with stature through socio-economic and nutritional pathways operative in Galton's cohort. Veto: the sample is not a random draw of all adult children; external validity to the general population is violated because small families and sub-adult offspring are excluded by design.

Weather/exogenous environment layer: no meteorological variables appear in [PAGE 1]; therefore a weather-conditioned veto has no data to act on and is formally null. However, unmeasured environmental variance — nutrition, disease, altitude, season of birth — is a latent confounder. Because Table 10 conditions only on \(\Delta H\) and \(S\), and does not adjust for environment, Galton's "inconsiderable effect" conclusion is ceteris paribus on shared environment, an assumption [PAGE 1] does not establish. Cross-layer adjudication: the physical and biomechanical vetoes are active and binding; the weather veto is null by absence of variables, but latent environmental confounding remains an unadjudicated threat to causal interpretation.
