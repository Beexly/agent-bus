<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage of the claims on [PAGE 1], with attention to multi-week experimental traps.

James's page is a node in an 1880s–1890s debate over the localization of negative after-images and the directionality of nerve currents. He cites Meyer and Féré for the optic-nerve afterimage [PAGE 1]; Binet's Le Magnétisme Animal (1887, p. 188) for the hypnotic red-cross/bluish-green cross demonstration [PAGE 1]; Parinaud as Binet's predecessor in localizing ordinary negative after-images in the "optical brain-centres" rather than the retina [PAGE 1]; Delabarre (American Journal of Psychology, II. 826) for the monocular closed-eye retinal-projection refutation [PAGE 1]; Sergi (Psicologie Physiologique, pp. 99, 189) for the theory of a universal "refluent wave in perception," which James dismisses [PAGE 1]; and Lombroso and Ottolenghi (Revue Philosophique, xxix. 70, Jan. 1890) for a crude reaffirmation of Sergi [PAGE 1].

The first historical trap is localization. Binet and Parinaud trace the after-image to higher cerebral centres; Delabarre shows that the closed eye's retinal process can be projected into the open eye's field, and Binet is "converted by the proofs" [PAGE 1]. A single-session demonstration cannot distinguish central origin from peripheral retinal persistence projected binocularly; only the monocular manipulation delivers the veto.

The second trap is temporal: multi-week suggestibility. A hypnotic subject tested once may produce a genuine after-image or a compliant report; run weekly over a month, expectation, demand characteristics, and repeated suggestion inflate the apparent frequency of "backward flow" and confabulate sensory provenance. James's hypnotic footnote has no longitudinal control, so the "genuineness of the result has been doubted" [PAGE 1].

The third trap is theoretical premature dismissal. James rejects Sergi's always-present refluent wave as hardly meriting serious consideration [PAGE 1], yet modern predictive-coding and corticofugal feedback architectures vindicate a weak form of re-entrant top-down flow in perception—just not the literal centrifugal current to the retina that would violate axonal conduction. The page thus preserves a genuine paradox: the phenomenology of imagination behaves as if backward flow exists, while the nerve physiology of the 1890s forbids it at the retina.</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus the external clock.

The listener at night hearing a far-off clock performs a recursive second-order attribution: "Did I hear the stroke, or only imagine it?" [PAGE 1]. Under crisis conditions—S_a below θ—the sensory likelihood P(S_a | real stroke) ≈ P(S_a | imagination), so the posterior collapses onto the prior habit P(real) = P(clock strikes on the hour). This is crisis habit regression: the mind regresses from veridical sensation to the habitual temporal model of the clock and recursively inserts a phantom final stroke to complete the expected rhythm. The clock is the phase reference; its inter-stroke interval T [s] normally resets the listener's internal oscillator φ. When the afferent signal drops out, φ drifts under the habit prior rather than the sensory likelihood, and a false positive is generated exactly at the expected strike epoch.

The violinist's diminuendo is the deliberate counter-trap [PAGE 1]. The bow continues its visual motion without touching the strings, so there is no string vibration f₁ = (1/2L)√(T_string/μ) [Hz] and no acoustic pressure p [Pa] at the listener's ear. The listener's imagination supplies the diminuendo, locked to visual bow velocity v_bow [m s⁻¹] rather than to any SPL. This counter-trap isolates imagination from sensation: if the report covaries with v_bow while the microphone reads 0 dB SPL, the percept is self-generated.

Recursive theory-of-mind structure. First-order belief: "a stroke occurred." Second-order belief: "I am imagining a stroke." At S_a < θ the second-order belief cannot be maintained; the listener attributes the phantom stroke to the clock (external agency) and loses the meta-cognitive tag "imagined." Breakdown occurs when the habit prior conflicts with ground truth—e.g., the clock has stopped. The phantom stroke is then exposed as false only after the expected interval T elapses with no real stroke, at which point the crisis habit regression is falsified by the null external event. The recursive loop thus has a falsification latency equal to one clock period T.</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and atmospheric veto proofs for the imagination claims on [PAGE 1].

Physical layer (optic nerve/auditory afferents). Axonal conduction is centripetal with velocity v [m s⁻¹]; retrograde initiation at the retina would require depolarization against the axial current and violate the refractory boundary of the cable equation. Therefore a literal J_ret > 0 to the retina is vetoed unless replaced by corticofugal feedback (central, not retinal). For hearing, the absolute threshold is p_th ≈ 20 µPa, equivalent to intensity I_th = 10⁻¹² W m⁻² (0 dB SPL). A far-off clock at night is subject to atmospheric attenuation α [dB km⁻¹] and geometric spreading: received level L_r = L₀ − αr − 20 log₁₀(r/r₀) [dB], with range r [km] and reference r₀. If L_r < 0 dB SPL at the listener's position, no physical sound reaches the cochlea, and any report of the stroke is imaginative, not sensory.

Biomechanical layer (violin). String fundamental f₁ = (1/2L)√(T_string/μ) [Hz], with length L [m], tension T_string [N], and linear density μ [kg m⁻¹]. When the bow does not touch the strings, T_string at the contact point is effectively zero for sound production, so string acceleration a_string ≈ 0 [m s⁻²] and radiated pressure p ≈ 0. A microphone and string accelerometer therefore veto the existence of an acoustic source; any reported diminuendo is imagination driven by visual bow motion [PAGE 1].

Weather/atmospheric layer. Nighttime temperature inversions and wind gradients refract sound and alter α [dB km⁻¹], potentially bringing a normally inaudible clock transiently above threshold or masking it. However, this layer can only modulate the physical signal S_a; it cannot create a veridical percept when S_a < θ. The veto proof is conjunctive: measured SPL below threshold at the ear AND zero string acceleration AND unidirectional axonal conduction together force the conclusion that the reported sound/stroke is imagination. The residual negative-afterimage paradox remains only as a statement that standard nerve physiology lacks a mechanism for literal retinal centrifugal flow, not as evidence that such flow occurs [PAGE 1].</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions. RLVR here denotes reinforcement signal derived from verifiable, instrumented outcomes rather than subjective report alone.

Scenario 1 — Monocular afterimage control (tests Delabarre vs. centrifugal retinal current). Procedure: fixate a colored spot with one eye on a white background; close that eye; view a plain surface with the open eye [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: repeat with both eyes open and dark adapted, no closure. Prediction: a negative after-image appears under monocular closure (retinal persistence in the closed eye projected into the open eye's field) but not as a retinal projection under the binocular open-field control. Veto: if the after-image appears identically under binocular viewing with no eye closure, the literal centrifugal current to the retina is supported; if it appears only under monocular closure, the centrifugal hypothesis is vetoed in favor of Delabarre's projection account. Verifiable readout: perimetric mapping of after-image locus [degrees visual angle] and duration τ [s].

Scenario 2 — Violin bow contact vs. no-contact with acoustic instrumentation (tests imagination vs. sound). Procedure: after pianissimo, the violinist bows without touching the strings [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: identical bow velocity v_bow [m s⁻¹] and visual trajectory, but (a) strings untouched, and (b) strings touched with a mute such that contact occurs but radiated SPL remains at background. Instrumentation: microphone SPL [dB re 20 µPa] and string accelerometer a_string [m s⁻²]. Prediction: in condition (a), SPL = 0 dB SPL and a_string ≈ 0, yet listeners report an imagined diminuendo covarying with v_bow; in condition (b), any report of a loud real sound while SPL ≈ 0 is vetoed as demand characteristic/confabulation. Breakdown: if SPL > 0 dB SPL during "no touch," the physical layer is violated and the imagination claim fails.

Scenario 3 — Hypnotic red cross vs. blank-field control (tests suggestion vs. backward sensory flow). Procedure: hypnotic subject is suggested a red cross, the cross is pretended removed, fixation on a dot, and the subject reports a "bluish-green cross" [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: identical hypnotic induction, suggestion framing, and dot fixation, but no red cross is ever presented (blank white paper only). Instrumentation: colorimeter reporting CIE 1931 chromaticity [x, y] of the subject's selected match; trial count N over repeated sessions. Prediction: in the primed condition, reported chromaticity clusters near the red complement (x ≈ 0.25, y ≈ 0.40); in the blank control, reports cluster near white (x ≈ 0.31, y ≈ 0.33) or are randomly distributed. Veto: if the blank-control match rate equals the primed rate, the result is compliance/suggestion, not sensory imagination or centrifugal flow; the afterimage claim is vetoed. Multi-week trap: running the same subject weekly increases compliance, so N must be matched across conditions to avoid a false-positive backward-flow inference.</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
