- Anomalous correspondence and strabismus screening: observers with anomalous retinal correspondence or small-angle strabismus may never experience the pin; mixing them with normal observers across weeks attenuates the effect unpredictably.
- The truncation at “by far the” [PAGE 94] removes the natural-scene generalization claim; any multi-week field claim that “natural lines in such planes are usually vertical” would be unsupported by this page and is a fabrication risk.
- Demand-characteristic cascade: telling an observer what to “fancy” [PAGE 94] installs a second-order belief (“the experimenter wants me to see a vertical staff”), which over weeks becomes self-confirming report rather than percept.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

First-order theory of mind: the observer infers an external object (the “pin”) from two monocular images. Under the binocular correspondence constraint, the observer’s visual system implicitly models the world as containing a line normal to the paper at O. When monocular ambiguity dominates (plane Φ through the vertical meridian [PAGE 94]), the observer regresses to crisis habits: gravity-aligned and ground-plane priors dominate, so lines are assumed upright and frontal-parallel unless disparity says otherwise.

Second-order theory of mind (counter-trap): the observer models the experimenter’s intention. The instruction to “hold them in a horizontal plane” and to look “along them” [PAGE 94] telegraphs the expected geometry. The report that one can “fancy the lines to be vertical staffs” [PAGE 94] may be a counter-trap: the observer produces the percept they believe is demanded, conflating imagination with perception. Franklin’s own verdict — “purely mental origin” [PAGE 94] — risks mistaking this demand-compliant introspection for a mechanistic explanation, while the page’s own retinal-correspondence account offers a physiological alternative. The counter-trap is to treat “purely mental” and “retinal rows” as mutually exclusive when both are co-required.

Third-order theory of mind (recursive self-monitoring): the observer monitors whether their report stays constant across the two eyes and across time. “Look steadily now at the point of intersection… with both eyes open” [PAGE 94] invites a stability check; if the pin flickers, the observer attributes instability to the stimulus rather than to their own vergence drift or suppression.

Clock: the percept has multiple timescales. (i) Stereo matching in V1 complex cells integrates over ~50–150 ms; (ii) vergence accommodation loop responds over ~0.5–2 s, so “after a moment” [PAGE 94] the vertical-staff impression consolidates as vergence settles; (iii) prolonged steady fixation risks Troxler fading, rivalry, or rectus-muscle strain, especially under atropinized accommodative effort [PAGE 94], so the “pin” may vacillate exactly because the clock of oculomotor innervation outruns the clock of lenticular accommodation under cycloplegia. Crisis habit regression (vertical prior) wins at short latency; counter-trap (demand compliance) wins under instruction; the clock determines which dominates as fixation persists.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof; exact breakdown regimes.

Physical veto (optics and stimulus geometry): The illusion requires the paper plane Π to be held horizontal and frontal to the eyes, with the two lines intersecting at the fixation point O [PAGE 94]. If the line of sight is not normal to Π at O, the two monocular images no longer fall on corresponding rows at the same retinal locus; the horopter through O shears and the “pin” normal to Π fails to form. Veto condition: head roll/torsion τ such that the torsional disparity between corresponding rows exceeds ε_Panum (≈ arcmin scale). Breakdown: any τ > ~2–5° (order-of-magnitude assumption; not from [PAGE 94]) destroys row correspondence and vetoes the perpendicular-pin percept.

Biomechanical veto (extraocular muscles and accommodation): Conjugate gaze requires medial- and lateral-rectus drives to satisfy u_MR − u_LR ≈ k·V_req(Z). Under atropinized accommodation, ΔP_lens ≈ 0 but u_MR remains coupled to accommodative effort A [PAGE 94]; if g·A exceeds fusional vergence reserves, single vision cannot be maintained without excessive u_LR, and the observer reports double vision or “vacillations” [PAGE 94]. Veto condition: cycloplegia plus sustained A > 0 with |u_MR − u_LR| beyond fusional range → diplopia; the illusion collapses into two separated images. Units: A in diopters (D), vergence V_req in rad or Δ, drives dimensionless. Additional biomechanical veto: strabismus, amblyopia, or anomalous retinal correspondence abolishes normal corresponding-row matching [PAGE 94 framework], so the pin does not appear.

Weather/environmental veto: The experiment is indoor and paper-based, so meteorological weather is not a direct factor [PAGE 94]. However, ambient luminance sets pupil diameter p (mm), which changes depth of focus and thus the effective tolerance for monocular blur in plane Φ; very small p increases depth of focus and can weaken the monocular ambiguity that the illusion exploits. Ocular surface dryness or tear-film breakup (seconds to minutes timescale) degrades the retinal image and lowers effective ε_Panum. Veto condition: tear-film breakup time < fixation duration or p < 2 mm under bright illumination → unstable monocular fixation → illusory pin fuses unreliably. These are physical optics vetoes, not betting quantities.

Adjudication order: (1) physical geometry veto (head tilt, plane alignment) is checked first because it removes the corresponding-row precondition; (2) biomechanical veto (strabismus, cycloplegia, fusional reserve exhaustion) is checked second because it determines whether binocular singleness is maintainable; (3) environmental/ocular-surface veto is third because it modulates image quality. If any veto fires, prediction of the “pin” percept is null; no odds or lines are involved.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions. RLVR here means reinforcement-learning-style verifiable reward conditions: each scenario specifies an observable manipulation, a binary veto condition, and a falsifiable prediction about the percept or oculomotor measure. No betting lines, spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds are referenced.

Scenario 1 — Head-torsion counterfactual. Manipulation: rotate the observer’s head by roll τ = 15° relative to gravity while keeping the paper horizontal and the lines fixated at O [PAGE 94 geometry]. Veto condition: measured ocular torsion (video-oculography) shifts corresponding retinal rows by more than ε_Panum. Prediction: the illusory line normal to the paper (“sticking up like a pin” [PAGE 94]) will not be reported; instead the observer sees the two drawn lines without a perpendicular third line, or reports diplopia/tilted rivalry. Verifiable: binocular report plus torsion in degrees; null result if pin persists despite torsion > ε_Panum.

Scenario 2 — Cycloplegic accommodation counterfactual. Manipulation: instill atropine to paralyze the ciliary muscle, then have the observer sustain accommodative effort toward the near paper while viewing the crossed lines binocularly [PAGE 94 footnote logic]. Veto condition: photorefraction confirms ΔP_lens ≈ 0 while accommodative drive A > 0 is sustained (verifiable in diopters). Prediction: medial-rectus drive increases without lenticular response; to preserve singleness the observer must recruit “correspondingly excessive” lateral-rectus contraction [PAGE 94], producing esotropic drift and breakdown of the pin into double images, with the reported depth of the line vacillating (“vacillating judgment” [PAGE 94]). Verifiable: cover test / prism cover in prism diopters (Δ) and continuous depth report; if singleness and stable pin persist under confirmed cycloplegia with A > 0, the vetoed prediction is falsified.

Scenario 3 — Monocular occlusion counterfactual. Manipulation: after the binocular pin illusion is established, occlude one eye while the observer continues to look at the intersection O [PAGE 94]. Veto condition: binocular correspondence constraint removed (one eye’s input eliminated). Prediction: the illusory vertical pin immediately collapses because the “corresponding rows” operator [PAGE 94] no longer has a binocular solution; the remaining monocular line lies in the ambiguous plane through the apparent vertical meridian [PAGE 94], whose direction-in-depth is underdetermined, so the observer cannot report whether the line leans toward or away from them. Verifiable: monocular vs. binocular report and fixation stability; persistence of a vivid 3D pin under monocular occlusion falsifies the correspondence account on [PAGE 94].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
