where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. The normal-law assumption is cited from [PAGE 314]: "sOR rather closely approaches a normal chance distribution."

Joint probability under the non-correlation assumption ([PAGE 314]):
\begin{align*}
\Pr(\text{all } N \text{ muscles react}) = \prod_{i=1}^{N} p_i(t)
\end{align*}
valid only if \(\operatorname{Cov}(sOR_i, sOR_j) = 0\) for \(i \neq j\).

Singularity and breakdown regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\sigma_{OR} \to 0^{+}\): \(p_i \to H(sER - \theta - \mu_{OR})\). Deterministic threshold; stochasticity vanishes.
\item \(\sigma_{OR} \to \infty\): \(p_i \to 1/2\) for finite \(sER\). Infinite inhibitory noise swamps the signal; mutual information between \(sER\) and reaction tends to zero.
\item \(sER \to 0^{+}\): \(p_i \to \Phi(-(\theta + \mu_{OR})/\sigma_{OR})\). Floor asymptote; if \(\theta + \mu_{OR} > 0\), \(p_i \to 0\).
\item \(sER = \theta + \mu_{OR}\): \(p_i = 0.5\). Maximum sensitivity to \(sER\); inflection of the probit curve.
\item Non-correlation breakdown: if common neuromodulatory drive induces \(\rho > 0\), the factorized joint probability is invalid; a multivariate normal copula with covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) is required.
\end{enumerate}

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The theoretical lineage on [PAGE 314] runs from Hull's 1943 neobehaviorism back through Thorndike's trial-and-error learning and the law of effect, Pavlovian reflex physiology, and Watsonian S-R psychology. The appeal to a "normal chance distribution" for sOR connects to the Galtonian/Quetelet tradition of error distributions and to Fechinian psychophysics, where sensory thresholds were modeled as stochastic. Hull's specific move—to treat inhibition as an oscillating, normally distributed potential with constant mean and range—anticipates later formalizations in signal detection theory (Green & Swets, 1966) and drift-diffusion / Ornstein-Uhlenbeck models of decision making (Ratcliff, 1978), where a latent accumulator competes against noise and a bound.

A multi-week trap is embedded in the stationarity assumption. Hull holds μ_OR and σ_OR constant "for a given organism," but over timescales of weeks, chronic stress, pharmacological state, sleep deprivation, or extended skill acquisition can shift the inhibitory operating point. A model fitted on day 1 will mispredict day-14 behavior if μ_OR has drifted upward (increased tonic inhibition) or σ_OR has widened (increased inhibitory volatility). The text itself flags this instability by asking whether "the range of oscillation vary[s] for a given organism," acknowledging that the constant-range hypothesis may fail across the very weeks during which an organism is observed. This is a non-stationarity trap: short-run parameter estimates do not survive long-run re-application without re-calibration.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when sER for a dominant, well-conditioned S-R bond is elevated while competing responses remain suppressed. Under acute stress, the organism regresses to the habit with the lowest effective threshold because sOR fluctuations rarely suffice to veto a strongly potentiated path; the inhibitory potential sOR, even when drawn from its normal distribution, cannot overcome the large sER − θ margin of the habitual response.

Counter-trap structure: if Agent B knows Agent A regresses to habit H under crisis, B can pre-position a counter-response C specifically conditioned to intercept H. In recursive theory-of-mind terms, A models B's anticipation of H and may suppress H in favor of a secondary response H'; B models A's suppression and readies C′ against H′. The regress alternates until one agent's sER crosses θ or the decision window closes.

Clock: The reaction must occur before a deadline τ. If sER ramps approximately linearly, sER(t) ≈ r·t, and sOR oscillates with characteristic period T, then the probability of crossing before τ depends on the phase of sOR at t = τ. If the deadline lands on an sOR peak (maximal inhibition), a clock veto occurs even when average sER is high; if it lands on an sOR trough, the reaction escapes. Recursive ToM therefore collapses at the boundary imposed by the clock: infinite regress is truncated by the physical necessity that some muscle must clear threshold before τ expires.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A reaction is veto-proof only if every layer simultaneously clears its threshold.

Neural layer ([PAGE 314]): Muscle i fires only if sER(t) − sOR_i(t) ≥ θ. The neural veto probability for muscle i is \(1 - p_i(t)\). Because sOR is non-correlated across muscles per [PAGE 314], the joint probability that no neural veto occurs across N effectors is \(\prod_{i=1}^N p_i(t)\). For any \(p_i < 1\), this product decays exponentially with N, predicting that complex multi-muscle reactions are substantially less probable than single-effecter twitches under identical sER.

Biomechanical layer: Even when the neural predicate passes, muscle contraction requires calcium release, cross-bridge cycling, and tendon force transmission. Fatigue raises the effective biomechanical threshold \(\theta_{\text{physio},i}(t)\) over seconds to minutes. Temperature and humidity (weather/environmental conditions) alter nerve conduction velocity and muscle contractility: cold increases tissue viscosity and slows conduction, delaying the rise of sER relative to the ongoing sOR oscillation, thereby giving inhibition more opportunities to veto; heat accelerates fatigue, raising \(\theta_{\text{physio}}\) faster and shortening the window in which \(sER - sOR_i \geq \theta\) can hold.

Environmental/weather layer: Extreme weather modifies afferent gain. High wind, precipitation, or thermal stress reduce the effective sensory input that contributes to sER, lowering the ramp rate r in sER(t) ≈ r·t. If r drops below the rate at which sOR samples its inhibitory distribution, the reaction never reaches θ before the biomechanical system fatigues.

Adjudication rule: A response is executed iff \(\forall i,\; sER(t) - sOR_i(t) > \theta_{\text{physio},i}(t)\) and the environmental gain is sufficient to sustain sER through the contraction latency. No single layer is sufficient; the veto is distributed and the joint probability is the product of layer-wise survival probabilities.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Inhibitory-noise manipulation. Verifiable intervention: pharmacologically increase σ_OR (e.g., via GABAergic modulation that broadens momentary inhibitory fluctuations) while holding sER and μ_OR constant. RLVR prediction: the probit slope \(\partial p_i / \partial sER\) at \(sER = \theta + \mu_{OR}\) decreases; threshold discriminability worsens; trial-by-trial reaction latency variance increases specifically near the crossover point. Verification: within-subject pre/post measurement of reaction probability as a function of calibrated sER; rejection if the slope does not shallow.

Scenario 2 — Induced inter-muscle correlation. Verifiable intervention: apply common descending drive or bilateral cortical stimulation to induce \(\operatorname{Cov}(sOR_i, sOR_j) > 0\), violating the non-correlation clause on [PAGE 314]. RLVR prediction: the observed joint reaction probability \(P(\text{all } N \text{ react})\) exceeds \(\prod_i p_i(t)\); the independence assumption is rejected by a likelihood-ratio test comparing the factorized model to a multivariate-normal copula with estimated ρ. Verification: simultaneous EMG or force recordings from multiple effectors; trial-by-trial covariance of inhibitory residuals computed after regressing out sER.

Scenario 3 — Clock-phase veto. Verifiable intervention: impose a fixed response deadline τ while sER ramps at known rate r and sOR oscillates with measurable period T (e.g., via ongoing neural oscillation or behavioral tremor). RLVR prediction: reaction probability before deadline is not monotonic in τ alone but oscillates with τ mod T; at deadlines phase-locked to sOR peaks (maximal inhibition), veto probability spikes even when mean sER is well above θ. Verification: reaction-time cumulants and hit/miss rates binned by deadline phase relative to the measured oscillation; rejection if hit rate is independent of phase.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
