When a remote generation with zero expected deviation is combined with an observed mid-parental deviation, the effect resembles pouring a measure of water into a vessel of wine; the wine is diluted to a constant fraction of its alcoholic strength whatever that strength may have been [PAGE 105]. The effective deviation after one admixture is D_eff^(g) = r_g D_MP + lambda_g * 0 = r_g D_MP [m]. Because the fraction is constant, the map from D_MP to D_eff is linear and homogeneous of degree one.

Fourth hop: iteration across generations as proportionate dilution. For every intermediate generation, the mid-deviation is intermediate between the zero deviation of remote ancestry and the observed deviation of the mid-parent [PAGE 105]. Combining it with the mid-parental deviation is therefore like pouring a definite mixture of wine and water, not pure water, into the wine. The process throughout is one of proportionate dilutions [PAGE 105]. <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton's 1889 Natural Inheritance [PAGE 105] sits at the hinge between pre-Mendelian biometry and the modern quantitative-genetic synthesis. Although this page does not use the later coinage "regression coefficient," it operationalizes exactly that object: a dimensionless shrinkage factor beta that pulls the expected offspring deviation toward the population mean P because deep ancestry contributes a zero-centered, random-sample component [PAGE 105]. The wine-water metaphor is thus an early, intuitive statement of what would become the breeder's equation, Fisher's infinitesimal model, and contemporary SNP-heritability frameworks (GREML, LDSC, GWAS regression). The invariant transmitted across this lineage is that parental extremity is an imperfect predictor of offspring extremity precisely because remote ancestors supply a regression-to-the-mean anchor.

The historical lineage runs: Galton (1889) → Pearson's product-moment correlation and regression (1890s) → Fisher's partitioning of genetic and environmental variance (1918) → Lush and Falconer's quantitative genetics → molecular quantitative genetics and GWAS. Each link preserves the core insight that observed deviation D_MP is diluted by an ancestral background whose most probable deviation is zero [PAGE 105].

Multi-week traps arise when this generational clock is mishandled. First, the stationarity trap: treating P as fixed when nutrition, disease, and secular trends shift the population mean over decadal scales, so remote ancestry is centered on a P that no longer matches the contemporary reference. Second, the aggregation trap: collapsing prod_g r_g into a single beta hides heterogeneity across developmental stages; early-life and adult environments may apply different retention factors. Third, the reification trap: mistaking the conditional expectation E[D_offspring | D_MP] = beta D_MP for an active causal force that pulls individuals downward over time. Regression is a statement about the recentering of a distribution, not a deterministic clock that acts on a single lineage week by week.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory-of-mind problem here is how an observer models a system whose current state is itself a diluted product of ancestral states. Crisis habit regression is the tendency, under stress or measurement, for an expressed trait to revert toward the population attractor P because the remote ancestral contribution carries zero expected deviation [PAGE 105]. In Galton's terms, the observed mid-parental deviation D_MP is the "wine," and the crisis strips away situational accentuation, exposing the underlying wine-and-water mixture whose strength is beta D_MP.

The counter-trap is over-correction: an observer who knows about regression may assume that deviation will vanish quickly, setting beta too low, while neglecting that the mid-parent is already a diluted composite of its own ancestors. The correct recursive step recognizes that D_MP itself has been compressed by prior r_g < 1 operations, so the signal is weaker than the raw genealogy suggests but not yet zero.

The clock is the generational count G between mid-parent and observation. Over a short clock (G = 1), retention r_g may be near one and deviation persists; over a long clock (G large), the product prod_g r_g drives D_trans toward zero unless offset by selection or environmental trend [PAGE 105]. The recursive danger is phase confusion: predicting immediate reversion when the model specifies multi-generational dilution, or predicting persistence when several retention stages have already compressed the deviation. A well-formed counter-model must track the clock's phase, the stationarity of P, and whether the observer is conditioning on the true mid-parent or on an error-contaminated proxy.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Human stature is bounded by growth-plate closure, endocrine limits, and nutritional ceilings. Galton's linear dilution D_trans = beta D_MP cannot operate indefinitely near these bounds. If the predicted offspring deviation places stature beyond the attainable physiological maximum (e.g., extreme values where skeletal allometry saturates), the linear law vetoes itself; the deviation is truncated rather than proportionately diluted. This is a physical saturation veto, distinct from a statistical one.

Biomechanical layer: Stature deviations do not map isometrically onto all biomechanical derivatives. If the trait of interest is stride length, center-of-mass height, or bone robusticity rather than stature itself, the beta estimated from stature [PAGE 105] does not transfer without a trait-specific Jacobian and genetic-covariance adjustment. Veto occurs when the target trait lacks the same additive structure as the stature data on which Galton's dilution metaphor is built.

Weather / environmental layer: Although Galton's text does not discuss weather, environmental variance (nutrition, disease, climate-driven food security) acts as a time-varying shift in P. A famine or epidemic between the remote-ancestry generation and the observation generation changes the reference mean, so E[D_A,g] = P_remote - P_current != 0, violating the zero-deviation premise [PAGE 105]. This environmental non-stationarity is a veto: the "water" poured into the wine is no longer zero-centered because the ancestral population mean has shifted.

Veto adjudication rule: Accept Galton's proportionate-dilution model only if (i) panmixia holds, (ii) P is stationary across the genealogical window, (iii) the trait is stature or a linearly scaled proxy with constant beta, and (iv) |D_MP| lies well within the non-saturated physiological range. Any one violation triggers a veto and requires a non-stationary, bounded, or multivariate extension.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions:

Scenario 1 — Assortative-mating violation. Construct a counterfactual subpopulation with strict positive assortative mating by stature (tall with tall, short with short). Galton's premise requires remote ancestry to be a random sample with zero expected deviation [PAGE 105]; under assortment, tall lineages have E[D_A,g] > 0. Prediction: the observed offspring–mid-parent regression slope beta_hat will exceed the panmictic Galtonian beta, and the ancestral-mean test will reject E[D_A,g] = 0. Scenario 2 — Secular trend / non-stationary P. Counterfactual: population mean stature rises by delta > 0 per generation due to nutrition. Remote ancestors are centered on P_{t-g} = P_t - g delta, so D_A,g = -delta g != 0 [PAGE 105]. Prediction: regressing offspring on mid-parent without a cohort intercept produces a spuriously high slope (pseudo-persistence) because the diluting "water" carries a negative deviation.
