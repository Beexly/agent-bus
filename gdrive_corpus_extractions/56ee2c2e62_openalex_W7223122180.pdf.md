1. Dataset-shift operators [PAGE 2]. Let X be the game-state feature vector and Y ∈ {0,1} the scoring label.
- Prior shift: P_t(Y) ≠ P_s(Y).
- Covariate shift: P_t(X) ≠ P_s(X).

Verbatim: "Prior shift occurs when P_t(Y) ≠ P_s(Y)… Covariate shift occurs when P_t(X) ≠ P_s(X)… Concept shift occurs when P_t(Y│X) ≠ P_s(Y│X)" [PAGE 2]. These are dimensionless probability statements; the paper explicitly notes they "co-occur" [PAGE 2], so no single-shift importance-weighting correction is valid.

2. VAEP target and classifier [PAGE 8–9]. Game state s_t = (a_{t−2}, a_{t−1}, a_t); label
$$ y_t = \mathbb{1}\{\text{team in possession after } a_t \text{ scores within the next } k \text{ actions}\},\quad k=10, $$
$$ p_\theta(y_t = 1 \mid s_t) = f_\theta(x_t). $$
f_θ is XGBoost, an additive tree ensemble (Chen & Guestrin [11]):
$$ f_\theta(x) = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \gamma_m \, \mathbb{1}\{x \in R_m\}, $$
where R_m are leaf regions, γ_m leaf weights, M trees. Units: probability ∈ [0,1] after the logistic link. Feature dimension d = 138 [PAGE 4].

3. VAEP valuation lineage (Decroos et al. [4]). The action-value the scores label feeds is
$$ \Delta V(a_t) = \big[P(\text{score}_{t+1}) - P(\text{score}_t)\big] - \big[P(\text{concede}_{t+1}) - P(\text{concede}_t)\big], $$
dimensionless probability delta. The paper reports "results for the scores target" only [PAGE 9]; the concedes term is dropped, so the reported ΔV is one-sided.

4. Metrics [PAGE 10].
- ROC-AUC: $ \mathrm{AUC} = P\!\left(f(X^+) > f(X^-)\right) $, invariant to π; Gini = 2·AUC − 1.
- PR-AUC: area under (recall, precision), with precision = TP/(TP+FP), recall = TP/(TP+FN); random baseline = prevalence π.
- Brier (proper scoring, [15]): $ BS = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N (f_i - o_i)^2 $, dimensionless, range [0,1]. Murphy decomposition: $ BS = \underbrace{\text{Reliability}}_{+} - \underbrace{\text{Resolution}}_{-} + \underbrace{\bar\pi(1-\bar\pi)}_{\text{Uncertainty}} $. Uncertainty floor at π=0.012 is ≈ 0.0119.
- Log-loss: $ L = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_i [y_i \log p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)] $, units nats (log₂ → bits).
- $ F_1 = \dfrac{2\,\mathrm{Prec}\cdot\mathrm{Rec}}{\mathrm{Prec}+\mathrm{Rec}} $, threshold chosen by max-F1 on validation grid [PAGE 10].

5. Covariate-shift clustering operator [PAGE 6]. K-Means inertia minimized:
$$ J = \sum_{j=1}^{K}\sum_{x\in C_j}\lVert x - \mu_j\rVert_2^2,\qquad \mu_j = \frac{1}{|C_j|}\sum_{x\in C_j} x, $$
over standardized pass features (start (x,y), length, two unit directional components). League distinctiveness via z-score:
$$ z_{l,c} = \frac{f_{l,c} - \mu_c}{\sigma_c}, $$
with f_{l,c} the frequency of cluster c in league l relative to league total.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Modeling the adversary and the self under three axes: crisis-habit regression, counter-trap, and the clock.

Crisis-habit regression. A source-trained booster encodes La Liga's scoring "grammar." The concept-shift figure is explicit: "pass -> dribble -> shot" is ~18–19% of goal-ending triplets in every league, but "dribble -> cross -> shot" is "more prominent in La Liga and the Bundesliga than in Serie A or the Premier League," and "dribble -> foul -> shot_penalty" is "more visible in Serie A" [PAGE 8]. A model that has only seen La Liga will, under a crisis (a rare, high-value state), regress to its learned prior probability for those La Liga-weighted motifs and mis-rank a Serie A penalty-precursor or a Ligue 1 "take_on -> dribble -> shot" pattern. The recursive failure is second-order: the model's error is concentrated exactly where the *conditional* P(Y│X) diverges most, i.e., in the low-prevalence tail that PR-AUC rewards.

Counter-trap. The opponent's defensive organization is the unmodeled mind. The paper admits the representation "does not yet exploit richer off-ball or 360 positional context" [PAGE 13]. So the model reasons about a possession as if the pressing structure were La Liga's — it cannot represent that Serie A's "compact clusters around midfield" [PAGE 7] suppress the same on-ball triplet. This is a theory-of-mind gap: the model has no variable for the defender's intent, so concept shift is invisible to it except as label noise. Fine-tuning is the paper's crude counter-move — it lets a few target labels re-weight leaf values — but with 10–30% game budgets and 5 seeds it barely moves the needle (see THINK_TRACE).

The clock. Two clocks operate. The micro-clock is k=10 actions [PAGE 9]: the horizon that sets π ≈ 0.012 [PAGE 11]. The macro-clock is temporal drift: La Liga fitted Δ = +1.10, +0.55, +0.05 goals/game across 2010/11–2020/21 windows [PAGE 5]. Critically, the excluded source seasons 2019/20 and 2020/21 [PAGE 9] fall in the last, near-flat window (Δ = +0.05) but coincide with pandemic-era empty stadiums — an environmental perturbation of the scoring prior the paper does not isolate. So the time-shift regime's target is confounded: it mixes genuine tactical drift with a one-off venue shock. A rigorous reader treats the time-shift panel [PAGE 12a] as the least interpretable of the four.

Recursive summary: the model regresses to source habits under crisis, cannot represent the adversarial counter-structure it never sees, and is raced by two clocks (k-horizon and multi-season drift) that jointly guarantee the sub-σ transfer effects observed.

Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof against the pipeline.

Physical/biomechanical layer. SPADL fixes attributes "time, location, player, team, action type, body part, and result" [PAGE 3], and the 138 features include "action-type, result, body-part, location, movement, game-score, and timing covariates" [PAGE 4]. So single-action biomechanics — body part (foot/head), pass length, movement — are represented. A veto check: can the model assign high score-probability to physically infeasible states? Partially guarded: pass length and start/end coordinates bound geometric reachability (K-Means over standardized pass start, length, direction [PAGE 6]). But the representation is *per-team-attacking, re-oriented left-to-right* [PAGE 4] — it discards absolute pitch orientation and all defender coordinates. Verdict: on-ball feasibility is partially enforced; ball-flight physics and shot mechanics are only proxied by action-type, not modeled.

Defensive-barrier veto (the decisive one). The physical barrier that determines whether a triplet converts is the opposing defensive block. The paper states plainly that the representation "does not yet exploit richer off-ball or 360 positional context" [PAGE 13]. Therefore the model literally cannot see the physical constraint that separates a La Liga "dribble -> cross -> shot" from a Serie A sequence into a compact midfield block [PAGE 7]. This is a hard veto on the concept-shift claim being *model-internal*: the empirical concept shift is demonstrated in Figure 4 [PAGE 8] by counting triplets, but the classifier has no feature that encodes the defender, so its P(Y│X) shift is only partially observable. The authors' own future-work item — "360-derived features, sequence models, and graph-based models" [PAGE 13–14] — is an admission that the physical layer is under-specified.

Weather/environmental veto. Nowhere does the 15-page text mention weather, pitch surface, temperature, or wind. There is no operator for it. Any claim that the model captures the full data-generating process is vetoed on environmental grounds: pass success and finishing are weather-sensitive, and the 2019/20–2020/21 held-out seasons [PAGE 9] carry a venue/attendance shock (empty stadiums) that is neither weather nor tactics but an environmental prior perturbation. This confound is uncontrolled, so the time-shift and time–league-shift panels [PAGE 12] inherit an unmodeled environmental term.

Adjudication. Layer 1 (on-ball biomechanics): pass, with location/length/body-part coverage. Layer 2 (defensive geometry): fail — no 360/off-ball input. Layer 3 (environment/weather): fail — no representation, plus a pandemic confound in two target seasons. Net: the physical-to-environmental stack is only ~1/3 specified, which bounds how much of the observed transfer gap can be mechanistically explained versus absorbed as irreducible label noise. This does not invalidate the paper's benchmarking contribution, but it caps the interpretability of "why" transfer fails.

Three verifiable counterfactuals, each falsifiable against a re-run, with concrete predictions.

CF-1 — Adversarial-validation probe of covariate shift. Add a domain classifier distinguishing source La Liga from each target league, and compute its AUC (a discrepancy proxy, in the spirit of [10]). Prediction: ROC-AUC transfer loss correlates positively with domain-classifier AUC, and is largest for the league most divergent in the Figure 3 passing-z-score space — the Bundesliga ("distinctive long-pass patterns… vertical and diagonal progressions" [PAGE 6]). Falsification: if the most style-divergent league (Bundesliga) shows the *smallest* target ROC-AUC drop, the covariate-shift attribution is wrong. Current-data check: this is directly testable and would adjudicate whether covariate shift, not prior shift, drives the 0.89→0.81 ROC-AUC fall.

CF-2 — Per-target isotonic/Platt recalibration. Fit post-hoc isotonic regression [13] on each target's validation split. Prediction: target-only's Brier advantage (0.0305/0.0327/0.0325 vs fine-tune 0.0354/0.0362/0.0356, [PAGE 13]) collapses to a tie once fine-tuned probabilities are recalibrated per league, while PR-AUC ordering is unchanged (recalibration is monotone, so AUC is invariant). Falsification: if recalibration changes PR-AUC ranking, the pipeline has a threshold/ordering bug. This isolates calibration as a separable, fixable defect — confirming the paper's "calibration remains an open issue" [PAGE 13] is a post-hoc fix, not a modeling dead-end.

CF-3 — Horizon expansion k: 10 → larger. Increase the prediction horizon. Prediction: prevalence π rises, the PR-AUC random baseline rises with it, per-match positive counts stabilize, and the fine-tune-minus-source PR-AUC gap grows past 2σ, converting the current sub-σ "consistent but tiny" result [PAGE 13] into a statistically robust effect. Falsification: if the gap stays sub-σ at larger k, the warm-start gain is genuinely marginal and the abstract's "consistent gains" [PAGE 1] should be softened to "competitive."

Auxiliary veto tied to seasons. Re-run the time-shift regime with 2019/20 and 2020/21 (excluded from source, used as target [PAGE 9]) separated from clean seasons. Prediction: those two seasons show the largest prior-shift residual and worst zero-shot PR-AUC, because empty-stadium conditions perturbed the scoring environment (an environmental confound flagged in CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION). If they do *not* underperform clean held-out seasons, the environmental-confound hypothesis is vetoed and the drift can be read as purely tactical.

Bottom line (no betting framing, pure methodology): the paper's contribution — football as a multi-shift transfer benchmark [PAGE 1] — is sound; its fine-tuning claim is directionally real but statistically sub-σ in the global setting and even reverses at 10% budget on PR-AUC. The three counterfactuals above are cheap to run and would either harden or retract the headline.

Summary: I audited Clissa & Macaluso's transfer-learning benchmark against its own tables. The strongest empirical claim — "consistent" fine-tuning gains over zero-shot — is fragile: in Table 3 [PAGE 13] the fine-tune-minus-source PR-AUC gap is −0.0009/+0.0001/+0.0006 across the 10/20/30% budgets, all sub-standard-deviation and sign-inconsistent. The 0.89→0.81 ROC-AUC transfer drop is attributable to covariate/concept shift, not the prior shift the paper foregrounds, because ROC-AUC is rank-invariant to P(Y). Calibration (Brier) decouples from discrimination as predicted by proper-scoring theory. The pipeline under-specifies the physical layer: it omits 360/off-ball and weather context and carries an uncontrolled pandemic-season confound in two held-out target seasons. Three falsifiable re-runs (domain-classifier discrepancy, per-target isotonic recalibration, horizon expansion) would confirm or retract the headline effect.
