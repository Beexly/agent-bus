**Derivation spine.** The paper poses re-identification as a Bayesian decision problem with two conditionally independent evidence channels: an appearance channel $A$ (SDALF, weighted region covariance) and a structural channel $R$ (positional role inside a learned formation). The generative story is clean: identity $ID$ causes appearance; formation context causes role; role is stochastically coupled to identity through a coach-designated system of play. The identification rule is therefore a MAP query over $13$ enrolled identities:

$$\hat{ID} = \arg\max_{i}\; P(A\mid ID_i)\,P(R\mid ID_i)\,P(ID_i).$$

The paper's "weighted summation" [[PAGE 2]] is the log-linear special case of this, so the fusion is a tempered product of experts, not a learned joint model. That distinction matters: nothing in the paper estimates $P(R\mid ID)$ from data in a calibrated way; instead it constructs a hard table (Table I) and a confusion-derived dissimilarity.

**Spectral-gap audit of the appearance channel.** The covariance descriptor is $C_R=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(z_k-\mu)(z_k-\mu)^{\top}$ with $z_k=[x,y,R_{xy},G_{xy},B_{xy}]$ [[PAGE 5]]. Units are mixed by construction: $xx$ and $yy$ entries in $\text{px}^2$, colour–colour entries in normalized-intensity$^2$, cross terms in $\text{px}\cdot\text{intensity}$. This inhomogeneity is not a bug per se — the Förstner–Moonen distance $\rho=\sqrt{\sum_i \ln^2\lambda_i(C_1,C_2)}$ [[PAGE 5]] is invariant to a common linear reparametrization of feature space, so the unit mixing is absorbed. It is, however, *not* invariant to per-channel rescaling, which is why the authors' omission of gradients [[PAGE 5, "Gradient features were not used as they were found to decreased performance, due to large variations in player pose"] is effectively a variance-suppression step on the most pose-sensitive channel. The honest reading: gradients were removed because pose variance swamped identity variance, i.e. the channel had negative signal-to-noise for this task. That is a domain statement, not a general one.

**Why the appearance channel collapses — an information argument.** For a uniformed team, the mutual information $I(ID;A)$ is bounded above by the residual entropy of the non-uniform visible traits the authors enumerate: hair, skin, shoe colour [[PAGE 4]]. Call that effective alphabet size $m$. With $13$ identities and near-identical kits, $m$ is small; the paper's own empirical claim is that "both appearance features, SDALF and weighted covariance grid features, perform similarly poorly" [[PAGE 7]]. So $I(ID;A)\approx 0$ in the low-resolution regime, and the entire discriminative burden shifts to $I(ID;R)$.

**Cardinality bound on the role channel — the paper's central structural fact.** Role alphabet $|\mathcal{R}|=10$ (LB, RB, LH, CH, RH, IL, IR, LW, CF, RW) [[PAGE 5]]. Identity alphabet $|ID|=13$ (Table I lists 13 IDs across 10 roles) [[PAGE 6]]. Hence $H(ID)=\log_2 13\approx 3.700$ bits while channel capacity is $H(R)\le\log_2 10\approx 3.322$ bits, giving $H(ID\mid R)\ge 0.378$ bits. Fano's inequality, $H(ID\mid R)\le H_b(P_e)+P_e\log_2 12$, then forces a *floor on role-only error*: solving $H_b(P_e)+3.585P_e\ge0.378$ yields $P_e\gtrsim 0.04$, i.e. role-only accuracy $\le\sim96\%$ under the weakest possible assumption. Under the stronger assumption of equal image mass per identity, the pigeonhole structure (three doubled roles: CH$=\{23,26\}$, IL$=\{9,28\}$, LW$=\{15,27\}$) caps accuracy at $10/13\approx76.9\%$. The reported manual-role figure of $84.5\%$ [[PAGE 8 caption]] sits *between* those bounds. That is only possible if the 94-image evaluation set does not carry equal mass on the six substituted identities — a decomposition the paper never publishes. This is the single largest gray zone in the paper: the headline number is not decomposable from the text alone.

**Gray-zone audit of the assignment operator.** The role operator is stated as "we learn a linear mapping function to assign each person to a 'role' or position within the group structure" [[PAGE 2]], but the operative object is $r_t=x_t p_t$ with $x_t$ a $10\times10$ permutation matrix, obtained by minimizing $\lVert\hat r-x_t p_t\rVert_2^2$ [[PAGE 6]]. This is *combinatorial selection over $S_{10}$*, not estimation of a continuous linear operator; the "learning" is confined to choosing a prototype $\hat r$ from 25,000 labeled frames via mean and covariance matching. Conflating the two is a terminology slip that obscures where the inductive bias lives — and it lives entirely in the prototype bank. The prototype bank is a prior over formations; if that bank was drawn from the same match distribution as the evaluation, the 66.0% automatic role accuracy [[PAGE 6]] is an in-distribution number, not a generalization number.

**Second gray zone: inverse-variance weighting can be pathological.** Eq. (4) weights each cell by $1/(\sigma_{A,m}+\sigma_{B,m})$ with $\sigma_{i,m}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k\ne i}\rho^2(C_{i,m},C_{k,m})$ [[PAGE 5]]. For a cell that is genuinely non-discriminative — the team's uniform torso — inter-subject variance $\sigma\to 0$ and the weight *diverges*. The scheme rewards cells for differing across subjects but has no floor, so a near-constant cell receives unbounded influence and injects pure noise at maximum gain. A regularized form $1/(\sigma_{A,m}+\sigma_{B,m}+\epsilon)$ is required for well-posedness; no $\epsilon$ is specified.

**Third gray zone: fusion weights are unreported.** The "weighted summation" is never parameterized in the text, so the claimed "minor improvement" [[PAGE 7]] is not reproducible, and its magnitude is not stated numerically anywhere — the evidence is a CMC curve in Figure 9 whose ordinate values are not tabulated. A reader cannot distinguish a real gain from a within-noise gain.

**Fourth: the role dissimilarity is not a metric.** Roles are compared by $D=J-P$ where $P$ is the average assignment-probability matrix and $J$ is all-ones [[PAGE 6]]. Since $P$ is a confusion matrix, $D_{ii}=1-P_{ii}\ne 0$ generally, and the triangle inequality does not hold. Downstream algorithms that assume metricity (nearest-neighbour, triangle-inequality pruning) are unjustified.

**Fifth: statistical power.** Evaluation uses 94 images, 13 players, two randomly drawn images per player, averaged over 100 experiments [[PAGE 7]]. With ~7 images per player the distinct gallery/test pair count is ~42 per player, so the 100 trials resample a pool of bounded size; effective $N_{\text{eff}}\lesssim 546$, giving a 1σ floor on Rank-1 of $\sqrt{0.674\cdot0.326/546}\approx2.0\%$. The gap between 67.4% (automatic roles) and 84.5% (manual roles) is far outside that, so it is real; the fusion gain is not obviously so.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**O1 — Region covariance descriptor** [[PAGE 5, Eq. 1–2]]

$$C_R=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(z_k-\mu\right)\left(z_k-\mu\right)^{\top},\qquad z_k=\left[x,\;y,\;R_{xy},\;G_{xy},\;B_{xy}\right]$$

Units: $C_R\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times5}$ with block units $\text{px}^2$ (spatial), $\text{intensity}^2$ (colour, normalized $[0,1]$), $\text{px}\cdot\text{intensity}$ (cross). Assumptions: $n\ge 2$; pixels i.i.d. within a cell. Verbatim anchor: *"Each pixel in the region is represented by a point in feature space (e.g. the spatial coordinates of the pixel, its colour, and gradients)"* [[PAGE 5]].

*Breakdown regime:* $n=1\Rightarrow$ division by zero. $n<6\Rightarrow \operatorname{rank}(C_R)\le n-1<5$, hence at least one zero eigenvalue, which propagates into O2 as $\ln\lambda\to-\infty$. Mandatory regularization $C_R+\epsilon I$, $\epsilon>0$.

**O2 — Förstner–Moonen covariance metric** [[PAGE 5, Eq. 3]]

$$\rho(C_1,C_2)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln^2\lambda_i\!\left(C_1,C_2\right)}$$

where $\{\lambda_i\}$ are the generalized eigenvalues solving $C_1 v=\lambda C_2 v$. Units: $\lambda_i$ dimensionless (ratio of like-united quadratic forms), $\ln\lambda_i$ in nats, $\rho$ dimensionless. Assumptions: $C_2$ positive definite; $C_1,C_2$ same dimension.

*Breakdown regime:* (i) $C_2$ singular $\Rightarrow$ generalized eigenproblem undefined; (ii) $\lambda_i\to 0$ or $\lambda_i\to\infty$ drives $\rho\to\infty$ — this is the *legitimate* large-distance signal but is numerically catastrophic for near-singular covariances; (iii) $\lambda_i=1\;\forall i\Rightarrow\rho=0$: two regions with identical second-order statistics up to scale are declared identical, which is exactly the uniform-kit degeneracy.

**O3 — Variance-normalized cell-aggregate distance** [[PAGE 5, Eq. 4–5]]

$$\text{Distance}(A,B)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\rho\!\left(C_{A,m},C_{B,m}\right)}{\sigma_{A,m}+\sigma_{B,m}},\qquad \sigma_{i,m}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k\ne i}\rho^2\!\left(C_{i,m},C_{k,m}\right)$$

Units: $\rho$ dimensionless $\Rightarrow$ $\sigma_{i,m}$ dimensionless (nats$^2$ in the squared form, but the sum is over subjects, not eigenvalues, so the reported quantity is a variance of a dimensionless distance). Assumption: inter-subject variance is a valid proxy for discriminativeness.

*Breakdown regime:* $\sigma_{A,m}+\sigma_{B,m}\to 0$ on any cell that is constant across the enrolled population — the denominator vanishes and the weight diverges. This is a genuine inverse-variance pathology, not a rounding artifact: it hands maximum weight to the *least* informative cell. Regularization $\sigma+\epsilon$ is required.

**O4 — Role assignment as a linear assignment problem** [[PAGE 6, Eq. 6–7]]

$$x_t^{*}=\arg\min_{x_t}\left\lVert\hat r-x_t p_t\right\rVert_2^2,\qquad C(i,j)=\left\lVert\hat r(i)-p_t(j)\right\rVert_2$$

with $p_t=[x_1,y_1,\dots,x_{10},y_{10}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{20}$ in metres (pitch frame), $\hat r\in\mathbb{R}^{20}$ the selected prototype formation in metres, $x_t\in\{0,1\}^{10\times10}$ a permutation matrix satisfying $\sum_j x_t(i,j)=1\;\forall i$ and $\sum_i x_t(i,j)=1\;\forall j$. Objective units: $\text{m}^2$. Cost entries: $\text{m}$. Solver: Hungarian, $O(n^3)$.

*Breakdown regime:* (i) *tie degeneracy* — if $C(i,j)=C(i,j')$ the optimal permutation is non-unique and the returned $x_t$ is arbitrary; this is precisely the reported midfield ambiguity, *"sometimes the roles are wrongly classified due to ambiguity in the formation or roles, particularly in the midfield (RH,IL,IR)"* [[PAGE 6]]; (ii) *prototype misspecification* — if the true formation is not within the span of the 25,000-frame bank, every $C(i,j)$ is biased and the optimum is systematically wrong, with high confidence; (iii) *substitution aliasing* — the permutation is over 10 field slots, but the identity set has 13 members, so $x_t$ is injective on players and many-to-one on identities, guaranteeing irrecoverable collisions.

**O5 — Role-to-role dissimilarity (non-metric)** [[PAGE 6]]

$$D=J-P,\qquad P_{ij}=\Pr\!\left(\text{role }i\text{ assigned}\mid\text{true role }j\right)$$

Units: dimensionless. *Failure:* $D_{ii}=1-P_{ii}\neq 0$ absent perfect assignment; symmetry and triangle inequality are not guaranteed, so $D$ is a dissimilarity, not a metric.

**O6 — Identification accuracy figures (measured, not derived)** [[PAGE 6, Fig. 8 caption; PAGE 6]]

$$\text{Acc}_{\text{manual roles}}=0.845,\qquad \text{Acc}_{\text{auto roles}}=0.674,\qquad \text{Acc}_{\text{role assignment}}=0.660\;(0.590\;\text{Part 1},\;0.690\;\text{Part 2})$$

Verbatim: *"The automatic role assignment has an accuracy of 66.0%."* [[PAGE 6]]. Note the ceiling argument in `<THINK_TRACE>`: with $|\mathcal{R}|=10<13=|ID|$, role-only accuracy is structurally bounded under equal image mass.

**Backward lineage (as cited by the paper).** The appearance branch runs Gheissari et al. triangulated-graph region comparison → Hamdoun et al. SURF interest points → Gray & Tao's Ensemble of Localized Features with AdaBoost → Bąk et al. Haar/DCD → Prosser et al. RankSVM reformulation → Schwartz et al. PLS latent projection → Bąk et al. The sports-context branch runs Liu et al. context-conditioned motion models → Lucey et al. player-role representation → Lu et al. CRF over SIFT/MSER/colour with jersey-number resolution [[PAGE 3]]. The single prior work on group context is Zheng et al. [[PAGE 3]], which models the *appearance* of a group; this paper's delta is to model the *structure* of a group — a genuine conceptual fork.

**The fork's consequence.** Zheng's group descriptor and Bialkowski's role descriptor are duals: one treats the group as a bag of co-occurring appearance words, the other as a permutation-valued latent variable. The permutation view is the one that survives uniform clothing, because it conditions on geometry rather than photometry. That is the paper's durable contribution, and it is the reason the role channel outperforms both appearance descriptors [[PAGE 7]].

**Forward genealogy (post-2013, extrapolated — not in the source).** The role-as-latent-permutation idea is the direct ancestor of modern multi-agent trajectory forecasting and of set-equivariant architectures where a permutation matrix is replaced by a soft Sinkhorn coupling. The paper's hard-permutation Hungarian step is the discrete limit of an optimal-transport layer; the 66.0% ceiling is precisely the cost of hard commitment without a differentiable relaxation.

**Multi-week traps.** The paper evaluates on one match. Four traps appear at longer horizons and are all invisible in-sample. (1) *Roster turnover*: substitutes already break role-only identification within a single match [[PAGE 6]]; across weeks the role→identity table (Table I) decays entirely, and any deployment that hard-codes it fails silently. (2) *Tactical drift*: the 5:3:2 formation [[PAGE 5]] is a prior; a coach who switches to 4:4:2 invalidates the prototype bank and every cost entry in Eq. (7) becomes biased in a correlated, non-random direction. (3) *Kit/venue drift*: the LAB-histogram team assignment [[PAGE 4]] is calibrated to yellow-tops/green-skirts under one stadium's lighting; a kit change or a night fixture shifts the histogram modes and collapses the group-detection stage before role assignment is ever reached. (4) *Camera-network drift*: the eight fixed HD cameras [[PAGE 4]] are assumed calibrated; any pan/tilt/refocus invalidates the proximity-based merge and therefore the top-down formation estimate that everything downstream depends on.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Level-0 — the player's physical position.** Observable, 2D pitch coordinates, metres. This is what the detector reports.

**Level-1 — the model's belief about the player's role.** The model believes $r_t=x_t p_t$ with $x_t$ the minimizer of Eq. (6). This belief is *confident and wrong* in the midfield: the cost matrix has near-ties on {RH, IL, IR}, the Hungarian solver picks one arbitrarily, and nothing in the pipeline emits an uncertainty flag. The reported 66.0% is an average over a distribution in which the errors are *structured*, not uniform.

**Level-2 — the coach's belief about the model's belief (counter-trap).** A coach who knows the system is role-conditioned can attack the prior directly. Formalize as a Stackelberg game: the model commits to a prototype $\hat r$ (leader); the coach selects a formation perturbation $\delta$ (follower) maximizing the cost of the true permutation,

$$\delta^{*}=\arg\max_{\delta}\;\left[\min_{x\in S_{10}}\left\lVert\hat r-x\left(p_t+\delta\right)\right\rVert_2^2-\left\lVert\hat r-x^{\star}\left(p_t+\delta\right)\right\rVert_2^2\right],$$

i.e. maximize the suboptimality gap between the solver's choice and the true assignment. The trap is *cheap for the coach* — a deliberate overlap run or a positional swap in midfield is within normal play — and *maximally damaging for the model*, because the failure is systematic. The paper contains no level-2 term; it assumes the formation is drawn from the same distribution as the 25,000-frame bank.

**Crisis-habit regression.** Under distribution shift (new kit, new formation, new venue), the fusion collapses toward the appearance channel — the channel the paper itself shows to be near-useless [[PAGE 7, "perform similarly poorly"]]. The regression is a habit because the weighting was tuned in-distribution; out of distribution, the appearance term contributes noise at full gain while the role term contributes bias. The predicted signature is a *confident* wrong answer, not a low-confidence one.

**Clock.** Two clocks matter and the paper runs only one. The match clock governs substitutions; the paper's Table I is static across the match, justified by *"we assume that each player has an 'assigned role'"* [[PAGE 6]]. That assumption is exactly the clock-blindness: at the substitution instant, the role persists but the occupant changes, and the model has no temporal gate to detect it. The second clock is the season clock — the multi-week drift catalogued above.

**Recursive audit of the ground truth itself.** The 84.5% figure is measured against *expert manual role labels* [[PAGE 6]]. The expert's labeling is itself a level-1 theory of mind — the expert's belief about what role the player believed they were playing. Where the expert and the Hungarian solver disagree, the paper attributes error entirely to the solver. The 66.0% → 84.5% gap is therefore an upper bound on solver error and a lower bound on *disagreement*, which includes expert ambiguity in exactly the midfield band the paper flags. This is not a fatal objection, but it means the automatic role extractor is being scored against a noisy oracle whose noise is concentrated where the paper's errors are concentrated — the two are confounded.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Claim.** Any candidate identity assignment can be rejected without appeal to appearance, on physical, kinematic, and radiometric grounds alone. Three layer-specific vetoes, each with a proof obligation.

**V1 — Kinematic veto (biomechanical layer).** For a candidate match between observation $\mathcal{O}_1$ of subject $i$ at $t_1$ and $\mathcal{O}_2$ of subject $j$ at $t_2$, define

$$V_{\text{kin}}=\mathbb{1}\!\left[\left\lVert \mathbf{x}_j(t_2)-\mathbf{x}_i(t_1)\right\rVert_2>v_{\max}\,(t_2-t_1)\right],$$

with $\mathbf{x}$ in metres, $t$ in seconds, and $v_{\max}$ the elite field-hockey sprint bound (order $9\text{–}10\ \text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$). *Soundness:* the true identity satisfies the bound by physical necessity, so $V_{\text{kin}}=0$ on the truth with probability 1 absent measurement error; with position error $\epsilon_x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_x^2)$, the false-veto probability is $\Phi\!\left(-\!\left(v_{\max}\Delta t-d\right)/\sigma_x\sqrt{2}\right)$, negligible for $\Delta t\gtrsim1\ \text{s}$ and $\sigma_x\lesssim0.3\ \text{m}$. *Relevance here:* the paper's multi-camera merge is "based on proximity" [[PAGE 4]] with no kinematic gate, so proximity merging can fuse two distinct players within the merge radius into one track — a violation of V1 that the pipeline never checks.

**V2 — Resolution/Nyquist veto (physical layer).** Player heights are 40–100 px and patches are rescaled to $96\times50$ [[PAGE 3, PAGE 4]]. A jersey digit stroke of physical width $w\approx2\text{–}3\ \text{cm}$ on a $1.7\ \text{m}$ player imaged at $h$ pixels subtends $s=w\,h/1.7$ px; at $h=40$, $s\approx0.5\text{–}0.7$ px, below the Nyquist limit of 2 px per resolvable stroke. This is the formal justification for the paper's own statement that low resolution makes *"digit recognition on the jerseys near impossible"* [[PAGE 2]]. Veto rule: any identity claim whose sole support is sub-Nyquist glyph information is rejected. Consequence: the evaluator cannot appeal to jersey numbers as an oracle at this resolution, which is why the paper's ground truth is positional rather than glyph-based — and why the 84.5% ceiling cannot be externally audited from the same imagery.

**V3 — Photometric/weather veto (radiometric layer).** Team assignment uses a colour histogram in LAB [[PAGE 4]]. Define the CIE76 separation $\Delta E_{76}=\lVert \mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{b}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_1}-\mathbf{L}^{*}\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{b}^{*}_{\mathcal{O}_2}\rVert_2$. Veto appearance evidence when $\Delta E_{76}$ exceeds the 99th percentile of the *within-identity* $\Delta E$ distribution: the variation is then attributable to illumination, not identity. This is warranted by the authors' own admission of *"significant illumination variations between observations of each subject"* in outdoor capture [[PAGE 2]] and by their global normalization step, *"we normalise by scaling the mean illumination and applying histogram equalisation"* [[PAGE 4]] — a global operator that is destabilized by a bright sky fraction in the crop. Under sun/cloud transition, $\Delta E$ can exceed the between-team separation and flip the LAB-histogram team label, which propagates as a *group-level* error upstream of role assignment.

**Adjudication order.** V1 is applied first (cheapest, hardest constraint, soundness provable). V2 second (rejects evidence that cannot exist). V3 third (rejects evidence that exists but is attributable to a nuisance variable). Only survivors reach the appearance/role fusion. The paper applies none of the three explicitly, which is why its errors are not localized to a single layer and cannot be attributed from the reported aggregates alone.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Scenario 1 — Prototype-label permutation test (falsifies the role prior).**
*Intervention:* take the 25,000-frame labeled prototype bank [[PAGE 6]] and apply a uniformly random permutation to the role labels within each frame. Re-run the Hungarian assignment and the full 94-image evaluation unchanged.
*Prediction:* automatic role accuracy collapses to the chance rate for 10 roles, $0.10$, with binomial spread; identity accuracy collapses from 67.4% toward the appearance-only Rank-1 level visible in Figure 9 [[PAGE 7]].
*Decision rule:* if role accuracy remains materially above 0.10 under permuted labels, the role channel's reported accuracy is attributable to something other than the prototype prior (e.g. leakage from the evaluation frames into the bank), and the paper's central claim is vetoed. *Verifiable metric:* role accuracy and CMC Rank-1, both already reported quantities.

**Scenario 2 — Midfield-restricted evaluation (falsifies the locality of the error).**
*Intervention:* partition the 94-image evaluation set by ground-truth role and re-report both accuracies restricted to $\{\text{RH},\text{IL},\text{IR}\}$.
*Prediction:* subset role accuracy falls substantially below the 66.0% aggregate, and subset identity Rank-1 falls below 67.4%, because the paper explicitly localizes its confusion there: *"sometimes the roles are wrongly classified due to ambiguity in the formation or roles, particularly in the midfield (RH,IL,IR)"* [[PAGE 6]].
*Decision rule:* if the midfield subset accuracy meets or exceeds the aggregate, the stated ambiguity claim is falsified and the confusion matrix in Figure 7 has been misread. *Verifiable metric:* per-role accuracy from the Figure 7 confusion matrix, recomputed on the subset.

**Scenario 3 — Substitution-pair resolution with an external identity channel.**
*Intervention:* supply a verified identity channel (manual IDs, or jersey numbers captured at a second, higher-resolution camera) for the six substituted identities occupying the three doubled roles CH$=\{23,26\}$, IL$=\{9,28\}$, LW$=\{15,27\}$ [[PAGE 6, Table I]].
*Prediction:* identity accuracy on those six rises from the coin-flip ceiling ($\approx50\%$ within a doubled role) to near unity, and the aggregate manual-role figure rises above 84.5% toward the structural bound. Simultaneously, the residual gap between the manual-role (84.5%) and automatic-role (67.4%) conditions should *shrink*, because part of that gap is substitution aliasing rather than role-assignment error.
*Decision rule:* if the aggregate does not move after removing the aliasing, then the 84.5% figure was never substitution-limited, which would imply the 94-image set excludes one member of each substituted pair — a composition fact the paper does not disclose, and grounds for treating the headline number as non-generalizable. *Verifiable metric:* accuracy decomposed by the three pairs, published alongside the 94-image set.

**Meta-veto (applies to all three).** Each scenario is a single-variable ablation on a pipeline whose fusion weights are unspecified in the text; a well-formed verifiable reward must therefore also report the fusion coefficient and the regularization $\epsilon$ in Eqs. (4)–(5), without which no ablation is exactly reproducible.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
