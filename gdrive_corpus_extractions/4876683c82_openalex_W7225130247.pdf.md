**Reconstructed planar homography (Equation 1, body absent in source).** The text states only that the 3×3 matrix has 8 degrees of freedom and is solved with RANSAC: "Di mana matriks di atas diturunkan menggunakan algoritma Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC) untuk meminimalisasi proyeksi titik-titik outlier yang menyimpang" [PAGE 6]. The standard DLT operator implied is

$$
\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ W' \end{bmatrix}
= H \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
H = \begin{bmatrix} h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13} \\ h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23} \\ h_{31} & h_{32} & 1 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
x' = \frac{X'}{W'},\;\; y' = \frac{Y'}{W'} .
$$

**Units.** $(u,v)$ in pixels; $(x',y')$ in metres on the 105 m × 68 m plane. $H$ is dimensionally inhomogeneous: $h_{13}, h_{23}$ carry metres, $h_{31}, h_{32}$ carry pixel$^{-1}$, and the remaining four entries carry m·pixel$^{-1}$. Fixing $h_{33}=1$ is the gauge choice that removes the 8th scale degree of freedom.

**Singularity / breakdown regimes.**
1. *Horizon singularity.* The pixel-to-metre Jacobian scale $s(v) = \lVert \partial(x',y')/\partial(u,v)\rVert$ diverges as $v \to v_h$, the horizon row. Broadcast wide-angle framings place the far touchline near $v_h$, so far-field dyad errors are heteroscedastic and cannot be summarised by a single aggregate MAPE.
2. *Degenerate support.* RANSAC requires non-collinear correspondences. Calibration points clustered on one half (typical broadcast framing) makes $H$ extrapolate poorly into the far half; the paper's three-zone segmentation (Kiri/Tengah/Kanan) [PAGE 6] is a piecewise remedy but introduces a discontinuity at zone boundaries, since no blending or hysteresis is described.
3. *Temporal staleness.* With optical-flow refresh every 3 frames [PAGE 9], $H_t$ is stale for up to 2 frames; during fast panning this alone can inject metre-scale error, consistent with the reported 40.94% excursion [PAGE 9].

**Dyad distance (Equation 2, body absent).** Reconstructed as

$$
d_{pq}(t) = \sqrt{\left(x'_p(t)-x'_q(t)\right)^2 + \left(y'_p(t)-y'_q(t)\right)^2}\quad [\mathrm{m}],
$$

bounded above by $\sqrt{105^2+68^2}=125.1$ m. Reported central tendencies: 6.53 m (low block) and 24.59 m (high line) [PAGE 11].

**Convex hull area (shoelace form).**

$$
A(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x'_i y'_{i+1} - x'_{i+1} y'_i\right)\right|\quad [\mathrm{m}^2],
$$

reported as 215.6 m² and 951.61 m² [PAGE 11]. The operator is undefined for $n<3$ and unstable when points are near-collinear (a perfectly flat back four yields a degenerate hull of ~0 m²).

**MAPE and its singularity.**

$$
\mathrm{MAPE} = \frac{100\%}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\frac{\hat d_i - d_i}{d_i}\right| .
$$

Breakdown: as $d_i \to 0$ the summand diverges; MAPE is also asymmetric, over-penalising over-estimation at small denominators. Reported values 7.93%–10.79% (dynamic), 11.61% (static anchor zone), 40.94% (panning spike), 10.42% (post-spike recovery) [PAGE 9].

**MOTA.**

$$
\mathrm{MOTA} = 1 - \frac{\sum_t \left(FN_t + FP_t + IDSW_t\right)}{\sum_t GT_t},
$$

dimensionless, bounded above by 1 and unbounded below. Breakdown when $\sum_t GT_t$ is small (sparse frames), where a single occlusion frame dominates. Reported 89.78% with 25 ID switches, 87 FP, 508 FN [PAGE 7]. Note the trade-off: DeepSORT records *fewer* ID switches (21) and *fewer* FN (299) but 503 FP, so BoTSORT's MOTA edge is almost entirely an FP-suppression effect [PAGE 7].

**MOTP anomaly.** MOTP is tabulated as 20.68% [PAGE 7], yet the base model scores 12.20% [PAGE 7]. Fine-tuning therefore *degraded* localisation precision while improving completeness—an unreported trade-off that undermines the use of bounding-box foot-points as homography inputs.

**Biomechanical veto inequality.** Let $v_{\max}\approx 10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $f_s = 30\ \mathrm{Hz}$:

$$
\Delta_{\max}^{\text{individual}} = \frac{v_{\max}}{f_s} \approx 0.333\ \mathrm{m\,frame^{-1}},\qquad
\Delta_{\max}^{\text{dyad}} = \frac{2v_{\max}}{f_s} \approx 0.667\ \mathrm{m\,frame^{-1}}.
$$

The paper's acceptance threshold is $\tau = 3.0$ m/frame [PAGE 10], giving $\tau/\Delta_{\max}^{\text{dyad}} \approx 4.5$ and $\tau/\Delta_{\max}^{\text{individual}} \approx 9.0$. The stated "ambang batas toleransi biomekanika" is therefore not a biomechanical bound at all but a coarse gross-failure tripwire.

**Throughput.** 67.7 GFLOPs, 20,033,116 parameters [PAGE 6]; measured 5.35 FPS (static) and 6.22 FPS (dynamic) [PAGE 9]. Against a 30 FPS broadcast source the pipeline runs at 0.18–0.21× real time, a hard contradiction with any real-time framing.

Team assignment descends from HSV clustering work by Hartono et al. [PAGE 12, ref. 12]. Geometry descends from Athanesious & Kiruthika's perspective-transform possession forecasting [PAGE 12, ref. 13] and RANSAC homography estimation [PAGE 6, ref. 23]. Tactical semantics descend from Caetano's player-dyad analysis [PAGE 11–12, ref. 3], Forcher's compactness-and-defensive-success study [PAGE 11, ref. 1], and Bueno's convex-hull/fractal team-shape work [PAGE 12, ref. 24], with Rico-González supplying the positional reference values [PAGE 13, ref. 29].

The multi-week trap is inheritance without audit. A practitioner who lifts this stack will silently inherit three unstated couplings. First, the HSV thresholds are *not* automatic: the static scenario uses $[0,50,25]$ and $[160,50,25]$ with a red-pixel ratio gate of $>0.15$, while the dynamic scenario relaxes to $[0,40,15]$, $[160,40,15]$ and $>0.05$ [PAGE 8]. Two hand-tuned parameter sets for two matches means the "objective" system contains a human-in-the-loop calibration step that will fail silently on a third kit colour or under stadium floodlighting. Second, the ROI definition drifts between sections: Section 2.7 specifies "area torso (20%-80% dari dimensi bounding box)" [PAGE 5] while Section 3.3 specifies "20% hingga 70% pada sumbu-Y dan 20% hingga 80% pada sumbu-X" [PAGE 8]. A 10-percentage-point discrepancy in the vertical crop changes how much shorts/grass contamination enters the hue histogram. Third, the MAPE small-denominator trap means teams will report flattering accuracy on stretched high lines and never notice metre-scale errors in the tight pressing distances that compactness analysis exists to measure. The second multi-week trap is the domain-shift confound: fine-tuning on the Roboflow "soccer-players" corpus of 10,914 images at 640×640 [PAGE 4] and evaluating on 1080p broadcast footage of Indonesia–Iraq and Bayern–PSG leaves open whether the 947 recovered detections [PAGE 7] reflect genuine domain adaptation or favourable test content. No held-out cross-match validation is reported.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three clocks are in conflict. The **event clock** is football's: a defensive line can compress or break in 0.5–2 s. The **pipeline clock** is the reported 5.35–6.22 FPS [PAGE 9], i.e. 161–187 ms per frame. The **confirmation clock** is the HSV voting logic: 10-frame fast-confirm plus 15-frame (static) or 5-frame (dynamic) re-verification with 3-frame revoke tolerance [PAGE 8]. At 5.35 FPS, fast-confirm alone costs 1.87 s—longer than most defensive transitions. Worst-case stale-identity duration is $(10+15+3)/5.35 \approx 5.2$ s in the static scenario. Any coach receiving a "dyad exceeded 15 m" alert [PAGE 11] on that latency is being told about a shape that no longer exists.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under sideline time pressure the coach cannot wait 1.9 s for identity confirmation plus inference latency, so the documented failure mode—"sangat bergantung pada pengamatan visual subjektif" [PAGE 2]—reasserts itself precisely in the highest-leverage moments. The tool's own latency reproduces the bias it was built to eliminate.

**Counter-trap.** The correct counter is to decouple cadence from causality: run confirmation logic on possession-segmented windows rather than per-frame, precompute per-phase aggregates (set-piece, open play, transition), and surface the 15 m breach only after the possession ends. This trades latency for validity and matches how analysts actually consume the data. The paper gestures at this with its GUI trend graph [PAGE 9] but frames the output as instantly actionable: "pelatih dapat langsung memberikan instruksi objektif" [PAGE 11].

**Adversarial reading of intent.** The authors know the latency problem—the conclusion concedes the system "belum mencapai kecepatan operasional real-time langsung (saat ini 6,22 FPS)" [PAGE 11] and recommends TensorRT. Yet the abstract markets an "automated data-driven" replacement for manual observation [PAGE 1]. The gap between the abstract's framing and the conclusion's concession is the document's core rhetorical fault line: a validated post-hoc instrument presented as a live decision aid.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Physical/kinematic veto — UPHELD.** The 3.0 m/frame acceptance bound [PAGE 10] is 4.5–9× the physically admissible dyad/individual displacement at 30 Hz. Verdict: the smoothness test cannot certify geometric correctness. A homography that is wrong by 2.5 m/frame on every frame passes silently. Recommended replacement bound: $\tau \le 0.67$ m/frame for dyads, with a robust estimator (median absolute deviation over a 15-frame window) rather than a per-frame maximum, since a max-statistic is dominated by a single outlier.

**Biomechanical veto — PARTIALLY UPHELD.** Convex hull areas of 215.6 m² and 951.61 m² [PAGE 11] are plausible for 10 outfield players in low-block and high-line configurations respectively (21.6 m² and 95.2 m² per player), but goalkeeper inclusion is undisclosed. Verdict: the areas are admissible only under a stated "outfield players + detected target team only" convention; without it the comparison is unfalsifiable.

**Weather / illumination veto — UPHELD.** HSV thresholds were separately hand-tuned per match [PAGE 8]. Hue is the least stable channel under floodlighting, wet pitch specularity, and shadow bands from stadium roof geometry; the 3×3 elliptical opening/closing [PAGE 5] removes salt-and-pepper noise but not chromatic cast. Verdict: the team-isolation stage has no demonstrated transfer across lighting conditions, and no ablation across illumination is reported.

**Sensor/optics veto — UPHELD.** Single wide-angle broadcast optics place the far touchline near the homography horizon singularity; error is heteroscedastic in image row but only an aggregate MAPE is reported across "15 sampel frame krusial" [PAGE 9]—3.9% of the 388 static frames and 0.6% of the 2,391 dynamic frames. Verdict: the validation sample is too small and too aggregated to certify per-zone accuracy.

**Statistical veto — UPHELD.** No confidence intervals accompany mAP, MOTA, or MAPE. The BoTSORT vs ByteTrack MOTA gap is 0.19 pp [PAGE 7], far inside plausible sampling noise for these frame counts. SEM error bars appear only on the density time series [PAGE 11]. Verdict: "signifikan" claims are unsupported by hypothesis testing.

**Veto 1 — Small-denominator MAPE inflation.** Counterfactual: recompute MAPE stratified into distance bins ($d<3$ m, $3\le d<10$ m, $d\ge 10$ m). Prediction: the $d<3$ m stratum will show MAPE materially above the reported 7.93%–11.61% band, because a fixed ~0.3 m absolute error yields >10% at $d=3$ m and >30% at $d=1$ m. Falsifiable by re-running the 15-frame sample with the raw $\hat d_i, d_i$ pairs, which the paper does not tabulate. If the low-block 6.53 m mean dyad [PAGE 11] is representative, a substantial fraction of dyads sit in the high-MAPE regime.

**Veto 2 — Stale-homography step artifact.** Counterfactual: instrument the per-frame homography and correlate the 40.94% excursion [PAGE 9] with the optical-flow refresh boundary (every 3rd frame) and with zone transitions (Kiri/Tengah/Kanan). Prediction: the largest errors will cluster at flow-refresh frames and at zone-switch frames rather than being uniformly distributed through the pan. Falsifiable by plotting error against frame index modulo 3; a uniform distribution would refute the staleness hypothesis and point instead to pure keypoint-track loss.

**Veto 3 — Threshold-sensitivity of the 15 m intervention rule.** Counterfactual: sweep the HSV red-pixel ratio gate from 0.05 to 0.25 and the ROI vertical crop from 20–70% to 20–80% on the same footage. Prediction: because ROI and gate jointly determine which players enter the hull, the reported 215.6 m² area will move by more than the between-scenario contrast can absorb at the extreme gate values, and the count of 15 m breaches will change non-monotonically. Falsifiable by reporting area and breach-count as functions of the gate. If the metrics are stable across the sweep, the pipeline is genuinely robust and the per-scenario threshold divergence [PAGE 8] is harmless; if not, the "objective" claim reduces to a claim about one hand-chosen operating point.
