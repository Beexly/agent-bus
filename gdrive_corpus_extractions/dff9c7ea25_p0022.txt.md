(b) NHL window mismatch. The 40-game range \(F_3\) covers roughly half an NHL season [PAGE 24]. Early-season outliers therefore persist into the postseason portion of the model's memory, creating a multi-week staleness trap where recent form is underweighted relative to distant history.
(c) Ownership oscillation. Raising \(F_{\mathrm{op}}\) increases \(FS\), which discourages selection, which lowers observed ownership, which may next cycle lower \(F_{\mathrm{op}}\) again [PAGE 23–24]. With a once-daily feed [PAGE 23], this feedback loop is sampled at 1 Hz per day and can overshoot or hunt around the threshold boundaries.
(d) Floor rigidity. \(GSF\) prevents weak athletes from falling below a minimum salary [PAGE 24], compressing the bottom of the value distribution and removing the price signal that would otherwise make low-owned, low-projection athletes uniquely rosterable.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
First-order contestant cognition: under a 200 fantasy-dollar cap, a contestant observes a high-ownership star whose \(FS\) has been raised by the \(OP\) disincentive and excludes that star to preserve cap space [PAGE 23].

Second-order operator response: the engine, aware that contestants avoid high-\(OP\) athletes, may reduce \(F_{\mathrm{op}}\) for an unpopular athlete as an incentive, lowering \(FS\) [PAGE 23].

Third-order contestant reaction: contestants pivot toward the newly cheaper athlete; its lineup inclusion \(L_{\mathrm{athlete}}/L_{\mathrm{total}}\) rises; if it crosses the 14.8% threshold in a 9-game series or the \(2\times\) average ownership mark, \(FS\) jumps by \$2 [PAGE 23–24]. The athlete is now more expensive precisely because it became more popular.

Crisis habit regression: after a star underperforms, contestants regress toward mean ownership (chalk safety). The engine detects rising ownership on that star, raises \(OP\), raises \(FS\), and further discourages selection. This is a negative feedback loop on roster share but a positive feedback loop on salary for the residual holders.

Counter-trap: target athletes who sit just below an ownership threshold—for example, 14.70% lineup inclusion in a 9-game NBA series—because they retain full projection value \(F_{\mathrm{PP}}\) without triggering the +\$2 \(OP\) bump. Conversely, avoid athletes measured at exactly \(2\times\) expected average ownership, where marginal entry by one additional contestant creates a discrete salary class jump.

Clock structure: the daily feed arrives only after real-world competitions conclude [PAGE 23], so every factor vector is \(t-1\) stale at lineup lock. There is no intraday update operator. Any injury, weather event, or late scratch between feed and lock is computed into \(FS\) only on the next cycle, making same-day lineup decisions a prediction on a frozen state vector.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: expression (2) contains no health or injury variable [PAGE 24]. Past performance factors \(F_i\) implicitly reflect availability but cannot forecast acute injury. Veto condition: an athlete is listed questionable at feed time but scratched before lineup lock; \(DS\) is computed from non-zero \(F_i\) despite zero expected output. Because the feed is post-competition only [PAGE 23], this stale-state salary is a hard veto for same-day lineups.

Biomechanical layer: the pitcher-specific \(n=2\) factor set accounts for lower playing frequency but not pitch count, cumulative innings, arm fatigue, or short-rest rotations [PAGE 23]. A pitcher with strong \(F_i\) over a 5- or 15-game range may be overvalued if biomechanical load indicators say otherwise; the source provides no biomechanical corrective term.

Weather layer: the text contains no weather operator. MLB and NFL are outdoor sports subject to precipitation, wind, and temperature; NBA is indoor. \(F_{\mathrm{PP}}\) is not shown to be weather-conditioned. Veto condition: an outdoor game forecast outside the historical envelope used to derive \(F_i\) invalidates the projection basis without any model adjustment.

Cross-layer adjudication: \(FS=\max[DS,GSF]\) [PAGE 24] is computed from a state vector captured after the day's competitions [PAGE 23]. If any physical-layer variable (health, weather, lineup status) changes after \(t_{\mathrm{feed}}\) and before lineup lock \(t_{\mathrm{lock}}\), the salary is calculated on an invalid state. No veto override or intraday recalculation is described in pages 23–24, so the adjudication rule is: any post-feed physical-layer change voids the salary's validity for same-day roster decisions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
