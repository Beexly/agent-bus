A major historical trap emerged from this genealogy: the **behaviorist straitjacket**. By insisting on observable $S-R$ bonds and rejecting introspection, Hull’s successors inherited a methodology blind to internal representations, language acquisition, and conscious experience. The "multi-week trap" refers to the decades-long dominance of operant conditioning paradigms (Skinner, 1938–1953) that validated Hull’s quantitative rigor while ignoring his more sophisticated hypothetico-deductive structure. This trapped experimental psychology in narrow laboratory contexts, delaying the development of cognitive neuroscience until Chomsky’s critique (1959) shattered the illusion that language could be explained by simple reinforcement histories.

The belief that society could be engineered via optimal reinforcement schedules led to ethically catastrophic applications in behavioral modification programs (e.g., Project MKULTRA, aversion therapy for homosexuality) that treated individuals as input-output machines devoid of autonomy. Hull’s own warning about "magical practices" was ironically fulfilled when his scientific program was used to justify magical beliefs in technological determinism and social control.
