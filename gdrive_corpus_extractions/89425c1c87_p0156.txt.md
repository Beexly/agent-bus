<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of this problem traces back to Kant’s *Critique of Pure Reason*, specifically the Transcendental Aesthetic, where space is posited as an a priori intuition rather than an empirical concept derived from outer experience. James, writing in 1890, is reacting against both the British Empiricists (who tried to build space out of discrete sensations of color and extension) and the German idealists. On [PAGE 1], James rejects the notion that the intellect "gives the spatial quality to them out of resources of its own." He argues instead that sensations themselves are "of extensive objects." This is a crucial pivot: it grounds spatiality in the raw data of consciousness (phenomenology) rather than in pure reason.

Historically, this connects to Helmholtz’s work on unconscious inference and the physiology of vision. Helmholtz demonstrated that we learn the geometry of space through sensorimotor contingencies—moving our eyes and hands teaches us the relationships between visual fields and tactile surfaces. James’s mention of the difficulty in feeling the length of our back while motionless directly mirrors Helmholtzian experiments on the importance of muscular sense (kinesthesia) in constructing spatial unity.

A multi-week trap lies in misinterpreting James’s critique of the "intellect" as a denial of cognitive processing. Modern neuroscience might label this "predictive coding," where the brain generates a model of space. However, James is careful to distinguish between the *source* of spatial quality (the sensation itself) and the *arrangement* (which requires more than mere existence). The trap is to modernize James too quickly into a Bayesian brain theorist, thereby missing his phenomenological insistence on the immediate givenness of extent. The "retrocausal" aspect is that our current understanding of embodied cognition validates James’s intuition that space is not computed but enacted through the body's interaction with the environment. The "orderly arrangement" is the result of evolutionary and developmental history conditioning the nervous system to fuse specific patterns of sensory input with motor output.
