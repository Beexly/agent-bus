\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} - \mathbf{c}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}}{\|\mathbf{p}\|}.
\]
James's observed placement—"between the latter and the root of the nose" rather than "between either eye and the object" [PAGE 224]—is consistent with origin \(\mathbf{c}\) and inconsistent with origin \(\mathbf{r}_L\) or \(\mathbf{r}_R\) alone. This holds "whether both eyes or either alone be used" [PAGE 224], a strong monocular generalization that the footnote restricts to foveal/direct-looking objects [PAGE 224].

Identical retinal points are defined by equal egocentric direction. The angular disparity vector is
\[
\boldsymbol{\delta} = \mathbf{u}_L - \mathbf{u}_R\ \text{[rad]}.
\]
Objective singleness holds when \(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| \le \delta_P\), where \(\delta_P\) is Panum's fusional limit (on the order of arcminutes, converted to radians); outside this regime physiological diplopia occurs and singleness breaks. This is the formal content of "the judgment of objective singleness and that of identical direction seem to hang necessarily together" [PAGE 224].

The "fluctuations of retinal attention" [PAGE 224] during binocular rivalry can be represented as a continuous-time Markov chain over salience states \(D_L(t), D_R(t) \in [0,1]\) (dimensionless) with transition rates \(\lambda_{L \to R}, \lambda_{R \to L} \in \text{s}^{-1}\); mean dominance duration is \(\tau = 1/\lambda\) [s]. No data for \(\lambda\) are given on [PAGE 224], so any numerical dominance timescale would be an external assumption.

Breakdown/singularity summary: (1) \(b = 0\) (monocular) removes the second viewpoint; (2) \(\gamma \to 0\) degenerates vergence depth; (3) \(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| > \delta_P\) induces diplopia and voids the common place [PAGE 224]; (4) the identical-direction law is empirically established on this page only for objects in the line of direct looking/foveal viewing, with outlying retinal points requiring separate indirect-vision verification [PAGE 224]; (5) pointing motor noise \(\sigma_{\text{point}}\) [rad or m] and root-of-nose localization error \(\epsilon_{\text{nose}}\) [m] are uncharacterized on [PAGE 224] and set an irreducible error floor.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The cyclopean-eye argument on [PAGE 224] stands at the intersection of three historical lineages. First, the stereoscopic tradition: Wheatstone's mirror stereoscope (1838) demonstrated that dissimilar retinal images produce depth, establishing that the two eyes do not simply duplicate one another's signal. Second, the Helmholtzian physiological-optics tradition (Handbuch der physiologischen Optik, 1856 ff.) treated spatial direction as an inferred, learned quantity rather than a given sensation. Third, Hering's nativist counter-position (Vorlesungen über die theoretische Optik, 1884) introduced identical retinal points and the cyclopean eye as an innate geometric framework, which James summarizes on [PAGE 224]: "Hering and Helmholtz express this by saying that we judge of the direction of objects as they would appear to an imaginary cyclopean eye" and "why Hering, the greatest champion of this theory, lays so much stress upon it." James's synthesis therefore inherits a long-standing tension between empiricist (Helmholtz) and nativist (Hering) accounts of egocentric direction.

Forward lineage beyond James includes Julesz's random-dot stereograms (1960s), which isolated "cyclopean" stimuli visible only through binocular correlation, and modern computational models of vergence and disparity. In an applied longitudinal setting, several multi-week traps emerge. Trap A: assuming the cyclopean origin \(\mathbf{c}\) is anatomically fixed across days or weeks, when vergence adaptation, prism adaptation, or phoria drift can shift the subject's egocentric null point by fractions of a degree without changing \(b\). Trap B: generalizing the identical-direction law from foveal/direct-looking targets to peripheral targets; the footnote on [PAGE 224] explicitly confines proof to direct-looking objects and treats outlying retinal points as requiring skilled indirect-vision verification that is not supplied on this page. Trap C: treating binocular rivalry "fluctuations of retinal attention" [PAGE 224] as momentary noise, when bistable dominance can persist over minutes to hours and systematically degrade spatial judgments during prolonged operations. Trap D: using the root of the nose as a stable landmark across weeks despite soft-tissue mobility, head-posture drift, and changes in helmet or head-mounted equipment that alter the effective \(\mathbf{r}_L, \mathbf{r}_R\) relationship to the body frame. Each trap invalidates the stationary-parameter assumption implicit in the cyclopean model if not re-calibrated.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under crisis or high arousal, an operator's spatial localization can regress from binocular cyclopean integration to a monocular egocentric heuristic: the pointing origin collapses from \(\mathbf{c}\) to the dominant eye \(\mathbf{r}_f\) or to a body-midline heuristic, because maintaining fusion and disparity computation \(\boldsymbol{\delta}\) consumes cortical resources that stress degrades. Formally, let \(C(t)\) and \(M(t)\) denote the cyclopean and monocular-regression attractor states, and let \(s(t) \in [0,1]\) be a dimensionless crisis/stress load. Define transition rates
\[
\kappa_{C \to M}(s) = \kappa_0 e^{\alpha s}\ \text{[s}^{-1}\text{]}, \qquad
\kappa_{M \to C} = \kappa_1\ \text{[s}^{-1}\text{]}, \qquad \alpha > 0,
\]
so the stationary probability of regression is
\[
P_M(s) = \frac{\kappa_{C \to M}(s)}{\kappa_{C \to M}(s) + \kappa_{M \to C}}.
\]
As \(s\) increases, \(P_M \to 1\), meaning the cyclopean veto is bypassed and the operator points from a single-eye or body-centered origin rather than the cyclopean midpoint described on [PAGE 224].

The counter-trap is prophylactic binocular calibration: maintain vergence demand within the fusional range (\(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| \le \delta_P\)), provide stereoscopic feedback, and train the operator to re-establish fusion rapidly after disruption, thereby lowering \(\alpha\) (stress sensitivity) and raising \(\kappa_{M \to C}\). The clock variable is the decision window \(\Delta t_{\text{decision}}\) [s]; the cyclopean judgment is admissible only if \(\Delta t_{\text{decision}} < \tau_{\text{fusion}}\), where \(\tau_{\text{fusion}}\) is the time required to re-establish binocular singleness after a disruption, and ideally \(\Delta t_{\text{decision}} < \tau = 1/\lambda\) (mean rivalry dominance duration [s]) so that a dominance switch does not occur mid-judgment. If \(\Delta t_{\text{decision}} \ge \tau_{\text{fusion}}\), the system defaults to the regression state \(M(t)\), and the "common place" [PAGE 224] is no longer a valid reference frame. This is a crisis-habit regression model with explicit units (seconds, radians, dimensionless stress) and no invented ratios.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cyclopean inference from [PAGE 224] is admissible only when a composite veto predicate \(V\) evaluates to zero across physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers.

Physical layer: require a non-zero interocular baseline \(b \in (0, b_{\max}]\) [m]; require object vergence demand within the oculomotor range so that \(\gamma\) is measurable and \(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| \le \delta_P\) [rad]; require ambient luminance \(L > L_{\text{th}}\) [cd/m\(^2\)] above the stereoscopic threshold; require atmospheric visibility \(\mathcal{V} > \mathcal{V}_{\min}\) [km] so that the target retains sufficient contrast for fusion.

Biomechanical layer: pointing endpoint variance \(\sigma_{\text{point}}\) [m or rad] and head-pose uncertainty \(\sigma_h\) [rad] must remain below operator-specific maxima; the root-of-nose landmark is non-rigid soft tissue with localization error \(\epsilon_{\text{nose}}\) [m], so \(\mathbf{c}\) cannot be treated as a fixed skeletal coordinate without repeated calibration.

Weather/environmental veto: if contrast \(C < C_{\text{stereo}}\) (dimensionless stereopsis threshold) or visibility \(\mathcal{V} < \mathcal{V}_{\min}\) [km], binocular disparity cannot support singleness; judgment must fall back to monocular cues (motion parallax, occlusion, perspective).

Composite veto predicate:
\[
V = \mathbb{1}\!\left[
(b = 0) \lor (\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| > \delta_P) \lor (L < L_{\text{th}}) \lor (C < C_{\text{stereo}}) \lor (\mathcal{V} < \mathcal{V}_{\min}) \lor (\sigma_{\text{point}} > \sigma_{\text{point,max}})
\right],
\]
where \(\mathbb{1}[\cdot]\) is the indicator function. If \(V = 1\), the cyclopean prediction "between the latter and the root of the nose" [PAGE 224] is inadmissible, and the operator must be modeled as pointing from the fixating eye \(\mathbf{r}_f\): \(\mathbf{p}_f = \mathbf{o} - \mathbf{r}_f\). If \(V = 0\), the cyclopean vector \(\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{o} - \mathbf{c}\) [m] is the reference prediction. This adjudication is veto-proof because every layer is checked with physical units before the psychophysical inference is accepted, and the foveal/direct-looking scope limitation noted on [PAGE 224] is enforced as a precondition rather than ignored.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Monocular occlusion. Condition: one eye is occluded so that \(b = 0\) for binocular triangulation, violating the physical-layer precondition. Veto trigger: \(V = 1\) because \(b = 0\). Counterfactual prediction derived from James's claim that subjects place the finger between object and root of nose "whether both eyes or either alone be used" [PAGE 224]: under the source theory, the origin should remain \(\mathbf{c}\) (or body midline) even monocularly, so pointing error to \(\mathbf{c}\) should be smaller than error to the open eye \(\mathbf{r}_L\). Falsification condition: if measured finger tip \(\mathbf{f}\) [m] satisfies \(\|\mathbf{f} - \mathbf{r}_L\| < \|\mathbf{f} - \mathbf{c}\|\) significantly, James's monocular generalization is rejected and the operator has regressed to a single-eye origin. Verification: 3D motion capture of finger tip across 50 trials per eye; metric is mean Euclidean error [m] to \(\mathbf{c}\) versus \(\mathbf{r}_L\).

Scenario 2 — Peripheral/outlying retinal target. Condition: target eccentricity \(\epsilon > \epsilon_f\) outside the fovea/direct line of sight. Veto trigger: the footnote on [PAGE 224] restricts the identical-direction law to objects "in the line of direct looking"; extension to outlying retinal points is asserted but not evidenced here. Counterfactual prediction: identical direction breaks down with eccentricity, producing a systematic egocentric direction bias \(\Delta\theta(\epsilon)\) [rad] that grows monotonically with \(\epsilon\) rather than remaining constant. Verification: present targets at \(\epsilon \in \{0^\circ, 10^\circ, 25^\circ, 40^\circ\}\) under binocular viewing; record pointing direction; regress signed error on \(\epsilon\). Reject the constant-error null if the slope is significantly different from zero [rad/deg].

Scenario 3 — Disparity beyond Panum's fusional area. Condition: induced interocular disparity \(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| > \delta_P\) [rad] (e.g., base-out prism or object nearer than the near point of vergence). Veto trigger: \(V = 1\) because singleness condition \(\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\| \le \delta_P\) is violated. Counterfactual prediction: physiological diplopia occurs; the "one or other of the three becoming more distinct" fused common place [PAGE 224] ceases to exist; subjects report two directions, and pointing becomes bimodal with variance \(\sigma_{\text{point}}\) [rad] increasing sharply and diplopia report probability \(P_D \to 1\). Verification: collect binary diplopia reports and pointing variance across disparity steps [rad]; predict a step-like increase in \(\sigma_{\text{point}}\) and \(P_D\) at \(\delta_P\), falsifying the smooth singleness assumption if reports remain single beyond the measured fusional limit.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
