**2. Rosenblatt delay system (AlNemer et al., [PAGE 26]).** System (2): ℵ''(ℓ)+Dℵ(ℓ−ζ)=ℏ(ℓ,ℵ(ℓ))+∆(ℓ,ℵ(ℓ)) dZ_H(ℓ)/dℓ, ℓ∈[0,ϖ]. The isometry that governs everything is Lemma 2 [PAGE 27]:
$$\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_0^\ell \sigma(\varsigma)\,\mathrm{d}Z_H(\varsigma)\Big\|^2\le 2H\ell^{2H-1}\int_0^\ell \|\sigma(\varsigma)\|_{L_2^0}^2\,\mathrm{d}\varsigma,$$
and its μ-th moment form (Lemma 3, [PAGE 28]): 𝔼‖∫Λ dZ_H‖^μ ≤ τ_μ(2H)^{μ/2}ℓ^{μH−1}∫‖Λ‖^μ. Existence/uniqueness (Theorem 1, [PAGE 29]) reduces to the contraction scalar 2^{μ−1}W₂+W₃<1 with W₂, W₃ built from ‖U∆‖, ‖Uℏ‖ and the delayed-matrix Mittag-Leffler bound ‖M_ζ(Dℓ)‖≤(ℓ+ζ)E_{2,2}(‖D‖(ℓ+ζ)²) (Lemma 4, [PAGE 28]). Hyers–Ulam stability (Theorem 2, [PAGE 36]):
$$\mathbb{E}\|\Pi(\ell)-\aleph(\ell)\|^\mu\le W\kappa,\quad W=\frac{3^{\mu-1}\varpi^{\mu+1}}{\mu+1}\big(E_{2,2}(\|D\|\varpi^2)\big)^\mu\exp\!\big(3^{\mu-1}(W_2+W_3)\big).$$
Worked example: H=0.75, τ_μ=1.15 ⇒ W₂=0.065, W₃=0.21752, 2^{μ−1}W₂+W₃=0.3475<1 [PAGE 37]. Breakdown: as H→1 the factor 2Hℓ^{2H−1} inflates and the admissible (U∆,Uℏ,ϖ) region collapses; the singular case D=0 is explicitly admitted (delayed-matrix functions remain defined for singular D), which is the paper's claimed novelty.

**3. L∞ interval-observer LMI (Zhang–Huang–Li, [PAGE 45]).** Error system e(k+1)=Γ_{φ(k)}e(k)+Dd(k) [PAGE 42]; the design LMI is
$$\begin{bmatrix}-\eta P_n & * & *\\ 0 & -\chi^2 I & *\\ P_nA-W_nC-\kappa_nQ_n(L_n\otimes I_N) & P_nD & -P_n\end{bmatrix}\prec0,\quad P_n\prec\theta P_m,\ \tfrac{1}{\tilde\tau}+\tfrac{\ln\eta}{\ln\theta}<0,\ \kappa_n>\tfrac{1}{a(L_n)}.$$
Units are discrete-time (dimensionless k). Breakdown regime: the average-dwell-time inequality 1/τ̃+lnη/lnθ<0 requires η∈(0,1), θ>1; if the switching is faster than τ̃ the L∞ bound κq‖d‖∞+V(0)ω^k [PAGE 43] diverges (ω→1).

**4. Chenciner (generalized Neimark–Sacker) degeneracy (Lugojan et al., [PAGE 107, 137]).** Codim-2 conditions r(0)=1, Re b₁(0)=0, L₂(0)≠0 with
$$L_2(0)=\tfrac12\big(\operatorname{Im}^2 b_1(0)+2\operatorname{Re} b_2(0)\big).$$
The regular parameter change (α₁,α₂)↦(β₁,β₂) degenerates iff its Jacobian vanishes at the origin, i.e. verbatim "a10b01 −a01b10 = 0" [PAGE 107]. Breakdown: on this variety the implicit-function theorem fails and the two non-degenerate bifurcation diagrams are replaced by many (the companion paper counts up to 32; the "further degeneration" paper reports 18) [PAGE 137].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Genuine scientific lineage (there are no "multi-week traps" in a math corpus; the only traps are historical dead ends):

- **Stochastic epidemic thresholds.** Kermack–McKendrick (1927) founded the compartmental SIR model and the R₀ threshold — the paper cites it explicitly [PAGE 23, ref 3]. The behavioral split (S₁ vs S₂) descends from Nguyen–Du–Nguyen, "Asymptotic behavior for a stochastic behavioral change SIR model," J. Math. Anal. Appl. 2024 [ref 4, PAGE 23], which Sabbar et al. generalize. The jump-diffusion layer descends from Applebaum-style Lévy-driven SDEs and specifically from Kiouach–Sabbar–El-idrissi (Math. Meth. Appl. Sci. 2021) [ref 14] and Privault–Wang (J. Nonlinear Sci. 2021) [ref 23]; the ergodic-auxiliary-system technique is credited to Sabbar et al., Chaos Solitons Fractals 2022 [ref 21]. The "dead end" being corrected: the predecessor used *bilinear* incidence and *only* white noise — the lineage's own stated limitation [PAGE 10].

- **Hyers–Ulam stability.** Ulam posed the stability-of-functional-equations question in 1940 (Wisconsin lectures); Hyers answered for the linear case in 1941 [PAGE 25, refs 30–31]. The stochastic-delay extension rides on Khusainov–Shuklin and Diblík et al.'s delayed-matrix representation [refs 10–11] and on the Rosenblatt process, introduced by Murray Rosenblatt in 1961 ("Independence and dependence," [ref 21, PAGE 38]) as the q=2 non-Gaussian Hermite process — deliberately chosen because fractional Brownian motion "fails in the concrete case of having non-Gaussianity" [PAGE 25].

- **Chenciner bifurcation.** Chenciner's three-part 1985–1988 series on elliptic fixed points [refs 21–23, 14] and Kuznetsov's codimension-two classification (the non-degenerate Chenciner bifurcation is one of eleven generic two-parameter cases) frame the degeneration program. Tigan–Lugojan–Ciurdariu opened the degenerate study (Int. J. Bifurcation Chaos 2020); the two reprinted papers are rungs on that ladder.

The only defensible "multi-week trap" reading: each lineage carries an assumption the next paper had to *relax* (bilinear→general incidence; Gaussian→Rosenblatt; regular→degenerate parameter change). Mistaking a relaxed hypothesis for a universal one is the recurring error the corpus corrects.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
There is no opponent, no adversary, and no strategic agent anywhere in this source, so "crisis habit regression vs. counter-trap vs. clock" has no referent. I will not invent one. The nearest *genuine* structure with the same mathematical shape — a system whose classification depends on reasoning about its own asymptotic self — is the fixed-point/threshold logic itself:

- In the SIR model, the "mind" being modeled is the *population's* aggregate behavior: S₂ encodes individuals who "proactively change their behavior and adhere to interventions" [PAGE 11]. But this is a compartment, not a reasoning agent; there is no belief hierarchy to recurse over. The honest statement is that the model has *first-order* behavioral feedback (transmission reduced via f₂) and nothing higher.

- The only true self-reference is the fixed-point construction in the Rosenblatt existence proof (Krasnoselskii: ℵ=O₁ℵ+O₂ℵ, [PAGE 29]) and the auxiliary-process ergodicity that the SIR threshold *quotes back into itself* (λ₁ is defined by π₁,π₂, which are themselves the long-run laws of the S-subsystem). This is a legitimate recursive dependency, and it is worth stating precisely: the threshold is not an equilibrium property but a fixed point of the process's own invariant measure. That is the real content; dressing it as "theory of mind" would be fabrication.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
No physical, biomechanical, or weather layer exists in this corpus; a "veto proof" here means an *impossibility certificate* — a condition whose failure rigorously kills a claim. These are genuine and checkable:

1. **Explosion veto.** If (A2) fails (α₁₀≤0), the stopping-time argument of Theorem 1 [PAGE 13–15] cannot bound 𝔼[F(s(D∧γ_β))] and γₑ=∞ is unproven; positivity/global existence is vetoed.
2. **Stationarity veto.** If condition (9) is not strictly positive, the auxiliary process (8) is not positive-recurrent, π₁ doesn't exist, and λ₁ is undefined — the entire threshold is vetoed. (Numerically 0.017>0 [PAGE 20] clears it by a thin margin; a slightly larger κ₁ or smaller (u+c) would veto the persistence claim.)
3. **Contraction veto.** In the Rosenblatt problem, if 2^{μ−1}W₂+W₃≥1 the operator is not a contraction and neither existence nor Hyers–Ulam stability holds; the example sits at 0.3475 [PAGE 37], comfortably sub-unit, but the inequality is a hard gate.
4. **LMI-infeasibility veto.** For the interval observer, if κₙ≤1/a(Lₙ) (algebraic connectivity of the switching graph) the coupling gain cannot dominate and no L∞-robust observer exists [PAGE 45].
5. **Transversality veto.** For Chenciner, a₁₀b₀₁−a₀₁b₁₀=0 [PAGE 107] vetoes the standard normal-form reduction; you cannot change coordinates, so every non-degenerate bifurcation diagram is invalid on that variety.

These are the honest analog of "vetoes": each is a proved collapse of a claimed conclusion under a violated hypothesis, with the exact inequality that separates valid from invalid.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three counterfactuals that are verifiable *inside the mathematics* (each with a checkable prediction), replacing outcome-betting with theorem-testing:

1. **Threshold flip.** Counterfactual: hold all Table-1 parameters but raise κ₃ (infective noise intensity) until λ₂ exceeds λ₁. Prediction (checkable from the λ-formulas [PAGE 16]): since ∂λ₂/∂(κ₃²)=½>0 and λ₁ is κ₃-independent, there is a critical κ₃* with κ₃*²=2(λ₁−u−h₁−h₂−∫[z₃−ln(1+z₃)]G₃). For the persistence parameters (λ₁=3.9876), κ₃*²≈2(3.9876−0.377−jump term); above it, the paper's own Theorem 2 flips to a.s. exponential extinction. Veto condition: predicted extinction is falsified if trajectories persist above κ₃*.

2. **Moment-collapse.** Counterfactual: set s→2⁺ in (A2). Prediction: α₁₀→u−½α₃−α₉; because α₃=max κₖ² and α₉>0, sufficiently strong noise makes α₁₀≤0, so the second-moment (mean-square) bound diverges even though the a.s. log-extinction result (which needs only λ₁<λ₂) still holds. Verifiable prediction: mean-square ‖I‖² blows up while (1/t)ln I→λ₁−λ₂<0 — a genuine divergence between almost-sure and moment stability, exactly the kind of claim a solver can falsify.

3. **Rosenblatt H→1 breakdown.** Counterfactual: fix the example's matrices and push H→1⁻. Prediction: the isometry constant 2Hℓ^{2H−1}→2ℓ, W₂∝(2H)^{μ/2} grows, so the admissible ϖ (time horizon for which 2^{μ−1}W₂+W₃<1) shrinks monotonically to 0; Hyers–Ulam constant W=…·exp(3^{μ−1}(W₂+W₃)) blows up. Falsifiable prediction: numerically integrating the error bound at H=0.99 with the [PAGE 37] gains must yield a strictly larger W than at H=0.75; if it doesn't, the isometry constant's H-dependence has been mis-stated.

Every counterfactual above terminates in an inequality or an integral that a reader can evaluate against the cited equations — which is the only honest form of "verifiable prediction" this source supports.
