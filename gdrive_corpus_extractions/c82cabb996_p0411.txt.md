James (1890) [PAGE 401] belongs to the Victorian habit-and-instinct tradition. In Principles of Psychology Vol. 1, James treats habit as the "enormous flywheel" of society and the nervous system as growing into modes of action; the Vol. 2 "Instinct" chapter extends this to successive developmental waves. Multi-week traps: James’s pedagogy assumes that catching a wave confers durable "headway." In practice, multi-week intervention studies reveal three trap structures. Trap A (the latency trap): a skill introduced one week after t_c is not learned one week later but disproportionately less, because synaptic consolidation and myelination schedules are not linear in calendar time. Trap B (the maintenance trap): a habit installed during an interest wave decays at rate λ once the external structure (school term, sports season, coach) is removed; without continued i(t) > 0 or urgent need N(t), h regresses exponentially on a timescale 1/λ that may be weeks, not years. Trap C (the substitution trap): missing the drawing/natural-history wave does not imply the individual will later become a botanist; the vacated niche is filled by whatever interests are currently available, often passive consumption rather than skilled production. James’s retrocausal story—adult sedentariness caused by a missed boyhood wave—therefore compresses a multi-week, multi-domain, multi-affordance process into a single deterministic arrow, ignoring that later waves (mechanics, physical law, worldly wisdom) may themselves carry motor or spatial components that could partially compensate if their own t_c has not closed.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock, modeled recursively over other minds.

Level 0 (object agent): the agent’s default policy under pressure is π₀ ≡ π_childhood, the habit installed when i(t) was maximal and h approached h_max during an open critical window (t < t_c). Per [PAGE 401], early-formed patterns—loose living or chastity, ball-playing or sedentary withdrawal—become the attractor to which behavior returns.

Level 1 (my model of your intervention): I model you as attempting to install an adult counter-policy π_adult via instruction, coaching, or willpower after the original wave has ebbed. The intervention succeeds only if delivered while Θ(t_c − t) = 1 and h is sufficiently overlearned that λ is small relative to the crisis timescale.

Level 2 (your model of my crisis response): you model that under a crisis operator C(τ)—acute threat, sleep deprivation, social threat, cortisol surge, τ measured in minutes to hours—I regress according to
\frac{d\pi}{d\tau} = -\kappa \bigl(\pi - \pi_0\bigr), \qquad \kappa \gg 1 \;\; [\text{time}^{-1}],
whose solution is
\pi(\tau) = \pi_0 + \bigl(\pi_{\text{intervention}} - \pi_0\bigr) e^{-\kappa \tau}.
As τ approaches crisis peak, π(τ) → π₀ regardless of how strongly π_adult was endorsed in calm conditions.

The counter-trap: believing that at the moment of crisis one can "choose" the adult habit through effort. This is valid only if π_adult was installed pre-closure (t < t_c) and consolidated (h ≈ h_max, λ small). It is invalid post-closure because γ_eff ≈ 0: the counter-habit was never encoded with sufficient synaptic strength to resist the crisis regressor.

The clock: t_c is a hard deadline. The counter-trap must be executed before closure; once the gate shuts, attempts to install π_adult during adulthood are vetoed counterfactuals. Recursive depth matters: I expect that you expect me to regress; you expect that I expect you to rely on my willpower; both of us misestimate the clock. The prediction: crisis-induced policy reversion to π₀ occurs within a timescale 1/κ (typically < 72 hours of sustained stress), and adult training delivered after t_c does not shift the asymptote π₀.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

James’s law ("strike the iron while hot") is necessary but not sufficient. Acquisition of any of the listed skills requires the conjunction of three layers:
\mathcal{V}(t) = \min\{A(t), P(t), I(t)\} > \theta,
where A(t) ∈ {0,1} is affordance availability, P(t) ∈ [0,1] is biomechanical/physical readiness, I(t) ∈ [0,1] is interest, and θ ∈ (0,1) is a threshold. If any layer is zero, \mathcal{V} = 0 and skill cannot accumulate, regardless of how "hot" the iron is.

Physical veto: neural plasticity is metabolically expensive. The human brain consumes roughly 20% of basal metabolic power (~0.2–0.3 W of the ~1.2–1.5 W resting metabolic rate in adults, proportionally higher in children). ATP availability, oligodendrocyte-mediated myelination, and GABAergic perineuronal-net formation impose t_c on biophysical grounds. Interest cannot accelerate myelination beyond its genetic-developmental schedule; the gate closes whether or not the pupil is eager.

Biomechanical veto: rowing requires posterior-chain force production and lumbar stability; sailing requires dynamic balance on a moving platform and load handling; skating requires single-leg lateral stability on a low-friction interface; riding requires core coupling to a large animal’s gait. A child may have I(t) ≈ 1 ("eager delight") yet P(t) ≈ 0 because the musculoskeletal system has not reached the strength, coordination, or anthropometric thresholds for that apparatus. James’s examples assume neuromuscular readiness that may arrive after the putative interest wave.

Weather/geographic veto: skating requires frozen water (ambient temperature below the freezing point sustained over days); sailing requires wind within a navigable range and access to a navigable body of water; fishing and shooting require terrain, season, and legal-access permissions. Let s(t) ∈ {0,1} denote seasonal/geographic affordance. If I(t) peaks while s(t) = 0 (e.g., interest in sailing during a landlocked winter), the wave cannot be ridden, and later access at t > t_c encounters γ_eff ≈ 0.

Veto proof / counterexample: a nine-year-old in a landlocked region develops intense interest in sailing during winter (I ≈ 1, A = 0, s = 0). The iron is hot, but there is no water, no wind, no boat. At age nineteen, coastal access and wind arrive (A = 1, s = 1, I adult ≈ 0.5). Because t > t_c, γ_eff ≈ 0, so h plateaus at a novice level despite abundant later opportunity. James’s determinism therefore fails under cross-layer constraints: interest without affordance or biomechanical readiness produces no headway, and late affordance cannot reopen a closed critical gate.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Veto: \mathcal{V}(t) = 0 during the interest wave because A·s = 0; later t > t_c yields γ_eff ≈ 0. Veto: crisis regressor κ dominates because π_adult was never consolidated pre-closure (γ_eff ≈ 0, λ high for the adult layer). Prediction: compliance rate reverts to the pre-intervention childhood baseline π₀ within < 72 hours of crisis onset, and implementation-intention "counter-trap" protocols fail to shift the crisis asymptote.
