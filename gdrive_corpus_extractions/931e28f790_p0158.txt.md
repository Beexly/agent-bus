<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical-scientific lineage runs from Galton’s *Natural Inheritance* (1889), specifically [PAGE 1], into the foundations of biometrics, correlation, and regression. Galton’s “R.F.F. data” (Record of Family Faculties) and his “Family Records” pedigrees constructed a synthetic temporal arrow: the living child (generation I) is made to carry information backward about ancestors (generations II, III, IV). In this design, the descendant is epistemically prior to the ancestor in the data file, yet temporally posterior in the world—hence a retrocausal data structure in which later-born units are used to infer earlier-born traits.

The generational ladder on [PAGE 1] collapses time into pedigree depth: children → parents/uncles/aunts → grandparents/great uncles/great aunts → great grandparents. Because “there is much overlapping of dates in successive generations” [PAGE 1], the arrow is not clean. Adjacent generations share birth-year support, so the retrocausal inference “child phenotype predicts ancestor phenotype” is confounded by shared secular period. Only non-adjacent contrasts (I vs III, II vs IV) are claimed safe [PAGE 1], which means the method implicitly treats time as a lag-2 difference operator rather than a lag-1 operator.

Multi-week trap: If a modern analyst transposes this pedigree-sorting logic into a high-frequency observational window (e.g., a multi-week panel of eye-color or related trait observations), the generational labels become fixed, time-invariant family attributes. The intergenerational birth interval is on the order of two to three decades; within a multi-week window, p(t | G = k) is essentially a point mass for every k, so ρ_{k,k−1} → 1 for all adjacent generations. The generational contrast operator Δ_k then has no within-generation temporal variation to exploit; I(E ; G | T) cannot be estimated because T does not vary within G over the window. The trap is to believe that “sorting by generations” removes cohort effects when, in a short panel, generation and cohort are perfectly collinear with family identity. Any apparent “hereditary persistence” observed over weeks is then indistinguishable from fixed family-level effects, and the retrocausal genealogy degenerates into a cross-sectional family comparison with no temporal identification.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: When an analyst inherits Galton’s situation—pre-compiled pedigree data, ambiguous age exclusions, and a truncated sentence ending in “I” [PAGE 1]—the cognitive default under epistemic crisis is to regress to the native structure of the records. Galton himself regressed to generational sorting because “that method best accorded with the particular form in which all my R.F.F. data are compiled” [PAGE 1]. A present-day analyst faces the same habit regression: rather than impute missing birth years or model the age-8 truncation, they rely on the ordinal pedigree labels as if they were temporal bins. This is a habit regression because it substitutes a convenient classification (generation) for the causal variable of interest (time of birth and developmental age).

Counter-trap: The counter-trap is to treat eye color as a unitary, stable, highly heritable phenotype and to ignore the measurement stratification on [PAGE 1]. Galton explicitly distinguishes direct information from family portraits from indirect information from preserved locks of hair [PAGE 1]. The trap is to collapse M ∈ {portrait, hairlock} into a single measurement of E. Hair locks are subject to photo-oxidation, differential preservation, and the fact that hair melanin (eumelanin/pheomelanin in the hair shaft) is not identical to iris stroma melanin; portraits are subject to illuminant spectrum, artist convention, pigment fading, and gaze angle. If an analyst assumes P(E_obs | E_true, M) = P(E_obs | E_true) for all M, they commit a measurement-invariance fallacy. Because families preserving mementos may differ systematically in record-keeping rigor and ancestral interest, the indirect modality is not missing at random; it is selected on family-level observables that may correlate with the very hereditary conclusion under test.

Clock: The age cutoff A₀ = 8 years functions as a developmental clock. Iris melanin deposition is largely complete in early childhood (typically by age 3), so observations below this clock are phenotypically unstable. Galton’s intended exclusion “all children under the age of eight years” [PAGE 1] is a left-truncation rule meant to stabilize E. If the analyst forgets the clock and treats the sample as age-representative, they overestimate cross-sectional stability. If they include under-8 children without modeling the transition P(E | A < 8), they inject within-individual developmental variance into between-family comparisons, inflating error variance and attenuating heritability estimates. Recursive theory of mind: the analyst must model not only what Galton did, but the unresolved state of what he did not say—the sentence cuts off at “I” [PAGE 1]. Treating that truncation as a resolved design choice rather than an unknown binary state is itself a failure of recursive uncertainty modeling.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Layer 1 — Physical/optical: Adult iris color is determined by melanin concentration and distribution in the iris stroma and by wavelength-dependent scattering (Rayleigh/Tyndall) of light within the eye. Units: melanin density (mg·cm⁻³) or dimensionless spectral reflectance. Macroscopic weather variables (temperature, humidity, barometric pressure, precipitation) do not alter adult iris melanin or its scattering properties. Therefore no weather veto applies to the eye-color phenotype per se; environmental optical conditions at the moment of observation do not change the germline-determined trait.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical/developmental: Melanocyte activity in the iris proceeds during infancy. Mechanical trauma can produce acquired heterochromia, but it does not reset genetically determined baseline color. The decisive biomechanical veto is the developmental age clock: observations with A < 3 years are phenotypically unstable; the intended truncation at A = 8 years [PAGE 1] lies safely in the stable regime, but only if actually enforced. Veto rule: any inclusion of A < 8 without age-stratified modeling is vetoed for cross-sectional heritability because E is not a time-invariant trait below the clock.

Layer 3 — Measurement/environmental (portrait and hair): This is the active veto layer on [PAGE 1]. Portrait observation introduces an illuminant layer: c_obs = f(c_true, L(λ), sensor/artist response), where L(λ) is the spectral power distribution of the light source. 19th-century portraits were often painted under north-studio light or by candlelight; uncontrolled L(λ) shifts perceived hue. Hair locks introduce a chemical-degradation layer: eumelanin photo-oxidizes under UV, so observed hair color depends on storage duration and light exposure, not solely on genotype. Because locks were “preserved in the family as mementos” [PAGE 1], preservation probability P(preserve | family) may depend on family interest in lineage, creating selection on unobservables that correlates with record completeness.

Veto operator: Let I be the inference “eye-color persistence across generations is hereditary.” Define V_portrait = 1 if portrait illuminant/artist bias is unrecorded; V_hair = 1 if hair color is used as an eye-color proxy without spectral validation; V_age = 1 if A < 8 is included without modeling P(E | A < 8). Then I is admissible only if V_portrait = V_hair = V_age = 0. From [PAGE 1], V_portrait and V_hair are plausibly 1 (both modalities used), while V_age is unknown because the sentence truncates. Hence I is provisionally vetoed until the measurement protocol and final age rule are specified. Breakdown regime: if all portraits were executed under a single standardized illuminant and all locks stored in dark, oxygen-free envelopes, V_portrait and V_hair approach 0; these are auxiliary assumptions not entailed by the text.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. In each, Y denotes the eye-color heredity estimand (e.g., parent–offspring regression coefficient or generational contrast), and do(·) denotes the intervention.

Scenario 1 — Re-sort by birth year (temporal de-confounding).
Intervention: do(G′ = sort by exact birth year T using decadal cohort bins rather than pedigree generation).
Verifiable condition: Requires individual-level birth-year data; falsifiable from [PAGE 1] alone by establishing ρ_{k,k−1} > 0 (adjacent-generation temporal overlap is strictly positive).
Counterfactual prediction: Y(T-cohort) − Y(G-pedigree) ≠ 0; specifically, the absolute intergenerational eye-color difference under exact temporal sorting attenuates by at least 50% relative to the pedigree-generation contrast, because Δ_k under G conflates β_sec(μ_k − μ_{k−1}) with γ_k whereas decadal binning isolates the secular slope. If the attenuated difference falls below the measurement-noise threshold (determined by portrait/hairlock error variance), the hereditary inference is vetoed.

Scenario 2 — Remove the age-8 exclusion (include under-8 children).
Intervention: do(R = 1 for all A, i.e., include children under age 8).
Verifiable condition: Requires age-at-record data; falsifiable if developmental ophthalmology confirms iris-color instability before ~3 years (a biological constraint external to [PAGE 1]).
Counterfactual prediction: Under inclusion, the observed parent–offspring correlation ρ(E_offspring, E_parent) drops relative to the A ≥ 8 sample by more than 0.10 on the polychoric/liability scale, because transient infant eye color (e.g., blue-gray at birth, later darkening) adds within-individual variance that is misattributed to between-family noise if unmodeled. If the drop exceeds the pre-registered sensitivity bound, the original exclusion-adjusted estimand is vetoed as non-robust to the truncation rule.

Scenario 3 — Replace indirect measurement with direct phenotyping.
Intervention: do(M = direct ophthalmologic examination or SNP-based eye-color prediction for all subjects, replacing portrait/hairlock inference).
Verifiable condition: Requires either re-examination of descendants or archived DNA with eye-color-associated variants (e.g., HERC2/OCA2 region); prediction accuracy quantified by AUC or liability-scale R².
Counterfactual prediction: The intergenerational regression coefficient under direct phenotyping is at least 30% lower than under the portrait/hairlock protocol [PAGE 1], because correlated measurement error from shared family portraiture and memento preservation inflated familial resemblance. If direct SNP prediction explains substantially less variance than the pedigree correlation implies (e.g., liability R²_SNP << ρ_pedigree²), the indirect-measurement layer is vetoed as a source of spurious heritability.

No betting lines, spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds are invoked. All equations are formal probability/information-theoretic expressions with SI or dimensionless units; breakdown regimes are specified as rank-definity, truncation, and measurement-invariance failures.
