The derivation tacitly assumes zero-mean errors, additivity of displacements on a rigid one-dimensional frame, independence of the two error sources, and constant b and c. Breakdown regimes: (i) b → 0 or c → 0 are non-singular but degenerate (one source dominates completely); (ii) correlated index–bar motion introduces a covariance term 2ρxy/(bc) and invalidates the simple precision weighting; (iii) non-Gaussian heavy tails make the squared-error minimizer not the maximum-a-posteriori estimate; (iv) if Q drifts in time (the bar’s restraint weakens), the single-observation posterior is stale and the “same for all a” variance claim fails heteroskedastically. The lineage runs from Gauss–Laplace error theory through Galton’s regression to mediocrity, Pearsonian biometry, Fisherian likelihood, and forward to recursive Bayesian filtering; the page is a static inverse problem, not yet a dynamic state-estimation problem.

Formal reconstruction from [PAGE 224].

Variables and units (SI): positions measured in metres (m). Let M be the frame origin (m). Let Q ∈ ℝ be the latent bar position (m), q ∈ ℝ the observed index position (m). Define signed errors x = MQ (m) and y = Qq (m). The observed displacement is a = Mq (m), so the kinematic constraint on the rigid frame is
x + y = a. (C1)

Error models (“ordinary law of error,” Gaussian) [PAGE 224]:
x ∼ Normal(0, σ_x²), with probable error c (m), so σ_x = c / 0.67448975.
y ∼ Normal(0, σ_y²), with probable error b (m), so σ_y = b / 0.67448975.
Independence: Cov(x, y) = 0 [PAGE 224].

Operator: Galton’s minimization is the constrained maximum of the joint Gaussian density, equivalently
minimize J(x, y) = x²/c² + y²/b²  subject to (C1) [PAGE 224].
Constants from the normal density (2πσ_xσ_y)⁻¹ and the factor 1/2 are dropped because they do not shift the argmin.

Lagrangian:
ℒ(x, y, λ) = x²/c² + y²/b² + λ(x + y − a). (L1)

First-order conditions:
∂ℒ/∂x = 2x/c² + λ = 0,
∂ℒ/∂y = 2y/b² + λ = 0,
∂ℒ/∂λ = x + y − a = 0. (FOC)

Solution for the most probable values [PAGE 224]:
x′ = a · c² / (b² + c²)      (m),
y′ = a · b² / (b² + c²)      (m). (S1)

Most probable position of Q measured from M when q is observed at distance a from M:
Q̂_MP − M = x′ = a · c² / (b² + c²). (S2)

Equivalent Bayesian reading (M taken as origin): prior Q ~ Normal(0, c² in PE units), likelihood q | Q ~ Normal(Q, b²). Posterior mean:
E[Q | q = a] = (a/b²) / (1/c² + 1/b²) = a · c² / (b² + c²), consistent with (S2).

Probable error v of Q [PAGE 224], obtained by substituting y = a − x:
Posterior variance v² = (1/c² + 1/b²)⁻¹ = b²c² / (b² + c²) (m², in probable-error-squared units).
The corresponding standard deviation is σ_Q|q = bc / √(b² + c²) · (0.67448975 conversion if b, c are PEs rather than σs). Crucially, v contains no a: “is the same for all values of a” [PAGE 224].

Information units: Fisher information of the fused estimate I = 1/b² + 1/c² (m⁻²); posterior variance v² = I⁻¹ (m²).

Breakdown / singularity regimes:
- b → 0: v → 0, Q̂ → a (perfect observation).
- c → 0: v → 0, Q̂ → 0 (perfect restraint to M).
- b → ∞: v → c, Q̂ → 0 (observation uninformative; total regression to M).
- c → ∞: v → b, Q̂ → a (prior uninformative; trust observation fully).
- Cov(x, y) ≠ 0: the quadratic form becomes x²/c² + y²/b² − 2ρxy/(bc); (S1)–(S2) are invalid.
- Non-Gaussian errors: the minimum of squared error is no longer the most probable point; v² = I⁻¹ fails.
- Time-varying Q (bar drifts off M): static posterior is stale; constant b, c assumption violated.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. The page is from Francis Galton, Natural Inheritance (1889), p. 224 [PAGE 224]. Galton frames the problem as physical apparatus—bar, index, frame YMY′, restraint about mean M—because his broader project is the measurement of hereditary transmission: offspring (q) as an index of parental latent quality (Q), with M the population mean or “mediocrity.” This is the same intellectual moment that produced Galton’s regression toward the mean (mid-1870s to 1886): extreme parents tend to produce less extreme children, not because of any causal pull, but because of the convolution of a latent parental value with independent observational and developmental noise. The equation x + y = a with x = MQ and y = Qq is precisely the decomposition of an observed deviation into a “true” hereditary component and a noise component.

Scientific genealogy backward: Gauss’s Theoria Motus (1809) and Laplace’s inverse probability establish the normal error law and least squares as the probability-minimizing criterion; Galton’s “ordinary law of error” [PAGE 224] inherits this directly. The constrained minimization x²/c² + y²/b² subject to x + y = a is the normal-inverse problem: given a noisy measurement, infer the latent state. Forward in time: the same algebra appears in Bayesian normal–normal conjugacy (posterior precision = prior precision + likelihood precision), in the scalar Kalman filter measurement update (posterior mean = precision-weighted compromise; posterior variance independent of the measurement value), and in modern sensor fusion. Galton’s phrase “the same for all values of a” [PAGE 224] is the static ancestor of the Kalman filter’s covariance propagation being independent of the innovation.

Multi-week traps. (1) Static-to-dynamic misapplication: Galton’s derivation is a single-observation posterior. If an analyst recurses this estimate week after week as though Q were fixed, but the biological or physical system actually drifts (the bar’s restraint about M weakens or shifts), the posterior variance v² remains falsely constant and the estimate becomes overconfident. (2) Probable-error versus standard-deviation confusion across weeks: because b and c are probable errors, not σs, propagating them through a multi-week recursion without the 0.6745 conversion compounds scale error by (0.6745)² per stage. (3) Independence erosion: Galton assumes index and bar movements independent [PAGE 224]. Over repeated weeks, the same observer, instrument, or environment induces correlated errors (shared operator, thermal drift), introducing covariance ρ and invalidating the precision-weighted formula. (4) Range restriction: the “same for all values of a” claim holds only under homoskedastic Gaussian errors; if extreme values of a are measured with different precision (e.g., parallax at the ends of the frame), the shrinkage factor c²/(b²+c²) becomes a-dependent and the linear estimate breaks down at the tails.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the observer’s mind as a recursive inference engine that must maintain a theory of the latent quantity Q under three pressures: crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

Crisis habit regression. Under crisis (high-stakes, noisy observation, large b relative to c), the rational posterior (S2) shrinks the observed deviation a toward the prior mean M by the factor c²/(b²+c²). Psychologically this is a habit of regression: the mind discounts the surprising observation and returns toward the habitual center. The “restraint” of the bar about M [PAGE 224] is a mechanical analogue of a prior or habit; when the observation is untrustworthy, the most probable state lies near M, not near q.

Counter-trap. The complementary error is overreaction: treating the latest observation q as if it were Q itself, i.e., setting b ≈ 0 so that Q̂ → a. This is the counter-trap of recency or overfitting. In the formalism it occurs when the observer underestimates b (believes the index reading is more precise than it is) or overestimates c (believes the latent state is diffuse). The observer then places Q at or near q, violating the independence and error-scale assumptions, and inherits the full observation noise as error in Q.

Clock. The clock is the temporal dimension Galton’s static page suppresses. Q is defined relative to M at the instant of observation [PAGE 224]. If action must occur after a delay, Q may drift (the bar moves under its restraint, or the biological process changes). The posterior mean computed at time t is then evaluated against a state at t + Δt. The clock converts a spatial inference problem into a tracking problem: the relevant quantity is no longer the single-observation posterior but the predicted state under process noise. If the observer ignores the clock and applies the static shrinkage repeatedly, they regress to a stale M while the true Q has moved—regression habit without recalibration.

Recursive theory of mind synthesis. An agent modeling another agent (or itself) must maintain a nested belief: “I believe Q is near x′; I believe you believe Q is near y′.” Under crisis, both agents regress to M; under counter-trap pressure, both overweight the latest q. The clock determines which bias dominates: fast clock (rapid change) favors trusting the observation (counter-trap territory, unless b is large); slow clock (stable Q) favors regression to M (habit territory). The formal danger point is misestimating the ratio c²/b²: if the mind thinks the observation is precise when it is noisy (b underestimated), it falls into the counter-trap; if it thinks the latent state is restrained when it is diffuse (c overestimated), it over-regresses. Galton’s result that v is independent of a [PAGE 224] means the confidence interval does not widen with the extremity of the observation—a fact the mind often violates, treating extreme a as more uncertain than moderate a even under the model’s assumptions.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto hierarchy for the validity of Galton’s inference [PAGE 224], adjudicated across physical, biomechanical, and environmental (weather) layers.

Layer 1 — Physical / geometric (first-order veto). The model assumes a rigid, fixed frame YMY′, a bar constrained to one-dimensional motion along it, and an index fixed to the bar, so that displacements add exactly: x + y = a [PAGE 224]. Veto condition: if the bar can tilt, slip, or rotate out of the frame’s line, or if the index has play relative to the bar, the scalar constraint (C1) is replaced by a vector relation and the ordinary law of error in one dimension no longer applies. Singularity: at frame failure (bar leaves YMY′), the latent coordinate Q is undefined and the posterior collapses.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical / operational (second-order veto). Independence of “the movements of the index relatively to the bar and of the bar relatively to the frame” [PAGE 224] requires distinct, uncorrelated noise sources. In a biological measurement context (Galton’s actual domain: human stature and inheritance), the same observer’s eye, hand, and instrument may produce both the reading of Q and the setting of q, introducing shared operator noise: Cov(x, y) = ρσ_xσ_y ≠ 0. Veto condition: any nonzero ρ invalidates the precision weighting x²/c² + y²/b²; the correct objective includes a cross-term −2ρxy/(bc), and the most probable Q is no longer the simple shrinkage (S2). Parallax and motor tremor make b observer-dependent, breaking the “in all positions” constancy of b claimed in the text [PAGE 224].

Layer 3 — Weather / environmental (third-order veto). Temperature changes expand or contract the frame and bar (thermal expansion), and humidity or friction alter the restraint about M, making c and b functions of ambient conditions and of position along the frame. Veto condition: if b = b(a, T, humidity) or c = c(T), then the posterior variance v² = b²c²/(b²+c²) is not “the same for all values of a” [PAGE 224]; it becomes heteroskedastic, and extreme values of a (which may correspond to extreme positions on the frame) have different error scales than central values. The linear-in-a estimator (S2) is then only a local approximation.

Adjudication rule. The physical-layer veto is absolute: without a rigid one-dimensional frame and exact additivity x + y = a, the whole derivation is inapplicable. If physical geometry holds, the biomechanical veto is next: independence must be verified (ρ = 0) or a covariance term must be added. Only if both hold does the environmental layer matter; it determines whether b and c may be treated as constants. Under all three layers holding (rigid frame, independent errors, constant Gaussian scales, ordinary law of error), Galton’s result (S2) and the a-independent probable error v [PAGE 224] are valid; otherwise the quoted “most probable position” is not the most probable position under the true generative model.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards) counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable prediction.

Veto Scenario 1 — Induced correlation between index and bar errors. Construct a counterfactual measurement system in which the same actuator moves both the index and the bar, so that x and y share a common noise component: set y = η + ε_y, x = η + ε_x with Var(η) = τ² > 0 and ε_x, ε_y independent. Verifiable: estimate Cov(x, y) from repeated calibration trials and reject Cov(x, y) = 0. Prediction: Galton’s shrinkage x′ = a·c²/(b²+c²) [PAGE 224] is no longer the maximum-a-posteriori estimate. The true posterior mean contains a correction proportional to τ² and to a, and the mean-squared error of Galton’s estimator exceeds the Bayes-optimal MSE by a factor that grows with τ²/(b²+c²). Moreover, the posterior variance of Q becomes dependent on a when the correlation is heteroskedastic, directly falsifying Galton’s claim that v is “the same for all values of a” [PAGE 224].

Veto Scenario 2 — Heavy-tailed (non-Gaussian) index error. Replace the ordinary law of error [PAGE 224] for y with a Student-t distribution with ν degrees of freedom (ν < 5, so kurtosis > 3) while keeping x Gaussian. Verifiable: collect residuals y − ŷ across many trials and reject normality via a kurtosis or Jarque–Bera test. Prediction: minimizing x²/c² + y²/b² is no longer the MAP criterion; the most probable Q is obtained by a robust (redescending or Huber-type) objective. The shrinkage factor ceases to be linear in a: for large |a| the estimate saturates (less weight on extreme observations) rather than following x′ ∝ a. The claim of a constant probable error v independent of a [PAGE 224] fails because the posterior spread under heavy tails depends on the observed residual magnitude.

Veto Scenario 3 — Position-dependent observation scale (weather/biomechanical heteroskedasticity). Let the index probable error grow with displacement: b(a) = b₀(1 + κ|a|), κ > 0, while c remains constant, simulating a frame whose readability degrades toward the ends (parallax, flexure, or temperature gradient). Verifiable: bin repeated observations by |a| and estimate b in each bin; reject b = constant via an F-test or likelihood-ratio test. Prediction: the posterior variance v²(a) = b(a)²c²/(b(a)²+c²) is now an increasing function of |a|, so v is not “the same for all values of a” [PAGE 224]. The shrinkage weight c²/(b(a)²+c²) decreases with |a|: extreme observations are under-weighted relative to Galton’s linear formula, and applying (S2) with constant b₀ over-shrinks near the center and under-shrinks at the extremes. A verifiable failure is a systematic U-shaped residual pattern (observed q minus predicted Q) versus a when Galton’s constant-b estimator is used.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
