We define a formal information-theoretic framework to quantify the cognitive processes described by Tolman [PAGE 1]. Let the environment be represented as a directed graph \(G = (V, E)\), where \(V\) are locations and \(E\) are possible traversable connections. An agent occupies state \(s_t \in V\) at time \(t\).

**1. The Means-End Field Operator (\(M\)):**
This operator maps a current state \(s_t\) and a goal state \(g \in V\) to a scalar value representing the expected efficacy of pursuing \(g\) from \(s_t\), incorporating all known paths. Formally:
\[
M(s_t, g) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi} \left[ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \gamma^k R(s_{t+k}, a_{t+k}) \mid s_t, g \right]
\]
where \(\pi\) is the agent's policy, \(\gamma\) is a discount factor (<1) accounting for temporal uncertainty, and \(R\) is the reward function (non-zero only at \(g\)). In Tolman's terms, this is the "purposive" value of a path. Units: Dimensionless utility (can be normalized to [0,1]). Breakdown Regime: Fails if \(\gamma \to 1\) (no future preference) or if the graph becomes non-Markovian due to hidden states.

**2. Probabilistic Inference Operator (\(\mathcal{I}_{field}\)):**
This operator updates the belief about unobserved parts of the graph based on observed sub-paths. Let \(O\) be the set of experienced edges. The belief distribution over a hypothesized edge \(e=(u,v) \notin O\) is:
\[
P(e | O) = \frac{P(O|e)P(e)}{P(O)}
\]
Here, \(P(O|e)\) is the likelihood of observing \(O\) if edge \(e\) exists, modeling "closure." For instance, if the agent has traversed \(A \rightarrow X\) and \(X \rightarrow B\) separately, it may infer the existence of a shortcut \(A \rightarrow B\). The prior \(P(e)\) encodes structural biases (e.g., straight lines preferred). Units: Probability density. Assumptions: Static environment, rational observer. Singularity: Occurs when \(P(O)=0\) (impossible observation) or when priors dominate all data (\(P(O|e) \approx P(O|\neg e)\)).

**3. Closure Among Partial Paths Metric (\(\mathcal{C}\)):**
Adapted from Maier's experiments [PAGE 1], we define closure strength between two partial paths \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) as the mutual information between their start/end configurations given the agent's internal map \( \mathbf{m} \):
\[
\mathcal{C}(P_1, P_2) = I(S(P_1); E(P_2) \mid \mathbf{m}) 
\]
where \(S\) and \(E\) are functions returning start and end vertices. High \(\mathcal{C}\) indicates the agent expects these paths to connect seamlessly. Units: Bits. Breakdown: Requires precise definition of \(\mathbf{m}\); fails if mapping is inconsistent or noisy.

These equations operationalize Tolman's qualitative claims: "infer...the unnecessary distance" becomes a comparison of \(M(s_t, g)\) via different routes, while "recognize as but the other end of A" corresponds to high \(\mathcal{C}\) between path A and its reverse.
