Veto: non-Hausdorff topology prevents a faithful embedding into a locally compact group with a unique Haar measure; open sets \(U\) cannot separate points, so the “weather” of neighborhoods loses discriminatory power and \(\mu(E)=\rho(E)\) is not well-defined on the trace.

Adjudication order: Layer 4 must hold before Layer 3’s density argument; Layer 2 must hold before Layer 1’s regularity limit can be taken. If any layer vetoes, Theorem G or Theorem H is inapplicable, and no downstream measure-theoretic conclusion is valid.

**Veto 1 — Remove local compactness of \(X\) in Theorem G.** Let \(X\) be an infinite-dimensional Banach space under addition, equipped with a Baire measure \(\mu\) (e.g., a Gaussian cylinder measure). Prediction: the stabilizer \(Y=\{y:\mu(yE)=\mu(E)\}\) need not be closed; indeed it may be dense but non-closed, or trivial. **Veto 2 — Remove regularity of \(\mu\) while retaining local compactness.** Let \(X\) be a compact group and \(\mu\) a finitely additive Baire measure that is translation-invariant on compact sets but not countably additive or regular on all Baire sets. Verifiable test: use a non-regular measure on the Cantor group invariant under a countable dense subgroup only on clopen sets; \(Y\) contains that subgroup but its closure forces full invariance, which fails on some Baire set. Verifiable test: take a group with the indiscrete topology; every nonempty open set is the whole group, so no nontrivial thick subgroup separates points and no Haar measure on a Hausdorff completion restricts faithfully. Theorem H’s existence claim fails.
