The clock is not eschatological judgment; it is the rate at which entropy (waste heat, wasted labor, wasted capacity) outpaces the information feedback needed to steer the system. The regress must be arrested before the absorbing barrier of social breakdown is reached.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather vetoes, with adjudication.

Physical veto. The Second Law of Thermodynamics and non-ergodic statistics jointly veto any deterministic progress narrative grounded in maritime commerce. A single storm can reduce W to zero [PAGE 29]; this is an absorbing physical boundary not cancellable by virtue, prayer, or expected-value calculation. The ensemble average over many voyages does not protect the individual shipowner, because N = 1 for that body and that capital. Physical law thus imposes a hard constraint on the “progress” credo: entropy is local and ruin is path-dependent.

Biomechanical veto. The sailor’s body occupies either quarter-deck or forecastle [PAGE 29]; both are exposed to the same environmental entropy. Under industrial automation—the later subject of Wiener’s book—the human body becomes a component in a larger machine. If feedback from the biomechanical system (fatigue, injury, skill degradation) is ignored, the open-loop control produces cumulative damage. The rentier’s absentee relation to Calumet and Hecla [PAGE 29] is the economic analogue: the investor receives returns without sensing the biomechanical cost borne by miners. Veto: any system that extracts human output while severing the feedback channel is physically unsustainable over time.

Weather veto. The “Great Freeze at Point Barrow” [PAGE 29] is an exogenous meteorological shock that destroys capital independently of human virtue or market prudence. It falsifies the proposition that progress is automatic or that virtue is proportionally rewarded. Weather, in Wiener’s cybernetic sense, is a disturbance input to the control system; without a feedback channel that anticipates or absorbs it, the system fails. The Stone Fleet disaster functions identically [PAGE 29]. These are not metaphorical entropies; they are concrete weather events that terminated an entire economic regime.

Adjudication. The source text itself adjudicates against linear progress by citing empirical disasters [PAGE 29]. Wiener’s normative layer adjudicates against dehumanization: even if capital successfully reallocates to rentier stocks, the ethical entropy of treating humans as absentee inputs accumulates. The verdict is double: progress is not physically guaranteed (weather veto), and it is not morally permissible without feedback (biomechanical veto). Any model that ignores either veto is invalid.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with testable predictions.

Veto Scenario 1 — Remove Calvinist arbitrariness; substitute meritocratic election.
Intervention: Replace the theological premise “The Elect are very few, nor can any amount of virtue secure a position among them” [PAGE 29] with a meritocratic channel where P(elect | virtue) > P(elect | vice), so I(C; E) > 0.
Rationale: If virtue reliably reduces salvation uncertainty, the psychic payoff to active risk-taking changes; the merchant may not interpret maritime loss as evidence of divine arbitrariness.
Prediction: New England capital retention in maritime ventures remains higher between the 1850s and 1870s; the shift into Calumet and Hecla-type mineral equities is delayed or attenuated.
Verification criterion: Archival shipping-investment and Boston capital-allocation records show no abrupt post-Stone-Fleet divestment from sea-based assets relative to a control region without the Calvinist elect doctrine.
Failure mode: If divestment occurs anyway, the veto is falsified; the driver was material (ice, whales, copper geology), not theological.

Veto Scenario 2 — Eliminate the Stone Fleet disaster and Point Barrow freeze.
Intervention: Set p_storm and p_freeze to zero for the New England whaling and merchant fleets in the relevant decade; no “disaster of the Stone Fleet” and no “destruction of the whalers in the Great Freeze at Point Barrow” [PAGE 29].
Rationale: Without the absorbing maritime shock, the empirical basis for capital flight collapses; the expected return to sea-based capital dominates rentier bonds.
Prediction: Boston fortunes remain committed to maritime and shipping equity longer; the “community of rentiers, of absentee landlords” [PAGE 29] emerges later or not at all from this shock.
Verification criterion: Counterfactual portfolio reconstruction from Boston probate and business records shows continued heavy weighting in ships, cargo, and whaling ventures past the historical transition date; Calumet and Hecla subscription lists lack the expected Boston concentration.
Failure mode: If capital still flees to copper stocks, the veto is falsified; the driver was the geological opportunity (Agassiz’s discoveries) noted in the text [PAGE 29], not the disaster alone.

Veto Scenario 3 — Suppress Wiener’s cybernetic feedback principle in postwar automation.
Intervention: Industrial firms adopt automatic machinery without Wiener’s mandated feedback from human operators to system design; workers are treated as pure machine components, open-loop inputs with no responsibility for output, no retraining channel, and no dignity constraint.
Rationale: This instantiates the rentier pathology at the shop floor: extraction without feedback, exactly the structure Wiener warns against.
Prediction: Manufacturing labor productivity rises while real wage share and worker skill autonomy fall; social entropy (measured by strike incidence, occupational injury rates, and labor-force nonparticipation) increases relative to firms that retain feedback loops (worker councils, retraining, participatory design).
Verification criterion: Panel data from 1950–1970 manufacturing establishments show that automation adoption without operator-feedback institutions correlates with rising injury rates and falling wage share, while automation with feedback channels does not.
Failure mode: If both classes of firms show identical labor outcomes, Wiener’s feedback-as-ethical-necessity claim is falsified for this domain; the open-loop vs. closed-loop distinction has no measurable social-entropy effect.
