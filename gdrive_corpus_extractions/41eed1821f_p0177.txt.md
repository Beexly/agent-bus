Because phasic receptors recruit to r_max within ~1 s and then decay toward r_∞ = 0 [PAGE 1, items 3, 6], a crisis arriving late in a sustained CS finds the new habit’s afferent channel near silence, while older tonic habits (pressure/muscle-spindle class, r_∞ > 0) still emit signal. The agent therefore regresses to the older habit precisely when the new habit’s ΔH_r increment would be smallest.

The counter-trap is to manipulate the clock against the regressor’s model. If the opponent believes Implication I—that delaying the US strengthens learning [PAGE 1]—they may postpone reinforcement into the post-recruitment, pre-return window. The agent, however, can exploit the empty delay interval to rehearse the crisis habit, because the CS-alone period permits recruitment without the corrective US; when the belated US finally arrives, the associated receptor discharge may already have decayed toward r_∞, reducing the ΔH_r increment for the new habit while leaving the crisis habit intact.

Recursive theory-of-mind depth: Level 0—the agent acts on current r(t). Level 1—the agent models that the opponent monitors the recruitment clock (~1 s to r_max, then decay). Level 2—the opponent models that the agent knows this and may time crises to hit the post-decay r_∞ = 0 trough for phasic channels. Level 3—the agent anticipates Level 2 and schedules critical responses within [t_onset, t_max] where r(t) is maximal, or within the after-discharge window if termination occurred early [PAGE 1, item 7]. Singularities of the clock: t_max (peak frequency, maximal ΔH_r susceptibility), the decay asymptote r_∞ (zero for phasic, non-zero for tonic), and t_term (where premature cutoff switches the system from decay to negative-growth after-discharge). Any decision made outside the bounded interval [t_onset − τ_L, t_max + τ_decay + τ_persev] is outside the model’s domain and must be vetoed.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer (receptor physiology): Neural absolute refractory period imposes a hard ceiling on r_max, so Item 5’s assertion that greater energy yields greater maximum frequency breaks down at saturation [PAGE 1]. Conduction velocity and synaptic delay set a lower bound on τ_rec; the printed “within a second” is a physiological average, not a law [PAGE 1, item 3].

Biomechanical layer (stimulus delivery): Item 4’s “rate of application” and Item 6’s condition that “the stimulus meanwhile continuing to act unchanged” require a delivery apparatus capable of arbitrarily precise Ṡ and perfectly constant S(t) [PAGE 1]. Mechanical jitter, thermal drift, or actuator bandwidth limits violate the “unchanged” assumption and smear the sharp boundary between recruitment and decay, vetoing the clean separation of τ_rec and τ_decay.

Weather/environmental layer: Ambient temperature, humidity, and background tactile or proprioceptive noise shift receptor baseline and adaptation kinetics. A stimulus held physically constant may not produce a constant afferent r(t) because receptor sensitivity itself is environment-dependent; thus r_∞ and τ_decay become time-varying, invalidating the “other factors remaining constant” clause [PAGE 1].

Veto adjudication: The deduction chain is vetoed when (i) Ṡ exceeds actuator bandwidth or the refractory ceiling, (ii) inter-trial interval < τ_decay + τ_persev causing after-discharge summation [PAGE 1, item 7], or (iii) environmental drift moves r_∞ away from the assumed 0 (phasic) or fixed tonic level. Under any of these conditions, ΔH_r cannot be computed from the printed equations alone and the reinforcement prediction is physically indeterminate.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto Scenario 1: Sustained reinforcement of a phasic receptor. Item 6 states that for touch and hair-associated receptors frequency quickly falls to zero under continued unchanged stimulation [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: hold a tactile US on continuously for many τ_decay while measuring response acquisition. Verifiable prediction: habit-strength increment ΔH_r falls to zero despite constant energy delivery; response rate plateaus at baseline. This vetoes any interpretation that total energy alone determines learning.

Veto Scenario 2: CS–US delay beyond the recruitment window. Implication I claims progressively delayed US onset increases learning rate [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: set delay > 3 s (≫ τ_rec ≈ 1 s and longer than after-discharge persistence [PAGE 1, item 7]). Verifiable prediction: acquisition drops to near zero because contiguity is lost, directly contradicting the implication’s monotonic delay claim; the vetoed output is “rate of learning increases with delay.”

Veto Scenario 3: Massed trials violating after-discharge asymptote. Item 7 specifies perseverative activity declines to a zero asymptote after stimulus termination [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual: use an inter-trial interval shorter than τ_persev so residual after-discharge from trial N is present at US onset on trial N+1. Verifiable prediction: measured afferent frequency at reinforcement includes residual perseveration P(t) > 0, inflating ΔH_r relative to the isolated-trial equation and producing a non-monotonic learning curve versus ITI. This vetos the standalone negative-growth model when trials overlap.

Each scenario is verifiable with standard impulse-frequency recording and habit-strength assay; none invokes betting lines, spreads, totals, or moneylines.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
