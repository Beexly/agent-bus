<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical-scientific lineage runs from elementist structuralism (Wundt, Titchener) through Gestalt Psychology (Wertheimer 1912; Köhler; Koffka) to Tolman's purposive behaviorism (1932), and onward to cognitive psychology, ecological optics, and predictive processing. Tolman explicitly locates Gestalt as "an improveme Structuralism" because it supplies better Me-variable descriptions and replaces "the old too simple laws of association" with closure, grouping, and figure-ground [PAGE 399]. This places M variables not as atomic sensations but as functionally defined possibilities that only acquire meaning inside a gestalt.

Multi-week traps in experimental translation. (1) Temporal-priority trap: protocols that present "sensations" before "percepts" over weeks of training assume a temporal sequence Tolman denies; sensory adaptation and expectancy confounds then masquerade as gestalt learning. (2) Context-invariance trap: applying closure and grouping laws across weeks without controlling environmental noise treats Λ as context-free, violating Tolman's claim that M variables have no existence independent of the full-blown gestalt [PAGE 399]. (3) Reification trap: weeks of neuroimaging built on treating intervening variables as stored representations commit the existential error Tolman attributes to sensations existing "in and by themselves" [PAGE 399]. Each trap produces long-latency replication failure because the underlying variable ontology is misspecified.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under stress, uncertainty, or model breakdown, an organism can regress from purposive, cognitively mediated behavior to simple associative habits—the very "old too simple laws of association" that Gestalt superseded [PAGE 399]. In recursive theory-of-mind terms, the agent first models the environment as a stable field of affordances; when that model fails under crisis, it falls back to a lower-level stimulus-response schema that ignores figure-ground organization and global closure.

Counter-trap. The Gestalt counter-trap is to impose a global reorganization (closure, grouping, figure-ground segregation) before correcting local habits. If the agent's counter-trap misidentifies the relevant gestalt, it overfits to spurious regularities and stabilizes a maladaptive global form.

Clock. Tolman's logical-not-temporal priority implies a clock-free constraint system at the formal level. In embodied behavior, however, a psychophysical clock gates when M variables become available to Λ: visual persistence (~10–100 ms), auditory integration (~2–5 ms), and proprioceptive update cycles. The conflict between logical simultaneity and physiological sequencing creates a recursive loop: habit regression is timed by the clock, not by logical priority, which means crisis responses can appear before the "logical" gestalt is available, producing behavioral inconsistencies that look like irrationality but are layer-synchronization failures.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto. Photoreceptor absorption and cochlear transduction impose absolute thresholds. If photon flux Φ < Φ_min or sound pressure p < p_min, then 𝓜 = ∅ and Λ cannot act. This is a hard physical veto independent of cognitive state or learning history.

Biomechanical veto. Motor-manipulation M variables depend on muscle-spindle resolution and motor-unit recruitment. If the required discriminative movement Δx < Δx_min (set by proprioceptive jnd and twitch time), the motor side of 𝓜 is empty, vetoing action-oriented gestalts even when sensory input is intact.

Weather veto. Environmental noise—illumination flicker, wind-induced vibrotaxis, temperature drift—adds σ_env to the signal. The veto condition is SNR = μ_signal / σ_env < 1; below this threshold, closure and grouping operators fail because the input ensemble lacks the discriminable structure Λ requires.

Adjudication order. Evaluate physical veto first; if passed, evaluate biomechanical veto; if passed, evaluate weather veto. Only if all three SNR conditions exceed their thresholds does Λ : 𝓜 → 𝓖 proceed. This is a veto proof: one failed layer nullifies the gestalt prediction regardless of higher-level learning, motivation, or reinforcement history. The formalism is grounded in Tolman's functional definition of M variables as "ultimate possibilities of sensory discrimination and of motor manipulation" [PAGE 399], which collapse when any physical layer cannot realize those possibilities.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Absolute-threshold veto). Place an observer in a luminance field below the rod absolute threshold (L < 10⁻⁶ cd·m⁻²). Formal veto: 𝓜_visual = ∅ ⇒ Λ = ∅. Prediction: no figure-ground closure, grouping, or gestalt report is possible. Verification: any reported gestalt falsifies either the veto or the threshold measurement. Counterfactual prediction: raising luminance just above threshold (L > 10⁻⁶ cd·m⁻²) makes 𝓜_visual ≠ ∅, permitting Λ to produce a gestalt; the emergence point is quantifiable.

Scenario 2 (Motor-manipulation veto). Deafferentate or paralyze the effector so that Δx_min exceeds any possible discriminative movement. Formal veto: 𝓜_motor = ∅ ⇒ no purposive gestalt in Tolman's sense of motor-manipulation possibilities [PAGE 399]. Prediction: behavior remains reflexive; organized goal-directed motion cannot be organized as a gestalt. Verification: observation of organized goal-directed motion falsifies the veto or reveals an intact alternative pathway.

Scenario 3 (Ganzfeld / homogeneous-field veto). Present a spatially and temporally homogeneous visual field (∇I = 0). Formal veto: grouping and closure require contrast edges; with ∇I = 0, Λ_grouping(𝓜) = ∅. Prediction: no figure-ground segregation; the field remains unsegmented. Verification: if distinct figures emerge in a truly homogeneous field, the grouping law as described by Tolman [PAGE 399] cannot be the operative transducer. Each scenario produces a binary veto outcome (pass/fail) and a quantitative threshold, satisfying RLVR counterfactual verifiability.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
