Roubíček's Example 1 [PAGE 156] is the canonical counterexample: with $U=[-1,1]$, $g(u)=u$, $C=\{+1,-1\}$, $U_h=[-1+h,1]$, $p=1-|u|$, the true minimizer is $\{-1\}$, yet "for $c<h/2$, $\min(P_{\varepsilon,h})=1$ and $\mathrm{Argmin}=\{+1\}$" — i.e. converging to the *wrong* admissible state because the penalty parameter $\varepsilon$ raced ahead of the mesh $h$.

**(ii) Counter-trap — the coordinated schedule.** The correct behavior is a *joint* limit. Roubíček's Theorem 3 [PAGE 157-158] requires a stability criterion $h\le\eta(\varepsilon)$ with $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}E(\varepsilon,\eta(\varepsilon))=0$; for the nonlinear-elliptic example the discretization error is $E(\varepsilon,h)=\mathrm{Const.}\,(h^\gamma+h^\beta+\varepsilon^{-1}(h^\gamma+h^\beta))$ and one may take $\eta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^q,\ q>\max(-1,-1/\gamma)$ [PAGE 159]. Kunisch's Proposition 1 [PAGE 93] imposes $\delta^2/\alpha\to0$, $\eta/\alpha\to0$, $\|F_N(P_cx_0)-y_0\|^2/\alpha\to0$; Theorem 2 further requires $\eta=O(\rho+\gamma_n^{1/2})$ and $\alpha\gtrsim\max(\delta,\gamma_n^{1/2},i_n,\gamma_n,c_N)$. Kurzhanski's continuity Theorem 6.1 [PAGE 111] demands $\varepsilon\to0,\ \delta_1/\sqrt\varepsilon\to0,\ \delta_2 e^{c/\varepsilon}\to0$.

**(iii) The clock.** All three reduce to the same imperative: the *regularization strength* and the *perturbation level* (data noise $\delta$, mesh $h$, dimension $n,N$) must be coupled so the bias term and the noise-amplification term vanish jointly. There is a unique admissible timing curve; miss it and either bias dominates ($\varepsilon$ too large — over-smoothed, biased estimate) or variance dominates ($\varepsilon$ too small — noise amplified, as in the $e^{c/\varepsilon}$ backward-heat term). Samarskii's peaking models [PAGE 165-166] add a *third* clock — the physical time to blow-up $t_*$ — which sets how long any control has to act before the S/LS/HS regime commits.

**Physical/analytic vetoes that override formal optimality:**

1. **Causality (physical realizability) veto — Osipov** [PAGE 137-138]. A reconstruction algorithm $D^h$ must satisfy: if two measurement histories coincide up to $\min\{t_1,t_2\}$, the outputs must coincide on $[t_0,t_1)$. This vetoes any non-causal (whole-interval) reconstructor masquerading as a dynamical one. Program reconstruction ($w(t)$ known for all $t$ at once) is explicitly a *different, non-realizable* problem class.

2. **Parabolic smoothing veto — Fattorini–Frankowska** [PAGE 58, 61]. For the elliptic/parabolic system, admissible variation sets $\{\bar z(t)\}$ "cannot contain an interior point in $C([0,t];C(K))$, for this would mean that every function ... could be impersonated by a trajectory of the system, which contradicts smoothing properties of parabolic equations." Consequently pointwise state constraints are handleable in the parabolic case but **not** in the hyperbolic case, where results are "restricted to constraints like (4.3) of integral type on the state variables: pointwise constraints such as those in [WH1] are not included" [PAGE 61]. Energy constraint $E(y(t))\le C$ [eq.(4.3)] is the admissible surrogate.

3. **Admissibility/boundedness veto — Lasiecka** [PAGE 103, (H-1)]. The observation-admissibility inequality $\int_0^T\|B_i^*e^{A^*t}x\|^2_{U_i}\,dt\le C_T\|x\|_H^2$ is a hard analytic gate. Verbatim: unbounded $B_iG(y)$ "may destroy the generation of the feedback semigroup, let-alone the exponential stability." TheRiccard feedback is admissible *only* because $B_2^*P\in L(D(A),U_2)$ [PAGE 114, (1.7)], not because a formal ARE solution exists.

4. **Free-boundary is tangential, not physical — Zolesio** [PAGE 179]. Minimizing the Stokes energy over the free boundary $\Gamma$ "does not leads to the physical free boundary ... but to the tangential problems (2.39), (2.41) or (2.43)." The necessary condition is the transport relation along streamlines $\frac{d}{dt}[\mathcal E(u)\!\cdot\!n\!\cdot\!n](x(t))=-[\tfrac12|\mathcal E(u)|^2-f\!\cdot\!u](x(s))$ integrated, i.e. a *tangential PDE*, not an algebraic free-surface law.

5. **Constraint-qualification veto — Roubíček** [PAGE 157]. Unconditional convergence requires (10) $C=\overline{\mathrm{int}_Y C}$ and (11) a uniform preimage bound; the remark notes (11) "is typically not fulfilled in optimization problems for systems governed by differential equations" where $Y=L^2$ is strictly coarser than the energy space — so the conditional Theorem 3 schedule is the physically honest regime.
