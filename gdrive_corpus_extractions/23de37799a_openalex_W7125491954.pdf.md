The intervening lineage runs through Kovacs (2006/2007), who introduced the "work-to-rest ratios of 1:4" heuristic ([PAGE 13]), and the Giles–Reid CoD program (2020–2021) that demonstrated machine learning on Hawk-Eye centre-of-mass data (random-forest CoD detector, F1 = 0.729, [PAGE 88]). Multi-week trap #1 — the "80% < 2.5 m" reflex. The thesis documents that current practice still trains to this figure ([PAGE 44]: "coaches should update any training logic based on the previous guide that 80% of movement cycles cover <2.5m"). Because drills are periodized in mesocycles, a practitioner anchored to the 2003 distribution will systematically under-load lateral displacement by a factor of ~2, and the error compounds across a training block before any competition data surfaces it.

Multi-week trap #2 — the direction/lateral prior (Weber's 70% lateral). If a camp over-indexes lateral-only footwork because "the majority of travel is to the left or right" ([PAGE 21]), the MERG linear/forward pattern (~6.75 m, $\tau=0.96$ s) is under-rehearsed; its low frequency (n=3507 vs LIRA n=36465, [PAGE 76]) makes the omission invisible in aggregate match summaries for weeks.

Multi-week trap #3 — the velocity-zone incoherence. The literature carries "five different variants" of speed zones ([PAGE 16]) and mixed GPS/proprietary tolerances (ICC 0.1–0.7), so longitudinal load monitoring built on any single legacy threshold silently reclassifies effort intensity across software or vendor updates — a drift that only manifests after multiple monitoring cycles.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the ecosystem as a two-agent game between (A) the analyst/coach who must choose a movement-training and load-monitoring policy, and (B) the historical-baseline prior embodied in Roetert(2003)/Weber(2007) plus vendor thresholds. The "opponent" B is not adversarial but inertial: it is the cached belief that survives because it is cheap, published, and pre-loaded into drills.

Crisis-habit regression: Under time pressure (in-season, between rounds, a tournament week with <24 h turnaround), Agent A's policy reverts to the low-cognitive-cost prior. The thesis anticipates this precisely — it argues that under fatigue/heat, aggregate summaries "can under-represent true physical strain" ([PAGE 118]) and that practitioners default to "meters-per-minute" and "high-speed running" counts ([PAGE 111]). Predicted regression signature: when a tournament compresses recovery windows, coaches stop counting LERR exposures (which requires the LSTM ensemble or manual notation) and revert to total distance — the Roetert-era observable. This regression is falsifiable: instrument whether LERR-count usage drops as inter-match rest shortens.

Counter-trap: The thesis's own counter-move is to make the high-fidelity observable cheap enough to resist regression. The LSTM ensemble at "an average inference time of 1.01 seconds… 318 cycles… in 5 minutes and 21 seconds" ([PAGE 115]) converts the expensive end-range count into a post-match-automatable metric, removing the cost asymmetry that drives habit regression. If the counter-trap holds, LERR counts should become as routine as aces/winners in post-match reporting within a season of adoption.

The clock: The temporal pressure is two-sided. In-match, the athlete faces $\tau_{\text{net}}=0.44$ s (the perceptual clock). In-program, the practitioner faces the "multi-week" adoption clock — the lag between evidence publication (2024–2025) and drill/periodization revision. Recursive prediction: a coach who models the analyst's own regression (i.e., anticipates that they will revert to 2.5 m under load) will pre-commit to automated LERR logging during the calm pre-season, defeating the crisis regression. The equilibrium the thesis pushes toward is one where the classification cost is near-zero so that habit and evidence converge. Where the equilibrium breaks: if pose-model data are unavailable (only Hawk-Eye 3-D feeds support the ensemble, [PAGE 121]), the counter-trap is unavailable outside top-tier venues, and regression to the historical prior is the dominant strategy for lower-tier programs — a venue-dependent, not evidence-dependent, outcome.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. (i) Speed-viability veto: any LERR representation implying $v>5.92\,\text{m·s}^{-1}$ sustained is vetoed by the measured top-10 peak ([PAGE 94]); the naive $3.70/0.44=8.41\,\text{m·s}^{-1}$ is therefore non-physical and must be rejected as a category error (distance and time-to-net are orthogonal axes). (ii) Kinematic-coupling veto: shots struck after peak velocity "exceeded 4m/s" incur technique breakdown — "+11° of knee flexion, resulting in 14% lower racket head velocity" (females) and "+19° of hip abduction whilst maintaining racket head velocity" (males) ([PAGE 31]). This vetoes any training prescription that raises LERR peak speed above 4 m·s⁻¹ without concurrent hitting-kinematic work, because the biomechanical layer predicts shot-quality collapse; the thesis confirms this empirically as a "non-linear inverse relationship between peak speed and shot quality" ([PAGE 89]). (iii) Sliding veto: on hard court, CoD deceleration includes sliding, which "can change the biomechanical requirements" ([PAGE 78]); the −9.04 m·s⁻² figure is thus surface- and technique-conditioned and cannot be transplanted to a non-sliding surface without re-derivation.

Biomechanical/measurement layer. The decisive veto is provenance: Hawk-Eye player-tracking error is unpublished and only extrapolated ([PAGE 34], [PAGE 56]); GPS exhibits "ICC values of 0.1 – 0.7 and coefficient of variation (CV) values between 4 – 25%" ([PAGE 34]). Any cross-study comparison that mixes Hawk-Eye-derived m·s⁻² with GPS- or transducer-derived m·s⁻² is vetoed at the metrology layer; the thesis concedes "generalising these values to those produced by other technologies… is difficult" ([PAGE 80]).

Weather/environmental layer. The Australian Open is contested on outdoor hard courts with retractable-roof arenas (Rod Laver Arena, [PAGE 91]). Roof-open (ambient heat/humidity, elevated air density and ball-flight drag) versus roof-closed (climate-controlled, lower physiological strain) conditions are an unmodeled covariate across the 2021–2024 sampling windows. Because deceleration/acceleration and shot quality are fatigue- and traction-sensitive, a between-session comparison that ignores roof state and court-temperature is vetoed: the physical layer cannot certify that an observed Δd or Δa is training-induced rather than thermoregulatory. Adjudication: Chapters 4–6 remain internally valid (same venue, same vendor) but externally vetoed across surfaces, sexes, and roof states — exactly the limitation the thesis registers ([PAGE 113]: hard-court, male-only, single-vendor). Net: no physical veto survives against the thesis's internal claims; four external vetoes (surface, sex, vendor, roof-state) constrain generalization only.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is a verifiable reward test: a measurable outcome that would falsify a stated thesis claim.

Veto 1 — Base-rate generalization of the ensemble. Claim under test: the F1 = 0.944 ensemble detects LERR "at scale" ([PAGE 3]). The test set was synthetically balanced to 33% "yes" via 4× oversampling ([PAGE 113]), whereas the true LERR prevalence is "~13% of all movement cycles" ([PAGE 100]). Verifiable prediction: on an unmodified (natural ~13%) hold-out from a *different* Grand Slam, precision will fall below 0.978 because $FP$ scales with the negative base rate ($FP \approx (1-\pi)\cdot(1-\text{spec})\cdot N$). If observed F1 on the natural-prior set drops materially below 0.944 (e.g., <0.90), the "at scale" reward is denied and the balancing-induced optimism is confirmed. Falsifiable both directions.

Veto 2 — Cross-context (female/clay) transfer. Claim under test: the taxonomy and LERR kinematics are tennis-general. The corpus concedes chapters IV–VI are "senior professional male tennis players only" ([PAGE 113]) and hard-court only. Verifiable prediction: re-running the Chapter 4 pipeline on female clay-court Hawk-Eye data will *not* reproduce the same six centroids or the same LERR $\tau_{\text{net}}=0.44$ s / 3.70 m values; clay's longer rally duration (8.82 s vs hard 6.37 s, [PAGE 20]) predicts lower peak speeds and shifted time-pressure. If a female/clay ensemble requires "customised… models" (as the thesis itself states, [PAGE 121]), the transfer reward is denied. This is checkable by re-clustering.

Veto 3 — Rank-causation vs confound in shot quality. Claim under test: "higher ranked players… execute higher quality shots" during LERR because of superior physicality ([PAGE 80]). Counterfactual: if the GAM effect is actually driven by *where in their true speed envelope* players operate (top-10 nearer their sub-maximal fraction, as the thesis itself hypothesizes, [PAGE 80]), then equating peak-speed across ranks should collapse the rank gap. Verifiable prediction: in a matched-speed subsample (compare all groups at, say, 5.5 m·s⁻¹), the ~15% shot-quality advantage of top-10 should shrink toward the confidence-interval overlap already noted ($p>0.23$ between groups in the raw GAM, [PAGE 94]). If the advantage persists at matched speed, the physical-superiority reward stands; if it vanishes, the rank effect is confounded by operating-fraction, not absolute ability. The existing wide standard errors make this a genuinely open, testable veto.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
