The fast clock (seconds to minutes) is the crisis/thermal clock that can abruptly raise \(g\) through shock or cold, or veto attention altogether through flight to comfort [PAGE 471]. The English couple's hour-long sit before Titian [PAGE 471] occupies the intermediate clock: sustained posture and February cold create a drifting somatic load that gradually erodes the aesthetic frame, demonstrating that the "clock" of the body runs continuously and can expire before the cognitive appraisal is finished.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto (temperature): on a "piercing February day" in Venice [PAGE 471], ambient thermal stress constitutes a layer-zero bodily veto. If \(T_{\mathrm{ambient}} < T_{\mathrm{comfort}} - \Delta T_{\mathrm{crit}}\), thermoregulatory demand captures the attention budget; the narrator abandons the gallery for sunshine, and \(P(\text{aesthetic dwell} > 60\text{ min}) \approx 0\) for most subjects. The English couple's survival beyond one hour [PAGE 471] indicates individual variance in thermal tolerance, not the absence of the veto.

Biomechanical veto: uninterrupted sitting for more than an hour [PAGE 471] induces postural fatigue and ischaemic discomfort. Somatic noise from the musculoskeletal system competes with the art-related bodily channel, reducing effective gain on aesthetic response and potentially collapsing the episode into a judgment of mere formal fitness ("nice spot on the wall" [PAGE 471]).

Weather veto: maritime February cold in Venice is exogenous to the artwork and is not encoded in the stimulus representation \(S\). It is an additive bodily load \(b_{\mathrm{weather}}\) that enters the same sounding-board channel as art-evoked response.

Adjudication rule: the emotional-aesthetic episode persists only if
\[
g_{\mathrm{art}}\,w_{\mathrm{art}} \;>\; g_{\mathrm{thermal}}\,w_{\mathrm{thermal}} \;+\; g_{\mathrm{postural}}\,w_{\mathrm{postural}},
\]
where \(w\) are attentional weights. When the inequality fails, the subject exits to sunshine [PAGE 471] or the report collapses to "Not so very bad" [PAGE 471]. Thus bodily context is never a neutral background; it is a veto layer with priority over cognitive appraisal when its load crosses threshold.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 (thermal manipulation). Verifiable setup: expert critics view Titian's Assumption in identical galleries, one climate-neutral (22°C) and one piercing February-like (8°C), with all cognitive content equal. Veto: ambient temperature below comfort threshold vetoes reported emotional intensity. Prediction: the cold group shows significantly shorter dwell time and lower self-rated emotional intensity, while cognitive appraisal labels (composition, color, subject) remain unchanged; self-report shifts toward "Not so very bad" [PAGE 471]. Verification: dwell-time logs plus Likert ratings; no betting lines involved.

Scenario 2 (pharmacological somatic blockade). Verifiable setup: within-subject, double-blind administration of a beta-adrenergic blocker that suppresses peripheral bodily response (\(g\to0\)) while sparing cognition, versus placebo, during viewing of high- and low-expressiveness artworks. Veto: if the sounding-board is pharmacologically muted, emotional intensity collapses while appraisal persists. Prediction: blocked subjects pass a forced-choice cognitive categorization task (neat/right/witty/generous [PAGE 471]) but show no skin-conductance differentiation between artworks, and they overvalue meaningless formal fitness ("foolish trick of words" [PAGE 471]) relative to placebo. Verification: skin-conductance records and preference rankings.

Verifiable setup: longitudinal daily exposure to the same poem or musical composition for four weeks, with daily skin-conductance and rated emotional intensity, plus weekly discrimination testing. Veto: repeated exposure habituates the sounding-board, causing false attribution of refinement ("Rien ne me choque" [PAGE 471]). Prediction: within-subject physiological and rated emotional intensity decline monotonically across weeks even as discrimination accuracy rises; the slope of emotional decline predicts later overvaluation of trivial formal features ("nice spot on the wall" [PAGE 471]) over expressive content. Verification: daily physiological time series and end-of-study preference rankings.
