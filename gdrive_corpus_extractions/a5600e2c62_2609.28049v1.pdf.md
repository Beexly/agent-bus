At two opposed actors (tennis, padel, combat sports), the *court* performs the identity assignment: a net fixes which half of the frame belongs to whom, so the task collapses to detection-plus-coordinate-read, which is why consumer products publish accuracy claims here that team-sport products conspicuously do not [PAGE 5]. The authors' own first system sat at this rung — a convolutional punch classifier on USA Boxing footage under the USOPC, which "worked, in the narrow sense of reaching high accuracy on held-out punches, precisely because a two-person combat sport removes the identity problem" [PAGE 3]. The genealogy then moves to baseball (internal, unpublished), then basketball, then volleyball, and the architectural conclusion is presented as retrofitted by necessity: the model shape migrated "from a single classifier reasoning over one clip in isolation, toward the track-level, temporally integrated composition of small models" only once full-roster sport made identity a separable problem [PAGE 3].

Downstream, the published record already contains the finding this paper re-derives on harder footage. Koshkina and Elder's jersey-recognition work reports that only "a small single-digit percentage of player crops are legible enough to attempt a reading at all," attributed explicitly to the fixed camera's lack of pan and zoom, not to the reader [PAGE 5]. Their long-term tracking companion finds appearance-based re-identification confidence decaying "from near-certainty to near chance within a few hundred frames" of a player leaving and re-entering [PAGE 5]. Somers et al. score complete game-state reconstruction in "the low twenties out of a hundred" even with professional camera work; the SoccerNet challenge line improves "by under a single point year over year" with organisers naming jersey-number recognition as the bottleneck; the 2026 edition, reframed around action-to-player attribution with ground-truth tracks supplied, still recovers "well under two-thirds" [PAGE 5]. Crang et al.'s independent validation of broadcast-derived tracking against optical ground truth reports position error "ranging up to double digits of metres," with most of the error attributable to non-detection rather than imprecision [PAGE 6].

**Multi-week traps — the failure modes that persist across an entire engineering quarter rather than a single experiment.** (1) *Scale-will-fix-it.* The paper's own cost-accuracy plane refutes it: nine readers spanning "two orders of magnitude" in price differ by "roughly ten percentage points," and "the most expensive reader is not the most accurate one" [PAGE 18]; Table 3 gives $3.585 → 0.561 versus $1.142 → 0.585 [PAGE 31]. (2) *More-frames-helps.* Directly contradicted in the neighbouring literature: "giving a model more of the video (more frames, a longer temporal context) can reduce its accuracy at exactly this kind of task rather than improve it" [PAGE 8, citing Li et al. 2026]. (3) *Zoom-and-recheck.* "the accuracy gained by teaching a model to look more carefully at a region of interest is dominated by what the model already knew before it looked" [PAGE 8, citing Ma et al. 2026], and iterative self-correction's apparent gain "disappears once a deployable stopping rule replaces the benefit of hindsight" [PAGE 8, citing Tripathy and Krishnan 2026]. (4) *Local chaining.* Every rule that excluded the previously-attributed player from the next contact's candidate set "reduced accuracy relative to treating each contact's attribution as an independent decision," because one wrong upstream decision "actively removes the correct candidate from consideration at the next step" [PAGE 33]; the multi-agent analogue is documented as accumulating error across stages, with "a far simpler two-call pipeline" beating "an elaborate five-agent one" [PAGE 8]. (5) *Fixed pixel threshold.* A threshold calibrated on one camera does not transfer [PAGE 33], with the focal-length confound making two cameras at identical distance hand the reader two different pixel heights for the same digit at the same instant; corroborated by multi-camera markerless pose validation showing error concentrated in view-specific tracking breakdowns [PAGE 33, citing Nakano et al. 2020; Johnson et al. 2023]. (6) *Confidence-as-abstention*, refuted in §6.8. The paper also names one trap against itself: "a sensitivity analysis correctly identifies how costly an error class is, not which component is responsible for it" — the authors built two hand-engineered segmenters in response to a correct sensitivity finding before confirming the existing segmenter was near ceiling [PAGE 15].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three nested belief models are in play, and they fail in different directions.

**Level 1 — the practitioner's model of the model.** The reflexive habit under accuracy pressure is to add capacity, context, or reasoning steps, because that reflex was trained by benchmark culture. The paper's own framing supplies the counter-evidence: models topping LongVideoBench at 80.6% collapse to 37.9% on TOMATO against a 95.2% human baseline [PAGE 8, Table 1], and every frontier model tested lands "under 60%" on agentic multi-turn video use [PAGE 8]. The regression-to-habit is therefore not stupidity; it is a policy transferred from a distribution where it worked. The counter-trap is to condition the investment on which stage is being attacked: for action, "more temporal context, more reasoning steps, and a more capable frontier model are all reasonable investments"; for identity, "none of them … are a reasonable investment," because "the information the problem needs was never distributed across time to begin with" [PAGE 23].

**Level 2 — the coach's model of the system.** A coach reading a box score has no access to the system's internal state; what they need is a calibrated statement of where to trust it. The paper's tiered design is a direct answer: confirmed attributions presented without qualification, disagreed attributions offered as a short candidate list for one-interaction confirmation, and unresolvable contacts routed to review rather than guessed [PAGE 34]. The recursive property is that each correction becomes a training signal that "measurably improves accuracy on the remaining unconfirmed contacts belonging to the same tracked player," propagating across the track rather than the single event [PAGE 34] — the human's model of the system and the system's model of the player co-adapt within a match.

**Level 3 — the system's model of the athlete.** Here the paper's mechanistic claim bites: an athletic action is "a repeated, trained motor pattern whose temporal signature a model can exploit, while a jersey number is a static fact a temporal signal cannot recover" [PAGE 1]. The authors' own analogy is gait and voice — you can recognise a friend from their walk "at a distance too far to make out a face," but "naming who is walking … still needs the face; the gait alone narrows it down without ever quite closing the gap" [PAGE 22]. That is precisely the shape of the reported result: context narrows the candidate set and supplies more *chances* at a legible frame, but does not manufacture a digit.

**Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.** The *crisis habit* is the mid-project pivot to hand-engineered fixes; the paper documents its own instance and its own cost. The *counter-trap* is abstention-by-agreement, which converts an unsolvable perception problem into a solvable routing problem. The *clock* is the constraint that adjudicates between them: §6.9 and §6.10 show that commercial video APIs sample at a fixed rate regardless of what is asked, and the worked example is decisive — the same 13.33-second rally with seven labelled contacts, sent at one frame per second, returned `{"contacts": []}`; at three frames per second with an identical prompt it returned ten contacts tracking the true serve–receive–set–attack–dig–set–attack pattern with roughly one-second drift; and at three frames per second with a generic "Describe what is happening in this video" prompt it produced fluent prose that "narrates a blocked point and a celebration that do not appear in the labelled ground truth" [PAGE 27–29]. Frame rate determines whether the information reaches the model at all; the prompt determines whether what arrived is structured into something countable. Neither substitutes for the other, and — the sharpest observation in the section — "general-purpose video understanding, as it is typically benchmarked, controls for neither" [PAGE 29]. Under time pressure, the practitioner's habit is to blame the prompt; the clock says the prompt was never the binding constraint at 1 fps.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto is established when a failure is attributable to a physical or environmental constraint rather than to model insufficiency. The paper supplies four, each with a distinct signature.

**Optical veto on rally outcome.** The ball "regularly measures under thirty pixels across" [PAGE 1]. At that scale the landing point is not resolvable, and the paper states the consequence without hedging: outcomes "cannot be read from the pixels at all" [PAGE 1], with a frontier model asked to classify rally ending defaulting "overwhelmingly to the single most common outcome" [PAGE 17]. This veto is *absolute for the optical channel* and is the reason Operator 2 exists. It cannot be overridden by resolution purchased later, because the constraint is the source footage, not the reader.

**Biomechanical veto, with opposite signs on the two stages.** As a *permissive* veto: the motor-pattern redundancy of a serve or spike is present "redundantly, across every frame of the jump" [PAGE 22], which is why compact temporal models beat prompted reasoning at spotting and why relative court side and outcome are recoverable at accuracy no prompted model matched. As a *prohibitive* veto: no motor sequence "encodes which specific athlete is performing it" [PAGE 23], so the same temporal machinery that solves action carries zero bits about identity. The adjudication is that biomechanics licenses temporal integration for one stage and forbids it for the other — the paper's central mechanistic claim, and the one that generalises past volleyball.

**Environmental vetoes on deterministic signals.** Three are demonstrated rather than asserted. (a) *Illumination/display:* frame-differencing to catch a scoreboard change "breaks on the flicker characteristic of an LED scoreboard display, which produces frame-to-frame pixel differences indistinguishable from an actual score update" [PAGE 14]. (b) *Occupancy/acoustics:* multi-court gymnasia make audio boundaries "noisier still," and Figure 8 shows the best fixed motion threshold still misclassifying "roughly half of live-versus-dead time" because dead time is "at points, more motion-heavy than the rally itself" [PAGE 16]. (c) *Contact-type acoustics:* across all 518 labelled contacts in one match, attacks and blocks clear a 2× onset ratio "a little over half the time" while digs, "the softest and most defensive touch in the sport," clear it "barely a quarter of the time" — and digs, receives, and sets "make up most of a match's contact volume" [PAGE 17]. The veto is severity-graded: audio is systematically least informative exactly where contact volume is highest.

**Geometric/occlusion veto.** Figure 10 documents thirteen simultaneous tracked candidates with "several pairs overlapping by more than two thirds of their area," found by programmatic search rather than chosen by eye [PAGE 18], matched by the same pattern in unrelated basketball footage [PAGE 19]. Occlusion by a teammate "is a leading cause of detection dropouts, track fragmentation, and identity switches," and same-uniform teammates are "close to indistinguishable to a tracker even before any occlusion occurs" [PAGE 31]. The adjudication matters because it reclassifies the dominant identity failure: the largest loss category is *not* an unreadable digit but a readable digit that was read and then not selected — "a selection failure conditional on identity already being available, rather than an identity-reading failure" [PAGE 31] — which is why resolution doubling, not architecture, was the lever that moved it.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is stated as a falsifiable intervention with a pre-registered prediction, a measurement, and an explicit veto condition. All three are constructible from quantities reported in the source.

**Veto 1 — the resolution-ladder sub-additivity test.** *Intervention:* double working resolution a second time, on top of the doubling the paper already performed, holding architecture, data, and split fixed. *Prediction:* the marginal gain is strictly sub-additive relative to the first doubling, and smaller than the gain from ensembling independent heads — because the paper reports that "doubling the working resolution … produced a larger accuracy gain than any architectural change we subsequently tried," and that across "roughly a dozen variants," only "two changes moved accuracy meaningfully: increasing working resolution, and ensembling multiple independently trained heads" [PAGE 19, PAGE 24]. *Measurement:* held-out identity accuracy on the five per-jersey matches of Table 6, reported per match rather than pooled. *Veto condition:* if the second doubling yields a gain equal to or greater than the first, the Operator 1 optical bound is falsified as the governing constraint and the ceiling must be re-attributed to model capacity. *Secondary prediction:* whichever single match gains least should be the $h=148$ px sideline match (0.340), since $\cos\theta$ caps its effective $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ regardless of $h$.

**Veto 2 — the reader-substitution invariance test.** *Intervention:* replace the nine readers in Table 3 with a strictly larger, later, more expensive frontier model, on the identical collage set, identical tracks, identical prompt. *Prediction:* pooled exact-match identity stays inside the reported $\approx[0.46,\,0.59]$ band and the new reader almost never abstains, because "a properly fine-tuned reader converges to an accuracy well below the strongest trained-specialist result, and, more importantly, almost never declines to answer even on inputs where no correct answer is visually recoverable" [PAGE 18]. *Measurement:* (i) pooled accuracy versus the Table 3 band; (ii) per-crop concordance with existing readers, which should be high on the legible subset and near-chance agreement on the illegible subset; (iii) abstention rate, predicted to remain near zero. *Veto condition:* accuracy above 0.70 with a non-trivial abstention rate would falsify the claim that the identity gap is "bounded by pixels available in the source footage, not by the reasoning capacity applied to those pixels" [PAGE 22]. *Discriminating sub-test:* the Figure 4 track, legible "at three of the seven sampled moments and unreadable at the other four" [PAGE 14] — a stronger reader must fail specifically on the four, not uniformly.

**Veto 3 — the rule-versus-perception substitution test for rally outcome.** *Intervention:* remove the serve-transfer rule entirely and replace it with a purpose-trained specialist outcome detector operating on the same footage, at the same working resolution as the event spotter. *Prediction:* recall of correct rally winners falls to the single-pass regime the paper reports — "only about a quarter of a match's true events at all" before agentic reasoning [PAGE 7] — and errors concentrate on the modal outcome, replicating the frontier model's default behaviour [PAGE 17]. *Measurement:* agreement with the 8,048/8,051-consistent ground-truth winners on held-out matches; confusion matrix by terminal event type. *Veto condition:* a perception-based detector reaching $\ge 0.99$ agreement without the rule would falsify the claim that "where a sport's own rules fully determine an answer, no perceptual method is needed, and every perceptual method we tried performed worse than simply consulting the rule" [PAGE 17]. *Companion prediction, transfer test:* on basketball footage the same substitution should behave differently, because scoreboard reading "recovers a team's running point total essentially exactly, while frame-by-frame event perception on the same footage substantially overstates it" [PAGE 35] — a divergence predicted by rule density, not by footage difficulty, and therefore a genuine out-of-sport discriminator between the two explanations.
