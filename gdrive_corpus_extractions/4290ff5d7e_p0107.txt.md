The discriminandum/manipulanda distinction in Tolman [PAGE 107] sits at a historical inflection between purposive behaviorism and modern expectance-based accounts of action. Tolman’s “expected discriminandum” anticipates the cognitive map and the representational content of place-cell and grid-cell systems: the animal does not respond to a proximal stimulus but to a substitutable region of an intrinsic sensory order. The “manipulandum” prefigures Gibson’s affordances (the solid surface affords walking; the liquid affords swimming), though Tolman grounds the relation in an objective substitution procedure rather than in direct perception. The genealogical chain runs from Brentanian intentionality (object-directedness of acts) through Tolman’s expectance and cognitive map, to ecological psychology’s effectivities and affordances, and forward to predictive-processing and reinforcement-learning formalisms in which an agent maintains a generative model over both sensory outcomes and motor consequences.

A critical multi-week trap emerges when this framework is applied longitudinally. The intrinsic order \(\preceq_{\text{org}}\) and the manipulandum correlation \(\Gamma\) are not fixed over weeks of training. Early in maze exposure, the rat’s sense-polyhedron is sparse; after weeks, over-training can collapse flexible expectance into habitual stimulus-response chains. If an experimenter substitutes a physical character after week four and observes no disruption, the result cannot be interpreted as evidence that the original manipulandum was incidental; the animal may have regressed to a rigid habit that ignores the missing support, or alternatively may have remapped its polyhedron so that the substitute now lies within \(\mathcal{D}\) or \(\mathcal{M}\). Thus null substitution results in multi-week designs are ambiguous between (i) true substitutability within the intrinsic order and (ii) habit-driven insensitivity to support violation. The trap is compounded because Tolman’s procedure requires discovering both substitutable and non-substitutable characters [PAGE 107]; a habit-regressed animal may fail to show the expected disruption for non-substitutable characters, producing false positives for “equally well” substitution.

Another lineage trap is the retrospective projection of metric structure onto the “color pyramid” analogy. Tolman explicitly grounds the polyhedron in “purely objective discrimination experiments” [PAGE 107], not in Euclidean geometry. Later theorists who treat the space as a fixed Riemannian manifold risk importing singularities (e.g., assuming smooth gradients of just-noticeable difference) that do not obtain under satiation, stress, or sensory adaptation.

The recursive theory-of-mind layer is that the observer must model whether the rat is modeling the maze as a space of expected supports or simply executing a regressed habit. First-order ToM: “the rat expects a solid surface.” Second-order ToM: “the observer models the rat as expecting a solid surface.” Third-order: “the observer models the rat as modeling the observer’s manipulation of the maze.” Crisis collapses this recursion to depth zero or one: the rat no longer maintains the nested expectance structure; it reacts.

The counter-trap is observer over-mentalization: attributing purposeful manipulandum-expectance to a behavior that is actually a fixed action pattern released by a releaser stimulus. To adjudicate, one must introduce a counter-trap test—violate the manipulandum while preserving the discriminandum and observe whether the act is suppressed. If suppression occurs, expectance is supported; if the act proceeds anyway, habit regression or a lower-depth controller is implicated.

The clock is the expectancy decay function over \(\Delta t\). Tolman’s “immediately-to-come” implies a finite temporal window. Define a critical latency \(\tau_c\) such that if the expected manipulandum \(m\) becomes available at \(t_m > t + \tau_c\), \(\operatorname{release}(A,t) = 0\) even if \(m \in \mathcal{M}(A)\). The clock is not a separate metaphysical entity but the organism’s decay of preparatory activation; once it crosses threshold, the behavior-act is aborted and must be re-initiated from a new expectance state. In multi-week protocols, \(\tau_c\) itself may shorten with training (habit speeds up) or lengthen under stress (cautious hesitation), further confounding cross-session comparisons.

Physical veto: A behavior-act is physically vetoed when no element of \(\mathcal{M}(A)\) satisfies the material constraints of the environment. Biomechanical veto: Climbing requires “an actual physical height to chımb over” [PAGE 107]. If the vertical extent \(h\) falls below the organism’s center-of-mass displacement threshold \(h_{\min}\), or if the available grip surfaces cannot support the normal forces required for limb propulsion, the climb act is vetoed. The veto condition is formal: \(\exists m \in \mathcal{M}(\text{climb})\) such that \(h(m) \ge h_{\min}\) and \(\text{grip\_feasible}(m) = \text{true}\); otherwise \(\operatorname{release}(\text{climb},t) = 0\).

Weather/medium veto: Swimming requires “a liquid to swim in” and vocalization requires “a gaseous medium to vocalize with” [PAGE 107]. If ambient temperature drops below the freezing point of the medium, the liquid becomes solid and the swim act is vetoed; buoyancy and drag coefficients change discontinuously at the phase transition. If the gaseous medium is removed (pressure \(P \to 0\)), the vocalization act is vetoed because phonation requires a pressure differential across the vocal apparatus; sound pressure level \(L_p\) cannot be generated in vacuum. Viscosity \(\eta\) and density \(\rho\) of the liquid also enter the Reynolds number \(Re = \rho v L / \eta\); outside the viable \(Re\) range for the organism’s locomotor gait, propulsion efficiency collapses and the swim act is vetoed.

Adjudication rule: For any act \(A\), compute the feasible set \(\mathcal{F}(A,t) = \{ m \in \mathcal{M}(A) \;|\; \text{physical\_constraints}(m, \text{environment}, t) \text{ satisfied} \}\). If \(\mathcal{F}(A,t) = \emptyset\), the act is vetoed at the cross-layer boundary. This veto is independent of the discriminandum \(\mathcal{D}\); a fully supported sensory expectation cannot overcome a missing or physically unrealizable manipulandum.
