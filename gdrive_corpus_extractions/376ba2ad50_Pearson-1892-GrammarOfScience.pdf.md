Pearson's worked cases: pure success ($q=0$) gives odds $(p{+}1):1$ — "two to one" for a single solidification of hydrogen ($p=1$), "1,000,001 to 1" for the sun risen $10^6$ times ([PAGE 141]–[PAGE 142]). Units: dimensionless probability. Assumptions: exchangeable trials, binary outcome, **uniform prior** ("equal distribution of our ignorance," [PAGE 145]). Breakdown regime: small *p* (estimate unreliable — Pearson: "only some likelihood"), any $q>0$ (miracle term, [PAGE 142]), and expressly the *infinite* future — Pearson's own footnote forbids extrapolating the bound to an infinite horizon ("We cannot, however, argue from this result for an infinite future of repetition," [PAGE 149]).

**(E2) Contingency table and independence** (p. 160). With cell counts $n_{\alpha\beta}$, margins $n_{\alpha\cdot},n_{\cdot\beta}$, total $N$, the expected cell under independence is

$$V_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{n_{\alpha\cdot}\,n_{\cdot\beta}}{N},$$

and independence is $n_{\alpha\beta}=V_{\alpha\beta}$ for every cell.

**(E3) Mean square contingency** (pp. 161–163). Summing the per‑cell quantity $\big[(n_{\alpha\beta}-V_{\alpha\beta})/V_{\alpha\beta}\big]^2\cdot(V_{\alpha\beta}/N)$ gives

$$S=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{(n_{\alpha\beta}-V_{\alpha\beta})^2}{V_{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{\chi^2}{N}.$$

This is literally $\chi^2/N$ — the historical germ of Pearson's chi‑square. Units: dimensionless. Range: $S=0$ under independence; $S=m-1$ under perfect (diagonal) association with $m$ classes of $A$; $S\to\infty$ as classification is refined toward a functional relation $B=f(A)$ ([PAGE 163]). Breakdown: asymptotic in $N$; Pearson notes (p. 163 footnote) unstated corrections for cell number and sample size.

**(E4) Coefficient of contingency** (p. 164).

$$C=\sqrt{\frac{S}{1+S}}\in[0,1),$$

the monotone map of $S$ onto the unit interval; $C\to 1$ only as $S\to\infty$, so for any finite table $C<1$ (a limitation Pearson flags).

**(E5) Correlation ratio** (pp. 174–176). Decomposing the total variance of $B$ into within‑array and between‑array parts, $u=\sigma^2_{\text{within}}/\sigma^2_{\text{total}}$ and

$$\eta^2=1-u=\frac{\sigma^2_{\text{array means}}}{\sigma^2_{\text{total}}}\in[0,1].$$

$\eta=1$ ⇒ belt collapses to a curve (functional/causal); $\eta=0$ ⇒ every array reproduces the population (independence). For near‑normal frequency $\eta\to|r|$ (Pearson's correlation), which is why $C$ and $\eta$ "take numerically very close values" in practice ([PAGE 177]).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage running *into* the text is explicit and traceable. Ernst Mach supplies the phenomenalist frame (the "Analysis of the Sensations," quoted at [PAGE 65], catalogued at [PAGE 100]); W. K. Clifford supplies the vocabulary of "ejects," "mind‑stuff," and "tribal conscience" ([PAGE 49], [PAGE 73], [PAGE 5]); Newton supplies the *regula philosophandi* against superfluous causes, quoted in Latin at [PAGE 92]; Kirchhoff supplies the descriptive definition of mechanics — "the complete description in the simplest possible manner of such motions as occur in nature" ([PAGE 115]); Clerk‑Maxwell supplies the demon that relativises the second law ([PAGE 83]); Laplace supplies the probability engine; Boole (*Laws of Thought*, [PAGE 146]) and Edgeworth ([PAGE 146]) supply the critique/defence of the uniform prior; Jevons, Venn, Mill, Herschel, Galton, Weldon and Yule are the immediate Victorian statistical‑inductive milieu ([PAGE 14], [PAGE 55], [PAGE 130], [PAGE 151]).

Running *out of* the text: (E3)–(E5) are the direct ancestors of 20th‑century mathematical statistics — $\chi^2/N$ becomes the chi‑square test, $C$ the contingency coefficient, $\eta$ the correlation ratio and ANOVA decomposition (Fisher). Pearson's own 1904 memoir "On the Theory of Contingency" (Mathematical Contributions XIII, cited [PAGE 202]) is the primary source. The "economy of thought" doctrine converges with Mach and later with logical positivism; the relativity chapter (Chapter X, per [PAGE 23]) ties the book to Einstein via Pearson's Galton Laboratory and E. Cunningham.

"Multi‑week traps" — read as the durable interpretive errors Pearson diagnoses: (a) the double sense of "law" (nomic routine vs scientific formula, [PAGE 87]–[PAGE 88]), which let Stoics, Hooker and natural theologians equivocate for centuries ([PAGE 88]–[PAGE 90]); (b) "force" as a fetish — "the will of the old spiritualist separated from consciousness" ([PAGE 119]); (c) the reification of "matter" and "atom" into experience‑transcendent reals ([PAGE 9], [PAGE 120]). These are the recurrent traps a careful reader must not spring.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Pearson's own theory of mind is explicitly recursive and self‑limiting. Other‑consciousness is an *eject* — inferred, not perceived ([PAGE 48]–[PAGE 50]); the self is "seated at the brain terminals of the sensory nerves," an exchange‑clerk who never leaves the switchboard ([PAGE 61]), with the telephone‑exchange model at [PAGE 44]–[PAGE 46]. Consciousness is the *interval* between sense‑impression and exertion, filled by the "resonance" of stored impressions; its measure scales with (1) the extent/variety of past impressions and (2) the "complexity and plasticity of the brain" ([PAGE 44]). Crucially, this is a physical‑substructural theory: destroy a commissure and you get aphasia or the loss of the moral sense ([PAGE 125]–[PAGE 126]) — mind as an individuated mechanism, "I act as I do, because I am I" ([PAGE 126]).

Against this, the *counter‑trap* is the projection of mind outward: inferring reason, will, or consciousness "beyond the barrier of sense‑impression" ([PAGE 132]), which Pearson classes with the centaur — "self‑contradictory" ([PAGE 53], [PAGE 58]). The recursive discipline that keeps the trap shut is *self‑criticism*: Darwin's nineteen‑year delay and Newton's eighteen‑year suppression of a result pending a missing link ([PAGE 33]–[PAGE 34]), Faraday's remark that not a tenth of a scientist's suggestions survive his own criticism ([PAGE 32]). The motto *La critique est la vie de la science* is the governing control law ([PAGE 7], [PAGE 31]).

The "clock" — crisis‑regression vs counter‑trap. Under epistemic crisis (an *anomy*, a breach of routine), the regression mode is to leap to the supersensuous: "our churches still offer up prayers for the weather" ([PAGE 105]); spiritualism and theosophy rush into the gap left by unsolved problems ([PAGE 56]–[PAGE 57]). Pearson's counter‑trap is disciplined doubt: "over‑doubt is more socially valuable than over‑credulity" ([PAGE 54]). His clock is the probability engine (E1): the routine has held so long that the odds against a breach in "our generation" are, on his own figures, "1000 millions to one," and "miracles have been proved incredible" ([PAGE 143]). The recursive move is that the reasoner must include *itself* (its diseased or ecstatic states) in the population of unreliable perceptive faculties when discounting an anomaly.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Model a "veto" as a lower (physical) layer overriding a higher‑order claim. Pearson deploys exactly such a veto against metaphysics. Let $P$ be the set of possible sense‑impressions. A conception $c$ has scientific validity iff it is (i) self‑consistent and (ii) deducible from the perceptions of the normal human ([PAGE 53]). The veto rule: if $c\notin\overline{P}$ (not even possibly a sense‑impression), then "knowledge($c$)" is *undefined* — "an unmeaning x among an unthinkable y and z" ([PAGE 75], [PAGE 98]). This formally vetoes the thing‑in‑itself and metaphysical supersensuousness ([PAGE 96]), while admitting atom/electron/ether as *provisional* constructs because they lie in $\overline{P}$ ([PAGE 65], [PAGE 96]).

Physical veto #1 (thermodynamic). The cleanest is Clerk‑Maxwell's demon: a being who "can see the individual molecules" and sorts fast from slow "will, without expenditure of work, raise the temperature of B and lower that of A, in contradiction to the second law of thermodynamics" ([PAGE 83]). This *vetoes the absolute status* of a paradigm natural law and proves Pearson's thesis that "scientific law does not… lie in a universe outside and unconditioned by ourselves" — natural law is relative to the perceptive faculty ([PAGE 84]). Verdict: the veto holds against naïve Laplacian necessity.

Biomechanical veto. Consciousness is welded to nervous structure: "Consciousness without a nervous system is like a man without a vertebral column — 'a chimera'" ([PAGE 58]); aphasia and the extinction of conscience follow from severing specific commissures ([PAGE 125]–[PAGE 126]). This vetoes disembodied will/consciousness as an explanation of motion ([PAGE 122]–[PAGE 127]).

Weather veto (the residual anomy). Pearson treats weather as the last great un‑reduced routine and predicts it will yield to law — "the discovery of a hundred or a five hundred years' period in the weather" ([PAGE 105]). This is a falsifiable cross‑layer claim: atmospheric dynamics *is* deterministic (Navier–Stokes + thermodynamics), yet sensitively dependent on initial conditions — a regime where Pearson's contingency layer (correlation bounded below 1) is the correct description and *causal* determinism is the correct micro‑description. The two layers do not contradict; they partition by scale, which is itself a partial veto on Pearson's stronger phenomenalist claim that the routine is contributed by the *recipient*.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual scenarios, each with the prediction Pearson's framework implies and the empirical verdict.

**(1) The demon's information ledger.** Counterfactual: if acquiring molecular information were thermodynamically free, the demon would violate the second law and Pearson's strong observer‑relativity ([PAGE 84]) would stand unqualified. Prediction under a strict phenomenalist reading: the law is merely perspectival and the demon breaks it. Actual resolution (Szilárd 1929; Landauer 1961; Bennett 1982): erasing one bit of the demon's memory costs at least $kT\ln 2$ joules (≈ $3\times10^{-21}$ J at 300 K), so once the demon is included as a physical information‑processor the second law is *rescued*. Veto verdict: this refutes the strong reading (law is subjective) while confirming Pearson's deeper point — there is no observer‑external standpoint; the demon is inside the same physics.

**(2) Molecular individuality.** Pearson hypothesises "an ultimate individuality, a variation within the class" even for inorganic matter — absolute sameness is only "statistical" ([PAGE 155]–[PAGE 156]). Counterfactual veto: if molecules were *absolutely* identical (his "conceptual" sameness), no physical process could ever distinguish two "like" molecules, and the sea could not, even in principle, sort them ([PAGE 155]). Prediction/test: any demonstration of unit‑level discreteness falsifies absolute sameness. Confirmed: isotopic separation (deuterium; $^{235}$U/$^{238}$U), Einstein's 1905 Brownian analysis and Perrin's determination of $N_A$, and modern single‑atom imaging all instantiate Pearson's individuality hypothesis. Verdict: his statistical‑sameness claim is vindicated; the "dead‑matter" absolute is vetoed.

**(3) Causation dissolves into correlation under refinement.** Prediction (Pearson's "inverted telescope," [PAGE 169]): as measurement precision rises, any alleged deterministic law resolves into a scatter/array with correlation coefficient strictly below unity; "nothing in the universe ever has or ever will exactly repeat itself" ([PAGE 153]). Counterfactual veto: if some pair of quantities yielded $\eta=1$ exactly under arbitrarily fine measurement, causation would be a real feature, not a conceptual limit, and Chapter V would be overthrown. Empirical verdict is *scale‑partitioned*: for idealised macroscopic systems $\eta\to 1$ (near‑functional), so the physicist's causation survives as an excellent approximation; for turbulent, meteorological, biological and social data the correlation is bounded away from 1, vindicating the contingency category (E3)–(E5). Net: Pearson is right that no *actual* experience demonstrates the causal limit, and right that the useful object is the array, not the curve — but the physicist's veto holds wherever the belt is empirically indistinguishable from a line.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
