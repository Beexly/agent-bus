Knight (1921) extends rent logic beyond land to any "restricted and restricting agency" [PAGE 187], making restriction analytically equivalent to a physical peculiarity. Lavergne's 1910 *Théorie des marchés Économiques* represents the French marginalist inclination to reify the "idée productrice" as a fourth factor [PAGE 187]. A. S. Johnson's *Rent in Modern Economic Theory* (cited at p. 120, note) represents the opposing view that ideas, because they multiply indefinitely, cannot be scarce factors [PAGE 187]. Knight's empiricist pivot—"the crucial test can only be the facts in the case"—anticipates the later uncertainty-and-profit theme of the book and the Chicago emphasis on empirical over axiomatic factor classification.

Forward lineage: Schumpeter's theory of entrepreneurial profit from temporary monopoly codifies the rent from exclusive control of a method; Arrow's (1962) information paradox formalizes Johnson's point that an idea is difficult to sell because disclosure destroys its exclusivity; modern intellectual-property economics treats \( \sigma(\mathcal{M}) \) as an endogenous legal investment rather than a given.

Multi-week traps: (1) Treating salability as exogenous ignores that patent prosecution, secrecy maintenance, and litigation operate on week-to-month timescales, so \( \sigma(\mathcal{M}) \) is time-varying and can flip during the commercialization window. (2) Treating diffusion cost as near-zero [PAGE 187] ignores tacit knowledge, complementary assets, and trainer scarcity, which make "rapid and general extension" sticky over multi-week adoption horizons. (3) Conflating maximum consumption with maximum welfare—explicitly distinguished in the footnote [PAGE 187]—means a policy optimizing for fastest diffusion may maximize short-run output while reducing welfare as labor and organizational routines adjust over weeks.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under Knightian uncertainty, the possessor of an effective method does not face a stationary payoff distribution. The recursive belief is: "Others will attempt to imitate or appropriate \( \mathcal{M} \), and because the future is non-stationary, I cannot insure against appropriation." Consequently, the possessor regresses to a crisis habit of restriction—secrecy, patents, non-disclosure, exclusive control [PAGE 187]. This habit raises \( \sigma(\mathcal{M}) \) toward 1 by investing in legal exclusion, even when the method's intrinsic information content \( I(\mathcal{M}) \) is low.

Counter-trap: The theorist or policymaker who assumes "the highest social advantage would require the most rapid and general extension" [PAGE 187] may pre-commit to compulsory diffusion or non-salability. The recursive counter-move by the possessor is to withhold discovery effort: if \( \mathcal{M} \) cannot be salable and cannot command capital value \( V_{\mathcal{M}} \), the expected return collapses from rent/wage hybrid to a mere execution wage \( w_{\mathcal{M}} \), and the incentive to search for \( \mathcal{M} \) erodes. The counter-trap is therefore not monopoly per se, but the destruction of the discovery margin by assuming diffusion costlessness.

Clock: The discrete-time trajectory of \( \mathcal{M} \) is \( t_0 \) (discovery), \( t_1 \) (exclusion decision, \( \sigma \in \{0,1\} \)), \( t_2 \) (imitation, reverse-engineering, or leakage), \( t_3 \) (general diffusion), \( t_4 \) (obsolescence). Over weeks, the wage/capital-value boundary migrates: before \( t_2 \), yield may be capitalized as \( V_{\mathcal{M}} \); after \( t_2 \), if the secret is out, yield reverts to competitive wage for executing the now-public method [PAGE 187]. The possessor's model of the regulator's model of salability determines whether \( t_1 \) occurs at all.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Restriction \( \rho \) cannot increase exergy or violate conservation laws. If \( \mathcal{A} \) is land, \( \partial Y / \partial \mathcal{A}_r \) is bounded by soil fertility, solar flux (W/m²), and water availability; limitation raises imputed rent but not physical productivity beyond thermodynamic bounds [PAGE 187]. Biomechanical layer veto: If \( \mathcal{M} \) is executed by human labor, the possessor's wage \( w_{\mathcal{M}} \) is bounded by cognitive channel capacity and metabolic work rate (bits/s, J/s). A method cannot be productively diffused beyond the number of agents capable of embodying it; tacit knowledge imposes a biomechanical ceiling on general extension. Weather/environmental layer veto: For location- or climate-specific methods (e.g., agricultural organization), \( \pi_{\mathcal{M}}(t) \) is stochastic in weather \( \omega_t \). Exclusive control is valuable only if \( \operatorname{Cov}(\pi_{\mathcal{M}}, \omega) \neq 0 \); in stable environments, the restriction rent \( r_\rho \) vanishes because the method offers no state-contingent advantage.

Veto proof: Knight's assertion that social advantage requires "the most rapid and general extension of the use of the best methods" [PAGE 187] is physically vetoed if the transfer channel—blueprints, trainers, machinery, or communication bandwidth—has finite capacity \( C_{\text{phys}} \) (bits/s or agents/period). If \( C_{\text{phys}} < \) demand for adoption, rapid general extension is impossible, not merely economically costly. Likewise, if \( \mathcal{M} \) is embodied in a unique physical agency (a single mine, a specific machine), restriction is indistinguishable from physical incapacity \( \omega \), and no policy intervention can separate the causality aspect from the social-policy aspect without a physical counterfactual (an identical unrestricted deposit) [PAGE 187].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Compulsory non-salability. A regulator mandates that all newly discovered production methods enter the public domain immediately upon invention, setting \( p_{\mathcal{M}} = 0 \) and \( \sigma(\mathcal{M}) = 0 \) by fiat. Verifiable via R&D expenditure and patent-filing time series before and after the policy. Prediction: \( V_{\mathcal{M}} \to 0 \) because the capitalization integral loses its revenue stream; yield shifts from capital value to execution wage \( w_{\mathcal{M}} \) [PAGE 187]; private discovery rate \( \lambda_{\text{disc}} \) falls measurably within 6–12 months; diffusion speed rises but total method stock stagnates due to discovery erosion.

Scenario 2 — Exclusivity breakdown by independent rediscovery. A second agent reverse-engineers \( \mathcal{M} \) without access to the secret, flipping \( \sigma(\mathcal{M}) \) from 1 to 0. Verifiable via patent interference records, trade-secret litigation, or publication dates. Prediction: \( V_{\mathcal{M}} \) collapses to scrap value; restriction rent \( r_\rho \to 0 \); the original possessor's income reverts to a competitive wage for executing the method [PAGE 187]; imitation cascades on a timescale of weeks to months depending on channel capacity.

Scenario 3 — Physical restriction versus strategic restriction. A unique agency (e.g., a single mineral deposit) is restricted not by legal exclusion but by depletion—"part of it had gone out of existence" [PAGE 187]. Verifiable via geological output and reserve data. Prediction: Causal productivity \( \Pi_c(\mathcal{A}_r) \) per remaining unit rises, but total output \( Y \) falls; rent appears as scarcity rent. Without a physical counterfactual (an observationally identical unrestricted deposit), social policy cannot attribute the observed income to causality versus strategic restriction, and any tax or expropriation policy based on imputed productivity is under-identified.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
