Breakdown: "non-overlapping bands" is idealized; a finite-order Butterworth has nonzero transition width, so spectral leakage occurs near cutoffs (1 Hz, 4 Hz). Nyquist constraint: $f_s \ge 2f_{\max}\Rightarrow f_{\max}\le 25$ Hz at $f_s=50$ Hz.

2. Slot cross-attention (competition normalized over slots) [PAGE 3]. For slot $i$, input frame $j$:
$$A_{ij}=\frac{\exp\!\big(\langle k(\mathbf s_i),q(\mathbf x_j)\rangle/\sqrt{D}\big)}{\sum_{l=1}^{S}\exp\!\big(\langle k(\mathbf s_l),q(\mathbf x_j)\rangle/\sqrt{D}\big)},\qquad \mathbf u_i=\frac{\sum_j A_{ij}\,v(\mathbf x_j)}{\sum_j A_{ij}},$$
then GRU+MLP, iterated $U=3$. Logits dimensionless; $D$ = slot dimension. With $S=8$, $D=32$: embedding $=256$ [PAGE 6]. Assumption: fixed learnable $\mathbf s_i^{(0)}$. Breakdown: if $S$ exceeds the number of distinguishable signal components, slots deactivate (norm collapse), consistent with the observed drop at 32 slots [PAGE 17–18].

3. SSIM loss (dimensionless; $\mathcal L_{\text{SSIM}}\in[0,1]$ by the paper's assertion) [PAGE 5]:
$$\mathcal L_{\text{SSIM}}=1-\frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y+C_1)(2\sigma_{xy}+C_2)}{(\mu_x^2+\mu_y^2+C_1)(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+C_2)}.$$
Units: $\mu\in\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\sigma^2,\sigma_{xy}\in(\mathrm{m/s^2})^2$; $C_1=(K_1 L_d)^2$, $C_2=(K_2 L_d)^2$ with dynamic range $L_d$. Breakdown/indeterminacy: for a locally constant window ($\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2\to0$) the ratio is $0/0$ without $C_2$ — hence the stabilizer's necessity.

4. MS-STFT loss [PAGE 5]:
$$\mathcal L_{\text{MS-STFT}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal F|}\sum_{n_{\text{fft}}\in\mathcal F}\Big[\mathrm{MAE}(|X_{n_{\text{fft}}}|,|Y_{n_{\text{fft}}}|)+\mathrm{MSE}(|X_{n_{\text{fft}}}|,|Y_{n_{\text{fft}}}|)\Big],\ \mathcal F=\{16,32,64,128\}.$$
Hann window length $n_{\text{fft}}$, hop $n_{\text{fft}}/4$. Time–frequency resolution floor (Gabor–Heisenberg), $\Delta t\,\Delta f\ge 1/4\pi$, realized per scale as $\Delta t=n_{\text{fft}}/f_s$, $\Delta f=f_s/n_{\text{fft}}$: at $n_{\text{fft}}=16$, $(\Delta t,\Delta f)=(0.32\,\text s,\,3.125\,\text{Hz})$; at $128$, $(2.56\,\text s,\,0.39\,\text{Hz})$ — the multi-scale set spans coarse-to-fine. Breakdown: spectral magnitudes are unbounded, so no inherent normalization.

5. Total objective (per band, weighted) [PAGE 5]:
$$\mathcal L_{\text{total}}=\alpha\,\mathcal L_{\text{MSE}}+\beta\,\mathcal L_{\text{SSIM}}+\gamma\,\mathcal L_{\text{MS-STFT}},$$
with (Table 5) low $=(\alpha{=}1,\beta{=}0,\gamma{=}0)$; mid $=(1,100,0.1)$; high $=(0,50,0.1)$ [PAGE 14]. The $\beta\gg\alpha$ scaling is required because $\mathcal L_{\text{SSIM}}$ is $O(1)$-bounded while MSE/MS-STFT are unbounded [PAGE 14].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage and the multi-week methodological traps.

Direct ancestry runs Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) → SlotFM [PAGE 1, 3]. The paper's single most consequential departure — replacing distributional slot resampling with "fixed learnable vectors" [PAGE 3] — is a targeted rebuttal to the object-centric assumption that slot permutation symmetry is harmless; for a *reading* head it is not (see THINK_TRACE). Signal-decomposition ancestry: 4th-order Butterworth bandpass justified by Fridolfsson et al. (2019) on human-movement frequency [PAGE 4, 10]. Loss ancestry is cross-domain transfer: SSIM from image restoration (Zhao et al., 2016) and MS-STFT from neural audio codec work (Défossez et al., 2023 / EnCodec) [PAGE 4, 10] — an instance of importing inductive biases across sensing modalities.

The accelerometer-SSL genealogy the paper positions against: Autoencoder (Haresamudram 2019), Masked Autoencoder (Haresamudram 2020), SimCLR (Tang 2020), Augmentation Prediction (Yuan 2024), REBAR (Xu 2024), RelCon (Xu 2025) [PAGE 2–3, 15–16]. Pre-training corpus CAPTURE-24 (Chan et al., 2024), "approximately 2,500 hours of data from 151 participants" [PAGE 6], with standard-deviation-weighted sampling from Yuan et al. (2024) [PAGE 6].

The recurring traps: (1) the *smoothing trap* — reconstruction with MSE alone collapses to conservative low-frequency predictions, cited to Cuturi & Blondel (2017) and Mathieu et al. (2015) [PAGE 4]; (2) the *invariance trap* — augmentation-based methods bake in feature invariances that help one task and harm another; the paper states rotation invariance aids activity recognition yet "the same invariance can hinder tasks like gait analysis, where orientation carries meaningful information" [PAGE 1]; (3) the *over-decomposition trap* — empirically, "performance begins to degrade from 6 bands onward" [PAGE 17] and 12 bands cause "a significant drop" [PAGE 17], because high bands are near-empty given sub-10 Hz energy [PAGE 16].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reinterpreted as modeling the assumptions ("beliefs") of competing method-designers and the adversarial fit between a method's inductive bias and a task's discriminative statistic — the closest honest analogue to intent-modeling for this source.

Each baseline encodes a designer's belief about "what must be preserved/invariant": SimCLR believes augmentations (scaling, time-warping, p=0.3) [PAGE 15] define semantic equivalence; Augmentation Prediction believes invariance to shuffle/rotation plus predictability of timeflip/permute/timewarp [PAGE 16]; Autoencoder/Masked-AE believe full reconstruction / masked reconstruction preserves information [PAGE 15]. SlotFM's designer-belief is weaker and more general: preserve *distinct frequency-and-time components* in separate slots, impose no task-specific invariance. The recursive layer is the downstream head's "theory of mind" over slots: Figure 4 shows heads learn to weight slots non-uniformly per task, e.g., "the 4th and 5th slots that attend to the beginning of the signal receive higher weights" for the brief, early-window Writing gestures [PAGE 8]. This is the head *reasoning about which slot-source to trust*, which only becomes possible because fixed init gives slots a stable referent — the counter to the resampling "regression to inconsistent semantics" failure the paper names: "each slot captures different information on each pass even for the same input" [PAGE 9]. The "clock" analogue is training/early-stopping dynamics: pre-training halts via early stopping after 40 epochs of 500 [PAGE 6]; downstream after 10 of 200 [PAGE 7] — the temporal budget within which slot–head co-adaptation must converge. The trap being countered: a head trained against a moving (permutation-varying) target never converges to a stable read-out policy.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical veto proofs (hard constraints that override any leaderboard claim).

Veto 1 — Nyquist. With $f_s=50$ Hz, recoverable band is $[0,25]$ Hz. Any downstream claim whose discriminative energy sits above 25 Hz is physically inadmissible under this pre-training pipeline. The paper honors this: TennisShot (120 Hz native) and Writing (100 Hz) are explicitly *not* downsampled and are zero/repeat-padded to fit [PAGE 14], i.e., handled outside the 50 Hz regime. Proof of consistency: Table 6's 3-band top edge is 20 Hz (<25) [PAGE 17].

Veto 2 — biomechanical spectral support. Human locomotion power is "typically under 10 Hz" [PAGE 16]. Therefore allocating dedicated bands above ~10 Hz (as the 12-band config does: 10–12,…,16–25 Hz [PAGE 17]) captures near-empty spectrum, and with per-band encoders shrunk to preserve total parameter budget (12-band → 1 ResBlock each), capacity is spent on noise. This *predicts and explains* the observed degradation "from 6 bands onward" [PAGE 17] — a physical veto on over-decomposition.

Veto 3 — accelerometer observable. A 3-axis accelerometer measures proper acceleration (linear + gravity projection); the 0–1 Hz band is dominated by the gravity term (orientation), which is exactly the signal that rotation-invariance augmentation would destroy and that gait orientation needs [PAGE 1]. Any pretext task that randomizes orientation therefore vetoes orientation-dependent tasks a priori. This is consistent with SlotFM outperforming augmentation methods on orientation-sensitive tasks while only trailing on Transportation/WalkDistance "by only a small margin" [PAGE 7].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable, verifiable veto scenarios with predictions, checked against the paper's own evidence.

Veto A — Aliasing counterfactual. Claim: "SlotFM captures the discriminative content of any target task." Intervention: apply SlotFM unchanged to a task whose class-separating energy is a transient above 25 Hz acquired at 50 Hz. Prediction (verifiable): accuracy collapses toward chance because aliased energy is indistinguishable from low-frequency content; no downstream head can recover it. Physical basis: $f_{\max}\le25$ Hz. This bounds the paper's generality claim.

Veto B — Structural-loss ablation counterfactual. Claim: "The embedding preserves high-frequency detail needed for peak-magnitude regression." Intervention: set $\beta=0$ on the high band (disable SSIM there). Prediction (verifiable, and supported): reconstruction "completely misses" sharp peaks [PAGE 16], so peak-dependent regressions (ThrowSpeed, JumpPower) degrade; the paper's own "w/o Loss regularizers -37.1% [regression]" [PAGE 9] corroborates the sign and rough magnitude.

Veto C — Determinism counterfactual. Claim: "Slots are usable as fixed embeddings." Intervention: restore distributional slot resampling. Prediction (verifiable, and confirmed): head read-out destabilizes and performance drops sharply — the paper reports "-15.9% [classification] / -25.0% [regression]," the two largest deltas in Table 4 [PAGE 9], exactly as the identifiability argument in THINK_TRACE predicts.

Summary: the paper's contribution is real and mechanistically coherent — frequency-and-time slot factorization, deterministic (fixed) slot init, and bounded+unbounded multi-scale reconstruction losses. The genuine limits are the Nyquist ceiling (25 Hz), sub-10 Hz energy concentration (capping useful band count), scale-dependent aggregation of the "4.5%" headline, and absent significance testing under high variance. The counterfactuals above are each testable against Table 2/3/4 and Table 6, so they are falsifiable rather than rhetorical.
