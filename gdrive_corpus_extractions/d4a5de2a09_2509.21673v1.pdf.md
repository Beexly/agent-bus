A baseline tuned to win one regime regresses to a habitual invariance that fails elsewhere. SlotFM's pitch is precisely that it refuses to commit to a single invariance: its reconstruction objective is deliberately *not* invariant, so it does not collapse to a habit.

Counter-trap (the mechanism that defeats the habit). Two coordinated counter-traps: (a) fixed learnable slots convert a nondeterministic operator into a stable, task-addressable codebook — Figure 4 shows tasks "assign more weight to certain slots," e.g., Writing emphasizing "the 4th and 5th slots that attend to the beginning of the signal" [PAGE 8], which is only possible if slot indices are semantically stable; (b) band-specific loss routing prevents the MSE habit from smoothing away the exact high-frequency structure that regression targets need. The self-attention head is a third, small counter-trap: "<10% of the head's total parameter count, it brings about a 9% performance boost" [PAGE 9], and the paper notes enlarging other baselines' heads "does not yield similar improvements" — i.e., the gain is structural, not capacity.

The clock (temporal budgets that gate the strategy). Multiple hard clocks govern feasibility: pre-training is 500 epochs with early stopping after 40 [PAGE 6]; downstream is 200 epochs, early stop 10 [PAGE 7]; windows are 5 s @ 50 Hz (Task Diversity) but 2 s @ 50 Hz with 128-dim embedding and only 4 slots (HAR benchmark) [PAGE 7]. The 2 s clock forces S=4, so the same model must remain useful under a 4× smaller slot budget — a genuine test of whether the counter-traps are clock-robust. The paper reports it wins 4 of 5 HAR tasks even cross-location (wrist→waist/leg) [PAGE 8], which is evidence the strategy survives a compressed clock and a sensor-placement shift.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/signal-processing veto proof (no "weather" variable exists in this dataset; I substitute the physically binding constraints).

Veto 1 — Nyquist (hard physical ceiling). At f_s = 50 Hz, f_Nyq = 25 Hz. Any spectral claim above 25 Hz is unrecoverable from the sampled data; the bandpass top edges (Table 6, max "16-25") are therefore not stylistic but forced. A model attempting to reconstruct content beyond 25 Hz would be reconstructing aliases — a physical veto, not a modeling choice. This validates the "0-25" single-band ceiling [PAGE 17].

Veto 2 — Human-motion bandwidth (biomechanical prior). The paper asserts "most of the motion in the signals lies in the lower frequencies, typically under 10 Hz" [PAGE 16], motivating fine low-frequency band granularity (e.g., 0-1, 1-2, 2-4, 4-6, 6-10 for the 6-band config). This is a biomechanical constraint: gait cadence (~1–3 Hz fundamental), gesture dynamics, and locomotion sit low; the "above 4 Hz" band exists to catch sharp transient peaks (heel-strike, ball impact in ThrowSpeed), which are physically real but low-energy. Any veto argument that "high frequencies are noise and can be discarded" is falsified by the JumpPower/ThrowSpeed regression gains once MS-STFT is added.

Veto 3 — Reconstruction-identifiability veto. If the encoder discards phase/peak information (MSE smoothing), the decoder cannot reconstruct peaks regardless of capacity; no downstream head can recover what the embedding never stored. The −37.1% regression drop when regularizers are removed [PAGE 9] is the empirical signature of this veto: the failure is upstream of any classifier/regressor.

Veto 4 — Cross-location transfer limit. Pre-training is wrist-only (CAPTURE-24, wrist-worn, [PAGE 6]). Orientation-dependent tasks (gait orientation) can be vetoed when the target sensor is on a different body segment; the paper concedes rotation invariance helps some tasks but "hinder[s] tasks like gait analysis" [PAGE 1]. SlotFM mitigates but does not repeal this veto — hence SimCLR edges it on Motionsense [PAGE 8], and SlotFM trails RelCon on WalkDistance [PAGE 7].

Adjudication: no veto fully blocks SlotFM within the tested regime; Veto 1 and 2 are satisfied by design (bands respect Nyquist and motion bandwidth), Veto 3 is the decisive one and is empirically confirmed by ablation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable, falsifiable scenarios with concrete predictions (all checkable against the paper's tables/appendix or re-runnable with the released code):

Veto 1 — Band-count over-decomposition. Counterfactual: increase bands from 3 to 6, 12 while holding total params constant. Prediction: performance peaks near 3–6 bands and degrades at 12, worst for tasks whose signal energy is concentrated at high frequencies. Verification: Figure 6 / Table 6 [PAGE 16–17] state "performance begins to degrade from 6 bands onward" and "Using 12 bands leads to a significant drop." Falsifier: if a re-run showed monotonic improvement with band count, the "narrower bands = less informative + smaller per-band encoder" mechanism is wrong.

Veto 2 — Resampling vs fixed slots. Counterfactual: revert to the original Slot Attention resampling of initial slots. Prediction: downstream classification drops ≈15.9% and regression ≈25.0%, because slot indices lose semantic stability and the head can no longer weight specific slots. Verification: Table 4 [PAGE 9], "w/o Fixed slot initialization −15.9% / −25.0%." Falsifier: comparable performance under resampling would refute the paper's core claim that consistent slot ordering is necessary for slot-as-embedding.

Veto 3 — Loss-regularizer asymmetry. Counterfactual: remove SSIM + MS-STFT, keep MSE only. Prediction: regression degrades far more than classification (−37.1% vs −13.2%), because peak/spectral fidelity is load-bearing for continuous targets (JumpPower, ThrowSpeed) but not for coarse class labels. Verification: Table 4 [PAGE 9] and Figure 5 reconstruction comparison [PAGE 16]. Falsifier: symmetric degradation across task types would refute the "MSE smoothing specifically harms high-frequency regression" mechanism.

Supplementary testable prediction (from the invariance argument, PAGE 1): a rotation-augmented contrastive baseline (SimCLR) should outperform SlotFM specifically on orientation-invariant tasks (Transportation, which mixes phone-pocket orientations) yet underperform on orientation-dependent gait tasks — partially confirmed by SimCLR/RelCon leading on Transportation and WalkDistance in Table 2 [PAGE 7]. If a re-run reversed this orientation dependence, the invariance-transfer thesis would be falsified.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Net assessment: the paper's mechanism (stable slot codebook × frequency decomposition × structure/spectrum-preserving losses) is internally coherent, its equations are well-posed with the SSIM pole explicitly stabilized, and its strongest claims are backed by ablation. The main honest caveats are the loose "over 15%" prose vs the −13.2% classification cell, the "above 4 Hz" vs "4-20 Hz" band-edge wording mismatch, the pooled 4.5% average across two heterogeneous protocols, and uncharacterized sensitivity to the fixed slot-update iteration count (U=3).
