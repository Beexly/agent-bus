$$
where $\Delta x$ is inter-receptor spacing [mm] and $\lambda_{\text{contour}}$ is the smallest boundary-feature wavelength [mm]. Singularity/breakdown regimes: (i) $\mathbf{v} \equiv 0$ (static contact) gives $I_{\text{contour}} = 0$; (ii) $v\,\tau_{\text{adapt}} > \lambda_{\text{contour}}$ causes temporal aliasing, where $\tau_{\text{adapt}}$ is the mechanoreceptor adaptation time constant [s]; (iii) slip invalidates the isometry assumption $\|d\boldsymbol{\gamma}/ds\| = \text{physical edge advance}$.

Length estimation under two-point exploration, as in Dunan’s blind subject drawing his hands apart to the ends of a book edge [PAGE 186], is a line integral:
$$
\widehat{L} = \int_{s_A}^{s_B} \left\|\frac{d\boldsymbol{\gamma}}{ds}\right\| ds = s_B - s_A \qquad [\mathrm{mm}],
$$
provided contact is maintained and digits do not slip. Additional breakdown regimes: low friction coefficient $\mu$ produces slip so that $\widehat{L} \neq L$; object deformation makes $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(s)$ indent rather than trace $\partial O$; receptor density below the Nyquist limit causes contour aliasing. All empirical claims above are anchored to [PAGE 186].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The source page [PAGE 186] situates James inside a transnational debate. On one side stand the British associationists—Brown, Bain, and J. S. Mill—who treated extension as derived from muscular or kinaesthetic cues; on the stand the German experimentalists—Wundt, Helmholtz, Sully, etc.—who modified but retained a privileged role for the "muscular sense" as the "origin and only revealer of objective extension" [PAGE 186]. James cites M. Ch. Dunan’s 1888 essay in the Revue Philosophique, "l’Espace Visuel et l’Espace Tactile," which argues that tactile surface area alone cannot yield extent because the congenitally blind do not measure objects by laying the hand flat upon them [PAGE 186]. James thereby looks backward at associationist psychology and forward to what would later be called active perception or sensorimotor contingency theory: the idea that perceptual content is constituted by the lawful sensorimotor contingencies generated by self-generated movement, a formalization that would not arrive for more than a century.

A multi-week trap in this lineage is the immobilization confound. In longitudinal haptic research, prolonged disuse—bed rest, cast immobilization, or microgravity—induces somatosensory cortical map reorganization and elevated tactile thresholds. If an experiment measures shape or length perception after weeks of static contact and concludes that touch is inherently impoverished, it commits the very error James attributes to the "surface-only" school: it confuses a disuse-induced motor deficit with a perceptual one. The corollary trap is sensory-adaptation fading: static haptic substitution displays (e.g., electrotactile or vibrotactile arrays) produce perceptual fading unless the user is required to move. James’s argument implies that any multi-week training protocol that suppresses exploratory motion will systematically underestimate haptic capacity, because the informational bottleneck is not the receptor sheet but the absence of the reafferent contour operator $C(t)$ derived above.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when an observer, confronted with ambiguous shape or extent under time pressure or stress, abandons active exploration and regresses to a low-information motor habit: high grip force, whole-hand static contact, and minimal fingertip displacement. In the formalism above, this habit drives $\|\mathbf{v}\| \to 0$, collapsing $I_{\text{contour}}$ to zero and forcing the system to rely on the degenerate static field $r(\mathbf{x}) \approx g(p_0) + \eta$, which cannot localize $\partial O$.

The counter-trap is to prescribe movement without respecting its dynamical constraints. Forcing the subject to trace contours at excessive velocity introduces motor noise into $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(s)$, corrupting the arc-length estimate $\widehat{L} = \int \|d\boldsymbol{\gamma}/ds\|ds$ and producing stick-slip artifacts when the friction coefficient $\mu$ cannot sustain the commanded slip velocity. Conversely, moving too slowly allows slowly adapting afferents to decay, reducing $C(t)$ below detection threshold. There is therefore an optimal exploratory band, not a monotonic "more movement is better" rule.

The clock governing this band is set by the peripheral tactile loop. Mechanoreceptor adaptation time constants $\tau_{\text{adapt}}$ range from tens of milliseconds (fast-adapting) to hundreds of milliseconds or seconds (slow-adapting), and afferent conduction over a ~1 m path at ~50–70 m/s imposes a reafferent delay of roughly 15–20 ms. The ideal exploration angular or linear rate $\omega^*$ satisfies $\omega^* \approx 1/\tau_{\text{adapt}}$; rates much faster than this cause temporal aliasing of the boundary-crossing transient, while rates much slower permit adaptation-driven contrast loss. A recursive theory of mind must model the observer not as a static template-matcher but as a controller that regresses to static contact under crisis, is vulnerable to over-driving the exploration clock, and can recover only by re-engaging the velocity-matched contour-scan regime James describes on [PAGE 186].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: The line-integral model $\widehat{L} = \int \|d\boldsymbol{\gamma}/ds\|ds$ assumes a rigid, mechanically stable boundary $\partial O$. If the object is soft, gel-like, or otherwise deformable, fingertip indentation displaces the boundary itself, so $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(s)$ no longer traces a fixed $\partial O$; the metric estimate is vetoed by object mechanics rather than by perceptual failure.

Biomechanical layer veto: Glabrous skin mechanoreceptors (Merkel, Meissner, Pacinian, Ruffini) have finite spatial density. The two-point discrimination threshold on the fingertip is on the order of 1–2 mm, which sets an operational Nyquist limit: contours with radius of curvature below receptor spacing are unresolvable regardless of movement vigor. The theory holds only when the exploratory aperture and the boundary curvature exceed this biomechanical sampling limit.

Weather/environmental layer veto: Ambient temperature alters skin viscoelasticity and peripheral nerve conduction velocity; humidity alters the skin–object friction coefficient $\mu$. Because the tracing model presumes stick–stick contact without slip, a low-$\mu$ condition (e.g., wet or icy surface) induces slip and invalidates $\widehat{L} = s_B - s_A$; a high-$\mu$ or desiccated condition induces stiction and high-frequency tremor in $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(s)$. Cold also slows transduction kinetics, effectively increasing $\tau_{\text{adapt}}$ and shifting the optimal exploration clock $\omega^*$. The James/Dunan account on [PAGE 186] is therefore a ceteris-paribus theory: it is valid only when the physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers are controlled; otherwise each layer issues an independent veto that breaks the inference from movement to extent perception.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Immobilization veto. Apply a selective digital nerve block or a rigid splint to the index finger so that the subject can rest the palm statically on raised embossed bars of unknown length but cannot draw a fingertip along an edge. Independent variable: motion allowed vs. blocked; dependent variable: length-estimation error. Prediction anchored to [PAGE 186]: the blocked-motion condition should show significant underestimation and high variance relative to the active-tracing condition, because static contact supplies $I_F(\partial O \mid \text{static}) \approx 0$. Veto rule: if blocked-motion error is statistically indistinguishable from active-tracing error, James’s claim that extent requires running the finger along the boundary is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Gradient-masking veto. During active tracing, superimpose spatially uncorrelated vibrotactile noise on the fingertip so that proprioceptive hand separation remains intact but the cutaneous pressure gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} p_O$ is corrupted. Independent variable: noise variance $\sigma^2$; dependent variables: contour-detection threshold and $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{L})$. Prediction: contour threshold rises monotonically with $\sigma^2$, and length-estimation variance scales as $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{L}) \propto \sigma^2$ because $C(t) = \|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} a(t)\|_2$ is degraded. Veto rule: if estimation error is invariant to $\sigma^2$, the surface-sensibility gradient mechanism is vetoed and a purely proprioceptive/motor account would be favored over James’s synthesis.

Scenario 3 — Static-sufficiency veto. Test congenitally blind and blindfolded sighted subjects on the exact book task Dunan describes [PAGE 186], comparing (a) hand laid flat on the object (static contact only) versus (b) fingertips drawn apart along the bounding edge. Prediction anchored to [PAGE 186]: the static condition yields near-chance shape/size discrimination, whereas active edge-tracing yields above-chance, metric-scaled length estimates. Veto rule: if static hand-flat performance equals active-tracing performance, Dunan’s and James’s claim that "surfaces alone give no perception of extent" is empirically vetoed, and the muscular/movement account loses its decisive experimental anchor.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
