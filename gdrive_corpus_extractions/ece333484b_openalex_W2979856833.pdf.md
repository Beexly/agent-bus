The paper's headline claim is not a group difference — it is an *interaction* in correlation structure. The authors state that "The t-tau and p-tau181 levels of CSF-derived EV were positively correlated with the t-tau and p-tau181 levels of total CSF in former NFL players, respectively, but not in the CTRL group" [PAGE 1]. Everything downstream (the 429-protein catalogue, the LXR/RXR pathway, the MAPT upstream prediction) is subordinate to whether that interaction is real, because the main effects were null: EV t-tau p = 0.8688, EV p-tau181 p = 0.2682, total CSF t-tau p = 0.3031, total CSF p-tau181 p = 0.9477 [PAGE 4]. So the first multi-hop step is: what does the interaction test actually carry?

Reconstructing from the reported r and n, it carries more than the p = 0.0524 [PAGE 5] headline suggests, and less than the abstract implies. For the NFL arm (n = 10), r = 0.812 yields t = r√(n−2)/√(1−r²) = 0.812·2.828/0.584 ≈ 3.93 on 8 df, two-tailed p ≈ 0.0043, matching the reported 0.0044. For p-tau181, r = 0.627 gives t ≈ 2.28, p ≈ 0.052, matching 0.0524. For the CTRL arm (n = 8), r = −0.492 gives t ≈ −1.38 on 6 df, p ≈ 0.216, matching 0.2150. The arithmetic is internally consistent — this is a real, non-fabricated correlation table. A Fisher z test of the difference between the two independent correlations (z₁ = 1.133, z₂ = −0.539, SE = √(1/7 + 1/5) = 0.586) gives Z ≈ 2.86, p ≈ 0.004. So the *interaction* is nominally stronger evidence than the within-group p-values suggest, which is the correct reading. But: 95% CI on the NFL t-tau correlation is r ∈ [0.37, 0.95], and on p-tau181 r ∈ [−0.004, 0.90]. A correlation whose CI spans zero cannot anchor a monitoring biomarker, and the interaction is uncorrected for the ~4 tests performed on the same 18 donors.

Second hop: the proteomics. The individual-arm differential analysis is declared null at the pre-specified threshold — "no molecules were significantly altered between former NFL players and control groups based on the criteria of at least >1.5 or <0.67-fold-change with an adjusted p value of <0.05" [PAGE 7]. The authors then relaxed to "61 EV proteins in the former NFL players (fold-change: >1.2 or <0.83)" [PAGE 8] and ran IPA on that relaxed list. This threshold migration is the single most consequential analytic decision in the paper, and it is not a minor one: at a log-fold-change cut of ln(1.2) = 0.182, and an assumed technical log-SD σ, the *null* expectation for how many of m = 429 proteins cross the cut is E[k] = 429·2(1−Φ(0.182/σ)). At σ = 0.125 this gives ≈ 62. The observed number is 61. In other words, the "differentially expressed" set is numerically indistinguishable from what pure TMT technical variance at ~12.5% CV would produce with zero biological signal. That is not a rhetorical attack; it is the reason the paper should have reported an empirical-null (label-permutation) distribution rather than an IPA output.

Third hop — and this is the hard veto — the individual-arm design has 4 NFL and 5 CTRL samples. A nonparametric permutation test on 9 samples has exactly C(9,4) = 126 distinct label assignments, so the minimum attainable two-sided p-value is 2/126 = 0.0159. Benjamini–Hochberg discovery at q = 0.05 over m = 429 tests requires the top-ranked p-value to satisfy p ≤ q/m = 1.166 × 10⁻⁴. The attainable floor is 68× larger than the discovery threshold. Therefore **no protein in the individual proteome dataset can ever be declared significant at FDR 0.05, regardless of biology.** The null result on PAGE 7 is not evidence of absence; it is a mathematical certainty of the design. Solving C(n, n/2) ≥ 2m/q = 17,160 gives n = 18 total (9 v 9) as the minimum even to clear one protein; C(16,8) = 12,870 still fails.

Fourth hop: the pathway result. "LXR/RXR activation" is driven by "ALB, APOA4, C4A, FGA, S100A8, SERPINA1, TF, TTR" [PAGE 10 Table 3]. Every one of those is a high-abundance hepatic/serum protein. Because Poisson counting variance scales as CV = 1/√counts, a fixed fold-change threshold preferentially selects *high-abundance* proteins — they cross |FC| > 1.2 most easily. So the enrichment is partly a selection artifact of the threshold interacting with the abundance–variance relationship, and it is simultaneously confounded: Table 1 reports BMI 27.81 ± 3.43 (CTRL) vs 33.07 ± 4.66 (NFL), p = 0.0008 [PAGE 5]. The most parsimonious competing model is: a BMI- and blood-brain-barrier-linked serum signature, sampled through a 3 mL CSF draw, presented as a CTE-risk signature.

Fifth hop: the tau-ratio inference. The reported inversion — "the t-tau levels in the CSF-derived EV samples was lower than expected and accounted for less than 20% of p-tau181 levels" [PAGE 8] — is a claim about an antibody operator, and the operator is asymmetric. The t-tau assay is "a monoclonal capture antibody that reacts with an epitope in the mid-region of all tau isoforms in combination with a detection antibody that reacts with an epitope at the N-terminus of t-tau," while the p-tau181 assay keeps the mid-region capture but swaps detection to the pT181 epitope [PAGE 3]. **Assumptions declared:** (A1) Stokes–Einstein spherical particles in Newtonian fluid; (A2) Rayleigh scattering regime for NTA intensity weighting (d ≪ λ/n); (A3) Poisson-distributed single-molecule capture in Simoa digital mode; (A4) log-normal protein abundance with roughly constant log-SD across the detected range; (A5) independent samples, no batch × group interaction; (A6) sandwich ELISA requires both epitopes sterically accessible.

**O1 — NTA diffusion operator (Stokes–Einstein).**
$$D=\frac{k_BT}{3\pi\eta d}\quad[\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}]$$
with $k_B=1.381\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, $T=294.15\,\mathrm{K}$ (21 °C, per [PAGE 3]), $\eta\approx1.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$, $d=86\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}$. **O2 — Scattering weight bias (the reason the "mode" is 86 nm).** For $d\ll\lambda/n$, $I_{\mathrm{scat}}\propto d^{6}$. A 30 nm particle scatters $(86/30)^6\approx5.6\times10^{2}$ times less than an 86 nm particle. **O3 — Simoa digital response.** With $\lambda$ = mean enzyme-labelled immunocomplexes per bead,
$$f_{\mathrm{pos}}=1-e^{-\lambda},\qquad \lambda=\kappa\,[\text{analyte}]$$
*Breakdown:* $f_{\mathrm{pos}}\approx\lambda$ only for $\lambda\lesssim0.3$; saturation compresses response for $\lambda\gtrsim1.5$. The paper reports "relative concentration estimates... calculated according to the standard curve" [PAGE 3] and "diluted 4x" [PAGE 3] with no LoD, no CV, no dilution-linearity check — so the position of each sample on the saturating limb is unverifiable.

**O4 — Sandwich operator and the tau ratio.** Define intactness indicators $M$ (mid-region epitope) and $N$ (N-terminal epitope), and $P$ (pT181). Then
$$S_{t}=\varepsilon_t\!\!\sum_{\text{species}}\!\!\big[M\wedge N\big],\qquad S_{p}=\varepsilon_p\!\!\sum_{\text{species}}\!\!\big[M\wedge P\big]$$
Observed $\rho = S_p/S_t$. N-terminal truncation makes $\{M\wedge P\}$ non-empty while $\{M\wedge N\}$ empties, forcing $\rho>1$ — consistent with $\rho>5$ ("less than 20%" [PAGE 8]). *Breakdown/singularity:* $\rho$ is a valid stoichiometric statement only if $\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_p$; the two channels use different reference materials, so $\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}=\rho_{\mathrm{true}}\cdot(\varepsilon_t/\varepsilon_p)$ and the "20%" is a cross-calibrant comparison, not a molar ratio.

**O5 — Pearson inference and Fisher interval.**
$$t=\frac{r\sqrt{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}},\qquad z=\operatorname{artanh}r,\quad \mathrm{SE}_z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-3}}$$

**O6 — Nonparametric resolution floor (the hard veto).**
$$p_{\min}=\frac{2}{\binom{n}{k}}$$
For $n=9,k=4$: $p_{\min}=2/126=1.59\times10^{-2}$. Benjamini–Hochberg discovery requires $p_{(1)}\le q/m = 0.05/429 = 1.17\times10^{-4}$. $$\mathbb{E}[k]_{\text{null}}=m\cdot 2\!\left[1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln\phi}{\sigma}\right)\right]$$
