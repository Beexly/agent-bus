<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source does not print formal equations. The following are the standard operators implied by its named methods, stated so that the paper’s claims can be tested. All probabilities are dimensionless and lie in [0,1]. Feature units are inherited from the input variables: counts are dimensionless, distances are metres, times are seconds, speeds are metres per second, and categorical variables are indicator variables. Weights carry inverse feature units so that the linear predictor is dimensionless.

Logistic regression, as named on [PAGE 6] where the paper says logistic regression is used when the “outcome variable is categorical” and predicts “probabilities using a logistic function, which adheres to the cumulative logistic distribution”:
\[
p(y=1\mid x)=\sigma(w^{\top}x+b),\qquad
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},\qquad
\operatorname{logit}(p)=w^{\top}x+b.
\]
For \(K\) classes:
\[
p(y=k\mid x)=\frac{e^{w_k^{\top}x+b_k}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{w_j^{\top}x+b_j}}.
\]
Assumption: observations are conditionally independent given \(x\), and the logit is linear in the supplied features. Breakdown regime: as \(z\to\pm\infty\), \(p\to 0\) or \(1\), the gradient \(\sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))\to 0\), and log loss diverges if the label is opposite. For multiclass softmax, large logits cause numerical overflow unless a log-sum-exp stabilizer is used. The paper gives no regularization, no class weights, and no separation diagnostics for the draw class despite reporting that “Logistic Regression faces challenges in accurately classifying draw outcomes” [PAGE 8].

Probit regression, also named on [PAGE 6] where the paper says “probit regression employs a cumulative normal distribution curve”:
\[
p(y=1\mid x)=\Phi(w^{\top}x+b),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal cumulative distribution. Units are as above. Breakdown regime: \(\Phi(z)\to 0\) or \(1\) as \(z\to\pm\infty\), with the same gradient-vanishing and log-loss divergence problems; extreme separation makes the likelihood non-identifiable in finite samples without regularization.

Naïve Bayes, used in the voting ensemble on [PAGE 6]:
\[
p(y=c\mid x)\propto p(y=c)\prod_{j=1}^{d}p(x_j\mid y=c),
\]
\[
\log p(y=c\mid x)=\log p(y=c)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log p(x_j\mid y=c)+\text{constant}.
\]
Assumption: features are conditionally independent given the class. Breakdown regime: when features are strongly correlated, the product overcounts evidence; when a feature value is unseen under a class, the product becomes zero unless smoothing is applied. The paper does not report smoothing.

Random Forest, named on [PAGE 4] and [PAGE 6]:
\[
\hat y=\operatorname{argmax}_{c}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}\{h_t(x)=c\}
\]
for classification, or
\[
\hat y=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_t(x)
\]
for regression. The variance of the ensemble average is approximately
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\hat y)=\rho\sigma^2+\frac{1-\rho}{T}\sigma^2,
\]
where \(\rho\) is the pairwise tree correlation, dimensionless, and \(\sigma^2\) has the squared units of the output. Breakdown regime: if \(\rho\to 1\), adding trees does not reduce variance; if \(T\) is small and trees are deep, the ensemble overfits; if the training distribution shifts, the forest average is not a causal estimate.

Voting by product of probabilities, explicitly stated on [PAGE 6]:
\[
S_c=\prod_{m=1}^{M}p_m(c\mid x),\qquad
\hat y=\operatorname{argmax}_{c}S_c.
\]
In log domain:
\[
\log S_c=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\log p_m(c\mid x).
\]
Breakdown regime: for large \(M\), the product underflows to zero in floating point; use log-sum. Also, product voting treats classifier probabilities as independent evidence, which is unjustified unless calibrated and conditionally independent.

SHAP attribution, named on [PAGE 6] where the paper says “Delve into SHAP values to unravel how each feature influences predictions”:
\[
\phi_j=\sum_{S\subseteq F\setminus\{j\}}
\frac{|S|!(|F|-|S|-1)!}{|F|!}
\left[v(S\cup\{j\})-v(S)\right].
\]
The units of \(\phi_j\) are the units of the model output \(v\): probability for a probability model, log-odds for a logit model, or metric units if the output has units. Assumption: a cooperative-game value with a chosen background distribution. Breakdown regime: with collinear features, SHAP values are not unique; signs and ranks can flip under different feature subsets or background samples. The paper reports no such stability check.

Evaluation metrics implied by [PAGE 6] and [PAGE 8]:
\[
\text{Accuracy}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\},
\]
\[
\text{Precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad
\text{Recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\qquad
F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R},
\]
\[
\text{Brier}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(p_i-y_i)^2,
\]
\[
\text{LogLoss}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left[y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\right].
\]
Accuracy, precision, recall, and \(F_1\) are dimensionless proportions. Brier is dimensionless for binary labels. Log loss is in nats when the logarithm is natural, or bits when base 2 is used. Class entropy:
\[
H(Y)=-\sum_{c=1}^{K}p_c\log_2 p_c
\]
is in bits. The paper’s “delicacy” language [PAGE 2] conflates accuracy and probability; the correct audit requires both discrimination metrics and calibration metrics.

The attacking and defensive quotients are not specified as equations on [PAGE 5]. A general audit form is

Q_A=\frac{N_A}{D_A},\qquad Q_D=\frac{N_D}{D_D},
\]
where \(N_A,D_A,N_D,D_D\) are nonnegative counts or rates. If numerator and denominator share units, \(Q\) is dimensionless; otherwise it has ratio units. Breakdown regime: \(D_A=0\) or \(D_D=0\) makes the quotient undefined or infinite. The source does not state how zeros, missing tackles, or low-event matches are handled. Omitted-variable bias for the physical layer can be written in the linear case as
\[
\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta_X]=\beta_X+\beta_Z\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Z)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)},

where \(Z\) is an omitted physical or environmental state, \(\beta_Z\) has outcome units per \(Z\)-unit, and the bias term has outcome units per \(X\)-unit. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
A football prediction model is not only a model of players; it is a model of agents who model each other. A coach models the opponent’s likely press, a player models the coach’s trust, and an opponent models the model if the output becomes actionable. The paper gestures at this when it asks for “Dynamic Lineup Optimization” based on “the team's objectives and opponent characteristics, considering player fatigue, form, and historical performance” [PAGE 3], and when it calls for “Adaptability to Changing Condition” under “changes in player form, team strategies, or external factors” [PAGE 3]. But the model operators do not include an explicit opponent-response term.

A compact recursive-theory-of-mind formalization is as follows. Let \(a_i(t)\in A\) be player or coach \(i\)’s action at time \(t\), and let \(\theta_j\) be opponent \(j\)’s type or policy parameter. A depth-\(k\) belief is
\[
b_i^{(k)}(\theta_j\mid h_t)
=
P\left(\theta_j\mid h_t,\;b_j^{(k-1)}(\theta_i\mid h_t)\right),
\]
where \(h_t\) is the history up to \(t\). The units are probability; the state space and action space are categorical or vector-valued. Breakdown regime: as \(k\to\infty\), the recursion need not converge if the game is non-stationary or if agents have misspecified models. In practical terms, a model that ignores the opponent’s adaptation is a depth-0 model.

Crisis habit regression can be represented as a policy mixture:
\[
\pi_i(a\mid s,C_t=1)
=
(1-\lambda_t)\pi_i^{\text{habit}}(a\mid s)
+
\lambda_t\pi_i^{\text{deliberate}}(a\mid s),
\]
where \(C_t\in\{0,1\}\) is a crisis indicator, \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\) is dimensionless, and \(\pi_i^{\text{habit}}\) is the trained or default policy. Under high stress, time pressure, or repeated failure, \(\lambda_t\) can fall toward zero, so behaviour regresses to habit. The statistical model trained on average states may then fail in crisis states because the conditional distribution \(P(Y\mid X,C_t=1)\) differs from \(P(Y\mid X,C_t=0)\). The paper has no crisis-state variable.

Counter-trap is the adversarial version. If opponents know the model or the coach’s habit, they can choose actions that shift the feature distribution:
\[
P_{\text{train}}(X,Y)\neq P_{\text{deploy}}(X,Y\mid \text{opponent adaptation}).
\]
The model’s most important feature can become an anti-feature. For example, a team that historically wins after high possession may face an opponent that concedes possession deliberately. The model would read high possession as positive while the counter-trap makes it neutral or negative. The paper’s SHAP analysis [PAGE 6] cannot detect this because SHAP explains the fitted model, not the opponent’s response to the model.

Clock is the temporal layer: match minute, season phase, recovery time, and injury-return time. The paper mentions fatigue and form [PAGE 3], but no clock variable is specified. If a player’s performance is predicted from season averages without a recovery clock, the model blends pre-injury, return-to-play, and fully fit states. Breakdown regime: when the clock variable is omitted, the model’s residuals are autocorrelated within player and within week, and cross-validation that ignores the clock overstates generalization.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto is straightforward: a statistical prediction cannot override a physical, biomechanical, or environmental state that breaks conditional exchangeability. Let \(Y\) be the performance or match outcome, \(X\) the paper’s model features, and \(Z\) the physical-environmental state. The model estimates \(p_M(y\mid x)\). For \(p_M\) to be valid under a state \(z\), we need
\[
P(Y=y\mid X=x,Z=z)=P(Y=y\mid X=x)
\]
for all relevant \(z\). If this equality fails, the model prediction is not admissible. In the linear case the omitted-variable bias is
\[
\mathbb{E}[\hat\beta_X]-\beta_X
=
\beta_Z\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Z)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}.
\]
In the nonlinear case, the discrepancy is
\[
D_{\mathrm{TV}}\left(P(Y\mid X),P(Y\mid X,Z)\right)
=
\frac{1}{2}\sum_y\left|P(y\mid X)-P(y\mid X,Z)\right|,
\]
which is dimensionless and lies in [0,1]. A veto is active when this discrepancy exceeds the audit tolerance \(\varepsilon\), or when \(z\) lies in a critical physical set \(Z_{\text{crit}}\).

The source itself supplies the missing layers. It lists “injuries to key players, home and away games, and goal differentials” as relevant [PAGE 2]. It calls for injury prediction using “player fitness, injury history, and playing conditions” [PAGE 3]. Its literature survey lists “Home Advantage, Player Injuries, External Influences” [PAGE 5]. But the proposed system’s deployed parameter set is “current form, attacking quotient, and defensive quotient” [PAGE 5]. Therefore the physical layer is acknowledged in the narrative and omitted from the operational model.

Biomechanical veto proof: sprinting, deceleration, and change-of-direction output depend on neuromuscular fatigue and prior load. If cumulative load \(L\) is elevated, the conditional distribution of high-intensity actions changes even at fixed tactical features \(X\). Since the paper’s model does not include \(L\), and since \(L\) can correlate with minutes played, schedule density, and player identity, \(Y\) is not conditionally independent of \(L\) given \(X\). The model’s prediction is therefore inadmissible in the high-load regime. Example audit bands, not source-derived, would be: rest interval less than 72 h, or a cumulative deceleration load more than two standard deviations above the player’s rolling baseline. The units are hours and metres per second squared respectively.

Weather veto proof: precipitation, wind, and heat change traction, ball flight, and thermoregulation. If \(W\) is wind speed in metres per second, precipitation intensity in millimetres per hour, or wet-bulb globe temperature in degrees Celsius, then \(P(Y\mid X,W)\) differs from \(P(Y\mid X)\) when \(W\) crosses a critical band. The paper does not include \(W\). Because weather can correlate with home advantage, season, and league, omitting it creates both bias and variance. The veto condition is not that weather always matters; it is that the model has no way to certify invariance across the weather domain.

Adjudication rule: the statistical layer may be used only when the physical layer is either measured or shown to be ignorable. If a critical state is present and unmeasured, the correct action is to veto the prediction, not to average it. The source’s own adaptive objective [PAGE 3] supports this rule in principle, but the proposed system does not implement it.
