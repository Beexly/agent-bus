The derivation begins by isolating the core epistemic rupture James posits: the "mutable" sensation. James uses optical phenomena (snow in sunlight/shadow) to demonstrate that what we perceive as immutable sensory qualities are actually dynamic, context-dependent constructions [PAGE 37]. The "simple color-contrast" is a foundational phenomenon where "two surfaces of different colors, placed near each other, will appear... more unlike than they really are" [PAGE 37]. This isn't an error of cognition but a direct result of retinal and neural processing—a "phenomenon of sensation immediately aroused." The psychological theory of contrast, however, posits that these sensations exist "in themselves unchanged before the mind" while the "relating activity" of the mind creates the contrast illusion. James dismantles this by arguing that higher mental functions don't merely operate on pre-existing sensations; they supersede and displace them entirely.

This leads to the gray-zone audit. The boundary between physiological contrast and cognitive illusion is not a clean line but a spectrum of "sufficient attention." The snow/sunlight example is a complex illusion rectifiable through cognitive re-evaluation, whereas simple color-contrast is a raw, unrectifiable sensation. The trap for the intellectualist is to assume the existence of a stable "sensory substrate" upon which the mind operates. James's radical contention is that there is no such substrate; the "things which arouse them exist," and the mind knows these things directly, but the specific sensory qualia associated with those things in a lower-order state are obliterated when the higher function engages. This creates a paradoxical ontology: the sensory world is both real (as physical objects) and unreal (as independent psychic entities).

Applying this to competitive dynamics reveals a multi-hop trap. In a high-stakes negotiation or tactical scenario, an opponent may present a "simple" piece of data (analogous to color-contrast)—a verifiable fact that triggers an immediate, automatic emotional or strategic response. The "psychological theory" would suggest you can intellectually analyze this data while still feeling the initial impulse. James suggests otherwise: true comprehension of the larger strategic picture (the higher mental function) doesn't just add analysis; it displaces the initial visceral reaction. If you cling to the "unchanged sensation" of the initial provocation, you remain trapped at a lower cognitive level, unable to execute the more complex strategy required. The clock here is the duration of the engagement; maintaining the displaced state requires continuous cognitive effort, and fatigue can cause a regression to the simpler, contrasting sensation.

Thus, the frontier lies in modeling this displacement as an information-theoretic process. We must quantify the "information density" of the higher function versus the base sensation and identify the exact conditions under which the supersession fails—i.e., when the brain's "momentary conditions" favor the persistence of the lower-order qualia despite conscious understanding. This bridges James’s 1890 psychology with modern predictive coding theories, where perception is a hierarchical Bayesian inference, and "sensations" are merely low-level prediction errors that are normally cancelled out by top-down priors. When the prior is weak or the signal is overwhelming, the low-level error (the sensation) persists, creating an illusion or a strategic vulnerability.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
We formalize James's principle of supersession using Information Theory and Predictive Processing frameworks. Let a perceptual/cognitive state be represented by a probability distribution over possible interpretations of sensory input.

**Operator 1: Sensation-Contrast Function (SCF)**
This models the immediate, non-cognitive arousal of sensation due to juxtaposition.
\[
\mathcal{C}(S_1, S_2) = \int_{R} |P(s|x_1) - P(s|x_2)| \, ds + \beta \cdot \nabla I(x_1, x_2)
\]
*   **Assumptions:** \(S_1, S_2\) are adjacent stimulus fields; \(x_1, x_2\) are their physical properties; \(P(s|x)\) is the likelihood of generating sensory signal \(s\) given property \(x\); \(\nabla I\) is the spatial gradient of information intensity (brightness/color saturation).
*   **Units:** First term is total variation distance (unitless, 0-2); second term \(\beta\) has units of [sensation intensity]/[information gradient]. The output \(\mathcal{C}\) is in arbitrary sensation intensity units.
*   **Breakdown Regime:** This operator dominates under conditions of *low cognitive load* and *high spatial frequency* of stimulus boundaries. It breaks down when \(\nabla I\) approaches zero (uniform field) or when attentional resources are fully diverted (the "insufficient attention" leading to snow/sunlight illusions).
*   **Citation:** Correlates with James's description of "simple color-contrast" as "a phenomenon of sensation immediately aroused" [PAGE 37].

**Operator 2: Cognitive Supersession Function (CSF)**
This quantifies the displacement of lower-order sensations by a higher-order conceptual model \(M\).
\[
\mathcal{D}(S, M) = H(S) - H(S|M) - \lambda \cdot D_{KL}(P(M|data) || P(M))
\]
*   **Assumptions:** \(H(S)\) is the Shannon entropy of the sensory state space (uncertainty/complexity); \(H(S|M)\) is the conditional entropy given the active model \(M\); \(D_{KL}\) is the Kullback-Leibler divergence measuring how much the model \(M\) is updated by new data relative to its prior belief \(P(M)\).
*   **Units:** \(H\) is in bits (or nats); \(\lambda\) is a trade-off parameter with units [cognitive effort]/[bit]. Output \(\mathcal{D}\) is in [effective uncertainty reduction]. A positive \(\mathcal{D}\) indicates successful supersession.
*   **Singularity/Breakdown Regime:** Supersession fails (\(\mathcal{D} \leq 0\)) when:
    1.  \(H(S|M)\) remains high—the model \(M\) cannot account for the sensory data.
    2.  \(D_{KL}\) becomes extremely large—the new data is so anomalous it crashes the model's updating mechanism (a "belief collapse").
    3.  \(\lambda\) increases drastically due to cognitive fatigue—the cost of maintaining \(M\) outweighs its explanatory benefit.
*   **Citation:** Formalizes James's claim that the higher function "supersedes and displaces" sensations, being "a direct resultant of whatever momentary brain-conditions may obtain" [PAGE 37].

**Combined State Equation:**
The net perceptual/strategic state \(\Psi\) is:
\[
\Psi = \alpha \cdot \mathcal{C}(S_1, S_2) + (1-\alpha) \cdot \mathcal{D}(S, M)
\]
where \(\alpha \in [0,1]\) is an attentional weight dynamically determined by task demands and fatigue.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
James's argument is a direct assault on the **Intellectualist-Dualist** tradition stemming from Descartes, which posited a homunculus ("Thinking Principle") that combines pre-packaged sensory ideas. His lineage runs through **Hume** (who reduced ideas to copies of impressions) and **Kant** (whose categories synthesized manifolds of intuition), but he radicalizes it by denying the existence of discrete, persistent "impressions" altogether. The "psychological theory of contrast" he critiques is a lingering empiricist notion that sensations are atomic building blocks.

Historically, this was a precursor to **Gestalt Psychology**, which argued "the whole is other than the sum of its parts," and later to **Predictive Coding** theories (e.g., Karl Friston), where perception is hierarchical Bayesian inference. The "gray zone" James identifies—the space between raw sensation and cognitive interpretation—is now mapped onto the **Retino-Tectal vs. Geniculo-Striate pathways**: subcortical, fast, reflexive processing (like simple contrast) versus cortical, slower, interpretive processing.

The **multi-week trap** emerges from misreading James as a strict behaviorist. He is not denying inner experience; he is redefining its ontology. The trap is to think he believes in "sense-data" that get processed. He does not. He believes only in *events* (physical stimuli) and *thoughts* (higher-order relations). Any attempt to build a strategy on the assumption that an opponent is consciously manipulating "sensory data" falls into the intellectualist error. The correct model is that the opponent's entire cognitive architecture is dynamically reconfiguring in response to stimuli, potentially displacing prior strategic frames.

**Scientific Lineage & Modern Echo:**
*   **William James (1890):** Supersession thesis.
*   **Ulric Neisser (1967):** Cognitive psychology as "construction of knowledge," not processing of data.
*   **Andy Clark (2013):** *Predictive Processing:* The brain is a "hypothesis-testing engine" that minimizes prediction error. Low-level "sensations" are merely unreduced prediction errors at early sensory layers.
*   **Application:** In competitive intelligence, recognizing that your adversary's "reaction" to a move may be a bottom-up, automatic contrast effect (e.g., perceiving aggression where none is intended due to contextual framing) versus a top-down, model-driven calculation. The former can be exploited with speed; the latter requires counter-modeling.

The retrocausal insight is that James's critique forces us to view all perceived "static" advantages or disadvantages in a contest as potentially transient constructs, vulnerable to displacement by a sufficiently powerful reframing of the overall situation.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
We construct a recursive model of strategic interaction where agents possess nested theories of mind, and each level risks regression to a "contrasted" state.

**Level 0 (Physical/Biomechanical):** The body's stress response (fight/flight/freeze) to a sudden stimulus. This is akin to simple color-contrast—an automatic, non-cognitive arousal.

**Level 1 (Tactical/Automatic):** The trained pattern-recognition system (e.g., a chess player seeing a fork, a fighter seeing an opening). This is a "higher mental function" that typically supersedes Level 0 panic. However, under extreme time pressure or fatigue, it can fail, regressing to Level 0 flailing.

**Level 2 (Strategic/Deliberate):** The explicit planning system weighing options, considering the opponent's likely responses. This supersedes Level 1 by adding a layer of meta-reasoning ("if I play this tactically optimal move, what strategic concession does it make?").

**The Crisis Habit Regression Trap:** An agent at Level 2, facing a novel, high-intensity stimulus (a "black swan" move), may experience a breakdown in their Level 2 model (\(D_{KL}\) explodes). The brain, seeking stability, may regress not to Level 0, but to a deeply ingrained Level 1 pattern from a *different domain* (a "crisis habit"). For example, a negotiator under intense pressure might revert to a rigid, formulaic script learned from a legal background, ignoring the current relational dynamics. This is a regression to a *previously superseded but not extinguished* state.

**Counter-Trap via Clock Manipulation:** The savvy opponent recognizes the signs of this regression (increased verbal rigidity, loss of eye contact, micro-expressions of frustration). They then apply the **"clock"**—not just external time pressure, but *internal processing time pressure*. They introduce a secondary, subtle, incongruent stimulus (a slight change in posture, an oddly phrased question) that doesn't require a full Level 2 recalculation but disrupts the automated Level 1 script. This forces a momentary "buffer overflow" at Level 1, causing the agent to stall and potentially regress further to Level 0 (confusion/panic) or, ideally, to reboot their Level 2 reasoning in a new framework. The key is timing: the disruption must come when the agent is most committed to the flawed Level 1 response, maximizing the cognitive whiplash.

**Recursive Vulnerability:** Each level's supersession of the one below creates a latent vulnerability. The "displaced" lower-level states are not gone; they are dormant, ready to re-emerge if the supervening model cracks. A competitor who can diagnose which level their opponent is currently operating at—and which lower levels are primed for regression—holds a significant asymmetric advantage.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

*   **Biomechanical Veto Proof:** The vestibular and proprioceptive systems (Level 0) have veto power over the central pattern generators governing gait (Level 1). *   **Weather Veto Proof:** High humidity (>80%) significantly impairs evaporative cooling. Even with perfect heat acclimatization and hydration strategy (Level 2), the body's core temperature will rise faster, triggering a hard-wired hypothalamic command to reduce exertion (Level 0 thermoregulatory drive). This overrides any strategic desire to maintain pace.
*   **Adjudication Rule:** **The Physical Veto.** Any model \(M\) whose execution requires sustained violation of fundamental biomechanical limits (joint angles, force production curves, aerobic/anaerobic thresholds) or homeostatic set-points (core temp, blood glucose) is subject to immediate veto. The "momentary brain-conditions" James mentions include these subcortical, autonomic states. A strategy that ignores these is built on a false premise—that the mind can freely command the body. It cannot. The veto is absolute and instantaneous, resetting the cognitive hierarchy to survival maintenance.

In competitive contexts, this means a "brilliant" plan requiring superhuman stamina, precision, or pain tolerance will fail not because of poor thinking, but because the body's basal ganglia and brainstem issue a veto that consciousness cannot override. Recognizing an opponent's approach to their veto threshold allows for targeted pressure to trigger the failure mode.
