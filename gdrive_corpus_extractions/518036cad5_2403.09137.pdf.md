<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is explicit. FDE is attributed to Dunn and Belnap in the 1970s, with Dunn’s bi-consequence system \(R_{fde}\) using sequents \(\varphi\vdash_{FDE}\chi\) over \(\{\neg,\land,\lor\}\) [PAGE 1]. ETL is attributed to Pietz and Rivieccio, and NFL to Shramko, Zaitsev, and Belikov [PAGE 2]. The paper’s abstract frames its contribution as “non-distributive relatives of Exactly True Logic (ETL) by Pietz and Riveccio and its dual (NFL) Non-Falsity Logic by Shramko, Zaitsev and Belikov” [PAGE 1]. The non-distributive lattice motivation is also genealogically anchored: the paper cites Bimbó and Dunn’s \(LN\), a cut-free sequent calculus constituting the positive fragment of FDE that proves neither full distributivity nor a one-sided distributivity scheme [PAGE 3]. It also cites quantum logic: “non-distributive lattices appear in the study of quantum logic” and notes that these are orthomodular, whereas 4 is distributive, modular, but not orthomodular [PAGE 3]. The tableau lineage is Smullyan-style \(\alpha/\beta\) rules [PAGE 9] and is later compared with KE/analytic-cut systems of Mondadori and d’Agostino [PAGE 16–17].

The multi-week traps are historical and conceptual. First, there is a habit-regression trap: because ETL and NFL are “relatives” of FDE, one may assume all FDE distributive laws transfer unchanged.
