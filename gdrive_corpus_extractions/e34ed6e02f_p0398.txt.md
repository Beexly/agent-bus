The counter-trap is the experimenter’s own setup: by removing eggs, the observer creates a perceptual trap in which the animal’s validated habit (sit when enough eggs are seen) can never be satisfied, so the animal emits the opposite behavior (lay more) ad infinitum until physiological limits bind. An observer who interprets this as the bird “not understanding” its own best interest commits an anthropomorphic error; the correct reading is that the percept–impulse loop is open and the negative-feedback gain is zero.

The clock is the endogenous physiological gate d(t) and the ovulatory cycle. No percept, however salient, can release laying or sitting unless the hormonal state is permissive. Thus the recursion is bounded: external percepts trigger action only within a time window opened by an internal clock. Crisis behavior reveals the clock’s precedence: when d(t) is low, even a perfect releaser p_t produces no motor output; when d(t) is high but e(t) is artificially held low by removal, the clock continues to accumulate drive with no behavioral sink.
