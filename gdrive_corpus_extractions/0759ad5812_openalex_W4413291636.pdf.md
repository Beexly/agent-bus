Because roster coverage is partial—only 14 (SD=5) players per team are instrumented and a mean of 5 (SD=2) are on-field at any instant [PAGE 4]—the authors aggregate across the variable-sized on-field set by computing per-window mean and standard deviation of five kinematic channels (field x, field y, velocity x, velocity y, velocity magnitude), producing a fixed 10-dimensional feature tensor [PAGE 5]. This design choice is the critical architectural hinge: static models (logistic regression, XGBoost) consume temporally averaged versions of these 10 features, whereas the recurrent models (GRU, LSTM) ingest the full 100-sample × 10-variable sequence [PAGE 6][PAGE 7].

Crisis habit regression. Under physiological or game-state crisis (e.g., late-game deficit, two-minute drill, injury timeout), players and coaches regress to high-frequency, low-variance habit plays: compressed formations, quarterback sneaks, or repetitive inside rushes. The detector’s training distribution is drawn from full-game play logs without explicit crisis-state labeling [PAGE 5–9]. If crisis habits produce aggregate motion signatures that fall outside the 10-second window statistics learned from average plays, recall will regress precisely when contextual workload and head-impact filtering matter most. The model has no uncertainty output to flag out-of-distribution play events; it issues a binary score smoothed by a Gaussian kernel (\sigma = 10 s) [PAGE 8], which may blur rapid crisis transitions into missed detections.

Counter-trap. Because the input is aggregate team motion rather than individual player identity or ball location [PAGE 6], an opposing staff cannot directly spoof a single player’s GPS trace to invalidate detection. However, they can manipulate the collective kinematic envelope: using no-huddle tempo to shorten inter-play intervals below the effective resolution of the sliding window, or employing pre-snap shifts that mimic play-start motion during non-play intervals. Such tactics would inflate false positives by shifting negative-class windows into the feature space of positive-class plays. The paper’s negative-class construction samples from inter-play intervals [PAGE 5]; if an opponent systematically shortens those intervals, the negative and positive class distributions overlap, degrading the F1 ceiling observed (89% for LSTM [PAGE 10]).

Clock. The game clock imposes a hard boundary on the labeling tolerance. The TP criterion |t_det − t_true| < 10 s [PAGE 8] assumes play starts are separated by more than 20 s. In clock-management situations (two-minute drill, spike plays, hurry-up), plays can occur within 15–20 s of one another. A single Gaussian-smoothed local maximum may then satisfy the tolerance for two adjacent ground-truth events, causing one detection to be counted as two true positives or one false positive to be masked. Additionally, the workrate calculation normalizes by minutes played [PAGE 12, Table 3]; clock stoppage and replay reviews mean that GPS-recorded elapsed time does not equal active play time, introducing systematic bias into per-minute intensity metrics if the play detector misses short clock-stoppage plays.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Physical layer veto (GPS and coordinate frame). The Catapult Vector S6 samples at 10 Hz [PAGE 4]. The coordinate transform \mathcal{T} requires four surveyed field corners [PAGE 9]; if corner coordinates are misregistered or if the field is non-rectangular due to temporary markings, the transform becomes a source of systematic bias. GPS in stadium environments suffers multipath and urban-canyon effects; dense steel structures can cause position jumps that alias as high-velocity events. There is no reported outlier rejection or dilution-of-precision filtering in the preprocessing [PAGE 5–6], so a position spike larger than the field dimensions can inject spurious variance into \boldsymbol{\phi} and trigger false play detections or mask true ones.

Biomechanical layer veto (head impact and workload). Instrumented mouthguards log head impacts, but analysis is stratified at 20 g [PAGE 14]. Sub-threshold impacts (< 20 g) are excluded from the impact-density profiles despite evidence that cumulative sub-concussive load contributes to injury risk; the detector therefore cannot support a complete biomechanical risk profile. Furthermore, Table 3 [PAGE 14] shows raw GPS overestimates minutes played by roughly an order of magnitude relative to play-filtered data (e.g., skill player raw 184.5 ± 56.2 min vs predicted 23.4 ± 7.5 min). If a coach used unfiltered GPS to monitor fatigue, they would systematically underestimate per-minute intensity and misjudge return-to-play readiness—a veto on any fatigue model that ingests raw tracking data without play segmentation.

Weather veto (not explicitly modeled). The paper reports no environmental covariates (temperature, precipitation, wind) [PAGE 4–18]. Adverse weather changes player movement (slower accelerations, reduced cutting) and can degrade GPS accuracy. If play signatures in rain or snow differ from the training distribution (drawn across 2021–2024 games without weather stratification), Recall—especially for the subtler pass and rush classes where static models already struggle [PAGE 10–11]—may drop below the reported 92–95% RNN levels. Because the study is limited to one Canadian league with likely homogeneous climate exposure [PAGE 4, 18], the model has not been veto-tested against extreme-weather kinematic regimes.

Singularity. At n(t) = 0 tracked players on field, the feature operator is undefined (division by zero in mean/variance). At n(t) = 1, variance terms are zero or undefined, collapsing the feature space dimensionality. The model’s claimed robustness at <2 players (Recall 87–88%, SD 10–12% [PAGE 11–12]) operates at the edge of this singularity; one additional dropout event pushes the system into the non-operable regime.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning verifiable reward (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios and predictions.

Veto Scenario 1 — Zero-player observability. Counterfactual: the tracking system records n(t) = 0 on-field players for an entire 10-second window (e.g., all sensors removed or powered off during a timeout). Verification: query the input mask count; veto if \sum_i \mathbb{1}[i \in \mathcal{O}(t)] = 0 for any window. Prediction: the aggregation operator \sigma over an empty set is undefined; the model must fall back to the class prior or output NaN. Precision and Recall on affected games will collapse to the random-guess baseline (F1 ≈ 0.5 for balanced classes) rather than the reported 89% LSTM F1 [PAGE 10].

Veto Scenario 2 — Sub-20-second inter-play interval (hurry-up / two-minute drill). Counterfactual: offensive tempo produces two play starts separated by \Delta t < 20 s. Verification: extract ground-truth play-start timestamps from official logs and compute pairwise differences; veto if any adjacent pair satisfies \Delta t < 20 s. Prediction: the 10-second detection tolerance |t_det − t_true| < 10 s [PAGE 8] becomes non-identifiable; a single smoothed peak cannot be uniquely assigned to two ground-truth events. False positives will rise and Recall will fall below the reported 92–93% [PAGE 10], because one detection event is credited to two true events while the second event’s dedicated detection is suppressed by Gaussian smoothing overlap.

Veto Scenario 3 — GPS multipath position jump. Counterfactual: stadium multipath causes a position error \| \Delta \mathbf{p} \|_2 > 50 m within one 10 Hz sample. Verification: inject synthetic outliers exceeding the field length (110 m Canadian field width is shorter, but longitudinal jumps > 50 m are detectable) into raw GPS and recompute velocity magnitude s = \|\mathbf{v}\|_2; veto if max(s) exceeds the 99th percentile of the training speed distribution. Prediction: velocity-magnitude spikes inflate \mu_s and \sigma_s in \boldsymbol{\phi} [PAGE 5], creating false-positive play scores during non-play intervals and potentially masking true plays by shifting the sequence out of the recurrent network’s in-distribution manifold. F1 on affected games will drop below the logistic regression baseline of 44% ± 8% [PAGE 10], triggering an automatic model veto.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
