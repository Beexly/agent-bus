<THINK_TRACE>
To execute the deep multi-hop derivation and gray-zone audit, we must analyze Tolman's assertion of "togetherness" as a fundamental constraint on learning. The core argument posits that a sign (stimulus), a means-end relation (behavioral pathway), and a significate (goal/outcome) must be presented in a configuration that sets them off as a unit or "figure" from the background. This is not merely temporal contiguity but a perceptual grouping—a Gestalt—that allows the cognitive map to form. Thorndike's experiment provides a stark contrast: pairs presented sequentially led subjects to associate the word with the *following* number due to habitual instructional frames ("belongingness"), even when specific numerical precursors were consistently paired with words multiple times. The failure to learn the reverse association (number -> word) despite 24 repetitions highlights that raw frequency is insufficient; the "togetherness" must align with the organism's anticipatory set.

The gray zone emerges in defining the boundaries of this "togetherness." Is it purely sensory co-occurrence? Or does it involve higher-order semantic framing, such as the subjects' expectation of a "word followed by number" structure? The trap lies in assuming that any consistent pairing creates a link. Instead, the link forms only if the pairing violates the expected "background" noise enough to create a distinct figure. If the environment is bombarded with millions of stimuli, the system requires a mechanism to filter and group. The breakdown regime occurs when the "togetherness" is too weak to overcome the prior distribution of expectations (the "habits of life"). In Thorndike's case, the "word-number" frame was so strong that the "number-word" connection remained latent, buried under the statistical noise of the general series. The derivation thus moves from simple stimulus-response to a complex interaction between environmental structure, internal expectancy, and the emergent properties of the cognitive map.

Furthermore, we must consider the recursive nature of this process. The "togetherness" that defines a figure-ground relationship for one trial becomes the background context for subsequent trials. If an agent learns that "whiteness" predicts "food," this specific gestalt becomes a new element in the environmental matrix. The challenge is determining when a previously learned "togetherness" should be overwritten or integrated. This is where the concept of "purposive behaviorism" diverges sharply from mechanistic S-R theories: the organism actively constructs meaning based on anticipated ends, not just passive associations. The "gray zone" is thus the space between mechanical repetition and meaningful interpretation, a boundary that cannot be crossed without the subjective experience of significance.

**Formalism:** We formalize "togetherness" as a constrained joint probability distribution over the feature space of Sign ($S$), Means-End Relation ($M$), and Significate ($E$), conditioned on the Background Context ($B$). The system seeks to maximize the mutual information between the components of the Gestalt while minimizing the entropy of the figure relative to the ground.

**LaTeX Definition:**
Let $\mathcal{G} = \{s, m, e\}$ denote the candidate Gestalt. Let $b \in B$ denote the background ensemble. The "togetherness" score $\tau(\mathcal{G}|b)$ is defined via the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the joint distribution of the candidate parts and the product of their marginals within the background context:
$$ \tau(\mathcal{G}|b) = D_{KL}\left( P(s,m,e | b) \,\|\, P(s|b)P(m|b)P(e|b) \right) $$
where $P(x|b)$ represents the probability of feature $x$ occurring in the given background. High $\tau$ indicates strong "togetherness" or "belongingness" (low likelihood under independent background statistics).

**Units:** Nats (natural units of information) or Bits.
**Assumptions:** 
1. The background $B$ is stationary over the learning epoch.
2. Perception acts as a low-pass filter, sampling discrete events from a continuous stream.
3. "Belongingness" is a heuristic derived from the prior distribution $P(S,M,E)$ established by evolutionary or experiential history.

**Singularity/Breakdown Regimes:**
1. **The Equiprobability Trap:** When $P(s,m,e | b) \approx P(s|b)P(m|b)P(e|b)$, $\tau \to 0$. Even with high frequency ($N \to \infty$), no learning occurs because the triplet provides no reduction in uncertainty relative to the background. This corresponds to Thorndike's failure to learn the number-word pair.
2. **Semantic Saturation/Collapse:** As the frequency of a specific Gestalt increases, $P(s|b)$ increases, potentially diluting the distinctiveness of $s$. If $s$ becomes part of the general background noise, the figure-ground separation collapses, and learning stalls.
3. **Overfitting Singularity:** If $\tau$ is maximized locally by spurious correlations in $b$, the agent forms rigid, maladaptive habits that fail under slight perturbations in the environment.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogy of "togetherness" traces back to the German Gestalt psychologists (Köhler, Wertheimer, Koffka) who rebelled against Wundtian elementism and early associative learning theories. They argued that perception is holistic, not built atom-by-atom. Tolman imported this into behaviorism, creating "purposive behaviorism," arguing that rats build cognitive maps of mazes, not just chains of muscle movements. The "sign-significate" dyad is a direct inheritance from semiotics (Peirce) and Gestalt psychology.

The retrocausal influence is evident in modern Cognitive Science and Reinforcement Learning (RL). The idea that an agent needs a "model" of the world—predicting consequences of actions—stems from Tolman’s insistence on mediating variables (expectancies). However, a multi-week trap exists in the current AI/ML paradigm: the obsession with massive scale and gradient descent often ignores the necessity of structural "togetherness." Large Language Models (LLMs) ingest billions of tokens, yet they struggle with true causal reasoning or planning because the training objective (next-token prediction) lacks the teleological "means-end" structure Tolman emphasized. They see co-occurrence but miss the purposive unity. The historical lineage shows a drift away from "purpose" toward pure statistical correlation, leading to systems that are fluent but lack grounded understanding of "belongingness." To recover robust intelligence, we must reintegrate the concept that learning requires a deliberate construction of figures against backgrounds, not just passive absorption of data streams.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In crisis situations (e.g., rapid environmental shift, unexpected reward omission), an agent may regress to primitive S-R habit loops, bypassing the higher-level cognitive map. This "habit regression" is a failure of the "togetherness" maintenance mechanism; the complex Gestalt (Sign-M-E) fragments under stress. For instance, a rat trained to navigate a complex maze might revert to thigmotaxis (wall-following) if the distal cues are obscured. The counter-trap involves recognizing that the breakdown of the figure-ground segregation is a signal to re-evaluate the background assumptions.

However, there is a critical "clock" constraint: the time required to reconstruct the Gestalt versus the time-to-collision or starvation. If the reconstruction of "togetherness" is too slow, the reflexive fallback wins. A sophisticated Theory of Mind in an artificial agent would monitor its own confidence in the current Gestalt representation. It would detect when the predictive error spikes (indicating a loss of "togetherness") and trigger a meta-cognitive switch: either engage in rapid exploratory behavior to gather new data for a new figure, or deliberately suppress the failing model to allow a simpler, robust heuristic to take over. The trap is getting stuck in an oscillatory loop between trying to force a broken Gestalt and reverting to panic-mode habits, wasting cycles without achieving adaptation.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Even if the computational layer successfully identifies a high-$\tau$ "togetherness," a veto can occur at lower physical or biomechanical layers. Consider the weather veto: a bird learns a specific visual pattern (cloud shape + light polarization) signifies rain (Sign -> E). However, if a sudden microburst changes the air density, the biomechanical ability to fly or the physiological need for immediate shelter overrides the predictive map. The "togetherness" of cloud-shape and rain is logically valid, but physically irrelevant in the face of immediate kinetic threat.

Another veto arises from motor capacity limits. An agent might perceive a perfect spatial-temporal "togetherness" between a target location and a reward, but the musculoskeletal system may be fatigued or injured, making the "means-end" relation physically impossible. The adjudication protocol must prioritize survival vetoes. A formal proof involves modeling the system as a hierarchy: $L_{cognitive}$ generates plans based on Gestalts, but $L_{biomech}$ and $L_{physio}$ provide hard constraints (inequalities on force, speed, energy). If the plan from $L_{cognitive}$ violates a constraint $C \in L_{biomech}$, the plan is vetoed regardless of how high the "togetherness" score is. This prevents the agent from attempting "impossible" actions suggested by a corrupted or overly optimistic cognitive map.
