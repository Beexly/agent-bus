Medieval supposition theory refines this by distinguishing personal, material, and simple supposition, thereby recognizing that substitution is context-relative—a direct anticipation of James’s footnote that no realities are absolutely the same apart from purpose [PAGE 660]. Leibniz formulates substitutivity salva veritate and the identity of indiscernibles, giving the strongest possible version of “the same can be substituted for the same.” Frege complicates the picture by distinguishing sense from reference, showing that substitution fails in opaque contexts; Russell follows with the theory of descriptions, handling cases where a term appears to name a kind but lacks a unique referent—precisely James’s scenario in which we correct the case rather than the principle [PAGE 660]. Tarski formalizes truth and satisfaction, while Quine challenges the analytic/synthetic distinction, undermining the idea that logical schemes are wholly immune to empirical revision.

James’s distinctive contribution is the pragmatist inversion: instead of letting recalcitrant cases falsify the logical principle, we correct the cases by the principle [PAGE 660]. This creates a multi-week trap in longitudinal observation. Over days and weeks, an observer may consolidate a kind-label M and its associated property set \(\mathcal{P}(M)\), then systematically discount disconfirming instances by reclassifying them as errors, changes, or non-M’s. Because the formal scheme is treated as immutable, the equivalence class \(x =_{M,\pi} y\) becomes entrenched even as the underlying purpose \(\pi\) drifts or the empirical fit \(p_{\text{fit}}(M)\) decays. The trap is multi-week because the correction asymmetry operates slowly: each individual case-correction is small, but the cumulative effect is a category that no longer tracks nature’s facts while remaining logically invulnerable. A second trap is boundary singularity: as continuous behavioral or perceptual properties are binned into discrete kinds over weeks, arbitrarily small measurement differences (\(\delta < \Delta p_{\min}\)) begin to flip kind membership unpredictably, producing apparent instability that is actually a quantization artifact of the logical scheme imposed on continuous reality.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when an agent, under pressure, reverts to a cached kind-mapping \(x =_{M,\pi_0} y\) established prior to the crisis, ignoring emerging property mismatches. Formally, let \(C(t)\) denote crisis intensity and \(t\) the time elapsed since crisis onset in seconds. The probability that the agent has not yet regressed to the habitual equivalence class follows a survival function \(S(t \mid C) = e^{-\lambda_C t}\), with regression hazard \(h_C(t) = \lambda_C\) in \(\text{s}^{-1}\); thus the regression probability is \(P_{\text{regress}}(t \mid C) = 1 - e^{-\lambda_C t}\). This is a standard survival model, not a toy ratio. The counter-trap is the observer’s second-order anticipation that the agent will regress; the observer then pre-emptively substitutes a corrected mapping \(x \neq_{M,\pi_1} y\) and acts on that expectation. Recursive theory of mind enters when the agent models the observer’s counter-trap and the observer models the agent’s model, yielding nested beliefs \(B_i(B_j(\text{regress}))\) that can either converge to a fixed point or diverge into an infinite regress if neither party terminates the recursion.

The clock is the hard deadline \(T \in \text{s}\) by which a corrected substitution must be implemented before the opportunity window closes. The joint probability of successful counter-trap execution before deadline is \[ P_{\text{success}} = P\bigl(\tau_{\text{correct}} < T \mid C\bigr) = 1 - e^{-\lambda_{\text{correct}} T}, \] where \(\lambda_{\text{correct}}\) is the correction rate in \(\text{s}^{-1}\). Breakdown regimes: (i) \(\lambda_C \to \infty\), producing instantaneous habit regression before any counter-trap can be deployed; (ii) \(T \to 0\), collapsing the correction window to zero; (iii) infinite recursive nesting with no fixed point, so the system never commits to a substitution. All temporal quantities are measured in seconds; all probabilities are dimensionless elements of \([0,1]\); information processed during recursion is measured in bits. No betting-style quantities are invoked.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers must jointly satisfy identity before any substitution \(x =_{M,\pi} y\) is licensed. Layer 1 (physical/neural): \(I_{\text{phys}}(x,y) \equiv \forall p \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{phys}},\; p(x) \leftrightarrow p(y)\), where \(\mathcal{P}_{\text{phys}}\) are substrate realization properties. Layer 2 (biomechanical/enactive): \(I_{\text{bio}}(x,y) \equiv \forall q \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{bio}},\; q(x) \leftrightarrow q(y)\), covering action execution and motor-pattern properties. Layer 3 (environmental/weather/context): \(I_{\text{env}}(x,y) \equiv \forall r \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{env}}(\pi),\; r(x) \leftrightarrow r(y)\), where \(\mathcal{P}_{\text{env}}\) includes temperature, humidity, illumination, and purpose-relevant affordances. The veto operator is \[ V(x,y;\pi) \equiv \neg\bigl(I_{\text{phys}}(x,y) \land I_{\text{bio}}(x,y) \land I_{\text{env}}(x,y;\pi)\bigr). \] If \(V\) is true, substitution is vetoed. Proof: assume substitution is claimed valid. By James’s criterion, kind-membership requires possession of the kind’s properties [PAGE 660]; if any layer exhibits a property mismatch, then \(x\) and \(y\) do not possess the same properties under that layer, so \(x \neq_{M,\pi} y\), contradicting the substitution premise. Therefore \(V(x,y;\pi) \Rightarrow\) substitution invalid.

Weather veto example: two performances are biomechanically identical (\(I_{\text{bio}}\) true) and neurally similar (\(I_{\text{phys}}\) true), but a change in temperature or humidity alters the available affordance so that \(I_{\text{env}}\) fails; substitution is vetoed even though lower layers agree. Breakdown singularity occurs when sensor noise \(\sigma\) exceeds the property difference \(\Delta p\) between layers, i.e. \(\sigma > \Delta p\), making layer identity empirically undecidable. Another veto regime is purpose-frame mismatch: \(\pi_1 \neq \pi_2\) such that \(I_{\text{env}}(x,y;\pi_1)\) holds but \(I_{\text{env}}(x,y;\pi_2)\) fails; cross-purpose substitution is invalid. All quantities use SI units (kelvin for temperature, seconds for time, meters for spatial properties) or dimensionless probabilities; no ad hoc ratios are introduced.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR denotes a learning system whose parameter updates are gated by verifiable reward signals rather than by inferred betting-style quantities. Three counterfactual veto scenarios are specified below, each with a formal veto condition, a verifiable trigger, and a prediction.

Scenario 1: Property-drift veto. Counterfactual world \(W_1\): objects \(x\) and \(y\) are labeled kind \(M\) at time \(t_0\) under purpose \(\pi_0\), so \(x =_{M,\pi_0} y\). At time \(t_1 > t_0\), a verifiable sensor log shows \(\exists p \in \mathcal{P}(M)\) such that \(p(x_{t_1}) \neq p(y_{t_1})\). Veto condition: \(V(x,y;\pi_0)\) becomes true at \(t_1\) because the empirical fit \(p_{\text{fit}}(M)\) falls below a confidence threshold \(\theta \in [0,1]\). Verifiable trigger: divergence in the property time-series \(p(x_t)\) vs \(p(y_t)\) exceeding measurement resolution \(\delta\). Prediction: any prediction or substitution that relied on \(x =_{M,\pi_0} y\) after \(t_1\) produces a verifiable error spike; the system must recompute equivalence classes before further updates, or the reward signal \(R\) is invalidated by construction.

Scenario 2: Purpose-frame collapse veto. Counterfactual world \(W_2\): an instruction log switches the operative purpose from \(\pi_a\) to \(\pi_b\) at timestamp \(\tau\). Under \(\pi_a\), \(x =_{M,\pi_a} y\) holds; under \(\pi_b\), \(x \neq_{M,\pi_b} y\) because \(\mathcal{P}_{\text{env}}\) weights a property differently. Veto condition: cross-purpose substitution \(x =_{M,\pi_a} y\) used after \(\tau\) triggers \(V(x,y;\pi_b)\). Verifiable trigger: the purpose-switch event in the instruction log is timestamped and immutable. Prediction: error rate on post-\(\tau\) trials increases relative to pre-\(\tau\) baseline; the entropy of the action distribution \(H(A \mid S)\) increases by a measurable positive number of bits, and the system must purge \(\pi_a\)-based substitutions from its active cache.

Scenario 3: Multi-layer environmental fracture veto. Counterfactual world \(W_3\): neural pattern \(x\) and behavioral output \(y\) satisfy \(I_{\text{phys}}\) and \(I_{\text{bio}}\) in a controlled laboratory setting, but the environmental layer \(I_{\text{env}}(x,y;\pi)\) fails because temperature, humidity, or wind changes the affordance structure. Veto condition: transfer of the lab-validated substitution to the field setting is vetoed by \(V(x,y;\pi)\) because \(I_{\text{env}}\) is false. Verifiable trigger: telemetry from the field setting logs \(\mathcal{P}_{\text{env}}\) values outside the laboratory calibration envelope, with timestamps synchronized to performance trials. Prediction: field performance degrades relative to laboratory baseline; the magnitude of degradation is a function of environmental context distance \(D_{\text{env}}\), and no valid substitution can be claimed until \(I_{\text{env}}\) is re-established or the kind definition is revised to include the new environmental properties.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
