\[

\]

\]

\]
[PAGE 6]. The shifted-window operation moves features by \(\lfloor M/2\rfloor,\lfloor M/2\rfloor\) pixels before partitioning [PAGE 6]. The paper reports a “grid of 4 × 4” windows [PAGE 9], but does not give a fully explicit \(M\) value in the quoted experimental settings.

\[
L_{CE}(p_i,y_i)=-y_i\log p_i
\]
[PAGE 7], where \(p_i\) is a predicted class probability in the interval \([0,1]\) and \(y_i\) is the true label. Triplet loss is
\[
L_T=\max(d_{a,p}-d_{a,n}+\text{margin},0)
\]
[PAGE 7], where \(d_{a,p}\) and \(d_{a,n}\) are Euclidean feature distances [PAGE 7]; the margin has the same unit as the feature distance and is set to 0.3 [PAGE 9]. Focal loss is
\[
L_F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-p_i)^{\gamma}L_{CE}(p_i,y_i)
\]
[PAGE 7], where \(n\) is the number of categories and \(\gamma\) is a dimensionless hyperparameter above zero [PAGE 7]. The fusion loss is
\[
L_{\text{Fusion}}=L_{CE}+L_T+L_F
\]
[PAGE 7]. The paper sets \(\gamma=2\) after an experiment on SoccerNet-v3 [PAGE 10]. Because “Players from the same team often look alike, as they wear the same team jerseys” [PAGE 2], a degraded crop with occlusion or motion blur can push the representation toward uniform color, shorts, socks, and body silhouette rather than identity-specific evidence. The paper names the crisis inputs directly: “low video resolution, camera movement, player pose, lighting conditions, and uniform similarities among players” [PAGE 1], and “occlusions and fast player movement” [PAGE 2]. In that regime, a global-feature habit is rational from the model’s perspective but wrong for ReID.

The counter-trap is the RFES design plus fusion loss plus re-ranking. RFEB restores local spatial extraction through a \(3\times3\) dilated convolution with dilation 2 and a \(7\times7\) receptive field [PAGE 6-7]. Fusion loss adds cross-entropy for classification, triplet for inter-sample similarity, and focal loss for hard or imbalanced samples [PAGE 7]. Re-ranking with k-reciprocal encoding then edits the initial ranked list using Jaccard and Mahalanobis distances [PAGE 8]. The recursive theory-of-mind point is that the counter-trap can itself become a habit: if the gallery is action-local and the top-k neighbors are structurally similar, re-ranking can reinforce a plausible but wrong neighbor set. The paper’s own numbers show the counter-trap is effective under its protocol, but not that it is identity-causal across actions.

The clock is the action boundary. The paper’s stated goal is “the same player in images taken at any given moment during a game from various camera angles” [PAGE 1], but SoccerNet-v3 labels reset across actions: “player ID tags do not stay the same from one action to another” [PAGE 8]. The evaluation clock is therefore not the match clock; it is the action-segment clock, and “only samples from the same activity are compared to one another” [PAGE 8-9]. A recursive observer must keep two clocks separate: the broadcast/game clock implied by the task statement, and the annotation clock actually used in the benchmark. The model is not tested on persistent identity across the full match clock, so any claim of full-game ReID exceeds the source’s evidence.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical-layer veto is admissible only through pixel evidence. The source’s input is an image tensor with height, width, and channel dimensions; it contains no separate sensor stream for force, weather, or joint angles. Physical vetoes that are explicitly represented include occlusion, low resolution, motion blur, lighting, viewing angle, and uniform similarity [PAGE 1-2]. These are visual-physical nuisances, and they can veto identity evidence by destroying or confusing the appearance signal. The paper’s RFEB and fusion loss are designed to counter part of this layer, but the paper does not provide a calibrated physical model of blur, illumination, or occlusion severity.

The biomechanical-layer veto is not proof-supported by this source. The paper mentions “player pose” as a challenge [PAGE 1], but it does not extract skeleton joints, gait cycles, ground reaction forces, or musculoskeletal variables. There is no equation in the source that maps joint angles or forces to identity. Therefore a biomechanical veto cannot be independently adjudicated from this paper; any pose effect must enter through the image pixels and the learned representation, not through a measured biomechanical layer.

The weather-layer veto is also absent. The source lists lighting and motion blur but does not report rain, wind, temperature, pitch wetness, or weather covariates. There is no weather variable in the input and no weather-stratified evaluation. A weather effect could only be inferred if it changes pixels through illumination, blur, or occlusion, and the paper does not measure that pathway. The adjudication is therefore asymmetric: physical/visual vetoes are admissible as image-level confounds; biomechanical and weather vetoes are not independently provable from the supplied pages. Any claim that RFES-ReID is robust to weather or biomechanics would require new data and new operators not present in the source.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
