<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is explicitly anti-molecular. Tolman opposes the molecular conception "frequently espoused by Watson, Weiss and Meyer" in favor of the molar conception adopted in the treatise ([PAGE 439]). Historically, this runs from Watson's 1913 behaviorist manifesto through Tolman's 1932 purposive behaviorism, into latent learning and cognitive-map theory, and forward into modern reinforcement learning and predictive processing, where expected future states mediate present action. The "retrocausal" character is teleonomic: a means-end relation—a representation of a future outcome—functions as a present behavior-support ([PAGE 439]), so the anticipated goal exerts causal influence on current behavior without violating physical causality.

Multi-week traps arise when this mediation layer is treated as static across longitudinal observation. Trap 1: stationarity of intermediating variables. Across weeks, \(\mathbf{m}\) drifts with learning, satiety, stress, and neuroplasticity; fitting one behavior-equation \(\Phi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{m};\boldsymbol{\xi})\) from week 1 and extrapolating to week 4 confounds parameter drift with treatment effects. Counter-trap: assuming intermediating variables are infinitely plastic. If \(\mathbf{m}\) is reset to a default each session, genuine consolidation and habit formation are missed. The clock is the third element: circadian and ultradian rhythms modulate \(\mathbf{i}(t)\) (e.g., cortisol core-temperature rhythm), so the behavior-equation parameters \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\) are time-of-day dependent. Ignoring the clock produces phase-locked residuals that are misread as learning or as treatment response. Over multi-week horizons, the conjunction of parameter drift and circadian modulation means that a null effect in week 2 can mask a strong effect in week 5 if sampling is not synchronized to the physiological clock.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when acute stress collapses the intermediating layer onto a habitual subspace: \(\mathbf{m}_{\text{crisis}}=\Pi_{\text{habit}}(\mathbf{m})\), where \(\Pi_{\text{habit}}\) projects onto previously overlearned molar patterns. Under crisis, the organism regresses to high-basal-ganglia policies and away from flexible, means-end-guided behavior supported by discrimianda and manipulanda ([PAGE 439]).

The counter-trap is recursive misattribution. The experimenter may attribute a theory of mind to the subject—modeling the subject as modeling the environment—when behavior is actually driven by immediate S-I reflexes; or the experimenter may deny purposive mediation when \(\mathbf{m}\) is genuinely operative. Formally, let \(\mu_A=P(\mathbf{E}\mid do(\mathbf{b}))\) be the animal's model of environmental response, and \(\mu_X=P(\mu_A\mid\text{data})\) the experimenter's model of the animal's model. Crisis breaks this fixed point when \(\mu_A\) collapses to a reflexive policy, making \(\mu_X\) systematically over-predict flexibility.

The clock operates through decision latency and inter-trial intervals. If crisis onset occurs faster than the sampling interval \(\Delta t\), the recursive structure is aliased: the experimenter observes post-crisis behavior and mislabels it as stable \(\mathbf{m}\). Adequate recursive theory of mind therefore requires sampling faster than the fastest physiological transition in \(\mathbf{i}(t)\) and probing whether behavior-supports \(\Psi(\mathbf{e})\) remain present during regression ([PAGE 439]).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical veto: A behavior-act requires a manipulandum of mass \(m_{\text{man}}\) and required force \(F_{\text{req}}\). The animal's maximum isometric force \(F_{\max}(\theta,\mathbf{i})\) depends on joint angle \(\theta\) and initiating physiological state \(\mathbf{i}\) (muscle fatigue, glycogen, temperature). Veto predicate:
\[
V_{\text{bio}} = \mathbb{I}\!\left[F_{\text{req}} \le F_{\max}(\theta,\mathbf{i})\right].
\]
Near joint limits \(F_{\max}\to0\), producing a biomechanical singularity.

Weather/environmental veto: Discrimianda perceptibility depends on visibility \(V[\mathrm{m}]\), precipitation rate \(\rho[\mathrm{mm/h}]\), and ambient temperature \(T_a[\mathrm{K}]\). If \(V<V_{\text{thr}}\) or \(T_a\) lies outside the thermoneutral zone, behavior-supports are degraded:
\[
V_{\text{weather}}=\mathbb{I}\!\left[V\ge V_{\text{thr}}\ \wedge\ T_{\min}\le T_a\le T_{\max}\right].
\]

Support-presence veto:
\[
V_{\text{support}}=\mathbb{I}\!\left[\Psi(\mathbf{e})\supseteq\{\mathbf{d},\mathbf{man},\mathbf{r}\}_{\text{required}}\right].
\]

Combined veto proof:
\[
V_{\text{total}}=V_{\text{bio}}\wedge V_{\text{weather}}\wedge V_{\text{support}}\wedge \mathbb{I}[\mathbf{m}\text{ recruited}].
\]
The act occurs iff \(V_{\text{total}}=1\). This is a veto proof because any single false conjunct prevents the behavior-act regardless of motivational strength or the apparent strength of environmental stimuli ([PAGE 439]).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Manipulandum-removal veto. Counterfactual: in a lever-press operant, remove the lever (manipulandum absent) while holding stimulus \(S\) (cue light) and initiating state \(I\) (food deprivation) constant. Veto: behavior-support manipulandum missing \(\Rightarrow\) press cannot "go off" ([PAGE 439]). Verifiable prediction: press rate drops to baseline/zero within the session despite intact \(S\) and \(I\). Falsifiable if presses persist at trained rates without the lever (would imply alternative manipulanda or superstitious chaining).

Scenario 2 — Physiological-boundary veto. Counterfactual: induce verified hypoxia or extreme fatigue so \(I\to I_{\mathrm{crit}}\). Veto: initiating state cannot recruit intermediating variables; channel \(p(\mathbf{b}\mid\mathbf{s},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{m})\) collapses to \(p(\mathbf{b}\mid\mathbf{s})\). Verifiable prediction: response probability becomes independent of \(\mathbf{m}\), and response latency diverges (\(\tau\to\infty\)) as \(I\) approaches the physiological boundary. Falsifiable if performance remains unchanged under verified hypoxia (would mean \(I\) was not the limiting variable).

Scenario 3 — Weather/occlusion veto on discrimianda. Counterfactual: introduce heavy visual occlusion reducing \(V\) below threshold while \(S\) and \(I\) remain constant. Veto: discrimianda unavailable \(\Rightarrow\) discrimination support missing. Verifiable prediction: binary discrimination accuracy falls to chance (0.5) even though \(I\) and \(\mathbf{m}\) are intact; response rate may persist but correctness collapses. Falsifiable if accuracy remains above chance under verified occlusion (would imply non-visual cue use or that \(V\) was above threshold).
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
