By the Radon-Nikodym theorem, there exists a unique (up to $\nu$-null sets) non-negative measurable function $p(E, \cdot): Y \to [0, \infty)$ such that for all $F \in \mathcal{B}$:
$$
\mu(E \cap T^{-1}(F)) = \int_F p(E,y) \, d\nu(y).
$$
This equation is the formal substitute for the heuristic difference quotient:
$$
\frac{\mu(E \cap T^{-1}(F))}{\mu(T^{-1}(F))} \approx p(E,y) \quad \text{as } F \downarrow \{y\}.
$$
**Units and Information Content:** If $\mu$ represents a probability measure on a state space $X$, then $p(E,y)$ has units of "probability per unit $\nu$-measure". The "information" gained by observing $V=y$ is quantified by the reduction in uncertainty about $U$, measured by the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the prior $\mu_X$ and the posterior $p(\cdot,y)$.

**Assumptions and Breakdown Regimes:**
1.  **Measurability:** The existence of $p$ relies crucially on the measurability of $T$ and the completeness of the measure spaces. If $T$ is not measurable, the pullback $T^{-1}(F)$ may not be in $\mathcal{A}$, breaking the definition.
