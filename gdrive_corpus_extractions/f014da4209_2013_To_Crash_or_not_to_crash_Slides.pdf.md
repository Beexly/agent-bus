<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical and biomechanical constraints can veto any analytical recommendation to crash. Let \(d\) be the distance in meters from a player to the projected rebound location, \(t_r\) the ball-flight-plus-bounce time in seconds, \(v_{\max}\) the player’s sprint speed in m/s, \(a_{\max,i}\) the player’s acceleration limit in m/s², \(\mu_s\) the shoe-floor static friction coefficient, \(g=9.80665\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\), \(h\) the required center-of-mass rise in meters, and \(J\) the collision impulse in N·s. A crash is physically inadmissible if
\[
\frac{d}{t_r}>v_{\max},\qquad
\frac{2d}{t_r^2}>\mu_s g,\qquad
mgh>E_{\mathrm{leg,max}},\qquad
J>J_{\mathrm{safe}}.
\]
The first inequality is a speed veto; the second is a slip/deceleration veto; the third is a vertical-jump energy veto; the fourth is an injury/contact veto. If \(t_r<t_{\mathrm{react}}+t_{\mathrm{decel}}\), reaction and braking time alone veto the crash. If \(a_{\max,i}(t)<2d/t_r^2\), that specific player cannot reach the rebound location even with perfect intent. These are not statistical preferences; they are hard constraints in SI units.

The weather veto is an indoor-microclimate veto. Even in an arena, temperature, humidity, condensation, and HVAC flow enter through shoe-floor friction and ball flight. Let \(\rho(T,p,RH)\) be air density in kg/m³ and \(F_D=\frac12\rho C_D A v^2\) the drag force in N; changes in \(\rho\) alter the ball’s rebound path and therefore the projected \(d\). More importantly, if humidity or condensation lowers \(\mu_s\), the friction limit \(\mu_s g\) can fall below \(2d/t_r^2\). The critical friction is
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}=\frac{v^2}{2dg}.
\]
If \(\mu_s<\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\), a crash that looks profitable on [PAGE 45]–[PAGE 52] is physically unsafe: the player cannot stop, land, or change direction without slipping. If \(\rho\) or \(RH\) is outside the calibration range used for the tracking model, the rebound-location distribution and the 8-ft/33-ft zone assignment [PAGE 23] are also outside their validated regime. The veto proof is therefore: when any physical, biomechanical, or microclimate inequality fails, the analytic recommendation is inadmissible regardless of the expected-points table. The model may still describe average behavior, but it cannot command a crash that the body and floor cannot execute.
