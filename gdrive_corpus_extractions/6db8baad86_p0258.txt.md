Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios:

Scenario 1 — Immigration of name-bearers. Verifiable condition: a closed-population Galton–Watson model with \(\mu<1\) predicts \(q=1\), yet a surname persists in a region over multiple generations because bearers continually migrate in. Veto: the model’s assumption of no immigration is false. Counterfactual prediction: if borders to bearer migration are closed at generation \(t\), the surname frequency enters subcritical decline and extinguishes with probability approaching 1 as generations proceed; if migration remains open, persistence is maintained despite \(\mu<1\).

Scenario 2 — Skewed sex ratio at birth. Verifiable condition: empirical birth records show \(\rho=0.6\) (more males), but the analyst assumes \(\rho=0.5\). The true mean is \(\mu=0.6\mathbb{E}[N]\), not \(0.5\mathbb{E}[N]\). Voto: using \(\rho=0.5\) misplaces the critical threshold \(\mathbb{E}[N]=1/\rho\). Counterfactual prediction: under the true \(\rho=0.6\), persistence requires \(\mathbb{E}[N]>1/0.6\approx1.67\); under the false \(\rho=0.5\), the analyst incorrectly demands \(\mathbb{E}[N]>2\). A population with \(\mathbb{E}[N]=1.8\) is supercritical in reality but judged subcritical by the misspecified model.

Scenario 3 — Surname change without genetic extinction. Verifiable condition: legal or cultural name-switching occurs at rate \(\lambda>0\) per generation, so a biological lineage survives but the surname does not. Veto: equating surname extinction with lineage extinction double-counts attrition. Counterfactual prediction: the observed surname extinction probability is \(q_{\text{name}}=1-(1-q_{\text{bio}})(1-\lambda)^{\text{generations}}\), which exceeds the pure branching extinction \(q_{\text{bio}}\) from the Galton–Watson fixed point [PAGE 241]. If \(\lambda\) is removed (names frozen), observed surname extinction drops to the biological branching value.
