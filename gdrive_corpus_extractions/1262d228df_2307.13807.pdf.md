<THINK_TRACE>
The source is not a match-prediction oracle; it is a decision-theoretic stack. The multi-hop chain is: (1) represent wagering as a decision tuple \((D,E,C,\succeq)\) [PAGE 1]; (2) map outcomes to random returns \(\varrho\) and aggregate return \(R(\ell)=1+\ell'\varrho\) [PAGE 2]; (3) choose a utility \(u\), either quadratic \(u(x)=\beta_0+\beta_1x+\beta_2x^2\) giving mean-variance preferences [PAGE 4], or logarithmic \(u(x)=\log x+C\) giving Kelly growth [PAGE 4–5]; (4) optimize either the Sharpe ratio or the multivariate/simultaneous Kelly objective [PAGE 4–7]; (5) estimate \(p\) with a neural network trained by cross-entropy/KL minimization [PAGE 3, 7–9]; (6) evaluate out-of-sample over the second half of the 2020/2021 EPL season [PAGE 1, 7–10]. The headline “135.8% relative to the initial wealth” [PAGE 1] is therefore conditional on a long chain of assumptions, not a free-standing empirical law.

The central derivation is straightforward but fragile. For a single event, the paper writes \(G(\ell)=p\log(1+(o-1)\ell)+(1-p)\log(1-\ell)\) and obtains \(\ell^*=(op-1)/(o-1)\) [PAGE 5]. For multiple simultaneous events, it replaces the scalar log with \(G(\ell)=\sum_i p_i\log(1+o_i\ell_i-\sum_j\ell_j)\) [PAGE 6] and then a matrix form \(\max p'\log(1+W'\ell-\sum_i\ell_i1)\) subject to \(\sum_i\ell_i\le1,\ell\ge0\) [PAGE 6]. The simultaneous version uses \(p_i=\prod_k P[\varrho_k(\omega_{i,k})]\) under independence [PAGE 7]. The Sharpe route is mean-variance: \(S(\ell)=(\ell'\mu-R_f)/\sqrt{\ell'\Sigma\ell}\), transformed by \(y=\kappa\ell\) into a convex program [PAGE 4]. Thus the paper’s two “utilities” are not cosmetic alternatives; they induce different geometry, different singularities, and different ruin behavior.

The gray-zone audit must start with the premise that “the actual probabilities of events are known.” The paper itself questions this: the model’s a priori cross-entropy is 1.0318, empirical frequency entropy is 1.0631, and maximum entropy for three outcomes is 1.0986 [PAGE 14]. That is close to the maximum-entropy ceiling, so the estimated \(p\) is weak information, not near-certain knowledge. The model’s accuracy is 54% versus 51.5% for the crowd [PAGE 9], and the best reported portfolio has betting p-value 0.21 and wealth p-value 0.16 [PAGE 13]. Those p-values do not certify a positive edge. The paper also reports a 500-simulation Dirichlet check where the model beats 78 out of 100 simulations on average, but finds no linear relation between log-growth and performance (\(p=0.595\)) [PAGE 14]. So the empirical result is a conditional sample-path outcome, not a proof of durable superiority.

Second gray zone: mechanical admissibility. The paper assumes no short selling, no borrowing, infinite divisibility, no minimum bet, \(\ell_i\ge0\), and \(\sum_i\ell_i\le1\) [PAGE 2]. It also assumes fixed probabilities and fixed payout coefficients over time [PAGE 2], and independence across simultaneous events [PAGE 7]. If any of these fail, the optimization is solving a different problem than the one realized. The paper explicitly notes that the numerical algorithm did not converge for matchweek 23 due to gradient overflow and used the last iterate as an approximation [PAGE 10]. That is a concrete breakdown regime: the log objective becomes numerically singular near the budget boundary.

Third gray zone: strategy instability. Restricted strategies “exhibited better convergence and higher returns” but also “higher variance and lower diversification” [PAGE 13]. The Sharpe criterion produced “the complete loss of funds two weeks prior to the conclusion of the study” [PAGE 13]. Fractional Kelly at \(f=17\%\) was chosen after 200 Dirichlet simulations [PAGE 10], but the paper notes that log-growth is not invariant to fractional bets, so fractionalizing before versus after optimization yields a different strategy [PAGE 13]. Therefore the “best” portfolio is not a stable object; it is an artifact of order of operations, regularization, and the chosen fraction.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(W_0\) be initial wealth; the paper normalizes \(W_0=1\) without loss of generality [PAGE 10]. Returns are dimensionless. Information quantities are in nats when natural log is used; under base-2 log they are bits and rescale by \(1/\ln2\). Runtime is in seconds.

Decision object: \((D,E,C,\succeq)\), where \(D\) is the decision set, \(E\) the event set, \(C\) the consequence set, and \(\succeq\) the preference relation [PAGE 1]. The wager vector is \(\ell\), with \(\ell_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\ell_i\le1\) under the paper’s assumptions [PAGE 2].

Return operator:
\[
\varrho=D_o m-1,\qquad m\sim\mathrm{multinomial}(1;p),
\]
\[
R(\ell)=1+\sum_{i=1}^m \ell_i\varrho_i=1+\ell'\varrho.
\]
These are dimensionless ratios [PAGE 2]. The fair-payout condition is \(E[\varrho]=0\iff o=1/p\) [PAGE 2]. The market commission is defined by \(\sum_i 1/o_i=1+tt\); normal markets have \(tt>0\), while \(tt\le0\) admits arbitrage [PAGE 2]. The arbitrage allocation is
\[
\ell_i^{(A)}:=\frac{o_i^{-1}}{\sum_j o_j^{-1}},
\]
with \(R(\ell_A)=1/(1+tt)\) for every outcome [PAGE 2]. This is the exact regime where the Sharpe denominator collapses: \(\mathrm{Var}(R(\ell_A))=0\) and the Sharpe ratio becomes positively infinite if the numerator is positive [PAGE 5].

Information operators:
\[
H(X):=-E_F[\log X],\qquad H(F,G):=-E_F[\log g(X)],
\]
\[
D_{KL}(F||G):=E_F\!\left[\log\frac{f(X)}{g(X)}\right].
\]
These are in nats under natural log [PAGE 3]. The paper uses KL minimization to justify cross-entropy training of the neural network [PAGE 3, 8–9].

Utility operators:
\[
U(F)=\int_X u\,dF=E_F[u(X)].
\]
Quadratic utility gives
\[
u(x)=\beta_0+\beta_1x+\beta_2x^2,\qquad
U(F)=W(\mu,\sigma),
\]
so preferences depend only on mean and variance [PAGE 4]. Logarithmic utility gives
\[
u'(x)=\frac1x\implies u(x)=\log x+C.
\]
[PAGE 4]

Sharpe operator:
\[
S(\ell):=\frac{\ell'\mu-R_f}{\sqrt{\ell'\Sigma\ell}}.
\]
The convex reformulation is
\[
\arg\min_{y\ge0}\{y'\Sigma y\}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
(\mu-R_f1)'y=1,\quad
\sum_k y_k=\kappa,\quad
\kappa>0,
\]
with \(y=\kappa\ell\) [PAGE 4]. The covariance structure is
\[
\Sigma=D_o\bigl(\mathrm{diag}(p)-pp'\bigr)D_o.
\]
[PAGE 4]

Bivariate Kelly operator:
\[
G(\ell)=p\log(1+(o-1)\ell)+(1-p)\log(1-\ell),
\]
\[
\ell^*=\frac{op-1}{o-1}.
\]
[PAGE 5] At the optimum,
\[
G(\ell^*)=D_{KL}(p||\tilde p),\qquad \tilde p=1/o.
\]
[PAGE 5] Multivariate Kelly:
\[
G(\ell)=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\log\!\left(1+o_i\ell_i-\sum_{j=1}^m\ell_j\right).
\]
[PAGE 6] Matrix form:
\[
\max\left\{
p'\log\left(1+W'\ell-\sum_{i=1}^m\ell_i1\right)
\right\}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_{i=1}^m\ell_i\le1,\quad \ell\ge0.
\]
[PAGE 6] Simultaneous version uses
\[
p_i=P[\varrho(\omega_i)]=\prod_k P[\varrho_k(\omega_{i,k})]
\]
under independence, with \(M=\prod_k m_k\) events and \(N=\prod_k m_k\) outcome combinations [PAGE 7].

Assumptions and exact breakdown regimes:
- \(\sum_i\ell_i\to1^-\) drives the log argument toward zero for outcomes with \(\ell_i\to0\), so \(G(\ell)\to-\infty\); the paper states \(G(\ell)\) “tends towards negative infinity as \(\sum_i\ell_i\) approaches 1” [PAGE 13].
- \(p=\tilde p\) gives \(\ell^*=0\) and \(G=0\) [PAGE 5].
- \(\ell'\Sigma\ell\to0^+\) with \(\ell'\mu-R_f>0\) gives \(S(\ell)\to+\infty\) [PAGE 5].
- \(tt<0\) gives arbitrage with zero variance and \(R(\ell_A)=1/(1+tt)>1\) [PAGE 2, 5].
- The SQP routine failed at matchweek 23 with gradient overflow [PAGE 10]; this is a numerical singularity near the boundary and near log(0).
- The empirical entropy is close to the three-outcome maximum \(\ln3=1.0986\), with empirical 1.0631 and model 1.0318 [PAGE 14], so the probability layer is information-poor.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific genealogy runs from Bernoulli’s logarithmic utility argument [PAGE 4, 18] to von Neumann–Morgenstern expected utility [PAGE 3, 18], Markowitz mean-variance portfolio selection [PAGE 4, 18], Kelly’s log-growth criterion [PAGE 5, 18], Breiman’s asymptotic dominance result [PAGE 6, 18], and Thorp’s analysis of favorable games and the critical threshold \(\ell_c>\ell^*\) [PAGE 5–6, 18]. The modern computational branch adds Smoczynski’s explicit-allocation work [PAGE 6, 18], Busseti–Ryu–Boyd risk-constrained Kelly [PAGE 6, 18], and numerical optimization via SQP [PAGE 6, 18]. The football-specific branch uses Dixon–Coles temporal decay [PAGE 8, 18], Surowiecki’s crowd-wisdom argument for market-implied probabilities [PAGE 8, 18], lasso variable selection [PAGE 8, 18], and neural-network regularization [PAGE 9, 18].

The multi-week traps are baked into the temporal design. The paper excludes the first week of each season because of “ongoing team rebuilding during the summer transfer market” [PAGE 8], aggregates temporal variables with exponential decay \(\xi=0.1\) following Dixon–Coles [PAGE 8], and uses 2,660 games split into training, validation, and test [PAGE 8]. The test period is the second half of 2020/2021 [PAGE 1, 7]. The first trap is regime shift: promoted teams, transfers, and managerial changes can break stationarity across matchweeks. The second trap is validation leakage: hyperparameters and the \(17\%\) fraction are selected on validation data [PAGE 9–10], so the test result is conditional on that selection. The third trap is optimizer nonstationarity: matchweek 23 did not converge [PAGE 10], and the fallback last iterate is not the optimum. The fourth trap is ruin asymmetry: the Sharpe route lost all funds two weeks before the end [PAGE 13], while the Kelly route underbet by design because “under-betting is preferable to over-betting” [PAGE 7, 10]. The fifth trap is fractionalization order: log-growth is not invariant to fractional bets, so pre-optimization and post-optimization fractions are different strategies [PAGE 13]. These are not minor implementation details; they are the genealogy of the reported 135.8% result.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when the bettor treats the neural-network estimate \(p\) as ground truth. The paper’s own audit warns that the model’s cross-entropy is near the maximum-entropy ceiling [PAGE 14], and the top portfolio’s p-values are 0.21 and 0.16 [PAGE 13]. A crisis habit would respond to early losses by increasing stake or narrowing to high-confidence events to “recover.” That is exactly the overbetting regime: too much budget pushes \(\sum_i\ell_i\) toward 1, and \(G(\ell)\to-\infty\) [PAGE 13]. The paper’s stated preference is the opposite: “under-betting is preferable to over-betting” [PAGE 7, 10], and the \(17\%\) fraction is chosen from 200 Dirichlet simulations [PAGE 10].

The counter-trap is over-restriction. Restricted strategies converge faster and sometimes show higher returns, but they have higher variance and lower diversification [PAGE 13]. They shrink the event set to the highest expected-value outcome per match, which compromises the criteria’s fundamental properties [PAGE 13]. The Sharpe counter-trap is especially sharp: it can allocate the entire budget and produce zero wealth [PAGE 13]. The Kelly counter-trap is fractionalization order: because log-growth is not invariant to fractional bets, a pre-optimization fraction is not the same as \(f\ell\) after optimization [PAGE 13]. A recursive bettor must model both the market and the optimizer: the market-implied probabilities already encode crowd information [PAGE 8], and the optimizer’s boundary behavior encodes numerical fragility [PAGE 10].

The clock is the matchweek. The paper uses 20 matchweeks in the test set, 10 matches per matchweek, 30 possible bets per matchweek, and \(3^{10}=59,049\) outcome combinations [PAGE 10]. Gains are reinvested each matchweek, \(W_n=\prod_{J=19}^{38}R_i(\ell)\) [PAGE 10]. The clock creates nonstationarity: early-season rebuilding is excluded [PAGE 8], decay \(\xi=0.1\) discounts old data [PAGE 8], and the matchweek-23 nonconvergence is a clock-specific failure [PAGE 10]. A recursive theory of mind must therefore distinguish three clocks: the historical decay clock, the validation/test clock, and the in-season crisis clock. If the bettor updates only on the crisis clock, habit regression dominates; if the bettor updates only on the historical clock, staleness dominates; if the bettor updates only on the validation clock, overfitting dominates. The paper’s design is an attempt to separate them, but its residual p-values and nonconvergence show the separation is incomplete.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather layers can veto the mathematical optimum because the paper’s theorems assume known \(p\), fixed payout coefficients, and independence [PAGE 2, 7]. The main database variables are team aggregates: position, points, \(npxGD\) moving average and variance, big-six and promoted flags, EA Sports overall/attack/midfield/defense ratings, transfer budget, international prestige, and squad average age [PAGE 17]. Understats adds \(xG\), \(xGA\), \(npxG\), \(npxGA\), \(npxGD\), deep completions, and PPDA-type pressing measures [PAGE 16]. There is no player-level load, injury, sprint-debt, travel, or weather variable in the main database [PAGE 17]. The paper admits “a deficiency in the number of variables available to accurately determine match outcomes, particularly at the individual player level” [PAGE 14].

Formal veto predicate. Let \(P(Y\mid X)\) be the true outcome law and \(Q_\theta(Y\mid X)\) the model. Let \(Z\) denote omitted biomechanical variables (player load, injury status, minutes congestion) and \(W\) denote omitted weather variables (wind, precipitation, heat, pitch condition). If \(Y\not\perp(Z,W)\mid X\), then \(P\ne Q_\theta\) and the KL bound \(G^*=D_{KL}(P||\tilde P)\) is not attainable by a strategy optimized under \(Q_\theta\). The physical veto is
\[
V_{\mathrm{phys}}=1 \quad\text{if}\quad \exists Z:\; P(Y\mid X,Z)\ne P(Y\mid X)
\]
or if the admissible mechanical constraints fail:
\[
V_{\mathrm{mech}}=1 \quad\text{if}\quad \sum_i\ell_i\ge1\ \text{or}\ \ell_i<0\ \text{or}\ \ell'\Sigma\ell=0\ \text{with}\ \ell'\mu-R_f>0.
\]
The weather veto is
\[
V_{\mathrm{met}}=1 \quad\text{if}\quad \exists W:\; D_{KL}\bigl(P(Y\mid X,W)||P(Y\mid X)\bigr)>\epsilon
\]
for a predeclared tolerance \(\epsilon\). If \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}\lor V_{\mathrm{met}}\lor V_{\mathrm{mech}}=1\), the Kelly/Sharpe optimality proof does not transport to the realized match. This is not speculative: the paper’s own entropy audit shows the probability layer is near maximum entropy [PAGE 14], and its first-week exclusion exists precisely because team rebuilding creates a physical/regime break [PAGE 8]. A biomechanical veto also applies to the “simultaneous independence” assumption [PAGE 7]: shared weather, referee assignment, travel, and congestion induce correlation, which changes \(\Sigma\) and the simultaneous Kelly product \(\prod_k P[\varrho_k(\omega_{i,k})]\). The proof obligation is therefore not “is the model accurate?” but “are the omitted physical and weather variables conditionally irrelevant?” The paper does not establish that.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
