Let the latent cognitive state be C_t ∈ 𝒞, comprising expectancy E_t, purpose P_t, and cognitive map M_t. Let the sign stimulus be S_t ∈ 𝒮 (Tolman’s term for the environmental cue that initiates purposeful action).

Forward (generative) operator:
B_t ∼ 𝒢(C_t, S_t; θ), with 𝒢 : 𝒞 × 𝒮 → Δ(𝒜).
Here θ are nuisance parameters capturing apparatus and biomechanical factors. Units: probabilities are dimensionless; time is measured in seconds; behavioral rates in m/s or choices per trial.

Inverse (Tolmanian) inference operator:
p(C_{1:T} | B_{1:T}, S_{1:T}) ∝ p(B_{1:T} | C_{1:T}, S_{1:T}) · p(C_{1:T}).
This is the formalization of “inferring … cognitions … from clearly defined behavioral data” [PAGE 1].

Information-theoretic test for cognitive measurability:
ℐ_t = I(C_t ; B_t | S_t) = Σ_{c∈𝒞} Σ_{b∈𝒜} p(c,b|s) log₂ [ p(c,b|s) / (p(c|s) p(b|s)) ] bits.
If ℐ_t > 0 above the measurement-noise floor, behavioral data carry information about cognition. ℐ is measured in bits.

Expectancy operator:
𝔼[O_{t+1} | S_t, H_t] = Σ_{o} o · p(o | S_t, H_t),
where H_t is the habit hierarchy and O_{t+1} is the anticipated outcome.

Assumptions: (A1) Markovian cognitive dynamics conditional on past experience; (A2) experimental control over S_t; (A3) sufficient sampling N_trials ≥ dim(𝒞).

Singularity / breakdown regimes: The cognitive state is locally unidentifiable when rank(J_𝒢) < dim(𝒞), where J_𝒢 = ∂𝒢/∂C is the Jacobian of the behavioral likelihood with respect to cognition. Exact failure occurs if p(b|c,s) = p(b|c’,s) for all b and some c ≠ c’, or when N_trials < dim(𝒞). In that regime, any cognitive interpretation is observationally equivalent to a pure muscle-twitch or habit model, and the inference collapses.

Verbatim anchors from [PAGE 1]: “invented an experimental method for inferring and measuring cognitions, expectations, insights and purposes in both animals and men from clearly defined behavioral data”; “give back to psychology its mind—its insights, its cognitions, its purposes”; “No longer were most psychologists brow beaten, by the threat of being damned as unscientific, into asserting that people were merely untidy and unnecessarly complex versions of the ‘molecular theoretician’s oversimplified model of a rat, dog, or even a pigeon’.”

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. Watsonian behaviorism (1913) excluded mental states as legitimate scientific objects; Tolman (1932) reintroduced them as latent variables inferred from behavior [PAGE 1]. The page documents the institutional permission granted after the Second World War for terms like “beliefs,” “cognitive dissonance,” “strategies,” and “plans” to enter even the most respectable journals. This lineage runs from purposive behaviorism through cognitive dissonance theory, the cognitive-map literature (O’Keefe & Nadel), and into modern reinforcement-learning models of expectancy.

Multi-week traps. In a longitudinal maze experiment spanning weeks, C_t is non-stationary: each trial updates the animal’s internal model. Interpreting early-week behavior with a late-week fitted cognitive model is retrocausal—later outcomes rewrite the meaning of earlier choices. Trap 1: assuming stationarity of C across weeks when the animal’s map is still consolidating. Trap 2: mistaking an extinction curve for a fixed habit. Trap 3: confounding the experimenter’s evolving expectations with the rat’s expectancy across sessions. Because [PAGE 1] notes the post-war opening to cognitive language, these traps became visible only after psychology licensed the very mental terms that make retrocausal reinterpretation possible.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The experimenter builds a model of the rat’s model of the environment—a second-order theory of mind. Crisis habit regression: when data are ambiguous, the researcher reverts to S-R habit language (“muscle twitch”) because it is computationally cheap and institutionally safe. Counter-trap: over-inferring insight or purpose from a single idiosyncratic choice, thereby importing mentalism through the back door. Clock: the discrete trial index t = 1 … T imposes a sampling grid. If the true cognitive timescale τ_C is much larger than the inter-trial interval Δt, then C appears block-constant but drifts across blocks; misalignment between the experimental clock and the cognitive clock generates pseudo-hysteresis and false “expectancy” effects. Formal condition: if τ_C / Δt > N_blocks, block-wise stationarity is violated and trial-level cognitive attribution is invalid.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Layers: L₁ physical apparatus (maze geometry, walls); L₂ biomechanics (turning radius, maximum running speed v_max, limb fatigue); L₃ environmental analogs of weather (temperature, illumination, odor currents); L₄ cognition (expectancy, purpose).

Veto rule: a lower layer L_v vetoes the cognitive explanation if p(B | do(L_v = l), C = c) = p(B | do(L_v = l)) for all c. Example: a wall blocks the goal arm; then no cognitive purpose to reach the goal can be expressed behaviorally regardless of C (physical veto). Example: if the rat is already running at v_max, latency differences cannot reflect cognitive expectancy (biomechanical veto). Example: temperature-induced changes in baseline locomotion mimic purposefulness without any change in C (environmental veto). Adjudication: retain the cognitive layer only when I(C ; B | L₁,L₂,L₃, S) > ε_bits, with ε set above the noise floor estimated from control conditions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Blocked goal path. Manipulation: after stable training, physically block the previously rewarded goal arm. Prediction under the cognitive-map/expectancy hypothesis: the rat initially directs behavior toward the blocked location (nonzero expectancy) and then exhibits vicarious trial-and-error; under a pure habit model, the rat repeats the same motor sequence regardless of outcome devaluation. Verifiable statistic: f_blocked = P(choose blocked arm | blocked). Formal veto: reject habit-only if f_blocked > 0.5 immediately post-manipulation while f_blocked ≈ 0 pre-manipulation (binomial, p < 0.01). Prediction equation: f_blocked(t) ∝ exp(−t/τ_ext) after the first violation.

Scenario 2 — Latent extinction. Manipulation: remove food reward but retain start-box cues and maze layout. Prediction: an expectancy violation signal δ_t = O_t − 𝔼[O_t] drives decay in continuation probability p_cont(t_ext) = exp(−λ t_ext). Verifiable: fit λ from choice persistence across extinction trials. Veto: if λ ≈ 0 (no decay) despite reward removal, the expectancy model is falsified; behavior is not guided by outcome expectation.

Scenario 3 — Sensory conflict (dark vs. light). Manipulation: run the same maze in darkness after light training. Prediction: if behavior depends on a visual cognitive map driven by sign stimuli S_t, choice accuracy A drops to chance in darkness: A_dark ≈ 1/|𝒜|. Veto: if A_dark ≈ A_light ≫ chance, the behavior is not mediated by a visual cognitive map (could be idiothetic or purely habitual), and the cognitive-map layer is vetoed for this modality.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
