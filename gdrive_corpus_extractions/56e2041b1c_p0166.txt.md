$$ m \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = - \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 C_d A \hat{\mathbf{v}} + m \mathbf{g} + \mathbf{F}_{mg} $$
Where:
- $m$: mass of the ball (SI: kg).
- $\rho$: air density (kg/m³).
- $C_d$: drag coefficient (dimensionless, approx. 0.25 for a spinning prolate spheroid).
- $A$: cross-sectional area (m²).
- $\mathbf{F}_{mg}$: Magnus force, dependent on spin rate $\omega$ and angle of attack. For a perfect spiral [PAGE 166], the angle is near zero, minimizing lift/drift, but any deviation amplifies $\mathbf{F}_{mg}$.

$$ |\mathbf{r}_b(t^*) - \mathbf{r}_r(t^*)| \le \delta_{catch} $$
where $\delta_{catch}$ is the reachable radius. The "slight check" implies the receiver's velocity magnitude $|\mathbf{v}_r(t)|$ is modulated to satisfy $\mathbf{v}_r(t^*) \approx \mathbf{v}_b(t^*)$, minimizing the relative closing speed and reducing the kinetic energy transferred during impact, thereby preventing a fumble.
