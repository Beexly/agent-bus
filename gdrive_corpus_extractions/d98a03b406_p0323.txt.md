\]
measured in currency units (USD). Under \(N\) independent and identically distributed exposures \(L_i\), the law of large numbers gives
\[
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N L_i \xrightarrow{P\text{-a.s.}} \mathbb{E}_P[L], \qquad

\[
\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}} \inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[u(W(a,\omega))],
\]
where \(u\) is a von Neumann–Morgenstern utility function (utils) and \(W\) is terminal wealth (USD). The breakdown/singularity regime is: if \(\mathcal{P}\) is not a singleton and no grouping principle exists, \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_P[\cdot]\) is not a linear pricing functional; hence no actuarially fair premium can be defined, and any “price” for bearing the exposure is a residual claim.
