The counter-trap is therefore a race over $t_{dead}$: offense snaps quickly or varies the “quick or continuous” motion [PAGE 1] to disrupt defensive timing; defense attacks the center at $t_{place}$ to force an immediate dead ball or loose ball. In crisis, the team that controls the recursive layer—offense anticipating defense’s center-alert, defense anticipating offense’s snap-mode variation—wins the temporal veto over whether the ball crosses the goal line before the whistle.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The goal line $\mathcal{G}$ is a mathematical plane; safety requires the ball to pass behind it ($b(t) \prec \mathcal{G}$), not merely touch it. A ball exactly on the line is not behind it, so $\mathbb{1}_{\text{safety}} = 0$ unless another clause applies. The side line extended behind the goal line ($\mathcal{S}_{ext}$) is a ray; intersection at its origin is a codimension-two geometric ambiguity that only the referee can resolve [PAGE 1].

Biomechanical layer. The snap requires hand or foot contact with a stationary ball on the ground, executed with “quick or continuous motion” [PAGE 1]. Human motor execution has a nonzero minimum movement time $\tau_{\min} > 0$ [s] due to neuromuscular activation and limb dynamics; the limit $\Delta t_{\text{snap}} \to 0$ is physically singular and illegal under Rule 6(a) [PAGE 1]. Grip coefficient $\mu$ between hand/foot and ball governs snap continuity; a wet or muddy ball reduces $\mu$, increasing the probability of fumble and the possession discontinuity $\dot{P}$ undefined [PAGE 1].

Weather layer. Wind velocity $v_w$ [m/s] advects a kicked ball; a crosswind can carry a punt from behind the goal line across $\mathcal{S}_{ext}$ unintentionally, triggering the kicking safety clause [PAGE 1]. Rain or freezing alters ground compliance, violating the precondition that the holder “places it upon the ground” stably [PAGE 1]; if placement is impossible, Rule 6(a) cannot be satisfied and no legal scrimmage can initiate except under a specific alternative provision.

Veto proof. A play is vetoed if any layer fails: (i) physical—ball does not break the plane of $\mathcal{G}$; (ii) biomechanical—snap duration violates $\Delta t_{\text{snap}} \ge \tau_{\min}$ or ball placement fails; (iii) weather—wind or ground state makes the required trajectory or placement physically unrealizable. Because Rule 6(a) makes scrimmage the default restart [PAGE 1], failure of placement is not a minor delay but a structural veto: until the ball can be placed on the ground, the scrimmage does not exist, and control reverts to whatever specific provision the rules authorize for that contingency.
