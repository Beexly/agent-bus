<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes what supervised spotters do under distribution shift: trained on broadcast priors (centre crop, professional court context, multi-camera ball visibility), they regress to those priors when the viewing geometry changes and emit almost nothing [PAGE 8, Result 1]. E2E-Spot makes no confusion and no spurious prediction on phone/court-level footage; its errors are all misses, a behavioral signature of a detector that recognizes nothing outside its training distribution rather than a detector that misclassifies [PAGE 8]. T-DEED scores exactly 0.000 on all three phone/court-level clips [PAGE 8, Table 3]. This is habit regression under crisis: the model's internal policy, optimized for broadcast appearance, defaults to silence when the broadcast context vanishes.

The counter-trap is RallyArb's arbitration layer, which deliberately refuses to learn broadcast appearance at all. It fits no parameter on event labels and decides legality from physical constraints: alternation (hit–bounce–hit–bounce), floor evidence (vertical velocity flip from descending to ascending with horizontal direction preserved), and wrist–ball proximity normalized by player-box height [PAGE 6–7, Appendix C]. This keeps bounce F1 above 0.8 on all phone/court-level clips including held-out grass-am [PAGE 8, Result 3]. But the counter-trap over-corrects in the temporal domain: hit hypotheses are anchored on wrist-speed peaks, which are not contact frames, so RallyArb's hits lag or lead contact by 0.105 seconds on average [PAGE 9]. It wins on bounce existence but loses on hit timing precision, showing that a rule-based counter-trap trades appearance sensitivity for temporal bias.

The clock couples all of this to frame rate and sampling geometry. Tolerance τ = round(0.15 ν) scales with capture rate: at 25 fps, τ = 4 frames (~160 ms); at 60 fps, τ = 9 frames (~150 ms) [PAGE 5]. The vision-language models sample at about 12 fps, an ≈83 ms interval that hard-bounds how close they can place an event [PAGE 8, Result 2]; Gemini bounce F1 falls from 0.406 to 0.195 at 0.04 s tolerance, partly a protocol artifact [PAGE 8]. The supervised spotters run on every second frame of 50/60 fps clips, which caps their exact-frame recall on those sources [PAGE 11, Figure 4 caption]. Annotation itself is clock-limited: second-annotator agreement is 0.777/0.804 at the exact frame but 0.990/0.957 at 0.04 s [PAGE 10, Figure 4 caption], meaning human labels also disagree below roughly 40 ms. The system rankings therefore depend on which clock—capture, sampling, or annotation—one privileges.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. RallyArb reads a ball track with per-frame validity mask plus a spline-completed copy used for lookup only; all directional evidence is read on real detections, never the spline, and any unreadable measurement abstains [PAGE 14]. Floor evidence is defined as a vertical flip from descending to ascending with horizontal direction preserved [PAGE 14]. These are physical vetoes: a cluster with clear floor evidence and no swing/redirect cannot be labeled a hit, and hit evidence removes the bounce hypothesis before scoring [PAGE 14]. Mandatory bounce anchors and hit anchors cannot be skipped in the Viterbi decoder [PAGE 14].

Biomechanical layer. Contact evidence is the ball-to-nearest-confident-wrist distance divided by player box height h_box [PAGE 14]. Bands are 0.5 at the wrist and 1.2 for clear separation; measured at the ball to avoid circularity with the wrist-speed hit detector [PAGE 14]. Real development contacts lie at d_contact ≤ 0.51 and phantom swing anchors at d_contact ≥ 0.90 [PAGE 14]. Hit-like clusters require a corroborated swing, ball at wrist with incoming ball, or confident trajectory redirect, plus absence of floor evidence [PAGE 14]. The wrist-speed hit detector uses full-width-at-half-maximum gating and per-side amplitude floors scaled by median player-box height [PAGE 14].

Surface/weather layer. The corpus spans clay, grass, and hard courts [PAGE 4, Table 1], but the paper models neither weather nor atmospheric drag; bounce physics enters only implicitly through slope-restitution (≤2.5) and energy-ratio (≤1.8) tests in the bounce detector [PAGE 14]. No veto depends on temperature, humidity, or wind, so cross-weather generalization is untested by design.

Breakdown regimes. The physical veto fails when geometry removes the ball from view: on felix the ball lies outside the 224 px centre crop at 15 of 32 tracked rally events, 6 of 21 on hard-pro, and 3 of 18 on grass-am [PAGE 11]. At low angles the far bounce and far hit fall within a few pixels of each other near the horizon [PAGE 4, Figure 1], so hard-pro and grass-am are scored near-half only because far-half events cannot be verified even by humans [PAGE 4]. On the grazing phone angle of felix, fine-tuned E2E-Spot scores bounce F1 0.200 versus RallyArb's 0.828 [PAGE 10, Result 5; Table A], demonstrating that when the ball is geometrically occluded or foreshortened, neither learned priors nor physical arbitration fully recover the event stream.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each checkable against released replay fixtures, frozen predictions, and the standard-library evaluator.

Veto 1 (centre-crop removal on E2E-Spot). Premise: published E2E-Spot uses a fixed 224 px centre crop, and the ball is outside that crop at 15/32 felix rally events, 6/21 hard-pro, 3/18 grass-am [PAGE 11]. Counterfactual: rerun E2E-Spot with full-frame input (no crop) on felix. Verifiable prediction: bounce F1 on felix must rise above the published 0.000 because the failures are geometric occlusions, not absent events. Upper bound is set by RallyArb's 0.828 on felix [PAGE 8, Table A]. Any result below 0.000 or above 0.828 falsifies the intervention or the reported occlusion counts.

Veto 2 (tolerance compression to exact frame). Premise: spotters execute on every second frame of 50/60 fps clips, capping exact-frame recall near 0.5 on those sources [PAGE 11]; Gemini bounce F1 falls 0.406 → 0.195 at 0.04 s [PAGE 8]. Counterfactual: score all test rows at τ = 0 frames (exact-contact match). Verifiable prediction: published E2E-Spot test bounce F1 cannot exceed ≈0.5 on felix/hard-pro/grass-am and should collapse toward zero as the tolerance sweep in Figure 4 approaches 0 [PAGE 10]. If E2E-Spot retains >0.6 bounce F1 at τ = 0 on any phone/court-level clip, the reported frame-subsampling or annotator frame offsets are misstated.

Veto 3 (contact-distance band tightening on RallyArb hits). Premise: RallyArb hit anchors accept contact distance up to 1.2 box heights for "clear separation," while real development contacts are ≤0.51 and phantom swings ≥0.90 [PAGE 14]; RallyArb places hits 0.105 s off contact on average [PAGE 9]. Counterfactual: retune the hit-anchor admission rule to require d_contact < 0.55 (tightening the 1.2 band) and re-score grass-am. Verifiable prediction: RallyArb grass-am hit F1 falls below the reported 0.556, because the current 0.556 relies on loose wrist-speed anchoring rather than contact-frame alignment [PAGE 8, Table A]. If hit F1 remains at or above 0.600 (Gemini's grass-am level) under the tightened band, the wrist-vs-ball measurement or the 0.105 s timing error is inconsistent with the released fixtures.
