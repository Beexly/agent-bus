  \[
  s,rP = D^{n} \cdot (s,\mathcal{H}_{r})^{1/n} + \ldots

  Typically simplified to \(s,rP = V \cdot D\), with \(V = (s,\mathcal{H}_{r})^{1/n}\) and \(n\) often taken as 1. Units: \(D\) is dimensionless (0 to 1), \(V\) in volts or arbitrary potential units.
- **Habit Strength Acquisition** (Postulate VI):  
  \[
  \Delta(s,\mathcal{H}_{r}) = K \cdot D^{t} \cdot S - (s,\mathcal{H}_{r})
  \]
  where \(K\) is capacity to learn, \(S\) is stimulus intensity dynamism, and \(t\) is exponent. Asymptotic habit strength depends on \(K\), \(D\), and \(S\).
- **Stimulus Intensity Dynamics**: Reaction potential varies monotonically with stimulus intensity (Postulate O). Formalized as:  
  \[
  s,rP \propto f(I)

Breakdown regimes: When \(D=0\), \(s,rP=0\); when \(s,\mathcal{H}_{r}=0\), no learning; if inhibition equals excitation, no response. Singularity at perfect learning (habit=1) or zero drive.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Hull's system emerged from early 20th-century behaviorism, reacting against mentalism and seeking predictive rigor. Key influences: Pavlov (conditioning), Thorndike (law of effect), Watson (behaviorism), and neobehaviorists like Tolman (cognitive maps). Hull integrated physiology (drive reduction akin to homeostasis) with logic (deductive structure). His work peaked in the 1940s-50s, dominating American psychology until cognitive revolution (1960s) challenged its completeness. Multi-week traps: Overemphasis on quantification led to neglect of unmeasurable constructs (insight, language). The system's complexity became a trap: endless auxiliary hypotheses (oscillation, conative arousal) saved anomalies but reduced falsifiability. Historically, it paved way for operant conditioning (Skinner) and information processing models.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Counter-traps involve introducing novel stimuli to break habitual patterns, leveraging generalization gradients. The "clock" refers to timing of reinforcement: optimal intervals maximize \(K\) and minimize interference. Habit regression is mitigated by varied practice, which strengthens multiple response pathways, preventing over-reliance on one habit.
