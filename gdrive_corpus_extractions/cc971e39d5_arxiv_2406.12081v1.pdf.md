<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators, units, assumptions, and breakdown regimes:

1. Feature-state exponential moving average. For track \(i\) at frame \(k\), the paper defines
\[
v_i^k=\alpha v_i^{k-1}+(1-\alpha)f_i^k,
\]
where \(v_i^k\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\) is the track embedding, \(f_i^k\in\mathbb{R}^{D}\) is the matched detection embedding, and \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) is dimensionless. The embedding coordinates are dimensionless normalized appearance features; \(D\) is a dimensionless count of feature channels. The paper states that “The feature vector used for association is updated whenever a new detection is matched to that track” [PAGE 3 of 6]. Assumptions: matched detections are correct enough that EMA does not collapse two identities; \(f_i^k\) is comparable across frames; \(\alpha\) is fixed or controlled. Breakdown: if \(\alpha\notin[0,1]\), the update is not convex and can amplify feature drift; if embeddings are not normalized, cosine distance becomes scale-sensitive; under occlusion, the paper itself notes that “the appearance of a player is affected by the player they are occluded by or occluding” [PAGE 5 of 6], so \(f_i^k\) can represent occluder texture rather than identity.

2. EIoU distance. The paper defines
\[
d_{1,j,k}=1-\mathrm{EIoU}(j,k),
\]
where \(\mathrm{EIoU}(j,k)\in[0,1]\) is the expanded intersection-over-union between track \(j\) and detection \(k\), and \(d_{1,j,k}\in[0,1]\) is dimensionless [PAGE 3 of 6]. Assumptions: expanded boxes are meaningful; expansion compensates for irregular motion and camera motion. Breakdown: \(\mathrm{EIoU}=1\) gives \(d_1=0\); if expansion scale \(e\) is too large relative to inter-player spacing, expanded boxes can overlap neighboring players and EIoU loses discriminative power. The paper’s expansion schedule is stated as \(e\to e+a\) with \(a &lt; e\) [PAGE 2 of 6], but implementation says “initial expansion of 0.3, and increase it by 0.3” [PAGE 4 of 6], so the exact schedule is internally inconsistent.

3. Cosine distance. The paper defines
\[
d_{2,j,k}=1-\frac{j\cdot k}{\|j\|_2\|k\|_2},
\]
where \(j,k\) are embedding vectors; \(d_{2,j,k}\in[0,2]\) is dimensionless when both vectors are nonzero and normalized [PAGE 3 of 6]. Assumptions: \(\|j\|_2\neq0\), \(\|k\|_2\neq0\), and numerical cosine similarity remains in \([-1,1]\). Breakdown: zero-norm vectors make the denominator zero; floating-point cosine \(>1\) yields negative \(d_2\), which is outside the intended distance range; identical embeddings give \(d_2=0\). The paper warns that “In situations where two players from the same team come close to each other, their embedding vectors can become quite similar” [PAGE 3 of 6], so low \(d_2\) can be ambiguous rather than identity-specific.

4. Harmonic-mean fusion. The paper defines
\[
H(j,k)=\frac{2}{\frac{1}{d_{1,j,k}}+\frac{1}{d_{2,j,k}}}
=\frac{2d_{1,j,k}d_{2,j,k}}{d_{1,j,k}+d_{2,j,k}},
\]
which is dimensionless [PAGE 3 of 6]. The paper says the harmonic mean “emphasizes the smaller of two values, thereby providing an average that skews towards the lower of the two measurements” [PAGE 3 of 6]. Mathematically, for positive \(d_1,d_2\), \(H\) lies between the minimum and the arithmetic mean and is closer to the smaller value; it is not the minimum. Assumptions: \(d_1,d_2&gt;0\). Singularity and breakdown: the reciprocal form is undefined at \(d_1=0\) or \(d_2=0\); the continuous extension is \(H=0\) if one distance is zero and the other is positive, and \(H=0\) if both are zero. If either distance is negative, the formula can become negative or hit a pole when \(d_1=-d_2\); numerical implementations must clamp to \(\epsilon&gt;0\). If one cue is very bad and the other very good, \(H\) can still remain small, so a single strong cue can dominate the fused cost.

5. Assignment operator. The paper forms a cost matrix from \(H(j,k)\) and uses the Hungarian algorithm [PAGE 2 of 6][PAGE 3 of 6]. Formally,
\[
\pi^*=\arg\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\sum_j C_{j,\pi(j)},\qquad C_{j,k}=H(j,k),
\]
with thresholding: detection score threshold 0.4, high-score threshold 0.6, discard threshold 0.5; if the linear-assignment cost is greater than 0.8 it is set to 1; if the appearance-based cost is greater than 0.3 it is set to 1 [PAGE 4 of 6]. These are dimensionless score-like quantities, not calibrated probabilities. Breakdown: threshold caps can mask a bad cue by forcing cost 1; threshold coupling can make the Hungarian solution discontinuous; if all costs are capped, the assignment becomes degenerate.

6. Tracklet-retention operator. The paper states that sports occur in a “closed environment” and that it preserves “all tracklets indefinitely” [PAGE 2 of 6][PAGE 4 of 6]. Let \(B_t\) be the tracklet buffer at frame \(t\); the unbounded rule is \(B_t=B_{t-1}\cup\{T_i\text{ active or lost}\}\), with no cap. Units: \(B_t\) is a set of track states, so its cardinality is dimensionless. Breakdown: memory and association search grow with the number of retained IDs; false positives can be retained and later re-matched; the closed-world assumption fails if players leave the league, camera, or dataset boundary; re-ID false positives can increase with long absence.
