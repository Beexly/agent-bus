Let \( H(t) \) denote habit strength at time \( t \) during extinction or acquisition. The fractional rate of change \( F \) is defined as:

F = \frac{dH/H}{dt} = \frac{1}{H} \frac{dH}{dt}

**Breakdown Regime:** When \( H \rightarrow 0 \), the fractional rate becomes undefined; Hull likely assumes a linearized regime near non-zero baseline. Singularity occurs at perfect extinction (\( H=0 \)), where further change is impossible.

**2. Stimulus Generalization Function:**  
For a conditioned stimulus \( S_0 \) with habit strength \( H_0 \), the generalized strength at distance \( d \) (in some perceptual metric) follows a decreasing function \( G(d) \):  
\[
H(S) = H_0 \cdot G(d), \quad \text{where } G(0)=1, \ G(d) \searrow 0 \text{ as } d \nearrow \infty.
\]  
Typically, \( G(d) \) is modeled as Gaussian or exponential decay. Extinction applied at \( S_0 \) reduces \( H_0 \), and the effect propagates via the same \( G(d) \), so new strength \( H'(S) = (H_0 - \Delta H) G(d) \).

**3. Units and Dimensions:**

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Hull’s treatment emerges from a specific historical trajectory: the behaviorist reaction against introspection, coupled with the rise of mathematical psychology. His use of “fractional increment” echoes Newton’s fluxions and thermodynamic efficiency ratios, aiming to ground psychology in physics-like laws. The genealogy includes:  
- **Pavlov (1927):** Demonstrated external inhibition and spontaneous recovery, hinting that extinction is not erasure.  
- **Guthrie (1952):** Later proposed “new learning” theory, directly challenging Hull’s associative decrement view.  
- **Kamin (1969) & Rescorla-Wagner (1972):** Formalized prediction error, providing a neurocomputational basis for why extinction decays faster (salience of absence vs. presence).  

**Multi-week traps:**  
1. **Erasure Fallacy:** Assuming extinction deletes the memory trace, leading to failed exposure therapies when renewal occurs after weeks.  
2. **Generalization Overreach:** Believing one extinction session covers all variants, ignoring that gradients shift under stress (e.g., anxiety narrows attentional focus, reducing effective generalization).  
3. **Visceral-Motor Split Blindness:** Treating all responses as uniform, missing that motor habits (e.g., drug-seeking) may persist even when emotional (visceral) arousal is extinguished, causing covert relapse.  

The retrocausal insight: Hull’s quantitative asymmetry (F_ext >> F_acq) presaged the discovery of synaptic depotentiation (LTP reversal) being faster than LTP induction, a principle now central to optogenetic erasure studies.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The extinction memory, stored with narrower generalization (due to specific contextual cues), fails to generalize to the novel threat variant.  

**Counter-trap:** Design extinction protocols that explicitly vary stimulus parameters to broaden the extinction gradient, using spaced retrieval to strengthen lateral connections.  

**Clock mechanism:** The “clock” is the half-life of extinction retrieval under stress; without periodic booster sessions, the extinction memory decays exponentially (as per F ≈ -½2), while the original habit, though weakened, may retain a slow-decay reservoir (F ≈ -1/14 equivalent). Thus, maintenance requires timed reactivation aligned with the faster decay curve.  

Recursive self-model: The agent knows it has been exposed, knows extinction occurred, but under amygdala hijack, the visceral channel bypasses prefrontal extinction recall, causing a split-self experience where “I know I’m safe” conflicts with “my body is preparing to flee.”
