"Crisis habit regression" describes the tendency of an investigative habit, under pressure of null results, to revert to its baseline of discontinued inquiry. In Galton's terms, the experimenter meets no affirmative evidence in the first generation and is tempted to abandon the line [PAGE 197]. That abandonment is a reversion to the mean of scientific practice: the null is interpreted as falsification, and the habit regresses to the status quo of non-pursuit.

The counter-trap is the inference that F1 nullity implies F2 nullity. Because Galton locates the acquired-faculty signal among the grandchildren [PAGE 197], the rational counter-habit is to persist to F2 despite a null at F1. The counter-trap is therefore recursive: the theory predicts that the most obvious evidence (at F1) is the least likely to be present, so the observer must suppress the crisis habit of quitting and instead extend the observational clock.

The clock is generational depth. Under Operator 3, the expected acquired-faculty magnitude decays as \lambda_A^n A_0; with \lambda_A < 1 [PAGE 197], the signal is progressively diluted, and by n = 2 it may fall to "barely recognizable" levels. The clock must therefore be read in generations, not weeks: an experiment stopped at n = 1 is stopped at the wrong phase of the decay cycle. A recursive theory of mind for this domain holds that (a) the observer's crisis habit is to regress to abandonment after F1 nullity; (b) the counter-trap is to recognize that F1 nullity is predicted by the model and not diagnostic; and (c) the clock variable n determines whether the signal is above or below the detection threshold \delta_det.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers can veto the Galtonian inference drawn from [PAGE 197].

Physical layer (germ-plasm / Weismann barrier): If an acquired faculty is purely somatic and the germ-plasm barrier segregates hereditary material from somatic modification, then transmission to grandchildren is physically impossible absent a germline mechanism. Galton's acquired-faculty claim is therefore vetoed at the physical layer unless a transmission route (e.g., epigenetic germline mark) is demonstrated. Without such a route, \lambda_A = 0 by physical necessity, not by statistical regression.

Biomechanical layer: If the faculty in question is motor or biomechanical rather than cognitive, developmental constraints and motor-plasticity limits can veto expression. An acquired motor faculty that exceeds the biomechanical range of the descendant's musculoskeletal system cannot be expressed even if transmitted; this creates a truncation regime in which \mathbb{E}[A_2] is censored by the descendant's phenotype space, independently of Galtonian dilution.

Weather / shared-environment layer: Macroenvironmental variables (climate, nutrition, pathogen load) covary across generations and can mimic or mask inherited signal. If grandparents and grandchildren are reared under different weather regimes, the observed attenuation may be environmental confounding rather than biological regression. The veto applies when environmental variance is not partitioned: the apparent \lambda_A < 1 may be a spurious estimate induced by unmeasured shared-environment decay.

Adjudication: The inference from [PAGE 197] holds only when (i) a physical transmission route is plausible, (ii) the descendant's biomechanical phenotype space admits expression of the faculty, and (iii) environmental variance, including weather-related confounds, is controlled or modeled. Failure at any layer voids the statistical attenuation claim and substitutes a layer-specific null.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a prediction:

Scenario 1 — F1 expression without attenuation. Counterfactual: a well-powered experiment finds an acquired faculty expressed in children (F1) at magnitude comparable to the founder, with no statistically significant decline in grandchildren (F2). Veto: this falsifies Galton's dilution/regression claim [PAGE 197], implying \lambda_A \ge 1 or absence of regression to the mean. Prediction under veto: the ancestral-stock contrast between "two gifted members of a poor stock" and "two ordinary members of a gifted stock" [PAGE 197] should collapse, because if expression is undiluted at F1 and F2, ancestral stock history would not modulate the outcome beyond the measured personal faculty.

Scenario 2 — Dense ancestral contributions. Counterfactual: deep pedigree data show parental hidden property is a sum over hundreds of ancestors with approximately equal non-zero weights. Veto: this falsifies Galton's sparsity claim that hidden property is not made up of contributions from "all or even very many" ancestors [PAGE 197], i.e., it rejects \|w\|_0 \ll |\mathcal{A}(p)| in favor of the infinitesimal additive model. Prediction under veto: conditioning on measured personal faculties should fully account for offspring outcomes; the poor-stock versus gifted-stock distinction should disappear because ancestral contributions would be uniform and already absorbed by the personal-faculty statistic.

Scenario 3 — Cross-fostering extinction. Counterfactual: a controlled adoption / cross-fostering design removes shared environment across generations, and the acquired faculty disappears entirely in both F1 and F2 despite being present in the founder. Veto: this supports Galtonian regression to baseline (\lambda_A \to 0) and indicates the founder signal was environmental, not transmissible through the germline. Prediction under veto: reintroduction of the founder environment in F2 should not restore the faculty if the physical/germline veto holds; if restoration does occur, the physical veto is overturned and environmental inheritance, not Galtonian dilution, is implicated.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
