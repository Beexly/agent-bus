The supplied source is not a live event log, a sensor stream, or an adversarial contest record. It is Tolman’s 1932 monograph on molar behavior, purposive behaviorism, sign-gestalt expectations, demands, means-end fields, and delayed reaction. Therefore the responsible GSE/GSI move is not to invent a target event or a contest prediction. The responsible move is to convert Tolman’s verbal operators into an auditable inference protocol, identify where the source licenses claims, and mark where it does not.

The first multi-hop derivation is from molar behavior to docility to purpose and cognition. Tolman begins by rejecting the molecular reduction of behavior to “receptor-process, conductor-process, and effector-process per se” and argues for behavior as an emergent molar whole: “Behavior, as such, is a molar phenomenon as contrasted with the molecular phenomena which constitute its underlying physiology” [PAGE 21]. He then claims that molar behavior is not merely directional but docile: it persists through trial and error and selects the more efficient tries relative to an end. That docility is the objective definition of purpose: “wherever a response shows docility relative to some end” [PAGE 14]. Cognition is defined in parallel by the contingency of behavior on environmental features proving to be so-and-so: if the goal, route, or support is altered, the behavior disrupts. Thus Tolman can say, “Behavior as behavior, that is, as molar, is purposive and ts cognitive” [PAGE 12]. The OCR preserves the typo “ts”; the claim is that purpose and cognition are immanent, objective determinants, not introspective entities.

The second move is from cognition to expectation. Tolman’s expectations are forward-pointing and are released by present stimuli but verified by later commerce with supports. This yields the crucial distinction between stimuli and supports: “Behavior cannot go off in vacuo. It requires a complementary ‘supporting’ or ‘holding-up’” [PAGE 85]. The rat’s expectation is not just a response to a cue; it is a setting for discriminanda and manipulanda that must actually hold up. The third move is from expectation to sign-gestalt. A mnemonization is a total expectation in which the stimulus for one part of a previously enjoyed complex releases the whole complex: sign, signified means-end relation, and significate. Tolman says the inference-like case is when “The expectation of this particular significate and signified means-end-relation is due to present or past experiences of other related parts of the total means-end field” [PAGE 141]. The fourth move is from sign-gestalt to field: common final path, multiple tracks, mutual alternativeness, reverse ends, closure, and hierarchicalness.

The gray-zone audit concerns overreading. Tolman repeatedly warns that purpose and cognition are objective, not mentalistic; the glossary and preface explicitly disavow a theological or introspectionist reading. The source also contains OCR corruption and incomplete experimental coverage: force-resistance preferences are proposed but not experimentally settled [PAGE 108], and perception versus mnemonization is acknowledged as a matter of degree. Therefore any GSE/GSI adjudication must not treat “expectation” as conscious foreknowledge, nor “demand” as a direct readout of reward magnitude, nor “inference” as human logic. The only defensible units are behavioral probabilities, disruption frequencies, information measures, and physical support/cost variables.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Tolman’s text is verbal, so the following are formal reconstructions, not quotations.

Let a behavior-act be a measurable trajectory \(b:[0,T]\to\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\), with time in seconds and state/action coordinates dimensionless. Let \(d_t\) be the physiological drive state, \(g\) a goal-object, and \(A_g\) the set of goal-approaching actions. Define demand as a choice probability under matched alternatives:
\[
D_{g\mid d}=\Pr(a_t\in A_g\mid d_t,\mathcal{A}_t),\qquad [D]=1.
\]
Assumption: alternatives are matched in support and effort; drive is observed or controlled. Breakdown: if \(A_g\) is undefined, \(D\) is not identifiable; if \(D=0\) or \(D=1\), log-information terms diverge or become zero-variance.

Let \(E_t=P(O_{t+\Delta}\mid s_t,m_t,\mathcal{H}_t)\) be a sign-gestalt expectation over later outcome \(O_{t+\Delta}\), given sign \(s_t\), means-object \(m_t\), and history \(\mathcal H_t\). Define docility as mutual information:
\[
\mathcal{D}=I(E;O)=\sum_{e,o}P(e,o)\log_2\frac{P(e,o)}{P(e)P(o)},\qquad [\mathcal{D}]=\text{bits}.
\]
Define disruption \(\Delta_t=1\) if \(O_{t+\Delta}\notin\operatorname{supp}(E_t)\), else \(0\). Assumption: finite outcome set and stationary task. Breakdown: if \(P(e)=0\) or \(P(o)=0\), the logarithm is undefined; if \(\operatorname{Var}(O)=0\), \(I(E;O)=0\) and docility is unidentifiable. This formalizes Tolman’s claim that continued behavior is contingent on environmental facts “actually proving to be so and so” [PAGE 17].

Represent the means-end field as a directed graph \(\mathcal F=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E)\). Each edge \(e=(u,v)\) carries a cost vector
\[
c(e)=(\ell_e,\tau_e,w_e),\qquad [\ell]=\text{m},\;[\tau]=\text{s},\;[w]=\text{J}.
\]
Preference is vector dominance: \(e\preceq e'\) iff \(\ell_e\le\ell_{e'}\), \(\tau_e\le\tau_{e'}\), \(w_e\le w_{e'}\), with at least one strict inequality. Assumption: dimensions are measured or experimentally paired; no scalar “least effort” is assumed unless empirically established. Breakdown: if costs are nonstationary, if the vector order is incomplete, or if the support is absent, the field model is undefined. Tolman explicitly treats spatial, temporal, and “force resisting distances” as related but not automatically reducible [PAGE 108].

For inference over common final paths, let \(\Pi(s,g)\) be the set of paths from sign-state \(s\) to goal \(g\). Then
\[
P(g\mid s,\mathcal F)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(s,g)}P(\pi\mid s,\mathcal F).
\]
If a block at \(m\) dominates every path to \(g\), then a test block at \(m\) should shift the posterior to an alternative goal \(g'\). This is the formal content of Hsiao’s common-final-path result and Tolman and Honzik’s replication [PAGE 164–169]. Breakdown: the graph must be learned, and perception must not be confused with mnemonization; otherwise the inference is not identified.

For delayed reaction, let \(s_c\) be a condensed sign and \(\tau\) the imposed delay in seconds. Define
\[
P_{\text{delay}}(\tau)=\Pr(\text{correct}\mid \tau,s_c),\qquad [P_{\text{delay}}]=1.
\]
The leash interval is
\[
\tau_L=\sup\{\tau:P_{\text{delay}}(\tau)-P_{\text{chance}}>\epsilon\},\qquad [\tau_L]=\text{s},
\]
with dimensionless \(\epsilon\). Breakdown: if \(\tau>\tau_L\), or \(I(s_c;g)\to0\) bits, or gross bodily orientation substitutes for the condensed sign, the delayed-reaction interpretation collapses. Tolman’s summary is exact: “Delayed reaction learning means three things” [PAGE 162], namely single-exposure evocation, holding “in leash,” and functioning on a condensed sign.

Fixation is the negative operator:
\[
\Pr(a_t=a_f\mid s_t,\mathcal F_t)\to1\quad\text{while}\quad I(E_t;O_t)\to0.
\]
This is non-docile persistence. Tolman calls fixations “both the antithesis and the nemests of sign gestalt-expectations” [PAGE 154]. A cross-layer veto \(V=1\) applies if the support set is empty, if \(w_e>W_{\max}\) in joules beyond the organism’s actuator capacity, or if the sign-goal mutual information is zero bits. The source licenses the first two through its support and force-resistance discussions [PAGE 85, PAGE 108]; the weather-specific extension is not source-internal.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s lineage is a deliberate correction of both molecular behaviorism and mentalism. Watson supplies the stimulus-response vocabulary and the muscle-twitch temptation; Tolman answers with the molar definition. Perry supplies the decisive criterion of docility: purpose is not a hidden soul but a behavioral readiness to vary and then select the effective act. McDougall supplies a list of purposive characters, but Tolman rejects McDougall’s introspective “psychic” residue. Holt, de Laguna, Kantor, and Weiss supply arguments that integrated behavior has properties not reducible to isolated reflexes. Woodworth supplies “reaction tendency”; Lewin supplies valency and Topologie; Köhler supplies insight and Gestalt field language; Hunter supplies delayed reaction; Hsiao, Tolman, and Honzik supply common-final-path inference; Blodgett, Elliott, Tinklepaugh, and Williams supply reward substitution and sign-for-food effects.

The multi-week trap is to collapse these lineages into one modern term. Week 1 trap: reading “purpose” as conscious intention. Tolman explicitly says purposes and cognitions are objective and functional. Week 2 trap: reading “demand” as reward magnitude. Demand is drive-relative; the same food is not equally demanded under different hunger or thirst [PAGE 60–64]. Week 3 trap: ignoring supports. A sign without discriminanda and manipulanda is not an expectation; Tolman’s “Behavior cannot go off in vacuo” is a hard constraint [PAGE 85]. Week 4 trap: treating transfer as reflex. Tolman treats transfer as modified sign-gestalt readiness, not a stamped-in connection [PAGE 151–152]. Week 5 trap: conflating perception and mnemonization. Tolman says the difference is one of degree, and pure perception is practically absent outside the newborn [PAGE 139]. Week 6 trap: missing fixation. A position habit can mimic expectation while being non-docile [PAGE 153–154]. Week 7 trap: overclaiming delayed reaction from gross orientation; Hunter’s rats required orientation, while Honzik’s method allowed some true delay via condensed signs [PAGE 155–162]. These traps recur across weeks because each substitutes a simpler label for Tolman’s multi-layer operator.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In a crisis—blockage, reward change, support violation, or delay—three mechanisms compete: habit regression, counter-trap, and clock. Habit regression is Tolman’s fixation: the organism continues a specific means-object response even when it is no longer docile. The position habit is the paradigm: the rat chooses a side “persistently and continuously” despite changed contingencies, and the initial sign-gestalt expectation has lost its cognitive character [PAGE 153–154]. The counter-trap is inferential re-routing: the organism uses the learned means-end field to infer that a common final path is blocked, or that a reverse end belongs to the same detour, and shifts to an alternative. This is the Hsiao/Tolman–Honzik result: after training with a block at A, a test block at B in the common section should shift choice away from Path 2 toward Path 3 [PAGE 164–169]. The clock is the delayed-reaction leash: the organism must hold the sign-gestalt expectation over \(\tau\) seconds, condense the sign to the part that remains, and then release behavior on the condensed sign [PAGE 157–162].

The recursive theory-of-mind point is that none of these three is a separate “mind.” They are competing descriptions of the same organism-environment system. Habit regression is high response probability with low docility; counter-trap is high inferential use of field structure; clock is successful retention and condensation of a sign. The failure modes are precise. If fixation dominates, \(I(E;O)\to0\) while behavior is stable; the organism is not “expecting” in Tolman’s sense. If the field is not learned, counter-trap inference is unavailable and the organism reverts to blind trial and error. If \(\tau>\tau_L\) or the condensed sign carries zero bits about the goal, the clock mechanism fails and the animal relies on gross orientation or chance. Tolman’s own summary supports this triage: delayed reaction requires single-exposure evocation, leash-holding, and condensed-sign release [PAGE 162], while fixation is the “nemests” of sign-gestalt expectation [PAGE 154]. Thus a crisis is not automatically “regression” or “insight”; it is an adjudication among docile inference, non-docile fixation, and temporal retention capacity.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical veto is the strongest in Tolman’s system. A purely purposive or cognitive explanation is vetoed if the required behavior-support is absent, because “Behavior cannot go off in vacuo” [PAGE 85]. The rat cannot run an alley without a floor, walls, free space, or a discriminable white-black difference; the discrimination box requires actual whites and blacks to support the choice. Therefore any GSE/GSI claim that ignores discriminanda and manipulanda is invalid. The source’s terms are exact: discriminanda are supports for sensory discrimination, manipulanda are supports for motor manipulation, and both are expected before they are enjoyed [PAGE 85–91].

The biomechanical veto is also licensed. Tolman treats spatial, temporal, and force-resisting distances as separate dimensions and suggests that rats may prefer the less force-resisting route, while noting that geotropisms and “up-desires” or “down-desires” may complicate the picture [PAGE 108–109]. Formally, if the required edge cost \(w_e\) in joules exceeds the organism’s actuator capacity \(W_{\max}\), the behavior is physically impossible regardless of expectation. A cognitive explanation cannot override an impossible climb, an unsteady substrate, or a barrier that exceeds the animal’s manipulation capacity. The source also notes the rat’s poor visual capacity and the role of kinesthesis, so sensory capacity thresholds are part of the veto layer [PAGE 91–92, PAGE 170].

The weather veto is not source-internal. Tolman provides no weather-specific experiments. Therefore weather can enter only as a cross-layer perturbation of supports, discriminanda, manipulanda, or force-resistance. A valid weather veto must show at least one of the following: the support set becomes empty; the sensory sign becomes uninformative, \(I(s;g)=0\) bits; the force-resistance cost exceeds \(W_{\max}\); or the delay interval exceeds \(\tau_L\). If those conditions are not measured, a weather-based explanation is an external add-on and must be quarantined rather than attributed to Tolman. This is the gray-zone rule: physical and biomechanical vetoes are source-grounded; weather vetoes require explicit external measurement.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: reward-substitution veto. Train hungry rats with a more demanded goal, then substitute a less demanded goal or remove reward. Tolman’s prediction is transient disruption and a jump in errors/times, followed by docile readjustment if the new contingency is learnable. Elliott’s bran-mash-to-sunflower-seed shift produced “random searching” and disruption [PAGE 74]; Tolman and Honzik’s reward-removal group degenerated after removal [PAGE 54–55]. Verification: measure error counts, run time, and searching frequency before and after substitution. Veto condition: if behavior shows no disruption and no change in \(I(E;O)\), the demand/expectation construct is not supported in that setting.

Scenario 2: common-final-path inference veto. Train rats with a block at A so they learn Path 1 shortest, Path 2 next, Path 3 last. Then test with a block at B in the common final section of Paths 1 and 2. Tolman’s prediction is that rats should avoid Path 2 and take Path 3 more often than in training, because they infer the common blockage [PAGE 164–169]. Verification: compare choice proportions for Path 2 versus Path 3 in test trials against training trials; require a statistically reliable shift. Veto condition: if rats continue to choose Path 2 at training levels, the inference/field interpretation is vetoed for that maze.

Scenario 3: delayed-reaction condensation veto. Use a delayed-reaction procedure in which the sign is a light or white curtain and the delay removes the distinguishing cue. Tolman’s prediction is that successful delay requires condensation of the sign to the part that remains, and that allowing an overt choice at presentation should facilitate condensation and improve delay performance [PAGE 157–162]. Verification: compare Honzik-style overt-choice presentation with Hunter-style restrained presentation; measure correct choices at delays of 7–45 seconds and estimate \(I(s_c;g)\) in bits. Veto condition: if delay success is independent of the condensed sign and is explained entirely by gross bodily orientation, the “true delayed reaction” interpretation is vetoed. These three counterfactuals are verifiable because each specifies an experimental manipulation, a behavioral measure, and a rejection condition.
