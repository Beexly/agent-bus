Bipartite assignment:
\[
\hat{\sigma}=\arg\min_{\sigma}\sum_{k_i\in K_\text{object}}\mathcal{C}_\text{match}(y_i,\hat y_{\sigma(i)}).
\]

Matching cost:
\[
\mathcal{C}_\text{match}=-\lambda_\text{cls}\hat p_{\sigma(i)}(c_i)+\mathcal{C}_\text{box}(b_i,\hat b_{\sigma(i)}).
\]

Box cost:
\[
\mathcal{C}_\text{box}=\lambda_{\ell_1}\|b_i-\hat b_{\sigma(i)}\|_1+\lambda_\text{iou}\mathcal{C}_\text{iou}(b_i,\hat b_{\sigma(i)}).
\]

Set-prediction loss:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{MOT}(y,\hat y,\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_\text{query}(y,\hat y_i,\pi).
\]

Per-query loss:
For $i\in\pi$:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{query}=-\lambda_\text{cls}\log\hat p_i(c_{\pi=i})+\mathcal{L}_\text{box}(b_{\pi=i},\hat b_i).
\]
For $i\notin\pi$:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{query}=-\lambda_\text{cls}\log\hat p_i(0).
\]

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The operational analogue of recursive theory of mind here is per-object identity maintenance under partial observability. The decoder self-attention gives “joint reasoning about the objects in a scene” [PAGE 3], and the joint set of object and track queries lets the model detect new objects “while simultaneously avoiding re-detection of already tracked objects” [PAGE 3]. This is not explicit mental-state inference; it is a recursive embedding update: “each new object detection initializes a track query with the corresponding output embedding of the previous frame” and the decoder “continuously updating the instance-specific representation of an object‘s identity and location in each track query embedding” [PAGE 3].

Crisis habit regression is the failure mode in which occlusion or crowding pushes the system back toward detection-plus-greedy-matching habits. The paper’s ablation shows that this regression is costly: without track queries, “Tracks are associated via greedy center distance matching as in [69] resulting in a huge drop of -3.0 MOTA and -14.1 IDF1” [PAGE 8]. The counter-trap is the track-query mechanism plus short-term re-identification: “The ability to decode an arbitrary number of track queries allows for an attention-based short-term re-identification process” [PAGE 4]. The counter-trap is deliberately bounded: track queries are inactive during a patience window and only re-identify if the score exceeds $\sigma_\text{track-reid}$ [PAGE 4, PAGE 9]. The clock is the temporal budget: autoregressive frame-to-frame propagation [PAGE 2–3], $T_\text{track-reid}=5$ [PAGE 9], frame-range sampling to simulate camera motion and low frame rates [PAGE 5], and an online runtime of 7.4 FPS reported for TrackFormer [PAGE 7]. Thus the correct recursive policy is: trust track queries for short-horizon identity continuity; distrust them when the clock exceeds the patience window or when motion invalidates embedded spatial information; fall back to object-query re-detection rather than unbounded identity carry-over.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
