RLVR veto triggers if H_yoked(Δt) ≈ 6.5 across Δt ≥ 0.5 s, demonstrating that the floor on page 171 is shock-alone sensitization rather than associative habit strength [PAGE 171].

Scenario 2 — Temporal jitter veto. Counterfactual: replace the fixed click–shock intervals with a uniform temporal jitter, Δt ~ U(0 s, 2 s), while holding overall shock rate constant. Prediction: predictive information I(CS; US) collapses to near zero, so no stable point of optimal stimulus asynchronism should be detectable and the posterior gradient should be statistically indistinguishable from noise. RLVR veto triggers if subjects nevertheless show a reliable acquisition peak at a "fraction of a second" despite jitter, implying control by a non-temporal cue and falsifying the asynchronism account [PAGE 171].

Scenario 3 — Backward-conditioning contingency reversal veto. Counterfactual: present the shock (US) 0.5 s before the click (CS) across matched acquisition sessions, then test responding to the click alone. Prediction per Pavlov's revised position and Hull's receptor–effector temporal postulate [PAGE 171]: responding should be very weak and unstable, showing rapid extinction within a few trials and no stable habit strength comparable to forward optimal asynchronism. RLVR veto triggers if backward conditioning yields stable, high-magnitude acquisition matching the forward optimum, which would falsify the claim that the stimulus must precede the reaction it evokes [PAGE 171].
