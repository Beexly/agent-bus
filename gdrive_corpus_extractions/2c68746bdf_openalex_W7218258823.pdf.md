<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**Provenance declaration.** The source PDF contains **zero** equations, zero units, and zero quantitative models. Every expression below is a *reconstruction*: it formalises a construct the paper names, using the canonical formulation from the works the paper itself cites (refs. 3, 11, 12, 18, 23, 24 on [PAGE 8]) or from the standard literature of the constructs invoked. Provenance is tagged per equation. No ratio is invented.

---

**(E1) Constraint-feasibility operator** — [SRC-ADJACENT: Newell's constraints triad; invoked p.1 "complex and dynamic interaction between individuals and the limitations of their surroundings"]. Let \(x\) be the performer state, \(t\) time, \(\mathcal{U}\) the action repertoire:

\[
\mathcal{A}(x,t)=\bigl\{\,a\in\mathcal{U}\;:\;\Phi_{O}(x)=1\ \wedge\ \Phi_{T}(x,t)=1\ \wedge\ \Phi_{E}(x,t)=1\,\bigr\}
\]

with \(\Phi_{O},\Phi_{T},\Phi_{E}\in\{0,1\}\) dimensionless feasibility indicators for organismic, task, and environmental constraints. Units: \(\mathcal{A}\) is a set of actions (count). **Assumptions:** constraints are treated as binary at the timescale of a single trial. **Breakdown regime:** \(\mathcal{A}=\varnothing\) — no affordance is available. This is exactly the failure the paper describes: "If difficulties are excessive, the learner may struggle to resolve the issue or find an unsatisfactory solution" [PAGE 4].

**(E2) Environmental information content** — [SRC-ADJACENT: p.1 "environments rich in information"]. Shannon entropy over the affordance set:

\[
H(\mathcal{A})=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_i\log_2 p_i \quad[\text{bits}]
\]

**Assumptions:** discrete, enumerable affordance categories; stationary \(p_i\) within a practice block. **Breakdown regimes:** \(H\to 0\) bits — fully predictable, which is the "stationary cone" pathology: "Removing the moving action from its original context and purpose (i.e., Moving the body toward a stationary cone or back) diminishes its significance and meaningfulness to the player" [PAGE 5]; and \(H\to\log_2 N\) bits with uniform \(p_i\) — pure noise, which destroys representativeness rather than enriching it. The optimum is interior, not maximal.

**(E3) Challenge-point ordinal relation** — [SRC-ADJACENT: ref. 12, Guadagnoli & Lee, *J Motor Behav* 2004;36(2):212–24, cited [PAGE 8]]. Functional task difficulty is a monotone function of nominal difficulty and skill:

\[
D_f=g(D_n,S),\qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial D_n}>0,\qquad \frac{\partial g}{\partial S}<0
\]

Units: **ordinal difficulty points** — the framework is explicitly not ratio-scaled, so no multiplication or division of these quantities is licensed. **Optimal learning band:** \(D_f^\star\in[S-\delta,\;S+\delta]\). **Breakdown regimes:** \(D_f\ll S\) (underload, disengagement); \(D_f\gg S\) (the p.4 failure mode, plus elevated injury and attrition risk at the physical layer — see cross-layer section). This is the formal content behind "challenges within learning exercises should be carefully calibrated to match the individual's abilities and action capabilities" [PAGE 4].

**(E4) Flow channel as a region, not an equation** — [SRC-ADJACENT: p.2 "striking a balance between challenge and skill level"]. 

\[
\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{(C,S)\;:\;\alpha\le C/S\le\beta\bigr\},\qquad \alpha,\beta\ \text{dimensionless, empirically estimated}
\]

Deliberately stated as an inequality region: no unique functional form for flow has been empirically established, so writing \(F=C/S\) as a scalar would be a fabricated ratio. **Breakdown:** \(C/S\to 0\) (boredom); \(C/S\to\infty\) (anxiety). Note that \(\alpha,\beta\) are **not constants across skill levels** — as \(S\) grows the channel translates, which is why a week-1 calibration is obsolete by week 6.

**(E5) Power law of practice** — [CANONICAL: Newell & Rosenbloom 1981; consistent with p.8 "a form of repetition that does not repeat itself"]. 

\[
T(n)=T_\infty+B\,n^{-\alpha}\quad[\text{s per trial}]
\]

**Assumptions:** stable task, rest intervals sufficient to prevent fatigue confound. **Breakdown:** \(\alpha>1\) is unphysical for this family; \(n\to\infty\) saturates at \(T_\infty\), meaning the principle silently stops working — the paper never states a saturation policy.

**(E6) Tau-coupling for interceptive timing** — [CANONICAL: Lee 1976; the paper's cricket-slip example, p.7]. Define the tau of a closing gap:

\[
\tau(t)=\frac{r(t)}{\dot r(t)}\quad[\text{s}],\qquad \dot\tau=k,\quad k\ \text{dimensionless}\ (k=-\tfrac12\ \text{for constant deceleration})
\]

**Singularity:** \(\dot r\to 0^{-}\) makes \(\tau\to\infty\) — tau diverges at the moment of closure, so the law carries no information in the final milliseconds; control must hand over to terminal guidance (haptic/ballistic). **Invalidity:** \(\dot r>0\) (retreating gap) gives negative tau and the law is undefined as a time-to-contact. The "unbreakable egg" analogy [PAGE 7] is precisely a constraint on the terminal-guidance phase, i.e. on grasp aperture and force rate, not on \(\tau\) itself.

**(E7) The variation operator ("repetition that does not repeat itself")** — [SRC-ADJACENT: p.8, plus the randomised-defender task p.4]:

\[
\theta_{n+1}=\theta_n+\sigma\,\xi_n,\qquad \xi_n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
\]

Units: \(\sigma\) carries the units of \(\theta\) (m, rad, or s). **Breakdown regimes:** \(\sigma=0\) — blocked, zero exploration, the classic poor-transfer condition; \(\sigma\) large enough that \(\theta_{n+1}\) leaves the representative region of the task — the task becomes a different task, and transfer is negative by construction.

**(E8) Lens-model achievement (representative design)** — [SRC-ADJACENT: refs. 3, 23, 24; Brunswik 1956]. With standardised cue weights \(\beta_i\), ecological validities \(r_{ei}\), and utilizations \(r_{si}\):

\[
r_{ach}=\sum_i \beta_i\,r_{ei}\quad(\text{dimensionless};\ -1\le r_{ach}\le 1)
\]

**Breakdown:** when the practice environment's validity vector \(\{r_{ei}\}^{practice}\neq\{r_{ei}\}^{competition}\), \(r_{ach}\) estimated in practice is a biased estimator of competition achievement. This is the formal statement of "Removing the moving action from its original context ... diminishes its significance and meaningfulness to the player" [PAGE 5].

**(E9) Order-parameter dynamics (the "nonlinear / disproportionate" claim)** — [SRC-ADJACENT: p.5 "system behavior is perceived as nonlinear, where alterations in system characteristics yield disproportionate outcomes"]:

\[
\dot\xi=-\frac{\partial V(\xi;\lambda)}{\partial \xi}+\sqrt{2Q}\,\eta(t)
\]

\(\xi\) = order parameter (task-specific units), \(\lambda\) = control parameter (e.g. defender count or speed), \(Q\) = noise intensity. **Breakdown:** at the bifurcation \(\lambda=\lambda_c\) the system shows critical fluctuations and hysteresis — performance becomes history-dependent, so pre/post comparisons across the bifurcation are invalid without a hysteresis protocol.

**(E10) Degeneracy of the solution set** — [CANONICAL: Edelman & Gally; supports "Customize" and "System Thinking", pp.3–5]:

\[
D=\bigl|\{\,\text{structurally distinct movement solutions achieving function }\mathcal{F}\,\}\bigr|\quad[\text{count}]
\]

**Breakdown:** \(D=1\) — a single solution is fragile under any perturbation (weather, opponent, fatigue); \(D\) is the measurable target of E7.

**(E11) Sampled-data correction bandwidth for feedback** — [CANONICAL: Nyquist/Shannon sampled-data bound; formalises "immediate feedback", p.2]:

\[
f_c\le\frac{1}{2\,\Delta t}\quad[\text{Hz}],\qquad \Delta t\ [\text{s}]\ \text{feedback latency}
\]

**Breakdown:** as \(\Delta t\) grows, \(f_c\) falls below the bandwidth of the error signal the athlete must correct, and the feedback becomes a post-hoc scoreboard rather than a control input. This is the quantitative reason the paper's wearable/AI claim [PAGE 2] cannot be evaluated as written: no latency is specified.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper's intellectual ancestry runs through four independent lineages that were never designed to meet, and that is where its fragility and its value both reside. Lineage one is **ecological psychology**: Gibson's affordances (the affordance is a relation between environmental properties and effector capabilities, not a property of either alone), formalised above as E1. The paper gestures at this without naming Gibson. Lineage two is **motor control and coordination dynamics**: Bernstein's degrees-of-freedom problem, Lee's tau-coupling (E6), and the Haken–Kelso–Bunz order-parameter program that underwrites E9. Lineage three is **experimental methodology**: Brunswik's representative design (ref. 23, [PAGE 8]) and its modern sport instantiation in Pinder, Davids, Renshaw and Araújo (refs. 3 and 24) — the direct source of the paper's fidelity argument. Lineage four is **instructional and motivational psychology**: Gee on games and learning, Ryan & Deci on self-determination, Csikszentmihalyi on flow — all three invoked in the text ([PAGE 2], [PAGE 3], [PAGE 6]) and all three absent from the bibliography. The paper is therefore a *cross-lineage splice*, and its weakest joints are exactly the joints where a citation should have been.

The retrocausal read matters because it explains which claims are load-bearing. The paper's own most defensible operator is E3, the challenge point, imported via ref. 12 — a framework that was constructed precisely to reconcile "difficulty" as a task property with "difficulty" as a learner property, and which is **ordinal**, not metric. Any downstream model that converts challenge point into a ratio has retroactively falsified the source it claims to use.

**Multi-week traps.** Seven traps are structurally predictable from this genealogy, and each has a characteristic horizon:

1. **Novelty decay of gamification.** Leaderboards and badges [PAGE 2] act on novelty and social comparison; the effect is largest in weeks 1–2 and typically regresses toward baseline by weeks 4–6. A week-1 evaluation will overstate the durable effect by an unknown factor.
2. **Contextual-interference inversion.** Random-variation tasks of the kind described on [PAGE 4] produce *worse* performance during acquisition and *better* retention/transfer at delayed test. Any evaluation with a short horizon inverts the true ordering — this is the single most reliable trap in the entire paper.
3. **Challenge-point drift.** \(D_f^\star\) translates as \(S\) grows (E3, E4). A task calibrated in week 1 becomes underload by week 5–6. The paper never states a recalibration cadence.
4. **Representative-design decay.** As fitness and skill rise, the task's cue-validity vector drifts from the competition vector (E8); scaling literature (Buszard, ref. 18, [PAGE 8]) exists precisely because equipment and area must be re-scaled jointly with the performer.
5. **Guidance dependence.** High-frequency augmented feedback (wearables, AI tracking, [PAGE 2]) suppresses intrinsic error detection; the self-controlled feedback literature the paper cites (refs. 14–16, [PAGE 8]) documents that feedback *after good trials* and *subject-controlled* feedback outperform yoked schedules — so "more feedback, faster" is not monotone.
6. **Load accumulation.** Chronic-load and injury literature (Malone et al., ref. 8) makes the physical layer a binding constraint over multi-week horizons regardless of pedagogical design quality.
7. **Observer contamination.** In the paper's own preferred model the coach "observes the athlete(s) without interfering and only offers guidance when specifically asked for it" [PAGE 3] — an inherently non-blinded design; the athlete's behaviour is conditioned on being observed by the person who will later judge the design.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model the coach as an agent holding a belief about the athlete's belief about the task, and about the coach. Three attractors govern that recursion.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under time pressure the coach reverts to the instructional habit that formed them — direct prescription — even while espousing the paper's principles. The paper encodes the pressure explicitly on [PAGE 4]: "With some level of success achieved in these more manageable problems, the coach can then introduce random variations in defender numbers without notifying the opposing team during the training exercise at that point." The clause "at that point" is a schedule the coach controls; under a session clock the coach shortens it. The regression is diagnostic: it appears precisely when \(D_f>S\) and the athlete's failure is aversive to the coach. The paper's own prescription — that a coach "only offers guidance when specifically asked for it" [PAGE 3] — is the counter-habit, and it fails hardest in the window where it is most needed.

**Counter-trap (learned dependency).** If the coach's questions are always followed by the coach's preferred answer, the athlete learns to model the question as a test with a known key rather than as an invitation to explore. The paper's own example is instructive because it half-avoids the trap: "Players do not necessarily need to verbalize their responses to in-game questions; instead, they can demonstrate their understanding by searching for, finding, and taking advantage of different opportunities to interact with their surroundings" [PAGE 3]. Demonstrating rather than verbalising is the correct counter, because it removes the coach's lexical key. But on [PAGE 4] the trap re-closes: "A coach may be able to. also manipulate the roles and positions of team game players to redistribute a player's game knowledge." Manipulation by the designer is not empowerment; the athlete who detects it will discount all future autonomy grants, and the discount is permanent, not session-local.

**The clock.** Three clocks run at once: the session clock (minutes — biases toward prescription), the season clock (weeks — force periodisation logic, ref. 11, [PAGE 8]), and the career clock (years — the only horizon on which the paper's autonomy claim, "an autonomous athlete—that is, an individual who learns to control his/her own perceptions, emotions, cognitions, and actions" [PAGE 8], is even observable). The three clocks disagree, and the paper never adjudicates between them. That silence is the recursive trap's fuel: a coach evaluated on the session clock will rationally violate a design optimised for the career clock.

**Third-order structure and detection windows.** The athlete models the coach's model of them. Signatures: dependency shows as reduced exploratory variance and increased latency-to-first-action when the coach is absent — detectable in 2–4 weeks. Genuine autonomy shows as preserved or increased variance plus preserved task success under coach absence — detectable in 6–8 weeks. A single evaluation window cannot separate the two, which is why an observation protocol without an absence condition is uninformative.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A pedagogical principle is vetoed whenever a lower-layer feasibility indicator evaluates to zero, irrespective of its pedagogical merit. The source supplies almost no layer discipline; the adjudication rule must be imported. Priority ordering: **tissue tolerance → metabolic capacity → informational fidelity → motivational design.** A higher layer may never override a lower one.

**Physical/metabolic veto.** The paper's reference list contains the veto evidence it never uses analytically: Bangsbo, Mohr & Krustrup on "Physical and metabolic demands of training and match-play" (ref. 7), and Malone et al. on chronic load and injury risk (ref. 8) [PAGE 8]. A flow-state design [PAGE 2] that raises cognitive load is not free: attentional demand competes with the metabolic budget of the session, and high-intensity interval work (ref. 9) has a fixed recovery cost. Veto condition: if the designed task's metabolic cost plus the athlete's accumulated chronic load exceeds the tolerance envelope, the design is invalid regardless of its informational richness. The paper's own "improve your reaction speed by 10%" target [PAGE 2] is meaningless without specifying whether this is simple or choice reaction time, the measurement device, and its test–retest reliability — and 10% is a plausible *and* implausible number depending on which of those is chosen, which is exactly why it functions as rhetoric rather than as a target.

**Biomechanical veto.** The cricket-slip case [PAGE 7] is the cleanest testbed. Required tangential grip force at contact is \(F_{\text{grip}}\ge m\,|\dot v|/(2\mu)\)-scaled by the friction coefficient \(\mu\) at the ball–hand interface. In dry conditions the "unbreakable egg" analogy maps cleanly onto a grip-force constraint. In wet conditions \(\mu\) falls sharply; the required force exceeds what the skin–ball interface can deliver, and the analogy now specifies an action that is physically unavailable. The affordance disappears — formally \(\mathcal{A}=\varnothing\) in E1 — and the correct response is equipment or condition substitution, not more analogy.

**Weather veto.** Three distinct channels, none addressed in the paper: (i) *precipitation* via \(\mu\), as above; (ii) *wind* via projectile deviation, which shifts \(r(t)\) and therefore the tau profile in E6, requiring re-tuning of the interceptive law — an indoor-trained timing solution transfers with bias; (iii) *heat* via cognitive decrement, which raises the effective \(D_f\) in E3 without any change in nominal task difficulty, silently pushing the athlete out of the optimal band. This third channel is the most insidious, because it looks like a motivational failure and is actually a thermal one.

**Adjudication verdict.** For every principle in the source, the veto test is: does the principle survive after (a) the tissue/metabolic budget, (b) the friction and projectile environment, and (c) the cue-validity match between practice and competition are all satisfied? Principles 3 (gamification), 6 (flow/immersion), and 7 (data-driven personalization) are the most veto-exposed, because each is a *motivational-layer* intervention that presumes the lower layers are already green. The paper presents them as peers of the constraint-based principles; the layered reading says they are strictly downstream.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario specifies an intervention drawn verbatim from the source, a measurable endpoint with units, a predicted direction, and an explicit **veto condition** that would falsify the paper's implied claim. All are pre-registerable and reward-verifiable.

**Scenario 1 — Gamified leaderboard, withdrawal design.**
Intervention: skill-based ranking and milestone badges as described in "Engagement Through Gamification" [PAGE 2]. Design: 6-week A-B-A withdrawal with a yoked non-gamified control; primary endpoint = **retention/transfer score at +2 weeks post-withdrawal** (percentage of correctly executed solutions in a representative task, with inter-rater reliability reported); secondary = within-session output (repetitions per minute). Prediction: gamified arm exceeds control on within-session output in weeks 1–3, difference attenuates to within the smallest detectable effect by week 6, and **reverses or nulls** at the +2-week transfer test. **Veto:** if the gamified arm retains superiority at +2 weeks with a confidence interval excluding zero, the novelty-decay model is falsified and the paper's implicit "engagement → learning" bridge survives.

**Scenario 2 — Randomised defender numbers (contextual interference).**
Intervention: the graded-to-randomised progression on [PAGE 4] — fixed defender numbers, then "random variations in defender numbers without notifying the opposing team." Design: three arms (blocked, progressive-random, immediate-random), 8 weeks, transfer test at +2 weeks against a live defence. Endpoint: **decision time (s) and solution-variety count \(D\) (E10)** in the transfer test. Prediction: blocked best at week 2, worst at transfer; progressive-random intermediate at week 2, best at transfer; immediate-random worst at week 2 with the highest injury/attrition incidence. **Veto:** if blocked practice is not inferior at transfer, the contextual-interference mechanism — the paper's strongest evidential leg — fails, and the graded progression is merely a motivational device. Secondary veto: if immediate-random collapse occurs at week 1 (high \(\sigma\) in E7), the paper's unstated assumption that randomisation is always tolerable is falsified.

**Scenario 3 — Analogy vs explicit instruction, with a weather crossover.**
Intervention: the cricket-slip analogy, "align their hands around the ball as if it were an unbreakable egg" [PAGE 7], versus explicit kinematic instruction, versus control. Design: 2 (instruction) × 2 (dry ball / wet ball) crossover; endpoint = **coefficient of variation of peak grip force (dimensionless) and catch-success rate (%)**, measured with instrumented gloves. Prediction: analogy arm shows lower grip-force variability and higher success in the dry condition, and **loses its advantage in the wet condition** because the analogy's constraint no longer maps onto the reduced friction coefficient; explicit-instruction arm is condition-invariant but lower ceiling. **Veto:** if the analogy arm preserves its advantage under wet conditions, then the analogy is encoding a generalizable timing constraint rather than a friction-dependent force constraint — a materially different theoretical claim from the one the paper advances.

**Scenario 4 (supplementary) — Feedback latency and withdrawal.**
Intervention: high-frequency wearable/AI feedback [PAGE 2] versus reduced-frequency feedback, both then withdrawn for 2 weeks. Endpoint: **error-detection latency (s) and self-correction rate without augmented feedback**. Prediction: high-frequency arm shows superior acquisition and a larger post-withdrawal decrement (guidance dependence), consistent with the self-controlled-feedback literature cited as refs. 14–16 [PAGE 8]. **Veto:** if no withdrawal decrement appears, "immediate feedback" is a safe default rather than a schedule to be titrated.

Across all four, the shared RLVR reward is the **delayed transfer endpoint**, not the acquisition endpoint — because every one of the paper's claims is vulnerable to the same inversion, and only a delayed, coach-absent, representative test can distinguish a durable skill ecology from a well-designed week one.
