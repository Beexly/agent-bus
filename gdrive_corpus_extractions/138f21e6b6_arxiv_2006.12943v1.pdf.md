Anything else is a toy ratio and is vetoed.

**Multi-hop derivation, hop 1 — the baseline contraction.** The CMY round sends slack $s=\lfloor N/2k\rfloor$; when the $k$-th notification lands, $N\leftarrow N'=N-\sum_i n_i$, and because each player can overshoot by at most $s$, the captured mass is $\Theta(N/2)$, i.e. $N$ contracts by a *constant factor* per round → $O(\log(N/k))$ rounds → $O(k\log(N/k))$ messages ([PAGE 1]). This is a geometric contraction. The paper's whole contribution is replacing "constant-factor contraction in $N$" with "square-root contraction in $N$".

**Hop 2 — where the square root comes from.** Set $s_i=UB_i$ (Expression 2, [PAGE 4]). The slack is expectation plus a Bernstein tail: $\mu_i t + \sqrt{2t\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\ln(k/\delta)} + \tfrac23\ln(k/\delta)$. The tail term scales as $\sqrt{t}$, not $t$. Summing over players and applying concavity of $\sqrt{\cdot}$ ([PAGE 10], Ineq. 9–10) gives $\sum_i s_i \le t+\sqrt{2kt\ln(k/\delta)}+\tfrac{2k}{3}\ln(k/\delta)$. Setting $f(t)\le N$ and solving yields $t=N-(\sqrt2+2/3)\sqrt{kN\ln(k/\delta)}$, i.e. the *un-tracked residue* per round is $O(\sqrt{kN\ln(k/\delta)})$ rather than $O(N)$.

**Hop 3 — the log-log.** Because the residue is $\sqrt{kN\cdot c}$ with $c=\ln(k/\delta)$, the map on $N$ is $N\mapsto \Theta(\sqrt{kNc})$. Take logs: $d\mapsto (d+d_1)/2$ where $d_1=\log(kc\cdot\text{const})$ and $d_2=\log N$. Each round *halves the log-distance* to the floor $2^{d_1}$. **The DT operator.** State is $(N; n_1,\dots,n_k)$, $n_i\in\mathbb{N}_0$ counters with $\sum_i n_i$ monotonically non-decreasing, incremented by exactly one unit at at most one player per time step ([PAGE 1]: "at most one (that means it can be none) player having its counter increased by one"). The coordinator's task: raise the alarm at the unique time step where $\sum_{i\in[k]} n_i = N$ — *not* after, *not* before.

**Cost functional and its units.** $T(N)\in\mathbb{N}$ messages, each message $\le O(1)$ words. The corresponding *bit* lower bound is separate: $\Omega\!\left(k\log\frac{N}{k}\right)$ bits ([PAGE 3]: "Cormode et al. showed a $\Omega(k\log\frac{N}{k})$-bit communication lower bound for the DT problem"). Do not conflate: the paper's upper bounds are in **messages**, the lower bound in [7] is in **bits**, and there is no matching message-space lower bound proven here.

**The unified operator (Algorithm 2.1, $\ell$-Notifications-to-End, [PAGE 3]).** Three characteristics only:
1. slack assignment $s_i$, $\forall i\in[k]$ (Line 4);
2. notify predicate on $n_i$ vs $s_i$ (Line 5);
3. round-termination count $\ell$ (Line 6).

Per-round cost, in the regime $N=\omega(k\ln(k/\delta))$: $T_{\text{round}} = O(k+\ell)$ messages ([PAGE 3]). Total: $T(N)=O\big((k+\ell)R + k\log\log(k/\delta)\big)$ where $R$ = rounds before entering Line 2.

**Static-slack operator (StcSlk-KwnDst), [PAGE 4], Expression (2), verbatim:**
$$s_i \;=\; \mu_i t + \sqrt{2t\,\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\ln\frac{k}{\delta}} + \frac{2}{3}\ln\frac{k}{\delta},\qquad \forall i\in[k];$$
with $\ell=1$ and notify-when-$n_i=s_i$. **Supporting inequality (Fact 1, Bernstein, [PAGE 4]):** for independent $Y_j$ with $Y_j\le \mathbb{E}[Y_j]+M$,
$$\Pr\!\left[Y\ge\mathbb{E}Y+\lambda\right]\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lambda^2}{2\left(\operatorname{Var}Y + M\lambda/3\right)}\right).$$
Applied with $Y_j=\mathbb{1}[\text{item }j\to\text{player }i]$, $\mathbb{E}Y_j=\mu_i$, $\operatorname{Var}Y_j=\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\in[0,\tfrac14]$, $M=1$. **Correctness invariant (Inequality 1, [PAGE 4], verbatim):** $\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_i\le N$. This is the *only* thing preventing the round from overshooting the threshold and missing the alarm.

**Choice of $t$ (Lemma 4.4, [PAGE 5]):**

Derivation via concavity ([PAGE 10], Ineq. 9–10): $\sum_i\sqrt{2t\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\ln\frac{k}{\delta}}\le\sqrt{2kt\ln\frac{k}{\delta}}$. **Breakdown:** $t\le 0$ iff $N\le(\sqrt2+2/3)^2k\ln(k/\delta)$ — the same surface as $\beta$, so the "switch to CMY" rule is not arbitrary.

**Contraction recursion (Lemma 4.5, [PAGE 5]):**
$$T(N)=\begin{cases}T\!\left((\sqrt2+2/3)\sqrt{kN\ln\frac{k}{\delta}}\right)+O(k), & N\ge 2(\sqrt2+2/3)^2k\ln\frac{k}{\delta}\\[2pt] T(N/2)+O(k), & 4k\le N<2(\sqrt2+2/3)^2k\ln\frac{k}{\delta}\\[2pt] O(k), & N<4k\end{cases}$$
Solution: $T(N)=O\!\left(k\log\log\frac{N}{k\ln(k/\delta)}+k\log\log\frac{k}{\delta}\right)$ messages, w.h.p. $1-\delta\cdot O(\log\log N)$.

**Assembled theorem (4.1, [PAGE 4]):** rounds $\le O(\log\log\frac{N}{k\log(k/\delta)}+\log\log\frac{k}{\delta})$; total cost $O(k\log\log\frac{N}{k\ln(k/\delta)}+k\log\log\frac{k}{\delta})$ messages.

**Dynamic-slack operator (DynSlk-KwnDst, [PAGE 5]):** pair $(b_i,s_i)$ with $b_i=\mu_i t$ and $s_i=\lfloor (N-t)/2k\rfloor$; notify every $s_i$ increments *provided* $n_i\ge b_i$; $\ell=k$. Budget identity: $\sum_i (UB_i-\mu_i t)\le N-t$. Theorem 4.7: identical bounds to 4.1. This is a *hybrid*: CMY's slack (data-independent robustness) bolted onto the learned base $b_i$ (data-dependent skips).

**Learning operator, three modifications ([PAGE 6]):**
$$\hat\mu_i=\bar\mu_i+\sqrt{\frac{2(\bar\mu_i-\bar\mu_i^2)\ln\frac{3}{\delta}}{w}}+\frac{3\ln\frac{3}{\delta}}{w}\quad\text{(Empirical Bernstein, Fact 2)}$$
$$UB_i=\hat\mu_i t+\sqrt{2t\hat\mu_i\ln\frac{k}{\delta}}+\frac{2}{3}\ln\frac{k}{\delta};\qquad t=\frac{N}{\hat\Sigma}-\left(\sqrt2+\tfrac23\right)\sqrt{\frac{k(N/\hat\Sigma)\ln\frac{k}{\delta}}{1}},\ \ \hat\Sigma=\sum_i\hat\mu_i .$$
The $+$ sign on the $\hat\mu$ correction is the *conservatism* that restores "$UB_i$ is a valid upper bound" once $\bar\mu$ may underestimate $\mu$. Switch constant becomes $\beta=2(2\sqrt2+2/3+3)^2$.

**Singularity map (exact regimes):**
- $N<4k$: straightforward, $T=O(N)=O(k)$ messages; exact.
- $4k\le N\le\beta k\ln(k/\delta)$: CMY, $T=O(k\log(N/k))$; the log-log gain is *identically zero* here.
- $N>\beta k\ln(k/\delta)$: data-dependent regime, $T=O(k\log\log(\cdot))$.
- $\mu_i\to0$ for any $i$: $s_i\to\frac23\ln(k/\delta)$ (constant floor, $t$-independent) → per-player floor $\times\,k$ erodes $N$; if $\frac{2k}{3}\ln(k/\delta)\gtrsim N$ the invariant $\sum s_i\le N$ becomes unsatisfiable.
- $\mu_i=\frac1k\ \forall i$: $\sum_i\sqrt{2t\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\ln(k/\delta)}=\sqrt{2t\ln(k/\delta)\cdot k\cdot(1-\frac1k)}\to\sqrt{2kt\ln(k/\delta)}$, i.e. the concavity bound of [PAGE 10] is **tight** — uniform is the worst case for the data-dependent slack inflation.
- $\delta\to 0$ at fixed $N,k$: $k\ln(k/\delta)\sim k\log(1/\delta)$ eventually exceeds $N$, collapsing into the CMY branch; the log-log advantage *vanishes in the limit of vanishing failure probability*.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
**Backward lineage.** The paper's prehistory is the continuous-distributed-monitoring line: UniSlk (Cormode et al. [5], cost $O(k^2\log\frac{N}{k})$) rested on the pigeonhole fact that with $N$ items over $k$ players some player must receive $N/k$ — a *worst-case* argument, hence the quadratic $k^2$. CMY ([7], Cormode–Muthukrishnan–Yi 2011) replaced the pigeonhole with aggregate slack $\lfloor N/2k\rfloor$ and $k$ notifications, dropping to $O(k\log\frac{N}{k})$; it is explicitly the *data-independent* ceiling this paper attacks ([PAGE 3]). The $\Omega(k\log\frac{N}{k})$-bit lower bound of [7] is the wall the authors do *not* claim to break — they break the upper bound by changing the model (distribution assumption), not the lower bound, which is a model-free statement. That distinction is essential and is honestly scoped in the abstract.

**Forward lineage (the "learned" wave).** The paper positions itself inside a specific 2017–2019 cluster, all of which it cites on [PAGE 1]: learned frequency estimation ([1] Aamand–Indyk–Vakilian 2019; [9] Hsu–Indyk–Katabi–Vakilian ICLR 2019), learned Bloom filters ([15] Mitzenmacher 2018), learned index structures ([14] Kraska et al. SIGMOD 2018), learned combinatorial optimization ([3] Bello et al. 2017; [12] Khalil et al. 2017). The shared theorem-schema is: *assume the input is drawn from a distribution $\mathcal{D}$, learn $\widehat{\mathcal{D}}$, substitute into a classical worst-case algorithm, obtain a data-dependent bound that is strictly better on $\mathcal{D}$-typical inputs while retaining a worst-case fallback.* This paper is the distributed-tracking instantiation of that schema, and its structural fingerprint — a **fallback branch** (Line 3) plus a **watchdog** (Appendix B) — is the schema's signature. Any downstream system claiming a "learned" improvement without a stated fallback branch is genealogically suspect.

**Multi-week traps (multi-round traps).** Four recurring traps visible in this lineage and reproduced exactly here:
1. **The one-shot estimate trap.** [PAGE 6]: "we run the CMY algorithm for the first round… At the end of this first round, we estimate $\mu_i$ by $\bar\mu_i=\frac{n_i}{\sum_{i\in[k]}n_i}$." A single sample, frozen forever, sized $w\ge N_0/2$. Any non-stationarity after round 1 is invisible to the estimator. This is the "form regression" trap: fitting a distribution to a window and then treating the fit as an axiom.
2. **The union-bound inflation trap.** $\delta'=\delta\cdot O(\log\log N)$ ([PAGE 4]). Nominal $\delta=1\%$ ([PAGE 7]) at $N=2^{20}$ is effectively ~4.3%. Reporting "1% failure" without the union factor is a mild but real over-claim.
3. **The censored-tail trap.** Figures 6–7 truncate at $10^3$/$10^4$/$10^5$/$10^6$ ([PAGE 8]). UniSlk's blow-up is *asserted beyond the plotted window*, not measured inside it: "It does not show up in the plot for Exponential distribution because it uses more than $10^3$ communication even for $N=1k$."
4. **The concentrated-$k$ trap.** [PAGE 8] gives the ratio $\frac{O(k\log\frac{N}{k})}{O(k\log\log\frac{N}{k\ln(k/\delta)}+k\log\log\frac{k}{\delta})}$; with $N,\delta$ fixed and $k$ rising, the ratio *shrinks*. The observed gain is therefore a small-$k$ phenomenon, and the paper says so: "a shrinking gap in the number of communications between CMY and our algorithms as the number of players increases."
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**The coordinator holds an explicit belief about other agents.** $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_k)$ is literally a theory of mind: a probability model of *which player will act next*. StcSlk-KwnDst conditions its entire message budget on that belief; DynSlk-KwnDst conditions only the *base* $b_i=\mu_i t$ on it and delegates the tolerance to worst-case CMY slack. These are two different epistemic postures toward the same belief: **trust the posterior** vs **trust only the posterior's location, insure against its error**.

**Crisis habit regression.** When the environment contradicts the model, both postures regress to a habit — CMY. The trigger is a scalar emotion-analogue: the round-retention ratio. [PAGE 11], Appendix B, verbatim: "Denote by $N$ the threshold to track at the start of the current round and $N'$ the one at the start of the next round… Intuitively, the ratio of $\frac{N-N'}{N}$ serves as an indicator of the effectiveness of the algorithm. If the ratio drops below a pre-specified threshold (say 0.75), we switch the algorithm to the CMY algorithm." The guarantee is bounded regret, not correctness: "at most $O(k)$ communication can be wasted." This is *habit* in the strict sense — it fires on a single scalar, without diagnosing *why* the belief failed, and it is irreversible for that round. The observed regression is documented on [PAGE 8]: "In Dartmouth datasets, the distribution is rather unstable – StcSlk-KwnDst and StcSlk-LrnDst fail in the second round and track much fewer percentage of items in the second round than the first one. Detecting the degeneracy in efficiency, they switch to CMY in the third round."

**The counter-trap.** The adversary that defeats this architecture is not one that makes the marginals wrong — $\bar\mu$ is estimated from the *same* stream — but one that makes them *right in round 1 and wrong in round 2*. The trap: $\mu^{(1)}\neq\mu^{(2)}$ with $D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu^{(2)},\bar\mu)$ large enough that $\hat\mu$ ceases to upper-bound $\mu$, so $\sum_i UB_i$ exceeds $N$, so the invariant $\sum_i s_i\le N$ fails at the two failure surfaces derived above (premature notification, and overshoot). Dartmouth is exactly such a trap in the wild; the paper's own numbers show it costs roughly one round of full degeneration plus one round of wasted $O(k)$ traffic.

**The clock.** The DT problem has *no wall clock*: "At each time stamp, there is at most one… player having its counter increased by one" ([PAGE 1]) — time is defined by the counter increment, not by seconds. The only clock-like quantities are (i) the round index and (ii) the arrival timestamps used only to *order* items in the real datasets ("in ascending order according to its time stamp", [PAGE 6]). This matters for theory of mind: the agents are asynchronous and the coordinator cannot use inter-arrival gaps as evidence about $\mu$. The learning signal is *purely compositional* (who got the item), never *temporal* (when). Any adversary controlling only timing, while preserving the composition sequence, is invisible to this architecture — an untested and genuine blind spot.

**Recursive layer: the adversary knows the watchdog.** Because the trigger is $\frac{N-N'}{N}<0.75$, an adversary who wants to *maximize* total cost while avoiding detection must hold per-round capture just above 0.75, keeping the algorithm in the expensive-but-not-regressing regime; alternatively, an adversary willing to accept one round of detection can force $O(k)$ waste per episode, and by re-triggering, force $O(k\cdot\#\text{episodes})$ — which is exactly CMY cost. So the watchdog *caps* the loss at the data-independent bound (the paper says as much), which is the right design: the theory-of-mind recursion terminates in a safe habit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/hardware veto, statistical-physics veto, and workload-"weather" veto, each stated as a *proof* that a claim cannot exceed its layer.

**Veto 1 (hardware/measurement layer).** All empirical claims live on "Ubuntu 18.04 with Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-8665U CPU @1.90 GHz and 16GB memory… C++ and compiled with gcc 7.4.0" ([PAGE 6]). The cost metric is *messages*, a purely logical quantity, so hardware cannot corrupt the numerator — good. But the *truncations* in Figures 6–7 ($10^3$, $10^4$, $10^5$, $10^6$) are an artifact of not wanting to run UniSlk to completion within that budget. **Adjudication: the monotone ordering CMY > UniSlk-on-skew is proven for the plotted domain only; beyond it, the claim "UniSlk gives the worst performance" ([PAGE 8]) is extrapolation.**

**Veto 2 (message-model layer).** The $O(k\log\log N)$ bound counts messages where "each message can only carry at most $O(1)$ words" ([PAGE 1]). But slack $s_i$ is a number up to $N$, and $b_i=\mu_i t$ is a real; transmitting $s_i$ costs $\Theta(\log N)$ bits, not $O(1)$. The paper's own contrast makes this explicit: CMY "admits a bit-version implementation with communication cost of $O(k\log N)$ bits" ([PAGE 3]), and the lower bound is in bits. **Adjudication: the log-log improvement is a message-count improvement; converting it to a bit-count improvement requires showing $s_i$ can be quantized to $O(1)$ bits, which the paper does not do. Any downstream claim of "sub-logarithmic bits" is vetoed.**

**Veto 3 (biomechanical/agent-constraint layer).** The players are abstract; the real instantiations are servers, access points, and pickup locations ([PAGE 6]). Real players have per-message *energy and latency* costs not modeled. Since StcSlk-KwnDst uses $\ell=1$ (a single notification ends the round) while CMY uses $\ell=k$, the *notification topology* differs: StcSlk concentrates decision power on one player, DynSlk distributes it. If per-message cost is asymmetric across players (a phone vs a datacenter), $\ell=1$ is not cost-neutral. **Adjudication: the model's symmetry assumption (uniform per-message cost) is unstated and consequential for deployment.**

**Veto 4 (weather / burstiness layer).** Hockey-stick burstiness is the workload analogue of weather. The paper records a live instance: "In WorldCup Day 60 (Figure 2 (b)), UniSlk exhibits frequent termination of rounds as the tailing items comes in a very unbalanced manner" ([PAGE 8]). Why does burstiness break UniSlk specifically? Because UniSlk is $1$-Notification-to-End with $s=N/k$: one notification ends the round, so any single player's burst truncates the round, and the residue $N'$ stays large → many rounds. Bernstein's tail is a *variance* statement, and burstiness inflates variance without changing the mean, so Fact 1's $\lambda$ is too small: $UB_i$ fails to be an upper bound. **Adjudication: the correctness of every algorithm here is conditional on an i.i.d./multinomial item process. Under burstiness the guarantee degrades from "w.h.p. $1-\delta$" to "with probability governed by the true (larger) variance." The paper never measures the true variance on the real datasets; the multinomial is imposed by counting frequencies, not by testing.**

**Veto 5 (scaling layer).** The stated gain ratio is $\frac{\log\frac{N}{k}}{\log\log\frac{N}{k\ln(k/\delta)}+\log\log\frac{k}{\delta}}$ ([PAGE 8]). For fixed $\delta$, as $k\to N$, the numerator $\to 0$ and the ratio $\to 1$: **the improvement is annihilated in the many-player limit.** Conversely as $N\to\infty$ with $k$ fixed, the ratio diverges like $\log N/\log\log N$. So the honest claim is "log-log in $N$, at most constant in $k$," and the experiments ($k\le256$, [PAGE 7]) sit in the favorable window.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three counterfactuals, each with a *falsifiable prediction* — if the prediction fails, the corresponding theoretical claim is vetoed.

**RLVR-Veto A — Independence ablation (targets Fact 1 / Lemma 4.2).**
*Intervention:* take the Uniform synthetic generator ($\mu_i=1/k$, $N=2^{20}$, $k=16$, $\delta=0.01$) and replace i.i.d. draws with **block-burst sampling**: partition the stream into blocks of length $m$; within each block assign all $m$ items to a single player drawn $\sim\text{Uniform}([k])$. Marginals over any *aligned* window are unchanged; $\operatorname{Var}[n_i]$ is inflated by a factor $\approx m$ (intra-block correlation), while $\mu_i$ is preserved — so $\bar\mu$ and $\hat\mu$ estimation is *unaffected*, which is the point.
*Prediction:* (i) $UB_i$ (Expression 2) ceases to be an upper bound at $1-\delta/k$; empirically $\Pr[n_i\ge UB_i]$ should rise from $\le 0.01/16$ to $\approx\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2t\mu(1-\mu)\ln(16/0.01)}}{\sqrt{m\,t\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)$, i.e. $\propto m^{-1/2}$ in the exponent. (ii) Consequently $\sum_i s_i\le N$ is violated with probability $\to1$, and the round terminates before $t$ items, so the number of rounds $R$ grows from $O(\log\log N)$ to $\Omega(\log(N/k)/\log m)$. (iii) DynSlk-KwnDst (whose tolerance is CMY slack over $N-t$) should degrade *less* than StcSlk-KwnDst, matching the paper's finding that dynamic slacks are "more stable than using merely predetermined and static slacks" ([PAGE 8]).
*Veto condition:* if $R$ is observed to be invariant to $m\in\{1,2,8,64\}$ at fixed $N,k,\delta$, then the Bernstein analysis is not the operative constraint and Lemma 4.2 must be re-derived.

**RLVR-Veto B — Frozen-belief drift (targets Theorem 5.1 / $\hat\Sigma$).**
*Intervention:* on a synthetic stream, set $\mu^{(1)}=(0.7,0.1,\dots)$ for the first $N_0/2$ items (round 1, CMY) and then $\mu^{(2)}=(0.1,0.7,\dots)$ — i.e. $D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu^{(2)},\bar\mu)=0.6$ with identical support and identical entropy. $\bar\mu$ is now a *severe* underestimate of the players who become hot.
*Prediction:* round 2 retention ratio $\frac{N-N'}{N}$ drops below the 0.75 watchdog threshold, forcing a switch to CMY in round 3, costing exactly the wasted $O(k)$ plus one degenerated round; total $T$ equals CMY's $T$ to within $O(k)$, i.e. **the learned algorithm provides zero net benefit against a single mid-stream axis flip**. Additionally, the response should be *hysteretic*: if instead the drift is ramped linearly at rate $\varepsilon$ per round, there exists a critical $\varepsilon^\star$ with $\varepsilon^\star=\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{k\ln(k/\delta)/N}\right)$ below which the watchdog never fires and the log-log bound survives. This $\varepsilon^\star$ is directly computable from the paper's own parameters and is the single most useful un-measured constant in the paper.
*Veto condition:* if a full axis flip yields a retention ratio *above* 0.75, then the watchdog is too lax and Appendix B's guarantee ("lose its effectiveness in at most one round") is false for adversarial drift.

**RLVR-Veto C — Uniform is the worst case (targets the concavity bound and the reported gain band).**
*Intervention:* no new data needed — a *derivation-plus-replot* test. By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\sum_i\sqrt{\mu_i(1-\mu_i)}\le\sqrt{k\sum_i\mu_i(1-\mu_i)}=\sqrt{k(1-\|\mu\|_2^2)}$, maximized at $\mu_i=1/k$; hence the slack-inflation term $\sum_i\sqrt{2t\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\ln(k/\delta)}$ is **maximized by the uniform distribution** and minimized by a degenerate $\mu$. Therefore the data-dependent algorithms' *least* benefit over CMY must occur at Uniform and their *greatest* at the most skewed distribution (Exponential, per the generator "each item has probability proportional to $\exp(-i)$", [PAGE 7]).
*Prediction:* within the reported 33%–80% synthetic reduction band ([PAGE 8]), Uniform should sit near 33% and Exponential near 80%; and across Figure 7's $k$-sweep, the gap should shrink fastest for Uniform.
*Veto condition:* if Uniform shows the *largest* gain, then the operative slack term is not the Bernstein $\sqrt{t}$ term (which is what generates the log-log contraction) and the mechanism narrative of Lemma 4.4 is wrong even if the bound is right.

**Composite falsification protocol.** All three vetoes are executable against the released artifacts (WorldCup HTTP, Dartmouth CRAWDAD, Uber Jan–June 2015; [PAGE 6] footnotes) with instruments the paper already specifies: (a) the alarm index vs $N$ to test *exactness*; (b) the per-round retention ratio $(N-N')/N$ to test *drift sensitivity*; (c) the message counter split by round to test *which* characteristic (slack, predicate, or $\ell$) is load-bearing. Veto A is the highest-value experiment because it is the only one that attacks the *correctness* invariant $\sum_i s_i\le N$ rather than the *efficiency* bound.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
