IMU portability permits field-based deployment outside the laboratory [PAGE 503], but the absence of environmental covariates means thermal effects on cuff pressure, blood flow, and muscle viscoelasticity are unmeasured veto candidates. In warm environments, cuff pressure readings may drift; in cold environments, muscle stiffness alters lunge kinematics independently of training. Because these were not controlled or reported, they remain unadjudicated external vetoes.

Veto proof: The study’s safety monitoring and exclusion criteria function as a veto architecture that prevented adverse events during six weeks of BFR [PAGE 505]. However, the lack of individualized Limb Occlusion Pressure (LOP) means the true degree of arterial versus venous occlusion varied across limb circumferences [PAGE 511], a biomechanical veto that was not experimentally removed. The design is therefore veto-resistant for safety but not veto-proof for dose precision.

Scenario 1 (Pressure miscalibration): Veto condition—if individualized Limb Occlusion Pressure (LOP) were measured via Doppler ultrasound and found to be below 130% of resting systolic blood pressure in any participant, the fixed protocol would impose complete or near-complete arterial occlusion rather than venous restriction [PAGE 511]. Verification: pre-intervention LOP measurement for all sixteen participants; flag any limb where LOP < 130% SBP. Prediction: participants with LOP below the fixed pressure would report paresthesia, pain, or premature fatigue, leading to session non-completion or dropout; the BFR group’s effect sizes would attenuate or become negative relative to GLE, and adverse events would emerge, contradicting the reported zero adverse events [PAGE 505].

Scenario 2 (Intensity decay without reassessment): Veto condition—if 1RM were reassessed at week three and rear-leg strength had increased by more than 15% over baseline, the fixed 30% BFR and 70% GLE loads would fall below intended relative intensities for the remaining three weeks [PAGE 511]. Verification: mid-intervention 1RM retest at week three for both groups. Prediction: the BFR group would be undertrained (stimulus below the low-load BFR hypertrophic threshold), causing effect sizes for kinematics and strength to decay toward zero by week six; the GLE group would experience reduced mechanical tension, potentially eliminating its strength advantage. Observed post-test differences would shrink, and the between-group effect for rear-leg strength (currently r=0.21, p=0.491 [PAGE 507]) would move closer to null.

Scenario 3 (Balance measurement ceiling): Veto condition—if static balance were reassessed with a more sensitive protocol (eyes-closed single-leg stance or Star Excursion Balance Test) rather than the eyes-open Wii balance board protocol used [PAGE 505], the null balance finding might reverse. Verification: administer eyes-closed single-leg balance and SEBT at pre- and post-test. Prediction: because collegiate fencers already possess sport-specific postural skill near a ceiling [PAGE 510], the eyes-open Wii measure lacks sensitivity; a dynamic or eyes-closed challenge would reveal between-group differences, with BFR showing superior proprioceptive acuity via increased knee joint position sense [PAGE 503], falsifying the current conclusion of “no meaningful effects” for balance.
