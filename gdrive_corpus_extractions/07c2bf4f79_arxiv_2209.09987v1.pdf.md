**The three-way contest.**

*Crisis habit regression.* Under distributional stress, the pipeline does not degrade gracefully; it **falls back to a scalar comparison**. The named instance is the fall detector: when the ST-GCN skeleton is incomplete, control passes to "the shape of the bounding box obtained in the robot detection phase is used" [PAGE 21]. The same regression appears in pass detection with the frozen band `50 < d < 70` [PAGE 25], in the illegal-defender polygon test `> 3` [PAGE 22], and in possession's strict `<` [PAGE 25]. A model of the operator's decision rule is therefore: *when evidence is weak, substitute a threshold that was tuned on one venue and never re-derived.* The consequence is that error is not random — it is **correlated with scene difficulty**, so any aggregate (a season heatmap, a possession percentage) is biased in the direction of the hard cases. A coach or referee reading `P_0 = 58 %` is reading a number whose error bar is a function of how often the referee's whistle stopped play near the ball.

*Counter-trap.* Each rescue is itself an attack surface.
- **Manual homography clicking** [PAGE 11] introduces an unauditable human parameter; the pipeline's reproducibility now depends on operator eyesight, and the two field frames in Figure 5 [PAGE 13] are shown but their residuals are not tabulated.
- **EMA appearance memory** [PAGE 17] is a counter-trap against occlusion, but with fixed `α` the memory horizon is `1/(1−α)` *frames* — so the same protection is 6.67× longer in wall-clock terms on the low-end tier, meaning the model clings to a stale appearance long enough for a robot to walk across the pitch.
- **The fallback aspect ratio** [PAGE 21] is a counter-trap against low resolution that is itself a function of camera geometry (`csc ψ`, E9) and therefore *most wrong exactly where the ST-GCN is most needed* — the far touchline.
- **One-shot K-Means association** [PAGE 23] is a counter-trap against GameController noise that freezes the error instead of averaging it out.

*Clock.* Two clocks fight here, and neither is reconciled.
1. **The frame clock.** Every temporal parameter in MARIO is indexed to frames: the 5-frame pass horizon [PAGE 25], the unreported death window `m` [PAGE 16], the EMA memory [PAGE 17], the ST-GCN temporal convolution window [PAGE 21]. The report's own hardware table makes the frame interval span `1/20 s` to `1/3 s` [PAGE 31]. Consequence: **no temporal statistic in MARIO is comparable across the three hardware tiers the report itself defines.** A pass count is a property of the laptop, not only of the match.
2. **The match clock.** Data association happens "in the first few frames of the game" [PAGE 23] — an irreversible commitment made at `t ≈ 0` with the least possible evidence and the most imprecise GameController data. Every later statistic is conditioned on that commitment. This is a clock-locked prior: information arriving at minute 8 cannot revise a decision made at second 3, because no re-association is described.

**Synthesis.** The recursive model of the pipeline's behaviour is: *commit early (association), commit hard (thresholds), commit late (fallback)*. The first commitment is made once and never audited; the second is never re-derived per venue; the third only engages after the primary model has already failed. The system's self-model therefore has no channel through which it can report "I am currently in the fallback regime" — no flag, no residual, no coverage statistic is emitted alongside any published number.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A **veto** here means: a lower-layer physical constraint that makes a higher-layer statistic *non-identifiable*, with no module in the pipeline able to detect the non-identifiability.

**V1 — Optical veto (physical).** The homography is exact only on `h = 0`; the operators measure objects at `h_robot ≈ 0.3 m` and `h_ball ≈ 0.035 m`. From E3, `ε = X_p h/(Z_c − h)`. For `Z_c` unstated (assumption A3), the bias is *unbounded in the report's own terms*: at `Z_c = 10 m` and `X_p = 10 m`, a standing robot is displaced `≈ 0.31 m` outward while the ball is displaced `≈ 0.036 m`. **Veto proof:** the possession operator compares `‖b − r_0‖` to `‖b − r_1‖`. Both robots receive the same sign of radial bias (both stand), so possession is *approximately* immune — but the heatmap superposition of robots and ball [PAGE 23–24] is not, and any "distance from ball to nearest robot" statistic is contaminated by ≈ 0.27 m of pure geometry at the field edge. Since the report never states `Z_c`, no reader can bound this. **Adjudication: VETO sustained for any absolute-distance statistic; NOT VETOED for relative possession.**

**V2 — Occlusion / association veto (biomechanical + optical).** SPL restarts place several 0.57-m bipeds inside a 1–2 m radius. IoU-based association (E4) cannot separate coplanar overlapping boxes of the same class, and the report concedes the family's failure: "a high number of ID switches and failures in case of occlusion" [PAGE 16]. With identity keyed to track ID [PAGE 24] and semantic binding frozen at `t ≈ 0` [PAGE 23], an occlusion-triggered switch re-labels a track permanently. **Veto proof:** the eight-module architecture contains *no* identity-consistency module — the modules listed on pages 6–7 are calibration, background subtraction, homography, tracking/localisation, pose, fall, illegal defender, and data-association/statistics. There is no re-identification check, no cross-validation of jersey number against pose, no anomaly flag. **Adjudication: VETO sustained on all per-ID statistics (heatmaps, trackmaps, per-player pass counts).**

**V3 — Pose-graph veto (biomechanical).** ST-GCN's normalisation `Z_{vu}` is computed from the *observed* subgraph. A missing limb changes `Z` and the fixed adjacency, biasing the posterior rather than merely widening it [PAGE 21]. NAO has no articulated torso and no soft tissue: "falling", "diving", and "getting up" differ mainly in the orientation of two rigid links, and in a 2D projection at ~7-px-scale features these are degenerate. **Veto proof:** the fall classifier's own fallback is aspect ratio, which by E9 equals `(W/L)·csc ψ(P)` and therefore carries a *guaranteed* nadir singularity and a large azimuthal anisotropy. **Adjudication: VETO sustained on the fall count as an absolute quantity; a per-stratum (by image row) reporting scheme would lift the veto.**

**V4 — Illumination / weather-adjacent veto (environmental).** SPL is indoor, but the relevant environment is *lighting transition*: bank switching, shutter/exposure adaptation, and moving shadows from personnel. IMBS-MT is non-recursive [PAGE 10] with no adaptation law, so a step `δI > κσ_m` marks the entire frame foreground (E7). A foreground-saturated frame yields spurious blobs; those blobs enter the tracker as detections and can satisfy the IoU gate for an existing track, corrupting the Kalman update rather than being rejected. **Veto proof:** no module tests foreground *fraction* against a plausibility bound, so a 100 %-foreground frame is accepted. **Adjudication: VETO sustained on any statistic computed without a foreground-fraction plausibility gate.**

**V5 — Resolution veto (physical, joint with V1).** Ball apparent diameter `d_px = f_{px} D/Z` (E11) is ≈ 7 px under A8. IoU at that scale is a step function of a single-pixel boundary error. The measured gap mAP@0.5 = 0.953 versus mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.822 [PAGE 30] is precisely this effect quantified — a 13-point collapse under strict localisation. **Adjudication: VETO sustained on shot/goal-line decisions, which are the statistics with the highest external stakes.**
