**4. Information-Theoretic Formulation**
Let \(P, Q\) be probability measures. Define \(\mu(E) = P(E) - Q(E)\). Then \(\|\mu\|_{TV} = \sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)| = \frac{1}{2} \int_X |p-q| d\nu\) for a dominating measure \(\nu\). The Hahn decomposition yields:

**Proof Integrity Check**: The proof assumes the existence of an infimum \(\beta\) over negative sets. This requires the collection of negative sets to be non-empty and bounded below. If \(\mu\) takes both infinities, \(\beta\) might be \(-\infty\), and the construction fails. Also, the step "let \(k_1\) be the smallest positive integer..." implicitly uses the axiom of choice to select such \(k_1\) and the corresponding \(E_1\). In constructive mathematics, this selection may not be possible without additional structure (e.g., computability). Thus, in algorithmic information theory, the Hahn decomposition may not be effectively computable from oracle access to \(\mu\), imposing a computational veto on its practical use.
