If this scheme were re-estimated weekly on the same population—say, recruits measured weekly over a month—the trap would be to assume that F(s) is fixed across weeks. Training, nutrition, illness, and seasonal temperature would shift Q(p) week to week. Galton's instruction to rub out the scaffolding [PAGE 1] removes the audit trail: once the perpendiculars are gone, one cannot distinguish a genuine population shift in strength from an artifact of changed binning, changed group count, or changed smoothing intensity. A second multi-week trap is the reification of the grade axis: the 37th grade this week need not correspond to the same physiological strength as the 37th grade next week if the population composition changes. Without preserving the raw perpendiculars (the individual or grouped measurements), the scheme becomes non-identifiable across time; the dotted curve appears stable while the underlying distribution has drifted. Level 0 (the measurer): records raw strength observations and sorts them into only seven groups, as on [PAGE 1]. Level 1 (the constructor): builds the scheme by raising perpendiculars at grades 37 and 70 to heights 70 lbs and 80 lbs, then joins them by straight lines, explicitly trading off speed against curve fidelity. Level 2 (the reader of the scheme): treats the resulting dotted curve as if it were nature itself, forgetting that Galton directs the rub-out of the scaffolding perpendiculars [PAGE 1], thereby erasing evidence of the coarse discretization. Level 3 (the analyst modeling the reader): recognizes that under deadline pressure the observer will regress to crisis habits—preferring fewer groups, faster construction, and a smoother-looking curve—exactly the failure mode Galton warns against when he says the seven-group trace "differs sensibly from a flowing curve" [PAGE 1].

The counter-trap is the opposite error: over-smoothing under the banner of "double minuteness" [PAGE 1], inventing differentiability, tail shape, and a central density from seven data points. The recursive loop closes on the clock: the scheme is advertised as enabling one "in a moment" to find the Grade [PAGE 1], so temporal pressure selects for coarse bins; the coarse bins produce a visibly angular trace; the analyst smooths it into a dotted curve; the reader mistakes the smooth curve for high-resolution truth; and the next observer, seeing the smooth curve, feels licensed to use even fewer groups because the output looks "flowing." The clock thus drives the information compression, and the compressed output becomes the justification for further compression. The theory-of-mind failure is that each level assumes the previous level preserved measurement provenance, when in fact the scaffolding was deliberately rubbed out.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Galton's scalar "Strength" [PAGE 1] is not an invariant physiological constant; it is an instrument-dependent reading. Grip or pull strength varies with dynamometer handle diameter, grip span, elbow angle, seated vs. standing posture, and whether the dominant or non-dominant hand is tested. If the seven groups were measured with different apparatus or protocols, the cumulative percentages F(70)=0.37 and F(80)=0.70 [PAGE 1] mix incommensurable measurement scales, and the resulting quantile curve Q(p) has no stable physical referent.

Biomechanical layer: strength output is bounded by finger-flexor force, tendon-pulley integrity, muscle fiber-type distribution, and hand anthropometry. Two populations with equal muscle quality but different hand sizes will yield different Q(p) curves even under identical effort. Treating strength as a single scalar therefore conflates morphology with contractile capacity, violating the assumption that the grade axis measures a homogeneous latent trait.

Weather veto: ambient temperature and humidity alter skin friction, evaporative cooling, and muscle contractility. Cold conditions below roughly 10 °C reduce grip strength; high humidity increases perspiration and slip; both shift the distribution of S_i leftward. If observations sorted into the seven groups were taken under different weather regimes, the pooled empirical CDF violates the i.i.d. assumption because the data are drawn from a mixture of climatic strata rather than one fixed F.

Veto proof: the scheme is admissible only if (a) the measurement protocol, apparatus, and posture are held constant across every observation entering the table on [PAGE 1]; (b) environmental covariates—temperature, humidity, time of day—are either constant or recorded and used to stratify the groups; and (c) the scalar "Strength" is explicitly defined relative to a fixed instrument so that 70 lbs and 80 lbs denote the same physical quantity in every group. If any condition fails, the perpendicular heights are not commensurable, no amount of "double minuteness" [PAGE 1] can recover a valid F(s), and the scheme receives a hard veto.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
