Veto condition: if skilled human forecasters also achieve ~70% empirical coverage at 90% nominal on these same questions, the LLM shortfall is characteristic of numerical forecasting generally rather than an LLM-specific pathology.

Weather layer: data assimilation and temporal dynamics. Descended from Richardson's numerical weather prediction lineage [PAGE 3], continuous forecasting ideally requires iterative data assimilation as new observations arrive. QuantSightBench uses a static corpus [PAGE 5] and explicitly notes it does not capture real-time information evolution or forecast updates [PAGE 9 limitations]. Veto condition: if resolution-critical information emerges after August 2025 and is absent from the corpus, the agentic setting cannot recover it; measured undercoverage then reflects data paucity rather than model miscalibration.

Cross-layer adjudication: The fixed corpus is veto-proof against temporal leakage but introduces a coverage floor set by corpus completeness. The breakdown regime is when ground truth depends on post-August-2025 events. In that regime, no amount of model calibration can achieve nominal coverage because the necessary evidence is structurally unavailable. The observed scale-dependent collapse at 100K+ [PAGE 13-14] operates independently of this veto and therefore constitutes a genuine model capability failure rather than a data artifact.

Scenario 1 (Temporal leakage veto). Counterfactual: the retrieval corpus is contaminated with articles published after each question's resolution date but before model inference. Ground truth becomes directly retrievable. Verifiable prediction: empirical coverage for all models would jump from the observed 61.5%–79.1% range to above the 90% nominal target, and MLIS would collapse as intervals narrow around leaked values. Verification: compare coverage on questions whose resolution dates fall within versus outside the January–August 2025 corpus window [PAGE 5].

Scenario 2 (Log-zero singularity veto). Counterfactual: the benchmark includes ground truth values y_i = 0 or negative quantities (e.g., signed financial balances or temperature anomalies) without an epsilon offset. Because MLIS applies log to l_i, u_i, and y_i [PAGE 6, Eq. 3], log(0) → -∞ and log(negative) is undefined over the reals. Verifiable prediction: aggregate MLIS becomes NaN or -∞ for affected questions, and if those instances are silently dropped, reported MLIS is biased downward. Verification: audit the 0–1 magnitude bin (n=5) [PAGE 13-14, Figure 7-8] for zero and sign values and recompute MLIS with and without exclusion.

Scenario 3 (Output-format veto). Counterfactual: a model ignores the required XML output format (<lower>, <median>, <upper>) [PAGE 15, Figure 10] and returns free text. The parser extracts no valid numerical interval. Verifiable prediction: coverage for that model on affected questions drops to 0% because no interval can contain y_i, MLIS is undefined, and the model is wrongly ranked as worst-calibrated despite possessing internally correct uncertainty reasoning. Verification: measure format compliance rate across models in the agentic setting and correlate non-compliance with coverage collapse.
