Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. Under performance crisis—such as a losing streak or a high-stakes knockout match—coaching staffs tend to regress to first-order, legible heuristics: tackle counts, pass totals, and distances covered. The source explicitly states that these conventional event parameters hardly deliver clues regarding the final outcome of a game [PAGE 15], yet they remain cognitively attractive because they are simple to communicate and historically familiar. This is crisis habit regression: under stress, the analyst-agent collapses the high-dimensional positional state space into a low-dimensional event summary.

The counter-trap operates at the second order. If Team B knows Team A has regressed to habitual event-data heuristics, Team B can pressure the exact passing lanes and spatial zones that Team A historically overuses, because Team A is no longer adjusting its cofigurations—directions, angles, speeds, and relative spacing [PAGE 9]—in response to real-time positional structure. Valentin Altenburg’s pre-2016 Olympic statement underscores this: the aim is to unsettle the opponent so frequently that even opposing analysts with 50-page glossy brochures cannot predict what will happen [PAGE 15]. Positional data are the counter to this trap because they expose the actual geometric state rather than the believed state.

The clock is the third recursive variable. The book promises that analysis based on positional data can be generated in just a few seconds [PAGE 15]. Short decision windows reduce the depth of recursive reasoning: instead of iterating “Team A thinks Team B thinks Team A will press high,” staff revert to zero-order habits. Thus the clock amplifies crisis habit regression, which in turn makes the counter-trap more effective. Breaking the recursion requires an admissible positional KPI layer that is (a) computationally available within the seconds-constraint and (b) vetted against physical and biomechanical vetoes, so that the team plays the observed cofiguration rather than the feared habit.

Biomechanical-layer veto. Computed velocity v_i(t) ∈ m/s and acceleration a_i(t) ∈ m/s² are bounded by human physiology.
