<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the authors as strategic actors whose forecast is performative, not merely descriptive. They write the field "will likely continue to increase... young scholars may consider this area as a promising research direction" [PAGE 5]. Level-1 belief: growth continues. Level-2 (their model of the reader): publishing an optimistic forecast *induces* entry, which self-fulfills the growth. The forecast is therefore partially endogenous — a reflexivity loop, not an independent measurement. Counter-trap: treating the 206/233/263 figures as neutral evidence when they function partly as recruitment.

Crisis-habit regression: the paper names a "crisis" of fragmentation [PAGE 2] yet documents that the field's own collaboration regressed to siloed habit — "internal cohesion, external isolation" [PAGE 8, 13], 10 of 12 clusters unlinked. Predicted remedy (more collaboration) collides with the incentive gradient the authors also record: siloing "may positively impact researchers' career development and funding" [PAGE 8]. So the rational individual actor keeps siloing even while endorsing collaboration rhetorically — a textbook collective-action gap. Their recursive model of *sports journals* is diagnostic: such venues "may lack the editorial expertise to assess highly technical AI-based methodologies" [PAGE 8], explaining why CS journals dominate and why cross-disciplinary co-authorship stays thin.

The clock: any real-time read of "impact" lags by the mean document age (~6.01 years) and by citation-lag bias [PAGE 14]. An actor optimizing for near-term bibliometric markers is thus misinformed about the newest work; an actor optimizing for field-entry is being courted by a performative forecast. Both clocks argue for discounting the headline optimism.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather constraints veto several "high-precision" claims, independent of the bibliometrics.

Veto 1 — Monocular scale/depth ambiguity. Any claim of precise "joint angle, direction of motion, and speed" from single-view CV [PAGE 12] is bounded by the pinhole invariance $(X,Y,Z)\sim(\lambda X,\lambda Y,\lambda Z)$: absolute metric length and out-of-plane joint angles are unrecoverable without a size reference, stereo, or a depth sensor. The paper's more reliable results (e.g., "multiple Kinect devices," ref 61) quietly rely on active depth sensing, confirming the veto on pure monocular pipelines. ACL/knee-valgus risk scoring [PAGE 11, refs 52–54] inherits this: 2D projection confounds out-of-plane rotation, so "precise... 3D joint angles" claims are conditionally valid only under calibrated multi-view capture.

Veto 2 — Latency vs event duration (the 3D+AI diving system). The system "captures body posture data within 1–2 s" [PAGE 10]. For a 10 m platform, free-fall time is $t=\sqrt{2h/g}=\sqrt{2\cdot10/9.81}\approx 1.43\ \text{s}$. A 1–2 s processing window equals or exceeds the entire aerial phase, so "real-time" here sits at the feasibility edge: feedback cannot precede landing for the shortest events. The claim survives only as post-hoc quantitative assessment, not in-flight correction.

Veto 3 — Weather/illumination for outdoor markerless CV. The paper proposes expansion to "surfing," football, martial arts [PAGE 10, 12]. Outdoor scenes inject rain, fog, glare, and low-light degradations that break the assumption of stable illumination and clean segmentation. Markerless pose estimation error grows monotonically as SNR falls; no algorithmic claim of "high precision" is weather-invariant. The paper concedes "high computational demands and costly hardware" [PAGE 12] but under-weights environmental stochasticity as a hard veto on accuracy guarantees.

Adjudication: the bibliometric conclusions (growth, concentration, siloing) are *not* vetoed — they are corpus facts. But every applied "high-precision real-time" claim in Sections 4.1–4.4 is conditionally vetoed unless (i) multi-view/depth sensing is present, (ii) event duration exceeds the processing window, and (iii) illumination is controlled. Where those fail, the claims reduce to qualitative or post-hoc.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
