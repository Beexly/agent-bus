If C is a compact subset of X, then π−1(C) is a compact subset of X; if E is a Baire set [or a Borel set] in X, then π−1(Ê) is a Baire set [or a Borel set] in X.” [PAGE 278] (The statement as OCR’d has a type slip: π^{-1} pulls back subsets of the quotient X/Y to subsets of X, so C should be read as a compact subset of X/Y.) Formal reconstruction from [PAGE 278], with SI/information units noted where applicable (this is pure topology and measure theory; no intrinsic SI units arise).

Let $(X,\cdot,^{-1},e,\tau_X)$ be a locally compact topological group, $Y\le X$ a closed subgroup, and $\pi:X\to X/Y$ the canonical quotient homomorphism,
\[
\pi(x)=xY .
\]
The quotient carries the quotient topology; $\pi$ is continuous and open, and every fiber is a coset,
\[
\pi^{-1}(\xi)=xY\cong Y .
\]
Assumption (compact fiber): $Y$ compact $\Rightarrow$ every fiber compact.

Assumption (bounded base): $U\subset X$ open with compact closure (“bounded,” [PAGE 278]). Define
\[
X_0 = UY = \{uy:u\in U,\ y\in Y\},
\]
and the text asserts $X_0$ compact.

Coset saturation operator:
\[
x_1\in X_0,\quad \pi(x_1)=\pi(x_2)
\;\Longrightarrow\; x_1^{-1}x_2\in Y
\;\Longrightarrow\; x_2\in X_0 .
\]
Neighborhood form: for every neighborhood $V\ni x_2$,
\[
Vx_2^{-1}x_1\ni x_1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
UY\cap Vx_2^{-1}x_1\neq\varnothing ,
\]
and, because $x_1^{-1}x_2\in Y$ and $V$ is arbitrary,
\[
x_2\in X_0 .
\]

Set-difference operator (reading “X0 − U” as $X0\setminus U$):
\[
X_0\setminus U = X_0\cap U^c .
\]
Since $U$ is open and $X_0$ compact, $X_0\setminus U$ is closed in $X_0$, hence compact. Images under the continuous open map $\pi$:
\[
\pi(X_0\setminus U)\ \text{compact},\qquad
\pi(C)\ \text{compact},
\]
and because $C$ is $Y$-saturated,
\[
\pi(X_0\setminus U)\cap\pi(C)
=\pi\bigl((X_0\setminus U)\cap C\bigr)
=\varnothing .
\]
In the Hausdorff quotient $X/Y$ (Hausdorff because $Y$ closed), disjoint compact sets are separated by opens. Hence there exists $D\subset X/Y$ open such that
\[
\pi(C)\subset D,\qquad
D\cap\pi(X_0\setminus U)=\varnothing,\qquad
D\subset\pi(U).
\]
Pullback:
\[
x\in\pi^{-1}(D)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\pi(x)\in D
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\pi(x)\notin\pi(X_0\setminus U)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x\notin X_0\setminus U .
\]
If additionally $x\in X_0$, then $x\in U$, giving
\[
C\subset\pi^{-1}(D),\qquad
\pi^{-1}(D)\cap X_0\subset U,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
C\subset\pi^{-1}(D)\subset U .
\]

Theorem B operator (compactness):
Given open cover $\mathcal K$ of $\pi^{-1}(C)$, for each $\xi\in C$ the fiber $\pi^{-1}(\xi)$ is compact, so $\exists$ finite $\mathcal K(\xi)\subset\mathcal K$ with
\[
\pi^{-1}(\xi)\subset U(\xi):=\bigcup\mathcal K(\xi).
\]
Theorem A (tube lemma) yields open $W_\xi\ni\xi$ in $X/Y$ such that
\[
\pi^{-1}(W_\xi)\subset U(\xi).
\]
Compactness of $C$ gives $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n$ with
\[
C\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^n W_{\xi_i}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\pi^{-1}(C)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^n U(\xi_i)
=\bigcup_{i=1}^n\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal K(\xi_i)}V ,
\]
a finite subfamily of $\mathcal K$. Hence $\pi^{-1}(C)$ compact.

Theorem B operator (Baire/Borel): Define
\[
\mathfrak A=\{E\subset X/Y:\pi^{-1}(E)\text{ is Baire [or Borel] in }X\}.
\]
$\mathfrak A$ is a $\sigma$-algebra. Since $\pi$ is continuous, every open $G\subset X/Y$ has $\pi^{-1}(G)$ open in $X$, hence Baire and Borel; thus $\mathfrak A$ contains all Borel sets, and the same argument gives Baire sets.

Breakdown / singularity regimes: (1) $Y$ non-compact $\Rightarrow$ fiber non-compact $\Rightarrow$ finite subcover of fiber fails. (2) $Y$ not closed $\Rightarrow X/Y$ non-Hausdorff $\Rightarrow$ no separating open $D$ guaranteed. (3) $\pi$ not open $\Rightarrow$ Theorem A tube unavailable. (4) $U$ not bounded / $X_0$ non-compact $\Rightarrow X_0\setminus U$ need not be compact and the separation argument collapses.

The specific operator $\pi:X\to X/Y$ with compact fibers and the tube-lattice proof of Theorem B echo Bourbaki’s Topologie Générale treatment of quotient mappings by compact equivalence relations, and they prefigure the modern locally compact group quotient-measure theorem in which $\pi$ preserves Haar measure up to a modular-function factor.

Multi-week traps embedded in this page. Trap A (type regression): a reader returning after a week sees “If C is a compact subset of X, then π^{-1}(C) is a compact subset of X” [PAGE 278] and stores $C\subset X$ rather than $C\subset X/Y$. Because $\pi^{-1}$ formally eats subsets of the quotient, that stored type error propagates into any later claim about measure preservation or disintegration, producing a false theorem. Trap B (compactness of the difference): the text treats $\pi(X0\setminus U)$ as compact because $X0$ is compact and $U$ is open. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis-habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock for the reader of [PAGE 278].

Crisis habit regression. Under time pressure, a mathematician regresses to the most superficially available inference: “preimage of compact is compact” is treated as a standalone fact about continuous maps. The regressed model skips the mediating structure—compact fibers, openness of $\pi$, and Theorem A’s tube—and concludes directly from continuity that $\pi^{-1}(C)$ is compact. That regression is exactly wrong: continuity alone gives compactness of images of compact sets, not preimages; the reverse requires the compact-fiber/tube machinery explicitly built on [PAGE 278].

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to force the three-stage proof object into working memory before accepting the conclusion: (i) fiber compactness ($\pi^{-1}(\xi)\cong Y$ compact), (ii) local trivialization via Theorem A (for each $\xi$ an open $W_\xi$ whose preimage lies in a finitely covered neighborhood of the fiber), and (iii) finite subcover of the base $C$. Only the composition of (i), (ii), and (iii) yields the finite subfamily of $\mathcal K$ covering $\pi^{-1}(C)$. Any crisis response that deletes stage (ii) is flagged as a trap.

Clock. The proof has an implicit temporal structure. The neighborhood argument for coset saturation is an “instant” point-topology step. The separation of $\pi(X0\setminus U)$ and $\pi(C)$ by an open $D$ is a short-window Hausdorff step. The finite subcover over $C$ in Theorem B is a long-horizon step: it requires quantifying over all of the compact base $C$, not just one fiber. A reader whose “clock” is set to the instant step will under-allocate verification to the long-horizon finite-subcover step and will miss that Theorem A must be invoked once per base point before compactness of $C$ collapses the infinite family to a finite one. The counter-trap is to re-sync the clock: verify the per-fiber finite cover, then the uniform tube, then the base finite subcover, in that order.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios (RLVR-style: each has a falsifiable prediction and a verification protocol).

Scenario 1 — Non-compact fiber veto. Counterfactual: drop the compactness of $Y$. Take $X=\mathbb R^2$ with addition, $Y=\mathbb R\times\{0\}$ (closed, non-compact), and $\pi(x,y)=y$ (quotient $\mathbb R^2/Y\cong\mathbb R$). Let $C=\{0\}\subset\mathbb R$, compact. Then $\pi^{-1}(C)=\mathbb R\times\{0\}$, which is closed and unbounded in $\mathbb R^2$, hence not compact by Heine–Borel. Prediction: the conclusion “$C$ compact $\Rightarrow\pi^{-1}(C)$ compact” is false. Verification: the open cover $\{(n-1,n+1)\times\mathbb R:n\in\mathbb Z\}$ of $\mathbb R\times\{0\}$ has no finite subcover. Veto triggered by violation of the compact-fiber assumption.

Scenario 2 — Non-Hausdorff quotient veto. Counterfactual: drop closedness of $Y$. Take $X=\mathbb R$ and $Y=\mathbb Q$ (dense, not closed). The quotient $\mathbb R/\mathbb Q$ with the quotient topology is indiscrete (only $\varnothing$ and the whole space are open), hence not Hausdorff. Every subset of an indiscrete space is compact, so take $C=\mathbb R/\mathbb Q$ itself (compact). But $\pi^{-1}(C)=\mathbb R$, which is not compact (cover by $(n-1,n+1)$ has no finite subcover). Prediction: compactness of $C$ in the quotient does not imply compactness of $\pi^{-1}(C)$. Verification: Heine–Borel failure of $\mathbb R$. Veto triggered by violation of the Hausdorff/separation assumption needed for the open set $D$ on [PAGE 278].

Scenario 3 — Non-open / discontinuous projection veto. Counterfactual: replace the continuous open quotient $\pi$ by an arbitrary discontinuous surjection $f:X\to Z$ between Polish spaces. There exist Borel sets $E\subset Z$ such that $f^{-1}(E)$ is not Borel in $X$ (standard descriptive-set-theoretic construction via a Borel isomorphism coding a non-Borel subset). Prediction: the clause “$E$ Borel $\Rightarrow\pi^{-1}(E)$ Borel” is false once continuity/openness of $\pi$ is removed. Verification: exhibit $f$ and Borel $E$ with $f^{-1}(E)\notin\mathscr B(X)$; membership is decided by the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, a countably generated measurable space in the second-countable case. Veto triggered by violation of continuity of $\pi$.
