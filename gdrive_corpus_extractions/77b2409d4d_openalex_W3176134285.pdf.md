But no AIC/BIC is reported and the authors rely solely on fit indices, which is under-specified given that non-nested comparison is not a χ² difference test.

**Hop 8 — Population composition gray zone.** From [PAGE 4]: total N = 299; soccer = 260, beach soccer = 14, basketball = 6, volleyball = 19. That is 87% soccer, 91% male (272 of 299). Power for basketball/volleyball sub-analyses is nominal only. Any generalization of the leadership → sacrifice path to volleyball or basketball is unsupported by the data, and the paper itself concedes [PAGE 8]: "due to the small number of female players, we did not consider gender differences."

**Hop 9 — Temporal clock.** Data were collected "at mid-season, before a training session" [PAGE 5]. Cross-sectional mediation cannot resolve temporal ordering: leadership → identification → sacrifice → performance could equally run backwards, be reciprocal, or be driven by a stable third variable (e.g., team success to date). The authors themselves state [PAGE 8] "as our findings were correlational and we used a cross-sectional design, we cannot make causal inferences between the constructs."

**Hop 10 — Synthesis.** The paper's conceptual arc is coherent within SIT; the load-bearing empirical vulnerabilities are: (i) SEM–correlation discrepancy, (ii) Abstract/Results model divergence, (iii) ICC-linked effective N, (iv) two-item outcome without reliability, (v) no model comparison statistics, (vi) skewed sample. Each is separately disqualifying in a strict replication regime, and together they justify the RLVR vetoes below.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper's intellectual genealogy runs through four convergent lineages, and each contains a *stale trap* that its own methodology inherits rather than resolves.

**Lineage 1 — Social identity theory.** Tajfel (1981) and Turner (1982) established the self-categorization framework; Haslam, Reicher & Platow (2011) extended it to leadership in *The New Psychology of Leadership* [PAGE 9]; Fransen et al. (2014–2020) operationalized it for sport. The paper stands squarely inside this lineage, quoting Drucker's "'Don't think "I". They think "team"'" as a verbal operator for the "us" construct [PAGE 3].

**Lineage 2 — Athlete leadership.** Loughead, Hardy & Eys (2006) defined the construct, and Fransen et al. (2014) decomposed it into four roles (task, motivational, social, external), now formalized in Table 1 [PAGE 2]. The paper groups all four into a second-order "general leadership quality" factor, which is a modeling move not directly tested by the original four-role literature. That collapse has not been validated across sports and is itself a multi-week trap if a coach or researcher treats the composite as four independent levers.

**Lineage 3 — Sacrifice and cohesion.** Prapavessis & Carron (1997) linked sacrifice to cohesion and to norm conformity; Cronin, Arthur, Hardy & Callow (2015) added transformational leadership mediation via "inside sacrifice"; Bandura, Kavussanu & Ong (2019) added authentic leadership. The current paper stitches these together into a leadership → identification → sacrifice → performance chain. **Trap 1:** The engagement of sacrifice as a mediator dates to Baron & Kenny (1986), Hayes (2009), and MacKinnon's bias-corrected bootstrap (2004) — all of which were specified for continuous, independently sampled data. The paper's own acknowledgment [PAGE 8] that "we cannot make causal inferences" is only partially honest: the *mediation language* is causally loaded even when the author hedges in the limitations. **Trap 2:** Cronin et al. (2015) used the same GSS scale with the same alpha/omega profile; the paper's replication of α = 0.89 is psychometrically consistent but reinforces a 25-year-old instrument whose validity in the *individual-inteam* versus *team-level* frame is still contested.

**Lineage 4 — Performance measurement.** Heuzé et al. (2006) used league standings; Dithurbide, Sullivan & Chow (2009) proposed team-referent performance self-report; Tenenbaum & Gershgoren (2011) legitimized subjective measures as "ecological and reliable" [PAGE 5]. The paper leans on this lineage to justify two one-item measures with no reported reliability. **Trap 3:** Since Podsakoff et al.'s common-method-variance critique (2003; extended 2012), the field has acknowledged that single-source perception data inflate shared variance between predictor and outcome. The paper neither reports a marker-variable test nor a Harman single-factor check.

**Multi-week traps that remain live:**

1. Cross-sectional "mediation" discourse — dormant since at least Maxwell & Cole (2007) but reactivated here because the title names "mediating role."
2. Composite "athlete leadership quality" treated as a single exogenous factor despite evidence [PAGE 2, see [PAGE 3] "we will focus on the general leadership quality grouping the best athlete leader in each of the four leadership roles"] that the four roles may have distinct functional signatures.
3. Perceived performance as redundant with sacrifice — the $r(\eta_2, \eta_3) = 0.40$ correlation plus the two-item outcome is at the edge of construct conflation. An evaluator who fails to distinguish "how much effort did I give" from "how well did the team do" cannot extract actionable inference from this paper.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The paper's epistemology is nested through three recursive mentalizing layers, and each layer compounds error in a distinctive way.

**Layer 1 — Player → leader mentalizing.** The measurement instrument asks each player to nominate the teammate who "best matched" each leadership role description; then rate: "To what extent do you think that this leader fulfills his role as leader well?" [PAGE 4]. This is a **second-order** mental state: the player is rating *their own belief* about *the leader's role fulfillment*. The paper does not measure the leader's self-perception, the coach's perception, or the peer-network centrality of the leader — all three of which are standard in Fransen et al.'s original 2014 work.

**Layer 2 — Researcher → player mentalizing.** The protocol [PAGE 5] required athletes to complete questionnaires "individually and without distractions or the presence of any person associated with the club environment." This is a tacit assumption that club-adjacent observers would bias disclosure — a counterfactual mental model of the respondent — but the paper does not test whether this instruction succeeded. No manipulation check was reported. The researcher must be mentalizing whether the player is mentalizing the club's evaluative gaze. This is the metacognitive leak.

**Layer 3 — Paper → reader mentalizing.** The Abstract [PAGE 1] is written for a reader who will not read the Results; it claims team identification mediates "inside sacrifice and perceived performance." The Results [PAGE 6] support only a *serial* mediation (ID → sacrifice → performance) plus a parallel leader → sacrifice → performance path. A reader who paraphrases from the Abstract will reconstruct a model not shown in Figure 1. This is a 20-year-old trap in the sport psychology literature (the "abstract amplification" problem) applied here in a specific, verifiable way.

**Crisis habit regression.** When a coach faces a mid-season losing streak, the conventional regression is: identify captains, mandate sacrifice, demand performance. The paper's own coefficients point to a different counter-trap: the largest path is sacrifice → performance (0.64), but the smallest coefficient is the leadership → identification link (a₁·d₁ = 0.10). A habit-regressed coach who intensifies leader mandates without building team identification will not walk the largest electromagnetic–coefficient channel; the counter-trap is to overload the leader-role construct — a mechanism this paper cannot support under its own numbers.

**The clock.** The cross-sectional design fixes the measurement to "mid-season, before a training session" [PAGE 5]. Sacrifice behaviors and perceived performance during a mid-season plateau differ from playoff or end-of-season dynamics. The paper's own Hypotheses 5a/5b [PAGE 4] presume an eager-forward causal clock (identification *precedes* sacrifice, which *precedes* performance), yet no temporal precedence was observed. An analyst who fails to freeze the clock at mid-season will over-extrapolate to playoff regimes — a recursive-Tom failure at the level of reading-the-reader.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The paper claims a causal arc from perception to performance. Three layers of physical/biomechanical/environmental evidence could each veto that claim, and none were probed.

**Biomechanical veto.** The 16-item inside-sacrifice scale includes items about "responsibilities I don't like" [PAGE 4] and personal sacrifice during practice and competition; the perceived-performance measure collapses team and individual judgments. Neither variable carries biomechanical content — no VO₂max, no training-load log (AU), no RPE, no injury count, no jump/vmax data. But the sample age range is 14–42 [PAGE 4], spanning adolescent maturation variance and adult performance variance that biomechanics literature treats as dominant drivers of performance outcomes. The paper's β = 0.64 for sacrifice → performance is not interpretable as a physical-performance claim, only as a perceptual claim. **Veto:** any inference that improving inside sacrifice will *physically* improve performance is unmediated and disproportional to the variance accounted for by maturation, training load, and injury — none of which is modeled.

**Weather veto.** The sample includes beach soccer (n = 14) [PAGE 4], an intrinsically weather-exposed sport. No environmental covariate — temperature, wind, sand state, precipitation — is reported. Sacrifice behaviors on a windy sand surface differ mechanically from sacrifice on a hardwood court (volleyball n = 19; basketball n = 6). The η₂ → η₃ path may be confounded by acute environmental load that the paper does not measure. **Veto:** the composite "performance" inference is not comparable across sports under uncontrolled weather; the sand/court distinction alone warrants cross-study replications before the composite is treated as a single construct.

**Physical-load veto.** No HRV, sleep, or training-load covariate was measured. The paper is therefore silent on whether the sacrifice → performance path survives after adjusting for acute fatigue or overtraining. The cross-sectional mid-season design specifically *cannot* rule out the interpretation that teams with high perceived sacrifice are simply teams in a low-fatigue window — a state-level confounder at the physical layer. **Veto:** the leadership → sacrifice path (β = 0.32) is physically unidentified; a within-team, repeated-measures design with load covariates would be required to sustain the causal reading.

**Summary judgment.** No physical, biomechanical, or environmental variable appears in Figure 1. All three layers are formally absent. Under the proposed adjudication rule, the paper's load-bearing inference (perceived leadership → perceived performance via sacrifice and identification) survives only as a **perception-layer** claim, not as a physical-performance claim.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto 1 — Cluster-adjusted CI fragility.**

*Setup:* Reported β(leadership → sacrifice) = 0.32 with 95% CI derived from MLR + COMPLEX. The COMPLEX correction inflates SEs proportionally to design effect; but with only 17 clusters, the $t$-distribution used for finite-sample correction is unreliable. Prediction: if the model were refit with a multilevel SEM (MSEM) at the team level using cluster-mean centering and with the ICC uniformly at its declared upper bound (0.37), the SE for β = 0.32 would widen by $\sqrt{\mathrm{DEFF}} = \sqrt{7.14} \approx 2.67$, and the 95% CI would become approximately [0.02, 0.62] — the lower bound touching the null. The path remains nominally significant (p < 0.05) but its practical range spans the boundary.

*Verification:* Refit the model as a two-level MSEM on the deposited dataset (raw data officially available per Data Availability Statement [PAGE 8]) and re-run with $k = 17$ cluster-robust and with a wild cluster bootstrap (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller).

**Veto 2 — Marker-variable / common-method attenuation.**

*Setup:* Leadership, identification, sacrifice, and performance are all collected from the same rater (the athlete) on the same occasion (mid-season). Podsakoff's single-source critique implies inflation of measured covariance relative to true covariance. The paper does not report a marker-variable test or a Harman single-factor check.

*Prediction:* Adding an unmeasured latent method construct (ULMC) or a marker variable (e.g., social desirability scale) would attenuate the composite SEM paths by a typical 15–30% range. Specifically, β = 0.32 would narrow to ≈ 0.22–0.27; β = 0.64 would narrow to ≈ 0.45–0.55; the serial indirect effect of 0.07 could cross zero at the low end of the attenuation band.

*Verification:* Administer the field's standard marker variable (e.g., the 10-item Marlowe-Crowne short form or the Lindell-Whitney marker) alongside the existing scales to a fresh sample of ≥ 300 athletes; refit and compare. Alternatively, Harman's single-factor test on the existing item pool would confirm whether a first factor explains > 50% of variance.

**Veto 3 — Objective-performance cross-validation.**

*Setup:* The perceived-performance scale is two one-item self-reports with **no reported α or ω** in Table 2 [PAGE 6] and a footnote admission [PAGE 5] that "there is no standardized and validated instrument to analyze performance in the sports context." The sacrifice → performance β = 0.64 is thus estimated with an outcome whose reliability is unknown.

*Prediction:* If the two-item perceptual outcome is replaced by or augmented with objective data (league standings, coach-rated performance, or match-level event data such as goals, assists, errors for soccer N = 260), the sacrifice → performance path narrows below β ≈ 0.25 and may cross null once objective and subjective outcomes are modeled as separate dependent variables. This would invalidate the serial mediation interpretation in Hypothesis 5b.

*Verification:* Re-run the SEM with objective performance as a distinct outcome using the raw dataset; if the objective path is non-significant and the perceived path remains 0.6+, the paper's conclusion reduces to a claim about perception of performance, not performance.

Each prediction is falsifiable, converges on the same dataset, and is bounded above by realistic correction magnitudes rather than arbitrary effect-size deflation.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
