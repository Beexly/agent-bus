Extreme traits (rare seeds) are less likely to populate the new island in significant numbers than common ones (common grasses). The "aspect" of the child island will be less extreme than the parent islands. This is the intuitive grasp of regression before the mathematical formalization.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s "Natural Inheritance" is the intellectual pivot point between Lamarckian fluid dynamics and Mendelian particulate genetics, though he ironically missed the latter. Historically, the lineage here is crucial:
1.  **Pre-Galton:** Blending inheritance was the dominant "fluid" model (like mixing paints). Traits were thought to dilute over generations.
2.  **The Galtonian Shift (Retroucausal Trap):** By using the *seed* metaphor, Galton intuitively adopted a *particulate* model without realizing it. He saw traits as discrete items transferred physically. However, he lacked the cytological evidence (chromosomes/meiosis) to formalize it. His "gray zone" was believing these particles still blended statistically to create regression, rather than segregating cleanly as Mendel later showed.
3.  **Multi-week/Multi-year Traps:** For decades after this text, the scientific community struggled to reconcile Galton's statistics (variation/regression) with Mendel's mechanics (discrete ratios). The trap was assuming statistical smoothing meant physical blending. It took the early 20th-century synthesis to realize that Mendel's discrete particles, when aggregated across populations, *produce* the very normal distributions and regression effects Galton observed. The "seeds" did not blend; their frequencies simply followed the Central Limit Theorem.
4.  **Genealogical Echo:** This page also plants the seed for modern *Epigenetics*. His explicit mention of "pre-natal circumstances" affecting the outcome alongside hereditary seeds anticipates the GxE (Gene-Environment) interaction models central to current biology. The "drifted earth" that forms the islets can be seen as the maternal uterine environment modifying the expression of the hereditary "seeds."
