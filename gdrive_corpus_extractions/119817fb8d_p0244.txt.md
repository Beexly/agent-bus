Scenario 1 — Constant vergence, variable retinal size. Apparatus: haploscope fixes vergence \(\nu\) via a stable fixation target while stimulus angular size \(s\) is varied. Verifiable condition: \(\Delta \nu = 0\), \(\Delta s \neq 0\). Prediction: the subject reports approach or recession in depth (\(\Delta d \neq 0\)) with no change in judged size (\(\Delta S = 0\)), replicating Wheatstone’s dissociation [PAGE 234]. Veto: any model asserting that size constancy is computed solely from convergence is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Identical retinal trajectory, voluntary versus passive generation. Apparatus: record the eye-position trajectory of voluntary pursuit of a moving dot, then replay it passively by gentle scleral pressure while the subject fixates a stationary point. Verifiable condition: same \(\boldsymbol{\rho}(t)\), different motor provenance (voluntary efference vs. passive external torque). Prediction: voluntary condition yields “a stationary field of view with one moving object”; passive condition yields “a sudden jump or twist of the same total field” [PAGE 234]. Veto: the model “retinal motion alone determines object motion” is rejected because identical retinal input produces opposite objective fields.

Scenario 3 — Filled versus empty interval at fixed endpoints. Apparatus: reproduce Fig. 63 [PAGE 234] with three lines whose extreme dots subtend identical angular separation \(\alpha\), varying only inter-dot fill \(c\). Verifiable condition: \(\Delta \alpha = 0\), \(\Delta c \neq 0\). Prediction: judged extent \(\Phi_{\text{filled}} > \Phi_{\text{empty}}\), with a signed illusion magnitude \(\Delta \Phi > 0\) measurable in radians. Veto: any strictly linear retinal-extent model \(\Phi = k\alpha\) is falsified. Each scenario produces a binary veto outcome (model survives / model vetoed) and a quantitative prediction (\(\Delta d\), \(\Delta S\), \(\Delta \Phi\)) that can be logged for RLVR reward shaping.
