<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
We formalize Tolman's concepts using Information Theory (Shannon units/bits) and Probabilistic Graphical Models, avoiding any fake ratios.

**1. Sign-Gestalt Readiness Operator ($\mathcal{R}$)**
Let $S$ be the set of environmental signs (stimuli) and $E$ be the set of possible ends (goals). A means-end readiness is a conditional probability distribution over actions $A$ given a sign-gestalt $G$:
$$ P(A | G, E) = \frac{\exp(-\beta \cdot D(G, E))}{Z(E)} $$
Where $D(G, E)$ is the perceived distance/metric between the current sign-gestalt and the goal, $\beta$ is an inverse temperature parameter related to decision noise, and $Z(E)$ is a normalization constant. Units: Distance in meters (SI), Probability dimensionless. Breakdown regime: When $D(G, E)$ exceeds the sensory range or cognitive map resolution.

**2. Multiple Trackness & Final Common Pathness**
Modeled as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) where nodes are locations/states and edges are paths. Multiple trackness implies multiple distinct paths $P_i$ leading to the same final common path $P_f$.
$$ I(P_i; P_f) > 0 $$
Mutual Information $I$ measures the dependency between knowing the initial track and the final path. Assumption: The animal infers the shared structure. Singularity: Occurs when paths diverge completely after a junction, breaking the common path expectation.

**3. Direction-Distance Correlation**
Defined as the covariance between perceived direction vector $\vec{d}$ and distance scalar $r$:
$$ \text{Cov}(\vec{d}, r) = E[(\vec{d} - \bar{\vec{d}})(r - \bar{r})] $$
Units: Meters squared (m²). This operator quantifies how well direction cues predict distance to goal. Breakdown: In featureless environments where directional cues are ambiguous.

**4. Closure Principle (Gestalt)**
Formalized as minimizing the free energy of the perceptual model given incomplete data. If $X$ is observed trajectory and $Y$ is the unobserved completion:
$$ Y^* = \arg\min_{Y} F(X, Y) $$
where $F$ combines prediction error and prior complexity. Assumption: The brain favors simple, continuous paths (Occam's razor). Regime: Fails under high entropy/noise where no simple closure exists.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman's "Means-End-Capacities" emerge from a retrocausal lineage tracing back to Aristotelian teleology, filtered through Kantian epistemology and early 20th-century Gestalt psychology. Historically, behaviorism (Watson, 1913) aggressively suppressed purposive explanations, reducing behavior to S-R bonds strengthened by reward (Thorndike's Law of Effect). Tolman's work in the 1930s was a direct counter-movement, reintroducing cognition ("cognitivism") into psychology without invoking unobservable mental states directly, but rather inferred from behavioral flexibility.

The specific terms—"Multiple Trackness," "Final Common Pathness"—derive from maze learning experiments (e.g., Buel, Gilhousen, Hsiao) conducted at UC Berkeley. These experiments were designed to trap S-R theorists: if rats could suddenly take novel shortcuts (multiple tracks) or recognize equivalent paths (final common path), it proved they had formed a "cognitive map" (a term coined by Tolman in 1948, rooted in these 1932 findings).

**Multi-week traps:** Modern neuroscience often falls into the trap of reifying Tolman's "capacities" as localized neural modules (e.g., "place cells" for direction, "grid cells" for multiple tracks). However, Tolman's framework is functional and phenomenological, not anatomical. The trap lies in assuming a 1:1 mapping between cognitive capacity and cell type, ignoring the distributed, dynamic nature of neural coding. Furthermore, contemporary reinforcement learning (RL) models often mimic Tolman's results but interpret them purely algorithmically, missing the *purposive* intent—the organism's active construction of meaning (sign-gestalt).

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Analyzing Tolman's text through a recursive theory of mind lens reveals a layered crisis in understanding animal cognition.

**Crisis Habit Regression:** The default S-R model assumes habits are rigid chains (A→B→C). When an animal encounters a blocked path, S-R predicts perseveration (repeating failed response). Tolman's "Reverse Ends" and "Multiple Trackness" demonstrate regression failure: the rat does not repeat; it redirects. This crisis forces a shift from habitual processing to goal-directed planning.

**Counter-Trap:** Experimentalists (like Tolman) set up mazes to trap S-R predictions. For example, in a "shift" experiment, if the goal moves, an S-R subject would go to the old location (response habit), while a cognitive mapper goes to the new location relative to cues (place habit). The "counter-trap" is that animals often perform intermediate strategies, showing both habits and maps co-exist. Tolman’s "Mutual Alternativeness" (greater in cats/dogs/monkeys) suggests higher-order metacognition: the animal knows it has choices and can evaluate alternatives, a trap for simple RL models that optimize only immediate reward.

**Clock:** The temporal dimension is critical. Tolman’s "readinesses and expectations" imply a prospective clock—the animal simulates future outcomes. In crisis (blocked path), the internal clock resets, triggering a search process governed by sign-gestalt memory. The speed of redirection correlates with the strength of the cognitive map, not just habit strength. Thus, behavior is temporally extended, not instantaneous.

**Physical/Biomechanical Veto:** A rat’s body size and limb kinematics impose constraints on turning radii and obstacle clearance. However, Tolman’s experiments control for this by using consistent apparatus dimensions across conditions. If a rat chooses a longer but wider path due to biomechanics, it’s still a purposive choice mediated by its map, not a veto of cognition. True veto occurs only if injury prevents execution entirely (e.g., paralysis), which is outside the scope of intact purposive behavior.

**Weather/Environmental Noise:** Wind might disperse olfactory cues, disrupting sign-detection. But Tolman’s "sign-gestalt" includes multisensory integration (visual, tactile). If wind consistently alters cue reliability, the rat updates its expectation (learned irrelevance), demonstrating adaptive cognition rather than a breakdown. Only catastrophic weather (flooding) that destroys the environment itself constitutes a veto, as the "ends" cease to exist.

**Proof:** In Tolman’s "Reverse Ends" experiment, if a maze arm is rotated 180°, the rat still navigates correctly if distal cues remain stable. This shows spatial cognition overrides local geometric changes—a veto of pure idiothetic (self-motion) navigation. Thus, cognitive maps are robust to moderate physical perturbations, but not to complete sensory deprivation.
