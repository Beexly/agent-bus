(iv) The A/B decomposition on the weather datasets uses *"residual masks ... by comparing the ground-truth image with an A-only rendering"* [PAGE 7] — GT-supervised segmentation, which is unavailable in real deployment; the reported 28.15/35.00/28.51 dB inherit that privilege. (vi) FPS is reported without resolution normalization in the main text; the supplementary corrects this at [PAGE 13] with *"MPix/s = FPS·H ·W/10^6"*, giving 136.4 / 115.9 / 124.2 MPix/s versus 52.5 MPix/s for 4DGS.

The scalability thesis (fixed-$K$ archive) is proven by Table 3 and my byte-level reconstruction. The *empirical quality* thesis is overstated by budget and test-split confounds. The *practical pipeline* thesis is contradicted at $t_0$ by GT initialization.

**O1. Multi-view capture operator (Eq. 1, [PAGE 3]).**
$$\mathcal{I}_t=\{\,I_t^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}\,\}_{i=1}^{N},\qquad \pi_i=(K_i,R_i,t_i),\quad R_i\in SO(3),\; t_i\in\mathbb{R}^3$$
Units: $I$ in relative radiance (dimensionless $[0,1]$ after tonemapping), $K_i$ in px, $H,W$ in px. *Breakdown:* the operator is undefined if $\{R_i,t_i\}$ are not common across $t$ (moving rig) — the authors concede this at [PAGE 8]: *"our method is tailored to static camera rigs and extensions to moving or zooming cameras, or to continuously changing intrinsics are not directly addressed."*

**O2. Fixed-budget Gaussian field (Eqs. 2–4, [PAGE 3]).**
$$\mathcal{G}_t=\{g_{t,k}\}_{k=1}^{K},\quad g_{t,k}=(\mu_{t,k}\in\mathbb{R}^3,\;\Sigma_{t,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}_{\succ0},\;\alpha_{t,k}\in(0,1),\;b_{t,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times16})$$
$$\Sigma_{t,k}=R(q_{t,k})\,\mathrm{diag}(s_{t,k}^{2})\,R(q_{t,k})^{\top},\qquad q_{t,k}\in S^3,\; s_{t,k}\in\mathbb{R}^3_{+}$$
Units: $\mu$ in m (Blender scene units), $\Sigma$ in m², $\alpha$ dimensionless, $b$ in radiance (W·sr⁻¹·m⁻² if physically calibrated). *Singularity regime:* $\Sigma_{t,k}$ loses positive-definiteness as any $s_{t,k,j}\to0$; then $\Sigma^{-1}_{t,k,2D}$ in O4 diverges. The guard is stated at [PAGE 4]: *"together with clamping $s_{t,k}$ to a valid interval,"* combined with the $\lVert s_{t,k}\rVert_2^2$ term in Eq. (16). Note the regularizer *penalizes large scale but does not repel $s\to0$*; the clamp is the only singularity barrier.

**O3. Archive information rate.** For fixed $K$ and per-Gaussian payload $b=59$ fp32 $=236$ bytes (verified against Table 3: $23.65\text{ MB}/10^5 = 236.5$ B),
$$\mathcal{A}=bKT\ \text{[bytes]},\qquad \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{info}}=\frac{bK}{\Delta t}\ \text{[B\,s}^{-1}\text{]}.$$
With $\Delta t = 1/30$ s, $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{info}} = 100{,}000\times236\times30 = 7.08\times10^{8}$ B·s⁻¹ $\approx 5.66$ Gbit·s⁻¹. *Breakdown:* the linear-in-$T$ law is only valid because Eq. (19) [PAGE 4] enforces $\lvert\mathcal{G}_t\rvert=K\;\forall t$; it fails immediately under densification (see O8).

**O4. Projection and screen-space covariance (Eqs. 5–8, [PAGE 4]).**
$$\tilde\mu^{(i)}_{t,k}=R_i\mu_{t,k}+t_i,\quad u^{(i)}_{t,k}=\Pi\!\left(K_i\tilde\mu^{(i)}_{t,k}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^2,\quad \Sigma^{(i)}_{t,k,2D}=J\!\left(R_i\Sigma_{t,k}R_i^{\top}\right)\!J^{\top}$$
Units: $\tilde\mu$ in m; $u$ in px; $J=\partial \Pi/\partial \tilde\mu \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times3}$ in px·m⁻¹; $\Sigma_{2D}$ in px². *Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (a) $\tilde\mu_z \le 0$ — Gaussian centre at or behind the camera plane, $\Pi$ undefined, $\Sigma_{2D}\to\infty$; (b) $|\tilde\mu_z|<\varepsilon$ — $J\sim f/\tilde\mu_z^{2}$ diverges, footprint exceeds the raster tile and the kernel is numerically rank-deficient; (c) $\Sigma_{2D}$ rank-deficient if the 3D covariance is flattened along the viewing ray, i.e. the Gaussian is edge-on. None of these are culled explicitly in the stated formulation.

**O5. Kernel, alpha, transmittance, composite (Eqs. 8–11, [PAGE 4]).**
$$G^{(i)}_{t,k}(p)=\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 (p-u^{(i)}_{t,k})^{\top}(\Sigma^{(i)}_{t,k,2D})^{-1}(p-u^{(i)}_{t,k})\right),\quad a=\alpha G$$
$$\hat I^{(i)}_t(p)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}T^{(i)}_{t,k-1}(p)\,a^{(i)}_{t,k}(p)\,c^{(i)}_{t,k},\qquad T^{(i)}_{t,k}(p)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-a^{(i)}_{t,j}(p)\right)$$
Units: $T\in[0,1]$ dimensionless, $c$ in radiance. *Breakdown:* the product form requires strict depth ordering; incorrect sort order produces non-monotone $T$ and energy non-conservation. With $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $G\le1$, $a<1$ so $T>0$ — no negative-transmittance singularity, provided the sigmoid parameterization of $\alpha$ is respected.

**O6. Per-time objective and regularizer (Eqs. 15–16, [PAGE 4]).**
$$\mathcal{L}_t(\Theta_t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{img}}\!\left(\hat I^{(i)}_t(\Theta_t),I^{(i)}_t\right)+\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\mathcal{R}(\Theta_t),\qquad \mathcal{R}(\Theta_t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\lVert s_{t,k}\rVert_2^2+\alpha_{t,k}^2+\lVert b_{t,k}\rVert_2^2\right)$$
Units: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{img}}$ dimensionless (Charbonnier with $\epsilon$ in radiance, or $\ell_1$ in radiance); $\mathcal{R}$ in m² (mixed-unit sum — a dimensional inconsistency inherited from standard 3DGS practice, not introduced here). *Breakdown:* $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\to0$ admits explosion of $s$; $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$ too large collapses Gaussians to points, and with densification disabled the field cannot recover the lost solid angle — this is the *fixed-budget collapse* regime.

**O7. Warm-chain recursion (Eqs. 17–18, [PAGE 4]).**
$$\Theta^{(0)}_t\leftarrow\Theta^{\star}_{t-1},\; t=t_0+1,\dots,T\qquad\text{or}\qquad \Theta^{(0)}_t\leftarrow\Theta^{\star}_{t+1},\; t=T-1,\dots,t_0$$
Propagated error: $e_{t+1}\le L\,e_t+\eta_t$ with $L=\lVert \partial F/\partial\Theta\rVert$ at the optimum, $e$ in parameter units, $\eta$ in parameter units. *Stability condition:* $L<1$. *Empirical estimate of the basin:* the distant warm-start probe gives $\Delta\mathrm{PSNR}=-15.70$ dB at large lag $\Delta$ [PAGE 13], i.e. the map is contracting only within a neighbourhood whose radius is smaller than the displacement accumulated over $\Delta \gg 1$ frames. *Breakdown regime:* $L\ge1$ (fast articulation, camera jitter, sync drift) ⇒ error accumulates monotonically, matching the authors' own caveat at [PAGE 8]: *"this can lead to accumulation of error over time due to warm-chain setting."*

**O8. Densification growth operator (Table 3, [PAGE 7]).**
$$K^{(S1)}_{n}=K_0+n\,\Delta K,\qquad \Delta K\approx1.67\times10^{5}\ \text{splats·frame}^{-1},\; K_0=1.884\times10^{5}$$
Fit residuals: $+167{,}393,\;+168{,}988,\;+166{,}585,\;+154{,}735$ — consistent with linear, incompatible with exponential ($r=1.456$ would yield $\sim2\times10^{19}$ splats at $n=119$). *Consequence:* $M^{(S1)}_{n}=bK^{(S1)}_{n}\approx 4.75$ GB at $n=119$, $\mathcal{A}^{(S1)}\approx 570$ GB over 120 frames. Gives 136.4 (DWS), 115.9 (SPK), 124.2 (SMP) MPix/s at $1920\times1080$ on A40, versus 52.5 MPix/s for 4DGS at $800\times800$ on RTX 3090. The authors correctly qualify: *"these numbers remain indicative only, as GPU architecture and implementation details differ across works"* [PAGE 13].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
**Lineage of the "geometry-over-time" argument.** The paper's thesis has a long prehistory that predates neural rendering by two decades. Seitz & Dyer's *View Morphing* [44] established that a camera pair constrains a view manifold geometrically; Buehler et al.'s *Unstructured Lumigraph* [10] formalized the "enough views make the reconstruction well-posed" argument; Hartley & Zisserman [19] supplied the rigidity theorem that the authors cite at [PAGE 1] — *"the scene at any given time t is already well-posed under principle of multi-view rigidity in projective geometry [19]."* The retromodern move in this paper is to re-import that classical count-the-constraints logic into a representation that was invented specifically to *escape* explicit geometry. The causal arrow is reversed relative to the 2021–2024 literature: NeRF [37] → Instant-NGP [38] → 3DGS [26] each added capacity to model the scene; this paper removes capacity (densification off, $K$ fixed, [PAGE 4] Eq. 19) and compensates with a *capture* assumption rather than a *model* assumption.

**The temporal-coupling research program as a multi-year attractor.** D-NeRF [42], Nerfies [40], HyperNeRF [41], Neural Scene Flow [30], HexPlane [11], K-Planes [16], Tensor4D [45], then 4DGS [51], D-3DGS [36], ST-GS [32], GaussianFlow [33], FreeTimeGS [50] — that is a five-year arc of increasingly elaborate canonical-space/deformation machinery. The paper positions itself at [PAGE 3] as a direct rebuttal: *"Unlike recent dynamic 3DGS methods [36, 51] that enforce temporal coherence via temporal coupling (e.g., deformation fields or canonical templates), we argue that in synchronized multi-view settings typical of sports, the scene at each time step is already strongly constrained by calibration and multi-view coverage."* Retrocausally, this reframes the entire deformation-field lineage as a *compensation for monocular capture* — i.e., the canonical-space machinery was never wrong, it was solving a different, harder problem (causal/monocular), and the field generalized it to synchronized rigs where it is redundant. That reframing is the paper's most consequential conceptual contribution, and it is defensible.

**The benchmark-fragmentation trap (multi-year).** The second contribution targets a persistent institutional pathology. [PAGE 3] is blunt: *"Differences in coordinate systems, camera intrinsics, temporal synchronization, and export formats often complicate prompt and just comparison between NeRF-based and 3DGS-based methods,"* and further notes NeRFstudio [47] *"is limited to rigid scenes"* while CMU Panoptic [23] supplies captures but not generation pipelines. This is a real multi-year trap: the field has repeatedly spent six-to-twelve months of engineering reconciling COLMAP-vs-OpenGL axis conventions before producing a single number. The Blender API's specific antidote — *"normalized to an OpenCV/COLMAP-style world-to-camera convention via explicit axis-conversion matrices"* and *"train/validation/test splits are deterministically assigned by camera name/suffix"* [PAGE 5] — is a governance fix as much as a software fix.

**The densification trap as a multi-week experimental hazard.** The S1/S2/S3 ablation [PAGE 7–8, PAGE 14] documents a failure mode that recurs across the field: densification produces monotone PSNR gains (Table 7: 28.386 → 33.818 dB across 15 frames for S1) while silently inflating capacity. The paper names it correctly at [PAGE 8]: *"enabling densification leads to continuous accumulation of Gaussians splats for the dynamic players over time, thus rapidly inflating the model capacity."* A practitioner who tunes on a 15-frame window will *always* select S1. The trap only detonates at frame ~50–120. This is the archetypal multi-week trap: the signal that discriminates the right choice lives outside the evaluation horizon.

**Retrocausal debt and inherited assumptions.** The paper inherits from 3DGS [26] the fp32/SH-degree-3 payload, the tile-based rasterizer, and the $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$ mixed-unit regularizer (Eq. 16 mixes m² with dimensionless terms). It inherits from TACV [57] the camera layouts and splits. And it inherits from the entire sports-broadcasting framing [56] the unstated assumption that the query time $t$ must be *exactly* a rendered frame instant — retrospective NVS on a discrete grid, not continuous time. That discreteness is doing enormous load-bearing work: it is what makes per-time optimization tractable and it is what makes "retrospective NVS" a lookup (`load Θ⋆_t`) rather than an interpolation. The architecture would need re-derivation for sub-frame queries.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**The community's crisis habit: add capacity when quality drops.** The reproducing reflex in dynamic NVS is: PSNR stalls → add a deformation MLP → add a canonical space → add a time-latent. This paper's crisis-habit regression is exactly the opposite direction — hold $K$ fixed, hold the loss fixed, hold densification off, and accept a 2–5 dB PSNR deficit relative to a densifying agent. The theory-of-mind bet is that reviewers trained on the capacity-inflation curve will read Table 4 [PAGE 8] as a *loss*: S1 reaches 31.253 dB at frame 5 while S2 reaches 28.221 dB, a 3.03 dB gap. The authors anticipate this and pre-empt it explicitly: *"While S1 achieves consistently better PSNR and LPIPS metrics, this empirical improvement is entirely driven by its continuously expanding number of splats and model capacity, which we have shown to be highly impractical for longer sequences"* [PAGE 8]. The counter-trap is to pre-commit to a *capacity-matched* comparison — but that experiment is never run, and a reviewer will notice.

**The counter-trap the authors fear most: "isn't warm-start just implicit temporal coupling?"** This is the sharpest recursive objection, and Section 8.2 [PAGE 13] is engineered wholly to answer it. The authors' defense — that a temporally distant checkpoint destroys performance — is a clever *reductio*: if warm-start encoded implicit temporal structure, the encoding would be time-local by construction. Their quantitative answer is $\Delta\mathrm{PSNR}=-36.94\%$. But the recursive objection recurses once more: a *basin of attraction* argument and a *temporal coupling* argument make identical predictions on this two-point probe. Distinguishing them requires the lag sweep, which is absent. The authors know this; the hedged phrasing *"supports the interpretation that"* [PAGE 13] rather than *"proves"* is the tell.

**The clock: three distinct clocks that reviewers will conflate.** (i) The *archive clock* — $T$ discrete time instants, at which the model is evaluated and stored. (ii) The *optimization clock* — 8,000 iterations per frame [PAGE 7], constant by design, which is what makes the method's train-time claim honest. (iii) The *rasterization clock* — the per-iteration wall-time, which is *not* constant (12.34 → 34.50 ms, [PAGE 14], Table 8) despite constant $K$. A reviewer reading *"stable rendering throughput (constant Gaussian budget)"* [PAGE 5] will expect clock (iii) to be flat; it is not, and the supplementary quietly documents a ×2.80 drift. The recursive failure mode is that the method's headline scalability property (clock ii flat) gets over-attributed to clock (iii).

**Predicted reviewer objections and author-side resources.** (1) *"GT-init at $t_0$ contradicts the SfM framing"* — anticipated partly by the Limitations section [PAGE 8], which names sparse/reflective/textureless initialization as the weak point, but not resolved by any experiment. (2) *"8k iterations vs 130K is not a fair fight"* — no defense in the text; the honest defense would be a matched-budget rerun. (3) *"4 test views out of 100"* — the deterministic camera-name split [PAGE 5] is offered as a reproducibility virtue, not as a coverage remedy, and would not satisfy a reviewer worried about near-view inflation. (4) *"only synthetic data"* — the rebuttal is the Blender framework itself, which is the deliverable that survives regardless of the numbers.

**The deployment-side mind.** For the practitioner who actually wants retrospective sports replay, the recursive model is different: they do not care about 3 dB; they care about whether the archive for a 90-minute match at 30 fps fits on a disk array. Using O3, $5{,}400$ s × 30 fps $= 1.62\times10^5$ frames × 23.65 MB = **3.83 TB** per event at $K=10^5$ — that is a real, computable deployment constraint that the paper gestures at (*"desirable for long sports events"* [PAGE 5]) but never quantifies. This number, not PSNR, is the paper's true contribution to the domain.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer L1 — Physico-optical (synchronization and shutter).** The method's entire geometric warrant is simultaneous exposure. The authors concede the veto condition themselves at [PAGE 8]: *"small errors in intrinsics, extrinsics, rolling-shutter effects, or sub-frame asynchronization can lead to multi-view inconsistencies that may degrade reconstruction quality and, this can lead to accumulation of error over time due to warm-chain setting."* Formalizing: let $\delta t_{\mathrm{sync}}$ be inter-camera temporal skew and $v_{\max}$ the peak surface speed of the subject. The induced world-space inconsistency is $\Delta x_\pi = v_{\max}\,\delta t_{\mathrm{sync}}$. **Veto threshold:** $\Delta x_\pi \gtrsim \sigma_{g,\min}$, the smallest Gaussian axis in O2. For a human limb at $v_{\max}\approx 8$ m·s⁻¹ and a Gaussian scale of $\sim5$ mm, veto triggers at $\delta t_{\mathrm{sync}} \gtrsim 0.6$ ms. Broadcast rigs commonly achieve 1–10 ms skew. **This layer vetoes real-rig deployment, not the synthetic results.** Rolling shutter adds a second term: intra-frame skew of up to one readout period, which the paper correctly flags.

**Layer L2 — Geometric (calibration drift and rigidity scope).** The rigidity claim of O11 is *per-time, per-articulated-part*. It holds exactly and is veto-proof in the synthetic regime: Blender exports exact intrinsics and extrinsics, so $\varepsilon_\pi \equiv 0$. In the real regime, residual reprojection error $\varepsilon_\pi$ (typically 0.3–1.0 px for a well-calibrated rig) enters the per-time solve as an effective noise floor on depth, $\sigma_z \approx \varepsilon_\pi\, z^2/(f\,B)$ with $B$ the baseline. **Veto condition:** $\sigma_z \gtrsim$ the depth extent of the subject at the far cameras. For $z = 30$ m, $f = 2000$ px, $B = 10$ m, $\varepsilon_\pi = 1$ px: $\sigma_z \approx 1\times900/(2000\times10) = 0.045$ m — fine. Increase $z$ to 80 m with the same rig and $\sigma_z \approx 0.32$ m — that is roughly one limb width, i.e. **veto** for stadium-scale far-side cameras. The stadium layout described at [PAGE 12] is precisely the geometry that triggers this.

**Layer L3 — Biomechanical (the local-smoothness premise).** The warm chain in O7 presumes $L<1$, which the paper justifies as *"dynamic motion in sports is typically locally smooth between adjacent frames"* [PAGE 2]. But sport contains genuine discontinuity events: ball strike, foot-plant, direction reversal, and the ball itself. **Veto condition:** $\lVert \Delta x_{\mathrm{subject}}\rVert / \sigma_g \gtrsim \kappa$, where $\kappa$ is the basin radius implied by the distant-warm-start decay. Using that probe as a crude calibration, the basin collapses somewhere between $\Delta\approx1$ and $\Delta\approx40$ frames — so $\kappa$ is likely on the order of a few centimetres to tens of centimetres per frame. A penalty-kick foot strike at $25$ m·s⁻¹ under a $1/30$ s sampling interval displaces $\approx0.83$ m — orders of magnitude beyond $\kappa$. **This layer vetoes the S-PK claim specifically**, yet S-PK posts 44.59 dB [PAGE 6] — which is only explicable if (a) the rendered sampling rate is far above 30 fps, or (b) the ball/foot region is a small pixel fraction. The paper does not report $\Delta t$, so this veto cannot be adjudicated from the text. That silence is itself the most important audit finding in the paper.

**Layer L4 — Meteorological/radiometric (weather and lighting).** The three weather datasets (Cloudy/Snow/Sunny, [PAGE 7], Table 2) are the paper's only weather layer, and they expose a genuine failure mode. The A/B decomposition depends on *"construct[ing] residual masks for each camera view by comparing the ground-truth image with an A-only rendering, followed by RGB differencing, thresholding, and simple morphological refinement"* [PAGE 7]. The authors state the veto condition explicitly: *"In practice, this A/B separation performs best when both components share identical camera parameters and similar lighting conditions; otherwise, the residual masks may become noisy."* Snow is the adversarial case: falling precipitation is (i) independently moving, (ii) present in *both* the A-only render and the capture, and (iii) high-contrast, so RGB differencing *does* separate it and *does* assign it to B. **Veto prediction:** under snow, false-positive dynamic Gaussians should appear as a diffuse volumetric haze; the observed PSNR of 35.00 dB (highest of the three) argues against severe contamination — most likely because the Blender snow is a particle system rendered identically in both A and A+B, so it cancels in the residual. **This is a synthetic-data artifact, not a robustness result.** Real snowfall, with stochastic particle motion and wind, would not cancel. Additional vetoes at this layer: (a) *specular highlights* on wet grass violate the view-dependent SH degree-3 model of O1/O5, since O5's $c^{(i)}_{t,k}$ is a function of viewing direction only and cannot represent a moving highlight on a *static* surface; (b) *long shadows* that migrate between $t$ and $t+1$ are assigned to B by the residual mask even though they are a purely geometric/radiometric effect of a static light — a systematic false positive that no amount of warm-starting removes.

**Layer L5 — Capacity veto (the only one the paper proves).** O8's linear densification law is a hard veto on S1: $\Delta K = 1.67\times10^5$ splats/frame is unbounded in $T$, so S1 is disqualified for *any* event longer than $\sim10^2$ frames regardless of PSNR. This layer is fully adjudicated in the paper's favour.

**Adjudication summary.** L5 is proven; L2 and L3 are conditionally survivable in the synthetic regime and undecidable in the real regime because $\Delta t$ is unreported; L1 vetoes real broadcast deployment at the millisecond scale; L4's snow result is an artifact of cancellation, not a robustness demonstration. No layer of the physics contradicts the *architectural* claim; several layers contradict the *deployment* claim, and the authors' Limitations section [PAGE 8] is unusually candid about exactly which ones.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each of the following is a **verifiable-reward** scenario: the outcome is a scalar that can be logged, thresholded, and adjudicated by a script with no human in the loop. All use artifacts already produced by the authors' pipeline (per-frame PSNR/LPIPS JSON, splat counts, archive bytes, per-iteration ms).

**Scenario 1 — Budget-matched quality counterfactual.**
*Intervention:* re-run D-3DGS [36], 4DGS [51], and ST-GS [32] on D-WS at exactly 8,000 iterations with the authors' own Blender-exported initialization, splits, and rasterizer settings. *Verifiable reward:* $\Delta_{1} = \mathrm{PSNR}_{\mathrm{TA\text{-}3DGS}} - \max(\mathrm{PSNR}_{\mathrm{baselines@8k}})$. *Prediction:* $\Delta_1$ shrinks from the reported ~14 dB (42.50 vs 28.17) to **≈4–7 dB**, because 8k iterations is far below the 130K/17K/30K budgets the baselines were run at, and 3DGS-family methods at 8k iterations converge to a fraction of their final PSNR. *Veto rule:* if $\Delta_1 < 3$ dB, the claim *"we achieve best PSNR and LPIPS score"* [PAGE 6] is retained but downgraded to a budget artifact; if $\Delta_1 > 8$ dB, the architectural claim is confirmed. *Secondary verifiable quantity:* $\Delta_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}$ must move in the same direction or the result is seed-noise-dominated.

**Scenario 2 — SfM-initialization counterfactual (the pipeline claim).**
*Intervention:* replace the Blender GT point cloud at $t_0$ [PAGE 6: *"we initialize the 3DGS model using a ground-truth point cloud exported from our Blender-based data generation plugin"*] with COLMAP-SfM geometry, keeping $K=100{,}000$, 8,000 iterations, and the frozen split. *Verifiable reward:* $\Delta_2 = \mathrm{PSNR}_{\mathrm{GT\text{-}init}} - \mathrm{PSNR}_{\mathrm{SfM\text{-}init}}$ on the 4 held-out cameras at frames $\{1, 10, 40, 80, 109\}$. *Prediction:* $\Delta_2 \in [2, 6]$ dB at $t_0$, decaying with $t$ as the warm chain partially repairs the geometry — but **not** recovering the SfM deficit, consistent with the authors' own caveat [PAGE 8]: *"the fixed-budget warm chain may not fully recover missing geometry."* *Veto rule:* $\Delta_2 > 6$ dB at $t_0$ vetoes the framing of the method as an SfM-initialized pipeline (PAGE 3–4) and forces the honest description to be "GT-geometry-initialized"; $\Delta_2 < 1$ dB confirms both the pipeline claim and the paper's practical premise. *Bonus verifiable artifact:* log COLMAP inlier count per frame; if inliers fall below $0.5K$, the fixed budget is provably undersampled and the whole chain is starting from a capacity deficit.

**Scenario 3 — Lag-sweep identification counterfactual (the mechanism claim).**
*Intervention:* sweep the warm-start source lag $\Delta \in \{1,2,3,5,10,20,40\}$ frames on D-WS frames 40–49, holding dataset, loss, and iteration count exactly as in Section 8.2. *Verifiable reward:* fit $\mathrm{PSNR}(\Delta)$ and extract the half-decay lag $\Delta_{1/2}$ at which PSNR falls to 6 dB above the from-scratch baseline; additionally, compute $\hat L = \lVert\Theta^\star_{t}-F(\Theta^\star_{t-\Delta})\rVert/\lVert\Theta^\star_{t-\Delta}-\Theta^{\star,\mathrm{loc}}_{t}\rVert$ as a logged parametric contraction ratio. *Prediction:* monotone decay with $\Delta_{1/2} \in [3, 10]$ frames and $\hat L$ increasing toward 1 with $\Delta$; PSNR($\Delta{=}40$) $\approx 26.8$ dB, reproducing the authors' single distant point [PAGE 13] and *closing the two-point gap*. *Veto rule:* if $\mathrm{PSNR}(\Delta)$ is **flat** below $\Delta = 10$, then the "local smoothness / basin of attraction" mechanism is falsified and the alternative — implicit temporal coupling through the shared parameterization — survives; the paper's central mechanistic claim would have to be withdrawn.
