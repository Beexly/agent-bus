where \(F\) is the fixed cost of research and experiment. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In conditions of Knightian uncertainty, decision makers exhibit crisis habit regression: faced with unforeseeable results of new processes, firms and individuals regress to liquid hoarding and the preservation of existing capital rather than diverting resources into research and experiment. The text’s emphasis on “the investment of present resources (in research and experiment)” [PAGE 1] is precisely the behavior that regresses under high uncertainty, because the payoff distribution cannot be formed.

The counter-trap is contrarian: if all agents regress and underinvest in new productive capacities, the relative return to the few who continue diverting resources rises, since competition is thinned precisely when uncertainty is highest. This is a second-order theory-of-mind loop: I invest because I expect others to regress; they expect me to regress; therefore the unconventional move has asymmetric upside.

The clock is the time-preference mechanism from the prior chapter, which Knight says his argument “take[s] up and continue[s]” [PAGE 1]. The diversion decision is an intertemporal allocation:
\[
\max_{\{C_t,K_t\}} \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t} U(C_t)\,dt
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\dot{K}_t = Y_t - C_t - \delta K_t ,
\]
where \(\rho\) is the rate of time preference. Because productive goods are “no longer fixed in supply” [PAGE 1], the clock is not merely discounting a fixed endowment; it is governing the rate at which the economy manufactures its own future supply of capital. Crisis habit regression lengthens the effective clock (higher \(\rho\), lower \(I_t\)), while the counter-trap shortens it by reallocating toward \(K_t\) when others flee.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The abstract argument on page 161 is logical and institutional, not physical, but three veto layers can bind its empirical application.

Physical layer: capital goods are subject to depreciation and destruction. A weather shock \(W_t\) (storm, flood, drought) can destroy part of the capital stock, \(\delta \to \delta + \eta W_t\), vetoing the assumption of “indefinite creation” [PAGE 1]. If weather risk is spatially correlated with the location of natural agents or production sites, the free-good limit for processes embedded in those locations is breached regardless of reproduction cost.

Biomechanical layer: processes “inhere in the persons of their possessors” [PAGE 1]. The human vessel has bounded memory, attention, and skill-acquisition bandwidth \(B_{\max}\). Even if an idea is non-rival and cheap to reproduce, the capacity to wield it is rival and scarce. This creates a hard veto on the “multiply them to the point of becoming free goods” [PAGE 1] conclusion when the bottleneck is the possessor rather than the idea itself.

Weather veto proof: no weather or biomechanical condition can invalidate the logical claim that zero reproduction cost plus zero excludability drives price to zero. However, they can veto the *empirical relevance* of that limit for a given class of capital goods. Agricultural capital, for instance, faces weather vetoes that software does not; embodied surgical techniques face biomechanical bandwidth vetoes that pure algorithms do not. The adjudication rule is: accept the free-good limit only where \(MC_{\text{rep}} \approx 0\), \(E=1\) or \(E=0\) with institutional enforcement, and where physical/biomechanical destruction \(W_t, B_{\max}\) do not bind the relevant margin.
