<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under jump risk, an agent’s model of another agent’s model can fail in three distinct ways. Crisis habit regression is the collapse of the posterior over the jump measure to a pre-crisis habitual diffusion: the agent keeps the drift and volatility terms and silently drops the integral in \(\mathcal{L}\). Formally, if the true generator has the jump term from [PAGE 1] “Basic material for SDEs with jumps —16,” habit regression replaces \(\mathcal{L}\) by
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{habit}}f(x)=a(x)f'(x)+\frac12\sigma^2(x)f''(x),
\]
so the agent’s predicted tail probabilities and density smoothness are structurally wrong even if the diffusion parameters are well estimated. Counter-trap is the opposite error: after observing a rare jump, the agent inflates the Levy intensity, overweights the jump term, and becomes excessively conservative. Recursively, if Agent A believes Agent B will overreact, A may preemptively overreact to B’s expected overreaction, double-counting the same jump. The clock error is temporal: “Markov chain approximation — 78” [PAGE 1] and “Summary of short-time asymptotic bounds —120” [PAGE 1] are not long-time forecasts. If the discrete clock step \(\Delta t\) is too coarse relative to the jump intensity, jumps are aliased into drift; if \(\Delta t\) is too fine, the approximation cost grows. The correct recursive test is to compare the full integro-differential generator with the habit generator and with an over-weighted jump generator, then check whether the observed small-time log-density follows the absolutely continuous or singular regime at [PAGE 1]. The decision layer is the HJB equation anchored by [PAGE 2] “Optimal consumption problem — 301” and “Viscosity solutions — 305”: the value function must solve the full jump generator, not a habit-truncated or panic-inflated one. Breakdown regimes: if the comparison principle fails, the recursive equilibrium is not unique; if the jump integral diverges, the agent’s value function is undefined; if the clock step is misspecified, apparent habit or panic can be a discretization artifact rather than a belief.
