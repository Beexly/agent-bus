<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

Supervised objective (Eq. 1, [PAGE 5]), cross-entropy over $n$ classes on $m$ samples:
$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}\!\left(y^{(i)}=k\right)\log\!\left(p_\theta(\hat{y}^{(i)}=k)\right)$$
Units: $\mathcal{L}$ is dimensionless (nats, since natural log); $p_\theta \in [0,1]$, $\sum_k p_\theta = 1$. The implementation adds class weighting and label smoothing $\epsilon=0.01$ ([PAGE 8]), i.e. targets become $y_k \to (1-\epsilon)\mathbb{I}(y{=}k) + \epsilon/n$. Singularity/breakdown regime: with $\epsilon \to 0$ and separable classes, the logit magnitude $\|\theta\|\to\infty$ and $\mathcal{L}\to 0$ but calibration collapses (overconfident softmax); label smoothing is precisely the regularizer that prevents this degenerate limit.

Continuous SSM (Eq. 2, [PAGE 5]):
$$h'(t) = A h(t) + B x(t), \qquad y(t) = C h(t)$$
with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ (evolution), $B \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times N}$, latent state $h(t)\in\mathbb{R}^N$. Units: $t$ in seconds (continuous time), $A$ carries $\text{s}^{-1}$.

Zero-order-hold discretization (Eq. 3, [PAGE 6]):
$$h_t = \bar{A} h_{t-1} + \bar{B} x_t, \qquad y_t = C h_t,$$
$$\bar{A} = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar{B} = (\Delta A)^{-1}\left(\exp(\Delta A) - I\right)\Delta B.$$
Assumption: $x_t$ piecewise-constant over each step $\Delta$. Breakdown regime: as $\Delta \to 0$, $(\Delta A)^{-1}$ diverges but the product $(\exp(\Delta A)-I)\to \Delta A$, so $\bar{B}\to \Delta B$ (finite); numerically, however, explicit inversion of $\Delta A$ is ill-conditioned when $\Delta A$ has near-zero eigenvalues — the practical fix (used in the Mamba reference impl.) is a series expansion of the discretized $B$, not literal matrix inversion.

Selection mechanism (Eq. 4, [PAGE 6]): $\bar{B}_t, C_t, \Delta_t = \text{Linear}(x_t)$. This makes the recurrence input-dependent (time-varying), breaking the convolutional/LTI structure and forbidding the fixed-kernel FFT acceleration of vanilla S4; Mamba instead uses a hardware-aware parallel scan with $O(N)$ state and $O(L)$ (linear in sequence length $L$) compute, versus $O(L^2)$ for self-attention [PAGE 4]. This linear-time property is the paper's stated efficiency motivation for low-latency inference.

Attention pooling (Eq. 5, [PAGE 6]): scores $e_t = w^\top h_t$ with learned $w \in \mathbb{R}^D$, weights $\alpha_t = \text{softmax}(e_t)$, pooled $T_{\text{visual}} = \sum_t \alpha_t h_t$. Units: $T \in \mathbb{R}^D$ (embedding space). Breakdown: with a single dominant $\alpha_t \to 1$ the pool degenerates to hard max-selection of one clip, discarding the run-up trajectory — exactly the failure mode the Mamba-over-sequence design is meant to avoid.

Late fusion (Eq. 6, [PAGE 7]): $\hat{y}^{(i)} = \text{FusionMLP}\big(\text{Concat}(T^{(i)}_{\text{run}}, T^{(i)}_{\text{kick}}, T^{(i)}_{\text{meta}})\big)$, with FC → BatchNorm → ReLU → Dropout. The pre-contact design is quantified on [PAGE 8]: 48-frame window = 32 run-up frames + 8 pre-contact + 8 post-contact.

[PAGE 9] gives the joint base-rate distribution I use above as trivial floors: 388 right-side / 234 left-side; 486 right-footed / 136 left-footed; label shares 294/103/225.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The kicker–goalkeeper dyad is a two-player game with asymmetric information, and the model is an attempt to invert the kicker's private intention from public pre-contact cues.

Habit regression (the kicker's own bias): empirical penalty data show kickers and keepers both overuse their dominant side. This paper's dataset mirrors that: 486/622 right-footed, 234 left-side pitch origin. A left-footed kicker's biomechanical envelope favors the keeper's right / kicker's natural side, which is why [PAGE 10] Figure 4 reports the largest gap for left-footed kickers (47% vs 56% right-footed, 3-class). Model-of-model: if the kicker knows the keeper has learned this prior (counter-trap), the kicker should anti-balance — shoot to the non-dominant side more than base rates suggest — which directly attacks the metadata branch's usefulness. The metadata feature is thus a double-edged cue: predictive only if kickers are habit-bound, self-defeating if kickers are strategic.

The clock: van der Kamp [ref 15] gives the temporal envelope. Flight time from 11 m is ~0.4–0.6 s; the model's window is 32 run-up + 16 contact frames ([PAGE 8]). "Crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock" resolves as: the run-up (pre-clock) carries habit signal, the contact-adjacent frames carry committed-intention signal, and any post-contact tail is contaminated by the goalkeeper's own action-bias dive (Bar-Eli), which is a confound the model cannot separate because the goalkeeper appears in-frame.

Recursive layer: the model implicitly assumes the kicker is Level-0 (habitual). If kickers are Level-1 (exploiting the model's published priors — the code is public per [PAGE 1]), the pre-contact cues become deliberately deceptive (a stutter-step or angled run-up used as a feint), and the HAR embedding's directional correlation degrades. The paper provides no adversarial-robustness test against intentional deception, which is the single biggest external-validity gap for any real deployment.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical veto (reaction-time budget). Penalty flight time from 11 m at ~25–30 m/s is ~0.37–0.44 s; human simple visual reaction is ~0.20–0.25 s plus ~0.15 s dive initiation. The paper's premise ([PAGE 1]) is that anticipation "before or very close to ball contact" is the only viable window. This is a hard physical constraint, not a modeling choice: no amount of embedding quality can create reaction time that does not exist. Consequently the practically useful operating point is the pre-contact frames only; the 8 post-contact frames ([PAGE 8]) help annotation-robustness but violate the anticipation premise for real-time use. Veto: any deployment claiming "early prediction" while relying on post-contact frames is physically disqualified.

Biomechanical veto (center class). The 10.7% center recall ([PAGE 10]) is not merely a data-imbalance artifact; it is biomechanically expected. A straight central penalty requires the run-up, hip orientation, and support-foot plant to be mutually consistent with "no lateral commitment," producing low-variance pre-contact cues. The information-theoretic content of the pre-contact signal for "center" is genuinely small, so the veto is: even a Bayes-optimal observer cannot exceed a modest ceiling on center detection from these cues alone. This bounds achievable 3-class accuracy and predicts the confusion structure the paper observes (center → left/right split 58.3%/31.1%).

Weather/lighting veto. The dataset is web-scraped broadcast footage with "variation in body pose, camera distance, angle, and lighting" ([PAGE 8], Figure 3). Rain, low sun, and night floodlighting alter silhouette contrast, degrading the ByteTrack bounding box ([PAGE 7]) and hence the isolated-kicker stream. The augmentation suite (Gaussian noise std 0.012, magnitude jitter std 0.04 — [PAGE 9]) is small and cannot simulate true occlusion by a diving keeper or a crowd-filled background. Veto: out-of-distribution lighting/occlusion is unmodeled; expected accuracy on such footage is below the reported CV numbers, and no confidence interval on that degradation is given.

Cross-layer adjudication: the three vetoes are mutually reinforcing — the physical reaction budget forces reliance on a low-information pre-contact window, the biomechanics of the center shot cap achievable accuracy there, and unmodeled environmental variance pushes real-world performance below lab CV. All three point the same direction: the reported 53.1% is an optimistic ceiling for clean, contact-centered, well-lit footage.
