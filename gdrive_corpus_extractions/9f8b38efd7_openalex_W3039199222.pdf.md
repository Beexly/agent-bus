<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage begins with 1950s neural networks that stalled due to limited compute and the XOR problem, then revived via Restricted Boltzmann Machines and big data [PAGE 3]. Handcrafted low-level features—SIFT, HOG, Optical Flow, haar-like—prevailed until Region-CNN (R-CNN) in 2014 demonstrated large gains by combining region proposals with CNN classification [PAGE 3]. The R-CNN family (SPP-Net, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN) improved accuracy but remained slow because of multi-stage pipelines and separately learned components [PAGE 3]. Single-shot detectors—SSD and YOLO—collapsed the pipeline into one network, enabling real-time detection and opening the possibility of real-time detection-based tracking [PAGE 3]. Tracking historically used Kalman filters with IOU distance and the Hungarian algorithm for data association [PAGE 4]. Deep SORT [9] replaced IOU with Mahalanobis distance and added a deep cosine metric CNN for appearance features [PAGE 4]. This paper (Kim, Heo, Kang, 2020, JBE Vol. 25 No. 2) is the immediate descendant: it inherits the Deep SORT detection–tracking chain but modifies the computational schedule (frame skipping) and the association memory (t-2 feature reuse) to satisfy CPU real-time constraints.

Multi-week traps embedded in this lineage: (1) Temporal error accumulation—over long video sequences, small per-frame association errors compound into persistent identity switches, especially under occlusion; (2) Detector latency compounding—because tracking is conditioned on detection output, any detector slowdown directly stalls the tracker, creating a serialization bottleneck; (3) Thermal throttling on sustained CPU loads—running YOLO-v3 continuously on a Xeon E5-2687W v4 can raise package temperature, triggering frequency scaling that further reduces fps below the measured 3.9 fps baseline; (4) Distribution shift—MOT16 benchmarks are fixed, but deployment in autonomous driving or surveillance introduces new weather, lighting, and crowd densities not represented in the benchmark, causing feature-space drift in the deep cosine metric.
