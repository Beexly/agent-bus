**1. Sign-Gestalt-Expectation Operator**  
Let $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1, s_2\}$ be the stimulus set (white entrance, black entrance).  
Let $\mathcal{R} = \{r_1, r_2\}$ be the response set (enter alley 1, enter alley 2).  
The sign-gestalt-expectation is a conditional probability map updated per trial $t$:  
$$\hat{E}_t : \mathcal{S} \to \Delta(\mathcal{R}), \quad \hat{E}_t(s) = P_t(R = r \mid S = s)$$  
where $\Delta(\mathcal{R})$ is the probability simplex over responses.  
Initial condition (pre-learning): $\hat{E}_0(s_1) \approx \hat{E}_0(s_2) \approx \text{Uniform}(\mathcal{R})$.  
Learning dynamics (associative gradient):  
$$\hat{E}_{t+1}(s) = (1-\lambda)\hat{E}_t(s) + \lambda \cdot \mathbb{I}[R_t = r^*(s)] \quad \forall s \in \mathcal{S}$$  
with learning rate $\lambda \in (0,1] \text{ trials}^{-1}$, $r^*(s)$ the rewarded response for stimulus $s$.  
[PAGE 228: "After learning, on the other hand, he differentiates between them, releases respectively different sign-gestalt-expectations for them"]

**2. Conscious Awareness / Sampling Operator**  
Define the sampling trajectory during trial $t$ as a continuous-time path in the choice chamber:  
$$\xi_t : [0, \tau_{\text{trial}}] \to \mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$$  
where $\mathcal{X}$ is the choice chamber manifold.  
The sampling behavior is a sequence of $N_t$ direction reversals (back-and-forth excursions):  
$$\mathcal{B}_t = \{ \tau_k \in (0, \tau_{\text{trial}}) \mid \dot{\xi}_t(\tau_k^-) \cdot \dot{\xi}_t(\tau_k^+) < 0 \}$$  
Cardinality $N_t = |\mathcal{B}_t|$ is the sampling count (dimensionless).  
Sampling duration: $\tau_s^{(t)} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_t} \Delta \tau_k$ where $\Delta \tau_k$ is dwell time at reversal $k$. Units: seconds (s).  
Tolman's identity claim:  
$$\text{ConsciousAwareness}_t \equiv \mathbb{I}[N_t \geq 1 \land \tau_s^{(t)} > \tau_{\text{thresh}}]$$  
where $\tau_{\text{thresh}}$ is a minimal dwell threshold (e.g., 0.5 s) to exclude motor noise.  
[PAGE 228: "We herewith define conscious awareness as consisting in the performance of a 'sampling,' or 'running-back-and-forth,' behavior"]

**3. Information-Gain Functional**  
The sampling policy maximizes expected mutual information between stimulus identity and reward:  
$$\pi^* = \arg\max_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{\xi \sim \pi} \left[ I(S; R \mid \xi, \hat{E}_t) \right]$$  
where $I(S;R \mid \xi, \hat{E}_t) = H[P(R \mid \hat{E}_t)] - \mathbb{E}_{s \sim P(S \mid \xi)}[H[P(R \mid S=s, \hat{E}_t)]]$ in bits.  
The visual "look" component implies $P(S \mid \xi)$ is updated via visual sensory likelihood $L(\xi \mid s)$.
