For the somatic contents listed on [PAGE 115] ("having a bone in the throat," "insects coming from the body"), endoscopic imaging and microneurography must show no physical correlate and no tactile afferent spikes during the reported sensation. Weather veto: fog, dim light, and glare elevate illusion rates but do not by themselves generate complete hallucinations; if reports persist indoors under controlled illumination with no weather correlate, weather is not a sufficient cause. Veto proof: the conjunction of null peripheral measurement, mismatched biomechanical signatures, and persistence under controlled weather yields the verdict that the object "happens not to be there" [PAGE 115], and that the sensation is a strictly sensational form of consciousness driven by $r_c$ rather than by $s_p$.

Scenario 1 (anechoic/sensory deprivation): Counterfactual veto—subject reports a clear voice while a calibrated microphone records $\le 20\ \mathrm{dB\ SPL}$ and fMRI shows auditory-cortex gamma activity without medial geniculate drive. Veto: external acoustic source. Prediction: $\mathcal{H} > \mathcal{H}_{\text{crit}}$ and $P(O{=}1 \mid s_p) > 0.9$ throughout the silence block; reports cease only when secondary auditory cortex is disrupted by TMS, isolating $r_c$ as the generator.

Scenario 2 (mirror-box / visuomotor conflict): Counterfactual veto—subject reports seeing an occluded phantom limb move while surface EMG of the residual limb is silent and no motor command is logged. Veto: reafferent proprioceptive source. Prediction: $\mathcal{H}$ spikes at visual-feedback removal; the phantom's velocity profile matches the internal efference copy rather than any peripheral afferent trace, confirming that the percept is driven by $r_c$ gain, not by $s_p$.

Scenario 3 (dopaminergic challenge vs. placebo, double-blind): Counterfactual veto—peripheral environmental stimuli are held constant while a dopaminergic agonist or saline is administered. Veto: stimulus-proportionality model ($r_c = G \cdot s_p$ with finite $G$). Prediction: under agonist, $\mathcal{H}$ increases dose-dependently with $s_p$ unchanged; pseudo-hallucination transitions to complete hallucination when $P(O{=}1 \mid s_p)$ crosses $0.95$; under placebo, $\mathcal{H}$ remains within noise bounds. This isolates $G$ (and prior precision) as the control parameter, consistent with James's claim that hallucination is an extreme of the perception process in which cerebral reaction is disproportionate to peripheral occasion [PAGE 115].
