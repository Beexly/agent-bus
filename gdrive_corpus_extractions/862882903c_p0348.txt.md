James's symbolic representation can be formalized within an information-theoretic and probabilistic framework. Let's define the operators and equations.

**Core Operators:**
1.  **Abstraction Operator, \(\mathcal{A}\):** Maps a concrete datum \(S\) to an abstract character \(M\). This operator is parameterized by a subjective interest/utility function \(U(P)\).
    \[ M = \mathcal{A}_{U}(S) \]
2.  **World-Conjunction Relation, \(\bowtie_W\):** Represents the objective, causal, or correlational link between two properties in the physical world. \(M \bowtie_W P\) means "\(M\) is inevitably conjoined with \(P\) in world \(W\)."
3.  **Inference Operator, \(\mathcal{I}\):** Derives a conclusion \(P\) from a premise \(S\) using an intermediate \(M\).
    \[ P = \mathcal{I}(S, M) \text{ where } M \bowtie_W P \]

**Formal Equation (Probabilistic Version):**
The "sagacity" in selecting \(M\) can be quantified as the maximization of **Mutual Information** \(I(M; P)\) subject to a constraint on the complexity of \(M\). This is akin to the **Information Bottleneck Method**.
\[
\text{Maximize: } I(M; P) - \beta I(M; S)
\]
Where:
- \(I(M; P)\) is the mutual information between the abstracted feature \(M\) and the desired outcome \(P\). High value means \(M\) is highly predictive of \(P\).
- \(I(M; S)\) is the mutual information between the abstracted feature \(M\) and the full data \(S\). This term penalizes \(M\) for retaining too much irrelevant detail from \(S\).
- \(\beta\) is a trade-off parameter related to the "importance" of \(P\). As James says, if \(P\) is of great importance, \(\beta\) is small, allowing a more detailed \(M\) (higher \(I(M;S)\)) to capture the nuance needed to predict \(P\) accurately. If \(P\) is of little importance, \(\beta\) is large, forcing a simpler, coarser \(M\).

**Units:** Mutual information is measured in **bits** (or nats), representing the reduction in uncertainty about one variable given knowledge of another.

**Assumptions:**
1.  The world conjunction \(M \bowtie_W P\) is stationary and ergodic over the observational timeframe.
2.  The subjective interest \(U(P)\) is stable during the reasoning episode.
3.  The abstraction \(\mathcal{A}\) is computable and yields a finite representation \(M\).

**Exact Singularity/Breakdown Regimes:**
1.  **When \(I(M; P) = 0\):** The chosen \(M\) is statistically independent of \(P\). The reasoning process yields no information about the goal. The inference \(\mathcal{I}\) is vacuous.
2.  **When \(I(M; S) = H(S)\):** The abstraction \(M\) is a lossless encoding of \(S\) (i.e., \(M\) contains all information in \(S\)). This violates the spirit of abstraction ("conceives it abstractly") and is computationally intractable for complex \(S\).
3.  **When \(\beta \to 0\):** The system prioritizes perfect prediction of \(P\) above all else, potentially leading to **overfitting** (\(M\) becomes a memorization of \(S\) for the training set).
4.  **When \(\beta \to \infty\):** The system prioritizes extreme simplicity, collapsing \(M\) to a constant (minimum description length), losing all predictive power for \(P\).
5.  **Non-Stationarity Breakdown:** If the world-conjunction changes (\(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(M \bowtie_W P) \neq 0\)), a previously sagacious \(M\) becomes obsolete. The reasoning process fails because its foundational assumption is broken.

The lineage here is direct: both see meaning and reasoning as triadic and effect-oriented.

James is a precursor. The formal equation provided (Information Bottleneck) is the modern computational embodiment of this instrumentalist principle. Bayesian inference similarly starts with a prior (interest/hypothesis about `P`) and updates beliefs based on evidence (`S`), effectively searching for the `M` that best explains `S` under the prior for `P`.

It maintains generative models (`M`) of the world to minimize prediction error about sensory input (`S`) in service of behavioral goals (`P`, e.g., survival, reward). James's psychology is the philosophical bedrock for this view: the brain's abstractions are shaped by what matters for action.
