Tolman's central derivation on page 258 treats feeling and emotion not as subjective epiphenomena but as "immanent determinants of behavior" embedded in a three-phase constitutive loop [PAGE 258]. The first hop is the ontological claim: "the so-called simple feelings, pleasantness and unpleasantness, and the so-called emotions, fear, rage, lust, and the like, are fundamentally alike" [PAGE 258]. This collapses the affect–emotion distinction into a single mechanism whose input is a "given stimulus-situation" and whose output is behavior organized around a "physiological significate." The second hop is the definition of the sign gestalt-expectation itself: a readiness to expect "certain types of resulting significates as lying in such and such types of means-end relation with respect to such and such given, immediately presented, signs" [PAGE 258]. Formally, this is a conditional expectancy structure over future physiological states given present perceptual signs. The third hop is the motoric release: the sign gestalt-expectation "tends to induce along with itself (through some physiological mechanism) incipient preparatory and consummatory activities," meaning visceral and skeletal incipient movements appropriate to approaching or withdrawing from the anticipated significate [PAGE 258]. The fourth hop closes the loop kinesthetically: "these incipient movements give rise to ... resulting auxiliary sets of organic and kinesthetic sensations, that is, discriminanda-expectations" [PAGE 258]. Thus felt emotion is, in Tolman's account, partly the reafferent perception of one's own incipient action readiness.

Gray-zone audit. The theory is under-specified at exactly the points where a dynamical model would require parameters. Tolman invokes "some physiological mechanism" between expectancy and incipiency without naming it, specifying its gain, or providing a time constant [PAGE 258]. The phrase "relatively generalized" sign gestalt-expectations lacks a variance or entropy measure: we are not told how sharp or diffuse the probability distribution over significates must be before it counts as an emotion rather than a mere expectation [PAGE 258]. The term "incipient" carries no threshold metric; there is no equation distinguishing an incipient movement that is aborted from one that is consummated. Most critically, the model risks circularity: the sign gestalt-expectation causes incipient movement (a → b), and incipient movement produces the sensation that is then identified as the emotion (b → c), yet the felt emotion is also described as a determinant that organizes the initial expectation. This is a cybernetic loop, but Tolman does not specify the loop's time delay, its stability criteria, or its breakdown frequency.

Breakdown regimes emerge when any link is severed. If the stimulus-situation S is novel, no sign gestalt-expectation G(S) can be assembled because there is no learned means-end relation to a significate. If the anticipated physiological significate never arrives within a critical temporal window, the expectancy undergoes extinction and incipient activity collapses. If incipient visceral and skeletal movement is physically blocked, phase (c) is starved of kinesthetic reafference, predicting emotional attenuation or misattribution. The footnote to Tolman's 1923 Psychological Review article (30, 217–227) [PAGE 258] shows this is a matured behaviorist position, but the 1932 text still presents the physiology as a black box. A rigorous treatment must therefore recast Tolman's qualitative phases as operators over probability distributions and activation vectors, with explicit singularity conditions where the model ceases to generate discriminable predictions.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(S \in \mathcal{S}\) denote a presented stimulus situation or sign. Let \(\Phi\) denote the set of fundamental physiological significates, each with valence \(v(\phi) \in \{+1,-1\}\) for desired versus undesired outcomes. Let \(\mathcal{M}\) denote the space of means-end relations linking signs to significates.

Phase (a) — Sign gestalt-expectation release. Define the sign gestalt-expectation operator
\[
\mathcal{G}: \mathcal{S} \to \Delta(\Phi \times \mathcal{M}),
\]
where \(\Delta\) is the probability simplex. For a given sign \(S\),
\[
\mathcal{G}(S) = \bigl\{(\phi_i, m_i, p_i)\bigr\}, \qquad p_i = P(\phi_i, m_i \mid S), \qquad \sum_i p_i = 1.
\]
Tolman's claim that the object or situation "is a sign for (will lead on to) the coming of some relatively fundamental, desired or undesired, physiological significate" [PAGE 258] is therefore a conditional expectancy over future physiological states. The information gained by the sign is
\[
\Delta H = H(\Phi) - H(\Phi \mid S) \quad \text{[bits]}.
\]
Singularity/breakdown: if \(H(\Phi \mid S) = H(\Phi)\), then \(\Delta H = 0\), the sign carries no expectancy information, and no sign gestalt-expectation is released.

Phase (b) — Incipient visceral and skeletal movement. Define the incipiency operator
\[
\mathcal{I}: \Delta(\Phi \times \mathcal{M}) \to [0,1]^{n_V} \times [0,1]^{n_K},
\]
mapping the expectancy distribution to a visceral activation vector \(\mathbf{v} \in [0,1]^{n_V}\) and a skeletal preparatory command \(\mathbf{k} \in [0,1]^{n_K}\), with onset time \(t_0 \in \mathbb{R}^+\) [seconds]. This formalizes Tolman's "incipient preparatory and consummatory activities... incipient stages of movements appropriate to the getting to or from the ultimate physiological significate and of reacting, both viscerally and skeletally, to the latter when reached" [PAGE 258].

Phase (c) — Auxiliary organic and kinesthetic sensations. Define the discriminanda-re afference operator
\[
\mathcal{A}: [0,1]^{n_V} \times [0,1]^{n_K} \to \mathcal{D},
\]
where \(\mathcal{D}\) is the space of organic and kinesthetic discriminanda-expectations, with dimensionality \(d = \dim(\mathcal{D})\) and information content \(I(\mathcal{D}; S)\) [bits]. This captures Tolman's claim that incipient movements "give rise to... resulting auxiliary sets of organic and kinesthetic sensations" [PAGE 258].

Composite emotion operator:
\[
\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{A} \circ \mathcal{I} \circ \mathcal{G}: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{D}.
\]

Additional breakdown singularities:
1. Motoric starvation: \(\|\mathbf{v}\|_2 + \|\mathbf{k}\|_2 = 0\). Phase (b) produces no output, so phase (c) receives no reafferent input; predicted emotional differentiation collapses.
2. Flat reafference: \(\mathcal{D}\) carries no discriminable structure (\(I(\mathcal{D}; S) \approx 0\)). The organism experiences diffuse arousal without discrete fear, rage, or lust.
3. Expectancy extinction: if significate \(\phi\) fails to arrive within the temporal horizon of \(\mathcal{G}(S)\), then \(p_i \to 0\) for all \(i\) and the sign gestalt is extinguished.

All conceptual definitions are grounded in Chapter XVII, page 258 [PAGE 258], including the footnote cross-reference to Tolman (1923), *Psychol Rev* 30, 217–227 [PAGE 258].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman's position on page 258 is explicitly historical: it cross-references "E C Tolman A behavioristic account of the emotions Psychol Rev 1923 30, 217 227" [PAGE 258]. The 1932 chapter therefore represents a decade of refinement of a behaviorist emotion theory that rejects both introspective subjectivism and blind reflexology. The lineage runs from William James's peripheral theory—bodily changes follow perceptions and the feeling of those changes *is* the emotion—through Cannon's critique of visceral specificity, into Tolman's purposive behaviorism, which inserts a sign-mediated expectancy stage between stimulus and bodily response [PAGE 258]. The crucial innovation is retrocausal in the strict sense of backward-projected causation: the expected future significate \(\phi\), desired or undesired, functions as a present determinant of behavior via the sign gestalt-expectation \(\mathcal{G}(S)\) [PAGE 258]. The goal is not yet present, yet it organizes current action through the organism's readiness to expect it.

Multi-week traps. In longitudinal observation across weeks, a researcher applying Tolman's model might fall into the trait-reification trap: treating a stabilized sign gestalt-expectation as a stable personality disposition (e.g., labeling an animal "fearful") when it is actually a context-bound contingency \(P(\phi \mid S)\) that is liable to extinction if the significate \(\phi\) stops arriving, or to latent inhibition if \(S\) is repeatedly presented without consequence [PAGE 258]. A second trap is the acclimation fallacy: assuming that the physiological mechanism linking expectancy to incipiency remains constant across weeks, ignoring metabolic, hormonal, or experiential recalibration of the \(\mathcal{I}\) operator [PAGE 258]. A third trap is temporal misalignment: measuring emotion only at the moment of significate arrival and missing the anticipatory incipient phase (b) that Tolman claims constitutes the core of the emotional determinant [PAGE 258]. The retrocausal structure means the present behavior is keyed to an expected future; across multiple weeks, if that expected future shifts, the present behavior must shift with it, even if the external sign \(S\) remains unchanged.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when the organism, under acute threat or deprivation, collapses from the full purposive sequence \(\mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{I} \to \mathcal{A}\) into a prepotent expression of phase (b) alone: undifferentiated visceral and skeletal incipiency without the discriminative appraisal normally supplied by phase (c) [PAGE 258]. In crisis, the sign gestalt-expectation may still fire, but the "appropriate" means-end relation collapses into generalized approach-withdrawal urgency; the observer sees rage or fear, but the mechanism may be nothing more than unvetoed incipient motor discharge [PAGE 258].

The counter-trap is the observer's reverse inference: because phase (c) produces organic and kinesthetic sensations that are then labeled fear, rage, or lust, an external viewer may assume that the observed incipient viscero-skeletal pattern *causes* a discrete emotion category, when Tolman's model only guarantees the production of discriminanda-expectations, not their veridical categorization [PAGE 258]. The organism may misattribute its own arousal, producing a false theory of mind about its emotional state.

The clock. Tolman's sequence implies a critical temporal window \(\tau_c\) between the release of the sign gestalt-expectation and the arrival of the physiological significate [PAGE 258]. If \(\phi\) does not materialize within \(\tau_c\) after incipiency onset, the expectancy undergoes extinction and the incipient movement is aborted; if \(\tau_c\) is not enforced, the system perseverates, producing chronic stress or sustained emotional tone without consummatory closure. Crisis habit regression is therefore a failure of the clock: the organism reverts to incipient patterns because the expected significate is either delayed beyond \(\tau_c\) or is represented as imminent regardless of actual temporal contingency [PAGE 258]. Recursive theory of mind enters when the organism monitors its own incipiency (phase c) and uses that self-observation to update the sign gestalt-expectation for future encounters with the same sign \(S\), creating a second-order loop in which felt emotion reshapes the very expectancy that generated it.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Physical layer. Sustained visceral incipiency \(\mathbf{v}\) incurs metabolic cost
\[
C(t) = \int_{t_0}^{t} c\bigl(\mathbf{v}(s)\bigr)\, ds \quad \text{[joules]},
\]
where \(c(\cdot)\) is the rate of energetic expenditure for autonomic and endocrine activation. A physical veto \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}} = 1\) is triggered when \(C(t) > C_{\max}\), terminating phase (b) to preserve homeostasis.

Biomechanical layer. Skeletal incipiency \(\mathbf{k}\) is bounded by force–velocity properties and fatigue. If the required preparatory force \(F\) exceeds the time-varying maximum \(F_{\max}(t)\), a biomechanical veto \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{bio}} = 1\) suppresses consummatory movement, leaving only incipient twitch or postural tone.

Weather / environmental layer. Ambient noise \(\eta\) corrupts the presented sign \(S\). If the signal-to-noise ratio \(\mathrm{SNR}(S) < \theta_{\mathrm{env}}\), the sign gestalt-expectation \(\mathcal{G}(S)\) cannot achieve sufficient information gain \(\Delta H\), and an environmental veto \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{env}} = 1\) prevents emotional engagement with the stimulus.

Composite veto proof. Define
\[
\mathcal{V} = \mathbb{I}\!\left[ C(t) > C_{\max} \;\lor\; F > F_{\max}(t) \;\lor\; \mathrm{SNR}(S) < \theta_{\mathrm{env}} \right].
\]
If \(\mathcal{V} = 1\), then \(\mathcal{I}\bigl(\mathcal{G}(S)\bigr) = \mathbf{0}\), and phase (c) receives no kinesthetic input; no discriminable emotion is generated. This cross-layer adjudication shows that Tolman's "physiological mechanism" [PAGE 258] must be understood as a constrained, veto-able control system rather than an automatic reflex release.
