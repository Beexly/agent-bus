Note the opponent's *future* \(x^j_{[H:T]}\) is inside the conditioning set. This is an oracle: at true forecast time \(t=H\), the opponent's future is unknown. So the impressive opponent-conditioned numbers are simulation numbers, not forecast numbers — the paper is careful to call it "primary formulation for simulating targeted tactical responses" [PAGE 3], but a reader who conflates simulation with prediction will over-credit the result. Gray-zone verdict: internally valid, externally mislabeled.

Hop 2 — Does the diffusion objective even match its sampler? Forward process \(x^{(s)}_f = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_s}x_f + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_s}\,\epsilon\) with \(\bar\alpha_s=\prod_{r=1}^s(1-\beta_r)\), \(S=100\), linear schedule [PAGE 16, Eq. 14]. Inference draws \(x^{(S)}_f\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\) [PAGE 17, Eq. 17]. This is only unbiased if \(\bar\alpha_S\approx 0\) (terminal signal-to-noise \(\to 0\)). The paper never states the \(\beta\) endpoints; with a naive linear schedule over just 100 steps, \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_S}\) is typically not negligible, so \(\mathcal N(0,I)\) does not match \(q(x^{(S)}_f)\) and injects initialization bias. This is the classic zero-terminal-SNR failure regime and is the single most under-audited assumption in the Methods.

Hop 3 — The normalization is anisotropic. Coordinates map \(x/52.5\) and \(y/34\) both onto \([-1,1]\) [PAGE 23 vs PAGE 16]. The MSE loss \(\mathbb E\lVert\epsilon-\hat\epsilon\rVert_2^2\) [Eq. 16] therefore lives in a space where one normalized unit is 52.5 m longitudinally but 34 m laterally. Physical longitudinal error is weighted \(\sim1.54\times\) more than lateral error in the loss. This silently biases generation toward longitudinal accuracy and partly explains why stretch-index/surface-area deviations dominate the error budget.

Hop 4 — Identifiability ceiling. The authors themselves concede 2D ambiguity: a shot off target over the bar is "indistinguishable from a Goal in the 2D plane" [PAGE 40]. This caps subtype recall structurally, visible in Table 19: Goal-kick recall@1 = 0%, Defended = 12.5%, macro recall@1 = 44.4% [PAGE 40]. The top-5 macro of 83% is a manifold-coverage statistic, not a decision statistic.

Hop 5 — Style conditioning trades geometry for structure. Team-conditioning Auckland FC *raises* 5s avg-ADE 1.65→2.04 m while cutting surface-area deviation 652→335 m² [PAGE 34, Table 8]. This is not a free win; it is a bias toward the team's modal shape, i.e., regression to stylistic prior at the cost of pointwise fidelity. League conditioning decays to baseline by 5s [PAGE 8], indicating the style signal is a short-horizon prior, not a persistent dynamical constraint. Multi-hop conclusion: GenTac is a credible *conditional generative simulator* and a weak *point forecaster*, and its metrics are only trustworthy once the oracle-conditioning, terminal-SNR, and anisotropic-normalization caveats are subtracted.
</THINK_TRACE>

Hierarchical loss (Eq. 21): \(\mathcal L_{event}=\mathcal L_{type}+\lambda\mathcal L_{sub}\), \(\lambda=1.0\).

Diffusion objective (Eq. 16): \(\mathcal L_{traj}=\mathbb E_{x_f,\epsilon,s}\lVert\epsilon-\hat\epsilon^{(s)}\rVert_2^2\).

Geometric metrics with units and provable bounds [PAGE 28–29]:
- \(\mathrm{ADE}=\frac1T\sum_t\lVert\hat p_t-p_t\rVert_2\). Supremum = pitch diagonal \(\sqrt{105^2+68^2}=\sqrt{15649}=125.1\text{ m}\) — exactly the stated range cap. ✓

- Kuramoto \(R=\frac1N\big|\sum_j e^{i\theta_j}\big|\in[0,1]\), dimensionless [PAGE 29].

Offense/defense metrics [PAGE 30–31]:
- \(\mathrm{OBET}=\dfrac{\sum_{k\in A}\mathrm{EPV}_k}{\sum_{k\in A\cup D}\mathrm{EPV}_k}\in[0,1]\) (dimensionless; requires an \(A/D\) control partition).
- Depth threat \(=\sum_{z=1}^{32}\dfrac{N^{atk}_z}{N_z}\dfrac{\mathrm{EPV}_z}{\mathrm{EPV}_{tot}}\in[0,1]\).
- Disruption \(=\mathrm{Clip}\!\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{Area}_{t+1}-\mathrm{Area}_{t-1}}{\text{Pitch Area}}\times100,\,-1,1\right)\), dimensionless; centered difference spans \(2\Delta t=0.08\) s.

(b) Anisotropic normalization: \(1\) normalized unit \(=52.5\) m in \(x\) vs \(34\) m in \(y\) → loss reweights axes \(1.54{:}1\). (c) Disruption metric saturates at clip \(\pm1\), losing resolution for large shape changes; the \(\times100\) is admittedly heuristic [PAGE 31]. (d) Nomenclature: "depth" is called "Length Threat" in Fig. 2b [PAGE 7] but "Depth threat" in text/tables [PAGE 8, 31, 37] — a labeling inconsistency, not a numeric one.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage is legible and dense. Generative backbone: DDPM-style forward/reverse diffusion [Eq. 14–18] descends from Ho et al.; the paper cites its neighbors directly — TacticAI [ref 17], TacEleven [ref 18], JointDiff [ref 19] — positioning GenTac as the first to unify continuous trajectory diffusion with discrete tactical semantics over *open play* rather than set pieces [PAGE 1]. Attention factorization \(A_t(A_s(\cdot))\) is explicitly lifted from TimeSformer [ref 31, PAGE 15]. Collective-structure metrics inherit the sports-science canon: stretch index / surface area from Bartlett and Frencken [ref 26–27], Kuramoto synchronization from Duarte et al. [ref 28]. Attacking value comes from Karun Singh's expected threat [ref 29] and the Friends-of-Tracking EPV grid [ref 36]; spatial dominance from Taki & Hasegawa dominant region [ref 30].

Multi-week traps (recurring methodological pitfalls this lineage keeps re-stepping into):
1. Deterministic-regression trap: prior art produced single MAP rollouts; GenTac escapes it via best-of-\(K\) (\(K=20\)) and min/avg reporting [PAGE 5]. But min-over-\(K\) is an optimistic estimator — reporting min-ADE as headline flatters the model, because with 20 draws the minimum is a lower-order statistic that shrinks mechanically with \(K\). This trap migrated, not vanished.
2. Oracle-leakage trap: opponent-conditioning feeds the opponent's ground-truth future (Eq. 6). This is the same leakage that plagued interaction-aware trajectory forecasting (Social-LSTM/Trajectron lineage) and resurfaces here as a "70% improvement" that is not deployable ex ante.
3. Reconstruction-contamination trap: broadcast-derived trajectories from SoccerFactory + post-refinement (GS-HOTA 64.2, GS-DetA 51.3, GS-AssA 80.5, [PAGE 24]) contain homography-induced positional jumps [PAGE 25]. The authors sequester these to train/val only and keep the test set public [PAGE 26] — a correct quarantine that breaks the trap for evaluation, though residual noise still shapes the learned prior.
4. Short-history style trap: league fine-tuning on 10 A-League / 7 Bundesliga matches yields gains that vanish by 5 s [PAGE 8, PAGE 36]. The lesson recurring across the lineage: stylistic conditioning is a decaying prior, easily overfit on tiny samples.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Real tactics are a recursive belief game: each team models the other modeling it. GenTac captures a degenerate, one-level, oracle version of this. Opponent-conditioning (Eq. 6) injects the opponent's *realized* future — an omniscient ToM shortcut rather than a predicted belief. The unconditioned joint model (Eq. 5) has zero ToM: it samples a coupled distribution but with no explicit opponent-model term. Neither setting represents "I think that you think that I will press."

Crisis-habit regression: under scoreline pressure the paper's own Limitations admit unmodeled latents — "coaching intent, player cognition, on-pitch communication, and broader match context (e.g., scoreline pressure or fatigue)" [PAGE 12]. Absent these, the generative prior will regress toward the training-modal behavior in high-leverage states (late-game protecting a lead), i.e., crisis regresses to habit. Counter-trap: a top opponent sets deliberate bait (inviting pressure to break lines); GenTac cannot represent intent-to-deceive because intent is not in tracking coordinates. Clock: the autoregressive rollout [Eq. 7–8] has no time-to-go conditioning beyond raw position index; game-clock state is not an input, so risk-vs-clock modulation is invisible to \(\epsilon_\theta\). Verdict: GenTac models observable kinematics, not the recursive cognition that drives tactical branching — consistent with its stated scope [PAGE 12], and a hard ceiling on any claim of "reasoning about the opponent."
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto proof (reject any rollout violating these hard constraints):
1. Speed cap. Centroid displacement obeys \(\lVert c_t-c_{t-1}\rVert_2\le\Delta t\,v_{max}\) [PAGE 29]. Individual displacement must obey \(\lVert p^i_t-p^i_{t-1}\rVert_2\le\Delta t\,v\). At 25 FPS (\(\Delta t=0.04\) s), \(v\le10\) m/s ⇒ per-frame step \(\le0.40\) m. Elite peak sprint reaches \(\sim12.4\) m/s ⇒ \(\le0.50\) m; sustained \(>\sim12\) m/s or \(>0.5\) m/frame is biomechanically implausible → veto. The paper's \(v_{max}\approx10\) m/s is a soft cap; a rigorous check vetoes the \(10\)–\(12.5\) m/s band as sprint-transient and \(>12.5\) m/s as impossible.
2. Acceleration cap. Human initial acceleration \(\lesssim 6\)–\(8\ \text{m/s}^2\); implying \(\lVert p^i_{t+1}-2p^i_t+p^i_{t-1}\rVert_2\le a\,\Delta t^2\approx8\times0.0016=0.013\) m as a jerk/curvature gate. Diffusion denoising does not enforce this; samples with super-human second differences must be vetoed.
3. Pitch-boundary cap. \(x\in[-52.5,52.5]\) m, \(y\in[-34,34]\) m [PAGE 23]. Any generated coordinate outside (beyond a small GK margin) is inadmissible; surface area \({}\le7140\) m² and stretch index \(\le62.5\) m are direct corollaries.
4. Non-penetration. Two entities cannot occupy the same point; \(\lVert p^i-p^k\rVert_2\ge\) body radius \(\sim0.3\) m — a soft veto for training-distribution realism.

Weather/cross-layer veto: the model ingests only BEV coordinates [PAGE 14]; surface state (wet/frozen pitch) modulates achievable \(v\), \(a\), and ball roll but is absent. Therefore any rollout implying dry-pitch kinematics in adverse weather is unfalsifiable by the model and should be vetoed on physical grounds. Proof of insufficiency: the input tensor \(T\in\mathbb R^{L\times(2N+1)\times2}\) has no channel for surface, wind, or fatigue, so weather realism is outside the model's support.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable veto scenarios, each a falsifiable test of a headline claim, with a concrete predicted outcome.

Veto 1 — Oracle removal (opponent-conditioning). Claim under test: opponent conditioning yields "min-ADE 1.30 m at 5 s" [PAGE 6]. Test: re-run Eq. 8 while replacing the opponent's ground-truth future \(x^j_{[H:T]}\) with GenTac's *own* sampled opponent future (a legal, deployable configuration). Prediction: 5s min-ADE degrades from 1.30 m back toward the unconditioned 4.55 m band (expect \(>2.5\) m), because the oracle term was carrying the gain. If instead it holds near 1.3 m, the model has genuine interaction structure — reject the veto.

Veto 2 — Objective-guidance causality. Claim: offensive guidance raises off-ball xT by \(+0.87\) and defensive guidance lowers it by \(-0.41\) [PAGE 8, Tables 14–15]. Confound: offensive fine-tuning uses only threat-segment (goal/saved/off-target) trajectories, so the "steering" may be regression to the fine-tuning subset's mean shape, not causal control. Test: guide with a held-out objective direction not present in fine-tuning (e.g., a synthetic "retreat" objective) and measure whether xT/depth/width move monotonically with the requested direction. Prediction: if responses are mere subset-regression, out-of-distribution objectives produce non-monotonic or null deltas (the veto fires); monotonic response refutes it.

Veto 3 — Event identifiability ceiling. Claim: event forecasting is "highly accurate" [PAGE 2]. Structural limit: goal vs shot-off-target are 2D-indistinguishable [PAGE 40]. Test: restrict evaluation to the confusable set {Goal, Shot off target, Shot saved} and report top-1 recall. Prediction: per Table 19 these already sit at 33.3 / 36.4 / 28.6% recall@1, so the confusable-set top-1 ceiling stays \(\lesssim 40\%\) regardless of \(K\); raising \(K\) lifts only top-3/top-5 manifold coverage, not top-1 discrimination. The 94.3% top-5 headline is therefore a coverage artifact for this subset — veto confirmed a priori.

Net adjudication: GenTac passes physical-layer vetoes only if per-frame kinematic and boundary gates are added post-hoc (they are not in the current pipeline); it fails deployability on Veto 1 unless re-evaluated without oracle futures; its counterfactual and event claims survive only under the narrow interpretations above. These are the exact regimes where its reported numbers should not be trusted at face value.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
