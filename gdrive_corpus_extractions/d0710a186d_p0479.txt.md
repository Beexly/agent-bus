<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The veto architecture is layered. Layer 1 (sign/cognitive) proposes $r_{\mathrm{new}}$ via the extrapolation operator $\mathcal{I}$ [PAGE 448]. Layer 2 (biomechanical/physical) computes energy expenditure $E[r_{\mathrm{new}}]\,[\mathrm{J}]$, joint torque $\boldsymbol{\tau}\,[\mathrm{N\cdot m}]$, and manipulanda compatibility $M(o,m)\in\{0,1\}^{k}$ [PAGE 448]. Layer 3 (weather/environmental) supplies friction coefficient $\mu$ (dimensionless), visibility range $\delta\,[\mathrm{m}]$, and wind force $F_{\mathrm{w}}\,[\mathrm{N}]$.

Veto operator. Execution of $r_{\mathrm{new}}$ is permitted iff
$$
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[E[r_{\mathrm{new}}]\le E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,[\mathrm{J}]\bigr]\,
\cdot\,\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[M(o,m)=1\;\forall o\in r_{\mathrm{new}}\bigr]\,
\cdot\,\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\mu\ge\mu_{\min}\bigr]\,
\cdot\,\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\delta\ge\delta_{\min}\bigr]\,
\cdot\,\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[F_{\mathrm{w}}\le F_{\max}\,[\mathrm{N}]\bigr].
$$
If any factor is zero, $\mathcal{V}=0$ and the behavior-adjustment loop is broken: no motor activity is supported, regardless of how strongly the sign gestalt favors $r_{\mathrm{new}}$ [PAGE 448].

Proof from source. Tolman defines manipulanda as object characters that support motor activity, derived jointly from the object's independent physical character and the organism's response-organ make-up; they are stand-on-able, pick-up-able, sit-in-able, etc. [PAGE 448]. If a platform's resistance exceeds the organism's force-generating capacity, then $M(o,m)=0$ for "stand-on-able" and the object is not a manipulandum for that organism. The cognitive extrapolation may still generate a route over the platform because sign objects are artifactual [PAGE 448], but the physical layer vetoes execution. Weather enters through $\mu$ and $\delta$: rain lowers $\mu$ below the threshold needed to exert the tangential forces required by the route, and fog lowers $\delta$ below the perceptual grasp required to even constitute $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{grasped}}$ [PAGE 448]. Thus the veto is not metaphorical; it is a necessary condition for the behavior-adjustments Tolman describes [PAGE 448]. A sign-gestalt preference that crosses a veto boundary is treated by the organism as if the route does not exist, producing an observable abort or approach-withdrawal pattern that is verifiable with force plates, motion capture, and environmental instrumentation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-with-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios, each with formal veto conditions, observable signatures, and falsifiable predictions.

Scenario 1 — Creative-instability ablation. Veto condition: $\gamma\to0$ via pharmacological or lesion manipulation of exploratory neuromodulation. Formal prediction: $\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{actual}},\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{grasped}};0)=\mathrm{id}$, so $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{novel}}=\varnothing$ and $P(\mathrm{adopt}\mid r_{\mathrm{new}})=0$ for any shortcut never overtly traversed [PAGE 448]. Observable signature: in a maze with a physically available but never-experienced shortcut, the ablated group's route entropy $H(\mathcal{R})$ collapses to $\log|\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{actual}}|$, and no subject selects the shortcut across $N$ trials. Falsification: if any ablated subject selects the shortcut, the zero-instability singularity is violated, implying creative instability is not necessary for inventive ideation [PAGE 448].

Scenario 2 — Manipulanda resistance veto. Veto condition: increase the mass or resistance of a "stand-on-able" platform such that $M(o,m)=0$ and $E[r_{\mathrm{platform}}]>E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,[\mathrm{J}]$ [PAGE 448]. Formal prediction: the cognitive layer still extrapolates $r_{\mathrm{new}}$ over the platform because sign objects are artifactual [PAGE 448], but $\mathcal{V}=0$ at the biomechanical layer. Observable signature: instrumented force plates show approach, attempted weight transfer, and abort; the subject remains on the ground route despite preferring the platform route in a non-motoric choice probe (e.g., looking-time or lever-press preference). Falsification: if the subject executes the platform route despite measured force exceeding $E_{\mathrm{cap}}$, then Tolman's manipulanda-relational definition is false [PAGE 448].

Scenario 3 — Acute time-pressure least-effort violation. Veto condition: $\tau_{\mathrm{crisis}}<\tau_{\mathrm{extrapolation}}$ imposed by a hard deadline. Formal prediction: under deadline pressure, $\mathcal{I}$ cannot complete, so the organism selects $r_{\mathrm{habit}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{actual}}$ even when $E[r_{\mathrm{habit}}]>E[r_{\mathrm{novel}}]\,[\mathrm{J}]$ [PAGE 448]. This directly probes Tolman's unproven least-effort principle [PAGE 448]: the principle is rejected as a universal law of acute choice. Observable signature: within-subject energy-time tradeoff curve shows $P(\mathrm{choose}\,r_{\mathrm{novel}})\to0$ as deadline approaches, while $P(\mathrm{choose}\,r_{\mathrm{novel}})$ is high under no deadline. Falsification: if subjects always select the minimum-energy route regardless of deadline, then least effort holds even under temporal veto, contradicting the timescale-gated architecture derived above.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
