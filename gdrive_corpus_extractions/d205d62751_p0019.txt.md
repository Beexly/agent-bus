The multi-hop derivation proceeds as follows. First, each inertial unit performs dead reckoning: angular rate integrates to orientation in SO(3), specific force is rotated into the navigation frame, gravity is subtracted, and the result is double-integrated to position. This process is inherently unstable: gyro bias $b_g$ produces orientation error growing linearly in time, $\delta\theta(t) \approx b_g t$, while accelerometer bias $b_a$ produces position error growing quadratically, $\delta p(t) \approx \tfrac12 R b_a t^2$. Consequently, uncorrected inertial-only tracking has a hard breakdown regime at $t_{fail} \sim \sqrt{2\epsilon_{tol}/\|b_a\|}$ for a position tolerance $\epsilon_{tol}$.

Second, relative drift between adjoining segments is suppressed by a kinematic constraint: for segments i and j sharing joint J, the claim requires setting the computed joint center of segment i equal to that of segment j, $p_i + R_i r_i^J = p_j + R_j r_j^J = p_J$. This removes inter-segment drift but leaves a global gauge freedom—overall position and orientation of the entire kinematic chain remain unobservable from inertial data alone. Third, external positional aiding closes this gauge by measuring at least one segment in an external frame and applying a correction that "eliminate[s] overall drift in the frame of reference of the tracking system" [PAGE 17–18].

Fourth, because multiple positional units and multiple inertial units exist, a data-association problem arises. Claim 27 generalizes this to a cost-function optimization over candidate assignments in an arbitrary frame, selecting the lowest-cost association [PAGE 18]. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The algorithm's trust structure is recursive: it trusts inertial predictions to predict positional-unit trajectories, then trusts positional measurements to correct inertial predictions, then trusts the corrected trajectories to re-predict positional units for the next association cycle. Under crisis—positional-aiding outage—the system regresses to pure dead reckoning, its default habit. This regression is dangerous because the very quantity used to validate association (predicted positional-unit trajectory) becomes increasingly unreliable as drift accumulates, creating a self-reinforcing error loop: bad predictions produce bad associations, bad associations produce bad corrections, and bad corrections produce worse predictions.

Counter-trap: an adversarial or noisy environment can present multiple positional measurements whose costs are nearly identical ($\Delta J<\epsilon_J$). A greedy lowest-cost selector will arbitrarily pick one, potentially locking onto a reflected or permuted assignment. The recursive fix is to maintain a distribution over associations or to veto updates when the cost gap falls below a threshold, preserving the previous transform rather than committing to an ambiguous one.

Clock: temporal synchronization is the hidden governor. Positional measurements (camera shutter, GPS epoch, UWB packet) are timestamped in the external frame; IMU samples are timestamped in the sensor clock. Associating across clock skew $\Delta t$ without interpolation injects error $\delta p \approx v\,\Delta t$. At human motion speeds (1–2 m/s), a 10 ms sync error yields ~1–2 cm error; at limb-swing speeds (3–5 m/s), the same skew yields 3–5 cm. The system must therefore treat clock offset as a state to be estimated, not a constant, and veto association when measured inter-sensor timestamp jitter exceeds the integration timestep.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
