Because SportsMetrics inputs consist exclusively of timestamps, play descriptions, team–player affiliation tables, and game recaps [PAGE 1–4], the intersection \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{phys}} \cap \mathcal{I}\) is empty. Therefore the physical/biomechanical/weather veto is triggered for every instance: the benchmark provably excludes the physical layer.

Biomechanical veto proof: player load, acceleration, deceleration, jump mechanics, and contact forces are not encoded in play-by-play text [PAGE 1–2]. The model cannot infer fatigue from timestamps alone; thus any causal claim about biomechanical degradation over the course of a game is vetoed by input absence. Weather veto proof: no atmospheric variable (temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind) appears in ESPN play-by-play descriptions [PAGE 3–4]; indoor arena games further nullify weather relevance, but the absence is structural rather than conditional. Physical veto proof: court dimensions, rim height, ball pressure, and altitude are constant background knowledge not provided per-instance; the model must import them from parametric memory, which violates the information-fusion requirement that all evidence be present in the context [PAGE 1–2].

Adjudication outcome: all performance metrics \(\Delta \text{Points}\) and \(\Delta \text{GScore}\) are conditional on the text–numerical subspace only. Any deviation attributed to physical, biomechanical, or weather factors is outside the measurable boundary of the experiment. The veto is absolute: the source corpus contains zero physical-layer telemetry, so no cross-layer correction can be computed from the provided data. This is not a modeling limitation to be overcome; it is a scope definition that excludes the physical domain by construction.
