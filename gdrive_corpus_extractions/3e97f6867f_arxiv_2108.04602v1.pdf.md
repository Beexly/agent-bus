The paper proposes JMODT, a joint detection-and-tracking pipeline for camera-LiDAR autonomous driving. The multi-hop derivation begins at sensor calibration, which gates every downstream operation: fused 2D-3D point-wise features require extrinsic alignment; without it, the region proposal network (RPN) consumes misregistered RoIs and the correlation network learns noisy pair-wise dependencies. The architecture then splits into parallel detection and correlation branches that share proposal features, avoiding the redundant computation of tracking-by-detection (TBD) cascades. Each selected proposal feature is assigned a unique ID label; adjacent-frame feature pairs are correlated by absolute subtraction, producing an M×N matrix for M proposals in frame t−1 and N in frame t. This matrix is row- and column-averaged to capture global inter-object context, then fed to softmax-ranked Re-ID and sigmoid-activated start-end estimators.

The gray-zone audit centers on empirically fixed hyperparameters masquerading as principled design choices. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under dense target distributions and heavy occlusion, a regressed system reverts to TBD-like behavior—detection confidence dominates, Re-ID features become ambiguous due to tiny inter-object appearance differences, and ID switches proliferate [PAGE 1]. JMODT counters this by forcing the correlation network to regress symmetric start-end features batched from N “start” and M “end” representations [PAGE 3], effectively giving the model a theory of other objects’ birth and death intentions across adjacent frames.

Counter-trap: The MIP objective applies a negative coefficient (x_cls − 1) to classification confidence [PAGE 5], which acts as a counter-trap against premature trajectory creation from false positives. If a detector hallucinates a vehicle, low x_cls drives y_cls toward zero unless offset by strong affinity y_aff or start-end y_se evidence. This regresses the “crisis habit” of accepting every detection as a track, replacing it with a joint optimization over detection confidence, appearance-motion affinity, and start-end probability simultaneously.

Clock: The real-time constraint is explicit: 0.01 s per frame on KITTI [PAGE 6], 50 training epochs with AdamW, batch size 12, cosine annealing at 2e-4 [PAGE 5]. The recursive theory of mind here is temporal: the Kalman filter predicts ˆµ_{t+1} from µ_t under constant velocity [PAGE 4], while start-end estimation reasons symmetrically about whether measurement d initiates a new ID or track k terminates an obsolete one [PAGE 4–5]. When the clock budget is violated—e.g., LiDAR point cloud density spikes or camera exposure lengthens—the parallel branches no longer execute within the frame period, causing track management to default to tentative tracks with n_miss = 1 and increasing fragmentation risk [PAGE 5].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: Camera-LiDAR extrinsic calibration is a single point of failure. Point-wise feature fusion [PAGE 2] assumes precise 2D-3D correspondence; sub-degree rotation or centimeter translation errors corrupt appearance affinity A_app and 3D box generation B_d, invalidating the 3D-DIoU metric’s geometric terms. LiDAR sparsity at long range further degrades box center (x,y,z) and size (l,w,h) estimates in the KF state vector [PAGE 4], breaking the observation model o_t.

Biomechanical veto: The constant-velocity KF assumption [PAGE 4] is valid for highway cars but invalid for pedestrians, cyclists, or cut-in vehicles with non-linear acceleration. The state vector lacks lateral jerk or angular acceleration terms, so complex motion regions (Table IV Case 1, Region 1 [PAGE 6]) induce motion affinity drift and ID switches unless compensated by high Re-ID appearance weight—yet β = 10α suppresses appearance [PAGE 5].

Weather veto: KITTI evaluation occurs under clear daylight [PAGE 5]. Rain, fog, or snow reduce LiDAR returns and camera contrast, simultaneously degrading point-wise fusion and 2D appearance features. No robustness numbers are reported for adverse conditions, so the system has no proven weather operating envelope.

Veto proof conditions: (1) Calibration error below the point-wise fusion tolerance (unquantified in source); (2) Object motion approximately constant velocity over one frame interval; (3) Visibility sufficient for both camera intensity and LiDAR reflectivity returns; (4) Detection confidence thresholds matched to the w_cls = 100 penalty scaling. Violation of any condition triggers a hard veto on track identity continuity.
