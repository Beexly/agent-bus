Given: A football team possesses \(N\) players. Let \(\mathbf{v} = (v_1, v_2, \dots, v_N)\) be the vector representing the individual utility contribution of each player during a specific phase of play, measured in signed displacement vectors (yards gained/lost). Assume each player has a participation indicator function \(p_i(t) \in \{0, 1\}\) such that \(p_i(t)=1\) iff player \(i\) is actively engaged in the play at time \(t\).

**Total System Utility (Waste):**
\[
U_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \int_0^T v_i(t) p_i(t) \, dt
\]
where \(T\) is the duration of the phase.

Define **Waste Function**:
\[
W = U_{\text{total}} - \max_{\mathbf{p}(t)} U_{\text{total}}
\]
subject to the constraint that \(\sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(t) = K\) (fixed number of participants per unit time) and the causality condition that \(v_i(t)\) is observable only when \(p_i(t)=1\).

Prove that the optimal policy satisfies \(p_i(t)=1\) for all \(i\) and all \(t\).

**Proof.**

Consider a policy \(\pi\) under which some player \(j\) is never deployed: \(p_j(t)=0\) for all \(t\). By construction, \(v_j(t)=0\) regardless of the true utility. Now construct an alternative policy \(\pi'\) identical to \(\pi\) except that player \(j\) replaces any participating player \(k\) (if available) at the marginal expense of reducing \(p_k\) by one unit. Under \(\pi'\), the modified contribution of player \(j\) is:
\[
v_j'(t) = 
\begin{cases}
v_j(t) + \Delta v & \text{if } j \text{ replaces } k \text{ with } v_k > v_j \\
v_j(t) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
where \(\Delta v = v_k - v_j > 0\) by definition of replacement. Since the active set remains unchanged (\(\sum_i p_i'=K\)), the new total utility becomes:
\[
U'_{\text{total}}(\pi') = U_{\text{total}}(\pi) + \int_0^T \Delta v \, p_k(t) \, dt \geq U_{\text{total}}(\pi) + \Delta v \cdot m
\]
with \(m\) being the Lebesgue measure of times when player \(k\) is engaged. Because \(\Delta v > 0\) and \(m>0\) (else \(k\) would never participate, contradicting optimality of \(\pi\)), we have \(U'_{\text{total}} > U_{\text{total}}\). Consequently, any policy with a zero-participation player is strictly inferior to one that deploys said player whenever another is active. By induction over all players, the maximal utility is achieved when every player contributes non-zero flow continuously. Thus, the infimum of the waste function is zero, and the optimal policy never wastes participants.

This guarantees that, for almost every realization of \(\mathbf{d}\), there exists a feasible set of participant engagements yielding positive expected improvement. \(\blacksquare\)
