The system embeds a first-order theory of mind about the broadcaster: it assumes that a highlight is lexically signaled by commentator excitement ("beauty," "century," "wicket," "DRS") [PAGE 9 line 10]. Under crisis conditions—high crowd noise, poor microphone placement, or rain-affected audio—the Whisper transcript degrades, and the pipeline's habitual regression is to lower the acceptance threshold τ (e.g., to 0.01 for low quality with crowd noise) to avoid missing events [PAGE 9 Table 1]. This regresses the model toward over-detection: sensitivity is preserved at the cost of precision, producing false positives such as the 7 incorrect timestamps in the test [PAGE 12 Table 4].

The counter-trap is the assumption that parallelism is universally beneficial. The designers parallelize all four stages, yet preprocessing is slower in parallel (1.4 s vs 0.9 s) because lock acquisition, thread management, and the GIL-bound Python bytecode outweigh compute gains [PAGE 13]. The correct recursive move is selective parallelization: only high-complexity stages (ASR, TF-IDF vectorization, cosine similarity) justify thread overhead; simple stages should remain serial, consistent with Alzahrani's finding [PAGE 5].

The clock constraint is broadcast near-real-time delivery. For a 30-minute clip the parallel wall time is 58 s (≈3.2% of content duration). For a full T20 match (~3.5 h ≈ 12,600 s), if speedup holds, estimated time is ≈403 s, but the serial merging phase (≈10.4 s for 30 minutes) scales with the number of detected segments and will dominate as match length and highlight count grow [PAGE 12-13]. If the broadcast clock budget is below the sum of serial merge time plus I/O, the system misses its real-time deadline regardless of ASR speedup. Thus the clock veto triggers whenever T_merge(n) + T_IO exceeds the allotted latency, forcing a fallback to serialized or sampled processing.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The audio channel is the system's sensory boundary. Stadium crowd noise, wind, rain, and compressor artifacts reduce the signal-to-noise ratio of the commentary track. Table 1 explicitly partitions the threshold space by audio quality and crowd noise, admitting that transcription fidelity is physically contingent [PAGE 9]. Biomechanical layer. Commentator speech under stress—rapid delivery, overlapping co-commentators, shouted exclamations—alters the articulation dynamics that Whisper models expect, increasing word-error rates and shifting the cosine-similarity distribution downward. Weather veto. Rain delays or high-wind conditions place the match in the "low audio quality with crowd noise" cell, where τ = 0.01 [PAGE 9 Table 1]. Because τ cannot be reduced below 0 without accepting every chunk, there exists a physical audio regime (SNR below the Whisper operating point) for which no threshold τ ∈ [0,1] simultaneously maintains precision and recall. Formal cross-layer veto condition:
\[
\mathrm{VETO} \equiv \bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{SNR}}_{\text{audio}} < \mathrm{SNR}_{\text{crit}}\bigr) \lor \bigl(\widehat{\mathrm{WER}}_{\text{Whisper}} > \mathrm{WER}_{\text{crit}}\bigr) \lor \bigl(\|v_{\text{chunk}}\|_2 = 0\bigr).
\]
If any clause holds, the NLP layer receives a semantically null or corrupted vector, making cosine similarity either singular (zero norm) or statistically meaningless; the correct adjudication is to suppress automatic highlight acceptance and route the segment to human review. This veto is provable because the acceptance predicate score ≥ τ is a continuous function of v_chunk; as \|v_chunk\|_2 → 0, the similarity gradient vanishes and the decision boundary becomes unidentifiable.
