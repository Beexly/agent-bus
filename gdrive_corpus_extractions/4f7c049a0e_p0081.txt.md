"Eliminated blinds more readily" maps to E[eta | H] > E[eta | A] or equivalently P(eta = 1 | H) > P(eta = 1 | A), where H and A denote the hunger-driven and activity-driven classes.

Consummatory measure: T_lap [s] = duration of milk lapping at g. Locomotory measure: bar v = ell_run / t_run [m s^-1], where ell_run is distance covered and t_run is running time. Verbatim basis for both: [PAGE 1].

Descriptive drive index (ordinal, explicitly non-causal): delta = T_lap / (T_lap + T_explore), delta in [0, 1], where T_explore is time spent in non-goal-directed exploration. Assumption: delta ranks subjects but measures no force or energy. Breakdown: undefined if T_lap = T_explore = 0; collapses to boundary values under satiation (T_lap -> 0) or pure exploration (T_lap -> 0 while ell_run is large).

Conditional blind-entry probability: P(b in B | C), C in {H, A}. Source claim: P(enter b before g | H) < P(enter b before g | A).

Satiety decay across repeated trials n: T_lap(n) = T_0 exp(-n / tau) + T_inf, where n is a dimensionless trial count, tau [trials] is the satiety time constant, and T_inf [s] is residual lapping. Breakdown regime: for n >> tau, T_lap ~ T_inf, destroying class separation based on initial T_lap; if milk volume V_milk < V_threshold, then T_lap -> 0 regardless of assigned class.

Singularity and degeneracy regimes. (i) Goal-devaluation singularity: if food is satiated or devalued, goal value V(g) -> 0 and the hunger/activity distinction collapses. (ii) Speed-efficiency degeneracy: if bar v_A >> bar v_H but eta_A << eta_H, locomotor vigor and goal-directedness are orthogonal dimensions, so bar v cannot be used as a proxy for motivational demand. All variable definitions are anchored to [PAGE 1].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman (1932) occupies a position between Watsonian methodological behaviorism and cognitive accounts of animal conduct. The page's operationalism—inferring "demand" for a goal from consummatory duration and running speed—echoes Hamilton's maze "blind alley" methodology and Small's (1901) rat-maze studies, while resisting Hull's strict drive-reduction formalism by treating behavior as purposive and goal-directed. Verbatim, the source treats rats as if the goal-box food was "relatively strongly demanded," a teleological idiom Tolman later developed through cognitive maps (Tolman, 1948) and latent learning (Tolman & Honzik, 1930). Vicarious trial and error (Muenzinger; Tolman) supplies the plausible mechanism at choice points: the rat pauses at a blind, effectively simulating outcomes before entry.

Forward lineage: the hunger/activity partition anticipates the model-based vs. model-free distinction in reinforcement learning (Sutton & Barto). A model-based agent maintains the transition graph G and computes d_G(s, g); a model-free agent caches state values and may run faster (higher bar v) yet select blinds habitually. Hippocampal place-cell representations (O'Keefe & Nadel, 1978) provide the neural substrate for d_G(s, g), and successor representations (Dayan, 1993) unify the two by caching expected future state occupancy.

Multi-week traps. Across weeks, latent learning predicts a nonlinear "sudden improvement" in eta once the cognitive map is available, independent of the initial T_lap classification. Homeostatic body-weight regulation causes T_lap to oscillate with feeding schedule; repeated handling reduces neophobia, altering bar v; and experimenter expectancy can bias blind-alley scoring if the scorer knows the drive class. A protocol that re-classifies animals weekly using initial T_lap will suffer from non-stationarity: the same rat may be hunger-driven in week 1 and activity-driven in week 4 purely because of circadian feeding phase or residual satiety.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three nested intentionality levels are present. Level 0: the rat acts in the maze. Level 1: the experimenter attributes a goal-state ("food demanded") to the rat based on T_lap at g. Level 2: the experimenter infers that this attributed goal-state caused the pre-arrival path (fewer blinds). The closing footnote ("13 Op. cit., p. 247") shows the experiment is itself a secondary theoretical layer, embedding one researcher's interpretation inside Tolman's synthesis.

Crisis habit regression. Under high motivational urgency, rats may regress from flexible map-based navigation to habitual stimulus-response paths, or urgency may sharpen goal focus and suppress blind entries. The source claims the latter for hunger-driven rats: stronger demand -> more direct path. But "crisis" is ambiguous. If the crisis is the deprivation state, high hunger should increase freezing or thigmotaxis rather than directness; if the crisis is the choice-point conflict at a blind, demand resolves conflict at the gate of g. The source does not disambiguate these.

Counter-trap. The experimenter's binary classification is a projection of human folk psychology onto the rat. [PAGE 1] provides no evidence that the rat represents itself as "hunger-driven" versus "activity-driven"; those are experimenter-imposed latent classes. A recursive theory-of-mind error occurs if we assume the rat has a stable preference ordering over goals that generalizes across contexts, when T_lap may reflect orosensory satiety, milk viscosity, or postingestive feedback rather than abstract goal value.

Clock. The temporal order of inference is inverted relative to the trial. The trial clock runs s -> blinds -> g -> lapping, but the diagnostic inference runs lapping -> demand -> path. Post-ingestive satiety or malaise can therefore retrospectively re-label a previously "hunger-driven" trajectory, collapsing the causal arrow unless trial-by-trial temporal alignment between consummatory and locomotor phases is preserved.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: maze geometry, lighting, floor texture, and odor gradients. Veto condition: if blind alleys are not discriminable via visual or tactile cues (identical texture, no depth change), then eta differences reflect sensory detection limits rather than motivational demand. Adjudication: counterbalance blind-alley positions, rotate the maze between subjects, and record path traces with an automated tracker blind to drive class.

Biomechanical layer: bar v depends on limb kinematics, muscle fatigue, and motor vigor, not only on goal value. Veto condition: if activity-driven rats show bar v_A > bar v_H yet eta_A < eta_H, then high speed coexists with low directness, violating any single-factor "drive" model. Adjudication: measure stride frequency and duty factor independently; separate vigor (bar v) from vector selection (eta).

Weather/environmental layer: ambient temperature and humidity affect milk evaporation, palatability, and evaporative cooling during running; barometric pressure alters odor-plume diffusion to blinds. Veto condition: uncontrolled thermal drift (e.g., > 2 °C session-to-session) or changing odor concentration invalidates T_lap as a stable demand index. Adjudication: log temperature, humidity, and pressure each session; standardize milk temperature and volume; test whether P(enter b | C) persists after controlling for ambient covariates.

Adjudication rule: the claim that "hunger demand causes fewer blinds" survives veto only if (1) eta_H > eta_A holds after sensory equalization of blinds, (2) bar v and eta are statistically dissociated across classes, and (3) the effect is stable across thermal/hygrometric blocks and across re-classification in later weeks.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verification-gated counterfactual veto scenarios, with predictions, using variables defined in SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS and grounded in [PAGE 1].

Veto Scenario 1 (Speed-efficiency decoupling). Counterfactual: experimentally increase bar v for activity-driven rats (e.g., mild novel-arena pre-exposure) without changing T_lap. Prediction: if eta remains low for activity-driven rats while bar v rises, any reward model assigning credit to "speed" for goal-directedness is vetoed. Verifiable test: regress eta on bar v within and across classes; the pooled slope should be approximately zero if motivation and vigor are orthogonal dimensions.

Veto Scenario 2 (Satiation time-constant veto). Counterfactual: measure T_lap and eta over n = 1..20 daily trials and fit T_lap(n) = T_0 exp(-n / tau) + T_inf. Prediction: once n > 3 tau, the correlation between the trial-1 T_lap-based class label and eta at trial n falls below significance (p > 0.05), because satiety erodes the hunger/activity partition. Verifiable test: compute the point-biserial correlation between class label (from trial 1) and eta at trial n; veto if it decays to null by the second week.

Veto Scenario 3 (Perceptual salience veto). Counterfactual: make blind alleys perceptually salient (distinct floor texture plus odor marker) versus non-salient (identical to the main path). Prediction: the group difference in P(enter b | H) vs. P(enter b | A) disappears in the salient condition, showing that the original effect was driven by differential detection of dead ends rather than differential goal demand. Verifiable test: a significant Class x Salience interaction on eta with a null simple effect of Class in the salient condition vetoes the motivational interpretation.
