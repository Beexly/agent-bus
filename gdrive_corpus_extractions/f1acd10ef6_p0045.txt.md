Crisis habit regression operates as follows: when anti-aircraft fire threatens, the aviator reverts to trained drill, collapsing the maneuver distribution toward a narrow set of rehearsed responses. The entropy \(H(\mathbf{a}\mid \text{crisis})\) drops relative to baseline, making the aircraft more predictable in the short term. This is the first-order theory of mind: the predictor assumes the pilot will behave according to statistical preferences. The counter-trap is second-order: the pilot knows the predictor expects crisis regression, and therefore deliberately breaks habit—executing an irregular turn or altitude change precisely when the predictor expects standardization. Wiener’s page supports this by noting that regular habits are “not a certain thing that he does universally” [PAGE 71], so there is always residual variance for the pilot to exploit recursively.

The clock variable is shell time of flight \(\tau\). The predictor must output \(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t+\tau)\) before impact, but the pilot’s evasive decision at \(t+\delta\) (\(\delta<\tau\)) is observed only after the shell has already been launched. If \(\tau\) exceeds a critical horizon \(\tau_c\) set by shell velocity, turn radius, and lethal radius, the conditional entropy \(H(\mathbf{x}(t+\tau)\mid\mathcal{H}_t)\) grows beyond the predictor’s channel capacity. In crisis, habit regression temporarily lowers entropy, but counter-adaptation restores it; the net effect is that the prediction error covariance \(P(t+\tau\mid t)\) inflates as \(\tau/\tau_c\to 1\). Wiener’s singularity statement—“no amount of skill would permit us to fill in the as yet unknown motion … between the time when the gun was fired, and the later time when the shell should arrive” [PAGE 71]—is the formal boundary condition: beyond \(\tau_c\), with arbitrary or counter-adapted evasion, the intercept problem is singular and fire-solution generation must veto.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three hard veto layers bound the evasion space and keep prediction from collapsing into the arbitrary-evasion singularity.

Physical layer: shell ballistics impose \(\tau=R/v_{\text{muzzle}}\) with drag-limited energy decay. If target range \(R\) exceeds maximum effective range \(R_{\max}\), or if shell time-of-flight \(\tau\) exceeds the predictor’s credible horizon, the fire solution is vetoed. Units: \(R\) [m], \(v_{\text{muzzle}}\) [m s\(^{-1}\)], \(\tau\) [s].

Biomechanical layer: Wiener explicitly identifies the pilot’s physiological ceiling: rapid turns generate centrifugal force that “will render him unconscious” [PAGE 71]. Formally, \(|a_n|=v^2/r\le a_{\max}\approx 9g_0=88.26\ \text{m s}^{-2}\). Any maneuver demanding \(a_n>a_{\max}\) is infeasible and removed from the action space. This layer is decisive: without it, the pilot could execute arbitrarily high-turn-rate evasion, driving \(H(\mathbf{a})\to\infty\) and making \(I(\mathcal{H}_t;\mathbf{x}_{t+\tau})\to 0\).

Weather layer: wind shear, density altitude, and temperature alter both shell trajectory (ballistic coefficient, drag) and aircraft turn performance (stall speed, lift available). If measured atmospheric state lies outside the ballistic or aerodynamic envelope, the solution is vetoed.

Adjudication proof: the intersection of these layers produces a compact, bounded maneuver set \(\mathcal{A}_{\text{feasible}}\subset\mathbb{R}^3\). Because \(\mathcal{A}_{\text{feasible}}\) has finite metric entropy, the mutual information between past history and future position remains strictly positive for \(\tau<\tau_c\), and prediction is well-posed. The veto layers are what prevent Wiener’s “perfectly arbitrary evasive action” from being physically realizable; they convert an unbounded prediction problem into a bounded statistical estimation problem.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The service allocator continues reapportioning channels according to cumulative usage \(f_i(t)\) over window \(W\) after a change point where \(\lambda_A\) falls by more than 50 percent and \(\lambda_B\) rises correspondingly. The veto freezes reallocation until \(|\Delta\lambda_i|/W<\epsilon\) for two consecutive windows. Prediction: after veto-triggered recalibration, Mr. B’s mean waiting time will fall in proportion to the new \(\lambda_A/\lambda_B\) ratio, and the variance of per-subscriber service delay will decrease monotonically over the next \(W\), verifiable from call-detail records.
