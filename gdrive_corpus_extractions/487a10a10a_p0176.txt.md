With minimal pads/helmets and historically high fatality, physical clearance is a hard veto. \(V_{\text{phys}}=1\) iff \(\exists i:\) player \(p_i\) fails medical readiness or protective equipment falls below contemporary safety threshold. Units: binary health state; no derived odds. This overrides all other layers because an unavailable player sets \(T_i = \infty\) and \(T_{11}=\infty\).

Biomechanical layer: Shifting with "mechanical precision" [PAGE 162] on grass/dirt requires adequate surface coefficient of friction \(\mu\) and traction. \(V_{\text{bio}}=1\) iff \(\mu < \mu_{\text{crit}}\) for cutting maneuvers, or if expected impact force in scrimmage exceeds tissue tolerance \(F_{\text{tissue}}\) [N]. Breakdown: acute lower-extremity injury rate \(\lambda_{\text{inj}}\) exceeds recovery rate \(\mu_{\text{rec}}\) [events/week].

Weather layer: Rain, mud, snow, or fog degrade shift precision and make "hurry" unsafe. \(V_{\text{weather}}=1\) iff visibility \(<\text{vis}_{\text{crit}}\) [m], surface water depth \(> \text{depth}_{\text{crit}}\) [m], or temperature \(< T_{\text{hypothermia}}\) [K].

Adjudication order: physical veto is evaluated first (player availability), then biomechanical (surface/load tolerance), then weather. Only if all \(V_\ell = 0\) does the practice proceed. This is a veto proof, not a trade-off; no compensation in one layer can override a veto in another.
