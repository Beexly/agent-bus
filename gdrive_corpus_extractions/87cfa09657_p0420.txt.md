That is a retrocausal trap: Tolman lacked the measurement technology to estimate w_i(C) in seconds, bits, or inverse temperature, so his "future psychology" remains a counterfactual program rather than an executable model. Any claim that Tolman anticipated modern individual-differences RL must be verified against the actual units and breakdown regimes he did not provide.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Tolman attributes a first-order theory of mind to the rat: the animal is conceived not as a passive stimulus register but as an agent with purposes, expectancies, and a means-end hierarchy navigating the maze. The recursive layer is the experimenter's theory of the rat's theory of the environment—Tolman models the rat as representing the maze and its own goals within it. Crisis habit regression enters when the animal, placed in a novel or stressful maze configuration, reverts from purposive behavior to habitual stimulus-response chains. In the proposed bright/dull comparison, a "dull" animal's apparent failure to weight frequency or recency appropriately may reflect stress-induced habit regression rather than a genuine deficit in means-end capacity or retentivity. The crisis thus masquerades as a capacity difference.

The counter-trap is the complementary error: assuming that a null interaction between capacity stratum and stimulus-law weighting falsifies Tolman's purposive-behavior framework. A null result may instead reflect insufficient statistical power, mismatched retentivity time constants, ceiling effects in maze performance, or floor effects in discriminability. Attributing a stable, observable capacity trait to the animal when the design cannot isolate it is a theory-of-mind error projected onto the subject.

The clock is the temporal substrate of the experiment. Spaced repetitions require an inter-trial interval (ITI) measured in seconds; recency operates on an eligibility-trace decay τ_rec in seconds; frequency accumulates over a dimensionless trial count. If the experimental clock (ITI) is not matched to the subject's biological clock (τ_ret(C)), the stimulus laws cannot be fairly compared across strata. A bright rat with τ_ret = 300 s tested at ITI = 30 s will show massed-practice characteristics; a dull rat with τ_ret = 30 s tested at ITI = 300 s will show accelerated forgetting. The clock mismatch is then misattributed to capacity rather than to scheduling. Valid inference therefore requires that the experimental clock be calibrated to each stratum's measured retentivity constant before any claim about differential weighting of frequency versus recency can be adjudicated.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The maze must be physically traversable by Rattus norvegicus. With a typical body width of roughly 6–8 cm, maze corridor width must exceed body width plus clearance (≥ 10 cm). If corridor width is below the biomechanical threshold, the animal cannot reach the manipulanda, and means-end capacity c_ME is unobservable regardless of motivational state. Running surface friction, incline, and rung diameter determine traversal speed v in m/s; if v falls below the minimum required to complete a trial within the session timeout, the trial is hard-vetoed at the physical layer.

Biomechanical layer. Retentivity, ideation, and consciousness-ability presuppose intact sensorimotor integration. Fatigue, illness, or locomotor impairment reduces c_LOCO below the threshold c_LOCO,min required for maze completion. Formal veto: if c_LOCO < c_LOCO,min, then all cognitive weightings w_i(C) are undefined for that subject, because behavior cannot be emitted. A strain difference in running speed that is independent of cognition therefore vetoes any inference that the strain difference in w_i is psychological.

Weather layer. Although Tolman's experiments were conducted indoors, ambient laboratory conditions operate as a weather layer on the rat. The thermoneutral zone for the laboratory rat is approximately 24–30 °C; deviations beyond ±5 °C impose thermoregulatory stress that alters corticosterone, retentivity τ_ret, and frequency sensitivity. Relative humidity outside 40–60% RH degrades olfactory cue persistence in the maze, directly modulating discriminanda capacity c_DISC measured in bits. Veto condition: if |T_lab − T_thermoneutral| > 5 °C or RH ∉ [40, 60]%, the stratum comparison is invalid because capacity is environmentally modulated rather than trait-stable.

Adjudication. A claimed difference in w_i between bright and dull strata is admissible only if all three layers pass simultaneously: physical dimensions permit traversal, biomechanical capacity permits trial completion, and weather-layer variables are held constant or modeled as covariates. Failure at any layer induces a hard veto on the psychological inference; the data cannot support a conclusion about capacity-dependent stimulus laws unless the animal was physically able to perform, biomechanically uncompromised, and environmentally buffered.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (verifiable counterfactual) veto scenarios for Tolman's capacity–stimulus interdependence hypothesis, each with a falsifiable prediction.

Scenario 1 — Genetic mismatch veto (genetic layer). Counterfactual condition: the "bright" and "dull" strains used in a replication are not actually differentiated for the specific maze apparatus (different visual cues, manipulanda, or reward contingencies than those used in Tryon's original selection). Prediction: no significant strain × repetition-schedule interaction on learning rate; w_freq(C_bright) ≈ w_freq(C_dull) within confidence bounds. Verification: fit a mixed-effects model with strain, schedule, and their interaction; if the interaction term is not significant (p > 0.05), the genetic precondition for differential weighting is vetoed and Tolman's interdependence claim fails for that apparatus.

Scenario 2 — Retentivity equating veto (temporal layer). Counterfactual condition: inter-trial intervals are individually titrated so that each rat's effective memory decay τ_ret is equated across strata (for example, dull rats receive shorter ITIs to compensate for faster forgetting). Prediction: the differential weighting of recency versus frequency disappears; w_rec(C) converges across bright and dull groups. Verification: fit trial-by-trial choice persistence with an exponential eligibility trace exp(−Δt / τ_ret); if fitted τ_ret is statistically equal across groups and the recency weighting no longer differs by stratum, the temporal mechanism underlying the capacity distinction is vetoed.

Scenario 3 — Biomechanical confound veto (locomotor layer). Counterfactual condition: maze running speed differs systematically between strains because of body mass or limb length, independent of cognitive capacity. Prediction: apparent capacity effects on stimulus-law weighting are fully mediated by completion time; when running speed is entered as a covariate, the strain effect on each w_i becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero. Verification: run an ANCOVA with strain and running speed as predictors of w_i; if the strain term drops below the significance threshold after covariate adjustment, the cognitive-capacity interpretation is vetoed at the biomechanical layer.

Each scenario produces a binary outcome: if the stated counterfactual condition holds and the prediction is observed, the corresponding layer vetoes Tolman's claim that capacity level modifies stimulus-law weighting for that subject population.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
