Physical layer: The unit "lbs." in "Strengths of Pull ranging between 74 and 64 lbs." [PAGE 1] is force, but the source does not specify the dynamometer protocol (hand grip, back lift, leg lift, or finger pull). 64–74 lbf (≈ 285–329 N) is physiologically plausible for a grip or small-muscle pull but far too low for a maximal back or leg pull in adult men. Veto condition: if the measurement instrument and joint angle are not declared, the CDF on [PAGE 1] is incommensurable across studies and no cross-group comparison is admissible.

Biomechanical layer: Force production depends on muscle activation, physiological cross-sectional area (PCSA), moment arm, joint angle, and contraction velocity. Galton's curve assumes one fixed isometric protocol. Because PCSA scales approximately with length² under geometric similarity, the stature multiplier 1·08 [PAGE 1] cannot be linearly transposed to strength: doing so confounds length (m) with force (N·m⁻¹·kg·s² dimensions). Veto condition: applying 1·08 to strength is a dimensional veto. Strength-specific scaling must be derived from sex-stratified PCSA or direct force measurements, not stature.

Environmental / weather layer: Galton's anthropometric data were gathered indoors under controlled laboratory conditions. Field deployment introduces temperature-dependent vasoconstriction (reducing grip force in cold), humidity-dependent friction, and time-of-day circadian variation in strength. Veto condition: pooling lab-derived grades [PAGE 1] with field-collected strength without temperature and humidity covariates invalidates the Grade intercept because the underlying curve shifts with ambient conditions.

Adjudication order: (1) Protocol veto—undefined pull modality invalidates the unit; (2) Dimensional veto—1·08 stature factor barred from strength; (3) Skew veto—|γ₁| > 0.5 voids 50th-Grade mean comparison per Galton's cutoff warning [PAGE 1]; (4) Environmental veto—uncontrolled field conditions void lab-derived Grades. If any layer vetoes, the analyst must fall back to raw force time series in N with protocol metadata, not to interpolated Grades.

Scenario 1 — Small-n interpolation veto. Counterfactual: n = 30 strength pulls are recorded and the analyst uses interpolated M as the group representative. Veto rule: Galton's insignificance claim applies to n ≈ 100–200 [PAGE 1]; below n ≈ 50, order-statistic spacing exceeds one Grade. Verifiable prediction: a bootstrap 95% confidence interval for the median exceeds ±2 lbf (±9 N), i.e., more than 6% of the 64–74 lbf interval; consequently, classification at a 68 lbf cut-off flips in >15% of resamples. The point-M decision is vetoed; only the Grade-interval certificate (30 percent between 64 and 74 lbs [PAGE 1]) may be reported with its sampling band.

Scenario 2 — Cross-dimension scaling veto. Counterfactual: an analyst applies the 1·08 stature multiplier [PAGE 1] to female grip strength to predict male grip strength. Veto rule: strength is force (N), stature is length (m); a dimensionless multiplier on length does not yield force, and under geometric similarity force scales with L². Verifiable prediction: predicted male median = 1.08 × female median, but measured adult grip-strength ratios are ≈ 1.6–1.75 (≈ 48–62% higher), producing an error of ≈ 40–50%. The veto triggers and the fallback is sex-stratified empirical CDFs Q_men(p) and Q_women(p) with no transmutation factor.

Scenario 3 — Median–mean skew veto. Counterfactual: two groups (men and women) have identical 50th Grades but different means because one distribution is right-skewed by high-force outliers. Veto rule: per Galton's cutoff caveat [PAGE 1], if |γ₁| > 0.5, median equality does not imply mean equality. Verifiable prediction: Group A and Group B both have Q(0.5) = 70 lbf (≈ 311 N), but E[A] = 72 lbf and E[B] = 80 lbf. An analyst using only the 50th Grade concludes "no difference" (Type II error), whereas a mean-based test detects the difference with power < 0.30 at α = 0.05 when restricted to medians. The veto forces reporting of both Q(0.5) and trimmed means, with the interval probability P(64 ≤ S ≤ 74) [PAGE 1] as the primary decision certificate.
