Step one — operator recovery. The paper gives four explicit formulas on [PAGE 3]: an ACWR ratio with a protected denominator, a workload-spike rate, a three-term fatigue index, and three secondary indices. The first multi-hop derivation is algebraic, not empirical: with the same numerator and denominator, $\text{spike} = (L_7 - L_c)/\max(L_c,\varepsilon)$ and $\text{ACWR} - 1 = L_7/\max(L_c,\varepsilon) - 1 = (L_7 - L_c)/\max(L_c,\varepsilon)$ are identically equal whenever $L_c > \varepsilon$. They are presented as two features in a single numbered equation on [PAGE 3], but they carry exactly one degree of information. So the engineered design matrix has a rank deficiency of one. For the tree ensembles that is redundancy; for Logistic Regression, which the paper also fits and reports, it is exact collinearity, and the coefficient vector is not identifiable — only the span of the two columns is. The paper reports a Logistic Regression row in Table 2 [PAGE 5] without noting that its coefficient signs are arbitrary under this collinearity. That is a concrete, checkable defect that the paper's own honesty about the rule-based runtime does not cover.

Step two — the referent problem. This is the paper's central confessional claim, stated twice, on [PAGE 1] ("the live backend score is rule-based rather than produced by the serialized XGBoost estimator") and [PAGE 3] ("the online score and offline model metrics describe different implementations"). Formally: let $s_{\mathrm{ml}}(x) = \hat f_{\mathrm{XGB}}(\phi(x))$ and let $s_{\mathrm{live}}(x) = \mathrm{clip}\left(\sum_k w_k \mathbb{1}[g_k(x) > \tau_k], 0, 1\right)$. The reported ROC-AUC 0.9597 is an estimate of a functional of the distribution of $s_{\mathrm{ml}}$, not of $s_{\mathrm{live}}$. Any sentence of the form "the system achieves 0.9597 ROC-AUC" is therefore a substitution failure, not an exaggeration. The correct verification is a parity probe: $\lVert s_{\mathrm{live}} - s_{\mathrm{ml}}\rVert_\infty$ over a synthetic grid, plus the cardinality of the image of $s_{\mathrm{live}}$ (a threshold-sum can only take finitely many values, so its histogram must be multimodal, whereas a boosted-tree score is near-continuous). I predict a large divergence and a small image set.

**1. Acute:chronic ratio and spike rate [PAGE 3].**

$$\mathrm{ACWR}(t) \;=\; \frac{L_7(t)}{\max\!\left(L_c(t),\,\varepsilon\right)}, \qquad S(t) \;=\; \frac{L_7(t) - L_c(t)}{\max\!\left(L_c(t),\,\varepsilon\right)} \tag{1}$$

Units: $L_7, L_c$ in arbitrary units (AU) of session load, typically $\text{RPE}\times\text{duration[min]}$; $\mathrm{ACWR}$ and $S$ dimensionless. Assumption for use: $L_c$ is a strictly positive, fixed-window aggregate. **Algebraic identity:** whenever $L_c > \varepsilon$, $S(t) \equiv \mathrm{ACWR}(t) - 1$ exactly. The two "features" are affinely dependent, so the engineered matrix has rank deficiency one; for Logistic Regression the coefficient vector is non-identifiable (only the column span is). **Breakdown regimes:** (a) $L_c \le \varepsilon$: the ratio is clamped at $L_7/\varepsilon$ and grows without bound as $\varepsilon\to 0$, so a deloaded athlete can be assigned an arbitrarily large "risk" from a small absolute numerator; (b) the paper never specifies the chronic window, so the operator $L_c \mapsto \frac{1}{W}\sum_{j=0}^{W-1}L(t-j)$ is underdetermined in $W$; (c) if $W = 28$ d, the 7-day numerator is nested inside the denominator and, for a stationary load series, $\mathrm{corr}(L_7, L_{28}) = \sqrt{7/28} = 0.5$, making the ratio mechanically bounded and autocorrelated. The paper's own guard is on [PAGE 3]: "ACWR has known conceptual limitations and should not be treated as a causal intervention target."

**2. Fatigue index [PAGE 3].**

$$\mathrm{FI} \;=\; 1.5\left(10 - h_{\text{sleep}}\right) + 2.0\,\mathrm{RPE} - 2.5\,d_{\text{rest}} \tag{2}$$

Units: the three terms are $[1.5\ \text{per hour}]\cdot[\text{h}]$, $[2.0]\cdot[\text{CR10}]$, $[2.5\ \text{per day}]\cdot[\text{d}]$. The sum has **no coherent physical unit** — it is a pseudo-index, and its weights $1.5, 2.0, 2.5$ are stated on [PAGE 3] without an estimation procedure, prior, or sensitivity analysis. **Derived exchange rate:** $\partial \mathrm{FI}/\partial d_{\text{rest}} = -2.5$, $\partial \mathrm{FI}/\partial \mathrm{RPE} = 2.0$, so one rest day is worth $1.25$ RPE units — a hidden trade between a behaviour and a perceptual report that no one calibrated. **Range and breakdown:** over plausible inputs ($h_{\text{sleep}}\in[4,10]$, $\mathrm{RPE}\in[0,10]$, $d_{\text{rest}}\in[0,7]$), $\mathrm{FI}\in[-17.5,\ 29]$. Negative "fatigue" is physiologically vacuous; a well-rested, well-slept, low-RPE athlete returns a strongly negative index, which the downstream threshold logic will read as an extreme rather than as "no signal." The paper's disclaimer on [PAGE 3] is exact: "These are code-defined transformations, not validated physiological thresholds."

**3. Secondary indices [PAGE 3].**

$$R \;=\; \frac{30\,n_{\text{inj}}}{\max(d_{\text{rec}},\,1)}, \qquad D \;=\; \frac{n_{\text{decel}}\cdot s_{\text{sprint}}}{100}$$

$R$ has units $[\text{d}^{-1}]\times 30$; $D$ has units $[\text{count}\cdot\text{m}]/100$. **$R$ breakdown:** $\max(d_{\text{rec}},1)$ clamps day-0 to day-1, so a fresh re-injury and a one-day-old injury are indistinguishable; and the decay is hyperbolic, so $R$ halves every time $d_{\text{rec}}$ doubles — a scale-free memory with no half-life, contradicting the characteristic multi-week tissue-healing timescales the cited literature assumes. A player with five injuries three years ago scores $30\cdot 5/1095 = 0.137$, numerically near a never-injured player. **$D$ breakdown:** it is a *product* of two session-volume aggregates, so it scales roughly as the square of session size and is collinear with total volume; and because it omits body mass, momentum $p = m v$ is unrepresented — a 70 kg and a 100 kg athlete with identical deceleration counts and sprint distances receive identical strain. A long, slow, high-deceleration, low-sprint-distance session yields $D \approx 0$ despite high mechanical stress: a structural false-negative regime.

**4. Evaluation operators [PAGE 3, PAGE 5].**

$$P = \frac{TP}{TP+FP},\quad r = \frac{TP}{TP+FN},\quad F_1 = \frac{2Pr}{P+r} = \frac{2TP}{2TP+FP+FN} \tag{3}$$

Dimensionless. $F_1$ encodes an equal-cost assumption between false positives and false negatives; in injury screening those costs are asymmetric and no cost model is stated. $F_1 \to 0$ as $TP \to 0$ and is undefined when $P + r = 0$. $\mathrm{AUC} = \Pr\!\left(s(X^{+}) > s(X^{-})\right)$ is threshold-free and invariant to any strictly monotone transform of $s$, so ROC-AUC 0.9597 carries **no calibration content** — the paper states this on [PAGE 4]: "The reported ROC-AUC is a ranking statistic for this test split, not a probability calibration result."

**5. Inversion of the reported contingency table.** With $N=500$ holdout rows, prevalence $\pi$, precision $P$, recall $r$, accuracy $A$:

$$\pi = \frac{1 - A}{\,1 - r + r\,(1-P)/P\,},\qquad TP = r\pi N,\quad FP = TP\frac{1-P}{P}$$

Substituting $A=0.9280$, $P=0.8475$, $r=0.6494$ gives $\pi \approx 0.1540$, $TP\approx 50$, $FP\approx 9$, $FN\approx 27$, $TN\approx 414$. Check: $(50+414)/500 = 0.9280$ ✓ and $2(0.8475)(0.6494)/(0.8475+0.6494) = 0.73534$ ✓. The reported triple is therefore internally consistent; and the no-skill accuracy floor is $1-\pi = 84.6\%$, so the headline 92.8% is 8.2 points of lift, with balanced accuracy $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.6494 + 414/423) = 0.814$.

**6. Preprocessing-leakage operator.** Let $\hat\phi$ be the selector-plus-scaler fitted on $D_{\text{train}}\cup D_{\text{test}}$. For the scaler, the induced mean shift is bounded: $\left|\hat\mu_{\text{all}} - \hat\mu_{\text{train}}\right| \le \frac{n_{\text{test}}}{n}\left|\hat\mu_{\text{test}} - \hat\mu_{\text{train}}\right| = 0.2\,\Delta\mu$. For the **selector**, $\mathbb{1}[p_j < \alpha]$ is a step function and admits no Lipschitz bound: arbitrarily small rank swaps near the cutoff change the selected set discontinuously, so no magnitude guarantee exists. This is the exact singularity regime the paper flags but does not formalize on [PAGE 3]: "feature selection and scaling are fitted before the split, so information from the eventual holdout partition can influence preprocessing."

**7. Referent mismatch operator.** Reported metric $\hat M = M\!\left(\hat f_{\mathrm{XGB}}\circ\hat\phi,\ D_{\text{test}}\right)$; served score $s_{\mathrm{live}}(x) = \mathrm{clip}\left(\sum_k w_k\mathbb{1}[g_k(x)>\tau_k],\,0,\,1\right)$. Attribution of $\hat M$ to the deployed endpoint requires $s_{\mathrm{live}} \equiv \hat f_{\mathrm{XGB}}\circ\hat\phi$, which source inspection falsifies on [PAGE 1] and [PAGE 3]. $s_{\mathrm{live}}$ has finite image cardinality, so its score histogram is necessarily multimodal and its calibration error is not estimable from the reported AUC. The paper's prescription on [PAGE 5] is correct and unmet: "A feature attribution method should be computed for the exact deployed estimator and its transformed input."

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The operator set is not free-standing; it is the terminal state of a specific 2016 lineage. Soligard et al. [1] introduced the external/internal load distinction; Gabbett [2] popularized the training–injury paradox and, with it, the acute:chronic ratio as a monitoring idiom; Impellizzeri et al. [4] then demonstrated that the ratio is a conceptually unstable construct. PitchPulse inherits the idiom and imports the critique as a disclaimer rather than as a design constraint — [PAGE 2] cites the "methodological critique" while [PAGE 3] still implements the ratio as a live feature. Bahr & Holme [3] supply the study-design warning that the paper's own Section 4.5 then violates: a single fixed-seed holdout is exactly the design Bahr and Holme argue cannot support multivariable risk claims.

The estimator lineage is equally traceable and equally constraining: Breiman [5], Cortes–Vapnik [6], Chen–Guestrin [7], Pedregosa et al. [8], Fawcett [9], Lundberg–Lee [11]. Retrocausally, the 2016 ACWR decision pre-commits what the 2026 validation can ever discover: because $\varepsilon$, $W$, and the weights $1.5/2.0/2.5$ are fixed constants rather than estimated parameters, no future real cohort can recover an unbiased effect — the parameters are structural, not learned. The Lundberg–Lee citation is the sharpest instance: SHAP is cited as if implemented, yet [PAGE 1] states the factor descriptions "are not computed with SHAP." A citation is doing the work of an implementation.

Multi-week traps to flag: (i) the 21-day/28-day chronic-window folklore that propagates without a specified $W$; (ii) the "10% weekly progression" heuristic that survives in rule thresholds; (iii) the synthetic-data deferral trap — "we will validate on real players later" — which becomes permanent when the runtime path is a separate codebase from the training path, so effort spent validating the model never reaches the endpoint; (iv) the attribution-as-causation trap, where a future SHAP plot is read as a mechanistic claim; (v) the prototype-permanence trap, where a rule-based score deployed once acquires institutional authority that no later correction removes.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three stances collide here, and each has a characteristic failure mode.

**Crisis habit regression (first-order).** The authors' habit is the "we built an ML system" narrative: architecture diagram, four-classifier comparison table, a selected winner, a serialized artifact. Every element of that genre is present on [PAGE 2] and [PAGE 5]. The regression under crisis — the crisis being that the runtime path is not the model — is to preserve the genre while appending a disclaimer. The paper does this almost perfectly: it discloses the mismatch on [PAGE 1], [PAGE 3], and [PAGE 5], yet still leads the abstract with "XGBoost is selected by F1-score (0.7353; ROC-AUC 0.9597)." A reader who stops at the abstract absorbs the genre and not the correction.

**Counter-trap (second-order).** The reviewer who reads the disclosure and concludes "therefore the project is worthless" has simply inverted the habit and kept its structure. That is the trap: the disclosure is unusually honest — the authors inspected their own code and reported that the served function is not the trained function, which most papers in this genre do not do. The correct second-order move is to price the honesty positively and then ask the narrow question: what is the residual claim? The residual claim is exactly the software-architecture claim, and it survives. On [PAGE 5] the authors write: "Until that integration is implemented and verified, the API should identify its result as a rule-based prototype score." That is a correct specification of the deployed object, and it is verifiable.

**The clock (third-order).** The third stance is temporal: what does the system believe about its own output next season? A model trained on synthetic labels generated from constructed relationships has a clock that never advances — the generator is stationary by construction, so the trained function's error is constant in time. A real deployment's error is non-stationary: squad turnover, rule changes, surface changes, and coaching-periodization drift all move $P_{\text{test}}$ away from $P_{\text{train}}$. The paper has no temporal split, so the transport operator $T:\mathcal{D}_{\text{season}} \to \mathcal{D}_{\text{season}+1}$ is entirely unestimated, and [PAGE 6] concedes as much: "External validity is unknown because no real cohort, club, season, or injury surveillance dataset is supplied." The recursive point: the authors know the reader will discount the metric; the reader knows the authors know; the deployed system, however, does not know anything, because it is a threshold statement. That asymmetry — a self-aware paper producing an unaware artifact — is the actual failure to adjudicate.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A physical-layer veto is a condition under which a modelled quantity ceases to be a measurement of the thing it names. Four such vetoes apply here and each is provable from the operator definitions rather than from data.

**Thermal veto (weather).** RPE is an endogenous report that conflates internal load with ambient burden. If $\mathrm{RPE} = g(L_{\text{int}}) + b(\mathrm{WBGT}) + \eta$, then $\mathrm{FI}$ inherits the additive bias $2.0\,b(\mathrm{WBGT})$ — worth, at a modest heat-strain offset of $b = 2$ CR10 units, 4 index points, i.e. 16% of the entire plausible FI range $[-17.5, 29]$. The synthetic generator never sampled a WBGT axis, so no coefficient in the pipeline can absorb it. **Veto:** any FI-based recommendation issued above the heat-strain threshold is a measurement of weather, not of the athlete.

**Biomechanical veto (mass and momentum).** $D = n_{\text{decel}}\cdot s_{\text{sprint}}/100$ omits body mass entirely. The tissue-relevant quantity for deceleration stress scales with momentum and with the rate of momentum change, $F \approx m\,\Delta v/\Delta t$. Two athletes, 70 kg and 100 kg, with byte-identical session traces produce identical $D$: a 43% mass difference is invisible. **Veto:** $D$ cannot rank athletes across a squad, so any squad-level ordering derived from it is a category error, not a noisy estimate.

**Surface/congestion veto.** "Rest days" is an integer counter. A rest day containing long-haul travel, a double session split across a day, or a match-day-minus-one walkthrough all register as $+1$ in eq. (2) and subtract 2.5 index points. **Veto:** the recovery term is a calendar variable masquerading as a physiological one.

**Overlapping-window veto.** As derived above, nesting $L_7$ inside a 28-day $L_c$ forces $\mathrm{corr} = 0.5$ for a stationary series, bounding the ratio's dynamic range independently of athlete state. **Veto:** observed ACWR excursions are partly arithmetic, so attributing them to training behaviour is unlicensed.

None of these layers is modelled; the paper's [PAGE 1] sentence, "Training load is not a direct cause or stand-alone predictor of injury," is the correct general guard, but it does not discharge the specific dimensional defects above.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
