Scenario 2 (non-fundamental sequence vetoes uniform boundedness). Counterfactual: On $E = [0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure (finite), define $f_n(x) = n \cdot \chi_{(0,1/n)}(x)$. Each $f_n$ is a.e. finite, but the sequence is not fundamental a.e. at $x = 0$? Actually $f_n(x) \to 0$ for $x > 0$ and $f_n(0) = 0$, so it is a.e. convergent; to violate fundamentality, use $f_n = n\,\chi_{(0,1)}$ alternating? Better: let $f_n(x) = n$ if $x \in (0,1/n)$ and $0$ otherwise. This converges a.e. to 0. To violate "fundamental a.e." while remaining a.e. finite, take $g_{2k} = 0$, $g_{2k+1} = k\,\chi_{(0,1)}$. At each $x \in (0,1)$, the sequence alternates $0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 3,\dots$ which is not Cauchy, hence not fundamental a.e. on $(0,1)$ (positive measure). RLVR veto condition: sequence not fundamental a.e. on finite-measure $E$. Verifiable prediction: No constants $c < \infty$ and $F \subset (0,1)$ with $\mu(F) > 0$ satisfy $|g_n(x)| \le c$ for all $n, x \in F$ ([PAGE 90], exercise 2 contrapositive), because on any $F$ of positive measure, the odd terms are unbounded. Reward is 0 for any claim of uniform boundedness on positive measure.

Scenario 3 (a.e. convergence without norm convergence vetoes uniform a.e.). Counterfactual: On $([0,1], \mathcal{B}, m)$, $f_n = n\,\chi_{(0,1/n)}$, $f = 0$. Then $f_n(x) \to 0$ for every $x \in (0,1]$ and $f_n(0) = 0$, so $f_n \to f$ everywhere (hence a.e.). RLVR veto condition: $\|f_n - f\|_\infty = n \not\to 0$ (in fact ess sup is $n$). Verifiable prediction: $f_n$ does not converge to $f$ uniformly a.e., and $\{f_n\}$ is not essentially bounded uniformly; the equivalence in exercise (5) fails because the hypothesis of essential boundedness of the limit in the norm sense is violated ([PAGE 90]). The sequence is a.e. finite but the norms diverge. Reward is 0 for any agent asserting $\|f_n - f\|_\infty \to 0$.
