<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive problem is that front offices, agents, and analysts observe one another’s valuation models. If Team A reads the source as “choose the top-tier starting pitcher option” [PAGE 21], Team B can anticipate that demand and price starters accordingly. The counter-trap is to avoid overpaying for the consensus asset and instead exploit the undervalued bullpen piece, which is exactly the source’s [PAGE 9] “cheap option” and [PAGE 19] “easier and cheaper to find top-tier relief talent” observation. But that counter-trap is self-limiting: if every team follows it, relief prices rise and the inefficiency closes. The crisis-habit regression is the deadline or offseason panic pattern in which a club abandons its valuation process and pays for a “proven” ace or closer because it fears public criticism and immediate contention pressure. The clock is the contention window: starters require longer contracts and development time, while relievers can be acquired or converted faster but with higher year-to-year variance. The source’s recommendation is conditional on “payroll space and options” [PAGE 21], so the correct recursive reading is not “always starters” or “always relievers,” but “price the marginal inning and the marginal win under the current market’s mispricing.”

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical/biomechanical/weather veto is straightforward: the source’s conclusion on [PAGE 21] is said to stem from “Percent of Payroll, WAR, E.R.A, and AAV/WAR stats.” Those are financial and performance aggregates. A formal omitted-variable model is
\[
ERA_i=\alpha+\beta P_i+\gamma B_i+\delta W_i+u_i,
\]
where \(B_i\) is a biomechanical/workload vector and \(W_i\) is a weather/park vector. If \(Cov(P_i,B_i)\neq0\) or \(Cov(P_i,W_i)\neq0\), the simple slope \(\hat\beta\) from [PAGE 15] is biased. The breakdown regime is exact: if \(B_i\) and \(W_i\) are orthogonal to \(P_i\), omitting them affects precision but not the probability limit of \(\hat\beta\); if they are correlated with payroll share, the source’s ERA-payroll slope cannot be read as the causal effect of investment. Physical veto example: a starter’s marginal innings may be purchased at rising injury hazard and declining postseason availability; a reliever’s marginal innings may be purchased at high back-to-back fatigue risk. Weather veto example: a high-run park or extreme heat game changes ERA and bullpen usage without changing payroll share. Therefore the source can describe 2019 associations, but it cannot adjudicate the physical layer without additional data.
