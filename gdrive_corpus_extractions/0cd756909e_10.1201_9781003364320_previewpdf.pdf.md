The metric-design line runs Aaron Schatz's DVOA and Adjusted Sack Rate (Football Outsiders, Dec. 2003) [PAGE 15, 44], Brian Burke's expected-points/win-probability work (now ESPN) [PAGE 17], and the cross-sport analogy to Bill-James sabermetrics and the NBA "death of the midrange" via Seth Partnow [PAGE 13].

Multi-week / multi-season traps this genealogy exposes. First, the "Super Bowl hangover" survivorship frame [PAGE 31]: 15 of 51 Super Bowl losers missed the playoffs the next year, but conditioning on a franchise having *reached* a Super Bowl is itself a selection filter, so downstream "curse" statistics are not transferable to arbitrary teams. Second, the 2017 Eagles → league-wide 4th-down copycat effect [PAGE 16–17] is a textbook adoption shock: a discontinuity that contaminates any pre/post-2017 comparison of "aggressiveness," because the *population* of decision-makers changed, not just individual behavior. Third, and most methodologically important, the author's own panel spans 2020–2022 and the OL-combos↔sack-rate fit is consistent in 2021–2022 but breaks in 2020 [PAGE 20–21]. That is a multi-season regime trap: pooling across regimes where a hidden variable (release time distribution) shifted produces a Simpson's-paradox-shaped reversal. The lesson the source half-teaches is that a relationship validated in two of three seasons is not a relationship; it is two data points that happen to agree.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Two agents' models of the same game are in direct conflict, and the source stages it explicitly. The conventional-wisdom agent is Mike Tomlin: "I'm not going to make judgements based on those numbers. The game is the game. It's an emotional one played by emotional and driven men" [PAGE 14]. The analytics agent is Joe Banner/Brian Burke/Schatz: decisions should track quantified win probability, and the failure to "go for it" in +WP situations "cost teams a cumulative 170 percentage points of win probability" in week 1 of 2020–2021 [PAGE 15]. The ESPN staffer poll ranking Pittsburgh low and Baltimore/Cleveland/Philadelphia high [PAGE 14] is a second-order belief (analysts' beliefs about teams' beliefs), not a measurement of the teams.

The recursion deepens one level in the author himself, and honesty requires flagging it: the Adjusted Defensive Impact model was built by a self-described lifelong Steelers fan to prove TJ Watt deserved DPOY over Aaron Donald [PAGE 16–18]. He tells us the weights (0.9 solo, −0.14 unblocked, the games term) were chosen by him. Those are motivated-reasoning parameters — chosen by an agent with a known prior toward a known conclusion. This does not make the metric wrong, but a rigorous reader must treat the constants as analyst-selected, not estimated, and note the metric was constructed *after* the desired answer existed.

"Crisis-habit regression" reads cleanly here as the tendency of injured, losing teams to *revert* to conventional behavior — the 2022 Rams kept searching for a "starting five" narrative even as the data showed no metric punished OL turnover except adjusted sack rate [PAGE 20]. The "counter-trap" is the analytics department's job: to resist that reversion. The "clock," in the source's own physics, is literally the snap-to-throw timer — 2.19s vs. 3.04s is the variable that decides whether the emotional/conventional read ("terrible line, terrible sacks") is even measurable [PAGE 48].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source is candid that its metrics are context-blind, so the veto cases below are gaps the book itself acknowledges or leaves implicit. I state the physical/biomechanical reasoning correctly and mark where the excerpt is silent.

Physical veto 1 — release-time convolution. Already formalized in operator (5). Verdict: any claim of the form "many OL combos → more sacks" is vetoed whenever $f_\tau$ shifts left (quick QB). The source confirms the empirical instance (Roethlisberger/Rivers) [PAGE 48]. This is the strongest, source-endorsed veto.

Physical veto 2 — weather and the explosive-play threshold. The book defines $E_d=\mathbb{1}[\max_j y\ge 20]$ [PAGE 55] with no environmental covariate. Aerodynamic drag on a football scales roughly with the square of relative airspeed, so a stiff wind raises the terminal error and reduces achievable air distance on deep attempts, biasing $E_d$ toward 0 in wind/cold independently of talent. The source does not control for this; therefore cross-team explosive-play rankings (Panthers 82%, Colts 52% [PAGE 57]) are partially confounded by schedule weather. The excerpt gives no weather variable — flagged, not fabricated.

Biomechanical veto 3 — snap-count combinatorics vs. actual protection. The book's `starting_line` is "the five offensive linemen with the highest percentage of snap counts" [PAGE 36], explicitly conceding they "are [not] necessarily the same five that started the game." Protection quality is a function of block-shedding, leverage, and hand-fighting — quantities present only on film, which the author himself consults for the six-man Cleveland snap (Fig. 1.1, All-22) [PAGE 34]. So the primary independent variable (combinations) is a roster-churn proxy, not a biomechanical measure; it can be zero-information about individual blocking failure.

Adjudication: the only relationship that survives cross-layer scrutiny in the source is combos↔adjusted sack rate, and even it is admitted to be "not strong enough to begin using the term 'correlation'" [PAGE 20]. All four pass-protection relationships (win pct, pressure, QB hits, adj sack rate) [PAGE 38–46] and all three run-blocking relationships (power, stuffed, ALY) [PAGE 50–52] are vetoed as causal claims by either release-time confounding, roster-proxy measurement error, or $n=32$ sampling noise.

Three verifiable, source-grounded counterfactuals with falsifiable predictions:

Veto scenario 1 (release-time truncation). Hold OL combos high; set QB release fast ($\tau\approx2.2$s). Prediction: adjusted sack rate stays near league-best despite many combos, exactly as 2020 PIT/IND behaved [PAGE 20–21, 48]. Verifiable by regressing sack rate on combos with mean time-to-throw as a covariate; prediction is that the combos coefficient becomes more negative and statistically distinguishable once $\tau$ is held fixed.

Veto scenario 2 (elusiveness absorption). Hold line quality constant; vary RB elusiveness. Prediction: wALY moves opposite to raw ALY — WAS drops from 4.29 raw to a league-worst 2.14 weighted, while LAR/ARI rise into the top five despite bottom-four elusive scores [PAGE 54]. Verifiable directly from the book's own two figures; the prediction is that ranking(raw ALY) and ranking(wALY) are strongly negatively correlated for high-elusiveness backfields.

Veto scenario 3 (games-adjustment inertness). Take any pass-rusher; vary games played from 17 to 15. Prediction: Adjusted Defensive Impact changes by only $2\times0.0058=0.0116$, i.e. ~1.3% of a single 0.9-weighted solo sack — statistically indistinguishable from no durability adjustment [PAGE 18–19]. Verifiable by recomputing $I$ with the games term deleted; prediction is negligible rank change, exposing the adjustment as decorative rather than material.

Net: the source supports a weak, well-hedged claim (a *general* — not correlational — link between OL churn and adjusted sack rate) and vetoes every stronger causal reading via release-time confounding, roster-proxy measurement error, weather-blindness, and $n=32$ sampling noise.
