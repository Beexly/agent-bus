<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under competition pressure, human judges regress to fast visual heuristics, which fail because “the problematic movements of groups A, C, and D are instantaneous, it is easy to make visual errors” [PAGE 1]; the proposed system was explicitly designed to replace this judge-level failure mode [PAGE 1]. Counter-trap: the automated system regresses in the opposite direction—toward overconfident memorization of pyramid layers. Reporting four-layer recognition rates of 101–102 % [PAGE 6] is a meta-cognitive failure: the model asserts certainty beyond the probability bound, so it cannot express “I do not know” for ambiguous instantaneous elements. Clock: the sensor imposes a hard 30 Hz update (33.3 ms period) [PAGE 2]. If an aerobics difficulty element occupies less than one frame, the judge’s mental model, the athlete’s feint, and the system’s particle filter all operate on stale or missing observations; the recursion “judge models athlete, athlete models judge, system models both” breaks at the sensor clock. Recursive depth therefore has a maximum useful layer count set by Nyquist at 15 Hz, not by adding four-layer spatial pyramids [PAGE 5]. Any claim that deeper layers improve recognition of sub-frame events is a category error: temporal aliasing cannot be cured by spatial fusion.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: depth resolution is “only a few centimeters” [PAGE 2]; any aerobics scoring element requiring sub-centimeter joint angular resolution (finger flexion, toe point, inter-vertebral alignment) is physically unresolvable and must be vetoed before Fourier feature extraction. Biomechanical veto: the paper acknowledges “in the process of moving, the arms and legs will shift and rotate, so it is necessary to reconstruct the characteristics of the Fourier descriptors between each frame” [PAGE 4]; under self-occlusion or limb crossing, the silhouette ceases to be a simple closed curve c_i = u_i + j v_i [PAGE 4], violating the DFT manifold assumption and invalidating φ(n). Weather/environment veto: background subtraction claims “strong robustness to complex and changeable environmental factors” [PAGE 5], yet the Kinect structured-light advantage is specifically “under low light conditions” [PAGE 2]; infrared saturation from gym lighting, sunlight, or multi-camera interference breaks the depth map, and background subtraction fails when the moving target’s color matches the modeled background after thresholding [PAGE 5]. Adjudication rule: an output is admissible only if (a) reported rate Π ∈ [0,100] %, (b) φ(1) ≠ 0, (c) particle N_eff above degeneracy threshold, and (d) depth quantization ε_z below the biomechanical tolerance of the scored element. The four-layer column of Table 5.1 [PAGE 6] fails condition (a) and is therefore vetoed wholesale.
