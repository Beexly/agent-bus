<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the pipeline as an agent with a prior over what a jersey looks like and a habit it reverts to under stress. The habit is the training marginal: digit 2 is the modal class at roughly 2× the frequency of digit 6 [PAGE 7], and Figure 8b is normalized to that modal class [PAGE 7]. Under a *crisis* — a partially occluded, oblique, or motion-blurred crop — the evidence term collapses and the posterior regresses to the prior, so the predicted digit drifts toward 2, 4, 7, 9 (the classes the paper identifies as higher-accuracy) and away from 6 and 8. This is habit regression in the precise sense: it is not random error, it is *prior-dominated* error, and it is biased in a predictable direction. The correct counter-trap is not to lower the confidence threshold. The paper's own settings — "the optimal confidence threshold is 0.97" [PAGE 7] — show why: at 0.97 the surviving detections are already the easy tail, and lowering it admits exactly the crisis-regime crops where the prior dominates, converting abstention into confident prior emission. The counter-trap is therefore *threshold dilution*: a monotone trade of coverage for accuracy that looks like tuning and is actually an injection of prior into the output.

The second-order recursion is the analyst's theory of the system's theory. An analyst who sees a plausible game log with plausible jersey numbers has no signal that the log was produced by prior-completion rather than observation; the database format (play, quarter, start/end time, team, participating players [PAGE 8]) is uniform regardless of provenance. The system's output is therefore *epistemically opaque by construction* — there is no per-field confidence, no abstention token, no flag for "this number was assembled from a 0.55-IoU crop of an oblique player." An analyst modeling the system correctly should hold a belief that some fraction of rows are priors, and the paper supplies no way to estimate that fraction.

The clock is the counterweight, and it is the one place where the system reasons about an external rule-bound agent — the officiating and clock-operator process. "The plays of an American football game can be represented as a sequence of non-overlapping time segments of a running game clock" [PAGE 1] is a *grammar* supplied by the NFL and NCAA statisticians' manuals [refs. 2–3, PAGE 1], and the switching rule "40 s of the game clock or the period of 5 s of the snap clock" [PAGE 7] is that grammar reduced to a threshold. **Veto 1 — Decimation counterfactual (tests Operator 3).** Re-score the held-out 668-frame test set [PAGE 7] twice: once through the two-stage crop path as described, and once after globally resizing every frame to 416 × 416 before the identical two-stage pipeline. *Prediction:* digit mAP falls from 91.7% to below 40% (absolute), and the drop concentrates on players whose original bounding-box width is under 60 px, because $\alpha s_0<1$ px puts their stroke width below the Nyquist floor. *Falsified if* the resized-path mAP remains above 80% or if the error increase is uniform across box-size strata. This is a single-command experiment on data the authors already hold; it converts the paper's qualitative claim on [PAGE 4] into a measured constant.

**Veto 2 — Whole-number assembly counterfactual (tests Operator 6 and the left-to-right decoder).** Take the same 4,865 labeled proposals and re-score them at the *number* level rather than the digit level, using the paper's stated left-to-right assembly [PAGE 7]. *Prediction:* exact-match number accuracy is $0.917^{2}\approx0.841$ under independence and measurably *below* 0.841 if occlusion and obliquity correlate across the two digits as the paper's error analysis [PAGE 7] implies; additionally, errors over-index on numbers containing 6 or 8. *Falsified if* number-level accuracy is ≥ 91.7% or if the 6/8 over-index is absent. This directly quantifies the 7.6-point flattery built into reporting per-digit mAP.

**Veto 3 — Clock-rule ablation counterfactual (tests Operator 4 and the 62% figure).** Re-run play-window detection with the snap-clock branch disabled (game clock 40 s rule only) and, separately, with the snap-clock threshold swept over {4, 5, 6} s. *Prediction:* disabling the 5 s branch raises start/end accuracy above 62% at unchanged OCR accuracy, and the sweep produces monotone degradation away from 5 s at a rate eight times steeper than an equivalent game-clock sweep, because the snap-clock branch has only $5/0.5=10$ display LSB of margin. Quarter-transition accuracy should remain pinned at 100% under all three conditions, since quarter identity is a discrete state not carried by the threshold rule. *Falsified if* start/end accuracy is invariant to the branch ablation, or if quarter accuracy moves at all — the latter would indicate that the reported 100% [PAGE 9] is contaminated by the zero-aliasing defect where "Zero is designated as output for scenes in which clock information is absent" [PAGE 6] makes a genuine 0:00 indistinguishable from absence.

**Veto 4 (auxiliary) — Throughput counterfactual.** The paper reports four Quadro RTX 6000 GPUs for training [PAGE 7] but no inference rate. *Prediction:* end-to-end single-GPU throughput is bounded by the Tesseract per-frame OCR call, not by DETR or RetinaNet, and scales linearly with frame count; *falsified if* measured throughput is invariant to whether the clock subsystem is enabled. This is the missing operator that decides whether the system is a broadcast-indexing product or a proof of concept, and the source is silent on it.
