**1. Molecular Stimulus-Response Mapping:**
Let $\mathbf{S}_t = (s_{t,1}, ..., s_{t,n})$ represent the n-dimensional vector of receptor impulses at time $t$, and $\mathbf{R}_t = (r_{t,1}, ..., r_{t,m})$ the concurrent vector of effector movements. The fundamental postulate is that for any infinitesimal interval $\Delta t$, there exists a deterministic function $F$ such that:
$$\mathbf{R}_{t+\Delta t} = F(\mathbf{S}_t, \mathbf{P}_{t})$$
where $\mathbf{P}_t$ is the set of physiological parameters (e.g., neural conductivity, muscular tension). Units: Components of $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{R}$ are in SI units of electrical current (Amperes) and force (Newtons), respectively. $F$ is initially an unknown black-box operator.

**2. Molar Goal State Representation:**
A "goal" $G$ is defined as a stable attractor state within the joint phase space $(\mathbf{S}, \mathbf{R})$. It corresponds to a subspace where receptor inputs signal the satisfaction of a need (e.g., specific chemical gradients indicating satiation). Formally, $G = \{ (\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{r}) \mid \nabla_{\mathbf{s}} \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{s}) \approx 0 \}$, where $\mathcal{H}$ is an entropy-like functional representing homeostatic deviation [PAGE 25].

**3. Intuition as a Prior Distribution:**
The "intuition born of intimate knowledge" described by Hull can be modeled as a Bayesian prior $Q(\mathbf{R}|G)$ over response sequences given a goal. This prior is learned through the organism's own evolutionary and developmental history. The danger is mistaking samples from $Q$ for deductions from the objective dynamics $F$. The theorem to be proven is that $Q(\mathbf{R}|G)$ should be *derived* as the maximum entropy distribution consistent with constraints imposed by $F$ and reinforcement history, not assumed.

**Breakdown Regime:** The formalism breaks down if $F$ is non-stationary (changing learning rates) or if the mapping from $\mathbf{S}$ to needs is many-to-many, creating ambiguous drives. The singularity occurs when $\det(\frac{\partial \mathbf{R}}{\partial \mathbf{S}}) \rightarrow 0$, implying multiple molecular paths converge to identical macroscopic goals, making the reverse inference (from goal to path) ill-posed.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Hull’s rejection of goal-based explanation is a direct inheritance from Jody’s genetic logic and Pavlovian physiology. Jody’s *Principles of Science* argued that purposive explanations are merely shorthand for unobserved mechanical causes. Pavlov’s work on conditioned reflexes provided the empirical model: digestion (a "goal-oriented" biological process) could be fully explained by antecedent stimuli and acquired connections. Hull’s radical step was to extend this to all behavior, including "insight," by positing that even complex cognition arises from hierarchical S-R bonds formed via contiguous reinforcement.

The historical trap is the lingering vitalist intuition that organisms act *for* ends. This teleological view is reinforced by everyday language ("the heart pumps blood *in order to* circulate oxygen"). Hull’s genealogical correction is to reframe all purpose as a retrospective description of successful S-R chains. The lineage shows a shift from Aristotelian final causes to Humean constant conjunction, mediated by Watson’s environmental determinism. The risk is reducing agency to mechanism; Hull attempts to salvage agency by making it an emergent, statistically regular property of well-learned systems.

The multi-week trap involves the persistence of folk-psychological explanations. Even after accepting molecular primacy, researchers might unconsciously revert to goal-language when dealing with novel or complex behaviors, smuggling teleology back into the theory. This regression is detectable when hypotheses about *why* an organism does something precede hypotheses about *how* it does it.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical and biomechanical realities impose hard vetoes on purely psychological accounts. Consider the example of "winning a battle." At the molar level, this is a strategic goal. At the molecular level, it is a cascade of specific nerve impulses, muscle contractions, and weapon trajectories. A theory based solely on the general’s intent fails to predict whether a particular order will succeed if the soldiers’ muscles are fatigued (biomechanical veto) or if the terrain limits visibility (environmental veto). These lower-layer constraints are not optional additions; they are necessary conditions for the execution of any high-level plan.

Weather acts as a stochastic veto. A perfectly conceived fishing strategy fails if a storm alters water clarity and fish behavior. The Hullian system must therefore incorporate environmental variables as part of the $\mathbf{S}$ vector. The adjudication rule is: **Any proposed behavioral law that cannot, in principle, be falsified by a perturbation in biomechanics, physiology, or immediate environment is insufficiently molecular.** For instance, a law stating "organisms act to achieve G" is invulnerable to weather, thus failing the test. In contrast, a law stating "given stimulus configuration S_t and neuromuscular state P_t, response R_{t+1} occurs with probability p" is directly testable and can be falsified by showing that $R$ fails despite $S$ and $P$ being present.

**Scenario 1: The Phantom Goal.**
*   **Intervention:** An organism is presented with a situation identical to one previously leading to goal attainment, but a critical muscle group is temporarily paralyzed.

If the observed response matches the molecular prediction but not the intuitive "frustrated goal-seeker" stereotype, the goal-based explanation is vetoed.

**Scenario 2: The Decoy Reward.**
*   **Intervention:** After training an animal to press a lever for food (Goal: Food), the reward is replaced with a visually identical but nutritionally inert substitute (e.g., sucrose-free sweetener).
*   **Standard Prediction:** The animal will show a transient decrease in responding (expectancy violation) but may continue pressing if it perceives the sweet taste as satisfying.
*   **RLVR Veto & Formal Prediction:** The molecular theory specifies that reinforcement depends on the *actual* reduction of a specific drive state $D$ (e.g., caloric deficit). The decoy does not reduce $D$. Therefore, the value potential $V$ associated with the lever-press chain should decay according to the extinction law, irrespective of the sensory similarity. The formal prediction is an exponential decline in response rate governed by the derivative of $D$, not by a subjective sense of "cheating." Observation of continued high-rate responding despite zero $D$ reduction would falsify the pure drive-reduction model and necessitate incorporating cognitive expectation factors.

**Scenario 3: The Simultaneous Path Dilemma.**
*   **Intervention:** An organism learns two distinct motor sequences, A→B→Goal and C→D→Goal, with equal reinforcement. Later, it is placed in a context that activates both A and C simultaneously (e.g., overlapping cues).
*   **Standard Prediction:** The organism will choose one path arbitrarily or based on subtle preference.

The prediction is not arbitrary choice but a specific interference pattern—perhaps oscillation between fragments of A and C, or a fused hybrid movement—determined by the relative strengths $V_A$ and $V_C$ and the inhibitory interactions between response systems. Observing clean, exclusive selection of one full sequence would suggest a higher-level "decision" process not captured by simple competitive S-R activation, thereby vetoing the sufficiency of the base model.
