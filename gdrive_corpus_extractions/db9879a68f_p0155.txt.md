<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, with multi-week experimental traps.

Tolman's 1932 Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men synthesizes three lineages. First, Gestalt psychology: the page's footnote explicitly cites Köhler, Gestalt Psychology (1929), Ch. IX [PAGE 137], importing the idea that perceptual wholes are given by the inter-arrangements of stimuli rather than assembled from elements. Second, semiotic sign theory: the triad sign–significate–signified means-end-relation adapts Saussurean/Peircean sign relations into a behaviorist vocabulary, where a stimulus stands for a goal-object relation. Third, purposive behaviorism as an alternative to Watsonian molecular S-R: Tolman argues behavior is molar, goal-directed, and expectant, not merely elicited.

The specific experimental context is the rat maze, especially vicarious trial and error (VTE) at the entrance—"debating the entering of specific alleys" [PAGE 137]. This anticipates latent learning (Tolman & Honzik, 1930) and cognitive-map theory: unrewarded rats build a maze representation over days or weeks that is revealed only when reward is introduced.

Multi-week traps. (a) The early-termination trap: if an experimenter stops after week 1 of non-rewarded exposure, performance differences between rewarded and non-rewarded groups may be absent, leading to the false conclusion that no learning occurred; the cognitive map is latent and behaviorally silent until incentive changes. (b) The over-training trap: extended rewarded training can rigidify the flexible gestalt expectancy into a rigid habit hierarchy, so that later probe trials (e.g., moved goal) show regression to habit rather than purposeful re-routing. (c) The retroactive-interference trap: introducing a second maze or altered alley configuration in week 3 can degrade the week-1 map, making it appear that the original "expectation" never existed when it was actually overwritten. (d) The spontaneous-recovery trap: after extinction, a delay of days or weeks can restore the maze-running pattern, showing that the expectancy trace survived non-reinforcement; testing only immediately after extinction misses this. Recursive structure. The animal "debating the entering of specific alleys" [PAGE 137] exhibits a primitive self-model: it represents itself as an agent at a choice point, simulates entering alley A versus alley B, and anticipates the means-end relation between that movement and the goal-object. This is recursive in the sense that the animal's own future behavior is an object of its current expectation. It is not a full human theory of mind, but it is a theory of self-as-operator within a means-end geometry.

Crisis habit regression. Under stress—high hunger drive, foot-shock threat, crowded testing chamber, or time pressure—the animal is predicted to regress from the flexible gestalt expectancy to a habit-family hierarchy. The sign-gestalt unit $G$ fragments; behavior becomes stereotyped alley selection based on the strongest previously reinforced S-R bond rather than on the current inter-arrangement of remote stimuli. Observable signature: reduced VTE duration at entrance, increased path stereotypy, and insensitivity to sudden goal relocation.

Counter-trap. The experimenter faces two opposite errors. (i) Perceptual over-attribution: assuming the animal sees the food directly through the wire maze when in fact it is using an olfactory trace or a previously learned cue; this falsely credits $\mathcal{P}$ where $\mathcal{M}$ operates. (ii) Habitual under-attribution: assuming the animal runs by habit when it is actually using a direct perceptual gestalt of the food's spatial location; this falsely credits $\mathcal{M}$ where $\mathcal{P}$ operates. The counter-trap is to manipulate the availability of remote stimuli independently of learning history—for example, hiding the food behind an opaque barrier after the map is formed—and observe whether behavior collapses (perceptual dependence) or persists (mnemonic dependence).

Each aspect of the complex must be commercially engaged in succession; if the dwell time per aspect $\Delta t_{\text{aspect}}$ exceeds the working-memory decay constant $\tau_{\text{wm}}$ (order seconds), the gestalt fragments before integration. The entrance debate thus has a clock: total VTE latency must be less than the retention window for the means-end relation to remain bound to the goal representation. Formal operators, units, page citations, and breakdown.

Let the stimulus array be $\Sigma = \{S_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, with each $S_i$ a physical signal pattern measured in SI units appropriate to modality (photons·s⁻¹·m⁻² for vision, odor molecules·s⁻¹ for olfaction, pascals for sound). Let the goal complex be $\mathcal{G} = \{O_g, ME, O_s\}$, where $O_g$ is the goal-object (food), $ME$ is the means-end relation, and $O_s$ is the subsidiary term. Tolman's sign relation on [PAGE 137] is written as a semiotic arrow
$$S_i \rightsquigarrow \mathcal{G},$$
read as "stimulus $S_i$ arouses expectationally the entire complex."

Mnemonization operator $\mathcal{M}$. After learning history $\mathcal{L}$ over trials $t \in \mathbb{N}$,
$$P(\mathcal{G}\mid S_j;\mathcal{L}) \xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{} 1,$$
dimensionless probability. Upon integration,
$$G = \operatorname{fuse}(\mathcal{G}), \qquad R(S_j) = G,$$
where the release map $R:\Sigma \to 2^{\mathcal{G}}$ maps a single trigger to the whole fused unit. Time unit: seconds (s) for the trial axis; learning is trial-count dimensionless.

Perception operator $\mathcal{P}$. The definitive feature quoted on [PAGE 137]—"stimuli corresponding to all the parts of the total complex (the remote parts as well as the more immediate) are directly present"—is formalized as
$$\forall x \in \operatorname{parts}(\mathcal{G}):\quad S_x \text{ is directly present at } t_0.$$
Fusion is defined as prior to specific experience:
$$\left.\frac{\partial G}{\partial \mathcal{L}}\right|_{\text{perception}} = 0,$$
and instead arises from stimulus inter-arrangements:
$$G = \Phi\!\big(I(\{S_i\})\big),$$
where $I$ is a spatiotemporal inter-arrangement functional and $\Phi$ is the gestalt integration map. Information units: Shannon entropy $H(G)$ in bits; mutual information $I(S;G)$ in bits.

Stimulus combination for the perception diagram (Figure 47, cited but not shown on [PAGE 137]):
$$\Sigma_{\text{percept}} = \{S_{\text{goal}},\; S_O,\; S_{ME}\},$$
with $S_O$ indicating $O$, $S_{ME}$ indicating the means-end relation $ME$ which $O_s$ bears to $O_g$, and an enclosing oval denoting the fused dynamic whole [PAGE 137].

Breakdown and singularity/regime boundaries:
1. Occlusion singularity: if wire mesh is replaced by opaque partition, direct photon flux from $O_g$ to the entrance is zero, so $I(S;O_g\mid \text{direct}) = 0$ bits and $\mathcal{P}$ is undefined; the system must fall back to $\mathcal{M}$, requiring $\mathcal{L} > 0$.
2. Temporal binding singularity: if the inter-stimulus interval between remote and immediate parts exceeds the binding window $\tau_{\text{bind}}$ (order 10⁻¹–10⁰ s for rodent multisensory integration), successive separation dominates and the oval fails to bind.
3. Sensory-noise singularity: as $\sigma_S^2 \to \infty$, $I(S;G) \to 0$ bits, collapsing the gestalt into unstructured commerce.
4. Source truncation singularity: Figure 47 and the terminal clause of [PAGE 137] are absent from this page, so the exact arrow topology in the diagram cannot be verified from the supplied text alone.
