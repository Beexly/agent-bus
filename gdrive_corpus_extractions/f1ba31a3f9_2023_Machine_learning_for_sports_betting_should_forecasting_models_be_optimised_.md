In binary NBA games, classwise-ECE is symmetric, so the positive class is sufficient. The pipeline uses correlation filtering, sequential forward selection, L1 embedding, BO-TPE hyperparameter search, and test-set model selection. The calibration branch selects SVM with classwise-ECE 3.49%, while the accuracy branch selects logistic regression with accuracy 69.23% [PAGE 15]. The betting simulation then uses 2018/2019 season predictions, three strategies, and four fractional-Kelly rules. The headline numbers are average ROI 110.42% versus 2.98% and best-case ROI 902.01% versus 222.84% [PAGE 1]. But the gray-zone audit matters: the overall paired t-test across all strategy-rule pairs gives p=0.153, so the mean advantage is not significant at 10%; only strategy 3 gives p=0.053 [PAGE 23]. Thus the paper’s strong claim is conditional on “bet only when the model is highly confident.” The best-case 902.01% comes from betting on only 2.16% of games with an 80% win rate [PAGE 21]; that is a small, highly path-dependent sample. The accuracy-optimized strategy 3 counterpart won 85.14% of its bets but still lost profitability because “one final, unsuccessful bet saw these systems lose their profitability” [PAGE 21]. This is a variance warning, not a calibration disproof. The classwise-ECE constraint also matters: “we require the distribution of weights over the bins to be platykurtic” [PAGE 5], an arbitrary but necessary anti-collapse rule. Without it, a model predicting the base rate can achieve low ECE while carrying no discriminative information. The data split is chronological and therefore stronger than shuffled cross-validation, but the betting test is a single season; no out-of-season replication is offered. Feature engineering is also coarse: two player-level statistics, BPM and defensive rebound percentage, are used to represent star players and rebounders, but injuries, minutes restrictions, travel, and late lineup changes are not fully conditioned. The source itself warns that “Events such as the early injury of a star player can have a significant influence on the outcome of a game” [PAGE 3]. A further multi-hop issue is bookmaker correlation: “a highly accurate predictive model is useless as long as it coincides with the bookmaker’s model” [PAGE 4]. Calibration alone does not guarantee independent edge; it only guarantees that predicted probabilities are marginally honest. The correct reading is therefore: calibration is a better selection objective than accuracy for probability-sensitive decisions, but the ROI evidence is conditional, underpowered, and physically under-specified.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators. For multiclass labels \(Y\in\{1,\dots,K\}\) and probabilistic classifier \(f(X)=(\hat Y,\hat P)\), perfect calibration means \(\Pr(Y=k\mid \hat P_k(X)=\hat p_k)=\hat p_k\) for all \(\hat p_k\in[0,1]\) [PAGE 5]. Classwise-ECE is \(\mathrm{ECE}_{cw}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{|B_{j,k}|}{n}\left|y_k(B_{j,k})-\bar p_k(B_{j,k})\right|\), where \(B_{j,k}\) is the \(j\)-th probability bin for class \(k\), \(n\) is dataset size, \(y_k(B_{j,k})\) is the empirical occurrence rate, and \(\bar p_k(B_{j,k})\) is the mean predicted probability [PAGE 5]. Units: all probabilities are dimensionless in \([0,1]\); classwise-ECE is an L1 probability error, reported in probability units or percentage points. Accuracy is \(\mathrm{Acc}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}[\hat y_i=y_i]\), dimensionless [PAGE 3]. The Kelly fraction is \(f^*=p-\frac{q}{b}\), with \(q=1-p\), \(b\) the dimensionless net payoff ratio per unit staked, and fractional Kelly \(f_F=Ff^*\), \(F\in[0,1]\) [PAGE 4]. The paired test uses \(t=\bar d/(s_D/\sqrt{n})\), \(d_i=C_i-A_i\), \(df=n-1\), with \(n=12\) strategy-rule pairs in the overall test [PAGE 23]. Breakdown regimes: if \(|B_{j,k}|=0\), that bin contributes zero by convention; if all bins for a class are empty, ECE is undefined. If predictions are nearly constant at the base rate, ECE can be near zero while discrimination is absent; hence the platykurtic bin-weight constraint [PAGE 5]. If \(b=0\), Kelly is undefined; if \(f^*<0\), no stake is indicated; full Kelly can exceed one and invite ruin [PAGE 4]. If \(s_D=0\), the paired t-statistic is undefined; if \(n\) is small or comparisons are multiplied without correction, p-values are fragile [PAGE 23]. Information units: ECE is not an entropy or nats quantity; log-loss in nats would be a separate scoring rule. Verbatim anchors: “calibration is a more appropriate metric than accuracy for the sports betting problem” [PAGE 1]; “optimising the forecasting model for calibration leads to greater returns than optimising for accuracy, on average (return on investment of 110.42% versus 2.98%) and in the best case (902.01% versus 222.84%)” [PAGE 1].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from probability scoring and reliability analysis through modern calibration and bankroll theory. Mid-century proper scoring rules and reliability decompositions made forecast quality a distributional object, not only a label object; later work on probability calibration formalized reliability diagrams, Platt scaling, isotonic recalibration, and temperature scaling, with Guo et al. and Kull et al. directly cited by the paper for calibration and classwise-ECE [PAGE 5]. The Kelly line runs from information-rate and capital-growth theory through Thorp’s applications and fractional-Kelly practice; the paper cites Kelly, Hsieh and Barmish, Dotan, and Thorp for the growth-optimal stake and its ruin limitations [PAGE 4]. Hubáček et al. are the immediate sports-betting predecessor: they penalized correlation with the bookmaker’s model and argued that a highly accurate model can be useless if it duplicates the bookmaker’s model [PAGE 4]. The retrocausal shift is that much early sports-ML work optimized accuracy because the task was framed as winner classification; this paper reframes the task as probability estimation for value detection, so calibration becomes the ancestor objective and accuracy becomes a downstream label metric. Multi-week and multi-season traps: the paper uses 2014/15–2015/16 for initial training, 2016/17 for validation, 2017/18 for testing, and 2018/19 for betting [PAGE 14]. That chronological discipline is correct, but it creates one-season fragility. Within-season weekly retraining is not tested; covariate shift is tested with a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test at 1%, and features such as 3P% are dropped [PAGE 10, PAGE 30]. A multi-week trap is that early-season injury news, lineup changes, and schedule density shift conditional probabilities even when marginal ECE looks stable. Another trap is post-hoc strategy selection: strategy 3 is the only strategy with a significant calibration advantage, and its best case rests on very few bets [PAGE 21, PAGE 23]. The genealogy therefore supports calibration as a theoretically better selection target, but not as a universal guarantee of reproducible profit.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind has three layers: the bettor models the game, the bettor models the bookmaker’s model, and the bookmaker models bettor biases. The paper’s own recursive warning is that accuracy can duplicate the bookmaker’s model; “a highly accurate predictive model is useless as long as it coincides with the bookmaker’s model” [PAGE 4]. Crisis habit regression appears under drawdown: full-Kelly systems crash early, then the bettor is tempted to chase losses, switch strategies mid-season, or overbet on a few high-confidence games. The source’s strategy 1 result is exactly this regression: calibration-optimized systems bet on 90.58% of games but won only 34.54%, and full and half Kelly went to zero [PAGE 18]. Strategy 3 is the counter-trap: restrict to high-confidence value opportunities, which reduces exposure to model-bookmaker disagreement and avoids low-confidence games. But strategy 3 creates its own trap: with only 2.16% of games bet, one late loss can erase a season of gains; the accuracy-optimized strategy 3 systems won 85.14% of bets yet ended near break-even or negative because “one final, unsuccessful bet saw these systems lose their profitability” [PAGE 21]. The clock layer is chronological discipline: predictions must use only prior games, cross-validation shuffling is inappropriate, and the simulation iterates games in order [PAGE 3, PAGE 12]. The recursive lesson is that the bettor should not model the bookmaker as a fixed opponent; the bookmaker’s implied probabilities embed margin and public bias, and the bettor’s edge depends on conditional disagreement, not merely on being “right” about winners. Fractional Kelly is the behavioral counterweight: it prevents the full-Kelly habit from converting a small probability error into ruin [PAGE 4]. Calibration is the epistemic counterweight: it forces the bettor’s probability statements to match observed frequencies, which is the only stable basis for recursive stake sizing.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Cross-layer veto: a marginal calibration score cannot certify conditional calibration across physical, biomechanical, and environmental strata. Let \(S\) be observed features and \(U\) be unobserved physical state: injury status, minutes restriction, travel delay, sleep load, matchup stress, or weather-related travel disruption. The model estimates \(\hat p(Y\mid S)\), which is an average over \(U\). Even if \(\mathrm{ECE}_{cw}\) is low marginally, \(\hat p(Y\mid S,U=u^*)\) can be badly wrong for a specific game. The source admits the physical gap: “Events such as the early injury of a star player can have a significant influence on the outcome of a game” [PAGE 3]. It also admits limited player-level coverage: only two player-level box-score statistics are selected, BPM and defensive rebound percentage, to capture star players and rebounders, while team-level features ignore individual availability [PAGE 9]. Biomechanical veto: BPM and rebound percentage are season-to-date averages; they do not encode minutes load, back-to-back fatigue, joint load, or role changes. A star player returning from injury can shift the true probability without shifting the feature vector enough to recalibrate. Weather veto: the NBA is indoor, but travel delays, flight disruptions, and schedule compression can alter readiness; the paper includes no weather or travel covariates, so the conditional probability is unidentified in those strata. The adjudication is therefore: calibration is necessary but not sufficient. A claim of 902.01% ROI cannot be treated as physically invariant because it is a single-season, path-dependent result under unmodeled \(U\). The correct veto is not “calibration is wrong”; it is “the ROI estimate is not transportable unless the physical layer is conditioned or randomized away.” The paper’s use of late market probabilities also means the bettor is comparing against a bookmaker forecast that already incorporates some injury and travel information, so unmodeled physical variables can masquerade as calibration error or as false value.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — trivial-calibration veto. Verification: recompute the calibration branch with and without the platykurtic bin-weight constraint, then test whether the selected model’s predictions collapse toward the base rate. Prediction: without the constraint, classwise-ECE can fall below 3.49% while accuracy stays near the base rate and bin weights become concentrated; with the constraint, SVM remains selected or the low-ECE advantage shrinks. If the low-ECE model is degenerate, the calibration claim is vetoed as a selection artifact rather than a probability-quality gain. The source itself imposes the constraint because classwise-ECE can be gamed [PAGE 5].

Scenario 2 — out-of-season replication veto. Verification: freeze the entire pipeline, retrain on seasons through 2017/18, and evaluate the same three strategies and four fractional-Kelly rules on a different held-out season. Prediction: the 902.01% best case will not replicate; the calibration-minus-accuracy ROI difference will change sign or lose significance in at least one new season. This is verifiable by pre-registering the new season, reporting all strategy-rule pairs, and running the same paired test. If the advantage fails to persist, the strong “calibration generates greater profit” claim is vetoed as a single-season result. The original overall test already gives p=0.153 [PAGE 23].

Scenario 3 — physical-conditioning veto. Verification: add lineup-availability and injury-report features, or exclude games with late scratches, then recalibrate and rerun the betting simulation. Prediction: classwise-ECE will rise for the original model, the calibration-advantage estimate will shrink, and strategy 3 ROI will become more volatile because the original model was averaging over unobserved physical states. If conditioning on physical state removes the edge, the cross-layer physical veto is confirmed. The source’s limited player-level features and explicit injury warning make this a direct, verifiable stress test [PAGE 3, PAGE 9]. A fourth optional check is market-correlation veto: compare the model’s disagreement with late market probabilities against its disagreement with earlier market probabilities; if the edge exists only in one timing window, the claim is market-timing, not calibration.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
