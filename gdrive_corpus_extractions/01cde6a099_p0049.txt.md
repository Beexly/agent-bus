**Physical veto layer.** Knight's own footnote supplies the sharpest physical veto: "in a world where a breath could transform a brick factory building into a railway yard or an ocean greyhound there would be no need for economic activity or economic science" ([PAGE 1]). Formally, let $K(t)$ be the capital stock vector and let reallocation require energy $E_{\text{realloc}} > 0$ and time $\Delta t_{\text{phys}} > 0$. The frictionless assumption $\tau = 0$ implies $\Delta t_{\text{phys}} = 0$ and $E_{\text{realloc}} = 0$, violating the second law of thermodynamics for any non-trivial transformation of fixed capital. Therefore the physical layer vetoes Regime S2: perfect mobility is thermodynamically impossible, and $\tau > 0$ is a hard lower bound set by the speed of light, material strength, and construction lead times, not by market imperfection alone.

**Biomechanical veto layer.** Wage adjustment is not a continuous frictionless signal but a process mediated by human bargaining, contract renewal cycles, and information acquisition. The relaxation time $\tau$ in $\tau \dot{w} + w = w^*$ is bounded below by the time required for workers and firms to observe $\dot{w}^*$, negotiate, and update contracts. If a dynamic change arrives faster than human cognition and bargaining can process—formally if $\|\dot{w}^*\| > w_{\max}/\tau_{\text{human}}$—then the wage equation breaks down: $w(t)$ cannot track $w^*(t)$, $\Delta w(t)$ may become negative (actual wages above standard), and the profit interval is either destroyed or transformed into a loss interval for firms. This biomechanical veto invalidates the assumption of smooth, continuous wage pursuit on page 84 ([PAGE 1]) when shocks arrive at high frequency.

**Weather and exogenous shock veto layer.** Knight's framework treats dynamic change as endogenous invention and competition, but an exogenous weather shock (e.g., a sudden destruction of capital stock) shifts $Y(t)$ discontinuously. Let the shock be represented by $F(y,t) \to F(y,t) + \delta \Phi(y)$ where $\Phi$ is non-smooth. If $\|\delta \Phi\|$ exceeds the system's redundancy margin, the static standard $\mathbf{s}^*$ is not merely displaced but annihilated; there is no well-defined interval $\Delta w$ because the reference itself ceases to exist. This is Regime S3 (non-stationary reference) triggered by an exogenous physical event rather than by invention. The veto condition is: if the shock recovery time $t_{\text{rec}}$ exceeds the planning horizon of firms, profit cannot be interpreted as a temporary dynamic rent; it becomes a residual claim on survival, outside Knight's profit theory.

**Adjudication.** The economic model on [PAGE 1] is valid only in the intersection of the non-vetoed regimes: $\tau_{\text{phys}} > 0$, $\tau_{\text{human}} > 0$, and $\|\dot{\mathbf{s}}^*\|$ bounded by the system's information-processing and physical-reallocation capacity. Outside this intersection, the entrepreneur function does not merely earn zero profit; the concept of profit as a return to dynamic change loses its referent.

**Scenario 1: Zero-friction counterfactual.**  
Veto condition: Set $\tau = 0$ and $\gamma \to \infty$ in the wage-lag and profit-decay equations, equivalent to assuming competition works "without let or hindrance" ([PAGE 1]).  
Verification: Run a computational general-equilibrium simulation with instantaneous arbitrage and zero adjustment costs; introduce a one-time cost-reducing invention at $t=0$.  
Prediction: Profit flow $\pi(t)$ is identically zero for all $t > 0$; price drops to marginal cost immediately; the wage interval $\Delta w(t)$ is identically zero; no entrepreneur function emerges. This is a direct falsification of any model that claims durable profit can exist in a frictionless static-dynamic hybrid.

**Scenario 2: Infinite-mobility counterfactual ("breath" transformation).**  
Veto condition: Assume physical capital reallocation is costless and instantaneous, so a brick factory can become a railway yard "by a breath" ([PAGE 1]). Formally, set the reallocation energy $E_{\text{realloc}} = 0$ and the transformation time $\Delta t = 0$.  
Verification: Observe a real-world capital-intensive industry (e.g., steel or shipping) and measure the minimum time and capital expenditure required to repurpose a blast furnace or a vessel; compare to the zero-cost assumption.  
Prediction: Because $E_{\text{realloc}} > 0$ and $\Delta t > 0$ in all observed cases, the frictionless world is empirically empty; economic activity and economic science remain necessary. Any model that derives profit solely from the absence of friction while assuming costless capital transformation is internally inconsistent and predicts nothing about actual economies.

**Scenario 3: Knightian uncertainty counterfactual.**  
Veto condition: Replace the dynamic-change profit model with a standard expected-utility framework requiring a probability measure $\mu$ over all possible invention outcomes. Knightian uncertainty obtains when the relevant events are non-measurable, so $E_\mu[\pi]$ is undefined.  
Verification: Conduct a decision experiment in which subjects face an ambiguous urn (Ellsberg-style) representing an unknown invention payoff distribution; measure willingness to pay for the right to be the entrepreneur.  
Prediction: Subjects will not behave as if maximizing $E_\mu[\pi]\); instead they will exhibit ambiguity aversion and rely on judgment rather than probability. The standard profit-maximization equation $\pi(t) = p^\top y - c(y;\theta)$ breaks down because $\theta(t)$ cannot be assigned a probability distribution; the entrepreneur's return is a return to bearing non-measurable uncertainty, not to risk. If a model forces a probability measure onto this space, it misprices the entrepreneur function and predicts entry/exit rates that contradict observed behavior under true uncertainty.
