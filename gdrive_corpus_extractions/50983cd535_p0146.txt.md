U(c_0,…,c_T) = Σ_{t=0}^{T} β^t u(c_t), β ∈ (0,1) dimensionless,

where u: ℝ₊ → ℝ is strictly increasing and strictly concave (u′ > 0, u″ < 0), with Inada conditions lim_{c→0} u′(c) = +∞ and lim_{c→∞} u′(c) = 0. β is the pure discount factor (dimensionless), not to be confused with the market interest rate r ≥ 0 measured in per annum (dimensionless per year).

The intertemporal budget constraint under a free market is:

Σ_{t=0}^{T} c_t / (1+r)^t = Σ_{t=0}^{T} y_t / (1+r)^t = PV(𝒚),

where y_t is dated endowment income [dollars/year]. The interior first-order condition (Euler equation) is:

u′(c_t) = β (1+r) u′(c_{t+1}), ∀t,

equivalently the marginal rate of substitution between dates t and t+1 equals the market price ratio: MRS_{t,t+1} = u′(c_t)/u′(c_{t+1}) = (1+r)/β.

Knight’s identification claim [PAGE 1] is that r is an endogenous equilibrium object, r = r(β, technology, endowments, institutions), so observing r does not identify β without independent knowledge of technology and endowments. The “zero-point” correction replaces the corner comparison (all consumption today vs. none tomorrow) with a marginal perturbation around a reference stream \bar{c}_t:

ΔU ≈ u′(\bar{c}_t)(-ε) + β u′(\bar{c}_{t+1}) ε(1+r),

and time preference is measured by the sign and magnitude of ΔU at ε → 0, not by a discrete choice between c_0 = Y, c_{t>0} = 0 and c_0 = 0, c_T = Y.

Physical layer: biology imposes a subsistence consumption floor c̲ > 0 below which health fails. Knight notes men do not “compress all the satisfactions of a lifetime into the present moment and fast forever after” [PAGE 1], but the reason is physiological necessity, not an abstract preference for the future. If min_t y_t < c̲, intertemporal smoothing is physically vetoed: consumption equals income each period, and no inference about β can be drawn. Veto condition: if caloric or survival constraints bind, the marginal trade-off between enjoyment today and abstinence tomorrow is infeasible.

Biomechanical layer: the passage frames enjoyment and abstinence as pure consumption choices, but obtaining future goods typically requires labor or effort today. If disutility of labor enters the utility function, the intertemporal margin includes effort, and the simple MRS between dated consumption goods is insufficient to isolate time preference.

Weather layer: agricultural or seasonal income processes make y_t lumpy across the year. Without storage technology (silos, refrigeration, financial instruments), a lump-sum payment on January first cannot be smoothly distributed; spoilage or storage costs create an implicit transfer cost that looks like negative interest but is technological, not preferential.

Adjudication rule: Knight’s logical point holds only when (i) a free market permits exchange between dates at rate r [PAGE 1], (ii) the subsistence floor is non-binding, and (iii) storage and financial technologies permit low-cost, known-cost transfer of goods across time. If any layer vetoes—binding c̲, absent r, or prohibitive storage cost—the observed allocation is a constrained corner solution, and claims about time preference are invalidated.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Credit autarky). Veto condition: no functioning market where interest can be obtained [PAGE 1]; r is undefined. Observable implication: household consumption co-moves one-for-one with current income; no consumption smoothing. Prediction: in villages or households without access to credit, the marginal propensity to consume out of transitory income equals approximately one, and observed behavior mimics high time preference even though β is unidentified. Verifiable test: compare high-frequency consumption-income correlation in credit-constrained versus credit-access populations; veto confirmed if correlation ≈ 1 in the no-credit group.

Scenario 2 (Binding liquidity constraint with windfall). Veto condition: a lump-sum payment is received but borrowing and saving technologies are unavailable or prohibitively costly, violating the premise that present and future dollars are freely exchangeable at rate r [PAGE 1]. Observable implication: a large share of the windfall is consumed immediately regardless of the prevailing market interest rate. Prediction: at least 70% of an unexpected transitory cash transfer equal to one month’s income is consumed within 72 hours, with the remainder decaying exponentially over the following weeks; this occurs even when r is low and stable. Verifiable test: administrative data on stimulus payments or tax refunds, measuring spending within 72 hours and at 7-, 30-, and 60-day horizons.

Scenario 3 (Knightian uncertainty regime). Veto condition: future states over the planning horizon are non-probabilizable (Knightian uncertainty), so expected-utility discounting with a scalar β is ill-posed. Observable implication: the implied discount rate on future delivery includes an ambiguity premium unrelated to patience; Euler-equation residuals covary with ambiguity or uncertainty indices. Prediction: during high-ambiguity multi-week episodes, the liquidity premium on forward or future-dated contracts widens without any corresponding change in fundamental cash-flow risk, and measured “discount rates” rise while true β remains constant. Verifiable test: track bid-ask spreads or illiquidity discounts on dated delivery instruments across low-ambiguity and high-ambiguity weeks; veto confirmed if spreads widen purely with ambiguity measures while underlying risk proxies are unchanged.
