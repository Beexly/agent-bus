Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios:

Veto Scenario 1 — Equalized reinforcement rate, varied delay.
Design: Eight albino rats, 40 trials, paths with delays \(\tau_s = 30\,\text{s}\) and \(\tau_\ell = 60\,\text{s}\), but reinforcement is delivered on every trial regardless of path chosen (yoked equal reinforcement count), with path length held constant.
Verifiable criterion: If Hullian delay discrimination operates independently of reinforcement rate, \(\hat{p}_{40}\) should rise significantly above \(0.50\) (binomial test, one-sided, \(\alpha = 0.05\)). If preference remains at chance (\(0.47\text{–}0.53\)) despite the delay difference, the claim that delay difference alone drives acquisition is falsified.
Prediction: Under equal reinforcement, \(\hat{p}_{40} \approx 0.50 \pm 0.03\); any observed rise to \(0.76\) would require delay to be a primary reinforcer independent of reward frequency.

Veto Scenario 2 — Zero-difference singularity.
Design: Same apparatus, but both paths impose an identical delay \(\tau_s = \tau_\ell = 60\,\text{s}\), with visually distinct start arms to preserve discriminable stimuli. Run 40 trials with \(R = 8\).
Verifiable criterion: The model predicts \(p_n \to 0.5\) because \(h_n(\tau_s) - h_n(\tau_\ell) = 0\). A 95 per cent confidence interval for \(\hat{p}_{40}\) that excludes \(0.50\) indicates a non-temporal confound (side bias, odor cue, maze asymmetry).
Prediction: \(\hat{p}_{40} = 0.50 \pm 0.03\); deviation beyond \(\pm 0.06\) invalidates the assumption that the apparatus is temporally neutral.

Veto Scenario 3 — Absolute-magnitude overload at fixed difference.
Design: Compare Condition A \((30\,\text{s}, 60\,\text{s})\) with Condition C \((300\,\text{s}, 600\,\text{s})\), holding \(\Delta\tau = 300\,\text{s}\) constant while raising absolute magnitude tenfold. Counterbalance sides, \(R = 12\), 40 trials each.
Verifiable criterion: Page 161 asserts that acquisition rate depends on both the difference and the absolute magnitude of delays [PAGE 161]. If the absolute-magnitude term is negative (long delays weaken contiguity), Condition A should reach a given dominance level in fewer trials than Condition C despite identical \(\Delta\tau\). If Condition C learns faster or identically, the absolute-magnitude claim is falsified.
Prediction: Condition A reaches the dominance criterion in \(\approx 15\text{–}40\) reinforcements; Condition C requires \(>40\) reinforcements or never reaches criterion, reflecting the delay-discounting singularity as \(\tau \to\) minutes.
