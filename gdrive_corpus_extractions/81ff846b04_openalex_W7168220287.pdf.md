**Hop 2 — Label circularity.** Supervised injury-prediction models require ground-truth labels. So the pipeline trains on a noisy, systematically biased label to produce an "objective" output that is then validated against that same label. This is a closed loop, not an independent measurement. Any reported accuracy inherits the label noise floor — the ceiling is set by the reliability of the self-report instrument, not by the sophistication of the network.

## <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage runs four distinct threads, and the review's credibility depends on keeping them separate.

**Thread A — psychophysics of pain measurement (1940s–1990s).** The visual-analogue and numeric rating scales descend from Stevens' ratio-scaling tradition; their reliability ceiling is the psychometric foundation on which every AI claim here rests. The review never cites this literature, which is why it can assert "objective" without confronting the fact that its labels are ordinal self-reports [PAGE 1].

**Thread B — facial action coding (1978 → 2008).** Ekman & Friesen's FACS, then Prkachin & Solomon's PSPI, produced the AU-based pain index that refs [35]–[38] operationalize [PAGE 5, PAGE 8]. The retromcausal trap: PSPI was validated on adult postoperative and clinical populations, not on athletes under competitive arousal — so applying it to sports medicine is a population transfer the source never justifies.

**Thread C — wearable biomechanics (2000s → present).** IMU-based gait and asymmetry analysis matured in clinical gait labs; refs [15]–[18] move it to the field. The transfer introduces the drift regime in Eq. 5, which the review's Table 2 acknowledges only as "Device accuracy… remain challenges [15]" [PAGE 4].

**Thread D — injury prediction epidemiology (2016 → present).** This is where the deepest trap sits. The ACWR (Eq. 4) was popularized as a monitoring tool and then subjected to a sustained methodological critique — mathematical coupling between numerator and denominator, spurious correlation from shared terms, and arbitrary banding. The source's Table 4 reproduces the framing ("Load-injury modeling… Examines injury risk relationships in professional soccer [42]" [PAGE 8]) without carrying the critique forward. **Multi-week trap:** a clinician adopting ACWR banding will, in weeks 1–4 of any return-to-play, get maximal-risk flags generated by a vanishing denominator — and will interpret them as physiology. The trap is self-reinforcing because the flags are acted on (load reduced), which suppresses the injury, which "confirms" the flag.

**Multi-week trap #2 — automation bias compounding.** Each week the model runs without a documented false negative, clinician trust rises monotonically. By the point a real deterioration occurs, the prior against overriding the model is high. Page 6 hedges against exactly this: "Return to play guided by AI should hence be viewed as an aid that facilitates clinical reasoning, and not as a replacement for clinical judgment" — a hedge that is structurally insufficient, because the failure mode is not disbelief in the tool but over-belief in it.

## <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The athlete's nested model.** The athlete models the clinician's model of the athlete. Page 1 names the selection pressure: "athlete underreporting, subjective variability, and poor capture of dynamic changes during training or competition." Page 2 gives the mechanism: "players tend to underreport their pain due to protective biomechanics." The athlete's optimal report is a strategic signal, not a symptom readout — and any model trained on that report has learned the equilibrium of a game, not the state of a tissue.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under cumulative load and fatigue, athletes revert to the motor pattern that is *cheapest*, which is the pre-injury guarding pattern. Page 4 anticipates this precisely: "athletes may adopt an avoidance strategy for movement even without high subjective pain perception." The consequence for the model is distribution shift at exactly the moment of interest: the "crisis" sample is drawn from a different generative process than the "baseline" sample, so asymmetry metrics degrade toward pre-training means. A model that has learned "high asymmetry = injury" will now read high asymmetry as *return to baseline*, which is the opposite of the truth.

**The counter-trap.** The clinician's counter-move — trust the sensor over the athlete — creates its own regression: sensor adherence becomes a proxy for compliance. An athlete who wears the IMU less shows lower measured variance and thus "lower risk." The measurement instrument is now measuring the athlete's relationship to the instrument. Page 9's fusion optimism ("no indicators measuring pain, fatigue, and injury risk with a single sensor") is silent on this channel.

**The clock mismatch.** Three clocks run incommensurately: the tissue clock (weeks to months — collagen remodeling, tendon mechanical recovery), the sensor clock (milliseconds to seconds — IMU sampling at 100–1000 Hz), and the model clock (months — retraining and revalidation cadence). Any inference that treats a sensor-clock event as a tissue-clock event commits a category error. Page 6's biopsychosocial framing ("recovery from sports injuries is influenced by both biological and psychological factors") is the paper's only gesture toward reconciling the clocks, and it is programmatic rather than operational.

**Testable concession.** Page 9 states the honest boundary: "AI must only be regarded as an instrument of decision-making, and not an ultimate solution to the problem of injury and pain prevention and treatment." A clinician's recursive model of the model should encode exactly this: the tool's output is one more voice in the room, with known bias structure, and the athlete's underreporting is itself a data feature rather than a nuisance to be filtered.

**Veto scenario 1 — ACWR denominator collapse.** *Setup:* Athlete returns from 6 weeks of immobilization; chronic load denominator = 0.4 AU, acute load = 8 AU on day one. Eq. 4 yields ACWR = 20, and any banded model fires a maximum-risk alert. *Veto:* the ratio is undefined in the limit and unbounded near it; the alert carries no information about tissue state. *Falsifiable prediction:* in a held-out cohort restricted to weeks 0–4 post-immobilization, ACWR-based risk flags will achieve AUC ≤ 0.55 against prospectively observed injury — i.e., no better than chance. If AUC > 0.70 is observed on a pre-registered cohort, this veto fails and should be withdrawn.

**Veto scenario 2 — Gyroscopic integration drift masquerading as asymmetry.** *Setup:* A 90-second unanchored IMU trial reports a 9° knee-asymmetry reduction post-rehab. Eq. 5 predicts \(\sigma_{\hat\theta}\) of the same order for typical consumer \(\sigma_b\). *Veto:* the alleged treatment effect is within the instrument's drift accumulation. *Falsifiable prediction:* repeating the identical trial with a magnetometer or a markerless vision anchor will change the measured delta by a factor \(>2\); equivalently, bench-testing the same IMU on a rigid jig for 90 s will show \(>8°\) apparent rotation with zero true motion. If the anchored and unanchored deltas agree within 20%, the veto fails.

**Veto scenario 3 — Facial AU cross-population mis-calibration.** *Setup:* A deep-learning pain-face model trained predominantly on one demographic is deployed to a mixed-demographic squad under variable stadium lighting. *Veto:* Page 8's enumerated confounders (age, gender, ethnicity, morphology, illumination, camera view, dataset quality) each shift the AU→PSPI mapping; the composite shift is unbounded. *Falsifiable prediction:* on out-of-distribution faces at illuminance < 200 lux, the calibration slope of predicted vs. observed PSPI will fall below 0.70 and the intercept will shift by > 1 PSPI point, with the largest errors concentrated in the highest-pain cases (the clinically important tail). If calibration slope ≥ 0.85 is demonstrated across demographics under field lighting, the veto fails.

**Meta-veto across all three.** Each scenario is designed so that its defeat condition is a single, pre-registerable measurement. None can be argued away by narrative — which is precisely the failure mode of the source review, whose Tables 1–4 report no numbers, no confidence intervals, and no sample sizes, and whose Limitations section (Page 9) concedes "limited sample sizes, the heterogeneity of the definition of injuries, the accuracy of sensor devices used for detecting pain, and the absence of external validity." Until those gaps are closed, the correct disposition toward every claim in this paper is: *map accepted, instrument unvalidated, deployment vetoed.*
