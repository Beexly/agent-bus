Diminishing returns versus perpetual change. Let \(A\) be advertising effort. Diminishing returns imply \(\partial^2 U_{\text{add}}/\partial A^2 < 0\) with equilibrium \(A^{*}\) where marginal utility of advertising equals marginal cost. Knight’s favored perpetual-change regime implies either \(\partial^2 U_{\text{add}}/\partial A^2 \ge 0\) over relevant ranges, or a time-varying preference shifter \(\varepsilon(t)\) such that \(U_{\text{add}}(A,t) = U_{\text{add}}(A) + \varepsilon(t)\), \(\partial \varepsilon/\partial t \neq 0\), precluding any long-run rest point.

Units: \(x\) in physical units; \(p\) in currency per unit; \(w\) in currency; \(A\) in currency per period or hours per period; \(\pi\) in currency per period. \(U\) in ordinal utils (no SI unit).

Breakdown regimes: (i) disparagement or fraud violates the exclusion in footnote 2 [PAGE 1]; (ii) non-identifiable \(f(\omega)\) voids expected-utility maximization; (iii) consumer sovereignty fails if preferences are adapted endogenously to \(a\), making (2) circular; (iv) physical ornament that “interferes with its uses” [PAGE 1] imposes a hard engineering constraint \(U_{\text{phys}}(x) \ge \bar{u}\) that advertising cannot override. Verbatim anchors: “The business of want creation is, of course, very uncertain and aleatory or 'risky'” [PAGE 1]; “costs will then be equal to values throughout the system, the conditions of profitless adjustment being present” [PAGE 1]; “the consumer is the last court of appeal” [PAGE 1].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Knight’s page sits at a convergence of traditions. The first lineage is the uncertainty entrepreneur: Cantillon’s trader bearing risk, refined by Knight into the distinction between measurable risk and unmeasurable uncertainty [PAGE 1]. The second is subjective value: Menger and Mises treat utility as imputed by the individual, which anticipates Knight’s refusal to privilege physical over psychological utility [PAGE 1]. The third is equilibrium criticism: Marshallian partial equilibrium assumes given preferences and foreseeable results; Knight’s “profitless adjustment” [PAGE 1] is the limiting case where competition erases pure profit. Schumpeter later radicalized this into creative destruction, where perpetual change—not equilibrium—is the norm, echoing Knight’s guess that want creation is “a perpetual cause” [PAGE 1].

Multi-week traps. In dynamic markets, advertising exhibits habit formation over weeks. A firm that launches a persuasive campaign in week \(t\) captures share of mind; rivals observe and imitate by week \(t+2\) or \(t+3\). Because Knight’s competition equalizes gains only when results are foreseeable [PAGE 1], the unforeseeable consumer response to persuasion creates a multi-week window of quasi-rent. The trap is overinvestment: firms extrapolate the week-1 response curve into weeks 4–8, assuming diminishing returns have not yet bound, while rivals’ matching spend erodes the differential. If Knight is correct that change is perpetual, there is no week at which the trap snaps shut into equilibrium; instead a Red Queen dynamic persists. If diminishing returns obtain, the trap snaps shut at \(A^{*}\), but the firm cannot observe \(A^{*}\) in real time because consumer preferences are the “last court of appeal” and their verdict arrives with a lag [PAGE 1].

Historical veto: the assumption that organized social direction of consumption lies outside private competitive organization [PAGE 1] breaks down when regulation—advertising standards, truth-in-advertising, bans on puffing—redefines the feasible set \(A\) mid-stream, invalidating any multi-week forecast built on pre-regulation elasticities. The multi-week trap is therefore not merely competitive but regulatory: the very definition of permissible persuasion can shift while capital is sunk into campaign production.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Firm \(i\) does not maximize \(U\) or \(\pi\) in isolation; it maximizes over beliefs about Firm \(j\)’s beliefs about Firm \(i\). Let \(\sigma_i\) be firm \(i\)’s advertising strategy and \(B_j(\sigma_i)\) firm \(j\)’s conjectured response. Firm \(i\)’s problem is:

\[
\max_{\sigma_i} J_i\!\left( \pi_i(\sigma_i,\sigma_j(\sigma_i)),\; \mathcal{I}_i \right) \tag{4}
\]

where \(\sigma_j(\sigma_i) = \arg\max_{\sigma_j} J_j(\pi_j(\sigma_i,\sigma_j),\mathcal{I}_j)\). The regress terminates only at cognitive or budget bounds.

Crisis habit regression. Under Knightian uncertainty—when results cannot be foreseen [PAGE 1]—firms regress to habitual \(\sigma_i\) from prior periods rather than recomputing \(J_i\). The habit is a cached policy, not an equilibrium. Counter-trap. Firm \(k\), anticipating universal regression, deviates by maintaining or expanding persuasive spend while competitors cut back. Because consumer attention is finite, \(k\) captures disproportionate share of voice; if Knight’s “perpetual change” guess holds [PAGE 1], the deviation is not arbitraged away quickly, and \(k\) earns a temporary pure profit. Clock. The clock variable is the discount rate \(\rho\) and the speed of preference adjustment \(\lambda\). If \(\lambda < \rho\), preferences adjust slower than capital discounting, and advertising stocks depreciate before consumer verdicts arrive; the firm over-invests relative to the “last court of appeal” [PAGE 1]. If \(\lambda > \rho\), the court rules quickly and competitive imitation erodes rent within weeks. Recursive depth is bounded: level-\(k\) reasoning with \(k \ge 3\) becomes computationally intractable without heuristic fallbacks, which is precisely where habit regression enters.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Knight acknowledges that “decorative ornament often actually interfering with its uses” [PAGE 1]. Let physical efficiency be \(E(x) \ge \bar{e}\), a hard engineering constraint. Advertising can raise \(U(x,a)\), but it cannot raise \(E(x)\). If \(E(x) < \bar{e}\), the good is physically vetoed regardless of persuasive utility [PAGE 1]. Formally, the feasible set is \(X_{\text{feas}} = \{x : E(x) \ge \bar{e}\}\); advertising operates only on \(U\) over \(X_{\text{feas}}\).

Biomechanical layer. The consumer’s sensory and cognitive bandwidth limits perception of \(a\). Let attentional capacity be \(\bar{C}\) bits per decision. If the information content of advertising \(I(a;x) > \bar{C}\), the consumer cannot process the associated fact, and \(U(x,a)\) collapses toward \(U(x,0)\). The biomechanical veto is:

\[
I(a;x) \le \bar{C} \tag{5}
\]

otherwise persuasion is noise. This bound is independent of content truth or falsity; it is a channel-capacity constraint on the “last court of appeal” [PAGE 1].

Weather/exogenous layer. Exogenous shocks—macro demand shifts, supply interruptions, regulatory weather—shift the budget constraint \(p\cdot x \le w\) or the preference functional \(U\) itself. Let \(W(t)\) be a weather vector entering \(w(W)\) or \(U(x,a;W)\). A shock that lowers \(w\) below the price of the advertised bundle voids demand creation: no amount of puffing can generate purchases outside the budget set. The veto condition is:

\[
\min_{x\in X_{\text{feas}}} p(W)\cdot x > w(W) \;\Rightarrow\; D(p,a,W) = 0 \tag{6}
\]

Advertising-based forecasts conditioned on pre-shock parameters are invalidated.

Adjudication order: physical veto (\(E \ge \bar{e}\)) \(\to\) biomechanical veto (\(I \le \bar{C}\)) \(\to\) budget/weather veto (6) \(\to\) only then does Knightian utility comparison \(U(x,a_1)\) versus \(U(x,a_2)\) operate [PAGE 1]. Any layer can nullify the others.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Informative-only advertising regime. Counterfactual: a regulator prohibits all persuasive/puffing content, permitting only verifiable physical attribute disclosures. Veto condition: persuasive component \(a_{\text{pers}} = 0\); only \(a_{\text{info}}\) remains. Prediction: demand differentials between physically identical goods collapse to zero; premium attributable to “high-sounding names” vanishes; pure profit from want creation \(\to 0\) under competition [PAGE 1]. Verification: compare brand price dispersion and market concentration pre/post ban in a product class with stable physical attributes (natural experiment). Breakdown: if consumers cannot verify attributes even when disclosed, biomechanical veto (5) dominates and the prediction fails.

Scenario 2 — Real-time objective utility dashboard. Counterfactual: consumers access an immutable, real-time quality-and-price comparison for all substitutes. Veto condition: the consumer’s “last court of appeal” [PAGE 1] is supplied with exogenous objective \(\hat{U}(x)\) independent of advertising. Prediction: willingness-to-pay for associated-fact utilities converges to the cost of provision; cross-price elasticity between physically identical brands approaches unity; advertising spend shifts entirely to informative content or disappears. Verification: field experiment with a digital comparison platform; measure conversion and price premiums. Breakdown: if consumers distrust the dashboard or prefer experiential utility, Knight’s consumer sovereignty retains force and the veto is partial.

Scenario 3 — Randomized advertising budget holdout. Counterfactual: firms are randomly assigned persuasive advertising budgets while product quality, price, and informative content are held constant. Veto condition: \(A\) is exogenous and orthogonal to unobserved quality. Prediction: measured marginal return to persuasive advertising is zero after controlling for informative content; no persistent sales effect beyond the campaign window. Verification: RCT across geographically isolated markets with sales scanner data; regression of log sales on ad spend with market and time fixed effects. Breakdown: if advertising alters preferences endogenously (Knight’s perpetual change [PAGE 1]), the holdout itself changes the competitive equilibrium, violating stable-unit-treatment-value assumptions and biasing the estimate toward zero even when real effects exist.

Each scenario produces a binary veto: observed prediction falsified \(\Rightarrow\) Knightian want-creation channel rejected for that context; observed confirmation \(\Rightarrow\) channel survives but remains bounded by the cross-layer vetoes above.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
