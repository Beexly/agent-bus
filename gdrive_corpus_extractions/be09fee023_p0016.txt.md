<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source text [PAGE 13] specifies only algorithmic layers: CI computation, common-count filtering, top-N truncation, B-tree storage, and recency weighting. It encodes no physical, biomechanical, or weather constraints. Consequently, exogenous veto layers must be adjudicated above the CI ranking.

Physical layer: B-tree table regeneration is an offline batch process [PAGE 13]. Data-center thermal limits, disk I/O contention, or network partition can delay table rebuilds. If the served snapshot age exceeds Δ_max (seconds), a physical freshness veto forces fallback to a generic catalog ranking rather than a stale CI-ranked list.

Biomechanical layer: Human choice architecture is bounded by Hick’s Law; a similar-items list longer than the user’s decision capacity imposes motor and cognitive cost. Even if CI ranks M > N_max items above threshold, a biomechanical veto caps the rendered list at N_max items regardless of CI values, truncating attention before the algorithmic top-N [PAGE 13] is exhausted.

Weather veto: Regional severe weather (hurricane, blizzard, heatwave) disrupts last-mile delivery feasibility. An item with maximal CI (e.g., a snow shovel or perishable) is physically vetoed when local conditions make delivery impossible or irrational. Weather overrides CI because a 0.0667 similarity score [PAGE 13] cannot compensate for a zero delivery probability.

Adjudication order: Physical stock-out / freshness veto > biomechanical attention veto > weather relevance veto > CI ranking. Any vetoed item is removed prior to top-N selection and B-tree lookup [PAGE 13].
