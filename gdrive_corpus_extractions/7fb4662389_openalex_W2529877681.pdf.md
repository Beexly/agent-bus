Stabilizing selection [PAGE 16]–[PAGE 17]: \(G(t,z)=\{y\in F(t,z):\partial_tV(t,z)+\langle\partial_zV(t,z),y\rangle\le-\alpha(t,z)\}\); existence of Carathéodory \(f(t,z)\in G(t,z)\), \(f(t,0)=0\), such that \(z'=f(t,z)\) implies \(z(t)\to0\) as \(t\to\infty\), provided \(0\in F(t,0)\), \(p\in L^1(0,\infty)\), and an integral divergence condition on \(\inf_{\|z\|\ge r}(-\alpha(t,z))\) holds for every \(r>0\).

Exact singularity/breakdown regimes: superlinear \(F\) (no linear growth) \(\Rightarrow\) finite escape; non-absolutely-continuous tube \(P\) \(\Rightarrow\) viability kernel may be empty after a jump; lack of joint u.s.c. of \(F\) with non-Lipschitz \(V\) \(\Rightarrow\) viscosity equivalence fails; \(W\) violating \(H1/H2\) \(\Rightarrow\) Theorem 4.3 three-way equivalence collapses.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. The paper is an IIASA Working Paper WP-93-54 by H. Frankowska and S. Plaskacz, September 1993 [PAGE 1]–[PAGE 2]. Its intellectual ancestry runs through Lyapunov’s second method (1892), Aubin’s viability theory [3, 1991], Aubin–Cellina differential inclusions [4, 1984], Aubin–Frankowska set-valued analysis [5, 1990], Crandall–Lions viscosity solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations [6, 1983], and Frankowska’s contingent Hamilton–Jacobi work [7, 1989; 8, 1993]. The references also include Artstein on time-varying and retarded equations [1, 1984], Guseinov–Subbotin–Ushakov on derivatives for multivalued mappings in game-theoretic control [11, 1985], and Plaskacz on periodic solutions on compact sets [12, 1990]. The immediate technical forebears are Frankowska–Plaskacz–Rzezuchowski on measurable viability theorems [9, 10].

Multi-week traps embedded in this lineage. A system identified only through a time-measurable differential inclusion cannot be treated as if \(F(t,\cdot)\) were continuous in time; doing so hides the null-set exceptional times where the contingent viability condition can fail. Over weeks of operation, those failures accumulate: a trajectory that remains viable on every full-measure set can still exit the tube at a discontinuity of \(P\) or at a time where linear growth is violated. Another trap is the confusion between a viability subtube and the ambient tube: Theorem 3.5 shows the set of viable initial conditions is the closed viability kernel, generally a strict subset of \(\operatorname{Graph}(P)\) [PAGE 11]; assuming the whole tube is viable over a multi-week horizon is false whenever the Hamiltonian inequality fails on a positive-measure set. A third trap is substituting pointwise Lyapunov decrease for uniform stability: without \(W\ge aV\) or an equivalent positivity gap, Corollary 4.5’s exponential bound is unavailable, and asymptotic convergence in Theorem 5.1 gives no finite-week recovery guarantee. Finally, the Carathéodory selection in Theorem 5.1 is measurable in time but need not be continuous; a controller synthesized from it can exhibit chattering at the null-set times where measurability is the only regularity.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. Crisis habit regression is the dynamical tendency of \(z(t)\) to revert to pre-crisis trajectories once the viability condition \(\inf_{v\in F(t,z)}\langle p_z,v\rangle\le -p_t\) ceases to hold on a set of positive measure [PAGE 8], [PAGE 12]. When that contingent inequality fails, no absolutely continuous selection can keep \(z(t)\) inside \(P(t)\); the state regresses along whatever velocity \(F\) permits, typically toward the boundary or out of the viable set entirely. The counter-trap is precisely the viability kernel: Theorem 3.5 identifies the largest subtube of \(P\) that remains viable under \(F\), and Theorem 5.1 provides a stabilizing selection \(f\in F\) driving every trajectory to the origin [PAGE 11], [PAGE 16]–[PAGE 17]. Choosing \(f\) is the counter-trap to regression: it replaces arbitrary crisis-driven switching with a feedback selection that enforces \(\partial_tV+\langle\partial_zV,f\rangle\le-\alpha(t,z)\).

The clock is the integrability structure \(p\in L^1(0,T)\) and the Grönwall bound used in the proof of Theorem 3.1: \(\|z(t)\|\) is controlled by \(\exp(\int p(s)\,ds)\) times the initial radius [PAGE 8]–[PAGE 9]. If \(p\) is not integrable over the mission horizon, the clock runs fast enough to permit finite escape; if \(P\) is not absolutely continuous, the clock ticks through a constraint discontinuity that no absolutely continuous trajectory can cross. Regression wins when the clock speed \(p(t)\) exceeds the tube’s ability to absorb motion or when the viability subtube is empty; the counter-trap holds only on the closed viability kernel where the Hamiltonian inequality is valid almost everywhere. Recursive theory of mind thus predicts that an agent modeling the environment as smoothly time-varying will overestimate the viable horizon, while an agent respecting the \(L^1\) clock and the contingent condition will correctly restrict expectations to the kernel.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather veto proof. Physical layer: the state \(z(t)\in\mathbb R^d\) and its velocity \(z'(t)\in F(t,z(t))\) must admit an absolutely continuous realization [PAGE 4], [PAGE 6]. Biomechanical layer: the tube \(P(t)\) represents state constraints (e.g., joint limits, safe corridors) and must be absolutely continuous on \([0,T]\) [PAGE 7]; a discontinuous constraint set is a biomechanical veto because no physical trajectory can teleport across a jump in \(P\). Weather layer: \(F\) is only measurable in \(t\), modeling gusts or time-varying disturbances [PAGE 4], [PAGE 12]; absence of joint upper semicontinuity in \((t,z)\) is a weather veto unless \(V\) is locally Lipschitz [PAGE 12]–[PAGE 13].

Veto conditions (breakdown is automatic if any holds): (1) violation of linear growth \(\|F(t,z)\|\le p(t)(1+\|z\|)\), \(p\in L^1\) [PAGE 6] \(\Rightarrow\) finite escape, no solution on the full interval; (2) \(P\) not absolutely continuous [PAGE 7] \(\Rightarrow\) viability theorem inapplicable; (3) \(F\) upper semicontinuous in \(z\) alone, \(V\) not locally Lipschitz [PAGE 12]–[PAGE 13] \(\Rightarrow\) viscosity-supersolution equivalence fails; (4) \(W\) violating H1/H2 [PAGE 13] \(\Rightarrow\) Theorem 4.3 equivalence collapses; (5) \(W\not\ge aV\) for any \(a>0\) [PAGE 15] \(\Rightarrow\) no exponential decay guarantee, only monotonicity.

Veto-proof regime: \(F\) measurable in \(t\), u.s.c. in \(z\), closed convex images, linear growth; \(P\) absolutely continuous; \(V\) locally Lipschitz or \(F\) jointly u.s.c.; \(W\) satisfying H1/H2. Under exactly these conditions, the five-way equivalences of Theorems 3.1, 4.2, 4.3 and Corollary 4.5 hold, so no layer vetoes the Lyapunov conclusion.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto 1 — superlinear growth. Counterfactual: replace the linear-growth assumption (6) [PAGE 6] by \(F(t,z)=\{z^2\}\) in scalar dynamics \(z'=z^2\), \(z(0)=1\), on \([0,T]\). Verifiable condition: \(p(t)(1+\|z\|)\) cannot dominate \(z^2\) globally. Prediction: solution \(z(t)=1/(1-t)\) blows up at \(t=1\); no absolutely continuous solution exists on any interval containing \(t=1\). RLVR observation: the learned policy observes \(\|z(t)\|\) exceeding any preset bound before the horizon; veto triggers and the episode is terminated.

Veto 2 — discontinuous tube. Counterfactual: let \(P(t)=\{z:\|z\|\le1\}\) for \(t<t^\*\) and \(P(t)=\varnothing\) for \(t\ge t^\*\), violating absolute continuity [PAGE 7]. Verifiable condition: \(\operatorname{Graph}(P)\) has a jump at \(t^\*\). Prediction: no solution \(z(\cdot)\) can satisfy \(z(t)\in P(t)\) for all \(t\) across \(t^\*\); the viability kernel is empty after \(t^\*\). RLVR observation: at \(t^\*\) the state constraint monitor returns infeasible for every continuation; veto triggers, resetting to the last viable subtube.

Veto 3 — measurability-only time dependence with non-Lipschitz \(V\). Counterfactual: \(F(t,z)\) is measurable in \(t\) but discontinuous on a dense null set, and \(V\) is lower semicontinuous but not locally Lipschitz [PAGE 12]–[PAGE 13]. Verifiable condition: \(H(t,z,\cdot)\) fails upper semicontinuity, so the viscosity supersolution equivalence of Theorem 4.2 breaks down. Prediction: pointwise checks of \(\inf_{v\in F(t,z)}D_TV(t,z)(1,v)\le-W(t,z)\) may pass almost everywhere yet no viscosity solution in the Crandall–Lions sense exists; over repeated episodes, trajectories violate the Lyapunov decrease on an accumulating sequence of null times. RLVR observation: the critic reports local decrease at sampled times but the rollout exhibits net \(V\)-increase over the week; veto triggers and the policy is rolled back to the last viscosity-verified checkpoint.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
