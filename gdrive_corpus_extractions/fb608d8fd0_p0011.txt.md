(1) Inertial navigation: from gimbaled inertial platforms of the Apollo era (Draper Instrumentation Lab) through strapdown INS (Savage) to MEMS IMUs; the core propagation equations above are the direct descendants of those navigation filters, and the drift pathology is the classic Schuler/unbounded error growth of unaided inertial navigation. (2) Optical motion capture: stereophotogrammetry with retro-reflective markers (Vicon-style systems), limited by capture volume, camera count, and marker occlusion as described in the background [PAGE 1]. (3) Sensor fusion and attitude estimation: Kalman filtering (1960), Schmidt extended Kalman filtering for INS/GPS, Wahba's problem for vector attitude, and MEMS-orientation filters (Mahony, Madgwick) commercialized by Xsens (MTw, MVN). This patent hybridizes (1) and (2), using sparse optical markers to bound the cubic/quadratic drift of (1) while retaining the infrastructure-free wearability of inertial sensing.

Multi-week traps implied by the physics: (a) bias instability random walk causes b_a and b_g to drift over days-to-weeks, so a factory calibration grows stale and the \tfrac{1}{2} b_a t^2 bound is violated without recalibration; (b) temperature hysteresis changes IMU bias and scale factor, breaking the constant-bias assumption in the drift laws; (c) strap or garment loosening changes the lever-arm r and mounting rotation C_{sensor}^{segment}, invalidating the lever-arm compensation and injecting centripetal terms into the aiding innovation; (d) local magnetic anomalies (steel-reinforced floors, nearby equipment) violate the homogeneity condition required for shared magnetic-North heading [PAGE 1], and these anomalies are spatially fixed but vary by room, so a system calibrated in one lab fails in another after weeks of deployment; (e) soft-tissue artifact at the marker/IMU interface grows with movement intensity and is not averaged out over time, so weekly repeatability in gait or sport performance remains poor despite low instantaneous sensor noise.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The estimator's belief state can be modeled recursively. Layer 0 is the raw IMU propagation: the filter "believes" its integrated pose with covariance P growing according to the error-state model above. Layer 1 is the aiding correction: when optical position innovations arrive, the filter revises its belief; if innovations are consistent (normalized innovation squared within chi-square bounds), trust in the hybrid state increases. Layer 2 is the frame-alignment monitor: the filter must hold a belief about \Delta\psi between the positioning frame and magnetic-North frame; if this belief is wrong, the filter recursively trusts corrupted corrections. Layer 3 is the veto arbiter: a meta-belief about whether any layer is trustworthy.

Crisis habit regression: when optical aiding drops out (occlusion, camera loss), the system regresses to its default habit—pure inertial dead reckoning. Because the filter has no external observation to contradict it, its internal confidence (covariance) may remain artificially low if process noise is underestimated, producing overconfident drift. This is the mode the patent warns renders the solution unsuitable for VR/AR [PAGE 1].

Counter-trap: biomechanical joint constraints. The patent notes segments "remain linked at their joints" [PAGE 1]. These kinematic constraints act as pseudo-measurements that bound relative segment motion; they form a counter-trap to unbounded drift during aiding outages. However, they are inequality/soft constraints, not absolute observations, so they only slow—not stop—the growth of absolute position error.

Clock: drift accumulation imposes a hard time constant. For a position tolerance \tau_{pos} (e.g., 0.05 m for high-fidelity VR) and worst-case bias b_a, the maximum allowable unaided duration is T_{max} = \sqrt{2\tau_{pos}/|b_a|}; beyond T_{max}, the inertial-only mode must be vetoed. The recursive loop is therefore: propagate → innovate (if available) → check consistency → if consistent extend trust, if inconsistent regress to constrained inertial → start/continue clock → veto when T > T_{max} or frame-misalignment belief exceeds threshold.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto (drift bound): From the patent, drift > 1% of traversed distance is unacceptable for AR/VR [PAGE 1]. Veto condition:
\frac{1}{2}|b_a|t^2 + \frac{1}{6}g|b_g|t^3 > \tau_{pos}.
Using datasheet b_a = 10^{-3} m/s^2, b_g = 10^{-5} rad/s, \tau_{pos} = 0.05 m: the quadratic term alone gives t < \sqrt{2(0.05)/10^{-3}} \approx 10 s. Proof: if the estimator runs unaided longer than T_{max}, physical law guarantees the state estimate lies outside the tolerance ball; continued output is physically false and must be vetoed.

Biomechanical veto (rigid-body/marker placement): The patent specifies five positional units on hands, feet, and head for high-accuracy VR [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: number of aiding units N_a < 5 for VR mode, or marker placed on non-rigid tissue where soft-tissue artifact \epsilon_{st} > \tau_{st} (e.g., 10 mm during running). Proof: the rigid-body assumption a_M = a_{IMU} + \dot{\omega}\times r + \omega\times(\omega\times r) fails if the marker-sensor offset r changes due to tissue deformation; the innovation is then biased by \epsilon_{st}, violating the zero-mean measurement assumption and corrupting the filter.

Environmental/magnetic veto (weather-like context layer): The patent requires a homogeneous magnetic field for shared heading [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: magnetometer innovation \|m^b - (R_b^n)^T m^n\| > \gamma_m (e.g., 0.2 \mu T deviation) or temporal field variation \|\dot{m}^n\| > threshold. Proof: under ferromagnetic distortion, the yaw measurement is biased; fusing it rotates the position-aiding correction by \Delta\psi, creating lateral error \|p\|\sin(\Delta\psi). No amount of filtering recovers heading from a corrupted magnetic reference; the heading layer must be vetoed until the field is homogeneous or an alternative heading source is available.

Optical occlusion veto: If line-of-sight to sufficient cameras is lost, z_k is unavailable [PAGE 1]. Vector: immediately switch to inertial-only, start clock T_{max}; if occlusion persists beyond T_{max}, veto high-fidelity output and fall back to drift-bounded relative pose only.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Total aiding removal): Verifiable setup — subject walks a known 100 m path while optical aiding is physically disabled for T = 60 s; ground truth re-acquired at t = 60 s. Prediction — with consumer-grade b_a = 10^{-3} m/s^2, \delta p \approx \tfrac{1}{2}(10^{-3})(60)^2 = 1.8 m, exceeding any sub-meter VR tolerance; even with tactical-grade b_a = 10^{-5} m/s^2, \delta p \approx 18 mm plus gyro-coupled cubic terms. RLVR veto: trigger if re-acquisition error > 0.5 m; predicted outcome = veto fired, pure-inertial hypothesis confirmed.

Scenario 2 (Magnetic distortion): Verifiable setup — place a neodymium dipole near the waist-mounted IMU while subject stands still; record magnetometer and optical heading. Prediction — local field perturbation rotates m^b by > 30° within 1 s; if unvetoed, the filter fuses the corrupted magnetic vector and rotates the heading state by \Delta\psi > 30°, inducing lateral position error \|p\|\sin(\Delta\psi) on subsequent aided corrections. RLVR veto: trigger if magnetometer innovation > 5 standard deviations; predicted outcome = heading layer vetoed, position aiding continues in positioning frame only with yaw marked unobservable.

Scenario 3 (High-dynamic saturation/noise): Verifiable setup — subject performs sprints with peak horizontal acceleration 4 m/s^2 and angular rates > 300 deg/s; IMU accelerometer noise density N_a = 0.05 m/s^2/\sqrt{Hz} at 100 Hz sampling. Prediction — over a 10 s burst, white-noise position standard deviation \sigma_p \sim (0.05)(10)^{3/2}/\sqrt{3} \approx 0.91 m, plus possible accelerometer clipping if specific force approaches full-scale range. RLVR veto: trigger if normalized innovation squared (NIS) exceeds chi-square threshold or if \|\\tilde{f}\| > 0.9 f_{sat}; predicted outcome = inertial propagation vetoed during burst, output held at last aided state with covariance inflation.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
