The momentary clock runs over milliseconds: "we hold fast to the perception of their real shape, by mentally combining the pictures momentarily received with the notion of peculiar positions in space" [PAGE 259].

Crisis emerges on the oblique plane, where the recursive loop is violated. The imagined perspective figure does not combine with the prior sensation; it "seems less to combine with the one a moment previously seen by the eye than to suppress it and take its place" [PAGE 259]. The mind's model of its own combining process fails because the imagined component assumes priority over the real component.

The counter-trap is to assume that the complement always fuses seamlessly. James's own footnote refutes this: imperfect absorption lets the cross "detach itself from this and then look square," and a circular after-image projected on room surfaces becomes elliptical and "cannot then be easily made to look circular again" [PAGE 259]. A counter-trap researcher who expects constancy will misread elliptical after-images as failures of attention rather than as expected outcomes of projection geometry. The recursive vulnerability, therefore, is a clock mismatch: the developmental habit expects correction over years, while the momentary apperceptive combination can be hijacked by an oblique plane in a single fixation.
