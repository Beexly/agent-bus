**Adjudication target:** whether the paper's pipeline (multi-camera acquisition → SURF registration → seam fusion → panorama → foreground-mass "hot spot" → smooth tracking → rebroadcast) is internally coherent, physically realizable at the stated 640×480 real-time claim, and whether its central saliency operator actually tracks what the paper implies it tracks. <THINK_TRACE>
**Multi-hop derivation chain and gray-zone audit.**

Hop 1 — *From claim to operator.* The paper's headline claim is at [PAGE 1]: "The main purpose of this system is to meet such needs. It only needs to set up a few cameras on the scene to realize the automatic live broadcast of the tennis match." A "few cameras" plus automatic broadcast implies (a) geometric calibration across cameras, (b) a saliency operator that decides where to look, and (c) a control loop whose latency is below the perceptual edit threshold. The paper supplies (b) explicitly and (a)/(c) only partially — this asymmetry is the primary gray zone.

Hop 2 — *Reconstructing the geometry from the overlap constraint.* [PAGE 2] states the capture condition verbatim: "the overlapping range of the images captured by two adjacent machines is more than 1/5 of the width of the captured images." [PAGE 5] then reports "the overlapping area of two adjacent frames the percentage is 39.8%." Two readings are live: overlap-as-fraction-of-frame-width (ρ = 0.398 of 640 px = 254.7 px) yields panorama width W_p = N·w − (N−1)·ρw = 5(640) − 4(254.7) ≈ 2181 px; overlap-as-fraction-of-panorama-width yields W_p = 5(640)/(1 + 4(0.398)) ≈ 1235 px. The paper never disambiguates. This is a *measurable* ambiguity, not a rhetorical quibble — it changes the hot-spot window's fractional coverage from 29.3 % to 51.8 % of the panorama, which changes the entire editing cadence.

Hop 3 — *Testing the hot-spot operator against the object it purports to serve.* [PAGE 4] defines it: "the hotspot area is a sub-area in the video in which the sports foreground objects are the most; this sub-area The height of the video is equal to the stitched video, and the width is determined according to the aspect ratio of the specified broadcast standard (the specified broadcast standard in this article is 640*480, that is, the aspect ratio is 4:3)." So the operator is an **integral of foreground area over a 640-px-wide column window**, scanned along the panorama. Now compute the ball's contribution. With a 640-px sensor and 30° horizontal field, f ≈ 320/tan 15° ≈ 1194 px. A 0.067 m ball at 20 m subtends ≈ 4.0 px in diameter, i.e. ≈ 12 px² of foreground. A 1.8 m player at the same range subtends ≈ 107 px tall by ~40 px wide, i.e. ≈ 4×10³ px². Ratio ≈ 3×10⁻³. The window integral is therefore ~99.7 % player-mass. **The "hot spot" is a player-mass detector, not a ball-tracker.** The paper's inferential leap at [PAGE 4] — "The hotspots of interest in tennis matches are often in the area with the most sports targets" — is an *assumption stated as an observation*, and it is the load-bearing assumption of the whole broadcast layer.

Hop 4 — *Latency audit.* [PAGE 5] gives a hard budget: "It takes 31ms to stitch each frame of the 5 video streams to obtain a panoramic video frame. The average time to detect the location of the hot spot area of each frame of the panoramic video after splicing is 11ms." Serial sum = 42 ms → f_max = 23.8 Hz. A 30 fps broadcast has T_f = 33.3 ms. Therefore the paper's [PAGE 5] claim "The system satisfies the real-time broadcast at a resolution of 640*480" is only true under a **pipelined** architecture with N ≥ ⌈42/33.3⌉ = 2 stages in flight, which necessarily converts throughput-satisfaction into *added glass-to-glass latency* of at least one frame period. The paper never distinguishes throughput from latency — a classic real-time-systems gray zone.

Hop 5 — *Signed-quantity audit of the Fourier branch.* Equations (3)–(6) as printed are internally sign-inconsistent. Eq. (3) sets f₂ = f₁(x−x₀, y−y₀); the shift theorem then demands F₂ = e^{−j2π(εx₀+ηy₀)}F₁, which is what Eq. (4) prints — consistent. But Eq. (5) prints the cross-power spectrum as e^{−j2π(εx₀+ηy₀)}, whereas F₁F₂*/|F₁F₂*| = e^{+j2π(εx₀+ηy₀)}. Eq. (6) then inherits the wrong sign, so the printed pipeline returns (−x₀, −y₀). A reviewer can catch this with a single synthetic translation — it is deterministic, not statistical. This is the highest-confidence falsifier in the document.

Hop 6 — *Determinism trap in the NCC branch.* [PAGE 4] asserts "When ( )The value is1 NC i j ， is, the matching position is determined" yet two sentences later concedes "the pixel gray value between the reference image and the reference image cannot be absolutely the same" and pivots to "the position with the largest NC value is the best Match location." The paper thus specifies an unattainable identity criterion and then silently substitutes an argmax — two different algorithms with different failure topologies. Under texture-poor regions (uniform green court, painted lines, shadow bands) the argmax is flat and the substitution becomes unstable.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**A. Normalized cross-correlation (template matching), [PAGE 3]–[PAGE 4].**

$$\mathrm{NC}(i,j)=\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T(m,n)\,S(i+m,\,j+n)}{\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}T(m,n)^{2}}\;\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}S(i+m,j+n)^{2}}} \tag{1}$$

$$\mathrm{NC}(i,j)=\frac{\sum_{m,n}\big[T(m,n)-\bar T\big]\big[S(i+m,j+n)-\bar S\big]}{\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\big[T-\bar T\big]^{2}}\;\sqrt{\sum_{m,n}\big[S-\bar S\big]^{2}}} \tag{2}$$

*Units:* dimensionless. Eq. (1) ∈ [0,1] for non-negative intensities; Eq. (2) (zero-mean form) ∈ [−1,1]. `[PAGE 4]` defines the means verbatim: $\bar T=\frac{1}{MN}\sum_{m,n}T(m,n)$, $\bar S=\frac{1}{MN}\sum_{m,n}S(m,n)$.
*Assumptions:* brightness constancy; small rotation (≤ a few degrees) between the template and the search region; overlap ≥ template support so the denominator is non-degenerate.
*Breakdown regimes:* (i) $\sum_{m,n}(T-\bar T)^2 \to 0$ — a textureless template (bare court surface) makes Eq. (2) a 0/0 singularity; the denominator collapses and NC becomes pure sensor-noise correlation. (ii) Specular highlights on painted lines saturate the sensor ($S = S_{\max}$) and destroy the brightness-constancy premise. (iii) Scale change beyond the template's tolerance produces a broad, flat correlation plateau — argmax becomes non-unique.
*Paper's own hedge, [PAGE 4]:* "In practical applications, due to different shooting time or equipment, the pixel gray value between the reference image and the reference image cannot be absolutely the same."

**B. SURF registration, [PAGE 1].**

$$\det(\mathcal H_{\text{approx}}) = D_{xx}D_{yy} - \big(0.9\,D_{xy}\big)^{2}$$

*Units:* $D_{xx},D_{yy},D_{xy}$ are box-filter responses in intensity·pixel⁻², so the determinant is in intensity²·pixel⁻⁴; the 0.9 is the standard Hessian-correction constant, dimensionless.
*Assumption:* the dominant inter-frame transformation is a homography — valid **only** for a pure-rotation (fixed optical center) camera pair.
*Breakdown:* a translating camera has parallax, and no single homography exists. Quantitatively, $\delta = f b / Z$ px (with $f$ in px, baseline $b$ in m, depth $Z$ in m). At $f = 1194$ px, $b = 1$ m, $Z = 20$ m, $\delta \approx 60$ px — a seam ghost two orders of magnitude above the sub-pixel alignment the fusion step needs. **Singularity: $Z \to 0 \Rightarrow \delta \to \infty$** (a player crossing the seam at 3 m produces $\delta \approx 400$ px).

**C. Phase correlation, [PAGE 3]–[PAGE 4].**

$$f_2(x,y)=f_1(x-x_0,\;y-y_0) \tag{3}$$
$$F_2(\varepsilon,\eta)=e^{-j2\pi(\varepsilon x_0+\eta y_0)}\,F_1(\varepsilon,\eta) \tag{4}$$
$$\frac{F_1(\varepsilon,\eta)F_2^{*}(\varepsilon,\eta)}{\big|F_1 F_2^{*}\big|}=e^{-j2\pi(\varepsilon x_0+\eta y_0)} \tag{5}$$
$$\mathcal F^{-1}\!\left[e^{-j2\pi(\varepsilon x_0+\eta y_0)}\right]=\delta(x-x_0,\;y-y_0) \tag{6}$$

*Units:* $\varepsilon,\eta$ in cycles·pixel⁻¹ (spatial frequency); the cross-power spectrum is dimensionless (modulus normalized to unity); Eq. (6) is a Dirac comb in inverse-pixel (px⁻¹) whose peak coordinate is in **pixels**.
*As printed:* Eq. (5) carries the opposite sign to $\arg\{F_1F_2^{*}\}$, so the pipeline localizes the peak at $(-x_0,-y_0)$. Corrected form: $\mathcal F^{-1}\!\left[F_2F_1^{*}/|F_2F_1^{*}|\right]=\delta(x-x_0,y-y_0)$.
*Breakdown:* peak-to-sidelobe ratio collapses when the overlap is small — the paper concedes at [PAGE 4] "This method requires him to want to see a large overlap range… its application scenarios and registration accuracy are both limited." Also degenerate when the overlap region is periodic (repeated court markings, sponsor boards), producing multiple equal-height peaks.

**D. Foreground extraction and hot-spot operator, [PAGE 4].**

$$F_t(\mathbf x)=\mathbb 1\!\left[\big|I_t(\mathbf x)-B_t(\mathbf x)\big|>\tau\right],\qquad B_{t+1}=(1-\beta)B_t+\beta I_t$$

*Units:* $I,B$ in digital counts (DN) at 8-bit depth ⇒ $\tau$ in DN; $\beta$ dimensionless; background time constant $\tau_{\text{bg}}=-T_s/\ln(1-\beta)$ in seconds.
*Breakdown:* cloud-induced irradiance steps of 30–80 % within ~1 s shift $I_t$ globally by $\gg \tau$ at every pixel — the entire frame registers as foreground and the hot-spot window position becomes arbitrary.

$$x^{\star}_t=\arg\max_{x}\;\sum_{x'=x}^{x+W_h-1}\sum_{y}F_t(x',y),\qquad W_h=480\times\tfrac{4}{3}=640\ \text{px}$$

*Units:* window width in px; the objective is an area in px².
*Singularity:* when player mass is bimodal (server and receiver at opposite baselines, both inside the scan), the argmax is nearly flat between the two lobes; a single-pixel noise perturbation flips the window by hundreds of px.

**E. Tracking smoothing and latency budget.**

$$\hat x_t=\alpha\,\hat x_{t-1}+(1-\alpha)\,x^{\star}_t,\qquad f_c=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{\text{tr}}},\qquad \tau_{\text{tr}}=-\frac{T_s}{\ln\alpha}$$

*Units:* $f_c$ in Hz; $\tau_{\text{tr}}$ in s. *Breakdown:* $\alpha\to 1\Rightarrow\tau_{\text{tr}}\to\infty$ (window lags the rally and never arrives); $\alpha\to 0\Rightarrow f_c\to$ Nyquist (jitter passes unfiltered, producing visible camera snap).

$$T_{\text{tot}}=T_{\text{stitch}}+T_{\text{hot}}=31+11=42\ \text{ms}\;\Rightarrow\;f_{\max}=T_{\text{tot}}^{-1}=23.8\ \text{Hz}$$

*Verdict:* $f_{\max} < 30$ Hz. Real-time at [PAGE 5] is achievable only with $N\ge\lceil 42/33.3\rceil=2$ pipeline stages.

</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The document's technique stack predates it by decades, and its citation list does not trace that lineage. Normalized cross-correlation descends from 1970s stereo/motion-estimation work and the classic template-matching literature; zero-mean NCC (the actual Eq. 2) is the standard remedy for the DC-bias failure the paper never names. Phase correlation traces to Kuglin–Hines (1975) and the Fourier-shift registration tradition — Eq. (4)–(6) are a textbook restatement, which is precisely why the sign slip in Eq. (5) is diagnosable rather than debatable. SURF (Bay, Ess, Tuytelaars, Van Gool, 2006/ECCV–2008/CVIU) is cited by name at [PAGE 1] but appears in **none** of the eight references at [PAGE 5]; the entire reference list is Chinese-language live-video engineering from 2019–2021 plus one nursing-management paper (ref. [7], colorectal surgery live conferencing) that is topically orthogonal. Retrocaually, the paper's *real* ancestry — panoramic mosaicking (Brown & Lowe 2003; Szeliski's image-alignment survey), background subtraction (Stauffer–Grimson mixture-of-Gaussians), and virtual-studio compositing (the RT-SET Larus / ORAD BLUESET systems it name-drops at [PAGE 1]) — is fully omitted. That omission is diagnostic: it explains why the pipeline is assembled as a chain of off-the-shelf operators with no shared error model connecting registration residuals to hot-spot stability.

**Multi-week traps.** (1) The 20 %-vs-39.8 % overlap disambiguation will silently determine panorama width, and every downstream metric (window coverage, stitch cost per pixel, seam count) inherits the choice. (2) SURF's original term was patent-encumbered, so "just use SURF" is a deployment trap for anyone productizing the method commercially. (3) The paper's edge-detection conclusion at [PAGE 5] ("The new edge detection method developed in this research achieves the purpose of better retaining the details of the edge of the image, good suppression of noise") is asserted without a single quantitative edge-fidelity metric — no Pratt FOM, no ROC, no ground-truth contour set. An evaluator who inherits that sentence as a result will propagate an unsupported claim through an entire literature review.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Three nested models are in play, and each is under-specified.

*Designer → viewer.* The designer's model of viewer attention is operationalized as foreground mass. This is a **falsifiable theory of mind**, not a stylistic choice: it predicts that what a spectator wants to see is where the largest number of moving human pixels are. It is correct for rally coverage and wrong for the serve (where the informative content is a 4-px ball and a 1.8 m server), wrong for the challenge/replay moment, and wrong for the reaction shot.

*System → scene.* The system's implicit prior is "salient = moving = players." Because $x^\star_t$ is an integral over a 640-px window, the system literally cannot represent the ball as a target. When the paper says at [PAGE 4] "the hotspot area is a sub-area in the video in which the sports foreground objects are the most," the system's ontology is already fixed.

*Operator → system (crisis habit regression).* Under normal load, a director trusts the auto-window. Under crisis — break point, tie-break, a 30-shot rally — the director's habit regresses to manual cut, because the EMA tracker's group delay ($\tau_{\text{tr}} = -T_s/\ln\alpha$) becomes *visible* as the window trails the players. The regression is not irrational; it is the correct response to a latency the designer never published.

*Counter-trap.* The attractive fallacy is "NC = 1 ⇒ position determined" [PAGE 4]. The paper itself refutes this two sentences later. An operator who internalizes the deterministic reading will over-trust the matcher and stop monitoring seam quality; an operator who internalizes the argmax reading will under-trust it and intervene constantly. The document ships both readings without resolving them.

*Clock.* The hard constraint is $T_f = 33.3$ ms against $T_{\text{tot}} = 42$ ms. Every ToM failure above is amplified by that 8.7 ms overrun, because the operator's proprioceptive sense of "live" is calibrated to zero added latency, and the pipeline adds at least one frame period.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical veto — exposure vs. motion blur.** For blur ≤ 2 px: $t_{\exp}\le b_{\text{px}}Z/(f v)$. With $b_{\text{px}}=2$, $Z=20$ m, $f=1194$ px, $v=55.6$ m·s⁻¹ (200 km·h⁻¹ serve): $t_{\exp}\le 6.0\times10^{-4}$ s ≈ 1/1660 s. **Survivable outdoors** (photon flux at 10⁵ lux through f/2.8 delivers ~10⁴ e⁻/pixel), **marginal indoors** (~10³ lux ⇒ ~10² e⁻, shot-noise SNR ≈ 10, and the SURF Hessian response $\det(\mathcal H)$ falls below the detector threshold). Veto is conditional, not absolute — but the paper never states an operating illuminance.

**Biomechanical veto — Magnus deflection vs. the saliency window.** $F_M=\tfrac12\rho A C_L v^{2}$; with $\rho=1.2$ kg·m⁻³, $A=\pi(0.0335)^2=3.53\times10^{-3}$ m², $v=55.6$ m·s⁻¹, $C_L\in[0.1,0.3]$: $F_M\in[0.65,1.96]$ N, $a\in[11.5,34]$ m·s⁻². Over a 0.43 s service flight the lateral deflection is $\tfrac12 a t^{2}\in[1.1,3.1]$ m — i.e. **60–190 px at 20 m**. The ball exits the frame's central third during a single shot. Because the hot-spot operator is blind to the ball (Hop 3), nothing in the pipeline corrects for it.

**Weather veto — global illumination step.** Frame differencing with threshold $\tau$ fails when cloud-driven irradiance change $\Delta E/E$ exceeds $\tau$ scaled to the scene mean. Empirical cloud transients of 30–80 % in ~1 s put every pixel above threshold for several frames, producing a full-frame "foreground" that makes $x^\star_t$ a noise argmax. Rain on the front element additionally destroys SURF keypoints in the affected region, and the seam-fusion step propagates that degradation across a ~255-px band.

**Weather veto — low-sun shadow bands.** Painted-line shadows move slowly ($\sim10^{-3}$ m·s⁻¹), so they do not trip the frame-difference threshold often, but they do create long, low-texture regions where Eq. (2)'s denominator $\sqrt{\sum(T-\bar T)^2}\to 0$. The **NCC 0/0 singularity is a weather-dependent failure**, not a coding bug.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto 1 — Sign-convention verifier (deterministic, highest confidence).**
*Scenario:* take any real frame $I$ from the corpus at [PAGE 5]'s 640×480 capture spec, apply a known integer translation $(x_0,y_0)=(+37,-12)$ px, and run Eq. (5)–(6) exactly as printed.
*Prediction:* the recovered peak is at $(-37,+12)$, not $(+37,-12)$. Correcting Eq. (5) to $F_2F_1^{*}/|F_2F_1^{*}|$ restores $(+37,-12)$.
*Falsified if:* the printed pipeline returns the true shift. This is a one-shot, zero-variance test.

**Veto 2 — Ball-subtended-area / degenerate-saliency verifier.**
*Scenario:* construct a synthetic sequence in which all player silhouettes are masked to background and only the ball (≈ 12 px² at 20 m) and court texture remain; run the [PAGE 4] hot-spot operator.
*Prediction:* the variance of $x^\star_t$ across frames increases by ≥ 10× relative to the unmasked sequence, and the operator's output becomes statistically indistinguishable from a random draw over the legal window range. This confirms the operator's $O(10^{-3})$ ball-mass sensitivity.
*Falsified if:* $x^\star_t$ remains stable and tracks the ball, which would prove an unstated ball-detection term exists.

**Veto 3 — Latency-budget / throughput-vs-latency verifier.**
*Scenario:* instrument the exact [PAGE 5] configuration — 5 streams at 640×480, 39.8 % overlap, 31 ms stitch, 11 ms hot-spot — and attempt sustained 30 fps output.
*Prediction:* either (a) sustained output ≤ 23.8 fps, or (b) output reaches 30 fps only with ≥ 2 pipeline stages in flight, in which case measured glass-to-glass latency ≥ 2 frame periods (≥ 66.7 ms), not the 42 ms the serial numbers imply.
*Falsified if:* 30 fps and ≤ 42 ms glass-to-glass are simultaneously demonstrated on the stated hardware — which would require $T_{\text{stitch}} + T_{\text{hot}} < 33.3$ ms and therefore contradict the paper's own reported timings.

**Veto 4 (auxiliary) — Overlap-ambiguity resolver.**
*Scenario:* read the panorama width $W_p$ from the system's output metadata.
*Prediction:* $W_p \approx 2181$ px if 39.8 % is of frame width; $W_p \approx 1235$ px if of panorama width. The two differ by 1.77× and are trivially separable.
*Falsified if:* $W_p$ lies outside both bands, implying a third unstated convention.

---

**Bottom line.** The pipeline is a competent assembly of standard operators, and its measured numbers are plausible. But three load-bearing claims are not supported by the document: the "real-time" claim (23.8 Hz serial against a 30 Hz clock), the saliency claim (a 640-px foreground-mass integral is a player detector, not a ball tracker), and the registration claim (Eq. 5's sign convention is inverted, and the homography assumption fails under the translational parallax the paper's own multi-camera geometry creates).
