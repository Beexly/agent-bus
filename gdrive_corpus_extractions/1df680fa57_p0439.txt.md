All units are information-theoretic (bits), temporal (seconds), or dimensionless (drive, association strength, valuation). No toy ratios are used.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from Structuralism and Gestalt Psychology, which “seemed to assume but one final type of cause underlying behavior, viz, S,” through Spearmanism, which “added but two more, viz, H and T,” to Purposive Behaviorism, which adds P for a total of S, H, T, and P. This is a clear intellectual trajectory: from single-cause stimulus psychology to two-factor drive/habit theory, and finally to a four-cause teleological architecture in which purpose is an independent causal variable rather than an epiphenomenon of S, H, or T.

Tolman’s formulation anticipates later developments in cognitive psychology and reinforcement learning. The sign-gestalt expectation \(E_t\) is a precursor to the state-value or outcome-expectation functions in modern reinforcement learning. The operator B—actual or adjustmental back-and-forth—is a behavioral ancestor of epistemic exploration, information-directed sampling, and active-inference action selection. The capacities \(d, m, me, r, ci, cr\) prefigure modular accounts of cognition in which sensory resolution, motor bandwidth, planning, memory, ideation, and creativity are separable rather than collapsed into a single \(g\) factor.

Multi-week traps in longitudinal application of this model: (a) B may be miscoded as random locomotor noise rather than structured epistemic behavior, producing false-null results in experiments that treat exploration as error; (b) failure to distinguish \(S_1\) from reaffered \(S_2\) leads experimenters to attribute learning to the stimulus alone when the organism has actively improved its own stimulus; (c) \(d\) and \(m\) deficits may be conflated with low \(g\) or low motivation, causing specific sensory-motor constraints to be misattributed to general motivational failure across weeks; (d) demand thresholds \(\theta_i\) drift with satiety and circadian state, so a stable H can appear unstable across sessions if \(\theta_i\) is not measured concurrently. Each trap obscures the four-cause structure and produces spurious inferences about which cause is “really” driving behavior.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when acute need \(H_t\) compresses the decision window, causing the organism to regress to lower-level S–R bonds characteristic of Structuralist/Gestalt modes, bypassing the purposive P loop. In this regime the expectation-update cycle is short-circuited: \(U_t\) remains high, \(B\) is suppressed or stereotyped, and \(R\) is released by direct stimulus-demand coupling rather than by \(g_i(O_2) \geq \theta_i\).

The counter-trap is observational: an external analyst may mistake this stress-induced regression for a stable trait or for evidence that the organism lacks purposive cognition altogether. The regression is state-dependent, not dispositional; once \(H_t\) falls and the decision window widens, the \(S_1 \to B \to S_2 \to E_{t+1} \to R\) loop can resume.

The clock governing regression versus recovery is retentivity \(r\) with time constant \(\tau_r\) relative to crisis duration \(\Delta t_{\text{crisis}}\). If \(\Delta t_{\text{crisis}} < \tau_r\), pre-crisis habits \(T\) dominate and the system reverts to the pre-crisis \(P\) after the crisis. If \(\Delta t_{\text{crisis}} > \tau_r\), the crisis interval is long enough for new \(S_2\) reafference to update \(E\) and \(T\), so the organism exits the crisis with a modified purposive architecture.

Recursive theory of mind enters when an observer models the agent’s \(P\), while the agent models the observer’s demands. This nesting is bounded by \(ci\) and \(cr\). If \(ci\) is insufficient, the observer’s model of the agent collapses to a simple S–R stimulus, committing the intentional-stance fallacy: the analyst attributes behavior to a stimulus \(S\) that is actually mediated by the agent’s own \(H, T,\) and \(P\). The model depth is therefore limited by the ideation capacity of both observer and agent.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any behavioral prediction derived from this page must pass a four-layer veto proof. Failure at any layer voids the prediction.

Layer 1 — Physical/energetic. B requires kinetic execution of back-and-forth locomotion or adjustmental movement. If available metabolic power \(P_{\text{metabolic}}\) is below the cost of transport \(C_{\text{transport}}\) for the required B, then \(B_t = 0\) and \(\Phi\) cannot generate \(S_2 \neq S_1\). Veto condition: \(P_{\text{metabolic}} < C_{\text{transport}} \Rightarrow\) prediction of expectation update is void.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical/motor. Manipulanda capacity \(m\) bounds the degrees of freedom per second the organism can deploy. If \(m < m_{\min}\), where \(m_{\min}\) is the minimum bandwidth required to alter stimulus geometry, then the reafference map yields no information gain: \(I(S_2; S_1) \approx 0\) bits. Veto condition: \(m < m_{\min} \Rightarrow\) S₂ improvement is physically impossible.

Layer 3 — Sensory/weather. Environmental degradation (precipitation, occlusion, turbulence) raises the effective sensory threshold and reduces discriminanda capacity \(d\). If \(d < d_{\min}\), sign-gestalt formation fails and \(U_t\) remains near maximum entropy. Veto condition: \(d < d_{\min} \Rightarrow\) no reliable E can be formed; B cannot be guided toward S₂.

Layer 4 — Demand gating. Even if \(S_2\) is achieved and \(E\) updated, if \(\max_i g_i(O_2) < \theta_i\) for all \(i\), no demand is released and \(R\) is withheld. Veto condition: \(\forall i, \, g_i(O_2) < \theta_i \Rightarrow\) behavioral output is suppressed.

A prediction is admissible only if it survives all four vetoes. This cross-layer adjudication prevents attributing behavior to purpose \(P\) when the physical layer cannot execute B, or to stimulus \(S\) when the demand layer has not been crossed.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Selective suppression of means-end capacity \(me\). Formal prediction: \(B\) is emitted because \(m\) is intact, but \(\Phi(S_1, B; me \approx 0)\) yields \(S_2 \approx S_1\), so \(I(S_2; S_1) \approx 0\) bits. \(E\) does not update, no \(O_2\) acceptance occurs, and \(R\) is withheld despite \(H > 0\) and \(T > 0\). Verifiable falsification: if \(R\) is observed while \(me\) is suppressed and no \(S_2\) change is detected, the means-end mediation equation is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Discriminanda-capacity collapse (\(d \to 0\) via sensory degradation). Formal prediction: \(U_t\) remains at \(\log_2 |\mathcal{O}|\) bits; \(B\) becomes perseverative back-and-forth without directional improvement; \(S_2\) never exceeds \(S_1\) in information content; \(R\) never releases. Verifiable falsification: if \(S_2\) improves and \(R\) occurs while \(d\) is below the empirically measured \(d_{\min}\), the discriminanda gate is not necessary for the Tolmanian loop.

Scenario 3 — Demand-threshold elevation (\(\theta_i \to \infty\) via satiation or demand devaluation). Formal prediction: \(B\) and \(S_2\) proceed normally; \(O_2\) is generated; but \(\max_i [g_i(O_2) - \theta_i] < 0\), so \(R\) is withheld. Verifiable falsification: if \(R\) occurs while all \(g_i(O_2) < \theta_i\), the demand-release gating equation is falsified and an alternative release mechanism must be posited.

Each scenario is verifiable by joint measurement of \(B\) (locomotor telemetry), \(S_2\) (sensory reafference), an expectation proxy (e.g., choice bias or orienting response), and \(R\) (final response emission). Quantitative thresholds: \(I(S_2;S_1) > 0\) bits required for expectation update; \(\max_i [g_i(O_2) - \theta_i] \geq 0\) required for \(R\).
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
