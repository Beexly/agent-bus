Any economic prediction derived from the static method on [PAGE 16] is subject to veto by three exogenous layers. The physical layer imposes conservation and thermodynamic bounds; for any conversion process, useful work $W_{\text{out}}$ cannot exceed exergy input scaled by efficiency, $\eta\le\eta_{\text{Carnot}}=1-T_c/T_h$, with temperatures in kelvin. If the static model assumes frictionless substitution of energy inputs, but the physical layer binds at $\eta_{\text{Carnot}}$, the prediction is vetoed because no reallocation of $\theta$ can overcome the ceiling.

The biomechanical layer imposes human reaction and endurance constraints. Sustained cognitive or physical output is bounded by metabolic power (order $100\,\text{W}$ baseline) and recovery time $\tau_{\text{rec}}\sim 24\text{--}48\,\text{h}$. A static labor-supply function that treats hours $L$ as a smooth, continuously adjustable choice variable is vetoed when a shock requires $L>60\,\text{h/week}$ for more than two weeks: cumulative fatigue imposes a hard constraint $L(t)\le L_{\max}-\kappa\int_0^t L(s)\,ds$ that the isolated-force analysis cannot see because it suspends all other physiological forces.

The weather layer imposes non-stationarity. Let $W_t$ be a weather-driven shock process with historical support $\mathcal{S}_{\text{hist}}$. If a realized event satisfies $\omega^*\notin\mathcal{S}_{\text{hist}}$, then under the historical probability measure $P(\omega^*)=0$ and Shannon surprise $-\log_2 P(\omega^*)$ is undefined; the static method's sigma-algebra $\mathcal{F}$ is incomplete. The adjudication rule is: a static prediction $\hat{x}$ is admissible only if $\forall L\in\{\text{physical},\text{biomechanical},\text{weather}\}$, the layer constraint $c_L(\hat{x})\le0$ holds. A single violated layer vetoes the entire forecast, regardless of internal logical consistency.
