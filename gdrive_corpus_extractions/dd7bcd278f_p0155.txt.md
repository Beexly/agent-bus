The deictic phrase “results up to last spring” anchors the Merrifield data to spring 1888 relative to the 1889 volume, but it introduces a one-season environmental gap: a spring cohort’s developmental temperature, photoperiod, and food availability differ from an autumn cohort, creating cohort effects that can mimic or mask inherited change. The “small garden” further exposes the breeding establishment to ambient weather, so multi-week selection responses are confounded with seasonal reaction norms unless generations are reared under common-garden or climate-controlled conditions. Any multi-week extrapolation from this page must therefore synchronize collaborators to the same generational clock and season, or the hereditary signal is inseparable from weather-driven developmental plasticity.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes the experimenter’s cognitive reversion under load. Galton explicitly bounds the “convenient size” at 50–100 because schemes above 100 become “unmanageably large” [PAGE 155]. Under that clerical and custodial crisis, a human experimenter regresses to pre-biometrical habits: selecting by eye, culling anecdotal outliers, substituting convenience sampling for random sampling, and replacing formal schemes with narrative judgment. This is a habit regression triggered by informational overload—the very statistical discipline Galton advocates collapses when N exceeds working memory and manual record-keeping capacity.

The counter-trap is the belief that [50, 100] is a universally sufficient statistical comfort zone. It is not. For a rare recessive allele with frequency \(q \ll 1\), the expected number of homozygotes scales as \(N q^2\); detecting such an allele reliably may require \(N \gg 100\), violating Galton’s upper bound. Conversely, for a highly polygenic trait with small, additive effects, N = 50–100 may suffice to estimate the population mean but not to map individual quantitative trait loci. Treating Galton’s range as architecture-independent is itself a trap: the sufficient N depends on the genetic architecture (allele frequencies, effect sizes, dominance) and the estimand (mean, variance, regression coefficient, or variance-component decomposition).

The clock is the organismal generation-time governor on epistemic velocity. Moth breeds cycle on scales of weeks to months; human stature cycles on scales of decades. Galton’s call for collaborators must be synchronized to seasonal breeding clocks—a recruit beginning in autumn misses the spring cohort referenced as “last spring” [PAGE 155], introducing a one-generation phase delay and potentially a different environmental covariance structure. The maximum epistemic velocity is \(v_{\text{epistemic}} = G_{\text{observed}} / T_{\text{calendar}}\), bounded below by \(T_g\). Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock therefore interact: under time pressure (fast clock), the experimenter is tempted to expand N beyond 100 to compensate for limited generations, which triggers unmanageability and habit regression; under architectural complexity (rare alleles), the [50,100] band is too narrow, but expanding N re-triggers the manageability crisis. The only stable operating point is architecture-aware N within [50,100] (or an explicit justification for leaving it), climate-controlled generations, and collaborator onboarding synchronized to the seasonal breeding clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical layer: Mounting specimens “after their death” and remeasuring them “at leisure” [PAGE 155] records a preserved exoskeleton, not the live breeding phenotype. Chitin desiccation causes anisotropic shrinkage; relative humidity, time since death, and sclerotization jointly determine \(\Delta L_{\text{desicc}}\) [mm]. If humidity is not logged and held constant between mounting and remeasurement, the measurement layer injects systematic bias that is indistinguishable from inherited change across generations. Veto: any cross-generation morphological comparison using mounted specimens is vetoed unless pre- and post-desiccation dimensions are standardized and humidity is controlled.

Biomechanical/allometric layer: Human stature (endoskeletal, vertical, gravity-loaded) and moth morphology (exoskeletal, horizontal flight apparatus) do not share scaling exponents. Galton’s invitation to extend experiments to “altogether different species of moths” [PAGE 155] invokes cross-species transport, but trait means and regression coefficients scale allometrically (\(y = a x^b\), with species-specific \(b\)). Without allometric correction, heritability \(h^2 = V_A/V_P\) and the breeder’s equation response \(R = h^2 S\) are not transportable across taxa. Veto: cross-species hereditary generalization is vetoed absent species-specific allometric parameters and generation-time normalization.

Weather/environmental layer: The breeding establishment operates in a “small room, supplemented by a small garden” [PAGE 155], i.e., semi-natural conditions. Ambient temperature, photoperiod, and humidity induce phenotypic plasticity (reaction norms) in development time, size, and fecundity. If parental and offspring generations experience different weather, \(\operatorname{Cov}(G,E) \neq 0\), violating the breeder’s equation assumption of environmental stability and causing selection differentials to reflect weather rather than genotype. Veto: inheritance inference is vetoed unless generations are reared in common-garden or climate-controlled blocks, or weather is replicated and modeled as a random effect across seasons.

Adjudication rule: A claim of ancestral heredity or regression drawn from [PAGE 155] is admitted only if (a) \(N \in [50,100]\) with independent schemes; (b) specimens are measured live or under identical, humidity-logged preservation protocols; (c) comparisons are within species or allometrically corrected across species; and (d) temperature/photoperiod is constant across generations or replicated seasonally. Failure of any layer triggers a hard veto on the quantitative inheritance claim, regardless of the stature chapter’s broader theoretical framing.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (sample-size violation). Verifiable setup: an experimenter measures N = 35 moth parent–offspring pairs, claiming a Galtonian regression coefficient \(\beta = 0.30\) on [PAGE 155] authority. Verification: compute statistical power to detect \(\beta \neq 0\) at \(\alpha = 0.05\) given \(\sigma_y/\sigma_m\) and residual variance. Prediction: with N = 35 < 50, power falls below 0.5 for moderate effect sizes, and the 95% confidence interval for \(\beta\) includes zero; the coefficient is statistically indistinguishable from 0. RLVR veto: any claim of non-zero ancestral contribution from N = 35 is vetoed under the [50,100] design constraint.

Scenario 2 (post-mortem desiccation bias). Verifiable setup: mounted moth specimens are remeasured at leisure without humidity control, exactly as “mounted … after their death … remeasured at leisure” [PAGE 155] describes. Verification: measure a reference cohort live, then re-measure after standardized mounting at varying \(RH\) and post-mortem intervals \(t\); regress \(\Delta L\) on \(RH\) and \(t\). Prediction: mean length reduction exceeds 2% and correlates with time since death (\(p < 0.01\)); the bias magnitude is comparable to or larger than expected intergenerational response \(R\). RLVR veto: cross-generation morphological comparison using uncontrolled mounted specimens is vetoed because the desiccation layer dominates the hereditary signal.

Scenario 3 (cross-species allometric extrapolation). Verifiable setup: results from Merrifield’s breed are generalized to “altogether different species of moths” [PAGE 155] without correcting for wing-length-to-body-mass scaling. Verification: estimate the allometric exponent \(b\) in \(\log y = \log a + b \log x\) for two species; compare \(b_1\) vs. \(b_2\). Prediction: \(|b_1 - b_2| > 0.1\), so the same selection differential \(S\) produces different responses \(R = h^2 S\) due to scale-dependent variance structure; \(h^2\) is not transportable. RLVR veto: cross-species hereditary generalization from this page is vetoed absent species-specific allometric parameters and generation-time normalization.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
