The paper under analysis — Zhu & Zhu (2021), *Traitement du Signal* 38(2):529–538 — is a composite DL pipeline for dance action recognition and pose estimation. It has three interlocking operator families that must be traced as a single causal chain, not as disconnected sections. The first is the geometric pre-processing chain on [PAGE 2], a rotation–crop–scale–flip sequence whose sole purpose is to leave the underlying neural regression invariant to the four nuisance transforms while preserving label geometry. The second is a DeepPose-derived global + local convolution pair [PAGE 3–5], where the paper explicitly enumerates four defects of the baseline model — *"The dance action recognition and pose estimation are treated as a regression problem, adding difficulty to the training of the neural network model; (2) Some local information of the original image gets lost in the three pooling operations of the model; (3) Global reasoning makes the mapping between images and dance actions highly nonlinear; (4) Global reasoning and local optimization adopt the same neural network"* — and resolves them by splitting the output head into gp-a2 and gp-b2 (independent softmax heads over axis-aligned joint coordinates) and re-injecting a local convolution branch fused with the global thermal diagram at Eq (17). The third is the temporal sequence segmentation network on [PAGE 5–7], which sub-samples each video into L equal-length segments, computes a consensus PC over segment-level class posteriors via averaging (Eq 19–20), and then re-frames the same 3DCNN as a three-stage proposal/classification/positioning stack with an IOU-anchored overlap loss (Eq 21–22).

Multi-hop derivation. The paper's central claim — *"the full-effect expression of global and local features"* [PAGE 2] — is only defended empirically (Table 1, Figure 8). Mechanically, the argument is: DeepPose regresses (a,b) jointly through a single 1024-neuron FC layer, which conflates x/y error scales; splitting into gp-a2/gp-b2 gives each axis its own softmax, so the cross-entropy gradient in Eq (13), ∂L/∂ω_β = g·(Γ{β=m} − P(c_m)), becomes axis-decomposed and therefore linearly separable across coordinates. The local branch then re-injects what pooling destroyed by producing GTD_LC and fusing via (a_i*,b_i*) = REmax(ξ·GTD + (1−ξ)·GTD_LC) [PAGE 5]. This is, in effect, a soft spatial prior on top of a hard ROI threshold — but the paper never specifies ξ, μ, δ, γ, or the sparse-sampling stride. **1. Geometric pre-processing operator (rotation / crop / scale / flip) — [PAGE 2, Eqs 1–8].**
The rotation operator, as intended by the authors, rotates the (a,b) frame clockwise about the image centroid:
$$a_i^{s} = \left(a_i - \tfrac{P_a}{2}\right)\cos\Psi - \left(b_i - \tfrac{P_b}{2}\right)\sin\Psi + \tfrac{P_{sa}}{2}, \quad b_i^{s} = \left(a_i - \tfrac{P_a}{2}\right)\sin\Psi + \left(b_i - \tfrac{P_b}{2}\right)\cos\Psi + \tfrac{P_{sb}}{2}, \quad \Psi\in[0,\pi/2]. \tag{1}$$
Units: a_i, b_i, P_a, P_b, P_{sa}, P_{sb} are pixel counts; Ψ is a dimensionless angle in radians per the bound Ψ ∈ [0, π/2]. The rotated image size follows the standard bounding-box law *P_{sa} = P_a cosΨ + P_b sinΨ, P_{sb} = P_a sinΨ + P_b cosΨ* (Eq 2; the OCR string "P_a tanΨ sinΨ − P_a cosΨ" cannot be algebraically sound for a closed bounding box and is a transcription artifact — note this explicitly, per invariant 2).

**2. Local response normalization — [PAGE 3, Eq 9].**
$$v_i^{a,b} = \frac{u_i^{a,b}}{\left(\delta + \gamma \sum_{j=\max(0,i-m_C/2)}^{\min(M_C-1,i+m_C/2)} \left(u_j^{a,b}\right)^{2}\right)^{\xi}} \tag{9}$$

$$\mathcal{L}_{OL} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m:\,IOU_m>0}\tfrac{1}{2}\,RC_m^{2}\left(1 - \frac{OS_m}{IOU_m}\right)^{2}\!\upsilon, \tag{22 (reconstructed — OCR-garbled in source)}$$
Units: 𝓛_SM is softmax CE (nats); 𝓛_OL is dimensionless; μ_B and υ are dimensionless scale factors; OS_m is a scalar overlap prediction ∈ [0,1]; IOU_m ∈ (0,1].

*Breakdown regime:* IOU_m → 0 (implying a segment with no overlapping annotation) makes the OS_m/IOU_m ratio undefined — the paper's binary threshold rule on [PAGE 7] (*"the samples with an intersect over union (IOU) greater than 0.7 and smaller than 0.3 were judged as positive samples and negative samples, respectively"*) leaves the open interval (0, 0.3] as negative class but does not remove 0 from the denominator. This is an exact singularity and a guaranteed NaN in any first-epoch batch containing a zero-overlap proposal.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The operator lineage is traceable to four well-documented ancestors. (i) **Toshev & Szegedy's DeepPose (2014)** supplies the regression formulation the paper explicitly criticizes and then replaces with two softmax heads — this retrogenealogy matters because the paper's "improvement" is essentially a two-axis decomposition of a single regression loss, which was the standard remedy in 2015–2017 hand-pose literature before heatmap regression (OpenPose, HRNet) displaced it. (ii) **AlexNet (Krizhevsky et al., 2012)** supplies the LRN operator in Eq (9), whose γ, δ, ξ hyperparameters are reverse-compatible with the 2012 defaults but which was almost universally dropped in favor of batch normalization after 2015 — the paper's retention of LRN is an archaic branch, and the failure mode of LRN on low-magnitude feature maps (dark backgrounds) is the known reason for its abandonment. (iii) **TSN, Temporal Segment Networks (Wang et al., 2016)** supplies the segment-consensus architecture in Eq (18)–(20), with the averaging aggregator f; the paper directly inherits TSN's equal-length segmentation and sparse-sampling framing. (iv) **The three-stage proposal/classification/positioning 3DCNN** on [PAGE 6] descends from R-CNN's proposal-then-refine template imposed on the temporal axis; the IOU thresholds 0.7/0.3 and the NMS step at the end of [PAGE 7] are directly transplanted.

**Multi-week trap 1 — the "full-effect" redundancy trap.** Table 1 (mAP vs IOU) shows Our model vs Two-flow CNN: 46.2 vs 45.8 at IOU=0.1, 46.5 vs 42.3 at 0.2, 37.4 vs 36.2 at 0.3, 30.6 vs 28.8 at 0.4, 20.5 vs 19.3 at 0.5. The absolute deltas are 0.4, 4.2, 1.2, 1.8, 1.2 points — all within 3.1 points. Over a multi-week deployment, drift in class prior (new choreography) will move these deltas by more than the paper's claimed margin; the "superiority" claim needs a paired bootstrap to survive. The paper provides no standard deviations.

**Multi-week trap 2 — the "ballet corpus" over-generalization trap.** The results on [PAGE 7] are reported *"on the ballet dance action image set"* — a narrow, high-motion-coherence, high-light-control corpus. Over weeks, if deployed on modern/hip-hop or on outdoor/flashlit dancers, the rotation operator's Ψ ∈ [0,π/2] bound and the LRN stability floor δ will be violated continuously, and the Table 2 mean error of 6.6 (unitless) will inflate past the "space-time body" 9.8 baseline it currently beats.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis-habit regression.** Under input-distribution shift (new dancer body plan, altered illumination, occluded limbs), the global model gp-a2/gp-b2 has a soft-maximal habit: its posterior collapses toward the class corresponding to the *training-corpus mean joint coordinate*. This is a well-known behavior of axis-decomposed softmax heads — the mode is the argmax of an interpolated logit surface, which sits at the spatial mean of positive training samples. Fig. 9 [PAGE 8] shows the loss function values *"declined with fluctuations, and tended to be stable at about the 24,000th iteration"* — the stability is not convergence to truth, it is convergence to the mean of a narrow corpus. Under crisis, the model will not flag low confidence; it will silently emit a plausible mid-corpus pose. Recursive self-model failure: the positioning network's confidence score OS_m is correlated with RC_m in Eq (22), so the confidence surface *inherits the same collapse*, producing high confidence in the wrong pose. No calibration layer is present.

**Counter-trap.** Adversarially, an attacker can exploit the Kronecker separability of Eq (15) — because GTD = NO(c_a)^⊤ ⊗ NO(c_b), an input perturbation that pushes c_a and c_b independently toward a *jointly invalid* coordinate (e.g., a_i and b_i both maximal) will produce a GTD whose argmax is a pose point outside the anatomical manifold. Because global and local are fused with a *fixed, unspecified* ξ in Eq (17), the local branch cannot veto the global branch — it averages into it. A kinematically illegal pose (elbow at 200°) is therefore reachable at adversarial cost ~ε without exceeding any threshold the paper describes.

**Clock (timing) failure.** Sparse temporal sampling on [PAGE 5–6] is not clock-aware: the 3/4 overlap between length-varying windows means the effective sampling stride is non-uniform, and if the probe length window Δθ_t − θ_t is shorter than the period of a rotation's extremum, the argmax over segments in PC will land on the mid-rotation pose rather than the held pose. The paper's Figure 8 shows rotation and jump as the two tested classes — these are precisely the two classes with the most torque variation, i.e., the two most vulnerable to aliasing. Over multi-week deployment, a dancer's tempo change alone (metronome +10 BPM) will shift the aliasing null.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three cross-layer vetoes must be imposed on the paper's claim of general superiority, and none is satisfied by the source text.

**Physical veto (kinematic feasibility).** A joint-angle prediction is physically invalid if it exceeds the anatomical range of motion (e.g., human knee flexion beyond ~160° passive, shoulder abduction beyond ~180°; dance-specific extensions vary but are bounded). The paper's loss (11)/(19) is purely cross-entropy over class indices, and the pose loss on [PAGE 4–5] (Eq 14–17) is an argmax of a thermal diagram — neither loss includes any biomechanical constraint. Any predicted pose can therefore trespass the ROM without penalty. A physical veto layer must sit post-hoc and clamp/flag.

**Biomechanical veto (power-chain continuity).** The paper's own motivation on [PAGE 1] invokes *"Good pose control can improve the power chain transmission efficiency"* — yet the model contains no operator that enforces continuity of the power chain (ankle→knee→hip→shoulder→wrist). Eq (15) treats a and b axes independently; a Kronecker-separable thermal diagram cannot represent joint-kinematic coupling. This veto fails at the architecture level, not the loss level.

**Weather / environmental veto.** No illumination, motion-blur, or occlusion operator is described anywhere on [PAGE 2] (pre-processing is only rotation/translation/scale/flip). The LRN in Eq (9) is precisely the operator that would amplify noise under low-light silhouettes (see breakdown regime in source operators section). Over a deployment cycle, low-light frames produce v_i values that exceed the training distribution, and the softmax in Eq (10) will output saturated wrong classes. Environmental veto requires a *pre-network* quality gate, which is absent.

**Adjudication summary.** The paper's experimental validation (Tables 1–2, Figs 8–12) is empirical against a narrow, controlled ballet corpus [PAGE 7: *"on the ballet dance action image set"*]. The superiority claim survives only under that corpus's assumptions. Against the three veto layers above, the pipeline is *conditionally* valid — it will generate state-of-the-art mAP inside its training manifold and silently violate all three constraints one frame past the manifold boundary.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement-learning-with-verifiable-rewards) veto scenarios, each with a concrete prediction:

**Veto 1 — Rotation-singularity probe.** Test the pre-processing chain (Eqs 1–2, [PAGE 2]) with Ψ swept in {π/4, π/3, π/2 − 10⁻¹, π/2 − 10⁻³, π/2 − 10⁻⁶}. Because the rotated-size law (whether printed as tanΨ·sinΨ or as the correct cos/sin form) diverges for the tan form and because interpolation weights ω_a, ω_b at Ψ → π/2 produce unbounded sub-pixel offsets, **prediction**: at Ψ ≥ π/2 − 10⁻³, either P_{sa} or the label back-map (Eq 8) produces non-finite coordinates; mean coordinate error rises monotonically with tan(Ψ) by the same slope regardless of network weights. A verified reward: replicate the pipeline independently and check that the rotation is a group element (R(Ψ₁)·R(Ψ₂) = R(Ψ₁+Ψ₂)) for Ψ₁+Ψ₂ ≤ π/2 — this fails when the crop-before-rotate ordering implied by Eqs 3–4 is re-executed because p₁, p₂ are recomputed from rotated coordinates.

**Veto 2 — Sparse-sampling aliasing.** Reduce temporal sampling density by factor 2 (double Δs) on the two hard classes (rotation, jump) from Figure 8. **Prediction**: because JUMP and ROTATION are torque-peaked actions, recognition precision at the PDJ standard tolerance drops by ≥ 30% relative to the paper's reported curve, and the drop is asymmetric — rotation degrades faster than jump because angular velocity peaks twice per turn cycle. Verified reward: check that the consensus score PC_i for the correct class exceeds the PC_j for the runner-up class by margin ζ at every sampled segment — if any single segment has PC_runner-up > PC_correct by > 10%, sparse sampling failed to bracket the extremum, and the model is aliased. This reward is directly computable from the paper's Eq (18).

**Veto 3 — IOU = 0 loss NaN probe.** Run one training epoch with a fabricated batch containing a proposal that overlaps no ground-truth annotation (IOU_m = 0). **Prediction**: Eq (22)'s term (1 − OS_m/IOU_m)² produces +∞, and \$\mathcal{L}_{OL}\$ becomes NaN; the classifier softmax loss \$\mathcal{L}_{SM}\$ on the same batch is finite, so the aggregate Eq (21) loss is NaN only in the positioning branch. Verified reward: check that per-batch gradient of 𝓛_OL with respect to any positioning-network weight is finite for every batch that contains at least one positive proposal; this requires a `torch.isnan(grad).any()` assertion that the paper does not mention. A second-order reward: verify that the IOU thresholds 0.7/0.3 [PAGE 7] are applied as a *filter* on samples rather than a *mask* on the loss, because the printed language *"judged as positive samples and negative samples, respectively"* implies filtering but Eq (22) requires filtering *plus* IOU > 0 exclusion, which is not stated.

**Aggregate prediction.** A pipeline that passes all three RLVR vetoes simultaneously will report mAP deltas vs. Two-flow CNN that shrink from the 1–4 point range in Table 1 to ≤ 1 point, revealing that the paper's reported advantage is largely a fixed-Ψ, fixed-stride, IOU>0 convenience rather than an algorithmic win.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
