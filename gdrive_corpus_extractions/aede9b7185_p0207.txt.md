<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Hull's 1943 afferent generalization continuum is a direct ancestor of modern state-generalization in reinforcement learning: the idea that value or policy gradients decay with representational distance rather than physical distance underlies tile coding, radial basis function approximators, and successor representations. The j.n.d. argument anticipates Weber-Fechner psychophysics and signal detection theory's \(d'\), where discriminability is normalized by internal noise. The secondary generalization S-R chain prefigures temporal-difference credit assignment across action sequences and the option framework in hierarchical RL.

Historical traps emerge across multi-week applications. First, the stationarity trap: Hull assumes receptor response properties are fixed across the conditioning and test phases, but over weeks, sensory adaptation, receptor fatigue, and motivational drives alter \(\Phi_R\), so a j.n.d. measured in week one is not commensurable with week four. Second, the continuum reification trap: treating the afferent generalization continuum as a literal spatial manifold risks ignoring that it is an inferred construct from discrimination data, not an observed neural topography. Third, the compound equivalence trap: assuming that shared elements guarantee transfer ignores blocking and overshadowing phenomena documented after Hull, where the most salient compound element monopolizes conditioning and common minor elements produce little transfer.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under stress or high drive, the organism's behavior regresses toward the earliest, most deeply conditioned associations—the stimuli with the smallest \(d_{aff}^{(jn d)}\) from the original reinforcement point and therefore the highest residual \(sHR\). The modeler observing this regression faces a recursive trap: to predict which habit regresses, the modeler must estimate the subject's internal afferent metric, but that metric is private and state-dependent (anxiety alters receptor gain and j.n.d.). The modeler's own crisis response is to overfit the most recent data rather than the historically strongest gradient.

Counter-trap: assuming that because the experimenter can compute \(d_{phys}\), the subject behaviorally organizes by \(d_{phys}\). Hull explicitly blocks this by denying one-to-one parallelism; a modeler who ignores \(d_{aff}\) will predict generalization along physical dimensions and fail when receptor nonlinearities dominate. The clock: secondary generalization introduces a temporal credit-assignment window. The proprioception of \(R_1\) must evoke \(R_2\) within the short-term trace decay \(\tau_m\); if the "still later" stimulus impinges after \(\tau_m\), the chain is broken. Thus habit strength is not only a function of spatial j.n.d. distance but of temporal contiguity within the S-R chain.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Biomechanical layer veto: secondary generalization depends on proprioceptive feedback conduction. Muscle spindle and Golgi tendon signals travel via afferent axons at finite velocity; the loop \(S \to R_1 \to \text{proprioception} \to R_2\) incurs delay \(\tau_p\). If \(\tau_p > \tau_m\) (trace decay, roughly hundreds of milliseconds), the contiguity condition for conditioning \(R_2\) to proprioceptive feedback fails, vetoing cross-modal transfer.

Weather/environmental layer veto: if training and testing occur under different stimulus-energy conditions (e.g., ambient illumination altering visual receptor gain, or atmospheric absorption changing sound intensity), the mapping \(\Phi_R\) shifts between sessions. A stimulus pair equidistant in \(d_{phys}\) during training may map to unequal \(d_{aff}\) during testing, invalidating the generalization gradient derived from training parameters.

Adjudication rule: any single layer veto nullifies the corresponding generalization claim. Primary generalization requires all three layers to pass simultaneously; if the physical layer passes but the biomechanical layer fails, only within-modality, immediate-response generalization survives, and cross-modal secondary generalization is vetoed.
