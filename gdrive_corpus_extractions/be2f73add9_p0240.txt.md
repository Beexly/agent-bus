Biomechanical layer (oculomotor): extraocular muscles impose a maximum convergence angle γ_max and a near point d_near; the accommodation–convergence linkage couples lens focus to binocular alignment. When d_O requires γ>γ_max or when |d−d_O| forces disparity beyond Panum’s limit, fusion fails and the percept is vetoed into diplopia or suppression. This provides a hard physical floor to the attraction operator: d_app cannot be driven to zero or to d_O by vergence alone if the required muscle command is outside physiological range.

Weather/environmental layer: atmospheric scattering reduces contrast and luminance with distance, biasing apparent distance upward (aerial perspective). In James’s controlled tabletop demonstration this layer is effectively null, but if haze were introduced between penpoint and half-dollars, the monocular distance cue would push the coins away while the binocular convergence attraction pulls them toward d_O; the veto outcome depends on cue reliability weighting.

Cross-layer veto proof: define cognitive state K∈{known, novel}. If K=known, the semantic layer overrides 𝒜[d_O]: “we are never actually mistaken about the half-dollar being behind the pen-point” [PAGE 230]; d_app is set to the veridical depth order regardless of compression. If K=novel and the stimulus is on the median line, 𝒜[d_O] operates within bounds (i)–(iii). If biomechanical limits are exceeded or weather contrast collapses, the physical/biomechanical layer vetoes fusion and diplopia persists, preventing any single d_app from being computed. Therefore no layer permits the half-dollar to be judged at the same depth as the penpoint; the depth order is veto-protected even when apparent distance is attracted.
