Biomechanically, satiety is governed by gastric distension, cholecystokinin, leptin, and glucose homeostasis, producing a hard physiological bound at which μ(s) → 0 independently of calculation cost. If the biomechanical satiety threshold is reached before the cognitive margin binds, the agent stops because μ = 0, not because λ·C(s) is prohibitive. Knight's explanation of "irrationality" as calculation avoidance is then causally misattributed; the veto fires when the satiety limit is encountered in ordinary ad libitum consumption without any evident calculation burden.

Weather/environmental layer: The utility of laying in supplies depends on climate and infrastructure. Refration, humidity, and transport reliability determine whether a "common, limited means" can be diverted across time without loss. In environments where preservation is weather-dependent, the means available next week is not the same means available today; the alternative relation between wants collapses because the means itself is not common across states of the environment.

Adjudication rule: Knight's diminishing-utility proposition holds only when (i) storage is effectively costless and non-depreciating, (ii) wants are substitutable alternatives drawing on one common means, and (iii) cognitive calculation cost dominates physiological satiety as the binding constraint on stopping [PAGE 77]. Any single layer violation voids the "without significant exception" generalization [PAGE 77].</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three verifiable counterfactual scenarios under the RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) framework, each producing a falsifiable prediction that vetoes Knight's page-77 generalization if confirmed.

Veto Scenario 1 — Non-rival superabundance: Let M be a zero-marginal-cost, non-rival means (e.g., digital storage, broadcast information, ambient air). The constraint Σ x_i = M no longer binds because M is effectively infinite and replicable at zero cost. Prediction: measured marginal utility μ_i(x_i) does not decline as x_i increases; the relative-abundance condition ∂μ_i/∂(x_i/M) < 0 is undefined because M/M is constant. Verification: run an ad-libitum digital-content consumption experiment with zero price and zero search cost; if marginal utility remains flat or increasing across units, the common-limited-means premise is falsified.

Veto Scenario 2 — Strict complementarity: Let wants require fixed-proportion inputs (e.g., coffee and cream, left and right shoes). Utility is Leontief: U = min(x_1 / a_1, x_2 / a_2). Diversion of the common means to want 1 yields zero marginal utility unless want 2 is increased in the exact fixed ratio. Prediction: consumption bundles cluster on the fixed-ratio ray; deviations produce no utility gain, and there is no smooth diminishing-relative-utility curve for either want alone. Verification: observe household purchasing under rationing or price variation; if bundles do not adjust along a continuous diminishing-MU frontier but instead snap to fixed proportions, Knight's alternative-relation assumption fails.

Veto Scenario 3 — Zero-computation agency: Let C(s) ≡ 0 (λ = 0), as in an algorithmic controller with perfect optimization (e.g., an automated insulin pump or caloric dosing algorithm). The agent stops exactly at s*, the physiological setpoint, never short of it due to "bother." Prediction: the footnote's "fundamental irrationality" vanishes; consumption tracks the absolute satiety limit precisely. Verification: compare human ad-libitum intake (which stops short of s* due to calculation/effort costs [PAGE 77]) with closed-loop algorithmic intake; if the algorithm delivers to s* while humans stop at ŝ < s*, the divergence is explained by computation cost, not by an intrinsic law that any unit is like any other.
