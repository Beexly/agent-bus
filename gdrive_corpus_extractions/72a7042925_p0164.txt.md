The mathematical foundations of the Weber-Fechner law dictate that for any constant k,
\[
\log\left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right) = \log(k)
\]
where D_2 and D_1 represent any two delay intervals. In Hull's framework, this translates to equal weights w(D_2)/w(D_1)=k in the response hierarchy, meaning every proportional increase in delay adds a constant amount of resistance to action selection. For the exponential model,
\[
H(D) = H_0 \exp(-kD)
\]
with H_0 as baseline habit strength, k the decay constant, and D the delay. The ratio between two delays D_a < D_b becomes
\[
\frac{H(D_a)}{H(D_b)} = \exp(k(D_b - D_a))
\]
which depends linearly on the absolute difference, not just the ratio. Table 4 uses k = 0.05 and D_0 = 0.0462 to compute H(D) = exp[-0.05*D]. For the 1:2 ratio pair, D_a=1, D_b=2 gives H(1)/H(2)=exp(0.05)=1.05127, while for D_a=4, D_b=8 gives H(4)/H(8)=exp(0.2)=1.22140, yielding vastly different percentages despite identical ratios.
