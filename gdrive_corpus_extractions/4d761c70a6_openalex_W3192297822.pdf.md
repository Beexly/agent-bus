The source formalizes association-rule operators in Equations 2–4: support, confidence, and lift [PAGE 112]. Let \(A\) be an antecedent itemset of constraints and \(B\) the consequent, here effective or ineffective disposal, goal or no goal. All probability quantities below are dimensionless proportions in \([0,1]\), and lift is a dimensionless ratio.

\[
\mathrm{supp}(A\Rightarrow B)=P(A\cup B)
\]

\[
\mathrm{conf}(A\Rightarrow B)=P(B\mid A)=\frac{\mathrm{supp}(A\cup B)}{\mathrm{supp}(A)}
\]

\[
\mathrm{lift}(A\Rightarrow B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)}{P(B)}
\]

Breakdown regimes: if \(\mathrm{supp}(A)=0\), confidence is undefined because the denominator is zero; if \(P(B)=0\), lift is undefined. Small support thresholds produce unstable rules, which is relevant because Chapter 4 used a minimum support value to force rule generation under sparse constraints, while Chapter 6 notes that low-support rules can still be interesting when compared with random event frequency. The source also reports that rule confidence values ranged from 43% to 87% for effective kicks and 13% to 85% for ineffective kicks in Chapter 4 [PAGE 162].

The logistic-regression operator is described as “a mathematical modelling technique which is used to describe the relationship of several independent variables to a dependent variable” [PAGE 87]. For binary outcome \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) and predictors \(x_j\):

\[
\operatorname{logit}\Pr(Y=1\mid x)=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j x_j
\]

\[
\Pr(Y=1\mid x)=\frac{1}{1+\exp\left[-\left(\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_j x_j\right)\right]}
\]

Units: each \(x_j\) remains in its native unit, such as metres for location bins, seconds for time in possession, or a categorical indicator for pressure or shot type; \(\beta_j\) is a logit-scale coefficient per unit or category; \(\exp(\beta_j)\) is a dimensionless multiplicative factor on the modeled link-ratio. Breakdown regimes include complete separation or quasi-separation, high collinearity among correlated constraints, small effective sample size within location bins, and overfitting when many sparse categories are estimated. The source warns that logistic regression “cannot be used to solve non-linear problems” and requires large sample sizes [PAGE 88], which is a direct limitation when modelling interaction-heavy sport data.

\[
\mathrm{Accuracy}=\frac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN}
\]

\[
\mathrm{Precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}
\]

\[
\mathrm{Recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}
\]

\[
F_1=2\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Precision}\cdot\mathrm{Recall}}{\mathrm{Precision}+\mathrm{Recall}}
\]

These are dimensionless, with breakdown when denominators are zero. The source reports confusion-matrix results where CBA had the highest recall, 0.73, but low precision, 0.40, while logistic regression and conditional inference both had precision 0.85 and \(F_1\) values of 0.72 and 0.76 [PAGE 182]. The recursive model has three failure modes for analysts, coaches, and performance staff. Longer possession can allow pressure to reorganize, while shorter possession can prevent defenders from setting. <CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical-layer veto is decisive against any claim of universal shot “optimality.” Australian Football fields vary “between 135 and 185 m in length and 110 and 155 m in width” [PAGE 24], so location bins are not geometrically portable across venues without normalization. Wet turf, wind, and rain alter ball flight, traction, and kicking technique; therefore any equal-opportunity shot zone derived under dry, still conditions cannot be transferred without a weather-conditional modifier.

The biomechanical-layer veto concerns technique and individual capacity. The source states that “AF goal kicking technique has been shown to change with increased kick distance” [PAGE 90]. A rule that says location is dominant [PAGE 172] survives at the coarse level because it appears across multiple models, but it cannot be interpreted as a biomechanical invariant. Adjudication: the core claim that constraint interaction matters survives all three layers because it is supported by multiple model families and multiple data partitions [PAGE 159, PAGE 161, PAGE 162, PAGE 182]. The strong claim that a specific location–pressure–shot-type rule is universally optimal does not survive. It must be vetoed unless re-estimated under venue geometry, weather state, and individual biomechanical covariates. The practical proof standard is therefore conditional generalization, not unconditional transfer.
