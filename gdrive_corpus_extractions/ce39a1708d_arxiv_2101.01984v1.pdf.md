<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source defines a hierarchical single-branch network based on Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50 backbone and an extra L2-normalized linear layer for Re-ID [PAGE 3]. The formal operators are:

\[
s=Wx\in\mathbb{R}^{N+B},\qquad
s=[s_1,\ldots,s_N,s_{N+1},\ldots,s_{N+B}],
\]
where \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\) is a proposal embedding, \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{(N+B)\times d}\), \(N\) is the number of labeled identities, and \(B\) is the background-queue size. The paper calls this a “cosine distance” calculation, but the written operator is a linear projection; if \(W\) rows and \(x\) are L2-normalized, the entries are cosine similarities, not distances. Units: \(s_i\) are dimensionless logits; \(d,N,B\) are counts/dimensions; \(W\) and \(x\) have no physical SI unit. [PAGE 3]

\[
p_i=\frac{e^{s_i/\tau}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N+B}e^{s_j/\tau}},
\]
where \(\tau\) is a dimensionless temperature. \(p_i\in[0,1]\) is dimensionless probability. [PAGE 3]

\[
p(\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_i,\qquad
p(\Phi)=\sum_{i=N+1}^{N+B}p_i.
\]
These are the person and background probabilities. By construction \(p(\Lambda)+p(\Phi)=1\). [PAGE 3–4]

\[
L_{\mathrm{det}}
=-y\log p(\Lambda)-(1-y)\log p(\Phi),
\]
where \(y\in\{0,1\}\) is the binary person/background label. With natural logarithms, \(L_{\mathrm{det}}\) is in nats per proposal; with log base 2 it would be bits, but the paper does not specify the base. [PAGE 4]

\[
p(\text{id}=k,\Lambda)
=\frac{e^{s_k/\tau}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{s_j/\tau}},
\qquad k=1,\ldots,N.
\]
Formally, because the denominator excludes background, this is the conditional identity probability \(p(\text{id}=k\mid\Lambda)\), not the joint \(p(\text{id}=k,\Lambda)=p(\Lambda)p(\text{id}=k\mid\Lambda)\). The notation is a gray zone in the source. [PAGE 4]

\[
L_{\mathrm{OIM}}
=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\log p(\text{id}=k,\Lambda)\right],
\qquad k=1,\ldots,N.
\]
This is an expected log-likelihood in nats per embedding under natural log. [PAGE 4]

\[
L_{\mathrm{iHOIM}}
=L_{\mathrm{det}}+\lambda L_{\mathrm{OIM}},
\qquad
\lambda=2p(\Lambda)^2.
\]
\(\lambda\) is dimensionless and lies in \([0,2]\). The paper states: “where \(\lambda\) is the loss weight for \(L_{\mathrm{OIM}}\). It dynamically weighs the importance of the two tasks based on detection confidence \(p(\Lambda)\).” [PAGE 4]

The look-up table update is:
\[
w_k\leftarrow \eta w_k+(1-\eta)x,
\quad \text{if }x\text{ belongs to identity }k,
\]
with momentum \(\eta=0.5\). The paper reports \(\tau=1/30\), \(N=517\), \(B=500\) for MOT16, and \(N=2332\), \(B=2000\) for MOT20 [PAGE 4]. Training uses SGD with target learning rate 0.003, batch size 3, convergence at epoch 22, on a single GeForce TITAN Xp GPU [PAGE 4].

Assumptions and breakdown regimes:
1. Softmax denominator \(Z=\sum_{j=1}^{N+B}e^{s_j/\tau}\) must be finite and nonzero. If \(\tau\to0^+\), the distribution saturates to a hard max; ties become non-unique, gradients vanish or explode, and \(p_i\) becomes numerically 0 or 1. If \(\tau\to\infty\), the distribution approaches uniform, \(p(\Lambda)\approx N/(N+B)\), and the hierarchical discrimination collapses.
2. BCE logarithms require \(0<p(\Lambda)<1\) and \(0<p(\Phi)<1\) for the active class. If \(y=1\) and \(p(\Lambda)=0\), \(L_{\mathrm{det}}\to+\infty\); if \(y=0\) and \(p(\Phi)=0\), \(L_{\mathrm{det}}\to+\infty\). If \(p(\Lambda)=1\) while \(y=0\), the background log term is singular.
3. The Re-ID denominator \(\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{s_j/\tau}\) is undefined if \(N=0\), and numerically unstable if all labeled scores underflow. The conditional identity probability \(q_k\) is independent of \(p(\Phi)\) in Eq. (6), so the Re-ID term does not directly penalize background probability except through \(\lambda=2p(\Lambda)^2\).
4. \(\lambda=2p(\Lambda)^2\) has no mathematical singularity, but it has a behavioral one: if \(p(\Lambda)\to0\), the Re-ID gradient is suppressed; if \(p(\Lambda)\to1\), Re-ID is maximally weighted. This can reinforce easy, high-confidence detections and under-train hard or occluded identities.
5. The motion model is a Kalman filter: “we choose the Kalman Filter as motion model to predict object positions based on the existing trajectories.” [PAGE 4]. Standard Kalman gain requires a nonsingular innovation covariance. If that covariance is singular, the gain is undefined; if motion is nonlinear or occlusion causes track drift, fused proposals \(M_t\) can inject false positives into \(P_t\).
6. The source does not provide an absolute latency in seconds or SI units; it reports “about 5 times faster than the listed two-stage methods.” [PAGE 5]. That is a dimensionless speed ratio, not a wall-clock guarantee.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is tracking-by-detection \(\rightarrow\) one-stage joint detection/Re-ID \(\rightarrow\) OIM \(\rightarrow\) HOIM \(\rightarrow\) iHOIM. The paper cites Faster R-CNN [19] and ResNet-50 [20] as the detector backbone [PAGE 3], DeepSORT [2] as the association framework [PAGE 3], and OIM [12] and HOIM [15] as the Re-ID loss ancestors. The historical move is from two-stage systems that “require at least two compute-intensive components: an object detector and a Re-ID model” [PAGE 1] toward one-stage sharing of low-level features. The paper’s specific retrocausal claim is that a hierarchical structure can “explicitly model the inter-relationship between object detection and Re-ID” [PAGE 1], replacing separate branches that “do not study the competition of the two subtasks” [PAGE 3]. It also inherits motion-proposal fusion from tracklet-conditioned detection and candidate-selection work [PAGE 2], then reduces the HOIM queue set by removing the unlabeled-person queue: “By removing the circular quene for unlabeled person in HOIM [15] loss, our iHOIM loss is simpler and occupies less memory.” [PAGE 4].

Multi-week traps are not directly measured by the source; the paper reports MOT16/MOT20 experiments, not longitudinal multi-week deployment. The first trap is metric-scope drift: a reader may remember “state-of-the-art” and forget that Table 1’s MOT16 batch MPN has 55.9 MOTA / 59.9 IDF1 while “Ours” has 50.4 / 47.5 [PAGE 5]. The second trap is tracking-metric neglect: high MOTA can coexist with “high IDSw and FP” because the model “pays less attention to optimize tracking” [PAGE 5]. The third trap is buffer aging: the look-up table and circular queue have fixed sizes \(N,B\), with SMR updating them [PAGE 4]; over weeks, new identities and appearance drift can saturate or overwrite stored embeddings. The fourth trap is train/inference motion mismatch: the motion model is removed during training [PAGE 3] but used at inference [PAGE 3–4], so multi-week changes in motion patterns are not learned. The fifth trap is crowd-density domain shift: MOT20 reaches 246 pedestrians per frame [PAGE 4], so association ambiguity and proposal duplication can dominate any Re-ID gain.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under crowded scenes, the system regresses to detection-centric behavior. The paper’s own admission is that “the high IDSw and FP” arise because the model “focuses on improving the quality of detection and Re-ID, while pays less attention to optimize tracking.” [PAGE 5]. The “one-stage” label also regresses to a classic association habit: final trajectories still come from DeepSORT [PAGE 3]. Counter-trap: \(\lambda=2p(\Lambda)^2\) is meant to allocate effort dynamically, but if detection confidence is low for occluded persons, \(\lambda\) collapses toward zero and Re-ID learning is suppressed exactly where identity discrimination is hardest. If detection confidence is high on easy persons, Re-ID is emphasized on already-easy cases. That is a counter-trap, not a neutral weighting. Clock: the online constraint rewards low latency; the paper claims “about 5 times faster” [PAGE 5], but the cost is that future frames and global re-association are unavailable. The recursive theory-of-mind reading is that the authors anticipate the tracking weakness and pre-frame it as future work: “It may be a future research direction of this work.” [PAGE 5]. The reviewer should therefore separate the paper’s detection/Re-ID contribution from its association deficit, and should not treat the “one-stage” label as eliminating the DeepSORT clock-dependent habit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
