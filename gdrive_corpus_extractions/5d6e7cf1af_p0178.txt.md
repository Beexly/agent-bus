<THINK_TRACE>
The analysis of Tolman's "delay tests proper" reveals a profound cognitive architecture that predates modern computational models of working memory and attentional gating. The core distinction lies between the "presentation occasion" (stimulus encoding) and the "functioning occasion" (response execution), separated by a temporal gap where the critical stimulus (the light) is no longer present. This forces the organism to rely on an internal representation—the "sign gestalt-expectation"—that must be maintained in a state of readiness without being expressed as behavior.

Tolman’s framework is revolutionary because it posits that learning is not merely the formation of stimulus-response bonds but the acquisition of *expectations* about the significance of stimuli within a goal-directed system. The light over a door, through discrimination training, becomes a sign ($S$) that signifies the path to food ($O_1$). This is a relational knowledge structure: "If I interact with the entity defined by 'light-plus-door', then 'food' will follow." Conversely, a non-lighted door ($S_i$) signifies "non-food" ($O_2$). These are two distinct, contrasted cognitive maps.

The critical innovation of the delay paradigm is the dissociation of the sign from its immediate functional context. On the presentation occasion, the animal sees the light, which triggers the full expectation ("this specific door leads to food"). However, behavioral expression is inhibited ("held 'in leash'"). The animal must hold this entire expectancy complex in a quiescent but active state during the delay interval. When the functioning occasion arrives, the original sensory cue (the light) has vanished. The animal must now retrieve the stored expectation and apply it to the current environmental configuration (e.g., the spatial location of the doors) to guide its choice.

This process implies several sophisticated mental operations:
1.  **Symbolic Representation:** The light is not just a triggering event; it is a symbol whose meaning is defined by its relationship to a future outcome. The cognitive system must create and maintain a representation of this symbolic relation.
2.  **Temporal Bridging:** A mechanism exists to bridge the gap between perception and action, preserving the integrity of the expectancy across time despite the absence of the stimulus.
3.  **Inhibitory Control:** There is a top-down control process that prevents the premature discharge of the evoked response tendency, allowing for strategic waiting.
4.  **Contextual Re-mapping:** Upon the return of agency, the stored abstract expectation must be re-mapped onto the current perceptual field to select the correct motor program.

The gray zone here is the exact nature of the "held 'in leash'" state. Is it a sustained neural activation? A trace? A modified readiness of associated pathways? Tolman deliberately avoids mechanistic explanations, focusing instead on the functional, purposive level. His insistence that only a single exposure of the sign is needed underscores the efficiency and robustness of this representational system—it is not dependent on repetitive reinforcement during the delay itself but on the prior acquired meaning of the sign.

The failure point would occur if the retention of the expectation degrades over time (temporal decay), or if the transition from "held" to "executed" state fails due to a breakdown in the retrieval process. This model elegantly explains why animals can solve delayed reaction tasks after extensive discrimination training—they have built a library of sign-meaning relations that can be flexibly deployed, even when the signs themselves are transient. It frames intelligence not as reflexive chaining but as the dynamic manipulation of purposeful expectations within a cognitive map of the environment.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let us formalize Tolman's descriptive phenomenology into a set of operators and expectations using a notation grounded in information theory and state-space dynamics.

**Core Entities & States:**
*   Let $E_t$ be the **Environmental State** at time $t$, encompassing all physical configurations (lights, doors, food locations).
*   Let $C$ be the **Cognitive State** of the agent, specifically the currently active set of `Sign Gestalt Expectations`. We define a fundamental expectation as a tuple: $\langle S, \mathcal{O} \rangle$, where $S$ is a perceived sign (or composite of features) and $\mathcal{O}$ is the expected significate (outcome/goal object).
    *   From training: $C_{trained} = \{ \langle S_{light}, O_{food} \rangle, \langle S_{noLight}, O_{nonFood} \rangle \}$.

**Operators:**
1.  **Perceptual Encoding Operator ($\Phi$):** Maps an environmental state to a perceived sign.
    $\Phi(E_t) \rightarrow s_t$, where $s_t \in \{S_{light}, S_{noLight}\}$.
    *Assumption:* Perception is instantaneous and perfect for the relevant discriminanda.

2.  **Expectancy Instantiation Operator ($\Psi$):** Given a perceived sign $s_t$ and the trained cognitive state $C$, it activates the corresponding expectation.
    $\Psi(s_t, C) \rightarrow \text{ActiveExpectation}_t = \langle s_t, \mathcal{O}_t \rangle$.
    For example, $\Psi(S_{light}, C_{trained}) = \langle S_{light}, O_{food} \rangle$.

3.  **Retention/Quiescence Operator ($\Gamma$):** This is the operator for the delay period. It holds the `ActiveExpectation` in a ready but non-expressed state. We can model this as a function that takes the active expectation and the elapsed delay time $\Delta t$.
    $\Gamma( \langle s, \mathcal{O} \rangle, \Delta t ) \rightarrow \text{RetainedExpectation}_{\Delta t}$.
    *Key Property:* $\text{RetainedExpectation}_{\Delta t}$ must preserve the identity of the original sign $s$ and its mapped outcome $\mathcal{O}$, even though the physical $s$ is no longer in $E_{t+\Delta t}$.
    *Breakdown Regime:* The fidelity of retention decreases as a function of delay length, $F(\Delta t)$, approaching zero as $\Delta t \rightarrow \infty$. This defines the **Working Memory Span**.

4.  **Retrieval & Execution Operator ($\Xi$):** At the "functioning occasion," the retained expectation is retrieved and translated into a response $R$ targeting the currently perceived environment $E_{func}$.
    $\Xi( \text{RetainedExpectation}_{\Delta t}, E_{func} ) \rightarrow R$.
    This involves matching the stored sign identity (e.g., "left door was lit") to a current spatial referent (e.g., "the left door is still there") and generating the appropriate approach behavior toward the anticipated significate.

**Formalizing the Delay Test Sequence:**
*   **Presentation Occasion ($t_p$):**
    *   Environment: $E_{t_p}$ includes one door with light ($S$) and others without ($S_i$).
    *   Action: $\Phi(E_{t_p}) \rightarrow S$. Then, $\Psi(S, C) \rightarrow \text{ActiveExp} = \langle S, O_{food} \rangle$.
    *   Constraint: Motor output is inhibited. Only $\Gamma$ is engaged.

*   **Delay Interval ($[t_p + \delta, t_f]$):**
    *   Physical Sign disappears: $S \notin E_{t}$ for $t > t_p$.
    *   Process: The system maintains $X = \Gamma( \langle S, O_{food} \rangle, t - t_p )$. The content of $X$ remains structurally identical to $\langle S, O_{food} \rangle$ but exists solely within the cognitive state $C$.

*   **Functioning Occasion ($t_f$):**
    *   Environment: $E_{t_f}$ contains doors but no lights.
    *   Action: Inhibition is lifted. $\Xi(X, E_{t_f})$ must yield a response $R$ directed at the spatial location previously associated with $S$. Success requires that the mapping from abstract sign $S$ back to its spatial instance in $E_{t_f}$ is preserved.

**Information-Theoretic View:**
The "sign gestalt-expectation" can be viewed as a probability distribution over outcomes conditioned on a sign. Training establishes $P(O_{food} | S_{light}) \approx 1$ and $P(O_{nonFood} | S_{noLight}) \approx 1$. The delay test measures the ability to retain the conditional distribution $P(O|S)$ after the observation of $S$ has ceased. The performance is a function of the mutual information $I( \text{RetainedState}; \text{OriginalSign} )$ remaining after delay.

**Units:** The primary unit is the **Expectation Tuple** $\langle S, \mathcal{O} \rangle$, a cognitive data structure. Time is measured in seconds/days. Retention strength can be quantified in bits of preserved information about the original sign's identity.

**Singularity/Breakdown:**
*   **Temporal Singularity:** As $\Delta t \rightarrow T_{critical}$, where $T_{critical}$ is the species/task-specific memory limit, the retention function $\Gamma$ fails catastrophically. $X$ becomes undefined or random, leading to chance-level performance.
*   **Interference Breakdown:** If multiple conflicting signs are presented in quick succession, the system may fail to serially retain distinct $\langle S, \mathcal{O} \rangle$ tuples, leading to proactive/retroactive interference.
*   **Mapping Failure:** Even with perfect retention of "$S$ was on the left," a breakdown occurs if the agent cannot correctly identify "left" in the new lighting conditions of $E_{t_f}$.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 formulation emerges from a direct lineage of anti-reflexological thought. Its intellectual forebears include Kantian notions of synthetic a priori structures organizing experience and William James’s concept of "habit" as a predisposition toward certain lines of thought or action. More proximately, it reacts against Pavlovian and Thorndikian models. Where Watson saw the maze as a series of linked reflexes, Tolman posited a "cognitive map" [PAGE 1, implied by "sign gestalt-expectations"]—a topological representation independent of specific responses.

The historical trap is the assumption that any behavior occurring after a delay must be driven by some form of persistent *stimulus*. Early theorists might have imagined a fading "image" or a slow chemical trace acting as a pseudo-stimulus. Tolman’s genealogical insight is to break this chain. He retrocausally argues from the *purposefulness* of the final choice (going to the correct dark door) back to the initial perception. The later success validates the existence of a *meaning-based* intermediate state (the expectation), not a sensory echo.

The multi-week trap for a researcher following this lineage could be designing experiments that try to measure the "strength" of a supposed stimulus trace. One might attempt to vary inter-stimulus intervals or introduce masking stimuli, forever chasing a phantom physical cause. Tolman redirects the inquiry to the *structure of the expectation itself*: What is its content? How does it transform across contexts? The real variable is not the duration of a neural firing pattern, but the stability of the learned relation between sign and significate. This shift from physiological continuity to semantic coherence is the foundational move of cognitive psychology.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
From the rat’s perspective, the delay test imposes a severe crisis. The learned habit is clear: "See light -> Run to that door -> Get food." This is a simple, reinforced S-R sequence. The delay setup shatters this automaticity. The light appears, triggering the entire "getting-to-food" plan. The urge to run is presumably strong. Yet, running is blocked. This creates a conflict between the evoked consummatory drive and the imposed situational constraint.

The rat must regress to a more primitive, pre-habituated mode: anticipatory waiting. It cannot simply execute; it must *wait for permission* to execute based on a memory of what was seen. This is the essence of self-control and represents a major leap in behavioral flexibility. The "holding in leash" metaphor perfectly captures this internal struggle—the instinctual response is psychologically present and demanding release but is held back by a higher-order cognitive process (the understanding that waiting is required for eventual success).

The counter-trap for the experimenter is to underestimate this psychological drama. They might see the delay as a mere technical inconvenience. But for the subject, it is a test of executive function. The "clock" in the rat's mind is not measuring seconds but monitoring the completion of the "waiting phase" before the "action phase" begins. A rat that impulsively chooses at random during the delay is failing not because it forgot the light, but because it failed to inhibit the premature release of the expectant state. Thus, the delay test is a dual measure: of both **representational fidelity** (memory) and **behavioral inhibition** (self-control).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Even with perfect cognitive mapping and expectation retention, performance can be vetoed by lower-level systems.

1.  **Biomechanical Veto:** Suppose the rat’s hind paw is injured. The stored expectation is `<LeftDoor-Lit, Food>`. During the functioning occasion, it retrieves this and intends to run to the left door. However, the injury causes pain upon weight-bearing, triggering a nociceptive override. The motor program for "run to left" is aborted or altered mid-execution. The cognitive map is intact, but the body vetoes its implementation. The observed error would be misattributed to poor memory unless the biomechanical state is monitored.

2.  **Sensory Gating/Weather Veto:** Imagine the delay occurs in a sudden, intense auditory disturbance (e.g., a loud clang). This exogenous stimulus could disrupt the fragile "held in leash" state through startle-induced arousal or by consuming attentional resources. The expectation `<S_left, O_food>` may be prematurely discharged (leading to a frantic, possibly incorrect choice) or completely lost. Here, the external "weather" of the sensory environment directly corrupts the maintenance process (`\Gamma`), independent of the original sign's clarity.

3.  **Neuromodulatory Veto:** A surge in stress hormones (e.g., corticosterone) during the delay could shift the brain from a "cognitive/expectancy" mode to a "fear/arousal" mode. In this state, the value of the `O_food` significate plummets relative to the value of safety. Even if the spatial memory is flawless, the rat may choose to freeze or flee rather than pursue the food, effectively vetoing the goal-directed action initiated by the retained expectation.

These vetoes prove that Tolman’s cognitive layer, while necessary for successful performance, is not sufficient. It operates within a hierarchy where somatic, autonomic, and broad neuromodulatory states have ultimate authority over behavior. The delay test, therefore, is a probe of the *interface stability* between cognition and these other layers.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
We can construct Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) scenarios to test the limits and verify the logic of Tolman's model.

**Scenario 1: The Phantom Sign (Testing Representational Specificity)**
*   **Setup:** Train rat with standard light-food association. In the delay test, during the presentation occasion, flash the light briefly on Door A, then immediately (within 100ms) flash it on Door B before the delay starts. The total light exposure is minimal.
*   **Prediction (Tolman Model):** The rat's system processes one dominant sign-event. Due to primacy/recency effects, it should predominantly remember either Door A or Door B. Performance should not be perfect, showing a bias. This verifies that the expectation is bound to a *specific spatio-temporal instance* of the sign, not just to the abstract concept "a light occurred somewhere."
*   **Veto Condition:** If the rat performs at ceiling, it suggests the expectation is not tied to a specific door but is a generic "go to where light *was*" directive, weakening the claim for a detailed "gestalt."

**Scenario 2: The Meaning Shift (Testing Expectancy Transformation)**
*   **Setup:** After mastering light->food, add a new phase: Light now signals that food is available, but *only if* you first press a lever elsewhere. The delay test now has two phases: Presentation (light seen), Delay, Functioning (choice between going directly to a door vs. going to the lever).
*   **Prediction:** The rat's initial expectation `<S_light, O_food>` must be transformed. It should learn a new compound expectation: `<S_light, (Action_lever THEN O_food)>`. In the delay test, it should wait and then go to the lever first.
*   **Verification:** Success shows that the "held in leash" state is not a frozen copy of the old S-R bond but a malleable, rule-based expectation that can be updated with new contingencies. This is a key differentiator from simple associative models.

**Scenario 3: The Interfering Sign (Testing Capacity Limits)**
*   **Setup:** During the delay interval, after the light has gone out, briefly illuminate a *different* door with a novel color (e.g., blue light) that has never been paired with anything.
*   **Prediction:** The new, unconditioned stimulus (blue light) should capture attention and potentially overwrite or interfere with the retention of the original `<S_originalLight, O_food>` expectation. Performance should drop significantly compared to a silent delay.
*   **Veto Logic:** This probes the fragility of the `\Gamma` (Retention) operator. It verifies that maintaining the expectation is an active, resource-consuming process vulnerable to distraction, consistent with modern concepts of working memory load. If performance remains high, it suggests the expectation is stored in a protected, automatic store less susceptible to interference.
