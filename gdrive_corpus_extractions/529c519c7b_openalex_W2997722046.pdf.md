**1. Registration as projective estimation.** The playing surface is modelled as a plane $\Pi$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$; a broadcast frame is a perspective image. The map from field-plane coordinates $\mathbf{X}=(X,Y,1)^\top$ (metres, homogeneous) to image coordinates $\mathbf{u}=(u,v,1)^\top$ (pixels, homogeneous) is a homography:

$$\mathbf{u} \;\sim\; \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{X},\qquad \mathbf{H}\in PGL(3),\quad \mathbf{H}\equiv \lambda\mathbf{H},\ \lambda\neq 0 .$$

$\mathbf{H}$ has 8 degrees of freedom and is *dimensionless* (defined only up to scale), consistent with the paper's framing of *"the 2D-to-3D projection problem for sports fields"* [PAGE 1] and Fig. 1's *"2D-to-3D projection from a broadcast hockey video frame to a top-down view of the ice rink"* [PAGE 1].

**2. Physical decomposition (units explicit).** For a calibrated camera with intrinsics $\mathbf{K}$ (pixels), rotation $\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)$ (dimensionless), translation $\mathbf{t}$ (metres), plane normal $\mathbf{n}$ (unitless) and camera-to-plane distance $d$ (metres):

$$\mathbf{H} \;=\; \mathbf{K}\left(\mathbf{R} + \frac{\mathbf{t}\,\mathbf{n}^{\top}}{d}\right)\mathbf{K}^{-1}.$$

*Assumption:* pinhole camera, zero lens distortion, global shutter. *Breakdown:* $d\to 0$ (plane through the optical centre) makes $\mathbf{H}$ singular; strong wide-angle distortion violates the pinhole assumption and injects a systematic, non-projective residual that no homography — however well memorized — can absorb.

**3. DLT and its conditioning.** With four correspondences $\{(\mathbf{X}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)\}_{i=1}^{4}$:

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{X})\,\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{H}) = \mathbf{0},\qquad \mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{8\times 9},\qquad \hat{\mathbf{h}} = \arg\min_{\|\mathbf{h}\|_2=1}\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}\|_2^2 ,$$

solved by the right singular vector of $\mathbf{A}$ for $\sigma_9$. First-order perturbation bound:

$$\frac{\|\delta \mathbf{h}\|}{\|\mathbf{h}\|} \;\le\; \kappa_2(\mathbf{A})\,\frac{\|\delta \mathbf{A}\|}{\|\mathbf{A}\|},\qquad \kappa_2(\mathbf{A}) = \frac{\sigma_1(\mathbf{A})}{\sigma_8(\mathbf{A})}.$$

**Exact singularity regime:** $\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})\le 7 \iff$ three of the four image points are collinear or two coincide. As the configuration approaches collinearity with cross-track offset $\varepsilon$ (pixels), $\sigma_8 \sim \varepsilon$ and $\kappa_2 \sim 1/\varepsilon$; when $\varepsilon$ falls below the keypoint localization noise $\sigma_{px}$ (pixels, typically 1–3 px for line-intersection corners on broadcast footage), the estimate is noise-dominated regardless of history.

**4. Objective the paper implicitly proposes.** End-to-end temporal estimation replaces the two-stage pipeline with:

$$\hat{\mathbf{H}}_t = f_\theta\!\left(\mathbf{F}_t,\ \mathcal{M}_{t-1}\right),\qquad \mathcal{M}_t = g_\phi\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{t-1},\ \mathbf{F}_t\right),$$

where $\mathcal{M}$ is the memory state and $\theta,\phi$ are learned. The attention read at time $t$ over stored keys/values $\{(\mathbf{k}_i,\mathbf{v}_i)\}$ is

$$\alpha_{t,i} = \frac{\exp\!\left(\mathbf{q}_t^{\top}\mathbf{k}_i/\sqrt{d_k}\right)}{\sum_j \exp\!\left(\mathbf{q}_t^{\top}\mathbf{k}_j/\sqrt{d_k}\right)},\qquad \mathbf{r}_t=\sum_i \alpha_{t,i}\mathbf{v}_i,$$

$\mathbf{r}_t$ dimensionless, $d_k$ the key dimension (dimensionless). This is exactly the mechanism the paper imports: *"Additional priors from previous frames are used as input with a memory controller to select those that are most important, as in [5] and [6]."* [PAGE 1]

**5. The information-theoretic justification the paper gestures at but never writes.** For frames $\mathbf{F}_{1:t}$ and prior estimates $\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{1:t-1}$:

$$I\!\left(\mathbf{H}_t;\mathbf{F}_{1:t}\right)\ \ge\ I\!\left(\mathbf{H}_t;\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{1:t-1}\right)\quad\text{(bits)},$$

with strict inequality whenever $\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{1:t-1}$ is not a sufficient statistic for $\mathbf{H}_t$. This is the only defensible formal content of *"Using richer input data will increase the performance of the network"* [PAGE 1]. Note it is an inequality about *available* information, not a guarantee that a trained network extracts it — the abstract conflates the two.

**6. Smoothness prior the paper proposes to delete.** Existing correctors minimize, over a window, something of the form

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} = \lambda\sum_{t}\left\|\log\!\left(\mathbf{H}_t\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1}\right)\right\|_{\mathfrak{sl}(3)},$$

(with $\log$ the matrix logarithm into the Lie algebra, dimensionless, $\lambda$ in units of the inverse of the data term). The paper's proposal is to fold this prior into $f_\theta$; formally equivalent regularization, different estimator variance. **Breakdown of the smoothness assumption:** any hard cut, replay insert, or rapid operator whip-pan violates $\|\log(\mathbf{H}_t\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1})\| \ll 1$ by orders of magnitude, at which point the prior is not merely wrong but actively harmful.

**7. Real-time budget.** At $f=30\ \mathrm{fps}$ the per-frame budget is $B=1/f=33.3\ \mathrm{ms}$; at $60\ \mathrm{fps}$, $16.7\ \mathrm{ms}$. A memory bank retaining $T$ keyframes with per-key attention costs $O(T\,d_k)$ memory traffic per frame; without a pruning/eviction rule (the paper gives none) latency grows linearly and the *"approach real-time performance"* goal [PAGE 1] fails on long broadcasts. The cited workaround — *"saving the intermediate prediction every 5 frames"* [PAGE 1] — is a subsampling heuristic with no derivation for this task.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper sits at a specific convergence, and the convergence explains its blind spots better than its own related-work section does.

**Branch A — geometric/structured (2017).** Homayounfar, Fidler & Urtasun segment field and lines and recover the transform from *vanishing points of the field edges* [PAGE 1]. This branch's inheritance is classical photogrammetry: the estimate is a closed-form function of extracted line geometry. Its virtue is interpretability; its vice is brittleness — vanishing points estimated from near-parallel lines are catastrophically ill-conditioned, the same $\kappa\sim 1/\varepsilon$ pathology as DLT.

**Branch B — dictionary/template matching (2018–2019).** Sharma et al. build a dictionary from a few annotated frames by *"modeling the pan, tilt and zoom of a broadcast camera"* and smooth with *"Markov random field optimization and a camera stabilization algorithm"* [PAGE 2]; Chen & Little build a synthetic dictionary from a camera-pose engine, segment lines with two GANs, index with a Siamese network, and smooth with Lucas–Kanade [PAGE 2]. This branch inherits from image retrieval and from the older PTZ-camera-calibration literature. Its structural assumption is that the camera's motion manifold is low-dimensional and enumerable — true for a broadcast gantry, false for handheld or drone coverage.

**Branch C — learned homography (2016–2019).** DeTone et al.'s deep homography and Nguyen et al.'s unsupervised variant [PAGE 2] supply the regression backbone; Jiang et al. supply the error-regression formulation [PAGE 2] that the present paper explicitly adopts.

**Branch D — memory for video (2018–2019).** Yang & Chan's attended LSTM tracker, Oh et al.'s space-time memory [PAGE 2]. This branch inherits from NLP reading-comprehension architectures, as the paper itself notes: *"The use of memory networks originated in natural language processing, especially for question answering."* [PAGE 1]

**The retrocausal reading.** Each branch's correction stage was invented to patch the previous branch's residual failure, and each patch *hardened* the per-frame assumption rather than questioning it. Sharma's MRF exists because per-frame retrieval is noisy; Chen & Little's Lucas–Kanade exists because dictionary indexing is jumpy; Jiang's error network exists because the initial four-point estimate is biased. The lineage is a sequence of *aftershocks* around a fixed epicentre. The present paper's move — relocate the temporal prior *inside* the estimator — is the correct structural response, which is precisely why it is worth noting that it arrives three years after the components (attention memory, deep homography) were available.

**Multi-week traps embedded in this lineage.** (i) *The sufficiency trap*: assuming that because smoothing reduces variance on benchmark sequences, it has added information. It has not; it has added a prior. On a sequence whose statistics match the prior, both look identical, so the error hides. (ii) *The benchmark-leak trap*: broadcast datasets are dominated by gantry-mounted, smoothly panning cameras — exactly the regime where the smoothness assumption holds — so temporal and non-temporal methods converge in reported accuracy while diverging sharply on handheld, cut-heavy, or replay-laden footage. Any evaluation that does not stratify by cut density will report a null result and be misread as a refutation. (iii) *The transfer trap*: importing a keyframe interval ("every 5 frames") tuned on object-appearance memory into camera-motion memory without re-derivation.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The field's crisis habit.** Under deadline pressure — a two-page abstract, a workshop deadline, an industry partner (Stathletes, via Mitacs) — the community's regression is to *bolt a corrector onto a per-frame estimator*. Every cited method does this. The habit is reinforced because it is cheap, modular, debuggable, and each individual patch reports an improvement. The habit's cost is architectural: it forecloses joint optimization and it hard-codes a motion prior that the network cannot learn to distrust.

**The authors' model of the reviewer.** The abstract is written to pre-empt the three predictable objections: (1) "this is just more parameters" — answered by *"thereby reducing computational cost"* [PAGE 1]; (2) "why not simply smooth output?" — answered by *"without the need for an additional smoothing step"* [PAGE 1]; (3) "does temporal input even help?" — answered by assertion, *"Using richer input data will increase the performance of the network"* [PAGE 1]. Note that objection (3) is the only one that requires evidence, and it is the only one met with a bare claim. The recursive model is: the authors anticipate that reviewers will grant the premise, because the premise is the field's shared habit.

**The counter-trap.** The reflexive rebuttal — "temporal priors are just a smoothness prior in disguise, so nothing is gained" — is *wrong*, and dangerously so, because it is nearly right. It is right that any recurrent estimator with bounded memory implements an implicit prior. It is wrong that the prior is equivalent: conditioning on $\mathbf{F}_{1:t}$ versus on $\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{1:t-1}$ differs by everything the per-frame estimator discarded, and that discarded content is exactly what disambiguates repeated field markings (the same blue line appears many times on an ice rink; only history tells you *which* blue line). The counter-trap is thus a real trap: it wins arguments and loses accuracy.

**The clock.** The third player is the latency budget. A memory architecture is a *stateful* system, and stateful systems fail differently: they fail by staleness, not by noise. Under the 33.3 ms/frame clock, the engineer cannot afford unbounded attention, so pruning becomes mandatory — and pruning introduces a new failure mode (evicting the keyframe that would have bridged a cut) that per-frame systems simply do not have. The recursive insight: the proposal trades a *noise* failure mode for a *staleness* failure mode, and the paper's cost argument mentions only the savings, never the new liability.

**Recursive audit of the claim itself.** The authors believe the memory controller will "select those that are most important" [PAGE 1]. But attention selects by *learned similarity to the current query*, and the current query is derived from the current frame — which, immediately after a cut, is maximally misleading. The selection mechanism is therefore least reliable precisely when selection matters most. This is a genuine architectural objection that the paper's framing obscures.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A veto is a condition under which no amount of architectural improvement at the algorithmic layer can rescue the estimate. Four layers, each with a stated proof obligation.

**Layer 1 — Geometric/physical (hard veto).** If fewer than four non-collinear field features are visible in frame $t$, the homography at $t$ is *unidentifiable* — $\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{A})<8$. This is not a modelling choice; it is a rank statement. Broadcast close-ups, goal celebrations, and crowd shots routinely produce such frames. Memory can supply an *interpolated* estimate, but interpolation is not identification: its error is bounded only by how far the camera actually travelled, which is unknown during the gap. **Verdict: veto on identification; memory may only assert a prior, and any deployment must expose an uncertainty flag.** Estimated gap tolerance: $\lesssim 5$ frames at 30 fps ($\approx 0.17\ \mathrm{s}$) for gantry cameras, degrading to $\lesssim 1$ frame for handheld.

**Layer 2 — Biomechanical / operator kinematics.** The smoothing assumption is a statement about a *human camera operator's* control bandwidth. Human smooth-pursuit tracking has a closed-loop bandwidth on the order of a few hertz; within that band, pan/tilt/zoom trajectories are indeed smooth and $\|\log(\mathbf{H}_t\mathbf{H}_{t-1}^{-1})\|$ is small. Above it — whip-pans to follow a fast break, snap zooms to a foul — the assumption is violated by one to two orders of magnitude in a single frame. **Verdict: conditional veto, frequency-dependent.** The correct formulation is a bandwidth spec on the motion model (e.g., valid for angular rates below a stated threshold in rad/s), which the paper never states, and which is required to know when to trust memory.

**Layer 3 — Weather and illumination.** Outdoor football in rain, snow, low sun, or stadium shadow transitions degrades line segmentation and corner localization: the GAN-based segmenters cited [PAGE 2] and any keypoint detector operating on painted lines all lose precision when contrast collapses. Note the direction of causation: this veto acts *upstream* of the homography estimator, raising $\sigma_{px}$ (pixels). Combined with Layer 1's conditioning bound $\kappa\sim 1/\varepsilon$, a rise in $\sigma_{px}$ converts a merely difficult geometry into an unusable one. **Verdict: amplifier veto — weather does not break the algorithm, it lowers the conditioning threshold at which the algorithm breaks.** Indoor ice (the paper's Fig. 1 domain) is comparatively protected; outdoor pitch is not.

**Layer 4 — Signal/broadcast layer.** Compression artifacts, motion blur at high pan rates, interlacing, and graphics overlays (score bugs, lower-thirds) occlude or corrupt exactly the corner regions used as support. Overlays are a *hard* veto because they are persistent and structured: a corner hidden under a score bug for an entire half is unrecoverable from that frame's pixels. **Verdict: veto on keypoint availability; memory over the half can bridge it only if the camera returns to a configuration previously memorized — which is the one scenario where the memory architecture genuinely and uniquely helps.**

**Adjudication summary.** Layers 1 and 4 veto identification outright; Layer 2 vetoes the prior above a bandwidth threshold; Layer 3 modulates all thresholds. The paper's proposal is defensible only in the intersection: gantry-mounted, indoor or good-weather, no-cut, no-overlay footage — i.e., exactly its own figure.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario is a falsifiable intervention with an ex-ante prediction and an explicit refutation criterion. All are runnable on public soccer/hockey broadcast sets with annotated homographies.

**Veto 1 — Hard-cut injection.**
*Intervention:* take clean sequences, splice a camera cut at frame $t_0$ (switch to a different camera or a replay angle), keep everything else identical.
*Prediction:* the memory-conditioned estimator without an explicit cut detector shows a spike in median reprojection error (pixels) of $\ge 3\times$ the pre-cut baseline persisting for $\ge 5$ frames, because attention still assigns non-negligible mass to pre-cut keys; a variant with a learned gate on $\alpha_{t,i}$ (or an explicit frame-similarity reset) returns to baseline within $\le 2$ frames. Crucially, the per-frame baseline is *unaffected* by the cut.
*Refutation criterion:* if the memory variant shows no spike, the memory controller is already effectively gating on novelty and the paper's cost-saving argument is stronger than stated. If the gated variant also fails to recover within 2 frames, the failure is in the homography head, not the memory.

**Veto 2 — Keypoint occlusion ladder.**
*Intervention:* progressively mask $k=0,1,2,3$ of the four support corners for a run of frames, $k$ swept from 0 to 10 frames.
*Prediction:* per-frame DLT error is *undefined-to-infinite* for any $k\ge 2$ consecutive frames (rank deficiency, Level-1 veto); the memory estimator's error grows monotonically with gap length and crosses a usable threshold (say, field-space corner error exceeding a fixed metre tolerance) at a breakpoint near 5 frames — the same magnitude as the cited "every 5 frames" keyframe interval [PAGE 1], which would incidentally validate that heuristic as non-arbitrary.
*Refutation criterion:* if the memory estimator's error is flat in gap length, it is not using memory adaptively and is simply regressing the mean homography — a degenerate solution that the intervention exposes but that aggregate accuracy metrics would hide.

**Veto 3 — Near-collinear conditioning sweep.**
*Intervention:* on a synthetic field, sweep the cross-track offset $\varepsilon$ of the fourth support point from 200 px down to 0.5 px at fixed pixel noise $\sigma_{px}=1$ px.
*Prediction:* measured $\kappa_2(\mathbf{A})$ tracks $1/\varepsilon$; the *memory* estimator's error curve is statistically indistinguishable from the per-frame DLT curve in this sweep, because conditioning is a property of the current frame's geometry and history carries no information about it. Divergence between the two curves appears only at small $\varepsilon$ *and* only if the network has learned an implicit geometric prior strong enough to regularize — which is a testable, and per this paper's design unstated, claim.
*Refutation criterion:* memory-induced error reduction at $\varepsilon < 3$ px falsifies the "conditioning is frame-local" argument and would be the single most interesting result the architecture could produce — and the paper's two pages contain no evidence either way.

**Meta-veto.** All three interventions share a property the paper's evaluation design (if it had one) would miss: they are *stratified* manipulations. Aggregate reprojection error over a broadcast corpus is dominated by the easy majority regime, where per-frame and temporal methods coincide. The paper's stated benefit can only be observed in the tails, and the tails are where the cross-layer vetoes above live.

**Disposition.** The document is a two-page proposal with a sound structural instinct — move the temporal prior inside the estimator rather than bolting it on afterward — supported by a formally defensible information argument that the authors do not actually make, and an empirical claim that the authors do not test. Its three unaddressed liabilities are DLT conditioning under edge-on geometry, staleness after camera cuts, and unbounded memory cost against a 33.3 ms frame budget. The three RLVR interventions above convert the abstract's conditional verbs into measurable predictions.
