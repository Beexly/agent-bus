$$ f(M) = A \left(1 - e^{-k M}\right) + C $$
where $A$ is the asymptotic maximum learning capacity, $k$ is the rate constant for reward sensitivity, and $C$ accounts for baseline locomotor activity (observed in the zero-food group).

**Breakdown Regimes:**
1. **Singularity at $t=0$:** The metric approaches infinity if traversal time hits zero, representing a physical impossibility and a breakdown of the metric's linearity near instantaneous speeds.
2. **Reward Saturation:** As $M$ increases beyond the metabolic absorption limit of the chick, $\frac{df}{dM} \to 0$. Additional grains provide no incremental increase in drive reduction, causing the curve to plateau.
3. **Null Reinforcement:** At $M=0$, $f(0)$ reflects purely non-associative factors (spontaneous movement), serving as the intercept $C$.

Hull and his contemporaries who sought to reduce psychological phenomena to measurable physical variables. The use of chicks (*Gallus gallus*) in runway tasks traces back to earlier developmental studies, but the specific manipulation of *reward magnitude* connects deeply to the economic theories of value applied to animal behavior. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In the context of a twelve-day-old chick, "Theory of Mind" is rudimentary, limited to immediate sensory-motor contingencies. However, a recursive view can be applied to their developing predictive models. Initially, the runway presents a novel, potentially threatening environment. The first few trials involve high uncertainty: "If I move forward, what happens?" When the chick receives one grain, a simple association forms: Runway -> Rice.
As the trials progress and the reward magnitude varies between peers (though within-subject it is constant per group), the chick's internal state shifts from exploratory to goal-directed. The "crisis habit regression" occurs if a previously rewarded chick suddenly enters a phase where the runway feels longer or the tray empty; the behavior reverts to random wandering. In Grindley's design, this regression is minimized by the consistent presence of the reward during the learning phase.
Conversely, the "counter-trap" is the establishment of a robust expectation. The chick learns not just that rice exists, but how much is expected. A chick trained on six grains develops a stronger anticipatory drive than one trained on one grain. When placed back on the runway, the internal clock ticks down the distance; the higher the anticipated yield, the less tolerance the chick has for deviation or slowness, driving the velocity up and the $t$ down, thus inflating the score.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Even with perfect cognitive mapping, physical and environmental vetoes can halt or degrade performance, fundamentally altering the observed scores.
*   **Biomechanical Veto:** Twelve-day-old chicks have developing musculoskeletal systems. If a chick slips on the runway surface or suffers a minor leg fatigue injury, its traversal time $t$ will spike regardless of the six grains waiting at the end. The equation $100/t^2$ would plummet, misrepresenting "learning" as poor when it is actually a motor failure.
*   **Physical/Environmental Veto:** The "shallow tray" mentioned is a crucial variable. If the tray is too deep, a chick might struggle to extract the grains, delaying the onset of eating. Since the metric relies on the time to "begin eating," a physical impediment to accessing the food introduces a mechanical lag distinct from runway traversal speed.
*   **Weather/Physiological Veto:** Ambient temperature affects ectothermic-like regulation in young birds. Cold stress could increase general metabolic drive (hunger) but decrease motor coordination. If the room is too cold, the chicks might shiver, increasing $t$ despite high motivation.
These vetoes prove that the "mean score" is an emergent property surviving the filtering of subjects who remained physically capable and environmentally comfortable throughout the seven trials.

1.  **Scenario: The Over-Saturation Veto.**
    *   *Counterfactual:* Introduce a sixth group receiving twenty grains of rice instead of six.
    *   *Prediction:* The learning curve will not continue to rise proportionally. Instead, the score will plateau or even drop slightly due to digestive burden slowing the chick down immediately after reaching the tray, interfering with the precise measurement of "beginning to eat." The marginal gain in $sEr$ becomes zero or negative.
2.  **Scenario: The Diminishing Novelty Trap.**
    *   *Counterfactual:* Repeat the seven trials daily for two weeks without changing the reward magnitude, then switch a previously "one-grain" group to "six grains" on day 15.
    *   *Prediction:* A massive, transient spike in performance (a sharp drop in $t$) will occur on day 15 due to the sudden increase in incentive value, violating the steady-state assumption of the original growth function. This verifies that the original function describes acquisition, not re-acquisition after a change in valuation.
3.  **Scenario: The Decoy Distraction Veto.**
    *   *Counterfactual:* Place a highly visible but inaccessible piece of shiny foil halfway down the runway during the final three trials.
    *   *Prediction:* Despite the strong reinforcement at the end, the chicks' attention will be captured by the novel visual stimulus, increasing traversal time significantly. This demonstrates that the "index of learning" is contingent on the absence of competing salient stimuli, proving the metric measures focused approach behavior rather than pure memorization of the path.
