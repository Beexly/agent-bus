**1. Cohesive-zone failure of the glial–neuronal interface.** From [PAGE 244]: "the stretching or breaking of the bond between glial cells and neurons… if it breaks entirely, that's it. The neuron dies." A monotonic traction–separation law:

\[
\sigma(\delta)=\sigma_{\max}\,\frac{\delta}{\delta_c}\exp\!\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\delta_c}\right),\qquad 0\le\delta\le\delta_c
\]

Units: \(\sigma\) in Pa, \(\delta\) in m, \(\sigma_{\max}\) in Pa, \(\delta_c\) in m. The work of separation is exact:

\[
\Gamma=\int_0^{\delta_c}\sigma\,d\delta=\sigma_{\max}\delta_c\,(e-2)\approx 0.718\,\sigma_{\max}\delta_c
\]

with \(\Gamma\) in J·m⁻². **Assumptions:** quasi-static, monotonic, isothermal loading; a single dominant interface; continuum validity at the bond scale. **Breakdown regimes:** (i) \(\delta_c\to 0\) produces a brittle singularity in traction; (ii) cyclic loading invalidates monotonicity, requiring a damage variable \(D\in[0,1]\) with \(\sigma=(1-D)\sigma_{\max}(\delta/\delta_c)e^{1-\delta/\delta_c}\) and \(dD/dN=(\Delta\delta/\delta_c)^m\), \(N\) in cycles; (iii) if \(\sigma_{\max}\) is below the detection floor, the model is unfalsifiable in vivo.

**2. Energy-deficit crisis.** From [PAGE 41]: "an imbalance in energy demand and available resources." Let \(P_{\text{supply}}\) and \(P_{\text{demand}}\) be in W (equivalently mol ATP·s⁻¹ multiplied by the phosphorylation potential in J·mol⁻¹):

\[
P_{\text{net}}(t)=P_{\text{supply}}(t)-P_{\text{demand}}(t),\qquad
T^{*}=\frac{C}{P_{\text{demand}}-P_{\text{supply}}}
\]

with buffer capacity \(C\) in J and endurance time \(T^{*}\) in s. **Assumptions:** constant power over the epoch, single buffer compartment, no task switching. **Breakdown regimes:** \(P_{\text{demand}}\to P_{\text{supply}}^{+}\) gives \(T^{*}\to\infty\) (marginal state, model uninformative); \(P_{\text{supply}}\to 0\) gives catastrophic collapse with no finite \(T^{*}\); and real cognition is intermittent, so the constant-power assumption fails exactly where the thesis reports failure — "If I read for 10 minutes the pain escalates… If 20 minites, unslung syllables" [PAGE 166].

Units: probabilities (dimensionless). **Assumption:** the likelihood \(P(S\mid M)\) is label-invariant while the prior \(P(M\mid L)\) is not. **Breakdown regimes:** \(P(S)\to 0\) (denominator collapse); and base-rate neglect, where the clinician effectively fixes \(P(M\mid L)\) at 0.5 and the label term vanishes.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The thesis supplies its own lineage, and it runs backward from the present definitional mess. [PAGE 13] traces the Edwin Smith Papyrus (c. 1700 BCE, copied from c. 3000–2500 BCE) with "27 case descriptions relating to head and brain injury"; Al Rhazes around 900 CE distinguishing open from closed injury "while maintaining that there could be physiological damage without the presence of a lesion"; Berengario da Carpi in the sixteenth century proposing that the soft brain impacts the hard skull; John Bell in 1795 insisting that "it is the injury of the brain alone that is dangerous" [PAGE 14]. Martland's 1928 "punch drunk syndrome" [PAGE 15] then converts repetitive trauma into a causal claim, even though his microhaemorrhage theory was wrong. The genealogy is retrocausal in the precise sense that each era re-reads prior cases through its own lesion criterion: the Egyptians saw open wounds, the medievals saw commotion, the twentieth century saw microscopy, and the twenty-first sees diffusion tensor metrics [PAGE 33].

The literary genealogy is parallel and equally retrocausal. [PAGE 17] reads *Don Quixote* against Alcázar's *De vulneribus capitis*; [PAGE 18] reads Louisa's personality change in *Persuasion* as a disciplining of the energetic woman; [PAGE 18] reads the knock on the head in *A Connecticut Yankee* as a portal; [PAGE 20] reads shellshock as the moment when injury becomes tragic rather than comic. Each reading retrofits a modern symptom ontology onto an older text.

Multi-week traps follow from this. Trap one: the *restitution default*. [PAGE 86] quotes Frank: "chaos is the opposite of restitution: its plot imagines life never getting better." Because restitution is the marketable arc, a patient at week six who is not improving is reclassified as non-compliant rather than as a slow recoverer — and the thesis records exactly this pressure: "It is hard not to feel pressure to either be inspirational or be silent" [PAGE 67]. Trap two: the *label drift trap*, where the same symptoms are re-described from "post-concussion syndrome" to "outdated concept" between appointments [PAGE 40], so the patient's own record becomes internally contradictory. Trap three: the *citation-monoculture trap* [PAGE 31], where a sport-derived definition becomes the default for non-sport populations, importing return-to-play timelines into falls, crashes, and domestic violence. Trap four: the *historical-diagnosis trap*, where Henry VIII's jousting is retro-diagnosed by headline [PAGE 75], reinforcing the stereotype that brain injury is a large athletic male problem even though [PAGE 16] notes that "75% of all concussions are the result of 'falls, being struck by or against an object, and motor-vehicle crashes.'" Each trap is a multi-week failure mode because it operates on the timescale of appointments and edits, not on the timescale of the injury itself.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under crisis, agents regress to previously reinforced policies rather than executing a freshly computed optimum. The thesis documents three regressions with unusual clarity. The patient regresses to the *good-student policy*: "I want to be a better patient, the best patient, the most patient patient" [PAGE 173], and "I am trained for the standardized test" [PAGE 173]. The clinician regresses to the *checklist policy*, which [PAGE 44] grounds in the literature: "Healthcare practitioners have been shown to use inaccurate prognostic guesses with brain injury patients instead of applying clinically validated tools." The institution regresses to the *triage policy*, where the label determines disposition [PAGE 38].

Formally, treat the encounter as a level-\(k\) recursion truncated by working memory. Let \(B_k(s)\) be the agent's belief at recursion depth \(k\), and let cognitive capacity be bounded by the energy budget in the previous section:

\[
a_{k+1}=\arg\max_{a}\sum_{s}B_k(s)\,U(s,a),\qquad k\le k_{\max}(P_{\text{net}})
\]

**Breakdown regime:** as \(P_{\text{net}}\to 0^{-}\), \(k_{\max}\to 1\), so the patient can model only their own immediate state and cannot model the clinician's model of them. This is not a character flaw; it is a resource constraint, and it is why [PAGE 45] describes a feedback loop: "The more acute memory-related symptoms an individual experiences, the more likely they are to continue to expect poor memory function and to relate that to their injury, which creates a negative feedback loop."

The counter-trap is overcorrection into hyper-advocacy. The thesis names it: "Be a terrier, an acquaintance urges. / Keep after them. / Nip at their heels / you'll get better care" [PAGE 189]. But the same poem undercuts the advice: "But I can't remember my address when I call for an appointment, / my phone number when asked" [PAGE 189]. Hyper-advocacy presupposes intact executive function, which is precisely the impaired faculty.

The clock layer is temporal discounting. Let \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) be the daily discount factor and \(u_t\) the utility of a rehabilitation action:

\[
V=\sum_{t=0}^{T}\gamma^{t}u_{t}
\]

**Breakdown regimes:** \(\gamma\to 1\) produces rumination and the infinite counterfactual ledger — "if I train enough. / if I wait a year, two. / if I get strong enough" [PAGE 234]; \(\gamma\to 0\) produces myopia and the abandonment of slow therapies. The therapeutic window is intermediate \(\gamma\), and it is unstable because pain shifts it within a single day: "pain like I commit / to this dose of tramadol / and move the clock forward" [PAGE 192]. Theory of mind and time preference are therefore coupled: the patient who cannot simulate the clinician's future self also cannot simulate their own.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudication requires an explicit precedence rule. I use: *temporal precedence plus measurement validity outranks narrative coherence*, and *objective biomechanical evidence outranks inferred motive*, but *first-person pain report cannot be vetoed by any imaging result*.

**Physical layer (veto strength: high).** The kinematic and cohesive-zone layers can veto a purely psychological reading of a symptom. If angular deceleration exceeds a tissue threshold, \(\delta\ge\delta_c\), then bond failure is a sufficient mechanism [PAGE 244], and a claim that the symptom is "just stress" is physically underdetermined rather than established. Conversely, the physical layer is vetoed by its own detection limits: because [PAGE 32] states that "no abnormality is seen on standard structural neuroimaging studies," a normal scan cannot veto the injury. The veto is one-directional.

**Biomechanical layer (veto strength: medium-high).** The WFTDA rule history on [PAGE 74] is a natural experiment: the 2013 "no impact/no penalty" clause for secondary head contact was replaced in 2019 by "any forceful contact to the head or neck should be penalised regardless of impact." A model that treats head contact as incidental is vetoed by the rule change and by the reported injury frequency.

**Weather layer (veto strength: low, non-sovereign).** [PAGE 58] lists "barometric pressure" among triggers that "might spark a multiday headache." This is a legitimate physiological candidate via intracranial pressure and vestibular sensitivity, but it has low specificity and is heavily confounded by sleep, screen time, and workload, all listed in the same sentence. The adjudication rule: weather may *modify* a probability but may not *override* a vestibular finding or an energy-budget explanation. Formally, admit the term only if

\[
\Delta \mathrm{AIC}=\mathrm{AIC}_{\text{base}}-\mathrm{AIC}_{\text{base+weather}}>2
\]

in a pre-registered diary model. Otherwise it is a candidate, not a cause.

**First-person layer (veto strength: absolute against erasure).** Woolf's constraint governs: "let a sufferer try to describe a pain in his head to a doctor and language at once runs dry" [PAGE 11]. No cross-layer model may infer the absence of pain from the absence of a marker. The correct output of adjudication is therefore a *conjunction* of layers with explicit unknowns, which is what the thesis argues for at [PAGE 156]: "Brain injury is best understood when discussed from multiple angles, angles which include medicine, the patient experience, and the social and cultural environment."
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
