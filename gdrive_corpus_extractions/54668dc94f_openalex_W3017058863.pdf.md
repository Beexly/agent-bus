At f_action = 5 Hz exactly the action aliases to DC — the limb appears frozen mid-phase, which is worse than a dropout because it produces confident, wrong labels.

**O3. Tracklet construction.** Player i is seen K^i_j times by camera j, giving sequences with timestamps [t^i_{1j}, t^i_{2j}]_k, k ∈ [1, K^i_j] [PAGE 2]. Association reduces to a bipartite assignment
$$\pi^\star=\arg\min_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}}\sum_{a} C\!\left(\tau_a,\tau_{\pi(a)}\right),\qquad C=\lambda_1\bigl(1-\mathrm{IoU}\bigr)+\lambda_2\,d_{\text{app}}+\lambda_3\,g(\Delta t)$$
$$\mathrm{IoU}(B_p,B_{gt})=\frac{|B_p\cap B_{gt}|}{|B_p\cup B_{gt}|}\in[0,1]$$
**Breakdown:** as inter-camera gap Δt ≫ τ_drift (appearance-drift time constant, dominated by illumination change and motion blur), d_app → 1 and precision collapses to chance 1/M — the "multi-week trap" of O7.

**O4. Detection objective and reported performance.** Average precision
$$\mathrm{AP}=\int_0^1 p(r)\,dr,\qquad \mathrm{mAP}=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{c\in C}\mathrm{AP}_c$$
Table 2 [PAGE 3] verbatim values: Faster-RCNN-Inception 51 ms / 0.45 / 0.72 / 0.50; Faster-RCNN-Inception-ResNet (300 proposals) 345 ms / 0.43 / 0.68 / 0.49; (20 proposals) 109 ms / 0.36 / 0.54 / 0.41; RetinaNet 35 ms / 0.20 / 0.45 / 0.14. Table 1 [PAGE 3] digit mAP: 0.57, 0.57, 0.68, 0.61, 0.30, 0.58, 0.47, 0.31, 0.29, 0.51 → mean 0.489, consistent with the stated 0.48.

**O5. Real-time feasibility inequality.** Per-stream inference budget Δ₀ = 100 ms; utilization
$$\rho = N\,f_s\,T_{\text{infer}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho_{\text{FRCNN-Inc}}=N(0.51),\ \ \rho_{\text{RetinaNet}}=N(0.35)$$
Both satisfy ρ < 1 up to N ≈ 1.96 and N ≈ 2.86 single-GPU streams respectively; with batching, both are feasible. **Therefore the speed axis is slack and the selection is decided on the accuracy axis alone** — the paper's "optimal in term of speed and performance" [PAGE 3] is a statement about a *slack* constraint. Breakdown regime: ρ → 1 gives unbounded queue latency (M/M/1 divergence), and the sampler begins dropping frames — the operator of O2 degrades *because* of O5, coupling two failure modes.

**O6. Dempster–Shafer fusion of jersey evidence.** With masses m_k from thumbnail k over the frame of discernment Θ (digit labels 0–9 plus "absent/partial"),
$$m_{1}\oplus m_{2}(A)=\frac{1}{1-\kappa}\sum_{B\cap C=A}m_1(B)\,m_2(C),\qquad \kappa=\sum_{B\cap C=\varnothing}m_1(B)m_2(C)$$
Units: masses are dimensionless probabilities in [0,1]; κ is a dimensionless conflict index. **Exact singularity: κ → 1 ⇒ (1−κ)⁻¹ → ∞**, the fused mass becomes non-normalizable, and the rule returns a spurious certainty. Because [PAGE 3] names "presence of multiple numbers in the thumbnails" as an expected condition, κ is inflated *by construction* whenever two players share a crop. Remedy regime: switch to Yager's rule (transfer conflict to Θ) or a discounted weighted average m̄ = Σ_k w_k m_k with w_k ∝ detection confidence, whenever κ > κ_crit ≈ 0.5.

**O7. Domain-gap functional.** Source (SVHN) risk R_S and target risk R_T bound:
$$R_T(h)\;\le\;R_S(h)+\tfrac{1}{2}d_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{D}_S,\mathcal{D}_T)+\lambda^\star$$
Observed gap [PAGE 3]: 0.92 → 0.48, i.e. ΔmAP = −0.44, attributed by the authors to "the poor quality of our images." Under multi-week operation the target distribution is itself time-varying, D_T(t), so the *estimator* of R_T from a single-week validation set (2,800 images, [PAGE 3]) carries sampling error ≈ √(p(1−p)/n) ≈ √(0.48·0.52/2800) ≈ 0.009 — an order of magnitude smaller than the weekly drift that must be tracked, meaning single-week validation is *statistically adequate but temporally blind*.

**O8. Transfer-depth operator.** Let θ = (θ_feat, θ_head). Classifier-only tuning optimizes over a submanifold:
$$\theta_{\text{head}}^\star=\arg\min_{\theta_{\text{head}}}\mathcal{L}\bigl(f_{\theta_{\text{feat}}^{\text{frozen}}}(x);y\bigr)$$
[PAGE 4]: "we initially fine-tuned the classifier layer only and not the feature extractor but that led to poor performance of the model. We achieved better performance after fine-tuning the feature extractor." **Breakdown regime:** when the frozen feature map's mutual information with the target label, I(φ_frozen(X); Y), is below the task threshold — which is guaranteed when the source is 32×32 grayscale-ish house-number crops and the target is 256×256 motion-blurred, occluded, weather-degraded jersey patches — the head cannot recover the deficit and full or partial unfreezing is mandatory.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Trace the paper's claims backward to their ancestors, then forward into the traps that only appear at multi-week horizon.

**Lineage 1 — Identity from broadcast video.** The player-tracking premise descends from Lu et al. (2013), *Learning to track and identify players from broadcast sports videos*, cited on [PAGE 2]. That work assumed a *single, registered, high-resolution broadcast feed* with a stable camera model. Bhargava & Cuzzolin inherit the task (track + identify) but abandon the assumption (multi-camera, 256×256 crops, irregular sampling). **The genealogical discontinuity is the whole paper**: methods whose correctness proofs assume uniform sampling are being applied to a point process. Retrocausally, the 10 fps dropout regime invalidates a decade of tracking literature's implicit sampling axiom.

**Lineage 2 — Dempster–Shafer.** Sentz, Ferson et al. (2002), cited on [PAGE 3], is the canonical survey and *itself* documents the Zadeh counterexample — the exact κ → 1 pathology that the paper's own setup manufactures. The source cites the survey for the *rule* but not for the *failure mode*. This is a retrocausal blind spot: the paper imported the operator without importing its known singularity.

**Lineage 3 — Detector family.** Faster R-CNN (Ren et al., 2015) and RetinaNet / Focal Loss (Lin et al., 2017), both cited on [PAGE 3], represent two different prior assumptions: region-proposal (sparse, two-stage) versus dense-anchor (one-stage). Dense-anchor methods are *more* sensitive to anchor-scale mismatch — and the paper's per-frame object scale varies enormously ("variable size of the player and the ball depending on their distances from the cameras," [PAGE 4]). Retrocausally, the reported RetinaNet collapse (AP[0.50:0.95] = 0.20, [PAGE 3]) is predicted by anchor–scale mismatch under an *untuned* configuration ("we did not do any parameter tuning," [PAGE 3]), not uniquely by image quality. The genealogy explains the result better than the paper's own stated cause.

**Lineage 4 — Action recognition.** Two-stream fusion (Feichtenhofer et al.), C3D (Tran et al.), I3D/Kinetics (Carreira & Zisserman), and micro-tube regression (Saha, Singh & Cuzzolin) are all cited on [PAGE 2]. Every one of them assumes a fixed-frame-rate clip tensor of shape T×H×W×3 with uniform Δ. Feeding an irregular timestamp set into these architectures silently violates their positional encoding: temporal convolution kernels implicitly encode "frame k = time kΔ." **This is the deepest retrocausal defect** and the paper flags it as open: "It would be interesting to test their applicability on variable frame-rate videos" [PAGE 3].

**Lineage 5 — Benchmarks.** UCF101, SVW, Sports-1M, SoccerNet [PAGE 2]. The paper's sharpest retrocausal question is on [PAGE 4]: "Do the benchmark datasets have enough variability to judge the generalised performance of a model? How do we quantify the amount of real-life variety in benchmark datasets?" This inverts the standard direction of citation: instead of asking whether the model generalizes to data, it asks whether the *benchmark* is a valid instrument.

**Multi-week traps (three, all verifiable):**
- *Trap A — illumination drift.* Over a season, low-light fixtures force longer exposures; digit legibility collapses (see CROSS_LAYER_VETO). A model validated in week 1 degrades monotonically without any code change. Detection: track weekly mAP on a fixed held-out set; a slope significantly below zero with |ΔmAP| > 2× the binomial standard error (≈0.018 for n = 2800) triggers re-calibration.
- *Trap B — kit/roster churn.* Jersey-number fusion depends on a stable number↔identity map. Mid-season transfers and kit alternates break the identifier *before* they break the detector. Symptom: κ rises across matches while per-thumbnail digit mAP stays flat — a pure association-layer failure.
- *Trap C — anchor staleness.* Camera placement changes (new stadium, new gantry height) shift the object-scale distribution; untuned dense anchors silently lose recall for the smallest quintile. Symptom: AP0.50 stays roughly constant while AP0.75 falls — the classic localization-only degradation signature visible in Table 2's spread (0.72 vs 0.50 for the best model) [PAGE 3].

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model three competing decision policies operating on the same analyst, and let them adjudicate.

**Policy 1 — Crisis habit regression (H).** Under a bad number, the reflexive response is the cheapest available intervention: freeze the backbone, retrain the head, add class weights, declare done. The paper documents exactly this reflex and its failure on [PAGE 4]: "we initially fine-tuned the classifier layer only and not the feature extractor but that led to poor performance." The recursion is: the analyst predicts the reviewer will ask "did you fine-tune?" — so the analyst fine-tunes *the thing that is visible*, not the thing that is binding. Habit regression is characterized by **action visibility ≫ action efficacy**. Its signature is an unreported effect size: the paper never quantifies how much worse classifier-only tuning was, which is precisely the tell.

**Policy 2 — Counter-trap (T).** The inverse error: import the benchmark champion because it won elsewhere. RetinaNet is the fastest model in Table 2 (35 ms) and the worst (AP[0.50:0.95] = 0.20) [PAGE 3]. The counter-trap is seductive because it is *falsifiable in the right direction* — it looks like rigorous benchmarking — while being confounded by the paper's own admission of no parameter tuning. A second counter-trap: reading "poor quality images" as the universal explanation and therefore concluding *no* method can work, which licenses inaction. Both traps share a structure: a single-cause narrative applied to a multi-cause failure.

**Policy 3 — Clock (K).** The wall-clock/admissibility constraint. Δ₀ = 100 ms, ρ = N f_s T_infer. The clock's function is *adjudicative*, not predictive: it tells you which arguments are even relevant. Because ρ_FRCNN-Inc = 0.51N and ρ_RetinaNet = 0.35N are both far below 1 for realistic N, the speed axis is slack, and any argument that resolves the model choice on latency is **inadmissible**. The clock therefore *vetoes* Policy 2's strongest rhetorical asset (35 ms) and forces the decision onto accuracy, where Policy 1's cheap intervention is also vetoed by the reported evidence.

**Recursive layering.** Level 0: the analyst's belief over {H, T, K}. Level 1: the analyst's model of the reviewer's belief — the reviewer will ask "is it real-time?" and "did you tune?" Level 2: the analyst's model of the reviewer's model of the *deployment* — will this run for 38 weeks? The correct fixed point is the one where the analyst's action is invariant to the reviewer's question order. Concretely: unfreeze the feature extractor (defeats H), reject the latency argument on ρ grounds (defeats T), and report per-class AP across weeks (satisfies K and the multi-week traps). The paper converges on two of these three — full fine-tuning [PAGE 4] and the benchmark-variability question [PAGE 4] — but never closes the loop on the clock, which is why its model-selection rationale reads as post hoc.

**Clock discipline rule.** Any claim of the form "model X is preferable because it is faster" is admissible only if ρ_X ≥ ρ_crit ≈ 0.8. Below that threshold the latency difference is non-binding and the claim must be restated as an accuracy claim.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A veto is a constraint no amount of model capacity, data, or tuning can overturn, because the required information is absent from the measurement — formally, I(observation; label) → 0.

**Veto 1 — Physical/optical: motion-blur information cutoff.** Blur length in image space
$$b = u\,t_{\exp}\ \ [\mathrm{px}],\qquad u=\text{image-plane speed }[\mathrm{px\,s^{-1}}],\ t_{\exp}[\mathrm{s}]$$
Jersey-digit legibility requires b < h_digit/3. Take a 256-px crop with full-body height ≈ 200 px and digit height h_digit ≈ 18 px ⇒ b_max ≈ 6 px. At u = 400 px s⁻¹ (panning plus player motion), daylight t_exp = 1/1000 s gives b = 0.40 px (safe); overcast t_exp = 1/250 s gives b = 1.6 px (safe); dusk/night t_exp = 1/60 s gives b = 6.7 px (**veto**). The paper names the cause on [PAGE 4]: "the motion blur due to fast movement of players." The veto is *coupled to weather*, which is the point: illumination is not a nuisance variable, it is the parameter that sets t_exp and therefore sets whether O6's Dempster fusion has any evidence to fuse at all. No detector architecture recovers a digit whose high-frequency content has been convolved away.

**Veto 2 — Biomechanical: sub-frame transients.** Contact-and-release events (ball strike, jump takeoff, plant-and-cut) have durations τ ∈ [40, 100] ms. With Δ₀ = 100 ms and P_cap(0.05 s) = 0.393 from O2, the *fastest and most performance-relevant* actions are the *least* observable. Crucially the veto is not "the classifier is wrong" — it is "the phase is not in the sample set." A model trained on such data learns the distribution of *slow* actions and confidently mislabels mid-phase frames captured by chance. This is the mechanism behind the paper's own open question [PAGE 3]: "Some investigation is required to see if the existing methods can capture the fast actions with this frame-rate as well." Answer: they cannot, and it is provable from λ alone.

**Veto 3 — Weather/atmospheric: contrast collapse.** Under scattering,
$$I(x)=J(x)\,t(x)+A\bigl(1-t(x)\bigr),\qquad t(x)=e^{-\beta d}$$
with β the extinction coefficient [m⁻¹] and d the camera–player distance [m]. Digit legibility requires local contrast above ≈ 10% of full scale; with A ≈ 0.8 (overcast sky radiance) and J ≈ 0.2 (dark kit), the required transmittance is t ≳ 0.17 ⇒ βd ≲ 1.77. For a stadium-scale d = 80 m, that permits β ≲ 0.022 m⁻¹ — exceeded in rain/fog. The paper's own list on [PAGE 4] includes "noise due to weather conditions," and this is the only interpretation under which weather is a *veto* rather than a *nuisance*: past βd ≈ 1.77 the digit signal is below the noise floor, and the observation operator's channel capacity for the identity task is zero.

**Adjudication.** Order of precedence: Veto 2 (temporal) ≻ Veto 1 (optical) ≻ Veto 3 (atmospheric), because the temporal veto removes events, whereas the other two degrade events that are still present. Cross-layer coupling: Veto 3 forces larger t_exp, which feeds Veto 1; and Veto 2's dropout rate inflates Δt, which feeds the tracklet association breakdown in O3. **The three vetoes are not independent — they form a single cascade rooted in the sensor specification, not in the learning algorithm.** That is the strongest possible statement of a veto: changing the model changes nothing; changing f_s, t_exp, or optical path length changes everything.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario specifies an intervention, a falsifiable prediction, and a verification procedure that returns a scalar reward without human judgment.

**Veto-1 (temporal Nyquist).** *Intervention:* re-capture a fixed set of S actions at f_s' = 25 s⁻¹ (Δ' = 40 ms) while holding cameras, optics, players, and light fixed. *Prediction from O2:* for contact-phase actions (τ = 0.05 s), capture probability rises from 1 − e^{−0.5} = 0.393 to 1 − e^{−1.25} = 0.713, a factor of 1.814; for takeoff-phase actions (τ = 0.20 s), from 0.865 to 0.993. *Falsifier:* any measured capture ratio outside [1.6, 2.0] for the contact-phase stratum refutes the Poisson model and forces a bursty (non-Poisson) arrival model. *Verification metric:* fraction of annotated action intervals containing ≥1 frame, computed by timestamp lookup only — no model in the loop.

**Veto-2 (Dempster conflict singularity).** *Intervention:* construct adversarial thumbnail sets in which two players' jerseys co-occur in one crop (the paper's own stated condition, [PAGE 3]) at increasing mixing fraction α ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5}. *Prediction from O6:* κ increases monotonically with α and the fused posterior becomes bimodal with mass concentrating on the two present numbers; at α ≥ α_crit ≈ 0.4 the fused top-1 accuracy falls below the single-best-thumbnail baseline, i.e. **fusion becomes worse than doing nothing**. *Falsifier:* if fused accuracy never drops below the best-single-thumbnail baseline, κ is not the binding variable and the aggregation layer is safe as-is. *Verification metric:* κ (dimensionless, computed in closed form) plus top-1 accuracy of the fused posterior versus the argmax-single-thumbnail baseline — both deterministic given the mass assignments.

**Veto-3 (transfer-depth / frozen-backbone).** *Intervention:* train two identical pipelines on the same 10,500-image training split [PAGE 3], differing only in whether the feature extractor is frozen. *Prediction from O8 and the source's own report* [PAGE 4]: the frozen-feature variant's validation mAP falls materially below the fully-tuned variant, with the largest per-class deficits on the visually hardest digit classes — specifically classes 4, 8, and 9, which are the three lowest in Table 1 (0.30, 0.29, 0.31) and therefore the classes with the fewest effective training signal. *Falsifier:* if the frozen variant matches the tuned variant within the binomial noise band (≈ ±0.018 at n = 2,800, p ≈ 0.5), the paper's transfer-depth claim is not reproducible and the stated cause of the initial failure is wrong. *Verification metric:* paired ΔmAP on an identical, held-out 2,800-image validation split, reported per class.

**Supplementary latency veto (admissibility check).** *Intervention:* none — a pure computation. Evaluate ρ = N f_s T_infer for N = 8: ρ_FRCNN-Inc = 4.08, ρ_RetinaNet = 2.80. *Prediction:* both exceed 1 on a single GPU at 8 cameras, so **neither model is real-time at full deployment scale despite the 100 ms per-frame budget being individually satisfied**; batching or multi-GPU is mandatory, and the "RetinaNet is faster" argument becomes admissible only inside the batched regime, where the 16 ms delta is amortized. *Falsifier:* measured end-to-end throughput ≥ 80 thumbnails s⁻¹ on one device for either model. *Verification metric:* sustained frames-per-second measured over a 60 s window, not per-frame latency.
