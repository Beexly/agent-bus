<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Stature S is bounded: skeletal biology imposes S_min ≈ 24 in (newborn) and S_max ≈ 108 in (extreme adult gigantism), with diurnal spinal compression of roughly 1–2 cm and age-related shrinkage in older cohorts. Galton’s Gaussian error model, implicit in b = PD/√2 [PAGE 129], assigns non-zero probability to S < 0 and S > S_max. With b ≈ 1.10 inch and a plausible σ of order 2–3 in, the central fit is sound, but the model’s unbounded tail is a physical veto for any application involving extremes (e.g., eligibility thresholds, sports anthropometry, or clinical growth charts). Veto condition: if P(S ∉ [S_min, S_max]) under the fitted normal is non-negligible for the subpopulation, the model is physically inadmissible outside the central range.

Biomechanical layer. Brothers within a fraternity share genetics, nutrition, and childhood environment; they are not independent draws [PAGE 129]. Method (4), “Pairs of Brothers taken at random” [PAGE 129], violates exchangeability unless the within-fraternity correlation ρ is explicitly modeled. Ignoring ρ shrinks the standard error of b artificially and inflates Type I error. Veto condition: if the intraclass correlation ρ̂ > 0, the i.i.d. derivation of b = PD/√2 is invalid; a mixed-effects or family-clustered estimator is required.

Weather layer. Stature measurement in Galton’s setting is indoor anthropometry; ambient weather has no direct causal effect on the length value in inches within a single measurement session. Absence of weather covariates is therefore not a model flaw. However, if data collection spanned seasons with systematic differences in shoe-wearing, posture, or time-of-day measurement, a seasonal confounding layer could exist. Veto condition: if measurement date correlates with stature residual at |r| > 0.05, add a season/time-of-day fixed effect; otherwise the weather veto is null.

Cross-layer adjudication rule. A statistical estimate b is accepted only if (i) the Gaussian central-fit residuals pass a goodness-of-fit test (e.g., Anderson–Darling p > 0.05), (ii) the predicted tail mass outside [S_min, S_max] is below a tolerance (e.g., < 10⁻⁶), and (iii) the within-fraternity correlation is either modeled or shown negligible. On [PAGE 129], methods (1)–(3) condition on fraternity structure and are therefore biomechanically more defensible than method (4)’s random pairs. Method (3)’s stability across Special (1.10) and R.F.F. (1.14) [PAGE 129] makes it the least veto-prone estimator, because variability of regression is less sensitive to the mean-level selection biases that afflict methods (1), (2), and (4).

Prediction: under homoscedasticity and missing-completely-at-random small families, the inclusive estimate b_incl lies within [1.00, 1.15] inch.
