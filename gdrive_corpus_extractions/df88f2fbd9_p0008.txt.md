Claimed stationary solution: r = [14/39, 10/39, 15/39]^T, Σ r_i = 1.

Random-surfer iteration [PAGE 5]:
p_1 = A p_0
p_2 = A p_1 = A^2 p_0
p_∞ = lim_{k→∞} A^k p_0, where p_∞ is the dominant eigenvector of A.
Normalization constraint: ||p_k||_1 = 1 ∀ k.

Transition matrix decomposition [PAGE 6]:
A = α (1/N) 11^T + (1−α) B,
where 11^T is the N×N all-ones matrix, α ∈ [0,1] is the random-jump probability, and B is the link-transition matrix:
B[i][j] = { 1/n_i if a forward link i→j exists; 0 otherwise },
with n_i = total forward links from node i. Units: all entries dimensionless probabilities in [0,1]; N is a dimensionless node count; k is a dimensionless iteration count.

Damping interpretation [PAGE 6]: (1−α) limits rank inheritance by children; α ≈ 0.15 models user teleportation after a few links. Logarithmic rank [PAGE 5–6]: compresses orders-of-magnitude dynamic range so that each unit increase corresponds to one order of magnitude above the minimum.

Singularity / breakdown regimes:
1. Dangling-node singularity: n_i = 0 ⇒ row i of B is all zeros ⇒ A is not row-stochastic ⇒ probability mass leaks; requires removal/reinsertion protocol.
2. α = 0 with cyclic graph and k ≫ 1: no teleportation ⇒ chain may be periodic ⇒ no unique p_∞; rank mass circulates in loops.
3. α = 1: pure teleportation ⇒ A = (1/N)11^T ⇒ rank independent of link structure; link signal annihilated.
4. Disconnected/reducible graph: multiple closed communicating classes ⇒ multiple stationary distributions; Perron–Frobenius uniqueness fails.
5. Norm drift: ||p_k||_1 ≠ 1 ⇒ p_k is not a probability distribution; cross-node rank comparison invalid.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week traps.

The PageRank disclosure sits at the intersection of three lineages. (1) Markov-chain theory: Markov (1906–1913) established that p_{t+1} = P p_t converges to a stationary distribution under regularity conditions; the patent’s p_{k+1} = A p_k is a direct instantiation. (2) Perron–Frobenius spectral theory (1907, 1912): guarantees a unique dominant positive eigenvector for strictly positive stochastic matrices, which is precisely why the damping term α(1/N)11^T is added—to make A strictly positive and thus primitive. (3) Eigenvector centrality and citation analysis: Bonacich (1972) defined node prestige as proportional to neighbors’ prestige; Garfield’s citation impact work (1950s onward) treated references as votes. Page, Brin, Motwani, and Winograd (Stanford, US 6,285,999 B1) combined these into the random-surfer model with explicit damping and teleportation.

Multi-week traps arise because the web graph is not static on the iteration timescale. The patent assumes a fixed graph during the ~100-iteration sweep [PAGE 5], but link creation, deletion, and spam-campaign injection occur on weekly or faster cycles. A p_∞ computed at week t is a stale proxy by week t+1 if edge velocity is high. The damping factor α ≈ 0.15 [PAGE 6] imposes a fixed 15% reset per step but contains no temporal-decay term for edge age; old links and new links are weighted identically in B. Link farms can engineer synthetic cycles that capture dominant eigenvector mass within weeks, and the patent’s counter-traps (damping, iteration cap, dangling-node removal) are reactive: spammers observe the stabilization behavior and adapt link velocity to mimic natural convergence. Absent any explicit time-decay or recency operator in the disclosed equations, multi-week drift between recomputations is an unmodeled gray zone that degrades ranking validity independent of iteration count.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

The recursive theory of mind here concerns how the system models the “random surfer” and how operators regress under ranking crises. The patent posits a surfer with finite patience: after following links, the surfer jumps randomly with probability α ≈ 15% [PAGE 6]. This is a behavioral theory of mind—users get bored and teleport—but it is asserted rather than empirically parameterized.

Crisis habit regression: when ranking instability, spam injection, or loop concentration is detected, the operator habitually regresses to two levers: increasing the iteration count toward the “100 iterations” ceiling [PAGE 5], or raising α to force faster mixing. Each regression temporarily restores apparent stability but raises computational cost and, if α is raised too far, washes out link-structure signal.

Counter-trap: the damping factor and dangling-node removal are counter-traps against rank monopolization by artificial cycles and probability-mass bleed. They create a second-order trap: over-damping (α too high) destroys the very eigenvector signal the algorithm seeks; under-damping (α too low) permits loop concentration. The operator must continuously tune between these attractors as the adversarial graph evolves.

Clock: the ~100-iteration convergence clock sets temporal resolution. Fewer iterations (e.g., two or more, as the patent notes can yield “valuable” information [PAGE 5]) speed the clock but sacrifice proximity to p_∞; more iterations improve accuracy but increase staleness because the graph mutates during computation. The random-surfer model thus embeds a clocked, recursive agent model: the surfer’s patience sets α, the operator’s crisis response sets k, and the graph’s weekly mutation rate determines whether the computed p_∞ remains a valid map of importance by the time it is consumed.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

Physical-layer veto: exact storage of A requires O(N^2) memory. For millions of documents, a dense N×N matrix is infeasible; sparse row storage is mandatory. Memory and bandwidth ceilings therefore veto exact eigenvector decomposition and mandate power iteration on sparse links. A hardware failure that loses a row of A vetoes column-stochasticity for affected nodes.

Biomechanical-layer veto: human navigation is not memoryless. Dwell time, back-button usage, direct URL entry, and session breaks violate the first-order Markov assumption encoded in A. The α ≈ 0.15 teleportation prior is an unverified assumption about user boredom, not a biomechanical law. If real surfer jump probability differs systematically from 15%, the computed stationary distribution misestimates true attention flow.

Weather-layer veto: meteorological weather has no direct effect on the mathematical convergence of A^k p_0. However, data-center “weather”—cooling failure, power fluctuation, or network partition—can veto physical computation. A network partition effectively disconnects the graph, producing reducible A with multiple closed classes and hence multiple stationary distributions, invalidating the unique-rank premise.

Adjudication hierarchy: mathematical vetoes (n_i = 0 singularity, reducibility, ||p||_1 drift) take precedence because they destroy the probability interpretation entirely. Physical-resource vetoes override exactness, forcing sparse approximation. Biomechanical and environmental vetoes are deployment constraints; they do not alter the formal convergence conditions but bound the domain in which the computed rank is meaningful.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 — Dangling-node retention: Childless pages are left in B during iteration instead of being removed. Because n_i = 0 produces a zero row, A is not row-stochastic and total probability mass is not conserved. Prediction: ||p_k||_1 decays geometrically below 1; after reinsertion, dangling nodes receive inflated rank relative to linked nodes because mass has leaked from the linked component. Verification: audit row sums of A (must equal 1) and log ||p_k||_1 each iteration; a monotone drop below unity confirms the veto.

Veto scenario 2 — Zero damping with deep iteration on a cyclic graph: α = 0 and k ≫ 1 on a graph containing directed cycles. Without teleportation, A may be periodic or reducible, with multiple eigenvalues on the unit circle. Prediction: p_k does not converge to a unique p_∞; instead rank mass oscillates or accumulates in cycles, producing loop-dominated ranks inconsistent with the random-surfer interpretation. Verification: compute the spectral radius ρ(A) and the second-largest eigenvalue modulus; if ρ(A) = 1 with |λ_2| ≈ 1 on a non-primitive A, power iteration fails to settle.

Veto scenario 3 — Omitted L1 renormalization: The norm-reset step “norm of p_n must be made equal to 1 after each iteration” [PAGE 5] is skipped. Prediction: ||p_k||_1 drifts exponentially from 1 (decay if ρ(A) < 1, explosion if ρ(A) > 1). After O(log(1/ε)) iterations, component-wise ranks lose comparability and the logarithmic transform is applied to a non-probability vector. Verification: record ||p_k||_1 per iteration in power iteration; any persistent deviation from 1.0 confirms the veto and invalidates the output rank scale.
