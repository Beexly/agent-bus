<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The patented chip-then-detect architecture descends from a deep lineage of visual attention and region-of-interest methods. Ancestors include foveated imaging and retinal ganglion sampling concepts from the 1960s, scale-space theory (Lindeberg, 1994), early region-of-interest coding, object proposal pipelines such as Selective Search and R-CNN, local feature detectors (SIFT, Harris corners), and modern pose estimation systems (Deformable Parts Models, OpenPose, HRNet). The disclosed contribution is the insertion of a deterministic cropping stage before keypoint detection, justified purely by the reduction in pixel count processed by the expensive detector Φ.

Multi-week operational traps. Over weeks of deployment, the assumptions encoded on [PAGE 15] erode. First, "known in advance" object types (players, ball in a football match) fail when the camera covers a different sport or an unfamiliar object class; a person-specific pose estimator will then be routed an invalid topology. Second, template matching drifts as kits, ball designs, field markings, or lighting conditions change seasonally. Third, camera repositioning or zoom changes invalidate fixed bounding-box priors, causing the chip Π_(b_k) to truncate the target. Fourth, model staleness: a detector trained on clear-day imagery degrades under rain, fog, or low-light without a recalibration trigger. Fifth, sensor aging (vignetting, hot pixels) changes chip statistics unevenly across the field of view, so a chip that was valid at installation may later fall below the detector's contrast operating point. These traps are multi-week because they emerge from slow environmental and sensor drift rather than single-frame failure.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. When step S3008 returns few or no keypoints, a monolithic implementation is prone to regress to full-frame pose estimation as a fallback habit. Because full-frame processing is exactly the costly path the chip was designed to avoid, this regression can push T_proc beyond the frame period T_frame, causing dropped frames and queue backpressure precisely under the load conditions where real-time performance is most needed.

Counter-trap. If the detector locks onto an occluded limb, a background edge, or a misclassified object, the chip recenters on the erroneous location in the next frame. The system then reinforces a wrong topology: keypoints are detected on the error region, the crop shrinks around that error, and subsequent frames confirm the hallucinated structure. This is a positive-feedback trap with no metacognitive check that the detected keypoint graph corresponds to the assumed object class.

Clock. Let T_frame be the camera frame period. If T_proc(S3008) + T_proc(S3010 overhead) > T_frame, the pipeline cannot sustain the input rate. The disclosure's "substantially real time" assertion [PAGE 15] provides no bound on T_proc relative to T_frame, so the clock can silently expire under load.

Recursive theory of mind. The method implicitly models the operator as knowing the object class before keypoint selection. If that model is wrong — for example, a ball is routed to the person-specific branch — the detector imposes human limb topology (arms, legs, torso, shoulders [PAGE 15]) on a spherical object. The system has no stated mechanism to detect that the resulting keypoint graph is physically incompatible with a ball, so it persists in a false belief about the scene structure.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto adjudication stack must evaluate three independent layers before accepting output from step S3008.

Physical layer (optics and sensor): Motion blur β_blur measured in pixels exceeds the characteristic limb width w_limb in pixels; defocus circle of confusion exceeds chip resolution; or sensor noise raises local variance above the detector operating point. Veto predicate v_phys = 1 if β_blur > w_limb or SNR < γ_min. Units: pixels for spatial blur; dimensionless for SNR.

Biomechanical layer: The detected keypoint graph G_k = (V_k, E_k) must satisfy human kinematic constraints. Joint angles θ_j must lie within physiological limits [θ_min, θ_max], and segment-length ratios ρ_ij must fall within biologically plausible bounds. A detected graph with a knee bending backward or a limb longer than the torso violates the biomechanical model. Veto predicate v_bio = 1 if any θ_j ∉ [θ_min, θ_max] or any ρ_ij outside [ρ_min, ρ_max]. Units: radians for angles; dimensionless for ratios.

Environmental/weather layer: Rain, fog, snow, or low illumination reduces contrast within the chip. If local contrast falls below the threshold required by Φ, or if precipitation introduces structured noise correlated with edges, the keypoint locations become unreliable. Veto predicate v_env = 1 if contrast C_chip < C_crit or precipitation mask density p_precip > p_crit. Units: dimensionless contrast and areal fraction.

Adjudication: The composite veto is V = v_phys ∨ v_bio ∨ v_env. If V = 1, output from S3008 is suppressed; the system falls back to full-frame reprocessing or flags the frame for manual review. This cross-layer proof ensures that no single layer (e.g., a clean optics path) can override a biomechanical or environmental failure.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
