If this inequality holds, \(g_c\) maintains itself through self-reinforcement—a true functional autonomy [PAGE 110]. If \(g_c < \Theta_{inh}\), the system undergoes total extinction.

Information/probability framing: the surprise of non-reward on trial \(n\) is \(I_n = -\log_2(p_{\text{reward}})\) bits. As \(p_{\text{reward}} \to 0\), \(I_n \to \infty\), driving inhibitory accumulation. No SI mechanical units apply; the model operates in trial counts and associative HU.

Assumptions: (a) homogeneous trials; (b) monotonic weakening of \(g_c\) as downstream links decay; (c) no spontaneous recovery modeled in this passage; (d) generalized extinction effects scale with trial immediacy.

Breakdown/singularity regimes: \(g_c \to 0\) produces finite-trial collapse; \(g_c \ge \Theta_{self}\) produces a pathological fixed point consistent with Mowrer's neurotic anxiety interpretation [PAGE 110]. The text explicitly vetoes the claim that secondary reinforcement "will necessarily suffer experimental extinction when the support of the primary need reduction is withdrawn" unless conditioning was sufficiently profound [PAGE 110].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage on [PAGE 110] traces backward through Hull's own earlier work and forward into clinical behaviorism. Pavlov's Conditioned Reflexes (1927) [ref 7] established the conditioned-reflex foundation. Hull's 1931 "Goal attraction and directing ideas conceived as habit phenomena" [ref 4, pp. 497-499] converts goal-directedness into a habit-chain mechanism, while his 1932 goal-gradient hypothesis [ref 5] quantifies how goal proximity strengthens approach. Bugelski's 1938 extinction-with-sub-goal study [ref 1] provides the empirical observation that secondary goal reinforcement retards but does not prevent extinction. Allport's concept of functional autonomy names the clinical endpoint: a conditioned reaction that persists after its original motive is withdrawn. Mowrer's 1939 stimulus-response analysis of anxiety [ref 6] imports Freudian anxiety into this Hullian frame, suggesting neurotic symptoms can become self-reinforcing. Skinner's 1938 Behavior of Organisms [ref 9] offers a parallel operant tradition, though Hull's drive-harmony formalism remains distinct.

Multi-week traps: In applied settings—token economies, habit-reversal protocols, or secondary-reinforcement classroom systems—behavior maintained by conditioned \(g_c\) may appear stable for weeks while primary contingency is faded. The text warns this stability is illusory unless original conditioning was profound. The trap is temporal: observers mistake delay for permanence. Because backward collapse occurs "as trial follows trial," a system can look robust during acquisition and then disintegrate rapidly once the terminal primary reinforcer is removed, especially under massed (immediate succession) trials where generalized extinction accumulates fastest [PAGE 110].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: when an organism faces acute primary need arousal (hunger, threat, pain), control regresses from the secondary chain \(S_s \to s_s \to g\) back to direct \(G\)-directed responding. The conditioned goal reaction \(g_c\) is weaker than \(g_u\) [PAGE 110], so under stress the organism abandons the longer, weaker secondary path for the shorter, stronger primary path.

Counter-trap: an external analyst or clinician observes secondary behavior persisting through low-stress acquisition and infers that the habit is stable or autonomous. This is a false positive. The text shows that secondary maintenance is parasitic on primary need reduction; once the crisis withdraws primary support, the habit chain enters backward collapse. The observer's error is ignoring the \(|g_c| < |g_u|\) inequality and assuming that persistence during reinforcement equals persistence after withdrawal.

Clock: extinction is not instantaneous. The text describes a "gradual collapse ... as trial follows trial" [PAGE 110]. The state variable is \(n\), the number of unreinforced presentations. If an intervention, re-exposure to primary reinforcement, or environmental change occurs before \(n\) reaches the backward-collapse threshold, the secondary behavior can resurge (spontaneous recovery or resurgence). The clock is therefore the trial count \(n\), and the critical regime is whether \(n\) has crossed the point where \(H_1\) hits zero. A system observed at \(n=5\) may look identical to one at \(n=50\), but their distance to extinction differs enormously.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: no neural substrate can maintain \(g_c\) indefinitely without metabolic support. The text's claim that a sufficiently conditioned \(g_c\) may withstand "an indefinitely large number of otherwise unreinforced presentations" [PAGE 110] is bounded by biological reality; indefinite self-reinforcement without primary need reduction implies infinite regress of neural firing with no energy input, which is physically impossible. Any model predicting perpetual secondary-only maintenance is vetoed.

Biomechanical layer veto: response emission \(R_k\) requires muscular activation and incurs fatigue. Repeated unreinforced trials generate inhibitory after-effects that summate with generalized extinction effects [PAGE 110]. The biomechanical cost of emitting the chain without reward accelerates \(H_k \to 0\), making the backward recursion faster than a purely cognitive decay model would predict.

Weather/environmental layer veto: the \(S_s \to s_s\) contiguity depends on stable stimulus presentation. Volatile or delayed secondary stimuli break the association \(S_s \to s_s \to g\), reducing \(\gamma g_c\) toward zero. Under environmental volatility, the secondary reinforcement term in the extinction equation vanishes, and the system reverts to primary extinction rate \(\lambda_{ext}\), guaranteeing collapse.

Veto proof from [PAGE 110]: the text states that secondary reinforcement "will nevertheless not suffice to prevent ultimate extinction" unless the conditioned connection is "very strong" [PAGE 110]. Therefore, any universal claim that secondary reinforcement is sufficient to maintain behavior independent of primary support is empirically vetoed by the source itself. The only admissible exception is the narrow functional-autonomy regime \(g_c \ge \Theta_{self}\).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios derived from [PAGE 110], each with a falsifiable prediction.

Veto Scenario 1 — Weak primary conditioning, strong secondary appearance.
Setup: A chain is established with minimal primary need-reduction pairing, so \(g_c\) is weak relative to \(g_u\). Primary reinforcement is then withdrawn while secondary stimuli continue to be presented unreinforced.
Veto: Because \(|g_c| < |g_u|\) and no functional autonomy threshold is crossed, secondary reinforcement cannot prevent extinction [PAGE 110].
Prediction: \(H_k(n)\) decays to zero within a finite number of trials \(N\), where \(N\) is inversely related to \(\lambda_{ext}\) and directly related to initial \(H_0\); no plateau or self-sustaining fixed point will appear.

Veto Scenario 2 — Massed versus spaced unreinforced trials.
Setup: Two matched chains receive identical secondary reinforcement histories, then enter extinction. Group A receives trials in immediate succession; Group B receives spaced trials.
Veto: Hull attributes accelerated inhibition to generalized extinction effects "especially where the trials are given in immediate succession" [PAGE 110]. Massed presentation vetoes the prediction of equal extinction rates.
Prediction: Group A's entire sequence collapses in fewer trials than Group B; backward recursion reaches chain link 1 sooner, and the terminal response \(R_4\) extinguishes before the spaced condition shows comparable decline.

Veto Scenario 3 — Alleged functionally autonomous neurotic symptom.
Setup: A clinical symptom is claimed to be maintained by self-reinforcement (Allport/Mowrer regime) after the original anxiety-provoking contingency is removed.
Veto: Functional autonomy obtains only if primary reinforcement was "sufficiently profound" to make \(g_c\) "very strong" [PAGE 110]. If the original conditioning was weak, the symptom is not autonomous.
Prediction: Under sustained unreinforced exposure, symptoms with weak original conditioning will extinguish according to the backward-collapse recursion. Only symptoms meeting \(g_c \ge \Theta_{self}\) will persist beyond the generalized-extinction horizon; persistence in weakly conditioned cases falsifies the autonomy claim.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
