Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 (resolution veto): If effective HMD resolution is constrained to \(R_{\text{HMD}} \approx 667 \times 375\) px because the smartphone screen is split for binocular projection [PAGE 6], then fine brand-attribute discrimination (e.g., “refined,” “individual,” “perfect”) cannot exceed screen-based planar projection. Verifiable prediction: in a replication with identical content, the VR condition will not show significantly higher approval than the screen condition on any detail-dependent attribute; it may match or fall below, consistent with the source where the screen group led on “confident” plus seven items while VR led only on “athletic” [PAGE 14]. Verification: two-proportion z-test on item-level approval, \(\alpha=0.05\), pre-registered.

Veto scenario 2 (vestibular veto): If a 360° training video contains camera angular velocity above the tolerated threshold (strong movement/deviation from horizontal [PAGE 6–7]), then cybersickness hazard \(h_{\text{ms}}\) exceeds \(h_{\text{crit}}\) and session attrition rises. Verifiable prediction: intensive athletes exposed to high-motion camera segments will show elevated Borg RPE relative to heart rate and higher dropout than a low-motion or no-video control; casual athletes will not show a commensurate motivation gain because the sickness confound dominates [PAGE 17–18]. Verification: mixed-effects model of RPE with fixed effects for motion class and athlete tier, random intercept for subject.

Veto scenario 3 (expertise-interference veto): If subjects have high domain-specific training frequency (≥3 sessions/week) and calibrated self-perception, then adding a presence-inducing HMD produces interference between virtual presence and physical self-perception, inverting the expected motivation effect. Verifiable prediction: in the intensive-athlete cell, the treatment group will report RPE that is dissociated from measured HR (subjectively harder or easier than objective), and SIMS motivation will not differ from control [PAGE 17–18]; in the casual-athlete cell, the treatment group will report subjectively easier training than the control [PAGE 16–17]. Verification: Bland–Altman analysis of RPE vs. HR and an interaction term (treatment × athlete tier) on SIMS gain score.
