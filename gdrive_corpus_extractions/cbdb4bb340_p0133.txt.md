Scenario 1: Wage spike with empty training pipeline.
Counterfactual: The market wage for aviators rises above \underline{w}(σ) (the minimum price equal to alternative earnings) because of a sudden demand surge. Standard elasticity models predict quantity supplied will increase.
RLVR veto: If N_{trained} = 0 in the relevant geographic market (all certified pilots are already employed or grounded), then Q_s^L(P; σ) = 0 for all finite P in the short run. The price may rise without bound but no transactions occur until the training lag τ elapses.
Verifiable prediction: In labor-market data, the wage for a specialized occupation can increase by more than 20% quarter-over-quarter while employment in that occupation remains flat or declines, provided the supply of new certified entrants is zero. Verification requires comparing wage growth to employment growth in occupations with licensing bottlenecks (e.g., commercial pilots) over a 3-month window.

Scenario 2: Land rent increase with amenity substitution.
Counterfactual: Rent on agricultural land rises, and conventional supply theory predicts an increase in acreage offered to cultivation.
RLVR veto: Applying the footnote logic [PAGE 118], if R crosses a threshold \tilde{R} where the marginal non-pecuniary utility of amenity use exceeds the marginal cultivation profit (\partial u_a/\partial A_a + \partial \eta/\partial A_a > \partial u_c/\partial A_c), landowners withdraw acreage from cultivation. Supply of land to marketable crops decreases as rent rises.
Verifiable prediction: In high-amenity counties, cropped acreage is negatively correlated with land rent over periods when suburban or recreational demand is rising. Verification requires parcel-level data showing conversion of cultivated land to lawns, gardens, or preserves concurrent with rising assessed rents.

Scenario 3: Derived-demand collapse despite abundant factor supply.
Counterfactual: Diamond cutters are available at wages below their reservation price, and factor supply is plentiful.
RLVR veto: Because demand for productive services is derived [PAGE 118], if the output price P_y falls to zero (e.g., jewelry demand collapses), then P_f = MPL \cdot P_y = 0 regardless of factor supply. No quantity of diamond cutting services is demanded at any positive factor price.
Verifiable prediction: During an output-demand shock, employment in a factor-specific occupation falls to zero even when factor wages are offered below historical reservation levels, because the marginal revenue product is nil. Verification requires observing factor employment and wages in a narrowly defined specialized occupation during an output-market collapse (e.g., industrial diamond cutters during a luxury-goods demand shock).
