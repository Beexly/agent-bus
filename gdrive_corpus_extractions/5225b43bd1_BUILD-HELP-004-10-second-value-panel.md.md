If the design system cannot guarantee CR_effective under measured glare, the panel is vetoed for that environment until a high-contrast variant exists. Pixel density and viewing distance also affect legibility of small trust notes.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical. Touch targets must be W_t ≥ 44 dp with D ≥ 8 dp spacing. Thumb-zone reach, motor-tremor tolerance, and vestibular safety all fall here. If the media slot contains autoplay video or parallax and prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is active without a static fallback or pause control, the variant is vetoed [PAGE 1]. Users with vestibular disorders or motor impairments cannot be expected to compensate through effort; the design must absorb the mismatch.

Layer 3 — Cognitive / linguistic / legal. Working memory k ≤ C_wm ≈ 4 ± 1; prose budget W ≤ W_max ≈ 31 words; reading rate r ≈ 4.17–5.0 words/s; copy must sound human and respect method boundaries [PAGE 1]. Missing responsible-play-safe disclosure on promotions/risk surfaces is an independent veto [PAGE 1].

Adjudication order. Safety and accessibility vetoes (contrast, touch-target size, reduced motion, responsible-play disclosures) override product and launch-gate considerations. If any layer fails, the component is removed or hidden behind a feature flag; rollback requires no data or billing changes [PAGE 1]. Weather-specific veto: if field testing in high-glare conditions shows CR_effective < 4.5:1 despite maximum design-system contrast, launch is vetoed until a compliant high-contrast variant ships. The veto proof is therefore not a single check but a conjunctive requirement across physical, biomechanical, and legal layers; failure in any one layer collapses the entire acceptance predicate.

Scenario 1 — Localization length counterfactual. If the panel is localized into German or French and copy expansion causes W > W_max such that t_read > 10 s at locale-specific reading rates, then veto launch for that locale. Verifiable prediction: German variants exceed the English word budget by roughly 30–50% and will fail the mobile readability check unless copy is rewritten or fluid typography is applied; the English source variant passes P(C | t ≤ 10 s) ≥ η.

Scenario 2 — Motion-sensitivity counterfactual. If the media slot contains an autoplay video or parallax background and the user's system has prefers-reduced-motion: reduce set, but no static fallback or pause control is implemented, then veto the motion-enabled variant. Verifiable prediction: analytics will show elevated exit rates and reduced next-action completion for motion-sensitive users; the static-fallback variant maintains P(C | t ≤ 10 s) ≥ η and passes the reduced-motion check in the test plan [PAGE 1].

Scenario 3 — Method-boundary / fake-certainty counterfactual. If panel copy includes proprietary GSE methodology or frames a risky or promotional outcome with implied guaranteed results (fake certainty), then legal and compliance veto the copy regardless of comprehension metrics. Verifiable prediction: copy audit flags disclosure language on promotions/risk surfaces [PAGE 1]; an unrevised variant cannot launch, while a responsible-play-safe rewrite restores compliance and allows the panel to ship under the feature-flag rollback contract [PAGE 1].
