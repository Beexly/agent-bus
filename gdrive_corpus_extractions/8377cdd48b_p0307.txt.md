Tolman extends the infant logic to “older children and even… adults” [PAGE 307], but adult eating lacks the dense, immediate suck–milk–satiation contingency. The organism remains orally generalized yet metabolically unquiesced, producing cyclic craving without the docility Tolman describes.

Multi-week trap 3 — Critical-period constraint on bottle acceptance. If bottle introduction occurs after the oral-tactual sensitive period (roughly the first months of life), the prior precision on breast is high and ∂H[g]/∂N for bottle is small. The same number of forced feedings produces far less g-updating in an older infant than in a neonate, creating an age-dependent asymmetry in docility that Tolman’s text does not explicitly parameterize but which is implicit in his “still later” fixation staging [PAGE 307].

2. Biomechanical veto. Sucking requires coordinated suck–swallow–breathe cycles. Let q_flow be the milk delivery rate of the object (mL/s) and q_max the infant’s maximum safe intraoral flow. Veto condition: q_flow > q_max ⇒ aspiration risk ⇒ motor inhibition of sucking regardless of g(o). Bottle nipples often deliver at different q_flow than the breast; this biomechanical mismatch explains initial refusal independent of nutritional content.

3. Weather / environmental veto. Ambient temperature T_amb alters metabolic demand and therefore the gain of hunger drive dh/dt. Cold stress raises caloric requirement, transiently increasing h and potentially overriding bottle refusal (hunger > novelty aversion). Heat stress raises the energetic cost of sucking effort, lowering the threshold for motor veto. Veto condition: thermal load L(T_amb, clothing) > L_thr ⇒ suppression of sucking commerce even if π(o | **x**) > θ.

Adjudication order. The physical layer vetoes first: if the object fails tactual conformance, the biomechanical and environmental layers are never evaluated. If physical conformance passes, the biomechanical layer evaluates flow safety. Only if both pass does the environmental layer modulate drive gain. This veto proof shows that Tolman’s “docility relative to satiation” [PAGE 307] is a necessary but not sufficient condition for commerce; it operates only downstream of hard physical and biomechanical gates.
