1. Conformal prediction under exchangeability (Shafer & Vovk, 2008 [PAGE 1]; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023 [PAGE 1]).
Let (X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^n be exchangeable and X_{n+1} drawn from the same distribution. Conformity scores A_i = A(X_i,Y_i). The prediction set is
C(x_{n+1}) = { y in Y : A(x_{n+1}, y) <= q_{1-alpha}({A_i}_{i=1}^n) }.
Coverage guarantee: P( Y_{n+1} in C(X_{n+1}) ) >= 1 - alpha. Units: probability (dimensionless, in [0,1]). Assumption: exact exchangeability. Breakdown regime: exchangeability fails under schedule drift or opponent-adaptation; then coverage is not guaranteed and can fall below 1-alpha.

2. Weighted conformal under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019 [PAGE 1]; Barber et al., 2023 [PAGE 1]).
Importance weight w(x) = p_test(x) / p_train(x). Weighted quantile q_w solves sum_i w_i 1{A_i <= q} / sum_i w_i = 1 - alpha. Units: w is dimensionless likelihood ratio; alpha is probability. Assumption: likelihood ratio bounded, sup_x w(x) < infinity, and support overlap p_test(x) > 0 => p_train(x) > 0. Breakdown regime: if p_test has mass where p_train = 0, w(x) -> infinity, the weighted quantile extrapolates beyond calibration support, and coverage collapses (formally, no finite-sample guarantee; singularity at support mismatch).

3. Conformalized Quantile Regression (Romano et al., 2019 [PAGE 1]).
Fit quantile hats q_low(x), q_high(x) on training data; compute conformity scores E_i = max{ q_low(X_i) - Y_i, Y_i - q_high(X_i) }; interval C(x) = [ q_low(x) - q_{1-alpha}(E), q_high(x) + q_{1-alpha}(E) ]. Units: same units as target Y (e.g., points, seconds). Assumption: exchangeable calibration sample. Breakdown: heteroscedasticity outside the calibration range inflates E and over-covers; mis-specified base quantile regression yields narrow intervals with under-coverage.

4. Entropic Optimal Transport / Sinkhorn (Cuturi, 2013 [PAGE 2]; Peyré & Cuturi, 2019 [PAGE 2]; Genevay et al., 2018 [PAGE 2]).
Primal: min_{Pi in U(r,c)} <Pi, C>_F - epsilon H(Pi), where H(Pi) = -sum_{ij} Pi_{ij} (log Pi_{ij} - 1). Sinkhorn divergence S_epsilon(P,Q) = OT_epsilon(P,Q) - 1/2 OT_epsilon(P,P) - 1/2 OT_epsilon(Q,Q). Units: cost C in feature-space units (e.g., meters or normalized embedding distance); epsilon carries units of cost. Assumption: finite cost matrix, strictly positive marginals r,c. Breakdown: epsilon -> 0+ recovers Wasserstein-1 but Sinkhorn fixed-point iterations suffer numerical underflow (singularity); epsilon -> infinity yields Pi = r c^T (independence), annihilating cross-distribution dependence.

5. Copulas and dependence (Patton, 2012 [PAGE 3]; Embrechts et al., 2002 [PAGE 3]).
Sklar's theorem: H(x,y) = C(F(x), G(y)) for a copula C:[0,1]^2 -> [0,1]. Lower tail dependence lambda_L = lim_{u->0} P( Y <= F_Y^{-1}(u) | X <= F_X^{-1}(u) ). Units: probabilities. Assumption: continuous marginals permit unique copula. Breakdown: Gaussian copula implies lambda_L = 0; empirical lambda_L > 0 indicates tail dependence the model cannot represent, causing exponential underestimation of joint extremes. Correlation (Pearson) is not a dependence summary; it misses tail and non-linear association.

6. Proper scoring and Brier decomposition (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007 [PAGE 3-4]).
Brier score for binary outcomes: BS = (1/N) sum_i (f_i - o_i)^2, units: probability-squared. Murphy decomposition: BS = REL - RES + UNC, where REL = (1/N) sum_k n_k (f_k - o_k)^2 (reliability), RES = (1/N) sum_k n_k (o_k - o)^2 (resolution), UNC = o(1-o) (uncertainty). Strictly proper scoring: expected score is minimized by truthful forecast f = P(Y=1). Breakdown: improper rules induce strategic misreporting; reliability term > 0 means forecast probabilities are systematically miscalibrated.

7. Calibration vs sharpness (Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery, 2007 [PAGE 3-4]).
Calibration: P(Y=1 | F = p) = p almost surely. Sharpness: concentration of the predictive distribution P_{Y|X}, informally measured by predictive entropy or interval width. Units: probability for calibration; units of Y for sharpness width. Breakdown: a sharp but miscalibrated forecast (reliability diagram curved away from diagonal) is decision-theoretically dominated; sharpness cannot compensate for failed calibration.

8. Glicko / Glicko-2 rating with uncertainty (Glickman, 1999 [PAGE 4]; Glickman, 2022 [PAGE 4]).
State: rating mu, rating deviation sigma (RD), volatility tau. Rating period update increases sigma^2 by tau^2 (inaction), then decreases sigma after games based on observation noise. Units: mu and sigma in rating points. Assumption: rating changes are normally distributed around true strength with observation error. Breakdown: as sigma -> infinity (long inactivity), the posterior over strength becomes diffuse and the rating carries negligible information; the system regresses toward the prior mean with high uncertainty.

9. Survival hazard and DLNM (Gasparrini, 2014 [PAGE 5]; Therneau, 2026 [PAGE 5]).
Cox model: h(t | X) = h_0(t) exp( beta^T X ), hazard rate units: 1/time. DLNM cross-basis: exposure-lag-response surface h(t) = sum_l CB( exposure, lag ) with distributed-lag structure. Units: exposure in workload units; lag in time steps; hazard in 1/time. Assumption: proportional hazards (beta constant over t); independent censoring. Breakdown: non-proportional hazards (beta time-varying) invalidates partial likelihood; h_0(0) may be singular for certain baseline distributions; misspecified lag knots misattribute acute vs chronic workload into current hazard.

10. Walk-forward overfitting gating (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014 [PAGE 5]; Bailey et al., 2017 [PAGE 5]).
Deflated Sharpe ratio accounts for selection bias across N backtests: SR_def = SR / sqrt(1 + (N-1) rho^2) approximately under their selection framework. PBO via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation estimates probability that a backtest superior to the threshold is spurious. Units: Sharpe is dimensionless (return/risk per sqrt-time); PBO is probability in [0,1]. Breakdown: when the number of tested parameter combinations grows combinatorially, even a null strategy appears to have positive edge; PBO -> 1 as trials -> infinity without correction.

11. Kelly information rate (Kelly, 1956 [PAGE 4]).
Log-optimal growth rate g = E[ log W ], where W is terminal wealth relative to initial. Information rate in bits per channel use R = max_{p(x)} I(X;Y). Units: bits/s or bits per transmission. Assumption: known channel probabilities and log-utility. Breakdown: parameter-estimation error in the true probabilities causes over-betting relative to log-optimal and positive probability of ruin; requires fractional scaling under estimation uncertainty.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
This section models how the forecasting system and its human components regress under crisis, how an adversarial counter-trap exploits that regression, and how the clock (time pressure, batch deadlines, server latency) modulates both.

Crisis habit regression. Under stress or high variance, forecasters and learning systems revert to ingrained habits rather than recomputing from first principles. In the human layer, Mellers et al. (2015) [PAGE 4] show superforecasters update frequently and precisely; Nikolopoulos et al. (2021) [PAGE 4] show that under expedited identification and small expert pools, identification of true skill degrades and extremized aggregation fails. The recursive step: when a crisis (e.g., sudden roster change, weather shift, opponent tactical inversion) compresses forecast issuance latency, the human forecaster substitutes recency weighting for calibrated probability. Formally, if the crisis horizon H is shorter than the normal update latency L, the forecast F reverts to a recency heuristic H_t rather than the calibrated posterior P(Y|X), and reliability REL in the Murphy decomposition increases [PAGE 3-4].

In the model layer, Glicko (Glickman, 1999 [PAGE 4]) increases rating deviation sigma during inactivity (bye weeks, postponements). A crisis that triggers mass postponements simultaneously inflates sigma across many teams. If the engine continues to consume mu as if sigma were stable, it treats diffuse posteriors as point estimates—a habit regression to pre-crisis confidence.

Counter-trap. An adversarial model or opponent strategist who knows the engine's regression habit can exploit the predictable diffusion. If the engine over-reacts to recent outcomes after high-variance weeks (recency habit), the counter-trap is to anticipate mean reversion in team strength estimates and position forecasts against the engine's over-weighted recent signal. If the engine under-reacts because sigma is inflated (Glicko diffuses), the counter-trap is to exploit stale mu values before the rating period update restores precision. The counter-trap is recursive: it models the engine's model of the opponent, creating a fixed-point where both sides forecast the other's regression.

Clock. Time pressure is an independent variable that degrades discrimination. Nikolopoulos et al. (2021) [PAGE 4] explicitly tie superforecaster identification to expedited conditions; the clock narrows the window for extremized aggregation and team deliberation. In the engineering layer, Batch 1 verification encountered server timeouts that dropped Bradley-Terry mirrors and corrupted the Bergmeir PDF [PAGE 6-7 gap list]. Those are clock-induced data gaps: the library is missing canonical references because the wall-clock download window closed before resolution. If Batch 2 repeats the same resolution failures, the engine's rating and walk-forward layers will be forced to rely on substitutes for multiple cycles, compounding epistemic uncertainty.

Recursive loop: crisis -> habit regression (recentism / sigma inflation) -> counter-trap (adversarial exploitation of predictable miscalibration) -> clock pressure (less time to detect regression) -> deeper regression. The only break is external verification: PBO gating [PAGE 5], reliability-diagram checks [PAGE 3-4], and conformal coverage audits must run on a wall-clock schedule faster than the crisis cycle, or the loop closes on itself.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The veto layer adjudicates across physical, biomechanical, and statistical constraints. Any single veto blocks engine output; all three must pass.

Layer 1: Statistical conformal veto. Weighted conformal coverage requires bounded likelihood ratio sup_x w(x) < W_max and support overlap. Verifiable quantity: the maximum importance weight on the held-out calibration slate. If sup_x p_test(x)/p_train(x) exceeds W_max, or if any test covariate lies outside the convex hull of training support, the prediction set C(x) has no finite-sample coverage guarantee and is vetoed. Singularity: support mismatch (p_train = 0 where p_test > 0) makes w unbounded; the conformal quantile extrapolates beyond observed conformity scores, and coverage collapses to an unquantified value below 1-alpha. Citation: Tibshirani et al. (2019) [PAGE 1], Barber et al. (2023) [PAGE 1].

Layer 2: Biomechanical / workload hazard veto. Gasparrini's DLNM cross-basis [PAGE 5] maps exposure-lag-response into current hazard h(t|X) = h_0(t) exp( f_crossbasis(workload history) ). Therneau's Cox counting-process implementation [PAGE 5] requires proportional hazards and independent censoring. A biomechanical veto fires if the estimated hazard exceeds a pre-specified physiological ceiling h_max, or if the proportional-hazards assumption is rejected (e.g., Schoenfeld residual test indicates time-varying beta). Missing original: Morton et al. (1990) Banister impulse-response [PAGE 6 gap list] is absent, so the fatigue curve's exact acute/chronic decay constants are not natively verified; the DLNM substitute carries structural uncertainty. If lag-knot placement cannot be validated against held-out injury or workload sequences, the cross-basis is vetoed until Batch 2 closes the gap.

Layer 3: Weather / exogenous covariate-shift veto. Weather is an exogenous distribution that can shift between training and test windows (e.g., temperature, precipitation, wind regime). If the total variation distance TV( p_train(weather), p_test(weather) ) exceeds delta, exchangeability is violated and conformal coverage is void. Because weather enters the covariate vector X, a shift in the marginal p(weather) induces a shift in p(X), activating the covariate-shift breakdown regime from Layer 1. Veto condition: estimated TV > delta OR likelihood-ratio weight for weather-conditioned strata exceeds W_max.

Adjudication rule: The engine output is released only if (i) conformal coverage audit passes, (ii) DLNM hazard is below h_max with validated lag structure, and (iii) exogenous covariate shift is within tolerance. A failure in any layer is a hard veto, not a down-weighting, because statistical guarantees (coverage), physical plausibility (hazard ceiling), and distributional validity (exchangeability) are non-compensatory: a sharp interval cannot compensate for failed calibration, and a low hazard cannot compensate for an unbounded likelihood ratio.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario A: Covariate-shift likelihood-ratio blowup.
Verifiable procedure: compute w(x_i) = p_test(x_i)/p_train(x_i) on the most recent calibration slate using density-ratio estimation; record M = max_i w(x_i). Veto threshold: M > 10. If veto fires, all conformal prediction intervals on that slate are withheld until the training window is re-anchored or weights are clipped with validated support overlap.
Prediction: conditional on M > 10, conformal coverage falls below 1 - alpha - 0.05 within 72 hours, detected by a reliability audit on realized outcomes. Coverage gap is the falsifiable signal.

Scenario B: Copula tail-dependence mis-specification.
Verifiable procedure: estimate the empirical lower tail-dependence coefficient lambda_L_emp from historical joint extremes of the relevant variables; compare to the Gaussian copula's lambda_L = 0. Veto threshold: |lambda_L_emp| > 0.2 (empirical tail dependence exists but the model assumes asymptotic independence). If veto fires, multi-leg joint-forecast dependence is recomputed with a non-Gaussian copula (e.g., Student-t or Clayton) or the joint forecast is withheld.
Prediction: conditional on veto activation, the reliability term REL in the Brier decomposition for joint exceedance events increases by more than 30% over the following two weeks relative to baseline, while resolution RES remains flat—indicating miscalibration, not lost information.

Scenario C: Agent fleet policy drift under decoupled training/execution.

Veto threshold: D_KL > 0.5 nats. If veto fires, fleet sweeps are paused and the deployed policy is re-synchronized to the training distribution or retrained.
Prediction: conditional on D_KL > 0.5 nats, fleet sweep reward degrades by more than 15% within five sweep cycles; reward recovery occurs only after re-synchronization, confirming the decoupling gap as the causal driver rather than environmental drift.
