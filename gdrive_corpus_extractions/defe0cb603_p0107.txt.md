Second hop: the testimony/séance examples. James posits that pre-existing mental images ("premonitory images") fill in sensory gaps under ambiguity (darkness, expectancy). This is predictive coding in proto-form. But note the unexamined assumption: that these "images" are sufficiently vivid to drive false positives across senses. This is a hidden variable.

Third, the footnote on language comprehension: "half the words... supplied out of our own head." This is stunningly modern—it anticipates Bayesian brain theories. But James treats this as obvious, not quantifying how much context versus acoustics contributes. The breakdown regime here is critical: when does top-down completion fail? He hints at it with the "unfamiliar language" example—without robust priors ("verbal objects"), faint cues aren't sufficient. So the singularity might be at low signal-to-noise ratios combined with weak priors. But what defines "weak"? Is it statistical familiarity or emotional salience? James leaves this open.

Now, gray-zone audit: James’s framework assumes perception is a unified faculty modulated by attention and expectation. But he never addresses cross-modal interference (e.g., does auditory suggestion enhance visual hallucination?). Also, his "lower vs higher senses" dichotomy is normative, not operational. James seems aware ("testimony... proverbially fallacious") but offers no correction factor. This is the ultimate singularity: a self-undermining epistemology where even the psychologist’s own perceptions can’t be trusted. Yet he proceeds to use examples from séances and hypnosis—data already filtered through multiple layers of construction. The paradox is unresolved.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
William James’s 1890 analysis emerges from dual lineages: post-Kantian German psychology (Helmholtz’s unconscious inference) and American pragmatism (rejection of static "ideas"). Crucially, Helmholtz framed perception as hypothesis testing, but James radicalized it by emphasizing *temporal flow*—perception isn't deduction from axioms but continuous adjustment amid uncertainty. His séance/hypnosis examples deliberately choose liminal states to expose mechanisms hidden in normal waking life. This creates a retrocausal trap: we now read James through neural networks and Bayesian models he never imagined, projecting contemporary rigor onto his intuitive sketches. For instance, his "premonitory images" map neatly to generative models, but James lacked computational tools to distinguish between predictive coding and simple priming effects.

Multi-week traps arise from historical reframing. In the 1950s, Gestalt psychologists criticized James for overemphasizing elements over wholes—but ironically, James’s "perception as totals" is precisely a wholistic claim! Modern reconstructions often invert his meaning: they treat him as precursor to feature-detector theories when he actually argued for emergent pattern completion. Another trap: neuroscientists cite the language footnote as evidence for "predictive brains," ignoring James’s caveat that this works only for familiar languages. They extract a universal principle from a bounded case study. Additionally, James’s focus on fraud (séances) gives his work moral urgency lost in sterile lab studies—today’s replication crises echo his warnings about contaminated data, yet we’ve forgotten his ethical framing. The genealogy shows how scientific progress involves both continuity and creative distortion.

However, this very adaptation becomes exploitable in manipulative contexts, creating perverse incentives where deceivers design environments to maximize regressivity. <CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Perceptual constructs face veto from three constraint layers: physical/biomechanical limits, metabolic/weather conditions, and real-time sensorimotor feedback. These form non-negotiable boundaries that override even strong priors.

**Physical veto proof**: Vision requires photons entering pupil; if object is behind wall, no amount of expectation generates retinal image. Similarly, audition needs air vibrations—complete vacuum prevents sound perception regardless of anticipation. Biomechanically, eye muscles can't track imaginary moving targets beyond saccadic jump limits (~60°/sec); thus sustained "seeing" of spirits requires either actual motion or internally generated eye movements (hypnagogic jerks). Olfaction demands volatile molecules reaching receptor neurons—fake flower scent won't trigger response without chemical presence. These hard constraints create null hypotheses: any claimed perception violating physics must originate internally.

**Weather/metabolic veto**: Extreme cold impairs peripheral nerve conduction, dulling tactile discrimination—so even expecting texture differences may fail if receptors freeze. Dehydration reduces saliva production, flattening taste responses; hence thirsty person might miss subtle flavor notes despite focused attention. High humidity slows evaporation cooling, potentially altering thermal perception independence from visual cues. Such environmental modifiers directly throttle sensory channel fidelity, forcing reliance on weaker channels where priors dominate more easily (e.g., guessing temperature via sight when touch is compromised).

**Sensorimotor consistency check**: True external objects produce coherent multisensory predictions upon interaction. If "spirit" resists touch attempts inconsistently (sometimes solid, sometimes intangible), motor planning systems detect mismatch and flag anomaly. Conversely, consistent haptic feedback strengthens veridical perception. James’s séance example likely exploited momentary inconsistency tolerance—brief contact insufficient to build reliable proprioceptive model. However, prolonged interaction would inevitably generate conflicting evidence unless meticulously choreographed by fraudsters.

Adjudication protocol: When top-down construction conflicts with bottom-up constraints, resolution follows hierarchy:
1. Direct physiological impossibility → reject construct immediately.
2. Probabilistic conflict (e.g., unlikely object placement) → weigh prior strength vs. sensory reliability dynamically.
3. Ambiguous cases → default to conservative interpretation (Occam’s razor) unless compelling contrary evidence accumulates.

Thus, while James correctly identifies vulnerability to suggestion, he underplays the body's built-in skepticism mechanisms. Our perceptual apparatus isn't naively gullible; it continuously runs sanity checks against invariant world properties. Fraud succeeds only by carefully circumventing these checks within narrow temporal windows before contradictions emerge.
