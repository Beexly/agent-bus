**2. Visual Threat Encoding and Bayesian Updating Operator**

The **Threat Belief State** $B_t(\mathbf{x})$ at time $t$ is a probability distribution over the hypothesis that the stimulus is dangerous, $D$. Initially, there is an **Innate Prior** $P_0(D|\mathbf{x})$, which varies by species and individual (explaining why some children cry at first sight, others do not) [PAGE 417]. Upon encountering evidence $E_t$ (e.g., the animal's movement, a story heard, direct negative interaction), the belief updates via Bayes' theorem:
$$
B_{t+1}(D|\mathbf{x}, E_{1:t+1}) = \frac{P(E_{t+1}|D, \mathbf{x}) B_t(D|\mathbf{x}, E_{1:t})}{P(E_{t+1}|\mathbf{x}, E_{1:t})}
$$
The likelihood $P(E|D, \mathbf{x})$ captures how informative an event is under each hypothesis. For example, a frog struggling against a child's hand ($E$) might have high likelihood under $D$ (it's trying to escape harm?) and low under $\neg D$, thus increasing $B(D)$. The anecdote of the child who first mouthed the frog then later feared it without intervening stories suggests a maturation function $m(t)$ scaling the learning rate or altering the interpretation of $E$ [PAGE 417].

**3. Threatening Approach Kinematics Operator**

Define the **Collision Probability Estimate** $C(\mathbf{v}_{rel}, R_{obj}, R_{obs})$ as the probability that the objects' radii $R$ intersect within a short time horizon $\Delta t$:
$$
C \approx \Phi\left( \frac{\|\mathbf{v}_{rel}\| \cdot \Delta t - d_0}{\sigma_d} \right)
$$
where $d_0$ is current distance, $\sigma_d$ models uncertainty in trajectory, and $\Phi$ is a sigmoid function mapping physical closeness to perceived inevitability. A "threatening way" [PAGE 417] also involves ambiguous or aggressive cues (e.g., bared teeth), which increase the estimated cost of contact. Thus, the overall **Approach Threat Value** $T_{app}$ combines collision probability and intent:
$$
T_{app} = C \cdot \mathbb{E}[Cost_{contact} | \text{aggression cues}]
$$
If $T_{app}$ exceeds a threshold, it triggers defensive behavior (freezing, crying).

$$
F = w_A S_A + w_V B_t(D|\mathbf{x}) + w_K T_{app} + \xi

where $w$ are weighting factors reflecting channel reliability, and $\xi$ is noise. The "marked" effect of noise heightening terror [PAGE 417] corresponds to $w_A$ being large, so even moderate $S_A$ significantly elevates $F$. When $F > \theta_F$, overt fear responses (crying, avoidance) are produced.
