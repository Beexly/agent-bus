Knight identifies that the cost of acquiring $H$ is borne by the worker, but the risk of losing it falls heavily on them if the business fails. We model the "Actuarial Value" of the worker's risk, $A_W$, relative to the owner's risk, $A_O$. Under perfect competition, returns should align with risk-bearing. However, Knight's psychological variable $\Psi$ acts as a friction coefficient against free contract:
$$ \text{Return}_W < \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial H} \iff \Psi(M) > \Psi(H) $$
Here, $\Psi$ denotes the willingness to trust outside control without collateral. Owners of $M$ demand guarantees; owners of $H$ (laborers) do not receive them. 

In terms of Information Theory, the "Uncertainty" ($U$) inherent in the outcome of a game can be quantified using Shannon Entropy, measured in bits:
$$ U(X) = - \sum_{i} p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i) $$
where $X$ is the random variable of the final margin of victory. "Risk" ($R$) is a subset of $U$ where the probability distribution $p(x_i)$ is known and stable. True Knightian Uncertainty arises when the parameters of the distribution itself are unknown or shifting dynamically due to in-game adjustments. 

Breakdown Regime: The equation for the preservation of $H$ breaks down when the intensity of play exceeds the biomechanical recovery rate. Let $\dot{H}$ be the rate of change in the health status of the player. 
$$ \dot{H} = \alpha \cdot (\text{Recovery}) - \beta \cdot (\text{Intensity})^n $$
If Intensity $> (\alpha / \beta)^{1/n}$, $H$ enters a catastrophic failure state (severe injury), rendering the specialized skill instantly obsolete. The "Safety First" movement mentioned in the text [PAGE 369] essentially attempts to cap the Intensity variable below this singularity point.
