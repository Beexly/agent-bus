<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Layer 1 — Physical/Environmental: Crosswind on the Thames generates lateral force $F_w = \frac{1}{2}\rho_{air}C_{d,air}A_{crew}v_{wind}^2$ [N], which must be countered by rudder input. Fog reduces visual acquisition of the starting line; since the crews are "not yet visible" [PAGE 83] until deployment, any visibility range $R_{vis} < L_{alignment}$ vetoes a visual start and mandates an audible signal.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical: A crab occurs when the oar handle strikes the rower or the blade buries beyond recovery. The resulting deceleration $\Delta v = \int F_{crab}dt/m_{shell}$ [m/s] typically costs 2–5 seconds. Because elite margins are often under one boat length ($\approx 2$ m), a single crab is a hard veto of victory regardless of tactical superiority.

Layer 3 — Tactical: Starting alignment, rudder trim, and stroke-rate sequencing are subordinate to Layers 1–2. If Layer 1 or Layer 2 fails, tactical optimization is undefined; the outcome is determined by the veto event, not by the race plan.

Adjudication rule: A physical or biomechanical veto at time $t_v$ overrides all subsequent tactical states. Post-veto win probability for an eight-oared shell is typically $\le 0.15$ if the veto occurs in the first 30 seconds, because recovery power cannot offset the lost displacement within the remaining race distance.
