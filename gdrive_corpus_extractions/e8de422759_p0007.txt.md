This end-game clock effect changes the conditional scoring rates and the correlation between home and away goals as a function of elapsed time, violating the assumption that a single \lambda, \mu, and \rho govern the entire match. Consequently, recursive theory of mind across the match clock and across weekly fixtures implies that any inference treating \boldsymbol{\theta} as immutable is regressing toward a habit regression that opponents and game states actively exploit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: pitch dimensions, ball aerodynamics, and goal dimensions impose hard bounds on observable scoring; no statistical parameterization can generate physically impossible goal sequences. If the data-generating process is near a physical boundary (for example, extremely low-scoring matches on poor surfaces), the Poisson tail assumptions of model (4.1) and its [PAGE 270] perturbation may assign non-negligible mass to implausible events or zero mass to feasible ones.

Biomechanical layer: player fatigue, sprint capacity, and injury accumulation vary across matches within a week. The static assumption on [PAGE 270]—that attack and defence parameters are constant through time—implicitly assumes players are physiologically identical from one fixture to the next. When fixtures are spaced three days apart versus seven days apart, biomechanical readiness differs, so \alpha_i and \beta_i cannot be treated as fixed without a veto condition relating rest days to intensity.

Weather layer: precipitation and wind reduce passing precision and tend to suppress scoring, especially in low-score events. If weather covaries with match scheduling or team strength, the estimated \rho may absorb meteorological confounding rather than genuine team dependence.

Veto adjudication rule: any inference that reports time-invariant \boldsymbol{\theta} while ignoring weather W_k, inter-match rest r_k, or pitch state P_k is vetoed whenever \partial \lambda_k / \partial W_k \neq 0 or \partial \lambda_k / \partial r_k \neq 0 in the physical layer, because the statistical estimand is not invariant to interventions on those layers. The Dixon–Coles model as described on [PAGE 270] contains no such exogenous covariates, so its out-of-sample predictions are vetoed under conditions of systematic weather or fixture-congestion heterogeneity.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios:

Scenario 1 — Rain-intensity counterfactual. Hold teams i and j and their estimated strengths fixed; compare match k observed under recorded precipitation > 5 mm/h with a counterfactual dry rendering of the same fixture. The static Dixon–Coles model, which lacks a weather covariate, predicts identical p(0,0), p(1,0), p(0,1), p(1,1) in both cases. Verifiable veto: if the observed frequency of 0-0 or 1-0 outcomes in wet matches exceeds the model's 95% prediction interval conditional on team strengths, the static \rho and \lambda are vetoed. Predicted direction: rain suppresses scoring, so wet matches should show elevated 0-0 frequency relative to the static forecast.

Scenario 2 — Managerial-change counterfactual. Team i appoints a new manager between match k-1 and match k. Hold the pre-change \alpha_i, \beta_i fixed and predict match k. Verifiable veto: if the out-of-sample log-likelihood of post-change matches under the static parameter vector falls by more than the likelihood-ratio threshold relative to a model with a post-change intercept shift, the time-invariance assumption on [PAGE 270] is rejected. Predicted direction: a tactically different manager alters attack/defence parameters, so static forecasts degrade after the change point.

Scenario 3 — Fixture-congestion counterfactual. Team i plays match k after three days rest versus the counterfactual same team after seven days rest, holding opponent fixed. Verifiable veto: if observed goals scored by the congested team fall outside the static model's prediction interval derived from its time-invariant \alpha_i, \beta_i, the model is vetoed. Predicted direction: shorter recovery reduces scoring intensity, so congested fixtures should underperform the static forecast; the magnitude of underperformance should scale with the inverse of rest days.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
