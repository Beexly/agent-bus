The lineage runs from Pavlov’s experimental extinction and cortical inhibition concepts (quoted at p. 233 and discussed on [PAGE 249]) through Hull’s 1943 deductive behaviorism, then forward to modern relapse theory. Rescorla and Wagner (1972) formalized extinction as acquisition of an inhibitory association, leaving the original excitatory sHr intact; Bouton’s renewal, reinstatement, and spontaneous-recovery paradigms operationalize exactly the phenomenon Hull describes on [PAGE 249]: after extinction under one drive, changing the drive or context recovers the response. Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy: Laboratory extinction on [PAGE 249] is typically measured within a session (tens to hundreds of unreinforced evocations). A habit “extinguished” in a low-drive clinic setting can reappear when the patient encounters a high-drive crisis weeks later, not because sHr regenerated, but because D_eff crossed the neutralization threshold again. This is a retrospective causal trap: the practitioner attributes failure to extinction technique, whereas the operative variable was the post-extinction drive surge. A second trap is irrelevant-drive carryover: if extinction occurs while an allied drive (e.g., hunger) is active, the required n* is larger than under pure relevant-drive conditions [PAGE 249]; if the applied setting later removes that irrelevant drive, n* appears artificially small, producing overconfidence in durability. Hull’s propositions therefore imply that extinction dose must be prescribed relative to the maximum expected drive over the follow-up interval, not relative to the drive present during the extinction session itself.

Ambient temperature and humidity alter evaporative water loss and therefore thirst drive independent of the experimental manipulation. A hot environment can unsate the animal, converting thirst from an irrelevant background state into a relevant or co-relevant drive, thereby changing α and D_rel mid-session. If weather co-varies with the drive manipulation, the ceteris paribus condition of IX and X fails, and the ambient layer vetoes the clean attribution of n* changes to drive intensity alone.

Adjudication rule: physical-layer vetoes (endocrine change) modify D_eff directly; biomechanical vetoes censor n* measurement; weather-layer vetoes invalidate the assignment of drives as relevant vs. irrelevant. Propositions IX and X are valid only in the intersection where the physical layer is stable, the biomechanical layer is unconstrained, and ambient conditions hold drive definitions fixed.

Three verifiable counterfactual interventions with predictions and explicit veto criteria, derived from [PAGE 249]. No betting lines or odds are referenced.

Scenario 1 — Castration counterfactual (tests Proposition X’s drive subtraction).
Setup: Acquire a lever-press habit in male rats under combined hunger + sexual drive. Post-acquisition, randomly castrate half the subjects; leave half intact. Run extinction under identical normal hunger drive and record unreinforced lever presses to criterion θ.
Prediction (Hull, [PAGE 249]): Castrated subjects require fewer unreinforced evocations: n*_castrated < n*_intact, with the difference scaling with the magnitude of the removed sexual-drive component D_sex.
Verifiable measurement: Cumulative unreinforced presses per session until press rate falls below θ for three consecutive trials.
Veto criterion: If n*_castrated ≈ n*_intact within sampling error, sexual drive was not allied to the hunger lever-press habit (α_sex ≈ 0), or postoperative confounds canceled D_sex removal; Proposition X’s assumption of additive allied drives fails for this drive pair.

Scenario 2 — Irrelevant-drive saturation (tests bounded alliance coefficient α).
Setup: Extinguish a thirst-established water-reinforced habit under water satiation while manipulating hunger drive D_hunger across three intensities: weak, moderate, extreme (beyond the range where hunger plausibly “allied” to thirst).
Prediction (Hull, [PAGE 249]): n* increases with D_hunger at weak and moderate levels, but plateaus once hunger ceases to be allied, i.e., α → 0. The asymptote equals the n* predicted by relevant-drive-only extinction.
Verifiable measurement: Compare n* across the three hunger intensities under identical thirst-satiation and water-availability conditions.
Veto criterion: If n* continues to increase linearly without bound as D_hunger escalates into the extreme/immobility range, then α does not decay to zero and the “materially less” clause of Proposition X is false; drive summation is unbounded rather than alliance-limited.

Scenario 3 — Post-extinction drive surge without reinforcement (tests re-evocation deduction, [PAGE 249]).
Setup: Extinguish a habit to criterion at drive D₀. After extinction, withhold all reinforcement, then acutely raise drive to D₁ > D₀ (e.g., food deprivation after sated extinction) and present the conditioned stimulus once.
Prediction (Hull via Pavlov, [PAGE 249]): Response probability jumps above the asymptotic extinction floor by an amount proportional to (D₁ − D₀)·sHr, specifically residual potential sEr = (D₁ − D₀)·sHr + θ above the extinction threshold.
Verifiable measurement: Magnitude of the first post-surge response relative to the final pre-surge unreinforced response and to a no-surge control.
Veto criterion: If the response remains fully suppressed despite the drive increase, extinction acted on habit strength sHr rather than merely neutralizing reaction potential, contradicting Hull’s neutralization account and invalidating the re-evocation deduction on [PAGE 249].
