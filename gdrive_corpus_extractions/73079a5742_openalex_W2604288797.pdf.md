The graph has no clock state variable, so a deliberate slowdown and a fast break are topologically identical once arcs are recorded. The model is descriptive of past arc structure, not predictive of adaptive defensive schemas that a coach would design against a high-IPM playmaker in a crisis moment.

Physical layer: Player load and fatigue are not node attributes; a player with IPM > 100 in game t may be rested in game t+1, vetoing any projection based solely on IPM_t [PAGE 11–19]. Biomechanical limits are ignored: all passes are topologically equal regardless of distance, velocity, or defensive pressure, except the binary contested/uncontested shot distinction [PAGE 5–6].

Weather/environment layer: For outdoor soccer and hockey, precipitation, wind, and temperature alter pass/shot completion, but the arc rules contain no environmental covariate. A stoppage due to weather draws “no arc,” exogenously truncating rank flow [PAGE 23].

Refereeing/administrative layer: Stoppages unrelated to the game (technical foul, injury, altercation) draw “no arc,” creating rank sinks that the model cannot distinguish as physical versus administrative events [PAGE 6].

Veto proof: Because IPM is purely topological over observed arcs, any physical-layer event that prevents an arc from existing (injury substitution, weather stoppage, referee whistle) vetoes the model’s output without appearing in the state vector. The model has no recovery, fatigue, or weather parameter, so it cannot self-veto; an external physical layer must adjudicate.
