The crisis arises at Level 3: the left eye reaches its extreme rightward limit and emits a strong afferent signal of rightward motion [PAGE 509]. The patient regresses under crisis to the most salient afferent channel — the left eye — and attributes its motion to the whole oculomotor system, producing the conviction that "the eyes are moving" even though the right eye stopped short [PAGE 509].

The counter-trap is to invert the causal arrow and treat the "bare will to look rightwards" as the direct generator of the spatial percept, as Mach's phrasing suggests [PAGE 509, n.]. The recursive model says instead that the will generates a motor command \(u(t)\); the percept is generated only when the plant's actual output fails to match the efference-predicted sensory consequence. The will is necessary but not sufficient; the perceptual error is computed at the comparison stage between \(\hat{\theta}\) and \(a\). James's critique of the putty experiment sharpens this: if peripheral pressure from the putty produces the same false position signal, then the percept need not originate in the will's efference copy at all [PAGE 509, n.].

The clock is the transient time constant. The text emphasizes that the left-eye afferent feelings "momentarily" overpower the faint right-eye central-position signal [PAGE 509]. Proprioceptive persistence and efference-copy decay operate on the order of \(\tau_p\sim 0.1\)–\(0.5\) s; after that window, central adaptation suppresses the mismatch and the vertigo collapses. Any model predicting a stable, enduring translocation from a single willed saccade attempt is therefore falsified by the text's own temporal bound. The "will" is the initiator; the afferent plant mismatch is the proximate cause; the duration is bounded by the physiology of the afferent channel, not by the intensity of volition.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: Mach's putty exerts a compressive force \(F_p\) on the orbit (units N). Because the eyeball is incompletely spherical, this pressure deforms the mechanical rotation path and creates retinal slip independent of the neural command [PAGE 509, n.]. If the pressure-induced globe displacement exceeds the retinal slip threshold for motion detection (roughly \(10^{-3}\)–\(10^{-2}\) rad), the physical layer alone can generate translocation; the central innervation explanation is then vetoed as unnecessary. James explicitly endorses this veto: the putty pressure "must give rise to peripheral sensations strong enough... to justify any amount of false perception... quite apart from the innervation feelings" [PAGE 509, n.].

Biomechanical layer: Extraocular muscles obey force-length-velocity properties; paralysis represents loss of motor-unit force output, \(F_m\simeq0\) for the right eye. The left eye operates normally until it hits the orbital hard stop \(\theta_L^{\rm stop}\) [PAGE 509]. A veto here applies if \(g_R=0\) but the patient still reports a unitary conviction of right-eye movement driven only by left-eye afference; this requires interocular afferent summation rather than independent eye channels, and it falsifies any model that treats the two eyes' proprioceptive streams as perceptually sealed.

Environmental/weather layer: The source text describes an indoor clinical/experimental situation, so meteorological variables are not operative. The nearest environmental analogue is target luminance and contrast at the extreme right of the visual field. Low contrast raises retinal position uncertainty \(\sigma_{\rm ret}\), which increases \(P_{\rm vert}\) via the afferent-noise term; high contrast sharpens \(s_R\) and may allow the right retinal slip signal to compete with the left-eye afferent burst, reducing the vertigo. This is a visibility veto, not a weather veto.

Adjudication rule: The central common-innervation explanation stands only if (i) orbital pressure is absent or anesthetized and translocation persists, and (ii) manipulation of left-eye afference predicts right-eye percepts. James's failed replication of Mach's putty effect and his admission that peripheral pressure suffices constitute a partial veto of Mach's strong claim [PAGE 509, n.]. The paralysis case itself supports a hybrid: the command is common, but the percept is driven by the afferent channel with the highest signal-to-noise ratio — here, the left eye at mechanical limit — bound by the efference-prediction error.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Prediction: because \(s_R=\theta_{\rm obj}-\theta_R\neq0\), the right retinal slip supplies a competing error signal, and the patient should experience diplopia or rivalry rather than a single conviction of right-eye movement [PAGE 509].
