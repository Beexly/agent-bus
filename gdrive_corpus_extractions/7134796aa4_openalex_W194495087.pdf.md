When the computer-vision layer fails—during "great confluence of player, such as corners or discussions with the referee" [PAGE 8], after a 2-second lost-player timeout [PAGE 8], or after same-team collision identity switches [PAGE 8]—the operator regresses to the manual web tool that "allows the user to manually change the player classified as referee and vice-versa" [PAGE 6]. This is a habit regression from algorithmic trust to human-in-the-loop verification, reproducing the very slow, manual logging process the paper seeks to replace [PAGE 12]. The regression is not graceful degradation; it is a binary switch to manual correction with no intermediate confidence scoring.

Counter-trap. An opposing analyst who reverse-engineers the failure modes can induce systematic tracking errors: concentrating players at corners to force collision merging [PAGE 8], exploiting Hue similarity between kits to break team separation [PAGE 6], or creating white-pixel clutter near sidelines to trigger false ball detections under V ∈ [75%, 100%] [PAGE 6]. Because passes are detected probabilistically with a fixed 3 m radius and 5-frame velocity window [PAGE 10], predictable congestion corrupts the pass log, which then poisons the ball-loss map [PAGE 11]. The counter-trap is therefore not adversarial machine learning in the modern sense, but an operational exploit of documented deterministic thresholds.

Clock. The system promises real-time information for in-game adjustment [PAGE 12], yet it carries hard temporal buffers: velocity computed over five frames [PAGE 10], lost-player reacquisition after 2 seconds [PAGE 8], and WebSocket/Socket.IO inter-module transport [PAGE 3]. If frame-processing latency exceeds the inter-frame interval, the pipeline backpressures or drops observations, and the "real-time" heat map lags actual pitch occupation. The clock thus couples physical match tempo to computational throughput; any stall in the vision module collapses the decision window for the coach.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Cameras are placed "in the stands" or use a "Full HD Handycam"; the field of view does not capture the entire pitch at kickoff, and players entering from left/right/top/bottom are treated as new unless recently lost nearby [PAGE 2, 8]. A zoom event changes projective geometry and invalidates the homography until pitch lines are re-detected [PAGE 8]. Veto: without full-pitch coverage or multi-camera registration, off-screen players are ontologically undefined until re-entry; no autonomous fusion across cameras is described.

Biomechanical layer. Player position is approximated by "the middle point in the bottom line of the box that limits the players, which corresponds in most cases to the coordinates of the players' feet" [PAGE 7]. This assumes an upright running stance. Sliding tackles, diving, goalkeeping dives, or crouching violate the feet-coordinate assumption and distort the vertical-rectangular blob model used to discard non-player blobs [PAGE 6]. Veto: non-upright postures break both the coordinate anchor and the shape prior.

Illumination/weather layer. HSV pitch thresholds are computed from "selected pitch patches" per frame [PAGE 5], and Hue values are adapted "dynamically in function of the video conditions (e.g., light)" [PAGE 6]. No model is provided for rain, fog, night-stadium glare, or high-dynamic-range sunlight. White kits and pitch lines share V ∈ [75%, 100%] with the ball [PAGE 6]. Veto: under adverse illumination or high reflectance, the ball classifier and team-separator cannot be disentangled by the published rules.

Veto adjudication. When any layer vetoes—occlusion at corners, zoom-induced calibration loss, non-upright biomechanics, or white-pixel confusion—the system's only documented remedy is the manual web tool [PAGE 6, 8]. There is no autonomous fallback, no confidence threshold that suspends automated output, and no graceful degradation path. Therefore full automation is vetoed under the conjunction of (a) player confluence, (b) camera zoom, or (c) illumination shift; the system defaults to human correction, re-introducing the latency the paper aims to eliminate.
