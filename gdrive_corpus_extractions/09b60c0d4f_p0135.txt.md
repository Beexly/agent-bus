The provided text from Francis Galton's *Natural Inheritance* (1889) outlines a critical analytical step in his study of heredity. The passage dissects the statistical architecture of stature transmission, demonstrating how the aggregate properties of Generation II are derived from the hierarchical organization of "Co-fraternities." 

First, we must establish the baseline. The general population (Generation I) has a mean ($M$) and a quasi-standard deviation ($Q$) of 1.7 inches. Galton introduces an intermediate construct: the "Mid-Parents." This system maintains the same population mean but exhibits a reduced dispersion ($Q = \sqrt{2}/2 \times 1.7 \approx 1.2$ inches). This reduction is a mathematical consequence of averaging two independent parent heights; the variance of the average of two i.i.d. variables is half the variance of the individuals, assuming no assortative mating correlation for this specific calculation step.

Next, the analysis moves to "groups" where the Mid-Parent height is expressed as $P \pm D$. Here, $D$ is the deviation from some reference point (likely the population mean), rounded to the nearest inch. Each such group forms a "Co-fraternity"—the children sharing that specific Mid-Parent stature range. Galton finds that the mean of each Co-fraternity regresses toward the population mean, taking the value $P \pm \lambda D$. The parameter $\lambda$ (lambda) represents the **regression coefficient**, a pivotal discovery. For human stature, Galton famously found $\lambda \approx 0.667$ or $2/3$. This means that while tall parents tend to have tall children, the children's average height is closer to the general population mean than their parents' height was—a phenomenon he termed "reversion" (now regression to the mean).

The "Mid-Co-Fraternity" system consists of these conditional means. Its overall mean remains the population mean, but its variability is further compressed by the factor $\lambda$: $Q_{mid-co} = \lambda \times 1.2 = 0.8$ inches. Finally, individual children ("Co-fraternals") scatter around their respective Mid-Co-Fraternity mean with a residual variability $Q_{resid} = 1.5$ inches. 

The masterstroke is the composition law. The total variance of Generation II is not simply the sum of the variances, because the Mid-Co-Fraternity means themselves are variable. The correct decomposition follows from the law of total variance: 
$Var(Total) = Var(E[Height|Group]) + E[Var(Height|Group)]$. 
In Galton's $Q$ notation, since $Q = \sqrt{Variance}$ (for a Normal distribution), squaring the components gives:
$(1.7)^2 = (0.8)^2 + (1.5)^2 = 0.64 + 2.25 = 2.89$, and $\sqrt{2.89} = 1.7$. 

This identity reveals the mechanism of **"statistical similarity"** across generations: the stability of the population distribution is maintained precisely because the variance lost through regression toward the mean ($\lambda < 1$) at the familial level is compensated by the spread of the family means themselves plus the within-family environmental/noise variance. If there were perfect inheritance ($\lambda=1$) and no new variation, the distribution would widen over time. If there were complete reversion ($\lambda=0$), all families would converge to the mean, erasing hereditary differences. The equilibrium $\lambda \approx 2/3$ is the natural selection's compromise.

Gray-zone audit: The text assumes the distributions are sufficiently Normal to allow this Pythagorean addition of $Q$s. Galton justifies ignoring "shortcomings" in normality. However, in real populations, skewness or kurtosis could slightly perturb this exact equality. Furthermore, the rounding of $D$ to the nearest inch introduces discretization error, though likely minor given the continuous underlying trait. The most profound gray zone is philosophical: Galton interprets this mathematical stability as evidence of a fundamental law of hereditary transmission, yet the model elegantly hides the biological mechanisms (genetic dominance, polygenic inheritance) which we now know underlie it. The analysis treats "Mid-Parent" as a single causal unit, smoothing over sex-specific effects.

The source provides the operational steps for decomposing hereditary variance. We formalize the operators using modern statistical notation, aligned with Galton's symbols.

Let $X$ be the stature of an individual in Generation I, distributed approximately normally with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$. 
Galton uses $M$ for the mean and $Q$ for the standard deviation (he called $Q$ the "quasi-standard difference," equivalent to $\sigma$ for a Normal distribution).

**Operator 1: Formation of Mid-Parent Variable**
Define the Mid-Parent stature as the average of the two biological parents:
$Y = \frac{X_{father} + X_{mother}}{2}$.
Assuming parents are drawn independently from the population distribution (ignoring assortative mating for this step), the variance of $Y$ is:
$\text{Var}(Y) = \frac{\text{Var}(X)}{2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$.
Thus, $Q_Y = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{2}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}}$.
From the text: $\sigma = 1.7$ inches, so $Q_Y = \frac{1.7}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 1.2$ inches. This matches "its Q was reduced to √×1·7 inch, or to 1·2 inch." Note: The symbol "√" here denotes division by $\sqrt{2}$.

**Operator 2: Grouping by Deviation D**
Express $Y$ as $Y = P \pm D$, where $P$ is a central value (likely the population mean $\mu$) and $D$ is the deviation $|Y - \mu|$, rounded to the nearest inch. Each unique value of $D$ defines a "Co-fraternity" group $G_D$.

**Operator 3: Regression within Groups (The λ Operator)**
For each group $G_D$, calculate the mean stature of the offspring (children). Galton finds this conditional mean follows:
$\mathbb{E}[Z | Y = P \pm D] = P \pm \lambda D$,
where $Z$ is the child's stature and $\lambda$ is the regression coefficient ($0 < \lambda < 1$). This is the **regression to the mean** operator. For human stature, $\lambda \approx 2/3$.

**Operator 4: Variance Decomposition of Generation II**
The distribution of $Z$ (offspring) is a mixture of the co-fraternity distributions. Let $V_{between}$ be the variance of the group means (Mid-Co-Fraternities) and $V_{within}$ be the average variance within groups.
- $V_{between} = \text{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Z|Y]) = \lambda^2 \cdot \text{Var}(Y) = \lambda^2 \cdot \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$.
  From text: $Q_{between} = \lambda \times 1.2 = 0.8$ inches, so $\lambda = 0.8 / 1.2 = 2/3$.
- $V_{within} = \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(Z|Y)]$. Text states each co-fraternity has internal $Q = 1.5$ inches, so $V_{within} = (1.5)^2$.
- Total variance of $Z$: $\text{Var}(Z) = V_{between} + V_{within}$.
  Thus, $Q_Z = \sqrt{ (\lambda \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2}})^2 + (1.5)^2 }$.
Plugging in numbers: $Q_Z = \sqrt{ (0.8)^2 + (1.5)^2 } = \sqrt{0.64 + 2.25} = \sqrt{2.89} = 1.7$.
This confirms $Q_Z = \sigma$, the original population SD.

**Units:** All measurements in inches. The equations are dimensionally homogeneous.

**Breakdown Regimes:** The Pythagorean addition holds exactly only if the conditional distributions are normal and homoscedastic (constant within-group variance). If the relationship between $D$ and the within-group variance is non-linear, or if higher moments (skewness) are significant, the simple square-root-of-sum-of-squares may fail. The text notes the distribution is "sufficiently Normal," implying these breakdown conditions are negligible for the observed data.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s 1889 analysis sits at a confluence of intellectual currents. Retrocausally, we can trace its lineage:
1. **Quetelet’s “Average Man” (1830s):** Adolphe Quetelet established that social and physical traits follow a “Normal Law” (Gaussian distribution). Galton adopts this, treating the population mean as a natural center of attraction.
2. **Darwin’s Evolution by Natural Selection (1859):** Darwin struggled with blending inheritance—if offspring are a blend of parents, variation would be rapidly erased. Galton’s work aims to solve this paradox by showing that while regression occurs, the population’s total variance is preserved through the interplay of between- and within-family variation.
3. **Fechner’s Psychophysics & Threshold Theory:** The practice of rounding deviations $D$ to the nearest inch echoes Fechner’s assumption that just-noticeable differences are discrete quanta. Galton applies this to physical measurement, accepting quantization as a practical necessity without modeling its impact on the continuum.
4. **Karl Pearson’s Future Formalism:** Although Pearson later developed correlation and chi-square, Galton here operates with raw grouping and visual inspection of histograms. His “co-fraternity” is a proto-stratification method, anticipating ANOVA.

**Multi-week traps for modern interpreters:** A reader might assume Galton believed in strict determinism. However, the presence of $\lambda < 1$ and the large residual variance (1.5 inches) show he understood inheritance as probabilistic. Another trap is viewing “reversion” as a loss of information; instead, it is a conservation law—the information about extreme parental height is partially transmitted but diluted, maintaining genetic diversity. Finally, one might misread “statistical similarity” as static equilibrium, whereas Galton saw it as dynamic homeostasis, possibly subject to natural selection altering the mean over long periods.
