<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

All equations below are transcribed from [PAGE 4] unless noted. The grid fixes the sample space at $|\mathcal{C}| = 60$ cells, which is the normalizer in Eq. 8.

**Accuracy (Eq. 1).** For image $i$ with ground-truth cell set $G_i$ and prediction $\hat y_i$:
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat y_i \in G_i\right]$$
Units: dimensionless probability in $[0,1]$. Assumption: each image weighted equally; predictions are single cells. Breakdown regime: degenerate if $|G_i| \to |\mathcal{C}|$ (any guess scores), which does not occur here ($|G_i|$ averages 2.08–2.42).

**Euclidean Error (Eq. 2).**
$$d_i = \min_{g \in G_i} \lVert c(\hat y_i) - c(g)\rVert_2$$
Units: pixels (image-plane). $c(\cdot)$ maps a cell to its center coordinate. Assumption: isotropic pixel metric; no perspective correction. Breakdown: unnormalized for image resolution — cross-image comparisons assume comparable canvas sizes, which the paper does not guarantee; also insensitive to *direction* of error (a systematically center-pulled miss and a random miss share the same $d_i$).

**Wasserstein / Earth Mover's Distance (Eq. 3).**
$$W(P,Q) = \min_{\gamma \ge 0} \sum_{j,k} \gamma_{jk} D_{jk} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_k \gamma_{jk} = P_j,\ \sum_j \gamma_{jk} = Q_k$$
Units: pixels (since ground cost $D_{jk}$ is Euclidean distance between cell centers). $P$ = empirical model distribution over 60 cells, $Q$ = human distribution. Assumptions: $P,Q$ are valid probability measures ($\ge 0$, sum to 1) — this is why the entropy normalization by 60 and the sampling counts ($n=50$ base/cue, $n=20$ CoT, $T=0.6$ on [PAGE 4]) matter: they define the empirical $P$. Breakdown regime: with finite samples the empirical $P$ is sparse; $W$ is consistent but high-variance at $n=20$, so CoT Wasserstein values carry larger estimation error than base/cue. If supports are disjoint, $W$ grows with $D_{jk}$ but stays finite on a bounded 60-cell grid (no divergence, unlike unbounded domains).

**Point-to-box distance (Eq. 4).**
$$\text{dist}(x,b) = \begin{cases} 0, & x \in \text{supp}(b) \\ d(x, \text{supp}(b)), & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
Units: pixels. Assumption: boxes $b \in B_i$ are player detections (YOLOv8 "person" class, manually de-noised of crowd per [PAGE 11]).

**Near Player Rate (Eq. 5).**
$$NR = \frac{1}{\sum_i T_i}\sum_{i,t} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\min_{b \in B_i} \text{dist}(p_{i,t}, b) \le \tau D\right],\quad \tau = 0.08,\ D = \text{image diagonal}$$
Units: dimensionless fraction. $p_{i,t} = c(\hat y_{i,t})$ is the $t$-th sampled prediction center; $T_i$ predictions for image $i$. Assumption: threshold scales with diagonal (resolution-invariant). Breakdown: near-player mass saturates when player boxes tile the frame (basketball, $\sim$20,067 px coverage) — NR loses discriminability there.

**Overlap Rate (Eq. 6).**
$$OR = \frac{1}{\sum_i T_i}\sum_{i,t} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\max_{b \in B_i} \frac{\text{area}(c(\hat y_{i,t}) \cap b)}{\text{area}(c(\hat y_{i,t}))} \ge \theta\right],\quad \theta = 0.02$$
Units: dimensionless. Threshold $\theta = 0.02$ (2% of cell area). Breakdown: division by cell area is constant, so stable; but $\theta$ is very permissive — the supplementary median overlap $\approx 0.56$ ([PAGE 12]) confirms OR is near-saturated and weakly diagnostic.

**Center Ratio (Eq. 7).**
$$CR = \frac{\sum_{j \in S} p_j}{\sum_{j \in S} q_j},\quad S = \{(r,c): r \in \{2,3,4\},\ c \in \{3,\dots,7\}\}$$
$S$ is a $3 \times 5 = 15$-cell central window ($|S|/|\mathcal C| = 25\%$). Units: dimensionless ratio; $CR > 1$ ⇒ center bias. $q$ is the "ground-truth prior" — **gray zone**: the paper never fully specifies $q$. The values $\sum_{j\in S} q_j$ are reported as the percent of GT balls in center (soccer 36.3%, volleyball 46.2%, basketball 56.3% on [PAGE 6]). Breakdown/singularity: $CR$ diverges as $\sum_{j\in S} q_j \to 0$ (a sport with no central balls); none occurs, but it is an exact pole of the operator.

**Normalized Entropy (Eq. 8).**
$$\hat H(p) = \frac{-\sum_{j=1}^{60} p_j \log p_j}{\log 60}$$
Units: dimensionless (bits/nats cancel). Range $[0,1]$. Assumption: uniform maximum-entropy reference over all 60 cells. Breakdown: if some cells are structurally unreachable, effective support $< 60$ and $\hat H = 1$ is unattainable — the normalizer should then be $\log|\text{feasible}|$; using $\log 60$ mildly deflates reported entropy.

**Threshold-fitting objective (supplementary, [PAGE 11–12]).**
$$O(\tau,\theta) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[NR(\tau,\theta) + OR(\tau,\theta)\big],\quad \tau_{\text{near}} \in [0.04,0.20],\ \theta \in [0.01,0.20]$$
Equal-weight average; the reported optimum sits on a plateau ($\tau = 0.08, \theta = 0.02$, within 1% of max), which is a robustness *argument*, not a proof of uniqueness.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The task demands genuine higher-order attribution. To localize the ball, an agent must model each player's *belief* about the ball ("player X is looking up-right ⇒ X tracks the ball up-right"), then compose these across agents into a scene-level estimate. Humans do this even under weak cues: in volleyball (players small, 6,600 px each, [PAGE 3]) they still aggregate directional information across 9.92 players to reach their *best* sport accuracy (72.0 px, Table 2), implying distributed second-order inference. Models fail the recursion.

Three coupled dynamics describe the failure:

**Habit regression under crisis.** When cues are ambiguous (volleyball), models collapse onto base-rate priors — center (Eq. 7) and near-player (Eq. 5). This is a regression to the training-distribution mode: 52.2% of GT balls are near players ([PAGE 6]), so "guess-near-player" is a decent prior that catastrophically fails exactly where the ball "is struck rather than held" ([PAGE 6]). Models predict near-player 90% of the time vs human 65–75% ([PAGE 6], Fig. 5). The lower the extracted signal, the harder the regression to the prior — a self-reinforcing trap, visible in the entropy collapse (LLaMA/Qwen volleyball $\hat H \approx 0.42$, Table 3).

**Counter-trap (CoT).** CoT is designed to force the recursion — three explicit sub-questions about location, gaze, and pose ([PAGE 4], [PAGE 11]). It partially succeeds at the *textual* level (more gaze language, Fig. 7c) but becomes a trap: the intermediate scene graph hallucinates (Tables 8–9 show duplicate/contradictory player coordinates), and the final answer inherits the corrupted belief state. Hence LLaMA volleyball degrades to 272.6 px under CoT (Table 2). The counter-trap improves the *narrative* of theory-of-mind without improving the *computation* of it — a decoupling the paper flags: "it doesn't lead to better predictions of where the ball is" ([PAGE 7]).

**The clock.** The paper deliberately removes the temporal clock ([PAGE 2]), and this is the deepest recursion trap. A single frame is a belief-snapshot with no dynamics; the model must infer intent from pose-as-evidence rather than motion-as-cinematography. Humans carry an internal physics-and-intent prior that lets them read a frozen spike-approach and extrapolate; models lack the composed prior, so with the clock deleted they default to the static manifold (center + bodies). The clock's removal is simultaneously the paper's *isolation instrument* and the *reason* the human–model gap is so large: the task tests precisely the inference that survives when motion is stripped.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical and biomechanical vetoes adjudicate which predictions are admissible before any social reasoning is credited.

**Projectile veto.** A struck ball obeys ballistic motion (ignoring drag), $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{v}_0 t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2 \hat{\mathbf{y}}$, $g \approx 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$. Any predicted cell must lie on a physically reachable arc consistent with a plausible contact point and launch speed. Predictions off every feasible arc are vetoed outright.

**Net-strike termination veto.** The paper's own Figure 8c example ([PAGE 8]) is decisive: a model places the volleyball prediction "directly in the middle of the image, at the net… an unlikely location for the ball, since play would have already terminated if the volleyball had struck the net." Quote verbatim: this is a hard biomechanical veto — a ball intersecting the net plane mid-court is a rally-ending event, so the pre-termination frame cannot show the ball there. Any center-net prediction is physically inadmissible, and the Center Ratio $R$ (Eq. 7) therefore counts a *physically impossible* mode as "central mass" — a metric-vs-physics conflict.

**Occlusion/coupling veto.** The authors' causal premise is that "players' gaze, posture, and positioning are causally coupled to the ball" ([PAGE 1]). This yields a veto in both directions: (i) if many agents' gaze vectors converge on a region, a prediction far from that convergence is vetoed unless a physical occluder explains the divergence; (ii) in volleyball, where the ball is struck away from players, the near-player prior is *anti-correlated* with truth — so a near-player prediction is vetoed whenever the receiving formation's gaze points away from that player. This is exactly the regime where models (90% near-player, [PAGE 6]) violate the physical coupling.

**Resolution/measurement veto.** Table 1 gives ball pixel areas (785–1631 px) and the grid imposes 60 discrete cells. When the ball footprint spans 2.08–2.42 cells, a prediction is admissible only if it overlaps the physical footprint after inverse-inpainting bounds; the metric's tolerance is already encoded in $G_i$. Cross-layer adjudication: the Euclidean error (Eq. 2) must be re-expressed in *ball-diameters*, since a 72 px error in a sport with 904 px ball area (volleyball) is a different physical miss than 113 px against a 785 px ball (soccer). Unifying the pixel metric with the biomechanical scale is the required cross-layer correction; the paper's raw pixel $d_i$ comparisons across sports are only conditionally valid.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Because ground-truth ball cells exist, this task admits *verifiable rewards*: $r = \mathbb{1}[\hat y_i \in G_i]$ (Eq. 1) is a checkable RLVR signal. Three counterfactual veto scenarios, each falsifiable:

**Scenario 1 — RLVR on the 3,000-image soccer set collapses center bias but entrenches near-player bias.** The scalable pipeline produced 3,000 soccer images ([PAGE 2]). Verifiable reward penalizes non-ball cells. *Prediction:* center ratio $R$ (Eq. 7) moves toward 1 (from Gemini's soccer 0.989 / LLaMA's 1.150, Table 3) and entropy $\hat H$ rises, *but* because 52.2% of GT balls are near players ([PAGE 6]), the policy over-fits the near-player mode; Near Player Rate (Eq. 5) stays high. *Falsifier:* if $NR$ drops to human 65–75% while $R \to 1$, the reward reshaped behavior rather than reinforcing a prior — veto the "mere prior entrenchment" hypothesis.

**Scenario 2 — volleyball improves more than soccer under RLVR.** The disambiguating cue in volleyball is gaze (the ball leaves the player), whereas the misleading cue (proximity) is anti-correlated. *Prediction:* verifiable-reward fine-tuning yields a larger volleyball accuracy delta than soccer, because the reward punishes the near-player prior precisely where it is wrong. Concretely, model Euclidean error in volleyball (currently 150–272 px, Table 2) should fall disproportionately versus soccer. *Falsifier:* if soccer improves more, the model is exploiting the multi-cell $|G_i|$ slack (2.27 soccer cells) rather than learning gaze — veto the "cue-grounding" explanation.

**Scenario 3 — CoT hallucination persists under outcome-only RLVR.** The LLaMA/Qwen CoT collapse (volleyball 272.6 / 271.5 px, Table 2) stems from corrupted intermediate scene graphs (Tables 8–9: duplicate coordinates). *Prediction:* an outcome-only verifiable reward on the final cell does *not* repair the intermediate reasoning trace; accuracy may recover somewhat, but the enumerated player positions remain internally inconsistent because no reward supervises the latent state. *Falsifier:* if the intermediate traces become consistent under outcome-only RLVR, the model self-corrects its belief graph without step-level supervision — veto the "reward-blind-to-process" claim. A process-supervised variant (reward per correctly enumerated player cell, verifiable against YOLOv8 boxes) is the decisive follow-up: it should beat outcome-only RLVR on volleyball if and only if the failure is genuinely in *composition* rather than *perception*.

Cross-cutting veto on all three: any observed gain must survive the resolution-normalized re-scoring from the adjudication section (error in ball-diameters, $CR$ net-strike veto applied). A reward that raises $\mathbb{1}[\hat y_i \in G_i]$ by exploiting the 2.08–2.42-cell ground-truth slack, without reducing physical/biomechanical violations, is a metric artifact — and is vetoed as evidence of improved visual social inference.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
