Breakdown / singularity regimes: (i) visual feedback delay \( L_v > \tau_{\text{buffer}} \) causes forward-model prediction \( \hat{\mathbf{x}}_T(t+L_v) \) to diverge from the actual target; (ii) \( \sigma_p \to \infty \) with no visual substitution removes proprioceptive closure and can eliminate voluntary movement, as in the anesthetic footnote cases [PAGE 620, footnote]; (iii) attentional load on the means \( \alpha_{\text{means}} > C_{\text{capacity}} \) collapses automaticity and induces conscious proprioceptive regression; (iv) if the target lies outside the reachable workspace \( \mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^3 \), no control policy can achieve \( \varepsilon \approx 0 \). James explicitly supplies no neural equations, stating the neural machinery inquiry is deferred to a later stage [PAGE 620].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage begins with James’s 1890 functionalist psychology and the Southard–Bowditch psychophysics published in the *Journal of Physiology*, vol. I, No. 3 [PAGE 620, footnote]. James’s remote-versus-resident consciousness distinction anticipates later sensorimotor dichotomies: Helmholtz’s unconscious inference about sensory cues, Sherrington’s proprioceptive theory, Head and Holmes’s postural schemata, and Bernstein’s construction of movements. The “two-second” localization-to-initiation buffer prefigures the short-term sensorimotor store and working-memory buffers formalized in mid-20th-century cognitive psychology. The same-hand/cross-hand cost anticipates limb-centered coordinate frames and interhemispheric transfer through the corpus callosum.

In the modern lineage, James’s phenomenological claim maps onto internal forward models and optimal feedback control (Wolpert, Kawato, Shadmehr; Todorov and Jordan), where the brain predicts sensory consequences and corrects via the most reliable channel. The dorsal “how” stream (Milner and Goodale) provides the neuroanatomical correlate for remote optical guidance of action, distinct from ventral perceptual identification. James’s refusal to specify neural machinery [PAGE 620] places him at the computational level of Marr’s framework, leaving algorithmic and implementational levels to later physiology.

Multi-week traps arise in skill acquisition and rehabilitation. In the first weeks of training, mean absolute error drops rapidly as the performer shifts from conscious proprioceptive monitoring to remote optical focus; the curve then plateaus at a floor set by sensory noise, not by effort. A specific trap is regression under pressure: after weeks of automaticity, crisis induces a return to resident hand/foot monitoring, re-introducing the 4.76 mm-class error penalty. Another trap is unilateral practice: because cross-hand transfer is “considerably greater” in error [PAGE 620, footnote], weeks of training with the dominant hand do not fully transfer to the non-dominant limb without explicit cross-hand practice. A temporal trap exists if drills are paced faster than the two-second buffer: repeated initiation at \( \Delta\tau < \tau^{*} \) encodes inaccurate mappings over weeks of practice, so tempo must be constrained, not merely maximized.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates as a second-order theory-of-mind failure. Under ordinary conditions the performer holds a first-order model: “I attend to the target; my hand fetches it” [PAGE 620]. Under crisis—observation, evaluation, or competition—the performer recruits a recursive model: “I am being watched; therefore I must monitor my own hand/feet to avoid error.” This shift from remote optical consciousness to resident proprioceptive consciousness is precisely the mechanism James identifies as degrading aim: “think of your hand, and you will very likely miss your aim” [PAGE 620]. The regression is self-fulfilling: the model of the observer watching the performer’s means causes the performer to attend to means, increasing endpoint error.

The counter-trap inverts this recursion. A performer or coach can induce the opponent to adopt resident consciousness by directing the opponent’s attention onto their own means—through timing pressure, gaze manipulation, crowd noise, or explicit cueing about foot position or grip—causing the opponent to regress and degrade their own accuracy. The defensive counter-trap is external-focus self-cueing: “keep your eye on the place aimed at” [PAGE 620], which immunizes the performer against induced self-monitoring by maintaining the remote optical reference.

The clock is the \( \tau^{*} = 2.0 \) s localization-to-initiation buffer [PAGE 620, footnote]. Crisis compresses decision time. If the available clock runs below \( \tau^{*} \), the performer faces a forced choice: initiate a rushed motor program with elevated \( \varepsilon \), or slow down to engage conscious proprioceptive monitoring, which also elevates \( \varepsilon \) through the resident-consciousness penalty. Recursive modelling of the opponent consumes clock cycles, pushing \( \Delta\tau \) below the buffer and triggering regression. Thus the crisis loop is: recursive social model → attentional shift to means → clock compression below 2 s → error increase → further crisis. The escape is to protect the 2 s buffer and maintain remote optical focus despite social recursion.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The James–Southard principle is not a universal law; it is vetoed whenever deeper physical, biomechanical, or environmental layers override the sensory-attentional claim.

Physical veto: reachability. Let the reachable workspace be \( \mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^3 \) [m] defined by limb length and joint range. If \( \mathbf{x}_T \notin \mathcal{W} \), then \( \inf \varepsilon > 0 \) regardless of attentional allocation. Visual remote focus cannot move the hand beyond anatomical limits. James’s beam-walking claim [PAGE 620] presupposes the beam is within the locomotor workspace.

Biomechanical veto: support and surface precision. James states we walk a beam better the less we think of foot position [PAGE 620]. Let beam width be \( w_{\text{beam}} \) [m], foot width \( w_{\text{foot}} \) [m], and required foot-placement precision \( \sigma_{\text{foot}} \) [m]. If \( w_{\text{beam}} - w_{\text{foot}} < k \cdot \sigma_{\text{foot}} \) for safety margin \( k \), or if surface friction \( \mu \) falls below a critical \( \mu_{\text{crit}} \) (ice, rain, loose gravel), resident foot monitoring becomes obligatory and remote optical focus is vetoed. Optical consciousness cannot compensate for an insufficient support polygon.

Weather/environmental veto: optical reliability. Visual cue precision \( \sigma_{\text{visual}} \) [mm] is a function of visibility \( V \), luminance \( L \), and contrast \( C \). In darkness, fog, glare, or heavy rain, \( \sigma_{\text{visual}} \) rises. The James ordering holds only if \( \sigma_{\text{visual}} < \sigma_{\text{proprio}} \). When vision is degraded or removed—eyes shut in the anesthetic footnote [PAGE 620, footnote]—some patients become inaccurate, and some cannot move at all, showing that the veto is absolute under deafferentation plus darkness.

Adjudication rule: deploy the remote optical strategy iff all conjuncts hold: \( \sigma_{\text{visual}} < \sigma_{\text{proprio}} \); \( \mathbf{x}_T \in \mathcal{W} \); \( \tau_{\text{onset}} - \tau_{\text{localize}} \approx \tau^{*} = 2.0 \) s; and terrain/support is predictable within \( k \cdot \sigma_{\text{foot}} \). Veto if any conjunct fails. Units: errors and sigmas in mm or m; time in s; friction dimensionless \( \mu \). The source supplies no neural equations and explicitly defers that inquiry [PAGE 620], so all neural implementation claims are extra-source assumptions subject to these veto conditions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Cross-hand transfer veto. Counterfactual: a subject locates the target with the left hand but must touch it with the right. Prediction derived from source: \( \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{\text{cross}}] > \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{\text{same}}] \), with the cross-hand error “considerably greater” [PAGE 620, footnote]. Verifiable test: 3D motion capture measuring endpoint error under same-hand versus cross-hand conditions. Veto condition: if \( \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{\text{cross}}] \approx \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{\text{same}}] \), a purely visual exocentric relay bypasses limb-centered proprioceptive frames and James’s intermanual cost is falsified; this should occur only under callosal compromise or when visual dominance fully suppresses proprioceptive handoff.

Scenario 2 — Sensory deafferentation veto. Counterfactual: total darkness or peripheral anesthesia of the reaching limb with eyes shut. Prediction: the visual advantage disappears; \( \varepsilon \) rises to the tactile baseline of 17.13 mm or movement fails entirely, mirroring the anesthetic patients who cannot move their limbs with eyes shut [PAGE 620, footnote]. Verifiable test: compare lit versus blindfolded reaching error in neurologically intact subjects and deafferented patients. Veto condition: if \( \varepsilon \) remains at 12.37 mm in darkness, a non-visual, non-proprioceptive channel must be supplying target information, falsifying James’s sensory-channel dichotomy.

Scenario 3 — Temporal buffer veto. Counterfactual: movement initiation is forced at \( \Delta\tau = 0.5 \) s or delayed to \( \Delta\tau = 12.0 \) s after localization, rather than the \( \tau^{*} = 2.0 \) s optimum [PAGE 620, footnote]. Prediction: \( \varepsilon(\Delta\tau) \) is U-shaped with a minimum near 2.0 s and significantly higher error at both 0.5 s and 12.0 s. Verifiable test: manipulate localization-to-initiation asynchrony while holding target and effector constant. Veto condition: if \( \varepsilon \) is statistically flat across \( \Delta\tau \in [0.5, 12] \) s, no two-second buffer exists and the footnote’s maximum reflects practice or ordering effects rather than a cognitive clock; the temporal operator would then be vetoed.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
