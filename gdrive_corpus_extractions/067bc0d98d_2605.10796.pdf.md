The paper’s inferential chain is: elite event counts \(X\) and goal difference \(Y\) train a model \(f\); \(f\) is then applied to NTHU event counts \(X'\) without retraining; SHAP and CIS produce global feature rankings \(I_j(D)\); Spearman rank correlations compare rankings across seeds, domains, and explanation methods. The strong interpretation is that low elite-to-NTHU rank agreement and low SHAP-CIS agreement indicate structural ambiguity in the target domain, not merely a noisier version of elite football. That interpretation is supported by convergence across two model classes, two explanation methods, and multiple elite leagues. The paper reports high elite-elite agreement and lower elite-NTHU agreement under both RF and MLP, with a striking seed-stability drop for MLP at NTHU: “RF remains highly stable (µ = 0.988), whereas MLP drops to µ = 0.231 and shows much wider dispersion (approximately 0.000–0.485)” [PAGE 12]. It also reports that “agreement between SHAP and CIS decreases substantially in the NTHU university football domain” [PAGE 14].

The gray-zone audit is whether this evidence identifies structure rather than sample size, annotation, model class, or physical/environmental mismatch. The paper itself concedes that “alternative explanations, including limited target-domain sample size, site specificity, and annotation variability, cannot be fully excluded” [PAGE 14-15]. This matters because NTHU data are only 17 official matches from one institution, used “exclusively for inference and interpretability analysis” [PAGE 6], and “Predictive metrics on university football are not reported, as the study does not aim to evaluate generalization accuracy across domains” [PAGE 7-8]. Thus the paper does not claim predictive generalization; it claims explanation instability. That is a weaker and more defensible claim. The rank-correlation operator is also not causal: a low Spearman correlation can arise from genuine reordering, small-sample rank noise, ties, annotation drift, off-support perturbations, or model-specific extrapolation. CIS is especially exposed because its directional targets are built from elite means and standard deviations, so a target-domain feature can be perturbed toward an elite reference that is outside its own support. SHAP is exposed because its background is elite training data: “Elite training data are used exclusively to construct SHAP backgrounds and CIS reference statistics (no university samples are used)” [PAGE 9].

The correct adjudication is therefore tiered. Tier 1, strongly supported: elite-derived global explanations are not method-invariant or seed-stable when transported to this small university target domain. Tier 2, moderately supported: the NTHU reordering is consistent with structural divergence rather than pure method artifact, because it appears across RF and MLP and across SHAP and CIS. Tier 3, not established: that university football has a specific alternative causal hierarchy, or that the observed rankings are free of sample-size, annotation, weather, surface, match-duration, and physical-load confounds. The paper’s own reframing is appropriately diagnostic: “explanation instability and method disagreement are not interpreted as methodological failures. Instead, they serve as diagnostic signals of structural ambiguity under domain shift” [PAGE 16]. The audit should thus preserve the diagnostic reading while vetoing causal overreach.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source defines the prediction target as goal difference:
\[
y=\text{Goal}-\text{Enemy Goal},
\]
reported as Equation (1) [PAGE 7]. Units are goals, or a dimensionless integer goal-difference count; positive values indicate winning performance and negative values losing performance. Breakdown occurs if “Goal” and “Enemy Goal” semantics differ across domains, if own goals or shootout events are coded inconsistently, or if event definitions are not aligned. The paper states that elite event semantics “were aligned to Opta-style definitions” [PAGE 4], and NTHU annotations were “manually performed by three certified referees following OPTA guidelines” [PAGE 6], but alignment is still an assumption, not a proof.

The SHAP operator is:
\[
\phi_j(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq F\setminus\{j\}}\frac{|S|!(p-|S|-1)!}{p!}\left[f(S\cup\{j\})-f(S)\right],
\]
Equation (2) [PAGE 8], where \(F=\{1,\ldots,p\}\) and \(S\) excludes feature \(j\). Units are the same as the model output, i.e. goal-difference units. Assumptions: a well-defined coalition value \(f(S)\), a fixed deterministic model, and an appropriate conditional or interventional value function. Exact computation is exponential in \(p\); TreeExplainer for RF and DeepExplainer for MLP are approximations whose breakdown regimes include strong feature dependence, off-manifold backgrounds, nondeterministic models, and unstable background sampling. The paper fixes “five random seeds (0, 1, 2, 3, and 4)” [PAGE 8].

Global SHAP importance is:
\[
I_j^{\mathrm{SHAP}}(D)=\frac{1}{N_D}\sum_{n=1}^{N_D}\left|\phi_j(x_n)\right|,
\]
Equation (3) [PAGE 8], in goal-difference units. It is undefined if \(N_D=0\); it is zero if all attributions vanish; it is not a causal effect and not a normalized probability. Large values mean the model relies strongly on feature \(j\), not that feature \(j\) causes performance.

The CIS reference target is:
\[
t_j=\mu_j^{\mathrm{elite}}+k_j\sigma_j^{\mathrm{elite}},
\]
Equation (4) [PAGE 8], in event-count units, where \(k_j\in\{+1,-1\}\). The paper sets \(k_j=-1\) for failure-type features and \(k_j=+1\) otherwise, with \(t_j\) lower-bounded at 0 for decrease perturbations. Breakdown occurs if \(\sigma_j^{\mathrm{elite}}=0\) and the target collapses to the mean, if the target lies outside the target-domain support, or if elite scale is not comparable to NTHU scale. The perturbation is:
\[
x_{n,\ell}^{(j)}=
\begin{array}{l}
\max(x_{n,j},t_j)\ \text{if }\ell=j\text{ and }k_j=+1,\\
\min(x_{n,j},t_j)\ \text{if }\ell=j\text{ and }k_j=-1,\\
x_{n,\ell}\ \text{if }\ell\ne j.
\end{array}
\]
Equation (5) [PAGE 9], in event-count units. If the feature is already beyond the target in the specified direction, the perturbation is a no-op and contributes zero. If features are strongly correlated, the single-feature perturbation creates an off-manifold sample; CIS then measures model sensitivity, not causal importance.

The unnormalized and normalized CIS are:
\[
\mathrm{CIS}_j(D)=\frac{1}{N_D}\sum_{n=1}^{N_D}\left|f\left(x_n^{(j)}\right)-f(x_n)\right|,
\]
Equation (6) [PAGE 9], in goal-difference units, and
\[
\widetilde{\mathrm{CIS}}_j(D)=\frac{\mathrm{CIS}_j(D)}{\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mathrm{CIS}_k(D)},
\]
Equation (7) [PAGE 9], dimensionless and summing to 1. The normalized form is undefined if the denominator is zero, i.e. if every feature perturbation leaves the prediction unchanged; it is also unstable if the denominator is near zero or if \(p=0\). “CIS is deterministic given a trained model” [PAGE 9], but that determinism does not remove model-extrapolation error.

Rank agreement uses Spearman correlations. For no ties:
\[
\rho=1-\frac{6\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)},
\]
dimensionless, with \(d_i\) the rank difference. With ties, Spearman is Pearson correlation on ranks. Breakdown regimes: \(n \lt 3\), all ranks tied, missing or differently defined features, and rank comparisons made across domains with different supports. The paper uses rank-based Spearman “to measure agreement between feature importance rankings across seeds, domains, and explanation methods” [PAGE 10]. Predictive validity is reported with MAE and RMSE in goal units:
\[
\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|y_i-\hat y_i|,\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}.
\]
Table 2 reports, for example, MLP \(1.180\pm0.009\) MAE and RF \(1.202\pm0.005\) MAE on the elite test set [PAGE 10]. These metrics are not reported for NTHU by design [PAGE 7-8], so they cannot support a target-domain predictive claim.

A further audit point: the supplied Table 1 contains “[PHONE]” placeholders in some cells [PAGE 5], limiting exact reconstruction of all league-season counts from the excerpt, although the prose reports Ligue 1 total 1065 and Bundesliga total 916, and notes that “Ligue 1 reduced from 20 to 18 teams” in 2024/25 [PAGE 5]. This is a provenance gray zone, not a fatal flaw, but it weakens independent auditability of the elite source-domain completeness.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s conclusion retroactively repositions an older research lineage. Reep and Benjamin’s 1968 work on skill and chance in association football [PAGE 2, PAGE 18-19] established that match outcomes involve stochasticity as well as skill. Carling et al.’s 2007 match-analysis framework [PAGE 2, PAGE 17] formalized event coding and contextual interpretation. Sarmento et al.’s reviews [PAGE 2-3] consolidated recurring elite indicators such as possession control, shooting efficiency, and defensive organization. The later XAI layer—SHAP from Lundberg and Lee [PAGE 8], DeepLIFT from Shrikumar et al. [PAGE 4], and football-specific XAI applications [PAGE 4]—treated attribution rankings as transparent evidence. The present paper’s retrocausal move is to say that this trust was conditional: explanation stability is not a property of the model alone but of the model-method-domain interaction. That is why the final framing of instability as a “diagnostic signal” [PAGE 16] recasts earlier assumptions about transferable interpretability.

The scientific lineage also includes competition-level comparisons: Franca et al. and Stafylidis et al. are cited for the claim that “Competitive level is known to affect physical, technical, and tactical characteristics of football performance” [PAGE 4]. The paper therefore sits between two traditions: elite performance-indicator research and domain-shift/XAI robustness research. Its novelty is not a new predictor but an audit of whether elite-derived explanations survive transport to university football.

Multi-week traps are easy to fall into. First is the elite-prior trap: training on elite data, then treating elite SHAP rankings as universal because the feature space is identical. The paper explicitly warns that “explanations derived from elite football models should not be directly transferred to university or amateur contexts, even when feature definitions are identical” [PAGE 15]. Second is the single-method trap: using only SHAP and calling it interpretability. The paper’s SHAP-CIS comparison shows elite RF agreement \(0.932\pm0.019\) and elite MLP \(0.826\pm0.050\), but NTHU RF \(0.420\pm0.018\) with median \(p=0.10\) and NTHU MLP \(0.323\pm0.178\) with median \(p=0.24\) [PAGE 14]. Third is the seed-lottery trap: reporting one seed or averaging seeds without checking rank reproducibility. Fourth is the sample-size trap: interpreting 17 NTHU matches as a stable target-domain hierarchy. Fifth is the annotation-drift trap: three referees with majority/median adjudication [PAGE 6] reduce but do not eliminate coding variance. Sixth is the model-class trap: RF’s stability can mask MLP’s instability, so a single architecture can produce false confidence. Seventh is the causal-language trap: SHAP and CIS are sensitivity/attribution operators, not causal identification. Eighth is the temporal-clock trap: elite seasons 2022/23–2024/25 and a three-year NTHU window may not be commensurate in match duration, schedule, or competition format [PAGE 5-6]. These traps compound over weeks of analysis if not pre-registered as audit checks.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when an analyst confronts domain shift and retreats to the elite hierarchy as the default explanatory frame. The habit says: if NTHU rankings differ, the target is noisy or the model is unstable; the elite structure remains the reference truth. The paper resists that habit by treating instability as informative, but the habit remains a live risk because elite data are large and NTHU data are small. The recursive problem is that the analyst’s prior about elite stability shapes what counts as “noise” in the target domain.

The counter-trap is overcorrection: treating the NTHU-specific rankings as a discovered local hierarchy, especially from the MLP SHAP results where seed stability collapses to \(\mu=0.231\) [PAGE 12]. A coach or reviewer could overread a rank shift—for example, “Touches rises from rank 5 (elite) to rank 2 (NTHU)” under RF CIS [PAGE 11] or “Saves rises from rank 8 (elite) to rank 2 (NTHU)” under MLP SHAP [PAGE 11]—as a tactical law. The paper’s own caution that sample size, site specificity, and annotation variability “cannot be fully excluded” [PAGE 14-15] is the counter-trap brake. The correct recursive stance is: the model’s explanation is evidence about model behavior under a target sample, not direct evidence about the coach’s tactical reality.

The clock is the third regulator. Elite data span three recent seasons and a fixed extraction window from June to July 2025 [PAGE 5]; NTHU data span 17 official matches over three years and “diverse competition formats, playing surfaces, and match durations” [PAGE 6]. A ranking comparison without a matched clock—same season phase, same match duration, same competition format, same surface—can confuse temporal/environmental mismatch with structural ambiguity. The recursive theory of mind must therefore model three observers: the model, the analyst, and the practitioner. The model sees feature attributions; the analyst sees seed and method variance; the practitioner sees actionable tactics. Stability must be negotiated across all three, not projected from one. The paper’s strongest stance is recursive but disciplined: “interpretability should be viewed as a domain-dependent property reflecting the degree of structural consistency in the underlying data” [PAGE 15].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Let \(X\) be event-count features, \(Y\) goal difference, and \(Z\) a vector of physical, biomechanical, and environmental covariates such as training load, player development, technique, fatigue, playing surface, weather, and match duration. The source model is \(f\) trained on elite \(P_S(X,Y)\). Explanation importance is a functional \(I_j(D)=F_j(P_D,f)\). Transferability of explanation rankings requires that \(I_j(S)\approx I_j(T)\), but if \(P_S(Z)\ne P_T(Z)\) and \(Z\) affects \(X\) or \(Y\), then \(P_S(X,Y)\ne P_T(X,Y)\) generally, and there is no mathematical guarantee that \(F_j(P_S,f)=F_j(P_T,f)\).

Physical layer veto: the paper notes university football has “limited training time, heterogeneous player skill levels, and variable team composition” [PAGE 2]. These are not cosmetic; they change event rates, roles, and execution. Biomechanical layer veto: pass success, tackle success, shot execution, and save events depend on technique and fatigue; a ranking shift in “Saves” or “Touches” [PAGE 11] can reflect execution constraints rather than a new tactical hierarchy. Weather/environmental layer veto: the source reports “diverse competition formats, playing surfaces, and match durations” [PAGE 6], but the reported feature space contains only 19 event categories [PAGE 7] and no weather or surface covariates. Therefore weather and surface are unobserved confounders, not controlled variables.

A hard veto applies if event semantics or match duration are non-comparable. Event counts are aggregated “at the match level” [PAGE 7]; if match duration differs, count features scale with duration, and CIS targets \(t_j=\mu_j^{\mathrm{elite}}+k_j\sigma_j^{\mathrm{elite}}\) [PAGE 8] become off-support. A soft veto applies if support overlaps but distributions shift: rankings may still be computable, but they are not causally comparable. The paper’s conclusion survives this veto only in its diagnostic form: it says instability is a signal of structural ambiguity, not that NTHU has a proven alternative causal structure. A strong claim that NTHU reordering is pure tactical structure is vetoed because physical, biomechanical, weather, surface, duration, and annotation layers are not fully identified. The appropriate adjudication is: accept “limited transferability of explanations”; reject “causal hierarchy established.”
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
