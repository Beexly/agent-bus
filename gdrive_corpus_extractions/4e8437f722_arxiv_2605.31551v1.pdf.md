**Provenance lock and first-pass audit.** The drive artifact is stamped `arXiv:2605.31551v1 [cs.CV] 29 May 2026` [PAGE 1], titled "SMART: SMPLest-X Mesh Adaptation and RAFT Tracking for Soccer Pose Estimation," with a four-page budget that forces every claim to be either tabulated or asserted in prose. That compression is itself an evidence channel: the paper's strongest claim (depth supervision dominance) appears as an un-tabulated sentence — "Depth supervision is the dominant factor: without it, local MPJPE stagnates at 0.067 m regardless of augmentation" [PAGE 3] — while its weakest claims (RAFT superiority) occupy a full ablation table, Table 3 [PAGE 3]. I flag this inversion early because it determines which multi-hop chains are load-bearing.

**Hop 1 — metric algebra and reconciliation.** The score is defined as "L = Global MPJPE + 5 × Local MPJPE, reflecting the 5× higher leverage of local pose quality" [PAGE 1]. Recomputing from the rounded components: val 0.370 + 5(0.055) = 0.645 vs. reported 0.647; test 0.324 + 5(0.054) = 0.594 vs. reported 0.593; baseline 0.602 + 5(0.090) = 1.052 vs. 1.053 [PAGE 2, Table 1]. The naïve-fine-tune row reconciles exactly (0.425 + 5(0.079) = 0.820) [PAGE 3]. The residual ±0.002 is exactly what three-decimal rounding permits once the local term is amplified 5×, so the scores are internally consistent under the assumption that they were computed from unrounded components. This matters downstream: any RLVR check that recomputes score from printed components will produce a ±0.003 band of false positives unless the tolerance is set explicitly.

**Hop 2 — the 5× weight is a solver-tempting gradient.** Because ∂L/∂E_local = 5 while ∂L/∂E_global = 1, the metric pays fivefold for root-relative fidelity. The paper accidentally demonstrates the exploit: "+ Naïve fine-tuning … improves global MPJPE to 0.425 m (score: 0.820), but local MPJPE worsens from 0.065 to 0.079 m" [PAGE 3]. So the model traded a 97 mm global gain for a 14 mm local loss — and the metric rewarded it (0.846 → 0.820) but only barely, because 5 × 14 mm = 70 mm of the 97 mm gain was clawed back. Generalized: a solver is indifferent whenever ΔE_global > 5·ΔE_local. This is the exact indifference hyperplane and it explains why depth supervision is the dominant lever: depth improves *both* terms simultaneously (see Hop 4), so it moves perpendicular to the trade-off surface rather than along it.

**Hop 3 — the per-frame-intrinsics trap.** Page 1 states "Kt varies per frame to account for continuous zoom changes in broadcast production" [PAGE 1]. But §3.2 says flow is fitted to "an inter-frame homography via RANSAC and decomposing to (Rt, tt)" [PAGE 2] with no mention of K-normalization per frame. A plane-induced inter-frame map is H = K_t(R + t nᵀ/d)K_{t−1}⁻¹; decomposing requires isolating G = R + t nᵀ/d, whose singular values obey σ₂ = 1 exactly. If both frames are normalized by a *single* K̄ = K_{t−1}, the effective matrix becomes (K_{t−1}⁻¹K_t)G ≈ diag(r, r, 1)G for zoom ratio r, and σ₂ ≈ r ≠ 1. With a 2× zoom across one second at 50 fps, r = 2^(1/50) ≈ 1.0139, injecting roughly 0.014 rad ≈ 0.80° of spurious rotation per frame — twenty times the 0.041° reported in Table 3 [PAGE 3]. Since they report 0.041°, they must be doing the K-normalization correctly but omitting it from the text, or Table 3 is measured on zoom-quiet WorldPose clips. Either way the paper's own diagnostic — "Sequences with aggressive zoom cause apparent pitch-plane deformation that confounds the homography model, raising global error by up to 2×" [PAGE 3] — is physically misattributed. A homography of a plane is *closed* under zoom; zoom cannot deform a plane. What zoom actually breaks is the **intrinsics-blind decomposition**, not the geometry. This is a real gray-zone finding: right symptom, wrong mechanism, and the wrong mechanism generates the wrong fix (they blame the homography model; they should blame the decomposition's K-homogeneity assumption).

**Hop 4 — why depth supervision dominates, quantitatively.** World placement is p_t = R_t p_t^(cam) + t_t [PAGE 2], so ∂p_t/∂p_z = R_t e_z, a unit-norm sensitivity: one metre of pelvis-depth error maps to one metre of world error. Lateral error from a pixel error δu at depth z is z·δu/f; at z ≈ 40 m, f ≈ 2000 px (1920-wide broadcast frame), 1 px ≈ 20 mm. So depth is the only channel with O(1) gain — consistent with "Ldepth is L1 on camera-space pelvis depth, the single most important factor for world-space grounding" [PAGE 2]. The 44 mm global improvement attributed to foot anchoring [PAGE 3] corresponds to only ~2.2 px of effective lateral equivalence, which is exactly the scale one expects from a geometric constraint, not a learned one.

**Hop 5 — rotation-residual integration.** Table 3 reports 0.041° = 7.16 × 10⁻⁴ rad for RAFT-small + MAD [PAGE 3]. At z = 40 m that is 29 mm of instantaneous lateral displacement — already 9% of the 0.324 m test global MPJPE in a *single* frame. If residuals were independent across frames, the random-walk accumulation over a 250-frame (5 s) window would be √250 × 29 mm ≈ 0.46 m, exceeding the reported total. Therefore the residuals must be strongly correlated (bias-like), not white — which is precisely why the paper reports the effect as bounded drift ("up to 2×" under zoom) rather than as diffusion. The correct diagnostic is not the per-frame residual but its integral; the paper reports only the former.

**Hop 6 — statistics of the ablation.** Table 3's headline gap is 0.043° → 0.041°, a 4.7% relative change, measured over 19 clips [PAGE 3]. If clip-level rotation-error dispersion is even 0.02°, the standard error over 19 clips is ≈0.0046°, larger than the reported effect. Meanwhile the largest single lever in Table 2 is foot-plane anchoring (0.820 → 0.714, Δ = 0.106) [PAGE 3]. So the paper's rhetorical emphasis (RAFT) is inversely related to its evidentiary strength (anchoring, depth).

**Hop 7 — temporal filter transfer functions.** Global smoothing uses "Gaussian (σ=3 fr), clamp at 0.35 m/fr"; local uses "independent Gaussian (σ=1.5 fr)" [PAGE 2]. A Gaussian of scale σ has half-power cutoff f_c = √(ln2)/(√2 π σ) ≈ 0.3466/σ cycles·frame⁻¹. At σ = 3: f_c ≈ 0.1156 cyc/fr = 5.78 Hz at 50 fps; at σ = 1.5: f_c ≈ 0.2311 cyc/fr = 11.55 Hz. Sprint stride frequencies (~2–2.5 Hz fundamental, harmonics to ~10 Hz) therefore survive the local pass but are attenuated in the *global* pass — which is acceptable for a root trajectory but means the root is low-passed below the limb band, so the root/limb decomposition is not spectrally consistent. White-noise variance gain of a Gaussian kernel is Σg_k² ≈ 1/(2√π σ): 0.094 at σ = 3 (≈69% jitter-RMS reduction) and 0.188 at σ = 1.5 (≈34%). These are real closed-form numbers under the i.i.d. assumption, and they justify why the two-pass scheme beats a single filter. The 0.35 m/fr clamp at 50 fps equals 17.5 m/s — above human sprint speed — so it is a *teleport* gate, not a biomechanical prior.

**Hop 8 — the structural contradiction in §3.4 vs §5.3.** The learned 27-frame refiner "reduces local MPJPE by 22% on our WorldPose validation split" yet "competition performance degrades (score 0.680 vs. 0.647) as the refiner over-smooths fast actions not well-represented in its training distribution" [PAGE 3]. This is the cleanest transfer-failure signature in the paper: a model that wins on its own in-domain proxy and loses on the target. It is also the strongest argument that the Gaussian was tuned on the right distribution.

**Hop 9 — the val/test inversion.** Test (0.593) beats validation (0.647) by 8.3% [PAGE 2]. Since model selection used the validation split, validation should be optimistically biased. The inversion therefore requires an explanation outside selection: either the validation six sequences are intrinsically harder (a plausible reading given "6 validation, 14 test" [PAGE 1] and the disclosed aggressive-zoom failure mode), or the split's "matched camera-height and viewing-angle distributions" [PAGE 1] fails at the *sequence* level. That claim is about WorldPose, not the competition clips, so it does not license any assumption about competition val/test difficulty. This is an unregistered anomaly, and it is testable post hoc.

**Hop 10 — resolution floor.** Player crops are expanded "by 1.2× … then resized to 512 × 384 pixels" [PAGE 1]. In wide broadcast shots a player spans on the order of 100 px vertically; a 512-px resize is a 5× upsample that cannot create information below the input Nyquist limit. With a ~1.8 m player over ~120 px, 1 px ≈ 15 mm; a 3–4 px landmark localization floor is ≈45–60 mm — essentially the reported 54 mm local MPJPE [PAGE 2]. The local error is therefore plausibly sensor-limited, not model-limited, which reframes the "ViT-S could yield a 4× speedup with modest accuracy loss" claim [PAGE 3] as safe on the local term and risky only on the global term.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All quantities in SI metres unless stated; angles in radians internally, degrees where quoted from the source.

**1. Root and score operator.** With FIFA-15 joints j_{t,n,k} ∈ ℝ³ [m], k ∈ {0,…,14}, the root is verbatim "the mid-hip: rt = 1/2(jt,7 + jt,8)" [PAGE 1]. The competition functional is verbatim "L = Global MPJPE + 5 × Local MPJPE" [PAGE 1]:

E_global = (1/(|T||N|)) Σ_t Σ_n (1/15) Σ_{k=0}^{14} ‖Ĵ_{t,n,k} − J_{t,n,k}‖₂  [m]
E_local  = (1/(|T||N|)) Σ_t Σ_n (1/15) Σ_k ‖(Ĵ_{t,n,k} − r̂_{t,n}) − (J_{t,n,k} − r_{t,n})‖₂  [m]
L = E_global + 5·E_local  [m]

Assumption: the 5× amplification is a designer-chosen leverage, not a physical constant; unit is metres but the weighted sum is not a metric (it violates nothing formally but has no geometric interpretation). Breakdown regime: none mathematically, but the indifference hyperplane ΔE_global = −5·ΔE_local makes the objective non-identifiable along that direction — the "+ naïve fine-tune" row sits almost exactly on it [PAGE 3].

**2. Camera model and plane-induced homography.** Per-frame intrinsics K_t and radial coefficients k_t are provided, and "Kt varies per frame to account for continuous zoom changes in broadcast production" [PAGE 1]. For a world plane {X : nᵀX = d},

H_{t←t−1} = K_t ( R_{t←t−1} + (1/d)·t_{t←t−1} nᵀ ) K_{t−1}⁻¹

with exact structural constraint on G = K_t⁻¹ H K_{t−1}:

σ₂(G) = 1 exactly, σ₁ ≥ 1 ≥ σ₃, σ₁σ₃ = 1.

Breakdown: if both frames are normalized by one K̄ = K_{t−1} under zoom ratio r = f_t/f_{t−1}, then σ₂ ≈ r, and for r − 1 = 1.39 × 10⁻² (2× zoom in 1 s at 50 fps) the decomposition absorbs ~0.014 rad ≈ 0.80° of spurious rotation — 20× the 0.041° reported [PAGE 3]. Singular regime: r → 1 recovers exactness; large r triggers silent bias (never the |ΔR| > 60° gate, since 0.8° ≪ 60°). This is the *actual* mechanism behind the paper's "apparent pitch-plane deformation" language [PAGE 3], which is geometrically impossible: plane homographies are closed under zoom.

**3. Foot-ray anchoring and its exact singularity.** Verbatim: "The lowest foot pixel in the bounding box is back-projected to a camera ray using Kt and intersected with the known pitch-plane equation, yielding ground contact point ft; all joints are then translated so the lower ankle aligns with ft: Jt,j = Ŝt,j − Ŝt,ankle + ft." [PAGE 2, Eq. 1]. Formally, with camera centre c_t = −R_tᵀt_t and ray direction d_t = R_tᵀ K_t⁻¹ ũ_foot (normalized),

f_t = c_t + s·d̂_t,   s = (h_pitch − nᵀc_t) / (nᵀ d̂_t)  [m]
J_{t,n,k} = Ŝ_{t,n,k} − Ŝ_{t,n,ankle} + f_t  [m]

**Singularity:** nᵀd̂_t → 0, i.e. the foot pixel approaches the horizon line of the pitch plane. Then s → ∞ and f_t diverges. Sensitivity is the grazing-angle amplification:

‖δf_t‖ ≈ s·δθ / sin ψ,  ψ = angle(d̂_t, plane)

At a broadcast camera elevation of 10°–15° and a far player (s ≈ 50–80 m), sin ψ ≈ 0.17–0.26, so a 1-px angular error (δθ ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁴ rad at f ≈ 2000 px) yields 0.10–0.24 m of ground-point error. This is the dominant physical failure channel and it is *unbounded* in ψ — the paper's 2× global-error inflation under aggressive zoom is consistent with the far-touchline regime.

**4. Training objective.** Verbatim: "Ltrain = λ1L3D + λ2L2D + λ3Ldepth" with "λ1=1.0, λ2=0.1, λ3=0.5" [PAGE 2]. L3D is weighted MPJPE with "3.0 for wrists (5,6), ankles (11,12), big toes (13,14), and 1.0 otherwise"; Ldepth is L1 on camera-space pelvis depth. Sensitivity: ∂p_t/∂p_z = R_t e_z with ‖·‖₂ = 1, so depth error propagates 1:1 into world error, whereas pixel-lateral error scales as z/f ≈ 0.02 m per pixel at z = 40 m. Unit of λ: dimensionless; the 5:1 ratio between λ1 and λ3 is a *weighting choice*, not a calibrated inverse-variance, so the losses are not commensurate.

**5. Robust rejection operators.** MAD is defined as MAD = median_i |x_i − median_j x_j|; the robust z is z_i = 0.6745(x_i − x̃)/MAD (Gaussian-consistency constant), and the paper invokes "MAD outlier rejection (3σ threshold)" [PAGE 2]. Gray zone: "3σ" and "3 MAD-z" coincide only after the 1.4826 scaling in Leys et al. [PAGE 4, ref. 4]; the phrasing conflates σ with MAD units. Rotation gate: accept iff arccos[(tr(R_{t−1}ᵀR_t) − 1)/2] ≤ π/3, verbatim "|∆R| > 60◦ are rejected" [PAGE 2]. Breakdown: the gate is a *crash* detector, not a *bias* detector — it cannot see the sub-degree zoom-induced bias of §2.

**6. Temporal filters with units.** Global: "MAD (5×) + linear interp + Gaussian (σ=3 fr), clamp at 0.35 m/fr"; local: "independent Gaussian (σ=1.5 fr)" [PAGE 2]. For a normalized discrete Gaussian g_σ,

f_c = √(ln 2)/(√2 π σ) ≈ 0.3466/σ  [cycles·frame⁻¹]
noise gain Σ_k g_σ(k)² ≈ 1/(2√π σ)  [dimensionless]

σ = 3 fr → f_c ≈ 0.1156 cyc/fr ≡ 5.78 Hz @ 50 fps; variance gain 0.094 (RMS ×0.307).
σ = 1.5 fr → f_c ≈ 0.2311 cyc/fr ≡ 11.55 Hz @ 50 fps; variance gain 0.188 (RMS ×0.434).
The 0.35 m/fr clamp equals 17.5 m·s⁻¹ at 50 fps — above the ~12 m·s⁻¹ human sprint ceiling, so it is a teleport gate, not a biomechanical prior. Breakdown: at σ ≥ 5 fr the cutoff drops below the 4.5 Hz limb-harmonic band and the filter begins amputating kick mechanics — which is quantitatively the same failure the learned 27-frame refiner exhibits ("score 0.680 vs. 0.647") [PAGE 3].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The method's genealogy is a four-generation stack, and each generation imports an assumption the paper never re-derives.

*Generation 1 — parametric bodies.* SMPL-X encodes "human shape and pose as low-dimensional coefficients" [PAGE 1]; SMPLest-X (ViT-H, "687 M parameters") is the 2026 TPAMI scaling descendant [PAGE 1; PAGE 4, ref. 2]. The inherited assumption is a **fixed topology with a fixed vertex count** ("V =10,475 vertices" [PAGE 1]) and a fixed "per-joint vertex lookup: each of the 15 competition joints maps to a designated SMPL-X surface vertex, requiring no additional learned module" [PAGE 1]. Retrocaually, this is the paper's hidden rigidity: the 15 joints are read off a *surface*, so any shape-parameter error that changes body girth translates directly into joint displacement. A wrist vertex on a too-thick arm is displaced radially. This is why extremities receive 3× weight [PAGE 2] — the loss is compensating for a representational artifact, not a task property.

*Generation 2 — dense flow.* RAFT (2020) [PAGE 4, ref. 3] descends from the FlowNet lineage's all-pairs correlation volume. The imported assumption is *photometric constancy* on low-texture grass. The paper's own ablation shows the inheritance biting: "RAFT-small without filtering ties Lucas-Kanade, as dense flow over player silhouettes introduces noise that cancels the correspondence benefit" [PAGE 3]. The 714-landmark Lucas-Kanade baseline is the tournament's own ancestor; the descendant only beats it by 0.002° after MAD filtering.

*Generation 3 — geometric solvers.* EPnP (2009) [PAGE 4, ref. 5] and RANSAC homography decomposition are classical. The imported assumption is *a single calibration per solve* — the exact assumption per-frame zoom violates. The retromausal chain is therefore: 2009 EPnP pinhole assumption → 2026 per-frame-zoom data → silent sub-degree bias. The paper's contribution here is not a new solver but a **trigger condition**: "When field line intersections are detected via Hough transform, an EPnP … solve further corrects absolute camera position on the first frame" [PAGE 2] — i.e., the classical solver is invoked opportunistically only at t = 0, leaving frames 1…T−1 to pure incremental composition. That is the architectural root of the drift discussed in THINK_TRACE Hop 5.

*Generation 4 — data lineage.* WorldPose (ECCV 2024) [PAGE 4, ref. 1] supplies "pseudo-ground-truth 3D body poses" [PAGE 1]. Note the word *pseudo*: the supervision target is itself a model output. This is a second-order dependency — errors in the teacher become the student's ground truth, and the paper's remedy for the resulting distributional narrowing is a *split* ("89 clips at clip boundaries into a stratified split: 70 training and 19 validation clips with zero frame overlap and matched camera-height and viewing-angle distributions" [PAGE 1]) rather than a *label audit*. The chronology failure is documented: "adjacent clips share nearly identical camera angles, so the model overfits training-set viewpoints" [PAGE 3]. This is the canonical multi-week trap of video datasets — clip-boundary leakage — and it cost 14 mm of local MPJPE before being fixed.

*Multi-week traps, enumerated.* (a) Chronological split leakage — fixed. (b) Metric-gaming via the 5× local weight — partially fixed by depth supervision, still live on the indifference hyperplane. (c) Stateless-per-frame lifter — *unfixed*, explicitly: "SMPLest-X is per-frame only, making it fragile under occlusion" [PAGE 3]. (d) Learned temporal priors trained on a narrow action distribution — *abandoned* after a 0.033 score regression [PAGE 3]. (e) Baseline-lineage anchoring: "SAM-3D + MLP" (0.864) [PAGE 3] shows that grafting a learned placement head onto a weaker lifter generalizes "poorly across the range of camera heights" — a trap the authors escaped by switching lifters rather than by fixing the head.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three agents are in recursive play: the **metric designer**, who sets L = E_global + 5E_local [PAGE 1] expecting scale-consistency pressure; the **solver**, who reads that gradient and can trade absolute placement for root-relative fit (demonstrated in the naïve-fine-tune row [PAGE 3]); and the **evaluator**, whose held-out test set is 8.3% *easier* than validation [PAGE 2]. The recursion is unstable because the designer's model of the solver ("local accuracy implies careful geometry") is falsified by the solver's own ablation: local improved 0.065 → 0.079 *while* global improved 0.522 → 0.425 [PAGE 3]. Second-order inference: the designer's 5× weight was meant to prevent scale drift; it instead made pelvis depth (the one variable that moves both terms) the highest-leverage knob, which is precisely the variable they then had to supervise with Ldepth [PAGE 2].

**Crisis-habit regression.** Under occlusion the system reverts to its ancestral habit: a stateless per-frame regressor. The paper states the failure directly — "SMPLest-X is per-frame only, making it fragile under occlusion" [PAGE 3] — and the smoothing pass exists purely to paper over the regression: "Raw per-frame predictions exhibit jitter from detection noise and occasional misassociated crops under occlusion" [PAGE 2]. Regression signature is measurable: under occlusion the crop is misassociated, Ldepth is computed on the wrong person, and the 1:1 depth-to-world sensitivity converts a misassociation into a metre-scale jump — which is why the MAD pass needs a 0.35 m/fr teleport gate rather than a percentile threshold.

**Counter-trap.** The natural counter-move is to add temporal memory. The authors tried it and it backfired: the 27-frame sliding-window refiner "reduces local MPJPE by 22% on our WorldPose validation split" but "competition performance degrades (score 0.680 vs. 0.647) as the refiner over-smooths fast actions not well-represented in its training distribution" [PAGE 3]. This is the recursion's sharpest lesson: the counter-trap to a stateless model is a *distributionally narrower* model, and the narrowness is invisible on the proxy split. The resistance is diagnostic — the refiner learned the WorldPose action prior, not the physics.

**Clock.** The temporal substrate is fixed: broadcast at "50 fps" [PAGE 1], σ_global = 3 fr (5.78 Hz cutoff), σ_local = 1.5 fr (11.55 Hz cutoff), refiner window 27 fr (0.54 s), clamp 0.35 m/fr (= 17.5 m·s⁻¹, above sprint speed) [PAGE 2]. The clock is thus *over-provisioned for translation and under-provisioned for rotation*: a limb can move at 12 m·s⁻¹ (0.24 m/fr) and pass the gate, but the camera's 0.041° per-frame residual [PAGE 3] has no temporal gate at all beyond the 60° rejection. The habit, the counter-trap, and the clock all point at the same untested intervention — a *rotational* clock, i.e., a smoothed / integrated camera-pose prior, which the paper never ablates.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer A — physical veto (rigid-plane and pinhole closure).** The pitch is modeled as an exact plane with a known equation [PAGE 2]. Veto proof: a plane-induced homography is exact for *any* intrinsics pair, so zoom cannot deform it. Therefore the paper's stated mechanism — "aggressive zoom cause apparent pitch-plane deformation that confounds the homography model" [PAGE 3] — fails a physical closure test. The observed 2× global-error inflation is physical, but its cause is the *decomposition's* K-homogeneity assumption (§2 of the equations block), producing a spurious rotation of order |r − 1| rad. Veto status: the *observation* survives; the *mechanism* is vetoed and must be re-attributed before any fix is designed. Secondary physical veto: rolling shutter and residual radial distortion k_t are unmodeled; both produce frame-dependent apparent plane warp that mimics the reported symptom.

**Layer B — biomechanical veto (airborne and foot-contact semantics).** Eq. 1 snaps the skeleton so "the lower ankle aligns with ft" [PAGE 2], while ft is derived from "the lowest foot pixel" [PAGE 2]. Two independent vetoes: (i) **Airborne veto** — the paper concedes "foot-plane anchoring breaks when the player is airborne" [PAGE 3]. During a jump or dive the true ankle height reaches 0.3–0.6 m; the constraint forces it to h = 0, injecting 0.3–0.6 m of error, i.e., 1–2× the *entire* mean global MPJPE of 0.324 m [PAGE 2]. Even at a 3% airborne-frame rate, the mean bias contribution is ≈13.5 mm before the Gaussian pass smears it into neighboring frames. There is no airborne detector anywhere in the pipeline. (ii) **Joint-semantics veto** — the ray origin is the *lowest foot pixel* (heel or toe) but the snapped joint is the *ankle*. Ankle-to-toe horizontal separation is ~0.15–0.20 m; whenever the foot is near the ground and the toe is the lowest pixel, the ankle is systematically displaced by that amount along the ground. This is a signed, non-random bias, invisible to MAD (which is symmetric) and invisible to the local metric (which subtracts the root). Both vetoes are computable from GT annotations and neither requires retraining.

**Layer C — weather/illumination veto.** Broadcast augmentation covers "colour jitter (±0.4 brightness/contrast/saturation, ±0.1 hue)" [PAGE 2] — a global photometric transform. It does not cover *structured* illumination: hard shadow boundaries from stadium roof apertures, which are static in world coordinates but sweeping in image coordinates, and therefore (a) confuse Hough field-line detection used for the EPnP first-frame correction [PAGE 2], and (b) create dark contact regions under players that shift the "lowest foot pixel" toward the shadow centroid. Rain and wet pitch additionally specularize the grass, degrading RAFT's photometric constancy assumption on exactly the texture the paper cites as the reason for switching trackers ("substantially more reliable correspondences on broadcast grass textures" [PAGE 2]). Veto status: not tested, not disclosed, and structurally unaddressable by the stated augmentation. Note the corpus is 2022 World Cup footage [PAGE 1] — partially roofed stadia — so this layer is live, not hypothetical.

**Cross-layer conflict.** Layer A penalizes far players (grazing angle, s·δθ/sin ψ blow-up); Layer B penalizes contact-negative frames (airborne, toe-lowest); Layer C penalizes shadowed contact frames. All three converge on the *same* mechanism — the single-pixel foot ray — which is simultaneously the paper's best ablation (0.820 → 0.714 [PAGE 3]) and its least-guarded operator. The correct adjudication is not to remove the anchor but to gate it: an anchor should be applied only when (i) ψ exceeds a floor (say sin ψ > 0.15), (ii) the predicted ankle height is below a contact threshold, and (iii) the foot pixel's local gradient is consistent with a real silhouette rather than a shadow edge. None of the three gates exists in the described pipeline.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is computable from released artifacts (test annotations, per-clip errors, or a single retraining run) and each carries an explicit falsification threshold.

**V1 — Zoom-segmented decomposition audit.**
*Intervention:* partition the WorldPose validation clips (19 clips [PAGE 3]) into zoom-active and zoom-quiet windows using the provided per-frame K_t, then recompute Table 3's rotation error separately per partition, with and without per-frame K-normalization of the homography before decomposition.
*Prediction:* (a) the un-normalized variant's rotation error on zoom-active windows exceeds the zoom-quiet error by a factor ≥ 5; (b) with correct per-frame normalization the gap collapses below 1.5×; (c) the reported 0.041° is reproduced only under correct normalization, implying the paper's §3.2 text omits a step it actually performs.
*Falsified if:* the zoom-active/zoom-quiet error ratio is < 1.5 in both variants — which would mean per-frame intrinsics are not the bias channel and the "apparent pitch-plane deformation" attribution is correct after all.

**V2 — Airborne gating on the foot anchor.**
*Intervention:* using GT 3D joints, label each player-frame airborne iff min(h_left_ankle, h_right_ankle) > 0.10 m. Re-run the *existing* pipeline unchanged except for a gate that skips Eq. 1 on labeled-airborne frames and substitutes the smoothed prior root. No retraining.
*Prediction:* (a) the airborne subset's global MPJPE exceeds the overall mean by ≥ 2×; (b) gating recovers ≥ 15 mm of global MPJPE on the validation split, i.e., score improves by ≥ 0.015; (c) the recovery is concentrated in long sequences where the Gaussian σ = 3 fr [PAGE 2] currently smears a single teleport across ±9 frames.
*Falsified if:* gated improvement is < 5 mm — which would prove the airborne population is too sparse (< 1% of player-frames) for the mean metric to see, and would also mean the paper's own stated limitation [PAGE 3] is cosmetic rather than load-bearing.

**V3 — RAFT vs. Lucas-Kanade significance test.**
*Intervention:* paired bootstrap over the 19 clips comparing RAFT-small + MAD against the Lucas-Kanade baseline on downstream competition score (not on rotation error), holding foot-plane anchoring and the fine-tuned lifter fixed.
*Prediction:* (a) the score difference is ≤ 0.010 in absolute value; (b) the 95% bootstrap CI includes zero; (c) the 0.043° → 0.041° gap [PAGE 3] is inside one standard error given 19 clips, so the RAFT claim does not survive a properly powered test, whereas the foot-anchor claim (Δ = 0.106) does so with wide margin.
*Falsified if:* the paired CI excludes zero with |Δscore| > 0.010 — which would promote the camera tracker to a genuine second-order lever and demote foot anchoring to a tie.
