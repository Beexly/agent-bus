To formalize Tolman’s qualitative observations into a rigorous mathematical framework, we define the "Cognitive Map Field" $\mathcal{F}$ constructed by the agent over a temporal sequence of trials.

Let $S = \{s_1, s_2, ..., s_T\}$ be a sequence of actions chosen from a set of alternatives $A$. Each action yields an environmental feedback state $E_t$ and updates the internal sign-gestalt representation $G_t$.

**Operator 1: The Field Coherence Functional ($\Phi$)**
We postulate that the ease of forming a correct expectation (the "solution") is determined by the coherence of the accumulated field. Following Gestalt principles, whole patterns are more than the sum of parts. We define coherence using Mutual Information ($I$) and Entropy ($H$).
Let $X_t$ be the latent structural features of alternative $s_t$, and $O_t$ be the observed outcomes.
$$ \mathcal{F}(S) = I(X_S; O_S) - \lambda H(X_S | O_{t-1}) $$
Where $\lambda$ penalizes high conditional entropy (uncertainty) resulting from rapid switching.

**Operator 2: The Law of Temporal Sequences (Concentration vs. Alternation)**
For a two-alternative set $\{A, B\}$, we compare two temporal distributions: Alternating sequence $S_{alt} = (A, B, A, B)$ and Concentrated sequence $S_{conc} = (A, A, B, B)$.
Assuming a decay factor $\delta$ for short-term working memory activation, the effective signal-to-noise ratio ($SNR$) for identifying the unique features of $A$ in $S_{conc}$ is:
$$ SNR(A|S_{conc}) = \frac{\sum_{t \in A} P(X_A|O_t)}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \delta}} $$
In $S_{alt}$, the cross-contamination from $B$ increases the noise variance $\sigma^2$. Thus, $SNR(A|S_{conc}) > SNR(A|S_{alt})$, mathematically validating Tolman's intuition that a "concentrated bunch" aids in building "total relationships" [PAGE 418].

**Operator 3: Inventive Learning & Transfer Momentum ($M$)**
For inventive learning, let $D_{prev}$ be the "recent dwelling" duration on a preceding field-principle. The probability $P_{sol}$ of hitting upon the new solution depends exponentially on the alignment (dot product) between the active precedent principle vector $\vec{p}_{prev}$ and the target solution vector $\vec{p}_{target}$:
$$ P_{sol}(\vec{p}_{target} | D_{prev}) = \sigma \left( w \cdot (\vec{p}_{prev} \cdot \vec{p}_{target}) \cdot D_{prev} + b \right) $$
Where $\sigma$ is a sigmoid function. If $\vec{p}_{prev}$ is orthogonal to $\vec{p}_{target}$, increasing $D_{prev}$ actually drives $P_{sol}$ toward zero (interference/difficulty), aligning perfectly with the findings of Brown and Whittlell regarding how certain preceding types render new solutions "relatively difficult" [PAGE 418].

*Breakdown Regimes:* These equations fail (singularity occurs) if the environment is non-stationary—i.e., if the "true solution" changes faster than the decay rate $\delta$, rendering concentrated sampling a fatal error.
