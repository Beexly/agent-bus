The gray-zone audit must separate patent-claim novelty from operational validity. <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let the scene image be \(I:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}\), with pixel domain \(\Omega=\{1,\ldots,H\}\times\{1,\ldots,W\}\). Units: \(H,W\) in pixels; \(C\) in channels. The object localization output is a bounding region \(B=(x_0,y_0,w,h)\) with \(w,h\in\mathbb{N}\) pixels. The crop operator is
\[
I_c=\mathcal{C}_{B}(I),\qquad I_c(u,v)=I(x_0+u,y_0+v),\quad 0\le u<w,\;0\le v<h .
\]
Assumption: \(B\) lies inside \(\Omega\) or is padded. Exact singularity: if \(w=0\) or \(h=0\), then \(I_c\) has zero area and keypoint detection is undefined. If \(B\cap\Omega=\emptyset\), no object pixels exist. If the object extends outside \(\Omega\), the crop truncates the object and any keypoint outside the crop cannot be detected. The patent’s selection language supports both “selecting a region … of a predetermined size around the at least one object” and “selecting an outside portion of the image outside the region,” followed by “deleting the portion of the image of the scene outside the selected region … or deleting the selected outside portion” [PAGE 9]. Thus the crop is a hard masking operator, not a soft attention map.

The computational cost model is
\[
C_{\text{total}}=N_p c_p + A_c c_f + N_o c_o,
\]
where \(N_p\) is the number of candidate patches, \(c_p\) is patch cost in FLOPs per patch, \(A_c=wh\) is crop area in pixels, \(c_f\) is feature cost in FLOPs per pixel, \(N_o\) is the number of objects, and \(c_o\) is object-level overhead in FLOPs per object. For a full-frame baseline \(A_s=HW\), the ideal speedup is
\[
S=\frac{HW\,c_f+N_p c_p}{wh\,c_f+N_p c_p}.
\]
Assumption: fixed detector and fixed precision. Breakdown: as \(wh\to 0\), \(S\) approaches an overhead-bound constant and no longer improves; if \(wh\) is too small, keypoint accuracy collapses. The patent’s reduction is therefore not monotonic in speed versus accuracy.

Resampling and magnification are represented by
\[
I_d(u,v)=I_c(s_x u,s_y v),\qquad s_x,s_y>0,
\]
with \(s_x,s_y\) dimensionless scale factors. Downsampling requires an anti-alias prefilter when \(s_x<1\) or \(s_y<1\); otherwise aliasing corrupts keypoint texture. Upscaling \(s_x>1\) or \(s_y>1\) cannot create new optical information beyond the sensor modulation transfer function. Exact singular/breakdown regimes: \(s_x=0\) or \(s_y=0\) is undefined; \(s_x\) or \(s_y\) beyond the diffraction and sampling limit produces interpolated pixels, not evidence. The patent explicitly permits “magnifying the different image of the object to a predetermined size” [PAGE 11] and “performing a resolution adjustment … to upscale or downscale” [PAGE 11], so these limits must be audited.

For pose or keypoint detection, let \(y_{1:K}\) be \(K\) part locations in pixels. A generic detector computes
\[
\hat{y}_{1:K}=\arg\max_{y_{1:K}} p(y_{1:K}\mid I_c;\theta),
\]
with \(\theta\) the learned parameters. A Bayesian confidence with biomechanical prior is
\[
p(y_{1:K}\mid I_c)\propto p(I_c\mid y_{1:K};\theta)\,p_{\text{bio}}(y_{1:K}),
\]
where
\[
p_{\text{bio}}(y)\propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{E_{\text{bio}}(y)}{\tau}\right).
\]
Here \(E_{\text{bio}}\) may be in joules for an energy-style prior or dimensionless for a kinematic penalty, and \(\tau>0\) is a temperature in the same units as \(E_{\text{bio}}\). The patent states “generating a confidence score for the plurality of points in accordance with a biomechanical analysis of the model” [PAGE 14]. Breakdown: if \(\tau\to 0\), the prior dominates the image likelihood and the detector can reject valid unusual poses; if the prior covariance is singular, confidence is undefined. If joint angles are outside the calibrated range \([\theta_{\min},\theta_{\max}]\) in radians, or segment lengths deviate from \(\bar{L}\) in metres by more than a tolerance, the biomechanical veto should fire.

For multi-view confidence, let \(c_j\) be a scalar confidence in \([0,1]\) for keypoint \(j\). A consistency score can be written as
\[
c_j^{\text{multi}}=\sigma\!\left(\alpha\log p(y_j\mid I_c;\theta)+\beta-\gamma D_j\right),
\]
where \(D_j\) is a cross-view disagreement in pixels and \(\sigma(\cdot)\) is a logistic function. The patent describes “a second plurality of points obtained for that object from a second image of the scene” [PAGE 15], so \(D_j\) is supported by the disclosure. Breakdown: if all views share the same occlusion or calibration bias, \(D_j\) is falsely low and confidence is inflated. If \(D_j\) exceeds a threshold, the correct action is abstention or re-localization.

The three-dimensional model uses camera matrices \(P_i=K_i[R_i\mid t_i]\), with \(K_i\) in pixels and \(R_i,t_i\) in radians and metres. A 3D point \(X\) projects as
\[
\mathbf{x}_{ij}=\Pi(P_iX).
\]
Triangulation solves
\[
\hat{X}_j=\arg\min_X \sum_i \left\|\mathbf{x}_{ij}-\Pi(P_iX)\right\|_2^2 .
\]
The normal matrix is
\[
A=\sum_i \left(I-\mathbf{d}_i\mathbf{d}_i^{\top}\right),
\]
where \(\mathbf{d}_i\) is the unit ray direction, dimensionless. Exact singularity: \(\operatorname{rank}(A)<3\). This occurs when the baseline \(b=0\), when all rays are parallel or coplanar, when the object is at infinity, or when the points are collinear. The condition number \(\kappa(A)\) is dimensionless; large \(\kappa(A)\) means depth is unstable. A first-order depth uncertainty is
\[
\sigma_Z\approx \frac{Z^2}{f b}\sigma_d,
\]
with \(Z,b\) in metres, \(f\) in pixels, and \(\sigma_d\) in pixels. As \(b\to 0\) or \(Z\to\infty\), \(\sigma_Z\to\infty\). The patent’s triangulation path is explicit: “generating the at least one three-dimensional model of the object through triangulation of the plurality of two-dimensional models of the object” [PAGE 24]. Any 3D ROI feature identification must therefore be vetoed when \(\kappa(A)>\kappa_{\max}\) or \(\sigma_Z>\sigma_{Z,\max}\).

Feature identification is modeled as
\[
f^*=\arg\max_f p(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}};\phi),
\]
with entropy
\[
H(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}})=-\sum_f p(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}})\log_2 p(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}})
\]
in bits. The information gain relative to the prior is
\[
IG=H(f)-H(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}})\quad\text{bits}.
\]
If \(IG\le 0\), the ROI provides no evidence or contradicts the prior; the system should abstain. The patent’s feature path requires “performing feature identification on a portion of at least one of the plurality of images corresponding to the region of interest” [PAGE 21]. Breakdown: if the ROI is mislocalized in pixels, if perspective distortion is uncompensated, if motion blur exceeds the exposure-time blur budget, or if the identifying feature is occluded, then \(p(f\mid I_{\text{ROI}};\phi)\) is not identifiable from the available pixels. The source does not provide numerical thresholds, so all thresholds must be calibrated and reported with units before deployment.
