The lineage therefore runs from Knight’s theoretical incompleteness of the entrepreneur function, through Schumpeterian innovation rents, to modern Knightian uncertainty in macro-finance, where the failure to distinguish short-run fluctuation from long-run competitive equalization produces systematic over-investment traps.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (Reasoning-Layer Verifiable Rejection) counterfactual scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Transferability veto. Counterfactual: suppose half the enterprise’s investment is in land or specialized machinery that cannot be sold without loss, so \( \tau \to 0 \), consistent with [PAGE 304] “machinery, working-capital, land… which can be trans-.” Verification: observe capital-stock illiquidity and fire-sale discounts. Prediction: the realized entrepreneurial return \( r_E \) will not converge to \( r^* \) over any observable horizon; apparent “profit” is a liquidity premium, not Knightian pure profit, and entry/exit cannot arbitrage it away.

Scenario 2 — Risk-responsibility veto. Counterfactual: suppose no available asset—including “high-class ‘gilt-edge’ securities”—has a negligible risk-and-responsibility factor, so \( \rho \not\to 0 \) for all benchmarks. Verification: observe positive default or liability premia on all candidate benchmark securities. Prediction: the residual \( \Pi = (r_E - r^*)I \) is ill-defined because \( r^* \) does not exist; what accounting labels “profit” is entirely compensation for uninsurable uncertainty, and attempts to separate it from interest will yield statistically spurious residuals.

Scenario 3 — Technological-fluidity veto. Counterfactual: suppose technological change renders half the capital stock obsolete before the long-run average can be realized, so \( \phi \to 0 \) while \( T \) remains large. Verification: observe rapid depreciation or obsolescence of machinery within the investment period. Prediction: the expectational condition \( \mathbb{E}_t[R_{t+T}] = r^*_{\text{elsewhere}} \) fails because the alternative investment set itself shifts; capital exits the business not because the rate is low but because the asset’s technological identity has expired, producing a “profit” that is actually an avoidance-of-loss payment rather than a true surplus.
