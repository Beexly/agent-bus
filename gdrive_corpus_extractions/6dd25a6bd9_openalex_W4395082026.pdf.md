If it is an exponent, the units depend on the unknown units of \(x\) and \(c\); if it is a tuple, the expression is not a metric equation at all. A downstream skill score \(\hat y\) therefore has no declared SI unit or probability scale.

The second hop is kinematic. On [PAGE 6] the paper computes joint angle from three-point coordinates using vectors and a cosine relation. The displayed cosine denominator is \(|AB||AC|\), but the three points are \(A,B,C\) and the angle is at \(B\), so the denominator must involve \(|AB|\) and \(|BC|\). The numerator is written with a cross-product-like symbol, although a cosine law requires a dot product. If that typo is implemented literally, the angle is not the joint angle; if the denominator contains a zero-length segment, the quotient is undefined; if raw noisy coordinates push the cosine outside \([-1,1]\), \(\arccos\) returns NaN. This is a direct multi-hop failure: coordinate noise corrupts the angle, the angle corrupts similarity, similarity corrupts the skill score, and the skill score corrupts the coaching command.

The third hop is similarity. On [PAGE 6] the paper states “Closer proximity indicates a more homogeneous activity,” and defines distance as the Euclidean norm of differences between template angles \(T_i\) and action angles \(A_i\). This assumes synchronized, equal-dimensional, equally weighted joint-angle vectors. It is not time-warp invariant and is not invariant to missing joints, occlusion, or differing sampling rates. If one joint is missing, the entire vector can become NaN; if dimensions are mixed, radians and other quantities cannot be added coherently.

The fourth hop is sensor fusion. On [PAGE 8] the paper states “Accelerations in all three planes (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑧) may be detected using a tri-axial accelerometer,” then gives \(v_{xyz}=[DCM][IMU_x,IMU_y,IMU_z]^T\), \(\theta_{IMU}=\arctan(v_x/v_z)\), and \(\theta=\theta_{IMU1}-\theta_{IMU2}\). The arctangent is singular at \(v_z=0\) and is quadrant-blind; it should be \(\operatorname{atan2}(v_x,v_z)\). The DCM must be orthonormal and gravity must be separated from acceleration; otherwise orientation drift contaminates the joint angle. The angle difference also needs unwrapping modulo \(2\pi\).

The experimental gray zone is serious. On [PAGE 8] the paper reports “After 20 training iterations, the model achieves a test accuracy of 95.38% with a sampling interval of 5.” It does not report subject-disjoint splits, confidence intervals, architecture details, optimizer settings, or whether frames from the same athlete appear in train and test. The questionnaire on [PAGE 9] uses “Thirty college athletes and six coaches” and a “5-point Likert rating system,” which is ordinal, not an interval-scale measurement. The source also claims on [PAGE 2] that “This work is the first to tackle the issues of visual-inertial fusion turbulence and a latency period in mobile AR and VR,” but the paper does not present a turbulence model, latency distribution, or ablation isolating those effects. Thus the 95.38% figure is a point estimate on an unspecified split, not a validated generalization result.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source’s operators are partly categorical and partly kinematic. The reference archive \(T\), activity library \(X\), and instruction library \(C\) are described on [PAGE 5] as collections: \(T\) tracks skill-indicator scores or standards, \(X\) contains activities with group and difficulty labels, and \(C\) stores separate reinforcement commands. These are set-valued or categorical operators, not continuous measurements; they carry no SI unit unless a score scale is declared.

The first motion-capture relation is given on [PAGE 5] as
\[
m=(x,c,t)^3,
\]
where the source does not define units. If \(x\) is position in metres, \(t\) is time in seconds, and \(c\) is a categorical context label, then the expression is dimensionally ambiguous: an exponent would produce \(m^3 s^3\) for dimensionless \(c\), while a tuple would be a heterogeneous record. The feature map is
\[
p=e(x,c,t),
\]
and the skill map is
\[
\hat y=h(p)=h(e(m)).
\]
Assumption: \(e\) and \(h\) are single-valued and calibrated. Breakdown: if \(e\) is non-injective, different motions collapse to the same feature; if \(h\) lacks a declared codomain, \(\hat y\) has no unit; if timestamps are asynchronous, \(t\) is not a valid ordering variable. The paper gives no uncertainty propagation for this chain.

Joint-angle computation is on [PAGE 6]. For points \(A=(x_1,y_1)\), \(B=(x_2,y_2)\), \(C=(x_3,y_3)\),
\[
\vec{AB}=(x_2-x_1,\ y_2-y_1),\qquad
|\vec{AB}|=\left((x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
\[
\vec{BC}=(x_3-x_2,\ y_3-y_2),\qquad
|\vec{BC}|=\left((x_3-x_2)^2+(y_3-y_2)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The source then writes
\[
\cos B=\frac{\vec{AB}\times\vec{BC}}{|AB||AC|}.
\]
The corrected dot-product form, consistent with the law of cosines, is
\[
\cos\theta_B=
\frac{\vec{AB}\cdot\vec{BC}}{|\vec{AB}|\,|\vec{BC}|},
\qquad
\theta_B=\arccos(\cos\theta_B)\in[0,\pi]\ \text{rad}.
\]
Units: coordinates in metres, cosine dimensionless, angle radians. Singularities: \(|\vec{AB}|=0\) or \(|\vec{BC}|=0\) makes the quotient undefined; noisy coordinates can make the cosine fall outside \([-1,1]\), producing NaN; a 2D projection of a 3D joint biases the angle. The denominator typo \(|AC|\) instead of \(|BC|\) is a direct implementation hazard.

Action similarity is on [PAGE 6]:
\[
d(x,y)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_i-y_i)^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{distance}=
\left((T_1-A_1)^2+(T_2-A_2)^2+\cdots+(T_n-A_n)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
If \(T_i\) and \(A_i\) are joint angles in radians, the distance has units of radians. Assumptions: synchronized sampling, equal dimension \(n\), and comparable joints. Breakdown: \(n=0\) yields zero distance and false identity; missing joints yield NaN; mixed units make the sum invalid; Euclidean distance is not time-warp invariant and can be dominated by high-variance dimensions. The source’s statement “Closer proximity indicates a more homogeneous activity” [PAGE 6] is therefore conditional on those assumptions.

IMU relations are on [PAGE 8]:
\[
v_{xyz}=[DCM]\begin{bmatrix}IMU_x\\IMU_y\\IMU_z\end{bmatrix},
\]
\[
\theta_{IMU}=\operatorname{atan}\left(\frac{v_x}{v_z}\right),
\qquad
\theta=\theta_{IMU1}-\theta_{IMU2}.
\]
If \(IMU_x,IMU_y,IMU_z\) are accelerations, their units are \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\); if angular rates, \(\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\). The DCM is dimensionless and must satisfy \(R^TR=I\) and \(\det R=1\). Breakdown: \(v_z=0\) makes \(\arctan\) singular; \(\arctan\) is quadrant-blind; gravity must be removed; orientation drift corrupts \(\theta\); angle subtraction must be unwrapped. A robust replacement is \(\theta_{IMU}=\operatorname{atan2}(v_x,v_z)\), still conditional on a valid DCM.

For classification, accuracy is a dimensionless probability or proportion; a probabilistic confidence measure can be reported in bits by \(H(A\mid z)=-\sum_k p(a_k\mid z)\log_2 p(a_k\mid z)\). The source reports \(95.38\%\) on [PAGE 8] without confidence intervals or subject-disjoint validation. The Likert scale on [PAGE 9] is ordinal and unitless; arithmetic means of Likert codes require an explicit measurement model and are not SI quantities.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Reading the 2024 review backward shows a lineage from marker-based laboratory motion capture to markerless computer vision, then to inertial and visual-inertial fusion, then to AR and VR coaching interfaces. The paper’s own literature review cites Taha et al. on Kinect versus a low-cost IMU for badminton arm kinematics [PAGE 3], Bourdon et al. on training-load monitoring [PAGE 3], Gao on hidden Markov models and badminton landing/take-off errors [PAGE 2], Du on 3D CNN recognition of wrong actions [PAGE 3], and Host and Ivasic-Kos on human action recognition in sports [PAGE 4]. This genealogy explains why the paper mixes laboratory biomechanics, deep-learning action classification, and consumer AR/VR: these are separate scientific lineages with different validation norms.

The retrocausal trap is treating a 2024 “innovative application” claim as if it retroactively validates every earlier layer. A Kinect-versus-IMU comparison does not validate markerless multi-athlete tracking; a hidden Markov model for badminton serves does not validate a 3D CNN; a questionnaire about coach satisfaction does not validate joint-angle accuracy. Multi-week traps include calibration drift in cameras and IMUs, clothing and lighting changes, athlete habituation to AR feedback, coach novelty effects, and dataset leakage across sessions. If the same athlete appears in training and test across weeks, accuracy can be inflated by identity and clothing cues rather than skill. A second multi-week trap is label drift: coaches may redefine “correct” technique after seeing the system’s output, so the ground truth moves with the intervention.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive layer is athlete-coach-system mutual modeling. The athlete models the coach’s expectations and the system’s feedback; the coach models the athlete’s trust and compliance; the system implicitly models both through a skill score. Under crisis, habit regression appears: if the headset lags, the marker is occluded, or the score contradicts the coach, the athlete reverts to familiar verbal cues and prior movement habits. The counter-trap is over-trust: immediate AR feedback can create dependency, so the athlete stops using intrinsic proprioception and waits for the screen. The clock is the real-time constraint: latency and sampling interval determine whether feedback arrives before the next repetition. The source’s claim on [PAGE 2] about “visual-inertial fusion turbulence and a latency period” is precisely where recursive trust is most fragile, because a delayed or unstable score is interpreted socially as coach disagreement. The questionnaire on [PAGE 9] with “Thirty college athletes and six coaches” measures satisfaction, not whether the athlete’s internal model of the task improved. A safe design must therefore separate immediate feedback, delayed review, and coach override so that crisis regression does not become the default learning policy.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Admissibility requires a Boolean conjunction, not an average:
\[
\mathcal A_{\text{valid}}=\{a:V_S(m)=1\ \text{and}\ V_B(\theta,\tau)=1\ \text{and}\ V_W(\mathbf w,t)=1\},
\]
where \(V_S\) is sensor validity, \(V_B\) is biomechanical plausibility, and \(V_W\) is environmental validity. If any term is zero, the downstream skill score and coaching command are vetoed. For the physical layer, camera calibration, marker occlusion, and IMU orthonormality are necessary. For the biomechanical layer, joint-angle uncertainty can be expressed through
\[
C=\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|},
\qquad
\sigma_\theta=\frac{\sigma_C}{|\sin\theta|},
\]
with \(\theta\in(0,\pi)\) rad. This is singular at \(\theta=0\) and \(\theta=\pi\), and it becomes large when either segment length approaches zero. If \(\sigma_\theta\) exceeds the resolution needed to distinguish a safe from an unsafe technique, the biomechanical layer vetoes the score. For weather or airflow, the shuttle drag force is
\[
\mathbf F_D=\frac12\rho C_D A\|\mathbf v_s-\mathbf w\|(\mathbf v_s-\mathbf w),
\]
with \(\rho\) in \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), \(A\) in \(\mathrm{m^2}\), velocities in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), and force in newtons. The counterfactual drag perturbation is
\[
\Delta\mathbf F_D=\mathbf F_D(\mathbf v_s-\mathbf w)-\mathbf F_D(\mathbf v_s).
\]
If \(\|\Delta\mathbf F_D\|\) exceeds the force resolution assumed by the trajectory model, the environmental layer vetoes trajectory-based skill inference. Indoor badminton still has HVAC airflow; outdoor AR adds rain, fog, sun glare, and wind. The source does not model these layers, so its “real-life game” language on [PAGE 7] cannot by itself justify weather-robust claims.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: subject-disjoint validation. Intervention: hold out entire athletes, not random frames, and evaluate the same action classifier. Prediction: the reported \(95.38\%\) [PAGE 8] will not replicate if identity or clothing cues were present; a subject-disjoint accuracy below a pre-registered threshold, or a confidence interval that excludes the original point estimate, vetoes the generalization claim.

Scenario 2: joint-occlusion and viewpoint stress. Intervention: remove one joint coordinate for ten consecutive frames and shift the camera by a moderate angle. Prediction: the Euclidean distance in Eq. (10) will become NaN or will jump because the vector dimension is incomplete; if the action decision changes without an explicit missing-data imputation flag, the physical-layer veto triggers. A robust system must report uncertainty rather than silently substituting zero.

Scenario 3: IMU singularity and drift. Intervention: drive \(v_z\) through zero and add a small constant accelerometer bias over a short trial. Prediction: \(\theta_{IMU}=\arctan(v_x/v_z)\) [PAGE 8] will show a discontinuity or sign error near \(v_z=0\), and \(\theta=\theta_{IMU1}-\theta_{IMU2}\) will drift unless the DCM is re-estimated and gravity is removed. If the angle error exceeds the biomechanical tolerance or the sign flips across the singular point, the sensor-fusion veto triggers. These three tests are verifiable because they require only held-out subjects, controlled occlusion, and a known IMU perturbation, with the predicted failure modes stated before running the trials.
