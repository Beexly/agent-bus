<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The model descends from the classical football scoring literature: Reep et al. (1971) used a negative binomial for aggregate goals; Maher (1982) introduced independent Poisson scores by team; Dixon and Coles (1997) relaxed independence; Karlis and Ntzoufras (2000, 2003) inflated draw probability; Baio and Blangiardo (2010) placed Karlis–Ntzoufras in a Bayesian hierarchical frame [PAGE 2]. Whitaker et al. extend this by inserting player-level ∆ into the scoring intensity [PAGE 8, 16].

The player-rating lineage contrasts with TrueSkill (Herbrich et al., 2007), which cannot separate teammates who always play together—a fatal flaw in football where fixed pairings are common [PAGE 2]. McHale et al. (2012) collapse performance to one number, masking event-specific skill and mishandling injured/low-minute players [PAGE 2]. The present model instead estimates ∆ per event type and uses mean-field VI to scale to ~1.2 million events across 380 matches and 544 players [PAGE 3, 9].

Retrocausal trap: the paper validates 2013/2014-inferred ∆ by predicting 2014/2015 goal-count thresholds, effectively using the future season to grade the past. This is legitimate only if ∆ is temporally stable. The paper documents violations: Suarez transferred to Barcelona between seasons, collapsing Liverpool’s attack in the ability-augmented model faster than the baseline [PAGE 18]; Kane emerges from low minutes to high ability only after block 2 [PAGE 18]; Costa is identified immediately despite no 2013/2014 minutes [PAGE 18]. End-of-season block 5 shows the augmentation advantage nearly vanishing (AUC 0.62 vs 0.61), attributed to baseline saturation and late-season volatility (youth players, relegation motivation) [PAGE 19–20]. These are multi-week regime shifts that a static ∆ cannot track without a random-walk extension [PAGE 22].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The model embeds a theory of mind in which a player’s contribution to a match is determined by a latent skill ∆, scaled by minutes τ, and modulated by static own-team and opposition coefficients λ. Managers are modelled implicitly: τ_i,k is posited as a proxy for “how much a player interacts with their team” and “how put off they are by the opposition,” capturing formation importance without explicit tactical variables [PAGE 14].

Crisis habit regression: When a team enters a crisis (e.g., relegation battle), behaviour deviates from the stationary ∆ regime. Burnley exhibits a peak/trough after block 3 as they attack more to avoid relegation while conceding more in the process [PAGE 18]. The model, lacking a crisis state variable, regresses Burnley’s parameters toward the team mean rather than representing a motivated regime switch.

Counter-trap: Opposition adaptation is modelled only through λ_e2, a static “ability to stop” coefficient. If an opponent specifically marks a high-∆ attacker (e.g., Suarez), the model does not update λ within the season; it waits for the next block refit. This creates a lagged counter-trap vulnerability: elite attackers temporarily depress their own realised rates without the model attributing the drop to defensive adjustment.

Clock: Inference runs in blocks of 80 matches (final block 60), with block 0 = all 2013/2014 games and subsequent blocks accreting 2014/2015 matches [PAGE 16]. ∆ and team parameters only refresh at block boundaries. Injuries, suspensions, and transfers inside a block are invisible until the next refit, so the model’s “mental model” of a team is stale by up to one block (~80 matches, roughly one-fifth of a season).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: τ_i,k assumes a fixed 90-minute match with no extra time and treats substitutions only as exposure bookkeeping [PAGE 3, 5]. A red card, injury substitution, or goalkeeper change mid-match violates the constant-rate assumption over τ; the Poisson intensity η is taken as fixed for the whole exposure interval.

Biomechanical layer: No player fatigue, sprint distance, pitch location, or event sequencing enters the model. Event counts are assumed independent Poisson draws conditioned on ∆ and τ [PAGE 5]. Football exhibits strong within-match dependence (goals cluster after conceding, game-state effects), so the independence assumption is a structural veto for matches with late-game tactical shifts.

Weather layer: No weather covariate (rain, wind, temperature) is included. The home effect γ_e absorbs venue advantage but not match-day atmospheric conditions [PAGE 8, eq. (2)]. A match played in heavy wind that suppresses passing and shooting is indistinguishable from a low-∆ team match.

Veto rule: Any fixture with (a) a player listed in the starting lineup I_j^k who does not play, (b) material weather, (c) a red card or mid-match goalkeeper substitution, or (d) a promoted team with no prior block data violates model assumptions. Under these conditions the team-level f(q(∆)) term [PAGE 16] is contaminated by exogenous regime change, and the Poisson-log-normal hierarchy [PAGE 8] is no longer exchangeable across the block. The safe adjudication is to fall back to the baseline Baio–Blangiardo model (f(q(∆)) ≡ 0) for that fixture, or exclude it from prediction.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
