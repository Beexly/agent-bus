1. Information-theoretic entropy over corpus topics  
The philosophical article refers to the historical foundation of “quantitative theory of information” [PAGE 74]. If the corpus is coded into a finite topic set \(T=\{t_1,\dots,t_n\}\), with empirical probabilities \(p_i = P(T=t_i)\), the Shannon entropy is

\[
H(T) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \log_2 p_i \quad [\text{bit}].
\]

Assumptions:  
- Topics are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.  
- Each page or article receives a probability mass.  
- The coding is reproducible.  

Breakdown regimes:  
- If \(p_i=0\), the term is defined by continuity as \(0\log_2 0 = 0\).  
- If the distribution is not normalized, \(\sum_i p_i \neq 1\), the value is not entropy.  
- If one topic has \(p_i=1\), then \(H(T)=0\).  
- Maximum entropy is \(\log_2 n\) only when all topics are equiprobable.

2. Cross-domain dependency: mutual information  
To measure synergy between domains, such as economic texts \(X\) and medical texts \(Y\), define

\[
I(X;Y)=\sum_{x,y} p(x,y)\log_2\left(\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\right) \quad [\text{bit}].
\]

Assumptions:  
- Joint coding of article pairs exists.  
- \(I(X;Y)=0\) means statistical independence under the coding.  
- Positive value means shared information beyond marginal distributions.

Breakdown:  
- If any joint probability is zero while marginals are nonzero, the corresponding term is zero.  
- If coding categories change, the value changes; this is not an ontological constant.  
- Small corpus size makes estimation unstable.

3. Screening performance  
The oncology screening article states that screening aims at “early detection and less traumatic treatment at an early stage” [PAGE 88]. Let disease status be \(D^+\) or \(D^-\), and test result be \(T^+\) or \(T^-\). Define

\[
\mathrm{Se}=P(T^+|D^+), \qquad \mathrm{Sp}=P(T^-|D^-),
\]

where both are dimensionless probabilities. With prevalence \(\pi=P(D^+)\), the positive predictive value is

\[
\mathrm{PPV}=P(D^+|T^+) = \frac{\mathrm{Se}\,\pi}{\mathrm{Se}\,\pi + (1-\mathrm{Sp})(1-\pi)}.
\]

Assumptions:  
- The test is binary.  
- Disease status is verifiable by a reference standard.  
- Prevalence is known or estimable.

Breakdown regimes:  
- If \(\pi=0\), PPV is undefined even with perfect specificity.  
- If \(\mathrm{Se}=0\), the test cannot identify disease.  
- If \(\mathrm{Sp}=0\), false positives dominate when prevalence is low.  
- The source does not provide enough data to compute these values for Russian cancer screening [PAGE 85–89].

4. Circadian blood pressure operator  
The pediatric article reports hourly diastolic blood pressure monitoring and uses “mesor” language [PAGE 97–100]. A standard cosinor model is

\[
\mathrm{DBP}(t)=M + A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}+\phi\right)+\varepsilon(t),
\]

where:  
- \(M\) is mesor, in \(\mathrm{mmHg}\);  
- \(A\) is amplitude, in \(\mathrm{mmHg}\);  
- \(\tau=24\,\mathrm{h}\);  
- \(\phi\) is acrophase, in radians or hours;  
- \(t\) is clock time, in hours;  
- \(\varepsilon(t)\) is residual error.

Assumptions:  
- The rhythm is approximately sinusoidal.  
- Sampling is frequent enough to estimate \(M,A,\phi\).  
- The patient state is stationary over the observation window.

Breakdown:  
- If \(A\to 0\), the circadian model loses phase information.  
- The source identifies this as pathological: “unresponsive state of the vascular system” [PAGE 103].  
- Missing hours, treatment changes, or dialysis confound the estimate.  
- If the waveform is non-sinusoidal, a single harmonic is insufficient.

6. Nasal drug delivery constraints  
The intranasal formulation article states that “only small doses (1-200 µl) can be used” [PAGE 94] and that mucociliary transport moves particles to the nasopharynx “in 5-20 minutes” [PAGE 95]. Let administered volume be \(Q\) in \(\mu\mathrm{L}\), effective nasal absorptive area be \(A_n\) in \(\mathrm{cm^2}\), and residence time be \(\tau_c\) in minutes. A simple exposure proxy is

\[
E = \frac{Q}{A_n\,\tau_c},
\]

with units \(\mu\mathrm{L}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}\). This is not a bioavailability equation; it is a dimensional constraint on exposure.

Assumptions:  
- The dose remains in the nasal cavity during \(\tau_c\).  
- Absorption is not instantaneous.

Physical and biomechanical plausibility vetoes several strong readings.

1. Hypoxia resistance claim  
The article concludes that rural men have greater resistance to hypoxia than urban men [PAGE 137–142]. The measured variables include vital capacity, Stange test duration, Gench test duration, respiratory cycles per minute, chest excursion, and adaptive potential [PAGE 140]. However, the groups differ in age: urban mean age \(26.3\pm2.8\) years, rural mean age \(28.2\pm2.5\) years [PAGE 139]. The source also notes that caries damage was assessed by WHO criteria, but it does not provide a full causal model linking caries to oxygen transport. Physical veto: dental caries alone cannot directly determine systemic hypoxic tolerance unless it causes infection, pain, sleep disruption, or systemic inflammation. The observed difference may be due to habitual activity, occupation, body composition, smoking, or test familiarity. Therefore the claim is not vetoed as an association, but it is vetoed as a direct causal effect of caries or residence alone.

2. Non-traditional physical culture claim  
The adaptive physical culture article reports improved cardiovascular adaptation and reduced anxiety in students practicing non-traditional physical culture [PAGE 50–57]. The sample is small: 50 students, main group 30, control group 20 [PAGE 52]. The outcomes include ECG repolarization, PWC170, anxiety scales, and salivary immunity [PAGE 52–56]. Physical veto: the reported changes are plausible as stress reduction and autonomic modulation, but they are not proof of disease prevention. The study lacks blinding and detailed control of training load. Therefore the claim survives as a psychophysiological signal, but not as a clinical efficacy proof.
