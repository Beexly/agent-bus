At order one, having read the footnote logic on [PAGE 193], the firm recognizes that rivals may withhold supply when $f_i \cdot |\epsilon_D| \cdot \phi^{-1} > \kappa$; the best response to anticipated withholding is reciprocal withholding, producing tacit collusion and a partial-monopoly outcome without any explicit contract. At order two, the firm anticipates that if competition fully collapses, society will impose “some arbitrary system of distribution … based on ethics or political power or brute force” [PAGE 193]; therefore it lobbies for favorable regulatory status, preemptively accepts administered allocation, or merges to secure a position inside the authoritarian distribution mechanism rather than outside it.

Crisis habit regression enters here: under stress, agents abandon the competitive habit of decentralized bargaining and revert to command-and-control coordination, precisely because the text predicts that perfect competition “make[s] organization through free contract impossible” [PAGE 193]. The counter-trap is the symmetric error: assuming that because competition has historically decayed into authoritarianism, it must do so again, thereby neglecting that legal and moral restraints $R(t)$ may still hold and that the human-incapacity constraint may remain operative. The clock variable is the relative rate of change of power accumulation versus restraint adaptation. Define the breakdown time $\tau^*$ implicitly by
$$ R(\tau^*) = 0 \quad \text{while} \quad P_i(\tau^*) > S_c. $$
Before $\tau^*$, competitive habits may persist; after $\tau^*$, crisis habit regression dominates and the system recursively selects authoritarian coordination. The exact singularity is not a price level or a quantity but the institutional moment when $dP_i/dt > dR/dt$ and the social ceiling can no longer be enforced [PAGE 193]. Recursive theory of mind therefore predicts that firms will behave as if the authoritarian equilibrium is already partially realized, which accelerates its realization—a self-fulfilling prophecy rooted in Knight’s structural contradiction.</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The model on [PAGE 193] can be vetoed by physical, biomechanical, or environmental layers independent of economic assumptions. Physical layer: free contract requires signal propagation and contract enforcement within an adjustment window $t_{\text{adj}}$. If communication latency $\tau_{\text{comm}}$ exceeds $t_{\text{adj}}$, decentralized bargaining cannot converge before the state changes, rendering “organization through free contract impossible” [PAGE 193] a physical necessity rather than an economic tendency. Biomechanical layer: Knight’s saving constraint rests on the “human individual” being “normally incapable of wielding … much more industrial power” than society permits [PAGE 193]. This is a bandwidth limit: working memory, moral reasoning, and legal processing impose a finite channel capacity $C_{\text{human}}$ measured in bits per second of monitored counterparties. If artificial agents bypass $C_{\text{human}}$, then $H(P_i,R) \to 0$ regardless of the social ceiling $S_c$, and the veto removes the competitive fixed point entirely. Weather/environmental layer: an exogenous shock (crop failure, energy disruption, geopolitical blockade) reduces competing-supply flexibility $\phi$ in the footnote inequality [PAGE 193]. A sufficiently severe shock drives $\phi \to 0$, moving any $f_i>0$ into the profitable-withholding regime even if demand elasticity $\epsilon_D$ remains moderate.

Veto-proof adjudication: the model is falsified if the physical layer permits instantaneous global coordination ($\tau_{\text{comm}} \approx 0$) AND the biomechanical layer is bypassed by AI mediation ($C_{\text{human}}$ no longer binding), yet competitive pricing and decentralized contracting persist. In that counterfactual, the Hegelian self-contradiction on [PAGE 193] fails to activate, implying that some new restraint or institutional layer has replaced the human limitation Knight relied upon. Conversely, if a weather shock collapses $\phi$ and output restriction follows within the predicted window, the partial-monopoly mechanism is confirmed. The cross-layer test is therefore not about a single market statistic but about whether the binding constraint on industrial power is biological-legal (Knight’s regime) or technical-infrastructural (the post-Knightian regime). If the latter dominates, the authoritarian outcome on [PAGE 193] arrives not through political revolution but through the removal of the biomechanical friction that made competition tolerable.</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
