The TGF-β/astrocyte lineage traces to Wyss-Coray's early transgenic work (1997, 2000: chronic astrocytic TGF-β1 "promotes Alzheimer's disease-like microvascular degeneration") [PAGE 28, 43], through Luo's laboratory SBE-luc series (2005–2022: Lin/Luo 2005 "Global analysis of Smad2/3-dependent TGF-β signaling in living mice," Luo et al. 2007 EAE glia-dependent paper), and terminates in the current review [PAGE 17–54]. The chain runs *against* the naive assumption that TGF-β is universally protective: astrocytic TGF-β is detrimental in AD [PAGE 28], ALS [PAGE 31: "SB-431542 extends the survival time of SOD1G93A mice"], and MS/EAE [PAGE 33], yet protective in stroke [PAGE 25] and in PD neuronal signaling [PAGE 30: "C381... significantly reduces MPTP-induced dopaminergic neurodegeneration"]. TBI biomechanics lineage descends from Ommaya & Gennarelli (1974) [cited PAGE 79, ref 54], preserved nearly verbatim in Goldman's 2022 review.

Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy: (i) **A1/A2 binary trap** — the 2021 consensus explicitly recommends abandoning both labels because scRNA-seq revealed "heterogeneity... beyond these two distinct states" [PAGE 20]; any 2026-epoch publication still using A1/A2 markers inherits a two-year-old defect. (ii) **Losartan translation trap** — reviewed as "promising" for TBI/EAE/epilepsy but preclinically dosed at $0.68\text{–}9.78\times$ the FDA ceiling [PAGE 37]; clinical equipoise unresolved since 2014. (iii) **Repetitive mTBI trap** — most single mTBI recover, but "even mild recurrent brain injuries may induce cumulative effects" [PAGE 23], so single-hit outcome distributions underestimate career-scale risk (the breacher cohort [PAGE 13] is the sentinel population). (iv) **P. gingivalis → AD trap** — "Repeated oral application of P. gingivalis induces neurodegeneration and the formation of extracellular Aβ42 in young adult wild type mice" [PAGE 64] is a striking causal claim, but Dominy et al. (2019) itself is contested and the review's own open-access filter creates selection bias [PAGE 61].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Pre-injury substance use was "the most common pre-injury disorder (38.5%)" and NPS were "higher in patients with a pre-injury history (83.2%) than those without (63.6%)" [PAGE 105]. The recursive loop: patient reaches for pre-injury coping habits (alcohol, substance use) to manage post-injury dysphoria → habits potentiate the very NPS they were suppressing → clinician misreads regression as new primary psychiatric disease. Alcohol also carries direct physical consequence: "16 percent of brain injury patients aged 15 and above were intoxicated at the time of injury. The alcohol group had a fatality rate of 14.5 percent compared to 9 percent in the non-alcoholic group" [PAGE 81].

**Counter-trap.** The intuitive therapeutic move — suppress astrogliosis — is a trap in at least three disease contexts. Reactive astrocytes form the glial scar that is "considered a protective mechanism to prevent the spreading of secondary damage" [PAGE 23] and also release "inflammatory mediators to remove damaged tissue and to promote regeneration" [PAGE 23]. Luo concedes "attempts to block astrocyte reactivity globally have yielded inconsistent effects on functional outcomes" [PAGE 27]. The counter-trap inverts at each disease layer: TGF-β *inhibition* is beneficial in ALS [PAGE 31] but *activation* is beneficial in PD [PAGE 30] and in stroke [PAGE 25]. Any rigid intervention rule fails at the cell-type boundary, because "true astrocyte-cell specific targeting remains elusive" [PAGE 26].

**Clock.** Three clocks coexist. Fast clock: primary injury loads at 50–200 ms [PAGE 79]. Intermediate clock: anxiety declines 27% per year post-injury while "mood and substance-use disorder rates persisted steady" [PAGE 106]. Slow clock: TBI is "a risk factor for neurodegenerative disorders, including Alzheimer's disease and Parkinson's disease" [PAGE 23], with CTE accumulating p-tau perivascularly [PAGE 107]. Correct triage requires coordinating the three clocks — a 6-month post-TBI anxiety assessment captures the anxiety clock, misses the mood clock, and is blind to the neurodegeneration clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Biomechanical veto.** The loading-duration split at 200 ms is a hard veto on any mechanistic claim that ignores loading rate. Below 200 ms dynamic loading, "collision... brain tissues distort, and contusion could be because of this energy burden" [PAGE 79]; above 200 ms static loading, prolonged compression forces disfigurement or fracture [PAGE 79]. Skull fractures veto isolated TBI claims: "over 75% of all skull fractures are associated with some form of brain injuries" [PAGE 77].

**Physical/anatomical veto.** Brain-to-body mass ratio 0.02 combined with glucose flux 20% [PAGE 90] establishes that energy failure is the dominant failure mode — any ABI subclassification that omits glucose/oxygen deprivation is incomplete. Anoxia is vetoed by "metabolic homeostasis of the brain is destroyed resulting in major neuronal injury and cell death" [PAGE 78]. Elevated ICP at 15–20 mmHg is the mortality/morbidity veto [PAGE 86].

**Environmental/weather (occupational-toxicological) veto.** Repetitive low-level blast exposure among career breachers dysregulates "whole-blood gene signatures mostly related to chronic inflammation and immune response" [PAGE 13]. Gulf-War organophosphate (paraoxon) combined with mTBI produces "cognitive and behavioral deficits with neuroinflammation in the dentate gyrus" beyond either exposure alone [PAGE 13]. Waterpipe smoking at 6 months raises cerebellar DNA damage, lipid peroxidation, cytochrome C, IL-6, IL-1β, cleaved caspase-3, and NF-κB [PAGE 13]. Oral dysbiosis vetoes any "CNS-only" etiologic model: "P. gingivalis and A. actinomycetemcomitans have been linked to pancreatic cancer" and "augmented colonization... by T. forsythia and P. gingivalis have been implicated in esophageal cancer" [PAGE 62]; "the prevalence of CVD in patients diagnosed with periodontitis is 25–50% higher than in healthy individuals" [PAGE 59].

**Physiological veto on the "protective TGF-β" claim.** The claim that TGF-β is protective is vetoed by astrocyte-specific data in AD [PAGE 28: astrocytic TGF-β1 overexpression "promotes amyloid angiopathy"], in ALS [PAGE 31], and in EAE [PAGE 33: astrocyte-targeted TGF-β1 produces "an earlier onset and more severe paralysis"]. Conversely, the "harmful TGF-β" thesis is vetoed by stroke [PAGE 25] and PD [PAGE 30]. The veto resolves only at the cell-type × disease × temporal-stage level — which the toolbox cannot yet reach [PAGE 26].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**RLVR veto 1 — TGF-β inhibition in SOD1G93A ALS.**
Observed: "the inhibition of TGF-β signaling by a selective Tgfbr1 inhibitor, SB-431542, extends the survival time of SOD1G93A mice" [PAGE 31]. Counterfactual: remove SB-431542 → survival shortens toward GFAP-TGF-β1/SOD1G93A baseline. Prediction: a controlled re-run with the inhibitor withheld yields median survival time $T_{\mathrm{med}}$ decreased relative to treated, with direction $\Delta T_{\mathrm{med}}<0$ and dose-monotone effect across 1, 3, 10 mg/kg/day strata. Falsifier: $T_{\mathrm{med}}$ non-separation or reverse sign at the highest dose.

**RLVR veto 2 — Repetitive vs. single mTBI cumulative effect.**
Observed: "Most patients sustaining a single mTBI recover to full function without long-term neurological impairments" yet "patients with repetitive mTBI may experience long-lasting neurological symptoms because even mild recurrent brain injuries may induce cumulative effects and interfere with neuropsychological recovery" [PAGE 23]. Counterfactual: impose single-hit only → cumulative-effect slope should flatten toward zero. Prediction: with $N$ hits as dose, NPS prevalence follows a super-linear curve $p(N)=p_1\cdot N^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha>1$; single-hit arm recovers to $p\approx 0$ by 6 months while $N\ge 3$ does not. Falsifier: $p(N)$ linear or sub-linear with full recovery at any $N$.

**RLVR veto 3 — Oral P. gingivalis → Aβ42 production.**
Observed: "Repeated oral application of P. gingivalis induces neurodegeneration and the formation of extracellular Aβ42 in young adult wild type mice" [PAGE 64]. Counterfactual: withhold P. gingivalis application → hippocampal Aβ42 should not accumulate above young-wild-type baseline. Prediction: hippocampal $[\mathrm{A\beta42}]$ time-integral $\int_0^{T} [\mathrm{A\beta42}](t)\,dt$ is increased in the treated cohort with effect size $\ge$ 1 SD; and the effect is abolished by pre-treatment with an anti-P. gingivalis gingipain inhibitor (consistent with the Dominy et al. 2019 mechanism invoked in the review's reference chain). Falsifier: no separation in Aβ42 time-integral or effect abolished only at non-physiologic doses.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
