The result is a mutual misreading: the abstractor sees the particularist as irrational and sentimental; the particularist sees the abstractor as mechanical and pedantic [PAGE 674].

The counter-trap is the insertion of a decision clock \tau, a forced latency between stimulus and action. Formally, let c(t) denote the concrete case at time t and A(t), P(t) the instantaneous abstraction and particularism weights. The integrated judgment over the clock window is:

\[
\hat{U} = \int_{t-\tau}^{t} \bigl[w_A(s) \cdot A(s) + w_P(s) \cdot P(s)\bigr] \, ds
\]

where \tau is calibrated to the timescale of context change, not to the speed of argument. During \tau, the agent suspends Level-0 output and runs a Level-2 simulation: “If I regress to abstraction, what unique concrete fact am I erasing? If I regress to concretism, what universal principle am I violating?” Only after the clock expires is a judgment issued. Without this clock, crisis habit regression guarantees that the agent will treat the present case as either a mere class specimen or an inviolable singular, never both [PAGE 674]. The clock is therefore not a delay tactic but a recursive stability mechanism that prevents the moral system from locking into either extreme.

Physical veto V_{\text{phys}} \in \{0,1\}: V_{\text{phys}} = 1 if a physical resource constraint (e.g., lecture-hall seats, laboratory stations, dormitory beds) binds so tightly that no concrete individuation is possible. On [PAGE 674], V_{\text{phys}} = 0 because the text concerns the logic of justice, not facility capacity. However, in any real-world application of the Harvard Medical School argument, V_{\text{phys}} may activate: if there are N seats and M > N qualified applicants, the abstract class rule becomes the only implementable allocation procedure despite its acknowledged moral inadequacy. The singularity regime is N \ll M, where abstraction is forced by physical scarcity.

Biomechanical veto V_{\text{bio}} \in \{0,1\}: V_{\text{bio}} = 1 if an individual lacks the motor or perceptual capacity to perform the required role (e.g., surgical precision). James’s essence list includes “natural gifts” [PAGE 674], which gestures at biomechanical endowment, but he explicitly says we are not to blame or thank for them. V_{\text{bio}} = 0 within the textual argument because the moral claim concerns entitlement, not performance. In application, V_{\text{bio}} must be checked separately; a veto here does not falsify the justice argument but bounds its domain to physically capable candidates.

Weather veto V_{\text{weather}} \in \{0,1\}: V_{\text{weather}} = 1 if environmental conditions (travel disruption, venue inaccessibility) prevent the concrete case from being assessed in situ. V_{\text{weather}} = 0 on [PAGE 674], as no meteorological variable appears.

Adjudication proof. The veto composition rule is V = V_{\text{phys}} \lor V_{\text{bio}} \lor V_{\text{weather}}. If V = 1, the concrete-case assessment is aborted and the system reverts to an abstract proxy (e.g., lottery, class-based cutoff) not because the proxy is just, but because the concrete layer is physically unavailable. James’s thesis that no general rule decides sui generis versus class [PAGE 674] remains intact within the moral layer; the physical/biomechanical/weather layers are veto-proof in the sense that they cannot invalidate the moral reasoning, only circumscribe where it can be executed. Breakdown occurs when V = 1 persistently over a multi-week interval, at which point the institution is no longer practicing Jamesian tact but administering abstract rationing under constraint.
