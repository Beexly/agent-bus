Condillac’s dictum that we “are” light rather than merely see it [PAGE 1] frames sensation as an ontological immersion prior to representation. A. Seth’s Scottish Philosophy (p. 89) demotes sensations to “signs or occasions of our perceptions,” making them epistemically secondary but still necessary [PAGE 1]. Dewey’s Psychology (p. 34) supplies a functionalist version: sensations are hypothesized because no other resource can account for the complex phenomena directly present in consciousness [PAGE 1]. T. H. Green’s idealist protest—“Perception presupposes feeling” [PAGE 1]—shows that even a metaphysician committed to spiritual substance must tolerate sensation as a hypothetical underwriter of perception. James (Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2, 1890) synthesizes these by rejecting fractional composition in favor of integral mental states with gradable sensational-intellectual character [PAGE 1]. Hering’s physiological objection that the same retinal picture does not yield the same pure sensation first and last [PAGE 1] closes the loop by introducing temporal history into the sensory term.

This lineage anticipates later trouble spots. Husserl’s noesis/noema distinction echoes James’s refusal to split experience into sensation-plus-conception. Jackson’s “Mary” knowledge argument (1982) is essentially a twenty-first-century restatement of James’s born-blind pupil: complete physical/optical knowledge fails to convey the first-intention qualia of color. Nagel’s “what is it like” (1974) likewise inherits the Condillac-James insight that there is an indexical, non-transferable dimension to conscious states.

Multi-week traps. In longitudinal or repeated-measures research, three traps follow directly from this page. Trap A: assuming test-retest stability of phenomenal reports across weeks violates Hering’s non-identity claim; the same retinal stimulus will not yield the same pure sensation because associative history changes [PAGE 1]. Trap B: substituting weeks of propositional training (optics lectures, spectra, equations) for actual sensory exposure and then treating verbal reports as evidence of qualia commits the first-intention error; the blind pupil knows everything about light except what it is like [PAGE 1]. Trap C: retrospective introspection over weeks allows the intellectual coordinate \(i\) to colonize memory of an originally sensational state, producing confabulated “pure” sensations that were never pure to begin with [PAGE 1].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under epistemic or perceptual crisis—when high-level concepts fail to determine action—an agent regresses to Green’s position: perception presupposes feeling [PAGE 1]. Raw sensation becomes the default evidence because conceptual models are underdetermined by the immediate stimulus. This is not irrational; it is a functional fallback to the layer James says is never absent from mental life.

Counter-trap. The complementary regression is intellectualist: treating every mental state as if it were primarily conception, denying sensational residue even in “dullest feelings” [PAGE 1]. James blocks this by insisting that our most rarefied intellectual states involve bodily sensibility [PAGE 1]. Both extremes are practical fictions; the real state lies on the integral gradient.

Clock. The attentional clock determines where on the \(s\)-\(i\) gradient a mental state functions at a given moment. Immediate post-stimulus states are weighted toward \(s\) (sensational character) because sense-organ stimulation preponderates [PAGE 1]. Delayed reflective states shift toward \(i\) as associative processes dominate. The clock is recursive because the agent can only report its past sensation through present conception, so “what was it like?” is always answered in the current intellectual register, never by re-accessing the original first intention.

Recursive theory of mind. To model the born-blind pupil’s mind, the seeing agent must posit a first-intention \(Q\) that is by definition inaccessible [PAGE 1]. Conversely, the pupil models the seeing agent’s mind entirely through propositional description \(D\), missing the qualia residue \(R\) with \(H(Q \mid D) > 0\) bits. This creates an infinite regress of heterophenomenological uncertainty: each mind can simulate the other’s descriptions but cannot verify that the other’s first-intention states match its own.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Define the claim \(C\): “First-intention qualitative knowledge of light is not derivable from propositional optics alone.” A layer \(L\) vetoes \(C\) only if \(L\) entails \(\neg C\). All three layers return a false veto.

Physical layer. Classical electrodynamics describes light as an electromagnetic field with units V/m, Hz, and W/m². It has no output channel to phenomenal qualia and no law connecting field equations to the “first intention” of seeing [PAGE 1]. Veto(Physical, \(C\)) = false. Breakdown: attempting to reduce qualia to neural firing rates (spikes/s) repeats the category error; no mapping from spikes/s to “what light is like” is specified in the text or in physics.

Biomechanical layer. Sense-organ stimulation and reproductive/associative processes wax and wane independently [PAGE 1]. Peripheral input (joules, photons/s) neither necessitates nor suffices for the sensational character of thought, because central association modulates the integral state independently. Veto(Biomechanical, \(C\)) = false.

Weather/environmental layer. Ambient conditions alter stimulus statistics and arrival patterns but do not close the logical gap between description and qualia. The born-blind pupil in any weather still lacks the first intention of light. Veto(Weather, \(C\)) = false.

Adjudication: since every layer returns false, \(C\) stands as an irreducible phenomenological boundary condition. No cross-layer reduction is licensed by [PAGE 1]; James’s integral-state thesis explicitly forbids factoring mental states into sensory and conceptual fractions that could be assigned to separate physical layers.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning verifiable reward (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios are derived from [PAGE 1].

Scenario 1: Qualia acquisition via complete optical data.
Setup: An artificial agent is trained on the full corpus of optics (Maxwell equations, spectral distributions, neural correlates) but possesses no photic sensor. The verifiable reward is accuracy on optical questions.
Veto: No reward signal can license the claim that the agent knows what light is like in the first intention; the qualia channel is absent.
Prediction: The agent’s internal representation of “light” clusters with electromagnetic abstraction, showing zero correlation with any photic qualia channel (which does not exist). Forced to generate a description of seeing light, it emits propositional text only. Verifiable by representational similarity analysis aligning its “light” embedding with physics textbooks, not with sighted subjects’ sensory reports.

Scenario 2: Sensory-substitution equivalence.
Setup: A tactile-to-vision device converts camera input into skin stimulation. After weeks of training, the subject achieves high spatial accuracy.
Veto: The subject’s first-intention experience is not visual light qualia; the channel remains tactile even when conceptually labeled “vision.”
Prediction: Cross-modal similarity judgments cluster with tactile qualia, not visual ones. The subject fails a verifiable transfer test requiring matching a visual qualia target (e.g., “redness”) to the substituted signal above chance in phenomenal report space. Verifiable by mismatch in qualitative similarity space despite preserved spatial accuracy.

Scenario 3: Temporal invariance of pure sensation (Hering violation test).
Setup: Identical retinal stimulus \(r\) is presented at \(t_1\) and \(t_2\); the subject reports the pure sensation each time.
Veto: Because \(q_t = \mathcal{F}(r_t, H_{t-})\) and associative history differs, \(q_{t_1}\) cannot be assumed equal to \(q_{t_2}\) [PAGE 1].
Prediction: Intra-subject phenomenal reports diverge significantly (non-identical ordinal ratings) under constant physical stimulus, with variance exceeding measurement noise and correlating with prior-trial history. Verifiable by within-subject report entropy \(H(Q_t \mid r_t=\text{const}) > 0\) bits.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
