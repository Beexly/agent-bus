**Formal Definitions & Operators:**
Let \( P \) be the set of all products. Let \( C_u \subseteq P \) denote the set of products currently in user \( u \)'s shopping cart (Claim 46). A "set of related products" for a given product \( p \) is denoted \( S_p \), derived from historical co-purchase data (Item-to-Item CF basis).

**Combining Scores (Claim 45):**
For each candidate product \( q \in \bigcup_{p \in C_u} S_p \), define its raw score as:
\[
\text{score}(q) = \sum_{p \in C_u} w(p, q) \cdot I(q \in S_p)
\]
where \( w(p, q) \) is the similarity weight between \( p \) and \( q \) from the precomputed matrix, and \( I(\cdot) \) is the indicator function. The total score aggregates contributions from all cart items, so a product appearing in many \( S_p \) gets a higher score (Claim 45). The ranking reflects this aggregated score.

**Real-Time vs. Offline Processing (Claims 48 & 49):**
- **Offline Mode (Claim 49):** The core data structure—the similarity matrix \( W = [w(p,q)]_{p,q \in P} \)—is generated and stored via batch processing over large-scale historical transaction logs. This involves computing co-occurrence frequencies and applying smoothing techniques (e.g., cosine similarity adjusted for popularity bias).
- **Online Mode (Claim 48):** Upon detecting an "online action" (e.g., adding an item to cart), the system retrieves the relevant rows/columns from \( W \), performs the aggregation as above, and generates a ranked list substantially in real time.

**Information-Theoretic Units & Assumptions:**
- **Units:** Similarity weights \( w(p,q) \) are typically normalized to \( [0,1] \), representing conditional probability estimates or mutual information per unit interaction. Scores have units of "expected additional utility" or "recommendation strength."
- **Assumptions:** User preferences are stable enough for offline computation to remain valid short-term; cart contents accurately represent current intent (not just temporary storage); interactions are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples from an underlying preference distribution.
- **Breakdown Regimes (Singularities):** 
  - *Cold Start:* For new products (\( |P_{new}| > 0 \)), no historical data exists, making \( w(p_{new}, q) = 0 \). This breaks the model until sufficient data accumulates.
  - *Popularity Bias:* Highly popular items dominate \( S_p \) for many \( p \), causing homogenization of recommendations ("rich-get-richer"). The effective rank of \( W \) degrades toward low dimensionality.
  - *Real-Time Latency Singularity:* As \( |C_u| \) grows very large (e.g., bulk orders), the summation in the score calculation becomes computationally intensive, violating the "substantially real time" constraint unless optimized via approximate nearest neighbor search or caching strategies.
  - *Overfitting to Cart Noise:* If users frequently add items they don't intend to buy (wish-listing), the signal-to-noise ratio drops, and recommendations become irrelevant.
