There is a latent tension between the committee's stated non-partisanship and the inherent bias of selecting "graduates" who are products of specific institutional cultures. The dissolution of the old association and the "break in foot-ball legislation" that followed indicates a period of vulnerability where rule-making could have been captured by any single powerful entity. The new committee structure was thus a stabilizing innovation, but one that potentially insulated the sport's governing logic from direct athletic experience. This sets up a long-term trap: governance by alumni may optimize for tradition and liability mitigation at the expense of the sport's evolutionary vitality, a tension still evident in modern debates over rule changes regarding helmet use and contact.
