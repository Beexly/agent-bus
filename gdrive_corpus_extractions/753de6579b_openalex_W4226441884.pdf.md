Along characteristics \(\dot X_y(t)=a(U_y(t))=f'(u)\), \(U_y\) is constant, so \(u(t,x)=u_0(y)\) on \(x=X_y(t)\). The Jacobian of \((t,y)\mapsto(t,X_y(t))\) collapses when \(1+f''(u_0)\,u_0'\,t=0\); the first blow-up time is \(t^\star=-1/\min_y \tfrac{d}{dy}f(u_0(y))\). Example 9 ([PAGE 176–177]) realizes this exactly for Burgers' \(f(u)=u^2/2\): classical for \(t<1\), multi-valued for \(t>1\); Example 10 gives \(T=8/\sqrt{27}\) ([PAGE 178]). This is the first gray zone: **any** smooth datum with a compressive slope forms a shock in finite time, so classical \(C^1\) control (Greenberg–Li [ref 158], Li [ref 220]) is structurally inapplicable to traffic, and one must work in \(BV\).

Multi-hop step two: because shocks form, the correct solution space is \(BV(a,b)\), and weak solutions are non-unique without entropy admissibility ([PAGE 169–173], Example 11 shows a one-parameter family \(u_\alpha\) of weak solutions to the same Riemann datum). Kruzhkov's inequality ([PAGE 175], Def. 43) selects the physical one. So the attainable set \(\mathcal{A}(T,U_a,U_b,u_0)\) of [PAGE 25] (eq. 2.17) is a subset of \(BV\), not of any smooth class.

Step three: boundary stabilization. Take the quadratic Lyapunov candidate \(V(t)=\tfrac12\int_a^b (u-u^\ast)^2\,dx\) ([PAGE 34], eq. 2.29). Differentiating across the piecewise-smooth class \(PWS^+\) (Def. 2, [PAGE 33]) and using Rankine–Hugoniot on each front yields the boundary-plus-interior decomposition (eq. 2.31, [PAGE 35]). The decisive multi-hop observation is Proposition 1 ([PAGE 35]): the interior shock-sum \(\sum_i \llbracket \tilde u f-\!\!F\rrbracket - \tfrac{\tilde u(x_i^-)+\tilde u(x_i^+)}{2}\llbracket f\rrbracket \le 0\), i.e. **Oleinik-admissible shocks dissipate the Lyapunov functional for free**, so a boundary-only controller plus intrinsic shock dissipation can drive \(\dot V\le 0\). This is why the whole edifice of Chap. 2 works with controls only at \(x=a,b\).

Step four: the hierarchy of controllers. Greedy control maximizes instantaneous \(-\dot V\) (Prop. 4, [PAGE 39]) but Example 1 ([PAGE 41]) proves it stalls at \(u_m\), not \(u^\ast\): "The greedy control allows oscillations to exit from the right boundary but the solution does not converge to the steady state \(u^\ast=-1\)" ([PAGE 44]). The brute-force law \(\omega_a\equiv\omega_b\equiv u^\ast\) converges but injects boundary oscillation with \(\mathrm{TV}_t(u(t,b^-))\ge \tfrac{b-a}{4\varepsilon}(\hat u-k-u^\ast)\), unbounded as \(\varepsilon\to0\) ([PAGE 41–42]). The feedback law (2.36) ([PAGE 42]) restores asymptotic stability with BV bounds (2.37)–(2.39), and the nonlocal law (2.41) with switching time \(T_0=T_1-(b-a)/|f'(U)|\) ([PAGE 43]) minimizes convergence time — but only by reading the **entire** initial profile \(A=\sup_x u_0\).

Gray-zone audit. (i) The \(C^1\)-vs-\(H^2\) gap: Theorem 3 ([PAGE 23–24]) exhibits a system exponentially stable in \(H^2\) but not in \(C^1\), so "linearized stable ⇒ nonlinear stable" is false — the residual \(\rho_\infty(G'(0))<1+\tau\), \(\rho_2=\rho_0<1\). (ii) Theorem 5's Dini bound \(D^+w(x)\le f'(w)/(x f''(w))\) ([PAGE 27]) is singular at \(x=0\), so the attainable-set boundary is degenerate at the origin. (iii) The signal-continuum limit (Sect. 3.3) converges for strictly concave flux but **provably fails** for triangular flux under sustained spillback ([PAGE 65–66]).

**B. Exponential-stability radii** ([PAGE 22], eqs. 2.6–2.11): \(\rho_p(M)=\min\{\lVert D M^{-1}\rVert_p:D\in\mathcal D_n^+\}\); \(C^1\): \(\rho_\infty(G'(0))=1\); robustness: \(\rho_0(G'(0))=\max_{\theta_i}\rho(\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},\dots,e^{i\theta_n})G'(0))<1\); \(H^2\): \(\rho_2(G'(0))<1\). Units: dimensionless (Jacobian of a linear feedback).

**C. Lyapunov functional & dissipation** ([PAGE 34–35]): \(V(t)=\tfrac12\int_a^b(u-u^\ast)^2dx\) [veh²/m]; \(\dot V\) from (2.31); jump term \(\le 0\) (Prop. 1). Feedback set \(\bar u=(\hat u+\check u)/2\) with \(f(\hat u)=f(u^\ast),\ g(\check u)=g(u^\ast)\), \(g(u)=(u-u^\ast)f(u)-F(u)\) (eq. 2.34).

**D. Attainable set** ([PAGE 27], Thm 5): \(w\in BV\), \(f'(w(x))\ge x/T\) if \(w\ne0\); \(D^+w(x)\le f'(w(x))/(x f''(w(x)))\). Breakdown: singular as \(x\to0^+\).

**E. Network Riemann solver** ([PAGE 56], Def. 7): \(\sum_i f_i(\bar u_i)=\sum_j f_j(\bar u_j)\), wave-sign and idempotency \(RS(RS(u))=RS(u)\); properties (P1)–(P4) ([PAGE 58–59]). Flux (F): \(f(0)=f(1)=0\), unique max at \(\sigma\).

**F. Signal error operator** ([PAGE 64], eqs. 3.24, 3.27): Moskowitz \(N\) solves \(\partial_t N-f(-\partial_x N)=0\) [veh]; \(\lVert N^{\varepsilon_A}-N^0\rVert_\infty\le\eta_1(1-\eta_1)\varepsilon_A\min\{C_1,C_3\}\le\tfrac14\varepsilon_A\min\{C_1,C_3\}\) [veh]. Breakdown: triangular flux + sustained spillback adds \(\min\{C_1,C_3\}\eta_1 t\) (eq. 3.30), no vanishing.

**G. VSL input–output map** ([PAGE 92], eqs. 4.12–4.13): \(\mathrm{Out}(t)=\mathrm{In}(\tau(t))\,v(t)/v(\tau(t))\) [veh/s], \(\int_{\tau(t)}^t v=L\); cost \(J(v)=\int_0^T(\mathrm{Out}-f^\ast)^2dt\). Breakdown: needs \(v\ge v_{\min}>0\) (Lemma 3, Lipschitz const \(v_{\max}/v_{\min}\)).

**H. Moving bottleneck flux constraint** ([PAGE 128], eqs. 5.3–5.4): \(f(u(t,y(t)))-\dot y\,u(t,y(t))\le F_\alpha(\dot y)\), \(F_\alpha(\omega)=\max_{u}(\alpha f(u/\alpha)-u\omega)\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) [# lanes ratio]. Breakdown: constraint activates only if \(f(R(u_L,u_R)(\omega))>F_\alpha(\omega)+\omega R\); then an **undercompressive shock** \(\hat u_\omega\!\to\!\check u_\omega\) violates Lax ([PAGE 121]).

**I. Hamilton–Jacobi / Lax–Hopf** ([PAGE 141], eqs. 6.1, 6.3): \(f^\ast(x)=\sup_u\{ux-f(u)\}\); \(q(t,x)=\min_y\{q_0(y)+t f^\ast(\tfrac{x-y}{t})\}\). Triangular dual ([PAGE 158–160]): \(\mathrm{Dom}(f^\ast)=[-v,-w]\). Generalized/viability form (Thm 36, eq. 6.12): \(m_c(t,x)=\inf_{(u,s)}\{c(t-s,x+su)+s f^\ast(u)\}\), with inf-morphism \(\min_i\) (eq. 6.13). Breakdown: superlinear growth required; uniqueness of minimizer otherwise lost.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model the operator (traffic-management center) as a bounded-rational agent facing an incident clock.

**Crisis-habit regression:** Under incident time-pressure the operator reverts to the naive brute-force law \(\omega_a\equiv\omega_b\equiv u^\ast\). The book's prediction is precise and adversarial to intuition: convergence occurs but the downstream trace oscillates with \(\mathrm{TV}_t(u(t,b^-))\ge \tfrac{b-a}{4\varepsilon}(\hat u-k-u^\ast)\), "comparable to the oscillations in the initial datum" ([PAGE 41]) — a habit that "looks like it is working" (density descends) while injecting stop-and-go emission.

**Counter-trap:** The operator's trained counter-move is greedy feedback (Prop. 4). Prediction: fast cosmetic damping, but the state parks at \(u_m\) and never reaches \(u^\ast\) (Example 1). A second counter-trap, the nonlocal law (2.41), does fast-stabilize but only by conditioning on \(A=\sup_x u_0\) — i.e. it requires whole-profile omniscience the operator does not have in real time.

**Clock:** Feasibility is governed by characteristic travel time \(T_1=(b-a)(A-U)/(f(U)-f(A))\) and the switch \(T_0=T_1-(b-a)/|f'(U)|\) ([PAGE 43]); numerically the convergence-time surface is minimized at an interior \(T_0\), "for each fixed U there exists an optimal switching time \(T_0\)" ([PAGE 44]). In the freeway/MPC layer the clock is the receding horizon \(\Delta T=15\) min applied over \(\Delta\tau=5\) min ([PAGE 135]). The rational equilibrium is the feedback law (2.36): no whole-profile read, guaranteed BV bounds, asymptotic \(L^2\) decay — the strategy that survives when the operator models the clock explicitly rather than the shock.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical veto (continuum validity).** The PDE layer assumes \(u\) is a density field amenable to averaging. As \(u\to0\) the Knudsen-like number grows and the continuum flux \(f(u)=uv(u)\) ceases to be measurable; no lemma in the book licenses single-vehicle resolution. Adjudication: PDE claims valid for \(u\in[\,\cdot\,,u_{\max}]\) with \(u\) bounded away from 0; veto below.

**Biomechanical veto (actuator realizability).** Needle-like variations (Def. 17, [PAGE 94]) are measure-theoretic: instantaneous amplitude \(\delta v\) on \([\tau,\tau+\delta t]\). The realized controllers confirm infeasibility: the random-exploration policy has \(\mathrm{TV}(v)=753.5\) and the gradient method \(\mathrm{TV}(v)=70.8\), versus a fixed limit \(\mathrm{TV}(v)=0\) ([PAGE 85]). A human/actuator cannot track \(\mathrm{TV}\sim750\); the book itself flags the need for "a posteriori treatment of the speeds before implementation" ([PAGE 99]). Veto: raw optimal \(v(t)\) rejected; only smoothed/TV-penalized controls (Fig. 4.16) pass.

**Physical veto (capacity drop / relaxation).** LWR's stationary outflow is monotone in inflow up to \(f_{\max}\); measured outflow exceeds this only via the AR relaxation term \(-\tfrac{1}{\delta}u_i(v_i-V_i)\), \(\delta=0.005\) h ([PAGE 114, 120]). With finite driver reaction \(\delta>0\), outflow drops to 3527 veh/h at inflow 2500 veh/h — an effect LWR cannot represent. Adjudication: any first-order prediction near merge saturation is physically vetoed in favor of the second-order (AR) layer.

**Weather veto (parameter stationarity).** Every existence/stability result fixes \(f\) (hyp. (F); Thm 29–31, \(f\in\mathrm{Hyp}\)). Rain/snow reduce \(v_{\max},w\) and shift \(\sigma\), i.e. \(f\mapsto \tilde f\) time-dependently. The flux-stability estimate \(\lVert S^f_t u-S^{\tilde f}_t u\rVert_{L^1}\le L_f\hat d(f,\tilde f)\int_0^t \mathrm{TV}(S^{\tilde f}u)\) ([PAGE 90], eq. 4.5) bounds the error only for a **fixed** \(\tilde f\); a continuously drifting weather-forced \(f(t)\) violates the hypothesis and voids the bound. Veto: time-varying-weather claims are outside the certified regime.
