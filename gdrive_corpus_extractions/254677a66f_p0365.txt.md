The mechanical propulsion configuration described by Hull manifests a complex interplay between linear kinematics and rotational dynamics. Defining the following symbols:

- \(v_{belt}\) : velocity of the primary conveyor belt in meters per second (m/s)
- \(r_D\) : radius of the motor-driven pulley D in meters (m)
- \(\omega_D\) : angular velocity of pulley D in radians per second (rad/s)
- \(L_W\) : total length of the track W in meters (m)
- \(N_U\) : number of idler pulleys UU in the return loop
- \(f_m\) : operational frequency of the motor driving pulley D in Hertz (Hz)

The fundamental requirement for maintaining continuous belt tension yields the relationship:

\[
L_W = 2(L_c + \pi r_D) + N_U d_p \qquad [365]
\]

where \(d_p\) represents the pitch diameter of each idler pulley. Given that the belt traverses a half-revolution around pulley D, its tangential velocity equals that of the driven shaft:

\[
v_{belt} = \omega_D \cdot r_D \qquad [365] \]

Assuming perfect rolling contact without slip, the time required to complete one full loop of the track W is:

\[
T_{loop} = \frac{L_W}{v_{belt}} \qquad [365]
\]

Consequently, the rotational period of pulley D becomes:

\[
T_D = \frac{T_{loop}}{\pi} = \frac{2(L_c + \pi r_D) + N_U d_p}{\pi v_{belt}} \qquad [365]
\]

or equivalently, its frequency:

\[
f_D = \frac{1}{T_D} = \frac{\pi v_{belt}}{2(L_c + \pi r_D) + N_U d_p} \qquad [365]
\]

The drivetrain efficiency \(\eta\) accounts for energy dissipation due to friction at the pulleys and flexural losses within the belt. Therefore, the motor torque \(M_m\) must exceed the load torque \(M_D\) by:

\[
M_m = \frac{M_D}{\eta}, \qquad \eta < 1 \qquad [365]
\]

For the timing sequence involving microswitches S and probes p, let \(n_s\) be the number of switches per unit length along \(W\). Then the spatial separation between consecutive switches is:

\[
\Delta x_S = \frac{1}{n_s} \qquad [365]
\]

When a switch crosses a probe contact, the instantaneous rate of change of the grounding configuration generates an impulse function in the detection network. If \(\tau_S\) denotes the duration of this transient event, the corresponding Fourier transform exhibits broadband spectral content extending well beyond the fundamental modulation frequency \(f_m\).
