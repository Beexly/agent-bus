The recursive update is \(B_t = f(B_{t-1}, S_t, g(t))\), where \(g(t)\) is the time-varying anesthetic or pharmacological gain; when \(g(t)\) collapses toward zero, the model's spatial coordinate itself ceases to be defined rather than merely rescaling.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Physical veto. Apparent angular size is \(\theta = s/D\), where \(s\) is object size and \(D\) is distance. Luminance \(L\) and pupil diameter \(d\) determine retinal illuminance \(E\) in T, but \(\theta\) is independent of \(E\) above threshold. Therefore a gray glass that reduces transmittance \(\tau\) cannot make print "appear decidedly smaller" [PAGE 1] unless it also changes contrast, accommodation, convergence, or pupil size. Veto: the apparent-size claim fails under isoluminant angular-size matching; any measured reduction must be mediated by an ocular or cognitive variable not specified by James.

Biomechanical veto. Two-point discrimination on skin is bounded by mechanoreceptor spacing and cortical magnification factor \(M(\epsilon) \propto 1/(\epsilon + \epsilon_0)\), where \(\epsilon\) is eccentricity. The retina and skin have different receptor mosaics, so Weber's unexcited interval appearing smaller in vision but larger on touch [PAGE 1] reflects distinct neural architectures, not a single law of "interval feeling." Veto: one cannot transfer a cutaneous distance compression ("distances upon it seem less" [PAGE 1]) to visual egocentric distance without a cross-modal gain transform that James does not specify.

Weather veto. The room-enlargement claim concerns indoor gaslight alternation [PAGE 1]; at room scale the atmospheric scattering coefficient \(\beta \approx 0\), and there is no humidity, temperature, or wind variable in the text. Veto: any appeal to weather to explain the gaslight effect is extraneous to [PAGE 1] and physically negligible at these distances.

Adjudication outcome. Claims that survive veto: (a) luminance affects perceived brilliance/volume of feeling within Fechner/Stevens regimes (\(E > E_0\)) [PAGE 1]; (b) partial afferent gain reduction can compress perceived cutaneous distance provided \(g > 0\) and \(\Delta x \ge \Delta x_{\min}\). Claims vetoed: (a) apparent linear size changes from opacity alone without angular or accommodative change; (b) distance compression under complete block (\(g=0\)); (c) haschish body enlargement/contraction as a reliable, dose-independent law rather than a suggestibility-dependent state report.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto 1 — Photometric size-matching. Condition: Present identical print at fixed physical angular size; vary luminance only with calibrated neutral-density filters spanning photopic to mesopic ranges. James's page predicts judged size decreases as the glass darkens [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual prediction: if judged size is invariant across luminance above photopic threshold (\(L > 3\,\mathrm{cd/m^2}\)) in a forced-choice size match against adjacent unfiltered print, the Jamesian luminance-size operator is falsified. Verifiable veto trigger: \(p(\text{no shift}) > 0.95\) across \(n \ge 20\) subjects; breakdown if contrast falls below contrast threshold or if pupil/accommodation is not controlled.

Veto 2 — Complete cutaneous afferent block. Condition: Apply a pneumatic cuff or graded cooling to a forearm region until two-point discrimination is abolished (\(g = 0\), verified by absence of detection at suprathreshold separations). James predicts distances "seem less" [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual prediction: at \(g = 0\), the region is anesthetic; perceived distance is undefined, not compressed. Verifiable veto trigger: any subject reporting a nonzero but "smaller" distance under verified complete block falsifies the compression claim; expected outcome is null spatial percept. Singularity: as \(g \to 0^+\), distance estimate should diverge toward undefined/absence rather than asymptote to a smaller finite value.

Veto 3 — Cannabinoid body-schema dose-response. Condition: Double-blind, within-subject administration of THC versus placebo; measure estimated arm length, hand width, and phenomenal presence of the back via a body-image interface. James predicts enlargement, contraction, proportion changes, and absent back under haschish [PAGE 1]. Counterfactual prediction: body-part size estimates show no dose-dependent systematic shift; any reports of distortion correlate with suggestibility/dissociation scores rather than THC dose, while time perception shows the expected cannabinoid effect. Verifiable veto trigger: dose-response \(p < 0.05\) for body-size estimates supports James; \(p \ge 0.05\) with significant time-perception distortion vetoes the body-schema claim as stated.
