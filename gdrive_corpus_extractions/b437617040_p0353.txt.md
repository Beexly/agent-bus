**Operator 2: Consequence Cardinality & Sagacity Metric**

$$ \Sigma(c) = -\log_2 \left( \frac{\Gamma(c)}{C_{max}} \right) $$
where $C_{max}$ is the maximum possible consequence set size in the domain. Higher $\Sigma$ indicates greater sagacity (fewer, more potent consequences).

$$ P(c | T) = \frac{1}{Z(T)} \exp\left(-\frac{E(T, c)}{k_B T_{sys}}\right) $$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
This mechanism of reasoning traces back to Aristotle’s concept of the *unmoved mover* and the teleological nature of syllogisms—the end goal dictates the premises. However, it is deeply rooted in the 19th-century associationist psychology of Mill and Hamilton, who viewed thought as the chaining of ideas. James diverges by introducing a structuralist constraint: the geometry of the concepts themselves (their "consequence count") dictates their usability in reasoning. This anticipates modern Connectionist theories and Vector Space Models in NLP, where the "dimensionality" of a word embedding determines its inferential power. A multi-week trap emerges in modern LLM architectures: models trained on vast corpora often struggle with "chain-of-thought" reasoning because they lack the ability to dynamically suppress high-cardinality associations ($\Gamma(c)$ remains too high), resulting in hallucination rather than precise deduction. James's insight that mathematics aids reasoning precisely because it imposes artificial scarcity on consequences is a foundational lineage for Occam’s Razor in computational cognitive science.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
