The paper states: “At the h-th step, the saturated model for Alice takes the following form” [PAGE 9]. In LaTeX terms, the action equation is
\[
\mathbb{E}[A_h\mid S_h,B_{h-1/2},U_h,V_h]
=\alpha_z^A(U_h,V_h,S_h)B_{h-1/2}+\alpha_{u,s}^A(U_h,V_h,S_h),
\]
and the reward equation is
\[
\mathbb{E}[R_h^A\mid S_h,A_h,B_{h-1/2},U_h,V_h]
=\beta_a^A(U_h,V_h,S_h)A_h+\beta_z^A(U_h,V_h,S_h)B_{h-1/2}
+\beta_{az}^A(U_h,V_h,S_h)A_hB_{h-1/2}+\beta_{u,s}^A(U_h,V_h,S_h).
\]
The unobserved machine private information \(V_h\) is the source of confounding. The paper explicitly calls it “the so-called unobserved confounder in this two-player turn-based game” [PAGE 3]. Standard RL would condition on observed \((S_h,U_h,B_{h-1/2})\) but not \(V_h\), leaving a backdoor path through \(V_h\).

The second hop is the instrumental-variable move. Bob’s previous action \(B_{h-1/2}\) is proposed as an instrument for Alice’s current action \(A_h\). Relevance is Assumption 1(a): \(A_h\not\!\perp\!\!\!\perp B_{h-1/2}\mid(U_h,S_h)\); independence is Assumption 1(b): \(B_{h-1/2}\perp\!\!\!\perp V_h\mid(U_h,S_h)\). The paper says “Assumption 1 (a)-(b) are two well-known core IV conditions in the literature of causal inference” [PAGE 11]. The third hop is the critical gray zone: the exclusion restriction is not assumed. Because Bob’s previous action may directly affect Alice’s reward, the paper says “we treat Bob’s previous action as an invalid IV, where the IV exclusion restriction fails to hold” [PAGE 4]. Thus the derivation cannot use the usual valid-IV moment. Instead, it uses orthogonal covariance restrictions, summarized in Assumption 2(c), to “relax the IV exclusion restriction” [PAGE 14].

The fourth hop is identification. In the detour causal model, the paper writes
\[
\mathbb{E}[Y\mid S,U,V,A,B]
=\vartheta_a^*(S,U,V)A+\vartheta_z^*(S,U,V)B+\vartheta_{az}^*(S,U,V)AB+\vartheta_{u,s}^*(S,U,V),
\]
and
\[
\mathbb{E}[A\mid S,B,U,V]=\alpha_z^*(S,U,V)B+\alpha_{u,s}^*(S,U,V),
\]
as stated on [PAGE 13]. Theorem 1 then gives a system of linear moment equations in \(\vartheta_a^*,\vartheta_z^*,\vartheta_{az}^*\). The theorem is conditional: “if the following three linear equations with respect to \(\vartheta^*(S,U)\) have a unique solution, then \(\vartheta^*(S,U)\) can be non-parametrically identified by our offline data” [PAGE 14]. The uniqueness clause is not decorative; it is the singularity gate. If the coefficient matrix has zero minimum singular value, identification fails even if the moment equations are correctly specified.

The fifth hop is estimation. The paper defines the conditional moment restriction
\[
\mathbb{E}[W(Y,S,U,A,B,\vartheta^*)\mid S,U]=0
\]
on [PAGE 15], and uses a sieve minimum distance estimator on [PAGE 16]:
\[
\hat\vartheta_n\in\arg\min_{\vartheta\in H_n}
\hat L(\vartheta),\qquad
\hat L(\vartheta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \hat m(Y^i,S^i,U^i,A^i,B^i;\vartheta)^\top \hat m(Y^i,S^i,U^i,A^i,B^i;\vartheta).
\]
The convergence rate is
\[
\|\hat\vartheta_n-\vartheta^*\|=O_p\left(n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
as stated on [PAGE 18]. The exponent is dimensionless; \(n\) is a sample count; \(\alpha\) is smoothness; \(d\) is feature dimension; \(\varsigma\) is the ill-posedness exponent.

The sixth hop is pessimism. The paper does not merely plug in the SMD estimator. It builds confidence regions and solves
\[
(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)\in\arg\max_{\pi^A\in\Pi^A,\pi^B\in\Pi^B}
\min_{Q_1^A\in\mathrm{conf}^{A,Q,\pi}_1,\;Q_1^B\in\mathrm{conf}^{B,Q,\pi}_1}
\mathbb{E}_\pi\left[Q_1^A+Q_1^B\right],
\]
as in [PAGE 22]. The single-stage regret bound is
\[
\mathrm{Gap}(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)=O_p\left(n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
from [PAGE 23], and the multi-stage bound is
\[
\mathrm{Gap}(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)=O_p\left(H^2 n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
from [PAGE 26].

The gray-zone audit has several nontrivial risks. First, Assumption 2(c) is an orthogonality condition on latent effect modifiers; it is not directly testable from offline data. Second, the model is saturated only because actions are binary; multiple actions require a different basis representation. Third, the memoryless private-information assumption is what licenses backward induction and integration over \(V_h\) [PAGE 7]. Fourth, partial coverage is assumed but not guaranteed; Assumption 7 requires a positive minimum singular value [PAGE 23]. Fifth, the multi-stage proof propagates errors through the horizon, which is why \(H^2\) appears in Theorem 4. The paper’s own claim is appropriately conditional: “with only partial coverage assumption and the help of convergence of the SMD estimators, the upper bound on the performance gap implies that the regret ... converges to 0” [PAGE 24].

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The core operators are the instrumental-variable residualization, the interaction term, the conditional moment residual, the sieve minimum-distance loss, and the pessimistic confidence-region minimization. All are formal objects in the source, not invented ratios.

The basic causal model is stated on [PAGE 13]:
\[
\mathbb{E}[Y\mid S,U,V,A,B]
=\vartheta_a^*(S,U,V)A+\vartheta_z^*(S,U,V)B+\vartheta_{az}^*(S,U,V)AB+\vartheta_{u,s}^*(S,U,V),
\]
\[
\mathbb{E}[A\mid S,B,U,V]
=\alpha_z^*(S,U,V)B+\alpha_{u,s}^*(S,U,V).
\]
Here \(A,B\in\{0,1\}\) are binary action indicators, \(Y\) is an outcome or reward, \((S,U)\) are observed, and \(V\) is unobserved. The function \(\vartheta_a^*\) measures the direct action effect, \(\vartheta_z^*\) the instrument’s direct effect, and \(\vartheta_{az}^*\) the interaction effect. In the game, the analogous equations are Alice’s and Bob’s action/reward models on [PAGE 9] and [PAGE 10].

The identifying moments in Theorem 1 are built from the residualized instrument
\[
Z=B-\mathbb{E}[B\mid S,U]
\]
and the residualized treatment
\[
R=A-\mathbb{E}[A\mid B,S,U].
\]
The first two moment equations on [PAGE 14] are
\[
\mathbb{E}[Z R Y\mid S,U]
=\mathbb{E}[Z R A\mid S,U]\vartheta_a^*(S,U)
+\mathbb{E}[B Z R A\mid S,U]\vartheta_{az}^*(S,U),
\]
\[
\mathbb{E}[Z Y\mid S,U]
=\mathbb{E}[A Z\mid S,U]\vartheta_a^*(S,U)
+\mathbb{E}[B Z\mid S,U]\vartheta_z^*(S,U)
+\mathbb{E}[AB Z\mid S,U]\vartheta_{az}^*(S,U).
\]
The third moment uses the covariance restriction stated as Lemma 5 on [PAGE 31]:
\[
\mathrm{Cov}(B Z,(A-\mathbb{E}[A\mid B,S,U])Y\mid S,U)
=\mathrm{Cov}(B Z,A(A-\mathbb{E}[A\mid B,S,U])\mid S,U)\vartheta_a^*(S,U)
+\mathrm{Cov}(B Z,AB(A-\mathbb{E}[A\mid B,S,U])\mid S,U)\vartheta_{az}^*(S,U).
\]
These equations are linear in \(\vartheta^*=(\vartheta_a^*,\vartheta_z^*,\vartheta_{az}^*)\). Write the system as
\[
M(S,U)\vartheta^*(S,U)=r(S,U).
\]
The exact singularity regime is \(\det M(S,U)=0\) or \(\sigma_{\min}(M(S,U))=0\). In that regime, the uniqueness clause of Theorem 1 fails, and the SMD estimator is not point-identified. The paper’s own wording is conditional: “if the following three linear equations ... have a unique solution” [PAGE 14].

The conditional moment restriction is
\[
m(S,U,\vartheta)=\mathbb{E}[W(Y,S,U,A,B,\vartheta)\mid S,U],\qquad
\mathbb{E}[W(Y,S,U,A,B,\vartheta^*)\mid S,U]=0,
\]
with the representation \(W=\Phi(f)\vartheta+\alpha(f)\) on [PAGE 15]. The SMD estimator is
\[
\hat\vartheta_n\in\arg\min_{\vartheta\in H_n}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \hat m(Y^i,S^i,U^i,A^i,B^i;\vartheta)^\top
\hat m(Y^i,S^i,U^i,A^i,B^i;\vartheta),
\]
as on [PAGE 16]. The sieve space is
\[
H_n=\left\{\vartheta:\vartheta(\cdot)=\sum_{k=1}^{k(n)}a_k q_k(\cdot)\right\},
\]
where \(k(n)<\infty\) and \(k(n)\to\infty\) [PAGE 16]. The rate is
\[
\|\hat\vartheta_n-\vartheta^*\|=O_p\left(n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
from [PAGE 18]. The paper notes that this is “minimax-optimal when the function \(W\) defined in (12) is linear” [PAGE 18].

The confidence-region operator is defined on [PAGE 22]:
\[
\mathrm{conf}^{\diamond,r}
=\left\{\tilde\beta^\diamond:\hat L^{\diamond,r}(\tilde\beta^\diamond)-\hat L^{\diamond,r}(\hat\beta^\diamond)\le \eta^{\diamond,r}\right\},
\]
and the action-value confidence regions are
\[
\mathrm{conf}^{A,Q,\pi}_1
=\left\{Q_1^A=\tilde\beta_a^A A_1+\tilde\beta_z^A B_{1/2}+\tilde\beta_{az}^A A_1B_{1/2}:\tilde\beta^A\in\mathrm{conf}^{A,r}\right\},
\]
with the analogous Bob region. The policy-learning operator is
\[
(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)\in\arg\max_{\pi^A\in\Pi^A,\pi^B\in\Pi^B}
\min_{Q_1^A\in\mathrm{conf}^{A,Q,\pi}_1,\;Q_1^B\in\mathrm{conf}^{B,Q,\pi}_1}
\mathbb{E}_\pi\left[Q_1^A(S_1,A_1,B_{1/2},U_1)+Q_1^B(S_1,A_1,B_{1/2},U_1)\right],
\]
as on [PAGE 22]. The single-stage guarantee is
\[
\mathrm{Gap}(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)=O_p\left(n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
from [PAGE 23]. The multi-stage guarantee is
\[
\mathrm{Gap}(\hat\pi^A,\hat\pi^B)=O_p\left(H^2 n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+2\varsigma+d}}\right),
\]
from [PAGE 26].

Units and dimensional bookkeeping: the action variables \(A_h,B_{h+1/2}\in\{0,1\}\) are dimensionless indicators; action probabilities \(\alpha_z,\alpha_{u,s}\) are dimensionless probabilities; reward functions \(R_h^A,R_{h+1/2}^B\) carry reward units, and the coefficients \(\beta_a,\beta_z,\beta_{az},\vartheta_a,\vartheta_z,\vartheta_{az}\) carry reward units per indicator; the sample size \(n\) is a count; the horizon \(H\) is a count; the rate exponent \(\alpha/(2\alpha+2\varsigma+d)\) is dimensionless. The source uses no information-theoretic unit such as bits or nats; the probabilistic quantities are dimensionless. The confidence radii \(\eta^{\diamond,r}\) are in squared-loss units.

Breakdown regimes are exact and assumption-driven. If Assumption 1(a) or 1(c) fails, i.e. \(A_h\perp\!\!\!\perp B_{h-1/2}\mid(U_h,S_h)\) or \(B_{h+1/2}\perp\!\!\!\perp A_h\mid(U_{h+1/2},S_{h+1/2})\), the IV relevance coefficient \(\alpha_z\) may be zero and the first-stage matrix becomes singular. If Assumption 1(b) or 1(d) fails, the instrument is confounded by \(V_h\) or \(V_{h+1/2}\), and the moment equations are biased. If Assumption 1(e)-(f) fails, the zero-mean random-effect conditions fail and backward induction no longer integrates out \(V_h\) cleanly. If Assumption 2(c) fails, the orthogonality covariances in (6)-(9) are nonzero and the moment equations no longer identify \(\vartheta^*\). If Assumption 7 or 8 fails, the minimum singular value of the loss Hessian drops to zero; the paper explicitly requires \(\sigma_{\min}(\mathbb{E}[\Phi^\top\Phi])\ge c\) on [PAGE 23] and [PAGE 26]. If the ill-posedness exponent \(\varsigma\to\infty\) or the dimension \(d\to\infty\), the rate slows toward zero. If the behavior policy has zero support on an action used by the optimal policy, partial coverage fails even though the estimator is mathematically defined.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s lineage runs through four scientific strands. The first is classical instrumental-variable identification: Angrist and Imbens (1995) and Angrist, Imbens, and Rubin (1996), cited on [PAGE 3] and [PAGE 43], supply the relevance and independence logic. The second is invalid-IV and orthogonal-moment causal inference: the paper cites Lewbel (2012, 2018), Tchetgen et al. (2021), and Wang and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2018) on [PAGE 4] and [PAGE 5]. The third is confounded offline RL: Chen and Zhang (2021), Wang et al. (2021), Liao et al. (2021), Fu et al. (2022), Kallus and Zhou (2020), Namkoong et al. (2020), Bennett et al. (2021), Shi et al. (2021), Lu et al. (2022), and Miao et al. (2022), discussed on [PAGE 5] and [PAGE 6]. The fourth is two-player stochastic games: Littman (2001), Perolat et al. (2015, 2016a,b, 2018), Srinivasan et al. (2018), Guo et al. (2021), Jin et al. (2020), Cai et al. (2020), Zhong et al. (2021), and Hambly et al. (2021), listed on [PAGE 6].

The retrocausal genealogy matters because the paper’s novelty is not the invention of IVs; it is the reclassification of Bob’s previous action from a valid IV to an invalid IV with a direct effect and an interaction effect. The paper says it is “among the first in the literature to study offline policy learning in a two-player turn-based game under unmeasured confounding through the lens of causal inference” [PAGE 4]. That claim is historically situated between causal inference and cooperative game theory. Earlier confounded-MDP work often assumes valid instruments, linear transition dynamics, Gaussian latents, or single-agent structure; this paper instead uses a nonparametric saturated binary-action model and orthogonal restrictions.

The multi-week traps are the predictable failure modes a research program would hit if it followed the lineage without the paper’s caveats. Week 1 trap: treating Bob’s previous action as a standard valid IV, which ignores the direct effect \(\beta_z^A\) and the interaction \(\beta_{az}^A\). Week 2 trap: omitting the interaction term, which loses the paper’s stated generality: “we consider a more general setting where there is an interaction effect between the treatment and IV on the outcome” [PAGE 5]. Week 3 trap: assuming a Gaussian or parametric latent structure, which the paper contrasts with its “nonparametric saturated model” [PAGE 5]-[PAGE 6]. Week 4 trap: using an optimistic plug-in policy rather than pessimism; the paper explicitly uses “conservative estimators as proxies of the action-value functions” [PAGE 4]. Week 5 trap: assuming full coverage; the guarantee is only under partial coverage [PAGE 4], and Assumption 7/8 make that precise. Week 6 trap: reducing the two-player turn-based game to a single-agent confounded MDP, which loses the Alice-Bob alternation and the two-sided IV structure. Week 7 trap: ignoring horizon error propagation; the multi-stage theorem carries \(H^2\) [PAGE 26], and the proof carries higher-order horizon factors in intermediate lemmas. These traps are not speculative; each corresponds to a named assumption or theorem condition in the source.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory-of-mind layer is the alternation of Alice’s and Bob’s models under private information. Alice’s policy may depend on \(S_h\), her private \(U_h\), and Bob’s previous action \(B_{h-1/2}\); Bob’s policy may depend on \(S_{h+1/2}\), Alice’s previous action \(A_h\), but not on his private \(V_{h+1/2}\) [PAGE 7]-[PAGE 8]. Thus Alice is modeling Bob’s behavior while not observing Bob’s private state, and Bob is being modeled as responding to Alice’s prior action. This is a nested belief structure: Alice’s value \(Q_h^{A,\pi}\) depends on the expected continuation \(Q_{h+1/2}^{A,\pi}\), which in turn depends on Bob’s policy; Bob’s value \(Q_{h+1/2}^{B,\pi}\) depends on \(Q_{h+1}^{B,\pi}\), and so on. The paper’s backward induction on [PAGE 19]-[PAGE 20] and [PAGE 27]-[PAGE 30] is the formal clock for this recursion.

The “crisis habit regression” is the failure mode in which Alice, under distribution shift or unobserved confounding, falls back to the observed behavior policy \(b^A\) and standard RL. That is a habit because \(b^A\) is the only policy with direct empirical support, but it need not be optimal. The paper’s confounding argument is exactly this: without handling \(V_h\), “standard policy learning methods will in general induce the so-called confounding bias” [PAGE 10]. A habit-regression policy can look stable in offline data while being biased against the optimal policy.

The “counter-trap” is overcorrection. One counter-trap is to reject Bob’s previous action as an instrument entirely because the exclusion restriction fails. The paper avoids that by using an invalid-IV orthogonal-moment design. Another counter-trap is to assume full coverage or a unique loss minimizer automatically; the paper instead states Assumption 7 and Assumption 8 with \(\sigma_{\min}(\mathbb{E}[\Phi^\top\Phi])\ge c\) [PAGE 23], [PAGE 26]. A third counter-trap is to treat pessimism as a universal fix; pessimism only works inside the confidence regions, and those regions depend on the SMD rate and the partial-coverage condition. The paper’s own hedge is important: the regret result holds “with only partial coverage assumption and the help of convergence of the SMD estimators” [PAGE 24].

The “clock” is both the finite horizon \(H\) and the sample size \(n\). The horizon clock forces backward induction from \(H\) to \(1\), and the sample clock controls the shrinkage of the confidence regions. The single-stage rate is \(n^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+2\varsigma+d)}\) [PAGE 23]; the multi-stage rate multiplies by \(H^2\) [PAGE 26]. In theory-of-mind terms, the clock prevents infinite recursive reasoning: at each step, Alice integrates out Bob’s private information through Assumption 1(e)-(f), then moves one step backward. If the memoryless assumption fails, the recursion is no longer Markov and the clock breaks. If the sample clock is too slow, i.e. \(n\) small or \(\varsigma\) large, the confidence regions remain wide and the pessimistic policy can be conservative to the point of poor regret. Thus the recursive model is not a free-form belief hierarchy; it is a disciplined backward induction with explicit assumptions and explicit breakdown conditions.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A physical, biomechanical, or weather-layer veto cannot be adjudicated from the paper’s mathematics unless the alleged variable is represented in the state \(S\), Alice’s private information \(U\), or Bob’s private information \(V\). The paper’s state space is \((S,U,V)\) with observed \((S,U)\) and unobserved \(V\) [PAGE 13]. If a weather variable \(W\) affects Alice’s reward and Alice’s action conditional on \((S,U,B)\), then \(W\) must be part of \(S\) or \(U\); otherwise the reward model on [PAGE 9] is misspecified and the conditional moment \(\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{moment}}\mid S,U]=0\) on [PAGE 15] fails. If \(W\) affects Bob’s previous action and Alice’s action, then the IV independence condition \(B_{h-1/2}\perp\!\!\!\perp V_h\mid(U_h,S_h)\) in Assumption 1(b) is violated [PAGE 10]-[PAGE 11]. If a biomechanical constraint restricts the action space so that a behavior policy has zero probability on an action that the optimal policy would use, then partial coverage fails, and Assumption 7/8 cannot hold [PAGE 23], [PAGE 26].

The veto proof is therefore conditional and assumption-level, not layer-level. Suppose an omitted physical variable \(W\) is a common cause of \(A\) and \(Y\) given \((S,U,B)\). Then
\[
\mathbb{E}[Y\mid S,U,V,A,B]
\]
cannot be written using only \(\vartheta_a^*,\vartheta_z^*,\vartheta_{az}^*,\vartheta_{u,s}^*\) as on [PAGE 13]; an extra \(W\)-dependent term remains. The moment residual \(m(S,U,\vartheta^*)\) is then nonzero, so Theorem 1’s identification equations are invalid. If \(W\) also affects \(B\), then \(B\) is not independent of the latent confounder, so the IV relevance/independence pair is broken. If \(W\) is observed and included in \(S\) or \(U\), then there is no cross-layer veto; the model simply has a richer state. If \(W\) is unobserved and omitted, the only correct adjudication is that the paper’s assumptions fail and the convergence guarantee does not apply.

The same logic applies to “biomechanical” constraints. The paper assumes binary actions \(A=B=\{0,1\}\) on [PAGE 7]. If biomechanics make an action impossible for some states, the action space is state-dependent and the saturated binary regression is no longer saturated. If the behavior policy never takes an action in a region where the optimal policy would, the support condition fails. Weather or physical noise in the reward is harmless if it is mean-zero and included in the random effect \(\beta_{u,s}^A\) or \(\iota_h^f\), because Assumption 1(e)-(f) imposes zero conditional mean [PAGE 11]. It is harmful if it is correlated with \(A\) and persists in the latent \(V\). Therefore the cross-layer veto is not “weather defeats the model”; the veto is “an omitted common cause or a support restriction defeats the named assumptions.” No physical or biomechanical variable is specified in the source, so no such veto can be proven from the provided text.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto 1: IV relevance collapse. Counterfactual: set the behavior policy so that \(B_{h-1/2}\) is independent of \(A_h\) given \((U_h,S_h)\), violating Assumption 1(a) [PAGE 10]. Prediction: the first-stage coefficient \(\alpha_z^A\) is zero, the residualized instrument \(Z=B-\mathbb{E}[B\mid S,U]\) has zero covariance with the residualized treatment \(R=A-\mathbb{E}[A\mid B,S,U]\), and the coefficient matrix in Theorem 1 has \(\sigma_{\min}(M)=0\) or numerically near zero. The SMD loss becomes flat along at least one direction, the confidence region does not shrink at the stated rate, and the performance gap bound \(O_p(n^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+2\varsigma+d)})\) [PAGE 23] is not observed. A verifiable metric is the estimated minimum singular value of the first-stage Gram matrix; it should fall below the Assumption 7/8 threshold \(c\) [PAGE 23], [PAGE 26].

Veto 2: orthogonality/exclusion failure. Counterfactual: make Bob’s previous action directly affect Alice’s reward through the latent \(V_h\) so that one of the covariance restrictions in Assumption 2(c) is nonzero, e.g. \(\mathrm{Cov}(\vartheta_a^*(S,U,V),\alpha_z^*(S,U,V)\mid S,U)\neq 0\). The paper’s invalid-IV design explicitly relies on these orthogonality conditions to “relax the IV exclusion restriction” [PAGE 14]. Prediction: the conditional moment \(\mathbb{E}[W(Y,S,U,A,B,\vartheta^*)\mid S,U]\) is nonzero even at the true \(\vartheta^*\), so the SMD estimator is biased; \(\hat\beta_z^A\) and \(\hat\beta_{az}^A\) are biased; the confidence region may exclude the true action-value function; and the regret can fail to converge to zero at the stated rate. A verifiable metric is the held-out conditional moment residual \(\|\hat m(S,U,\hat\vartheta)\|\); it should not decay at \(O_p(n^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+2\varsigma+d)})\). Another is coverage probability of the confidence region for \(Q_1^{A,\pi}\), which should fall below nominal.

Veto 3: partial-coverage and support failure. Counterfactual: let the behavior policy \(b\) assign zero probability to an action that the optimal policy \(\pi^*\) takes with positive probability on a positive-probability state. This violates the partial-coverage spirit behind Assumption 7/8. The paper’s guarantee is explicitly under “partial coverage of the offline data” [PAGE 4], and Assumption 7 requires \(\sigma_{\min}(\mathbb{E}[\Phi^\top\Phi])\ge c\) [PAGE 23]. Prediction: the loss minimizer is not unique, \(\sigma_{\min}\) falls to zero, the pessimistic minimum over \(\mathrm{conf}^{A,Q,\pi}_1,\mathrm{conf}^{B,Q,\pi}_1\) can be driven arbitrarily low, and the policy gap does not vanish. A verifiable metric is the estimated support overlap between \(b\) and \(\pi^*\); zero overlap on a positive-probability state predicts failure even if the SMD estimator is numerically stable on the observed support. In the multi-stage case, the same veto compounds with horizon: the theorem’s \(H^2\) factor [PAGE 26] assumes the per-stage confidence regions shrink; if coverage fails at one stage, backward induction propagates the error to the initial value.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
