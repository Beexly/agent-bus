The source is a theoretical statistics paper about pairwise comparison models, not a physical-performance model. The core multi-hop derivation runs as follows. First, the Bradley-Terry-Luce model assigns each item a real-valued latent quality \(\theta_i^*\) and specifies the probability that item \(i\) beats item \(j\) by a logistic function of the latent difference [PAGE 2]. The parameter vector is constrained to a box and centered: \(\Theta_B=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\|\theta\|_\infty\le B,\sum_i\theta_i=0\}\) [PAGE 2]. The standard MLE is the minimizer of the negative log-likelihood over \(\Theta_B\) [PAGE 2]. The bias is the worst-case maximum absolute expectation error \(\beta(\hat\theta)=\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_B}\|\mathbb{E}\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_\infty\), while accuracy is the worst-case mean squared error \(\alpha(\hat\theta)=\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_B}\mathbb{E}\|\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2\) [PAGE 2]. The paper's central gray-zone observation is that a constrained MLE can have low MSE yet high bias. The mechanism is clipping: when a true parameter is at the upper boundary \(B\), random noise makes some estimates want to exceed \(B\), but they are capped, so the mean estimate is below \(B\); symmetrically, lower-boundary parameters are pushed upward [PAGE 3]. The paper states the sign pattern explicitly: the MLE “induces a negative bias (under-estimation) on the large positive parameters, and a positive bias (over-estimation) on the large negative parameters” [PAGE 3].

The proposed fix is not to remove the box entirely, because the unconstrained MLE can be infinite when an item wins all comparisons, giving unbounded MSE [PAGE 4]. Instead, the stretched-MLE replaces the box \(B\) by a finite \(A\) with \(A>B\), optimizing over \(\Theta_A=\{\theta:\|\theta\|_\infty\le A,\sum_i\theta_i=0\}\) [PAGE 4]. The proof strategy uses the unconstrained MLE as an intermediate. Under the high-probability event \(E_0\) that the unconstrained MLE exists and a concentration bound holds, the first-order optimality condition is analyzed [PAGE 18]. A deterministic \(\ell_\infty\) concentration bound of order \(\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{dk})\) is obtained [PAGE 20]. Then a second-order mean-value expansion converts the first-order condition into an expectation statement: the conditional bias of the unconstrained MLE is of order \(\widetilde{O}(1/(dk))\) [PAGE 20]. Finally, if \(\|\hat\theta(\infty)-\theta^*\|_\infty<A-B\), the unconstrained MLE already lies inside \(\Theta_A\), so it equals the stretched-MLE; the tail probability costs another \(O(1/(dk))\) [PAGE 21]. This yields the upper bound \(\beta(\hat\theta(A))\le c(\log d+\log k)/(dk)\) [PAGE 6]. The lower bound for the standard MLE is reduced to a two-item problem with an oracle construction; the two-item bias is \(\Omega(1/\sqrt{k})\), and the general case gives \(\Omega(1/\sqrt{dk})\) [PAGE 6, PAGE 22]. Thus the rate separation is between \(1/\sqrt{dk}\) and \((\log d+\log k)/(dk)\).

The gray-zone audit must state when this fails or is unproved. The theorem requires \(A\) finite and \(A>B\); if \(A=B\), there is no stretch and the clipping bias remains. If \(A\) is infinite, the unconstrained MLE can be infinite with positive probability, so MSE is unbounded [PAGE 4]. The constants depend on \(B\) or on \(A,B\), and the inequalities hold only for \(d\ge d_0\) and \(k\ge k_0\) [PAGE 6]. If \(B\) is underestimated, the conclusion itself warns that “an estimation smaller than or equal to the true B causes significant bias” [PAGE 11]. The sparse random-design extension is explicitly left open [PAGE 11], and disconnected comparison graphs are non-identifiable in the simulation setup [PAGE 11]. The 2-item unconstrained closed form has singularities at boundary empirical frequencies; the paper avoids them by bounding the true probabilities away from 0 and 1 using the box \(B\) [PAGE 16, PAGE 17]. There is no physical, biomechanical, or weather variable anywhere in the derivation; such layers can only enter by violating the fixed-parameter independent-Bernoulli assumptions.

\]
and the paper notes that a box constraint is necessary because otherwise “the estimation error can diverge to infinity” [PAGE 2]. The standard MLE is

\hat\theta(B)=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta_B}\ell(\theta),
\]
where \(\ell\) is the negative log-likelihood [PAGE 2]. The bias and accuracy operators are
\[
\beta(\hat\theta)=\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_B}\|\mathbb{E}\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_\infty,
\qquad
\alpha(\hat\theta)=\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_B}\mathbb{E}\|\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2,
\]
as defined on [PAGE 2] and [PAGE 3]. The stretched estimator is
\[
\hat\theta(A)=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta_A}\ell(\theta),
\qquad
\Theta_A=\left\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\|\theta\|_\infty\le A,\ \sum_{i=1}^d\theta_i=0\right\},
\]
with finite \(A>B\) [PAGE 4]. The main bias theorem gives
\[
\beta(\hat\theta(B))\ge \frac{c}{\sqrt{dk}},
\qquad
\beta(\hat\theta(A))\le c\,\frac{\log d+\log k}{dk},
\]
for all \(d\ge d_0\) and \(k\ge k_0\), with constants depending only on \(B\) or on \(A,B\) [PAGE 6]. The paper summarizes the point: “if A is a finite constant strictly greater than B, then our stretched-MLE has a much smaller bias than the MLE when d and k are sufficiently large” [PAGE 6]. The accuracy theorem gives
\[
\alpha(\hat\theta)\ge \frac{c}{k}
\quad\text{for any estimator,}
\qquad
\alpha(\hat\theta(A))\le \frac{c}{k},
\]
so the stretched-MLE remains minimax-optimal in mean squared error [PAGE 8]. The paper states: “Theorem 2.2 shows that using the estimator bθ(A) retains the minimax-optimality achieved by bθ(B) in terms of the mean squared error” [PAGE 8].

The estimator’s intellectual ancestry also includes Elo-style rating systems and the minimax analysis of pairwise-comparison estimation. Past work established that the constrained MLE is minimax-optimal in mean squared error [PAGE 2, PAGE 5], which creates the apparent paradox the paper resolves: an estimator can be accuracy-optimal and still systematically biased. The bias-correction lineage runs through Jackknife and finite-sample correction methods such as Quenouille, Cox and Snell, Anderson and Richardson, and Firth [PAGE 5, PAGE 12]. The paper distinguishes those methods because they expand asymptotic bias as an infinite series and do not directly yield worst-case finite-sample guarantees [PAGE 5]. A closely related modern line is high-dimensional logistic regression, especially Sur and Candès, which studies unconstrained MLE bias in the opposite direction for Gaussian predictors; the present paper instead studies finite-sample constrained BTL bias [PAGE 5]. A further lineage is regression toward the mean, but the paper notes that its fixed-index BTL bias direction is opposite to the usual regression-to-the-mean phenomenon [PAGE 5].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
A crisis habit regression appears when an analyst observes a top item winning all or nearly all comparisons. The naive reflex is: “the box is causing the bias, so remove the box.” That reflex is exactly the unconstrained MLE, which the paper shows can be infinite and can have unbounded MSE [PAGE 4]. The second-order mental model is: another analyst may overcorrect in the opposite direction, choosing \(A\) extremely large or applying an asymptotic debiasing correction without checking finite-sample MSE. The paper’s counter-trap is to keep \(A\) finite and only slightly larger than \(B\): the theory allows any finite constant \(A>B\), even an arbitrarily small constant gap, while preserving the \(O(1/k)\) MSE rate [PAGE 4, PAGE 6, PAGE 8]. The “clock” in this reasoning is the finite-sample threshold: the bias guarantee is not an identity for every \(d,k\); it holds for \(d\ge d_0\), \(k\ge k_0\), and constants depend on \(B\) or on \(A,B\) [PAGE 6]. The proof also runs on the high-probability clock of event \(E_0\) [PAGE 18]. A third-order mental model is: a practitioner may not know \(B\). The paper’s own recommendation is to estimate \(B\) leniently, because an estimate at or below the true \(B\) causes significant bias [PAGE 11]. Thus the recursive rule is: do not treat the printed \(B\) as exact; maintain a conservative upper bound and stretch above it, but do not stretch to infinity. The fairness mental model is also asymmetric: the standard MLE systematically underestimates the strongest items and overestimates the weakest ones [PAGE 3]. A counter-trap is to interpret that as a claim about individual noisy observations; it is an expectation statement over repeated comparisons. The clock discipline is to verify whether the graph is identifiable, whether the independence and fixed-parameter assumptions hold, and whether \(A>B\) in the actual implementation. If any clock condition is skipped, the bias improvement is not licensed by the theorem.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source contains no physical, biomechanical, or meteorological operator. Its equations contain only latent item parameters, pairwise Bernoulli outcomes, counts \(d\) and \(k\), and the box parameter \(B\) or \(A\) [PAGE 2, PAGE 14]. Therefore a physical or weather layer cannot be adjudicated from the paper’s internal proof. The only valid cross-layer veto is an assumption-violation veto. The BTL model requires a fixed \(\theta^*\in\Theta_B\) and independent comparisons [PAGE 2, PAGE 14]. If weather, injury, fatigue, equipment change, or rule change alters an item’s latent quality during the observation window, then the fixed-parameter logistic probability is no longer the data-generating process; the bias and MSE theorems do not apply. If the change induces correlation across comparisons, the independent-Bernoulli assumption is violated. If the comparison graph is disconnected or the sparse random design is in force, identifiability or the theorem’s scope breaks; the paper discards disconnected graphs in its sparse simulation because the problem is not identifiable [PAGE 11]. The complete-graph topology used in the accuracy proof is stated on [PAGE 47]. Biomechanical variables are not present, so a claim that the stretched-MLE directly controls biomechanics or weather is vetoed. A claim that physical nonstationarity is irrelevant is also vetoed, because nonstationarity changes the data-generating model, not merely the estimator’s bias. The paper’s internal veto conditions are: finite \(A>B\), \(d\ge d_0\), \(k\ge k_0\), fixed \(\theta^*\), independent outcomes, and an identifiable comparison graph. Outside those conditions, the proof is silent or the estimator can fail.
