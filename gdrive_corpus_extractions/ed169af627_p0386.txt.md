Forward lineage connects to the life-cycle and bequest literature: Modigliani's accidental and altruistic bequest models (1950s–1980s), Kotlikoff's strategic-vs-accidental bequest distinction (1988), and Piketty's capital/income ratio dynamics where \(r > g\) revives the importance of inherited wealth. Knight's uncertainty framework also anticipates modern treatments of Knightian uncertainty as a shifter of precautionary saving independent of bequest motives.

Multi-week traps: inheritance taxation recurs in legislative and electoral cycles, producing multi-week policy windows in which the bequest motive is scored as a static parameter. The trap is assuming behavioral invariance: Knight's "inducement to conserve" is regime-dependent and collapses when \(P \to 0\) or \(\tau = 0\), yet static revenue scoring often ignores this nonlinearity. A second trap is the conflation of intra-family bequest—natural under the family system—with voluntary extra-familial bequest, which Knight calls a "reverse tendency." Policies that ignore this psychological boundary condition mispredict how capital responds to changes in transferability. A third trap is the perpetuity illusion: legal property rights may be written as perpetual, but biological and political horizons are finite, so dynamic scoring that treats perpetual rights as immutable overstates the supply of long-horizon capital.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
First-order theory of mind: an agent saves because she expects her heirs to receive wealth at her death, activating the bequest motive \(B(W_T)\). Second-order regression under crisis: when mortality or expropriation salience rises, agents regress to hand-to-mouth consumption, hyperbolically discounting the terminal term \(e^{-\rho T}B(W_T)\) toward zero. Third-order counter-trap: sophisticated agents anticipate that others will regress and may accelerate pre-death transfers, inter vivos gifts, or trust formation to bypass the probate/confiscation clock. The clock itself is the mismatch between finite biological lifespan \(L\) (roughly 70–100 years) and the legal perpetuity clock of property rights; when \(L\) is made salient by crisis, the present-biased agent weights the present sacrifice more heavily than the remote bequest reward.

Recursive structure:

\[
\Gamma_i = \mathbb{E}_i\!\left[\Gamma_{-i} \mid \text{crisis salience}\right] \cdot \mathbb{I}\{P < P_{\text{crit}}\},
\]

where \(\Gamma_i\) is agent \(i\)'s bequest intention, \(\Gamma_{-i}\) is the aggregate expectation of others' behavior, and \(P_{\text{crit}}\) is the security threshold below which the counter-trap activates and agents switch from accumulation to liquidation. If everyone expects confiscation, everyone consumes down, aggregate capital falls, wages drop, and the marginal return to capital rises—creating a late, destabilizing incentive to save that arrives too late for the original cohort.

Verbatim grounding from [PAGE 386]: Knight notes that property in productive goods "enormously strengthens and isolates the private family sentiment," but under crisis that sentiment may regress from dynastic preservation to immediate kin or self-consumption. The recursive depth lies in the fact that each agent's saving decision depends on her belief about whether others will respect property rights at death; if those beliefs coordinate on insecurity, the bequest motive is vetoed regardless of the formal legal framework.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: capital depreciates at rate \(\delta\) and has durability \(D\). Perpetual property rights require either \(D \to \infty\) or continuous replacement investment. Veto condition: if \(\delta D \ge 1\) over the bequest horizon, the physical substrate cannot support perpetuity and the right is void ex post. Biomechanical layer: human lifespan \(L\) is bounded (~80–100 years); human capital is non-transferable, so only financial and physical capital can be bequeathed. Survival probability to transfer age \(p(L)\) enters multiplicatively, and the inability to bequeath labor income severs the link between current effort and terminal wealth. Weather/covariate layer: drought, flood, and climate shocks destroy agricultural productive goods with variance \(\sigma^2_{\text{weather}}\). Under incomplete insurance, expected terminal wealth declines approximately by \(\sigma^2_{\text{weather}}/2\) under log-utility, eroding the base over which \(B(W_T)\) operates.

Combined veto proof:

\[
\mathcal{V} = P \cdot \tau \cdot p(L) \cdot (1 - \delta D) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\sigma^2_{\text{weather}}}{\sigma^2_{\max}}\right).
\]

If \(\mathcal{V} < \varepsilon\) for a small tolerance \(\varepsilon\), the inheritance stimulus is vetoed regardless of legal theory. Adjudication order: (1) physical durability vetoes first—no asset exists to transfer; (2) biomechanical mortality vetoes second—no living transferor remains at the intended transfer date; (3) weather/covariate risk vetoes third—the asset is destroyed before transfer can occur. Legal property rights \(P\) cannot veto nature; they can only price risk. [PAGE 386] states that productive goods strengthen family sentiment, but if depreciation, mortality, or weather destroys those goods before transfer, the sentiment lacks a material substrate and the inducement to conserve collapses.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — Confiscation singularity: A 100% estate tax at death sets \(P = 0\) for terminal wealth and \(B(W_T) \equiv 0\). Prediction: the marginal propensity to save out of terminal wealth for decedents above the exemption threshold drops to zero. Verifiable via estate-tax panel data comparing saving rates of decedents in jurisdictions with and without estate taxes (e.g., U.S. state-level variation). If no statistically significant life-cycle decumulation difference appears, the bequest motive is weak or dominated by precautionary and consumption-smoothing motives; if a difference appears, Knight's inducement channel is partially validated but not sufficient.

Scenario 2 — Legal-protection null: Abolition of trademark and trade-name enforcement sets \(L = 0\). Prediction: brand valuation indices in weak-IP jurisdictions decay at the imitation rate \(\lambda_{\text{imit}}\) rather than persisting as perpetual rent. Verifiable via longitudinal brand-value data before and after IP enforcement weakening. Expected result: brand premium half-life falls below five years, falsifying the claim that legal protection converts want-creation expenditure into salable property [PAGE 386].

Verifiable via estate-composition data showing reduced financial-asset bequests and increased pre-death consumption in jurisdictions with forced heirship or restricted alienation. Expected result: the ratio of bequeathed financial assets to total estate falls by at least 30 percentage points relative to baseline, confirming that transferability, not mere ownership, is the operative stimulus [PAGE 386].
