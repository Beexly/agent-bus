<THINK_TRACE>
The paper's claim architecture rests on three load-bearing empirical assertions: (1) the ICM-20649's ±30 g / ±4,000 °/s range prevents saturation during football impacts; (2) 250 Hz sampling is sufficient (Nyquist-wise) for football-specific motion; and (3) validity is adequate (CMC > 0.8) versus a Vicon gold standard. Each deserves independent stress-testing.

Start with the data-rate chain, which is fully reconstructable from [PAGE 4]: "the total amount of bits per sample is 16." With 5 nodes, each carrying 6 channels (accel+gyro) at 250 Hz and 3 magnetometer channels at 100 Hz, the arithmetic closes exactly to the reported 144 kbit/s and the 22-player figure of 3.168 Mbit/s. This is a rare case where a paper's derived numbers are internally auditable and correct — a point in favor of the authors' engineering rigor.

Next, the sampling argument. [PAGE 9] reports dominant gait frequencies of 2.0/2.5/2.75/3.75 Hz (walk/jog/run/sprint) but also energetic content "between 80 and 125 Hz" for sprinting. The Nyquist frequency at 250 Hz is exactly 125 Hz. So the sprint spectrum runs flush against the fold-over boundary. The paper is honest about this, noting the shot transient ("almost 15 g within 12 ms") is captured in only ~3 samples, and that "even higher sampling rates than 250 Hz could be beneficial." The gray zone: any true content above 125 Hz (plausible for ball-impact shock) aliases back into the passband and cannot be separated post hoc. The authors attribute the 80–125 Hz energy to "associated lower magnitude components" of the shock rather than the main peak — a reasonable but not proven interpretation.

Third, validity. The abstract generalizes to "good validity (CMCs >0.8)" [PAGE 1], but Table B2 [PAGE 15] shows the blanket is an aggregation: knee CMCs are uniformly ≥ 0.930, while hip-angle CMCs dip to 0.628 (Kick 70%, left hip), 0.757 (Cut 100%, left hip), 0.780 (Cut 85%, right hip), and 0.788 (Cut 85%, left hip). The mean (0.864 left hip) rescues the summary, but per-condition hip validity during cutting/kicking falls below the stated threshold. This is the single most important caveat in the dataset, and it aligns with the paper's own mechanistic explanation on [PAGE 8]: "Errors in hip joint angles are likely to be larger than errors in knee joint angles, because the hip has a larger range of motion." So the CMC dips are not random — they localize to the joint (hip) and movements (cut, kick) where soft-tissue artifact (STA) and range-of-motion are maximal. That coherence actually strengthens the authors' credibility even as it qualifies their headline.

Finally, the n=1 lab validation (single male, 23 yr, 1.89 m) is a structural limitation the authors disclose, leaning on Wilmes et al. (2020) n=11 taped-IMU precedent for external support. The garment-vs-tape equivalence rests on matching RMSD/CMC ranges, not a paired statistical test.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All quantities below use SI or stated instrumentation units; only Eq. (1) is printed in the source, the rest are standard relations I apply to verify the paper's numbers.

Data rate (verifies [PAGE 4] "144 kbit/s"):
$$R_{\text{node}} = \underbrace{N_{ag}\,b\,f_{ag}}_{6\cdot16\cdot250} + \underbrace{N_{m}\,b\,f_{m}}_{3\cdot16\cdot100} = 24{,}000 + 4{,}800 = 28{,}800\ \text{bit·s}^{-1}$$
$$R_{\text{system}} = 5\,R_{\text{node}} = 144{,}000\ \text{bit·s}^{-1};\quad R_{22} = 22\times144\ \text{kbit·s}^{-1}=3.168\ \text{Mbit·s}^{-1}\ \checkmark$$

Nyquist limit (breakdown regime for [PAGE 9] spectrum):
$$f_{\text{Nyq}} = \frac{f_s}{2} = \frac{250\ \text{Hz}}{2} = 125\ \text{Hz}$$
Aliasing occurs for any true spectral component $f > 125$ Hz; content folds to $|f - k f_s|$.

FFT resolution (verifies 1,000-sample, 4 s window):
$$\Delta f = \frac{f_s}{N} = \frac{250}{1000} = 0.25\ \text{Hz}\quad(=1/T,\ T=4\ \text{s})$$

Impact-transient bandwidth (interpreting [PAGE 9] "15 g within 12 ms"):
$$N_{\text{samples}} = t_r f_s = 0.012\times250 = 3\ \text{samples};\quad f_{\text{bw}} \approx \frac{0.35}{t_r} = \frac{0.35}{0.012\,\text{s}} \approx 29\ \text{Hz (envelope)}$$
The near-step rise implies material energy up to $f_{\text{Nyq}}$, motivating the authors' higher-rate suggestion.

Angular velocity discretization:
$$\omega_i \approx \frac{\theta_{i+1}-\theta_{i-1}}{2\Delta t},\quad \Delta t = \frac{1}{f_s} = 4\ \text{ms}\quad[\text{units } ^\circ\text{s}^{-1}]$$

RMSD (error metric, Table B1):
$$\mathrm{RMSD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(\theta^{\text{IMU}}_i - \theta^{\text{Vicon}}_i\big)^2}\quad[^\circ\ \text{or}\ ^\circ\text{s}^{-1}]$$

CMC (validity metric, Table B2), extended correlation sensitive to offset, gain, and shape; built on Pearson $r$:
$$r = \frac{\sum_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)(\hat\theta_i-\bar{\hat\theta})}{\sqrt{\sum_i(\theta_i-\bar\theta)^2\sum_i(\hat\theta_i-\bar{\hat\theta})^2}},\qquad CMC\in[0,1]$$
$CMC=1$ denotes identical waveforms; $CMC<0.8$ indicates offset/scale/shape divergence.

Compression ratio ([PAGE 7], Eq. 1), dimensionless percent:
$$CR = 100\left(1 - \frac{\text{compressed filesize}}{\text{original filesize}}\right)\,\%$$
Reported: sprint $\approx 24\%$, walk $\approx 48\%$ [PAGE 9].

Runtime (verifies [PAGE 4] "12 hr"): interpreting "110 mAh" as mean draw $I\approx110$ mA against $Q=1{,}350$ mAh:
$$t = \frac{Q}{I} = \frac{1350\ \text{mAh}}{110\ \text{mA}} \approx 12.3\ \text{hr}\ \checkmark$$

Conductor sanity ([PAGE 5] $A=0.03\ \text{mm}^2$ silver-plated Cu):
$$R' = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{A} = \frac{1.68\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}}{3\times10^{-8}\,\text{m}^2} \approx 0.56\ \Omega\text{m}^{-1}$$
consistent with the sub-1 Ω contact resistance after washing [PAGE 5].

Breakdown regimes: (i) aliasing for $f>125$ Hz (impact shock); (ii) accelerometer clipping at $\pm30$ g and gyro at $\pm4{,}000$ °/s — the paper cites clipping with 16 g parts [PAGE 3]; (iii) STA-driven kinematic error growth with intensity; (iv) magnetometer register fault ("double readings," [PAGE 10]); (v) connector intermittency causing per-segment data loss [PAGE 9]; (vi) on-body RF blockage of the wireless link [PAGE 5].
