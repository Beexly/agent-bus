The counter-trap is adaptive market response. If a sufficiently large pool of participants adopts the same probability-ratio rule, market-implied probabilities shift toward model probabilities, compressing R toward 1 and eroding the edge ([PAGE 1]). However, because the strategy places relatively few bets ([PAGE 1]), its individual market impact is small; the trap is asymmetric. The edge decays not from volume but from the speed with which bookmakers can re-estimate p_market relative to the speed with which the model can re-estimate θ from new match data. If the market updates faster than the likelihood can be re-maximized (a heavy burden per [PAGE 1]), the opportunity window closes before the next inference cycle.

The clock imposes a hard boundary. Association football matches run for a fixed duration (90 minutes plus stoppage time), after which the goal count is final. The model aggregates a continuous-time point process into a discrete match-level count and conditions only on pre-match information ([PAGE 1]). No in-play events—red cards, penalties, weather shifts, tactical substitutions—are incorporated until the next match's estimation. Thus the learning clock stops at kickoff; any intra-match information is unmodelled noise that violates the constant-intensity Poisson assumption.

Physical layer: Pitch surface. Barnett and Hilditch (1993) demonstrate that artificial pitch surfaces affect home team performance ([PAGE 1]). The model on [PAGE 1] contains no pitch-type covariate; home advantage γ is pooled across all surfaces. Veto condition: any match where the home team plays on an artificial pitch while γ is estimated predominantly from grass fixtures must be excluded, because λ_h is biased and the Poisson rate is not invariant across surfaces.

Biomechanical layer: Player availability and in-game events. The model uses past performance aggregates and does not condition on injuries, suspensions, red cards, or substitution patterns ([PAGE 1]). A red card before the 30th minute changes scoring intensity mid-match, but λ_h and λ_a are fixed for the full duration. Veto condition: matches with early red cards, or teams playing with a significantly depleted starting XI relative to the historical average used in θ, violate the constant-rate assumption and must be vetoed.

Weather layer: Atmospheric conditions. Rain, wind, and temperature alter ball flight and player exertion, shifting goal rates ([PAGE 1] references external data sources but includes no weather covariate). Veto condition: extreme weather (waterlogged pitch, gale-force winds, extreme heat) invalidates the assumption that λ estimated from historical conditions applies to the current match.

Veto proof: Because the likelihood on [PAGE 1] has no exclusion indicator, no interaction term for pitch type, and no time-varying injury covariate, the model is structurally veto-prone. If any cross-layer sensor (pitch audit, medical report, weather observation) registers an abnormal condition, the match cannot be assigned a reliable λ_h or λ_a, and the probability-ratio R(ω) cannot be trusted. The model must either be augmented with these covariates via the proposed Bayesian structure ([PAGE 1]) or the match must be dropped from the estimation and prediction sets.

Scenario 1 — Unobserved player absence. Counterfactual: the home team's primary striker is injured and unavailable, but this fact is not reflected in the season-aggregate past performance used to estimate α_home. Verifiable test: compare the model-implied λ_h before and after the injury announcement; if λ_h is unchanged while realized home goals fall below the Poisson lower quantile, the model is miscalibrated. Prediction: the model issues a false-positive signal because p_model(home win) remains elevated relative to p_market, R(ω) exceeds c, and a wager is placed on an outcome whose true probability has dropped.

Scenario 2 — Dependence-parameter singularity. Counterfactual: maximum-likelihood estimation returns ρ λ_h λ_a ≥ 1 for a particular match or subset. Verifiable test: during optimization, check the constraint τ(0,0) = 1 − ρ λ_h λ_a > 0; if the boundary is reached or crossed, the joint PMF assigns non-positive mass to 0-0. Prediction: the likelihood surface has no valid maximum in that region, the match must be excluded from inference, and any betting signal derived from it is void.

Scenario 3 — Surface mismatch. Counterfactual: a match is played on an artificial pitch, but the model pools home advantage γ across all pitch types, omitting the Barnett and Hilditch (1993) surface effect noted on [PAGE 1]. Verifiable test: stratify historical matches by pitch type and test for a significant interaction between pitch type and home goals; a significant omitted interaction indicates systematic bias. Prediction: p_model for home goals on artificial pitches is miscalibrated relative to realized frequencies, so R(ω) is biased and any resulting betting signal has systematically incorrect expected value.
