Sustained high-stakes decision-making degrades working memory and attentional control, increasing Var(epsilon) and shifting the error distribution away from the zero-mean assumption E[epsilon | J, E] = 0. When decision density exceeds the cognitive recovery rate, epsilon becomes systematically negative, so observed failures are biomechanically determined rather than evidence of low ability ([PAGE 1]).

Weather layer. Exogenous market shocks—liquidity freezes, regulatory announcements, supply discontinuities—shift theta(t) discontinuously. If |Delta theta_weather| > Delta_crit, the reference class constructed from prior trials is invalidated; stationarity breaks and theta_hat_n estimates a defunct regime (breakdown regime (3) above).

Adjudication order. Vetoes are not additive in a toy ratio; they are layered constraints. A decision is inadmissible if any hard veto fires:

V = 1{ F > F_crit } OR 1{ |Delta theta_weather| > Delta_crit } OR 1{ n < n_crit }.

Physical and biomechanical vetoes override historical performance estimates because they act on E and epsilon directly; weather vetoes override them because they invalidate the probability space itself. Only when V = 0 does Knight’s multi-trial ability estimation problem ([PAGE 1]) become even formally meaningful.

Scenario 1 — Small-n veto. Verifiable rule: an evaluator observes n = 3 entrepreneurial decisions, all successes, and infers high ability. Apply the veto from the Hoeffding calculation: with epsilon = 0.10 and delta = 0.05, n_crit = 185; since n = 3 << 185, the inference is vetoed. Prediction: on a holdout set of the next 10 matched decisions, the observed success rate regresses toward the base rate, and the rank correlation between the initial theta_hat_3 and holdout performance is not statistically distinguishable from zero (permutation test, p > 0.10), confirming that the original streak was epsilon-dominated rather than ability-dominated.

Scenario 2 — Regime-shift veto. Verifiable rule: a manager’s theta_hat_n is estimated over n = 50 trials in a low-volatility regime; a central-bank announcement then shifts the market. Compute the distributional distance D_KL(P_pre || P_post) between pre-announcement and post-announcement return/outcome distributions; if D_KL > tau (tau set by a volatility-regime-change threshold), veto the use of theta_hat_n. Prediction: post-announcement, the strategy’s hit rate falls below the pre-announcement minimum by more than two standard errors, and the sign of outcomes is uncorrelated with pre-announcement J scores (out-of-sample verification), demonstrating stationarity breakdown.

Scenario 3 — Fatigue veto. Verifiable rule: a CEO makes eight high-stakes decisions within 36 hours with F > F_crit. Attribute observed errors to fatigue rather than low E: veto the interpretation that Pi < 0 reflects poor judgment. Prediction: in a within-subject crossover, the same CEO faces matched-difficulty decisions after 48 hours of rest (F < F_crit); the error rate decreases by a factor greater than 2 (paired t-test, p < 0.05), while pre-decision confidence calibration—an independent proxy for J—remains unchanged. This isolates the biomechanical layer as the vetoing cause, consistent with Knight’s margin of error ([PAGE 1]) but distinct from a capacity deficit.
