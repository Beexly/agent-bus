Multi-week traps: over extended training, repeated discrimination between \(S_0\) and nearby \(S_n\) narrows the generalization gradient \(\Psi(d)\), making behavior increasingly stimulus-specific. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when elevated drive \(D\) or acute stress narrows the effective behavioral repertoire: the organism ceases fine discrimination among \(S_0, S_1, S_2\) and instead emits the most strongly generalized habit, mobilizing \(s_{HR}\) across a wider zone of the stimulus continuum than under low-drive conditions. The counter-trap is differential reinforcement: by selectively reinforcing responses to \(S_0\) while extinguishing responses to neighboring \(S_n\), the experimenter steepens \(\Psi(d)\) (increases negative acceleration) and shifts the generalization peak. However, under crisis conditions the counter-trap can invert: the sharpened gradient collapses back toward the broad generalization curve, and the organism re-exhibits responses to stimuli that were previously discriminated away.

Credit assignment operates only within an eligibility window; if the response \(R\) is delayed relative to \(S\), or the goal \(G\) is delayed relative to \(R\), the temporal terms attenuate \(s_H\) before the generalization operator \(\Psi(d)\) is ever applied. Thus the clock sets an upper bound on how far backward in time a habit can be assigned, and generalization cannot rescue a habit that temporal decay has already erased.
