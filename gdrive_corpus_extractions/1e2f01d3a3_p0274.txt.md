**1. Aerodynamic Lift Model (Magnus Effect)**

The vertical force \(F_L\) acting on a spinning baseball is given by:

\[
F_L = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 A C_L(\omega)
\]

where:
- \(\rho = 1.225~\text{kg/m}^3\) (air density at sea level),
- \(v\) = instantaneous ball speed (m/s),
- \(A = \pi r^2 = 4.23 \times 10^{-4}~\text{m}^2\) (cross-sectional area, \(r = 0.0366~\text{m}\)),
- \(C_L(\omega)\) = dimensionless lift coefficient dependent on spin parameter \(\omega = \frac{\omega_{\text{RPM}} \cdot r}{v}\).

Empirical fits (e.g., [PAGE 274]) suggest \(C_L \approx 0.2 \cdot \omega\) for \(\omega < 0.5\), and saturates near \(0.35\) for higher spin. Typical fastball: \(v_0 = 40~\text{m/s}\) (~90 mph), \(\omega_{\text{RPM}} = 2500\). Then \(\omega = 0.0046\), yielding \(C_L \approx 0.00092\), so \(F_L \approx 0.00092 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.225 \cdot 40^2 \cdot 4.23 \times 10^{-4} \approx 0.012~\text{N}\). Compare to weight \(mg = 0.145 \cdot 9.81 \approx 1.42~\text{N}\). Thus lift alone is insufficient to cause true upward acceleration; however, it reduces effective gravity by about \(0.85\%\), enough to alter perceived trajectory.

m \frac{dv_x}{dt} &= -F_D \cos\theta \\
m \frac{dv_y}{dt} &= -mg + F_L - F_D \sin\theta \\
