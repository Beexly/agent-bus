The lineage traces to Clark L. The present page extends Hull’s earlier corollaries by introducing residual-drive aggregation and the stimulus compound Sp as a drive-bound component of the conditioned stimulus. The “Elliott experiment” cited in corollary VIII [PAGE 248] refers to a running-habit paradigm in which drive substitution was manipulated; Hull’s prediction that a strong non-conflicting second drive outperforms total dual satiation runs counter to naive drive-reduction accounts and foreshadows modern reinforcement-learning formulations in which motivational state gates the expression of cached policies without necessarily driving new learning.

Multi-week traps emerge when this single-page analysis is extended longitudinally. Residual-drive composition is likewise non-stationary; thirst that is a minor residual early in a protocol may become dominant if water access is restricted across sessions. Sp, treated here as a fixed component of the conditioned stimulus compound, can itself habituate or sensitize with repeated exposure, violating the assumption that withdrawal of the dominant drive produces a constant loss of habit strength.
