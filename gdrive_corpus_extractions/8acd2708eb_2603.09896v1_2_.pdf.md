Camera model. World-to-camera extrinsics $(R, t)$, intrinsics $K$. The camera center in world coordinates is $\mathbf{C} = -R^{\top}\mathbf{t}$.

Ray back-projection (Eq. 1, [PAGE 5]), for homogeneous pixel $\mathbf{p}$:
$$\mathbf{X}(\lambda) = -R^{\top}\mathbf{t} + \lambda\, R^{\top}K^{-1}\mathbf{p}, \qquad \lambda > 0 \ \text{(m)}.$$

Ground-plane intersection (Eq. 2, [PAGE 6]): solve $Z(\lambda)=0$, i.e. the third component,
$$\lambda^{*} = -\frac{\big[R^{\top}\mathbf{t}\big]_z}{\big[R^{\top}K^{-1}\mathbf{p}\big]_z} = -\frac{C_z}{\big[R^{\top}K^{-1}\mathbf{p}\big]_z}\ \text{(dimensionless)}.$$

Breakdown regime: as the pixel ray approaches the horizon, $\big[R^{\top}K^{-1}\mathbf{p}\big]_z \to 0 \Rightarrow \lambda^{*}\to\infty$; for pixels above the horizon $\lambda^{*}<0$, violating the $\lambda>0$ constraint — no valid ground projection. $K$ must be non-singular (finite focal length). This is the physical source of the perspective-ambiguity failure the paper later quantifies.

Mesh depth correction (Eq. 4, [PAGE 21]):
$$\mathbf{X}' = s\,\mathbf{X} + (1-s)\,\mathbf{C},$$
a similarity (uniform rescale about $\mathbf{C}$) with depth scale $s$ set by the lowest-vertex height correction. Units: $s$ dimensionless, $\mathbf{X},\mathbf{C}$ in meters. Breakdown regime: $s \le 0$ inverts the mesh through the camera center (nonphysical); the operator corrects only the radial (along-ray) component and cannot remove tangential or orientation error — foot penetration with a correct height but wrong yaw is left uncorrected.

Relative focal error (Eq. 5, [PAGE 21]):
$$e_{f_x} = \frac{\lvert f_x^{\text{pred}} - f_x^{\text{gt}}\rvert}{f_x^{\text{gt}}}, \qquad e_{f_y} = \frac{\lvert f_y^{\text{pred}} - f_y^{\text{gt}}\rvert}{f_y^{\text{gt}}} \quad (\text{dimensionless}).$$
Reported "Ours": badminton 0.55% / 0.72%, tennis 4.23% / 4.31%, table tennis 0.01% / 1.45% [PAGE 21] — versus DepthAnythingV3 at 38.89% / 38.24% (badminton). Singular only as $f^{\text{gt}}\to 0$.

Threshold Mean Relative Accuracy (Eq. 3, [PAGE 8–9]):
$$\text{T-MRA} = \frac{1}{10}\sum_{\theta \in C}\mathbb{1}\!\left[\frac{\lvert \hat{y}-y\rvert - T}{y} < 1-\theta\right], \quad C=\{0.5,0.55,\dots,0.95\},\ T=0.15\ \text{m}.$$
$\hat{y}, y$ in meters. Breakdown/saturation regime: at $y \to 0$ the term $\to -\infty$ and the indicator is unconditionally 1; more practically, whenever $\lvert\hat y - y\rvert < T$ the numerator is negative and the item passes all ten thresholds. Thus the metric grants guaranteed credit for $y \lesssim T/(1-\theta_{\min})$ and is undefined at $y=0$.

Localization metric ([PAGE 30]): binary accuracy at a 30 cm smooth threshold, deliberately set above the combined per-axis threshold since $15\sqrt{3}\ \text{cm} \approx 25.98\ \text{cm} < 30\ \text{cm}$, "this threshold is greater than the combined 3D distance threshold, 15 × √3" [PAGE 30]. Interpretation: a prediction can satisfy each per-axis 15 cm bound's Euclidean envelope and still fail, so the 30 cm gate is the binding constraint.

Perspective-ambiguity proxy (Section C.2, [PAGE 30–31]):
$$r = \frac{d_{\text{3D}}\,[\text{m}]}{d_{\text{2D}}\,[\text{px}]}.$$
This is not dimensionless — it carries units m·px⁻¹ and depends on focal length and resolution, so it is not comparable across sports/cameras without normalization. It is a monotone proxy, not a metric invariant; the authors acknowledge "not all reasoning processes are equally affected by perspective."

World frame (Appendix A.1, [PAGE 19]): right-handed, origin at the far corner from the camera; $x$ along court length positive toward camera, $y$ along width positive toward camera, $z$ perpendicular to the court plane. The classical-photogrammetry lineage supplies the engine: PnP calibration, SMPL-X [13] via PromptHMR [12], SAM3 [52] for detection, ball-from-calibrated-image [51], and table-tennis 2D-to-3D lifting [60].

The recurring multi-week trap embedded in this genealogy is monocular metric depth's failure on small/thin structures. The paper documents it three times: WildCamera and DepthAnythingV3 are "insufficiently robust" [PAGE 5]; "even when provided with ground-truth 2D locations and camera parameters, DepthAnythingV3 fails to produce accurate metric depth estimates" [PAGE 22], because "the ball occupies only a small image region" [PAGE 22]; and DepthAnythingV3 ball error reaches 1227 cm / 3168 cm (badminton/tennis) in Table 5 [PAGE 21]. The design response — convert depth estimation into human ground-projection annotation — is the direct genealogical correction to this failure. A second trap is the static-scene prior: because prior benchmarks are indoor/rigid, "models trained on existing spatial intelligence benchmarks generalize poorly to CourtSI-Bench" [PAGE 3], empirically confirmed by the null result "we do not observe consistent improvements over their respective base models" for SpaceR/VST/SpatialLadder/SenseNova-SI/Cambrian-S [PAGE 10]. The lineage predicts, and the data confirm, that non-rigid, articulated, high-speed human subjects are out-of-distribution for the inherited corpus.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Three nested belief models must be tracked: the VLM's implicit scene belief, the benchmark designer's intent, and the evaluator's ground truth.

The VLM belief, per Figure 5 [PAGE 11], is *procedurally* human-like: "identify the shuttlecock; locate each player's pelvis; ... compare relative distance," and it correctly performs ego-to-allo conversion ("switch to ... from camera"). The failures are localized to (a) 3D ball position from 2D ("Error in estimating 3D position of the ball"), (b) orientation under counterfactual pose ("a player stands on the far side of the court while facing sideways relative to the camera, leads to erroneous results" [PAGE 11]), and (c) absolute metric localization (predicted (1.2, 6.1, 0.0) vs GT (−1.55, 3.87, 0.03), with the human clue "Player 2 is outside the far baseline" [PAGE 11]). The model's belief is *topologically* correct but *metrically* and *orientationally* miscalibrated.

The designer's belief is adversarial: they deliberately rank distance items by the 3D/2D ratio to concentrate perspective-ambiguous cases (Section C.2) and curated a counterfactual-orientation failure case, anticipating exactly the perspective collapse the models exhibit. The recursive gap: designers model the model's failure, but the model does not model the projection geometry — it treats 2D proximity as 3D proximity.

A crisis-regression signature appears under output-format pressure: Gemini-3-Pro and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 "frequently generate uncontrolled intermediate reasoning or extended explanations, violating the output constraints," and their competence "is largely achieved only after applying an additional LLM to parse answers" [PAGE 10]. Under a harder spatial prompt plus a strict format, the models regress from compliance to verbose chain-of-thought — an instruction-following collapse masquerading as, and confounding, a spatial-reasoning score.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/biomechanical/geometric vetoes on the pipeline:

1. Ball-trajectory physics veto. The engine "approximate[s] this motion as a constant-acceleration problem" with "gravity, aerodynamic lift ... and air resistance" [PAGE 19–20]. Physically, drag $\propto \lVert \mathbf{v}\rVert^2$ and Magnus lift $\propto \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}$ are velocity-dependent, so constant-acceleration is only valid over short, low-spin segments. For high-spin table tennis the veto bites hardest — and the authors concede it by switching to a learned 2D-to-3D lifter [60] for that sport. Verdict: partially upheld; the approximation is conditionally valid and explicitly quarantined.

2. Mesh depth-plausibility veto. "Foot penetration or floating" [PAGE 6] is a physical non-penetration violation of raw PromptHMR depth. The Eq. 4 similarity transform enforces a corrected lowest-vertex height but only along the camera ray, so it restores vertical support but not lateral placement — a foot can rest on the plane yet be laterally displaced. Verdict: partially upheld.

3. Accuracy-claim veto. The "𝑐𝑚-level accuracy" claim [PAGE 3] is vetoed for tennis, where Table 5/6 show 29 cm ball-X and 27 cm pelvis error [PAGE 21–22], exceeding T = 15 cm and nearing the 30 cm localization gate. The claim holds for table tennis (sub-cm) and marginally for badminton, not uniformly. Verdict: over-general in the main text; correct only after per-sport qualification.

4. Proxy-dataset domain-gap veto. The certification uses a "purpose-built multi-view dataset" with "camera configurations similar to the source data" [PAGE 8] — not the RacketVision broadcast distribution itself. The error bars are therefore measured off-deployment. Verdict: the accuracy certificate is valid on the proxy, unproven on the actual corpus.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Veto 1 — Per-sport ground-truth-noise inversion. Hypothesis: because tennis reconstruction error (29 cm ball-X, 27 cm pelvis; [PAGE 21–22]) exceeds the 15 cm distance threshold, tennis distance/localization accuracy for *all* models is depressed by label noise, not model weakness. Verifiable prediction: a per-sport accuracy split (counts exist in Table 7 [PAGE 29] but per-sport accuracy is not reported) will show tennis Obj.-Line/Obj.-Obj and Localization systematically below badminton and table tennis by a margin correlated with the reconstruction-error gap. If published per-sport numbers show no such ordering, the veto fails.

Veto 2 — Format/convention attribution of the SFT gain. Hypothesis: a large fraction of the +23.5 pp [PAGE 9] is coordinate-convention and output-format learning, not metric perception. Verifiable prediction: a control fine-tune on the same 94 templates with randomized/held-out coordinate conventions but no metric perception demand will recover a substantial share of the gain on MCQ relational items while recovering little on numerical distance items; and the localization sub-task should show the smallest SFT gain (it already stays 0.0 on CourtSI-Ext [PAGE 13]). Observation of uniform gains across MCQ and numerical tasks would falsify this veto.

Veto 3 — Perspective-collapse floor. Hypothesis: restricting evaluation to the top-decile 3D/2D ratio cases drives object-object and object-line distance accuracy toward the localization floor. Verifiable prediction: extrapolating Figure 6 [PAGE 12], model accuracy at the highest-ambiguity bin falls below its (near-zero) localization accuracy and approaches chance, and the fine-tuned model's advantage over base narrows to within confidence intervals in that bin. If the fine-tuned model retains a large margin in the top-ambiguity bin, the perspective-collapse explanation is weakened. All three vetoes are decidable from data the authors already possess (per-sport, per-task, per-ambiguity-bin splits) and thus constitute fair, verifiable falsification tests rather than rhetorical objections.

Overall adjudication: CourtSI's contribution — a metric-anchored, human-in-the-loop reconstruction engine plus a human-verified benchmark exposing a real human–AI gap — is sound and well-evidenced for table tennis and, marginally, badminton. The claims that require qualification are the uniform "centimeter-level" accuracy across sports, the interpretation of the in-distribution SFT gain as general spatial reasoning, and the comparability of the perspective-ambiguity proxy across cameras. None of these overturn the paper; each bounds it.
