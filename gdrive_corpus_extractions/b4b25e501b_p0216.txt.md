Model each team as an agent maintaining a recursive model of its opponents' models, updated weekly under three regimes: crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock pressure.

Counter-trap. Clock pressure. Three clocks govern the theory of mind:
- The game clock: in 1914, clock management differed from modern football; a lead could be preserved by controlling possession, but the text provides no clock-specific data, so this clock is treated as a binding but unobserved constraint.
- The season clock: finite games mean injury accumulation \int \lambda dt is irreversible within the season. Concordia's "last few games were called off" is the season clock forcing an absorbing state before the schedule completes.

Under crisis, a team regresses to habit; the opponent anticipates the habit and lays a counter-trap; the team, recognizing the counter-trap, must either break the habit (requires e(t) and h(t) sufficient for learning) or collapse.
