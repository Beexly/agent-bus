\[ V(\Delta t) = V_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda \Delta t} \]
Here, \( V_0 \) is the base expected value, and \( \lambda > 0 \) is a tempo-sensitivity constant dependent on defensive preparation speed. **Unit:** Expected Points per Play (EPP). **Breakdown Regime:** When \( \Delta t \) exceeds a threshold \( \tau_{crit} \) such that \( V(\Delta t) < 0 \), the play is expected to lose yardage or result in a turnover. Calling signals "before the whistle has stopped" implies attempting to operate in the regime \( \Delta t \approx 0 \), maximizing \( V \).

**Operator 3: Adversarial Persuasion Gradient**
The act of "persuading" the defense is modeled as shifting the defense's posterior belief about the offensive tendency. Let \( \theta_D \) be the defense's internal parameter for offensive play preference. The offense aims to maximize the Kullback-Leibler divergence \( D_{KL} \) between the defense's updated belief \( Q(\theta_D) \) and its prior \( P(\theta_D) \):
\[ \mathcal{L}_{persuade} = \max_{\sigma} \mathbb{E}_{D_t} \left[ D_{KL}\left( Q(\theta_D | D_t, \sigma) \parallel P(\theta_D) \right) \right] \]

Counter-trapping is the deliberate exploitation of this regression. A sophisticated defense might show a look designed to force a particular read (e.g., a soft blitz that invites a quick slant). The average QB, operating at Level 1, sees the blitz and takes the slant. The elite QB, however, engages in counter-trapping: he recognizes the look as a *bait*, understands the defense is trying to predict his Level 1 reaction, and therefore chooses a third option (e.g., a check-down or a deep shot) that capitalizes on the vacated zone left by the blitzer. This requires maintaining the recursive loop under pressure.

The temporal clock is the ultimate constraint on recursion depth. Each second ticks away reduces the available processing time (\( \Delta t \)). Thus, there exists an optimal recursion depth \( d^* \) for a given \( \Delta t \): deeper analysis yields better decisions but risks timeout; shallow analysis risks predictable mistakes.
