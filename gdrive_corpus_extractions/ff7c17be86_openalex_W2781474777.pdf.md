(c) Causality (§3.7) and opacity (§3.5) are independent of generalization entirely — they are claims about what the learned object *is*, not about where it works. Bundling these three under one root is the paper's main rhetorical compression, and it is the point a hostile reader should press first.

**Step 3 — The gray-zone audit of the 1997 experiment.** Marcus is scrupulous on **[PAGE 16]** in noting the vintage networks *"lacked Lecun's powerful convolution technique,"* and on **[PAGE 17]** he concedes: *"The widely-adopted addition of convolution guarantees that one particular class of problems that are akin to my identity problem can be solved: so-called translational invariances."* So the experiment is an *existence proof of a failure mode*, not a proof of inevitability. Anyone citing "f(15)=14" as evidence that transformers cannot reason is over-reading the source. The defensible reading: hard-wiring a symmetry group into the architecture removes exactly the extrapolation deficit that the group generates, and does nothing for other groups. That is why **[PAGE 7]** quotes Hinton on the choice between *"replicating feature detectors on a grid that grows exponentially ... or increasing the size of the labelled training set in a similarly exponential way."*

**Step 4 — Conflation audit, Jia & Liang versus Lake & Baroni.** On **[PAGE 8–9]** Marcus reports that inserting distractor sentences dropped SQuAD accuracy *"from a mean of 75% to a mean of 36%"* across sixteen models. On **[PAGE 9]** he reports Lake and Baroni's finding that *"when generalization requires systematic compositional skills, RNNs fail spectacularly."* These are routinely cited together, but they are distinct: the first is adversarial distribution shift on a *i.i.d.-trained* model (the answer is still in the passage); the second is a structural expressivity failure. The 39-point collapse is evidence for §3.2/§3.9, not for §3.3. Marcus places them under different headings, which is correct — but his summary sentence on **[PAGE 9]** (*"the patterns extracted by deep learning are more superficial than they initially appear"*) does the blending work. I flag this because a counter-trap objection lives here: *if a model with retrieval augmentation fixes the distractor case, §3.3 is untouched.*

**Step 5 — Time-index audit.** The document is closed at the 2017 holidays (acknowledgment footnote, **[PAGE 1]**). Nearly every strong negative is an existence claim at a time index: *"there is, at present, no deep learning system that can draw open-ended inferences based on real-world knowledge with anything like human-level accuracy"* **[PAGE 10]**. Such claims are cheap to falsify and are therefore the *best* candidates for RLVR adjudication — a single reproducible counterexample retires them. Conversely, the structural claims (no hierarchical representation, no causal representation) are about inductive bias and survive counterexamples that use scaffolds.

**Step 6 — The vacuous-bound subtlety.** On **[PAGE 5]** Marcus writes *"where data are less than infinite, the ability of formal proofs to guarantee high-quality performance is more limited."* In §SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS I compute the classical uniform-convergence bound for the published Krizhevsky architecture (60M parameters, ~10^6 examples, **[PAGE 6]**) and show it is vacuous. Critical honesty requirement: a vacuous *bound* is not a proof that the *network* fails. Generalization in overparameterized nets is real and is not explained by uniform convergence. Marcus's sentence is defensible; a stronger reading ("no guarantee therefore no reliability") is not, and I refuse it below.

**Step 7 — Veto architecture.** The prompt's cross-layer veto apparatus maps cleanly onto this source because Marcus's strongest evidence is *physical*: stop signs misread as speed limits, 3D-printed turtles misread as rifles **[PAGE 13]**. Those are not statistical curiosities — they are admissible sensor inputs produced by a physical object, so no amount of "the test distribution was unusual" rescues the claim. That is what makes them vetoes rather than critiques.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**Notation.** Input space 𝒳, label space 𝒴, joint distribution P over 𝒳×𝒴. Training sample S = {(xᵢ,yᵢ)}ⁿᵢ₌₁ ~ Pⁿ i.i.d. Hypothesis f_θ ∈ ℋ, θ ∈ ℝᴺ. Loss ℓ: 𝒴×𝒴 → ℝ₊.

**E1 — Empirical risk minimization (the operator the paper describes).**
On **[PAGE 4]**: *"an algorithm called back-propagation allows a process called gradient descent to adjust the connections between units."* Formally,

  θ̂ = argmin_{θ∈ℝᴺ} R̂_S(θ),  R̂_S(θ) = (1/n) Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ ℓ(f_θ(xᵢ), yᵢ)

Units: ℓ dimensionless if 0–1 loss; **nats per example** if cross-entropy. θ is a pure number vector (weights and biases, no physical units). Assumption: differentiability of ℓ∘f_θ a.e. in θ.
**Breakdown:** non-differentiable losses; ℓ non-smooth at the argmin (ReLU kinks); gradient estimates with infinite variance (heavy-tailed REINFORCE returns — relevant to §3.2's Atari discussion).

**E2 — True risk and the generalization gap.**
  R(θ) = 𝔼_{(x,y)~P}[ℓ(f_θ(x), y)],   Δ(θ̂) = R(θ̂) − R̂_S(θ̂)

Units: dimensionless / nats. **Breakdown regime:** Δ is *undefined in the sense Marcus needs* when P_test ≠ P. All ten of his concerns are statements that this inequality of distributions holds in a domain he cares about.

**E3 — Uniform-convergence bound, evaluated on the reported architecture.**
For ℋ with VC dimension d, with probability ≥ 1−δ over S,

  R(θ̂) ≤ R̂_S(θ̂) + √( (d + ln(1/δ)) / (2n) )

Units: RHS terms dimensionless; n and d are counts. Substituting the published figures from **[PAGE 6]** — *"60 million parameters and 650,000 nodes was trained on roughly a million distinct examples"* — take d ≈ 6×10⁷, n ≈ 10⁶, δ = 0.05:

  √( (6×10⁷ + 3.0) / (2×10⁶) ) ≈ √(30.0) ≈ 5.48

Since the 0–1 risk is bounded by 1, the bound is **vacuous** (RHS > 1). **Explicit caveat, per the gray-zone audit:** this demonstrates that *classical formal guarantees do not cover modern deep nets* — which is precisely Marcus's claim on **[PAGE 5]** — and it does **not** demonstrate that the network generalizes poorly. Overparameterized networks generalize far better than VC theory predicts; the theory is the thing that fails here, not the network.

**E4 — Training space and the extrapolation deficit.**
Marcus's informal object: the net *"could learn their training examples, and interpolate to a set of test examples that were in a cloud of points around those examples in n-dimensional space (which I dubbed the training space)"* **[PAGE 16]**. Formalize as the ε-hull ℋ_ε(S) = {x : dist(x, conv(S_𝒳)) ≤ ε}, with dist in the input metric (pixels for vision, token embeddings for language — units: pixel intensity or ℓ₂ distance in embedding space).

  Δ_ext(ε) = 𝔼_{x∉ℋ_ε(S)}[ℓ] − 𝔼_{x∈ℋ_ε(S)}[ℓ]

The 1997 result is Δ_ext > 0 with x = odd integers and ℋ_ε(S) covering the evens. **Breakdown/singularity:** as ε → 0⁺, Δ_ext → the full out-of-support error; the net has no mechanism to reduce it, and **[PAGE 16]** notes *"Adding more hidden units didn't help, and nor did adding more hidden layers."*

**E5 — Convolution as a group-equivariance operator.**
On **[PAGE 4]**: convolution *"constrains the neural connections in the network such that they innately capture a property known as translational invariance."* Precisely, with (T_δ f)(x) = f(x−δ) and (C_k f)(x) = ∫ k(x−x′) f(x′) dx′:

  C_k T_δ = T_δ C_k  ∀δ

Units: x in pixels (or metres on the sensor plane); k dimensionless. **Breakdown:** equivariance holds *only* for the group generated by translations (plus the dihedral subgroup if flipping is trained in — cf. the mirror-reversal augmentation on **[PAGE 6]**). For rotations, scales, and 3-D viewpoint it does not hold, which is exactly the capsule-network complaint quoted on **[PAGE 7]**.

**E6 — Exponential cost of covering a transformation group.**
Let G be a d-parameter transformation group discretized at angular/spatial resolution Δθ. The number of group elements to cover is |G| ≈ (Δθ)^{-d}. Hard-wiring requires feature detectors, or labels, on that order:

  N_F = Θ(Δθ^{-d})  or  n_labelled = Θ(Δθ^{-d})

Units: dimensionless counts; Δθ in radians or pixels^-1. **Breakdown:** exponential in the *number of generators* d, not in Δθ. For 3-D rotation (d = 3) at Δθ = 1°, N ≈ (360)³ ≈ 4.7×10⁷ per feature type. This is the quantitative content of Hinton's *"grid that grows exponentially"* **[PAGE 7]**.

**E7 — Adversarial minimal perturbation.**
  δ* = argmin_{δ} ‖δ‖_p  s.t.  argmax_c f_θ(x+δ)_c ≠ y

Units: ‖δ‖_∞ in normalized intensity units [0,1] (equivalently 0–255 grey levels); margin in nats. For an affine classifier the closed form is δ* = (margin)/‖w‖_q. **Breakdown:** the required radius shrinks with input dimension and with margin decay; empirically the paper reports *"real-world stop signs, lightly defaced, that have been mistaken for speed limit signs"* and *"3d-printed turtles that have been mistake for rifles"* **[PAGE 13]** — i.e. δ realizable by paint and by a printer, and therefore physical, not digital. Note **[PAGE 14]**: *"Four years later, despite much active research, no robust solution has been found."*

**E8 — Association versus intervention.**
Deep nets estimate P(y | x). Causal claims require the do-operator:

  P(y | do(x)) = Σ_z P(y | x, z) P(z)   (z = valid adjustment set, backdoor criterion)

Units: probabilities (dimensionless); mutual information in bits. **Breakdown/singularity:** whenever a backdoor path x ← z → y exists with P(z) ≠ P(z | x), P(y|x) ≠ P(y|do(x)) and the gap is unbounded in principle. This is the height/vocabulary case on **[PAGE 13]**: *"kids get bigger as they learn more words, but that doesn't mean that growing tall causes them to learn more words."* **Honesty clause:** the correct claim is *not* that a network cannot represent a causal graph — it can if the graph is in its hypothesis class — but that ERM on i.i.d. data supplies no signal that selects the causal direction among observationally equivalent structures.

**E9 — Non-stationarity bound.**
  |R_test(θ) − R_train(θ)| ≤ L · TV(P_train, P_test),  TV(P,Q) = sup_A |P(A) − Q(A)|

Units: TV dimensionless ∈ [0,1]; L = sup loss, same units as ℓ. **Breakdown:** any guarantee evaporates as TV grows. This is the formal skeleton of §3.8 and the Google Flu Trends case on **[PAGE 13]**, and of *"systems such as politics and economics that are constantly changing."*

**E10 — Non-identifiability of parameters under hidden-unit permutation.**
For a network with layer widths n₁,…,n_L, the symmetry group 𝒮 = ∏ₗ S_{nₗ} acts on θ with f_{σθ} = f_θ. Hence the fiber over any function has dimension ≥ Σₗ log(nₗ!) ≈ Σₗ nₗ log nₗ. Units: nats of parameter-space volume. **Consequence:** any "read off the meaning of node i in layer j" interpretation is one arbitrary representative of a huge equivalence class — the mathematical content of **[PAGE 11]**: parameters are identified *"only in terms of their geography within a complex network (e.g., the activity value of the ith node in layer j in network module k)."*

**E11 — Stylized hype/funding loop (explicitly a model, not a measurement).**
Let B(t) be annual AI investment (USD·yr⁻¹), P(t) a benchmark error (dimensionless), τ a lag in years between measured progress and headlines. Then B(t+1) = α·g(P(t−τ)) with α in USD per unit hype. Linearizing, the loop is unstable — producing boom/bust oscillation — when the loop gain L = α|g′|·τ exceeds the damping margin. **This is an illustrative dynamical sketch with stated assumptions, not a measured quantity from the source;** it is offered only to structure the "AI winter" reasoning on **[PAGE 17]** and the *"trough of disillusionment"* **[PAGE 18]**.

</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper's own genealogy is asserted on **[PAGE 2]**: *"deep learning has historical roots going back decades (Schmidhuber, 2015)"* — but it attracted little notice until 2012. The intellectually load-bearing lineage is different and runs backwards from 2017 through 1988. Marcus cites Fodor and Pylyshyn (1988) on **[PAGE 9]** for *"similar concerns ... with respect to an earlier breed of neural networks,"* and his own *The Algebraic Mind* (2001) for the conjecture that single recurrent networks *"would have trouble systematically representing and extending recursive structure."* The retro-causal claim is that the 2017 empirical papers — Lake and Baroni's *"Still not systematic after all these years"* **[PAGE 9]**, Jia and Liang's distractor collapse **[PAGE 9]**, Kansky et al.'s Breakout perturbations **[PAGE 8]** — are *confirmations of a 1988–2001 prediction*, not new discoveries. Hinton's *"Science progresses one funeral at a time"* **[PAGE 2]** is quoted to license exactly this framing: the field did not refute the argument, it outlived its authors' attention.

The full chain: Rosenblatt's perceptron → Minsky–Papert's XOR limitation → Werbos/Rumelhart–Hinton–Williams backpropagation → LeCun (1989) convolution **[PAGE 4, PAGE 11]** → Elman (1990) simple recurrent networks **[PAGE 9]** → Marcus (1998a, 1998b, 2001) on variable binding and training-space extrapolation → Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) on systematicity → Krizhevsky–Sutskever–Hinton (2012) **[PAGE 6]** → Sabour–Frosst–Hinton (2017) capsules **[PAGE 7]** → Besold et al. neurosymbolic and Graves et al. differentiable neural computers **[PAGE 20]**. Note the retro-causal inversion: the 2017 capsule paper is cited *as evidence for* a 1998 conjecture, yet it is itself an unproven architectural proposal. Citing it cuts both ways.

**Multi-week (and multi-decade) traps, enumerated from the source:**

1. **The 1970s AI winter.** **[PAGE 17]**: after the Lighthill report (1973) *"suggested that AI was too brittle, too narrow and too superficial to be used in practice."* Cycle length: ~2 decades of recovery.
2. **Google Flu Trends.** **[PAGE 13]**: *"initially did a great job of predicting epidemiological data on search trends, only to complete miss things like the peak of the 2013 flu season."* Cycle length: ~5 years from launch to public discredit. The trap is that validation on pre-2013 data could not detect the drift.
3. **Facebook M.** **[PAGE 17]**: launched August 2015 *"with much publicity as a general purpose personal assistant, and then later downgraded."* Cycle: ~18 months.
4. **Chatbots generally.** **[PAGE 17]**: *"have not lived up to the hype they received a couple years ago."*
5. **Self-driving.** **[PAGE 18]**: *"After peak hype, self-driving cars enter the trough of disillusionment."* Cycle: ~3–5 years; note that Marcus flags this as a *conditional* risk, not a prediction.
6. **The 1990s' own version.** **[PAGE 14]**: Pinker and Prince (1988) documented networks producing *"membled"* and *"toureder"* — *"Children rarely if ever make mistakes like these."* The same class of error, re-observed a decade later, was still being reported as novel.

The structural lesson: each trap has the same shape — validation set drawn from the same regime as training, deployment into a shifted regime, then public reversal. That is E9 with a time lag, and it is why the paper's title word is "appraisal" rather than "critique."

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The three-way game.** Model the field as three agents with different clocks: the *practitioner* (habit), the *adversary* (counter-trap), and the *historian* (clock). Marcus's text is a historian's move played into an adversary's gap.

**Crisis habit regression.** The habitual response to benchmark stagnation is to re-approximate the infinite-data limit. Marcus names the habit explicitly on **[PAGE 5]**: *"In a world with infinite data, and infinite computational resources, there might be little need for any other technique."* The regression is well-documented in the source — Krizhevsky's net scaled to 60M parameters **[PAGE 6]**; data augmentation manufactured extra examples from existing ones **[PAGE 6, fn 6]**; Capsule Networks propose more architecture rather than more data **[PAGE 7]**. The recursive self-model is on **[PAGE 18]**: *"AI could get trapped in a local minimum, dwelling too heavily in the wrong part of intellectual space, focusing too much on the detailed exploration of a particular class of accessible but limited models that are geared around capturing low-hanging fruit."* The pathological recursion: **short-term benchmark gain raises the perceived value of the habit, which increases allocation to the habit, which further narrows the explored region.** This is a positive-feedback loop with no natural damping term — which is why Marcus calls it a *local minimum* rather than a mistake.

**Counter-trap.** The adversary's response is pre-committed because the habit is forecastable. If you scale parameters, I will scale perturbations. The source documents the escalation sequence precisely: Szegedy et al. (2013) first note spoofability → *"Four years later, despite much active research, no robust solution has been found"* **[PAGE 14]**. Between those dates: fooling images **[PAGE 13]**, defaced stop signs **[PAGE 13]**, 3D-printed turtles **[PAGE 13]**, adversarial attacks on *policies* rather than classifiers (Huang et al. 2017, undermining DQN and A3C) **[PAGE 8]**. The trap's structure is asymmetric: each defense is fitted to the observed attack family, so defense surface grows with the *observed* attack set while attack surface grows with the *unobserved* one. The RL analogue on **[PAGE 14]** — *"the high-interest credit card of technical debt"* — is the same asymmetry expressed in maintenance terms: *"comparatively easy to make systems that work in some limited set of circumstances (short term gain), but quite difficult to guarantee that they will work in alternative circumstances."*

**Clock.** Three clocks run simultaneously and they disagree.
- *Field clock*: Chollet on **[PAGE 2]** — *"we've entered diminishing returns territory in 2016-2017."*
- *Generational clock*: Hinton on **[PAGE 2]** — *"Science progresses one funeral at a time."*
- *Funding clock*: the AI-winter oscillator **[PAGE 17]**, with the self-aware observation that *"hype is still a major concern."*

The recursive theory-of-mind move is that **the field's model of its own future determines its present allocation, which determines which futures are reachable.** Marcus makes this explicit on **[PAGE 23]**: Hinton is *"courageous enough to reconsider his own beliefs,"* revealing he is *"deeply suspicious"* of back-propagation, and *"entirely new methods will probably have to be invented."* When the pioneer's self-model flips, it changes the counterfactual value of every graduate student's project — hence the second epigraph: *"The future depends on some graduate student who is deeply suspicious of everything I have said."* That is a theory-of-mind statement about *how arguments propagate through a research community*, and it is the paper's actual mechanism of action.

**Where the recursion fails.** Marcus does not model the counter-trap against *himself*. If §3.3 is wrong because a later architecture achieves compositional generalization, then the retro-causal credit he claims for 1988/1998 evaporates — the concerns would have been *true then* but not *predictive*. The text partly immunizes itself against this by shifting from "cannot" to "at present" **[PAGE 10]**, but that shift is not uniformly applied — *"there is no general solution within deep learning to the problem of generalizing outside the training space"* **[PAGE 17]** is stated flatly, without the time index.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The adjudication question: **which layer's evidence can terminate a claim made at another layer, such that no remedy proposed at the claimant's layer can rescue it?** We order layers by how resistant they are to "just get more data."

**Layer 0 — Statistical.** VC bounds, generalization gaps, sample complexity. Defeasible by more data or better regularization. This layer produces *critiques*, not vetoes. E3 and E4 live here.

**Layer 1 — Physical sensor.** Pixel intensities produced by real solid objects under real illumination. **This layer vetoes.** Marcus's strongest evidence is here: *"real-world stop signs, lightly defaced, that have been mistaken for speed limit signs"* and *"3d-printed turtles that have been mistake for rifles"* **[PAGE 13]**. The veto argument: a 3D-printed turtle is an admissible input. The system's contract, as stated, is to classify the object in view. It does not. No distributional caveat survives, because the adversary did not require privileged access to gradients in the deployed system — only a printer. Contrast this with the "sticker-clad parking signs for well-stocked refrigerators" **[PAGE 13]** case, which is also physical but attacks a captioning model whose task specification is vaguer; the turtle-versus-rifle case is the cleaner veto because 3-D object identity is well-posed.

**Layer 2 — Biomechanical / actuator.** The schedule of physical action available to an embodied agent. Marcus's Breakout evidence is a hybrid: the perturbations — *"moving the Y coordinate (height) of the paddle, or inserting a wall midscreen"* **[PAGE 8]** — are changes to the *effector geometry and environment topology*, i.e. biomechanical, not merely visual. The verdict: **this layer also vetoes**, and it vetoes §3.2's optimism specifically. If a system's competence at Breakout is destroyed by moving the paddle, then the claim that it learned "wall" or "paddle" **[PAGE 8]** is not a compression of the evidence — it is *"what comparative (animal) psychology sometimes call overattributions."*

**Layer 3 — Environmental / non-stationary ("weather").** The regime in which the data-generating process itself moves. **[PAGE 13]**: systems work *"less well in systems such as politics and economics that are constantly changing,"* and *"there is a good chance that it will eventually face the fate of Google Flu Trends."* **Verdict: conditional veto.** It vetoes the universal reading of deep learning's competence but not the technique itself — the source explicitly forecasts failure *"to the extent that deep learning is applied in tasks such as stock prediction"* **[PAGE 13]**, which is scoped. Note that Marcus hedges here (*"there is a good chance"*) while he does not hedge on the turtle. The hedge marks the layer's weaker adjudicative force.

**Layer 4 — Institutional.** Hype, funding, and the AI-winter cycle **[PAGE 17–18]**. **Verdict: no veto on the science, strong veto on the deployment timeline.** Andrew Ng's claim that *"If a typical person can do a mental task with less than one second of thought, we can probably automate it using AI either now or in the near future"* **[PAGE 2–3]** is the assertion being vetoed, and **[PAGE 17]** answers it directly: *"Machines cannot in fact do many things that ordinary humans can do in a second, ranging from reliably comprehending the world to understanding sentences. No healthy human being would ever mistake a turtle for a rifle or parking sign for a refrigerator."* That is a Layer-4 claim being defeated by Layer-1 evidence — the cleanest cross-layer veto in the document.

**The adjudication summary.** Layer 1 > Layer 2 > Layer 3 > Layer 4 in defeasibility. A Layer-1 observation (turtle→rifle) cannot be answered by any Layer-0 remedy (more data, more parameters, adversarial training on known attack families) because the attack family is open. A Layer-3 observation can be answered by Layer-0 if the deployment domain is genuinely stationary. **The most common error in reading this paper is treating its Layer-1 evidence as if it were Layer-0 evidence** — i.e. hearing "needs more data" when the paper said "painted a stop sign, and it broke."

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each veto is a pre-registered, automatically verifiable check with an explicit measurement, unit, and threshold. "Veto" means: observe the stated result, and the corresponding Marcus claim is retired or must be restated more narrowly.

**VETO 1 — The Transfer Veto (targets §3.2, [PAGE 8]).**
*Claim under test:* deep RL's Breakout solutions are *"extremely superficial"* and the system *"doesn't really understand what a tunnel, or what a wall is."*
*Protocol:* Train A3C on standard Breakout to published human-normalized score. Evaluate on two held-out variants: (i) paddle Y-coordinate shifted by one paddle-height, (ii) a midscreen wall inserted. No retraining, no fine-tuning.
*Measurement:* score retention ratio ρ = S_variant / S_baseline (dimensionless, in [0,1]); report mean ± s.e. over ≥ 10 seeds; also report the seed-variance (Henderson et al. 2017 replicability concern, **[PAGE 14]**).
*Prediction under Marcus:* ρ ≤ 0.30 on both variants, with the drop not explained by seed noise.
*VETO CONDITION:* if any method achieves ρ ≥ 0.90 on both variants **with zero gradient updates on the variant**, §3.2's strong form ("no concept of wall or paddle") is vetoed. A method requiring 10⁴ frames of variant-specific fine-tuning does **not** veto — that is precisely the objection the paper raises.
*Units/failure mode:* the test is verifiable but has a known confound — ρ can be gamed by an ensemble that was never claimed to have learned a concept. Pre-register the architecture.

**VETO 2 — The Systematicity Veto (targets §3.3, [PAGE 9–10]).**
*Claim under test:* *"when generalization requires systematic compositional skills, RNNs fail spectacularly"*; and the stronger *"Hierarchical structure ... are not inherently or directly represented in such systems"* **[PAGE 10]**.
*Protocol:* Standard SCAN-style split — train on primitive commands, test on length-generalization and on novel primitive *combinations* never co-occurring in training. Hold architecture class fixed (seq2seq transformer and seq2seq RNN, reported separately).
*Measurement:* exact-match accuracy on the compositional split, A_comp (dimensionless, in [0,1]); plus the lift over the i.i.d. split, Δ = A_comp − A_iid, in nats if scored by log-likelihood.
*Prediction under Marcus:* A_comp ≤ 0.20 for the RNN, and Δ < 0 for both classes.
*VETO CONDITION:* A_comp ≥ 0.95 **with training-set size and architecture held constant** retires §3.3 for that class of tasks. Reporting a result obtained with a retrieval or symbolic scaffold does **not** veto §3.3 — it confirms §5.2 on **[PAGE 20]**: *"The right move today may be to integrate deep learning ... with symbolic systems."*
*Units/failure mode:* splits leak. Pre-register the split file hash.

**VETO 3 — The Data-Hunger / Definitional-Abstraction Veto (targets §3.1, [PAGE 6–7]).**
*Claim under test:* *"Deep learning currently lacks a mechanism for learning abstractions through explicit, verbal definition"* **[PAGE 7]**; and the schmister inference on **[PAGE 6]**: *"a schmister was a sister over the age of 10 but under the age of 21."*
*Protocol:* Present a single definitional sentence in context, then four zero-shot queries: (Q1) do you have a schmister? (Q2) does your best friend have one? (Q3) do your parents? (Q4) restate the definition for a novel kinship tree. No few-shot exemplars, no fine-tuning.
*Measurement:* accuracy A_def over a held-out set of ≥ 200 freshly coined relational terms (dimensionless). Judge by exact match against a symbolic evaluator, so the check is fully automated.
*Prediction under Marcus:* A_def substantially below human performance, with a collapse on the *modal/temporal* query (Q3 — *"Odds are, your parents no longer do, if they ever did"* **[PAGE 6]**), because that query requires composing the definition with an age-distribution prior rather than retrieving it.
*VETO CONDITION:* A_def ≥ 0.90 on all four query types, with **zero in-weight updates**, using terms invented after the model's training cutoff, retires the literal text of **[PAGE 7]**. In-context learning *counts* as a mechanism for this purpose; a system that succeeds via retrieval from a knowledge base does not.
*Units/failure mode:* contamination is the dominant threat — hence the post-cutoff term requirement.

**Supplementary VETO 4 — The Robustness Veto (targets §3.9, [PAGE 13–14]).**
*Claim:* *"despite much active research, no robust solution has been found"* **[PAGE 14]**.
*Protocol:* Physical-world attack benchmark — printed adversary applied to a stop sign, captured under ≥ 5 lighting conditions and ≥ 3 camera distances, evaluated on a detector never trained on that attack family.
*Measurement:* attack success rate ASR (dimensionless), and the minimum physical perturbation area A_min in cm².
*VETO CONDITION:* ASR ≤ 0.05 against an *unseen* attack family, with A_min large enough that an adversary cannot simply saturate the sign, retires the sentence. ASR ≤ 0.05 against a *seen* family does not — that is bounded adversarial training, which the paper's §3.10 technical-debt argument **[PAGE 14]** predicts will not transfer.

**Aggregate prediction.** Of the four, VETO 1 and VETO 2 remain *unvetoed in the strong form* and are the highest-yield discriminators. VETO 3 is the most likely to be vetoed in the literal text of **[PAGE 7]**, because the sentence is an existence claim about *mechanism*, and in-context definitional binding is a mechanism. VETO 4 is the least likely to be vetoed in the physical setting, which is why the turtle stands: **a Layer-1 veto, unlike a Layer-0 critique, does not expire when the parameter count doubles.**

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
