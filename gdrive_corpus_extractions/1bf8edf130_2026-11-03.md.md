log [ P(Y=j+1 | x) / P(Y=j | x) ] = α_j + βᵀx, j=1,...,J−1.
Points-based tables are sufficient statistics only under constant-sum scoring rules. NHL 2-1-0 and 3-point soccer violate this, so standings derived from points alone are not sufficient for the ordinal outcome. Units: points dimensionless; log-odds dimensionless. Breakdown: non-constant-sum rules induce dependence on match-specific allocation, invalidating table sufficiency [PAGE 1].

3. Field Converter pose error (arXiv 2609.10498).
Root error reduction: e_root,init = 49 cm → e_root,TCN = 10 cm. Δe = 39 cm. Operator: ray-ground intersection root initialization r₀ = argmin_r || π_camera(r) − u_pixel ||₂², followed by Temporal Convolutional Network refinement r_t = TCN(r_{t−1:t−k}). Units: centimeters (SI length) [PAGE 1]. Breakdown: if broadcast camera is uncalibrated or the ground plane homography is singular (e.g., pitch markings occluded by standing water), ray-ground intersection has no unique solution and TCN refinement cannot recover metric depth.

4. BMASH header spotting (arXiv 2609.39300).
Fusion operator: Φ_header = Fuse( VideoSwin(frame_clip), Ψ_ball_presence, Ψ_ball_motion ). Output: header event timestamp τ̂. Breakdown: broadcast frame rate below the Nyquist rate of head-impact angular velocity aliases the header onset; ball-presence feature dropout under motion blur vetoes detection.

5. SoccerNet-FoulRet (arXiv 2610.09742).
Retrieval operator: q ↦ rank( v ∈ V_fouls by sim( E(q), E(v) ) ), with 693 human-verified queries. Relevance is defined by refereeing interpretation, not pixel contact magnitude [PAGE 1]. Units: cosine similarity dimensionless; query count dimensionless. Breakdown: zero-shot embedders remain weak; semantic gap between visual contact and referee foul decision creates irreducible label noise.

Units: physiological signals in SI (HR in bpm, accelerometry in m/s²); risk score dimensionless probability [0,1]. Breakdown: N=9 yields high-variance parameter estimates; external validity to professional players is not established.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: BMASH header spotting and Field Converter pose estimation depend on broadcast video physics. If shutter speed is too slow relative to ball velocity, motion blur induces ball-presence dropout; if rain coats the camera dome, the calibrated ray-ground intersection in Field Converter loses metric reference. Veto: any header or pose output derived from frames where pixel blur exceeds the ball diameter is physically inadmissible.

Biomechanical layer: PART’s injury model ingests wearable physiology from N=9 collegiate tennis players. If the wearable sampling rate is below the Nyquist rate of relevant HRV or acceleration transients, aliasing vetoes the input layer. Biomechanical load in tennis (serve kinematics, change-of-direction deceleration) operates at frequencies exceeding 1 Hz; sub-Hz sampling cannot reconstruct peak load.

Refereeing/semantic layer: SoccerNet-FoulRet defines relevance by refereeing interpretation, not by physical contact magnitude [PAGE 1]. A sliding tackle with high force may be deemed fair, while minimal contact may be whistled. Veto: physical contact sensors alone cannot adjudicate foul retrieval; the semantic layer (referee decision) overrides the physical layer.

Weather/environment: Outdoor soccer broadcasts are subject to precipitation and lighting changes that alter camera calibration and field homography. Field Converter’s 10 cm root error assumes a stable calibrated broadcast; standing water on the pitch or stadium-light flicker breaks the ground-plane assumption and vetoes world-grounded pose output.

Cross-layer veto proof: A system passes only if (1) video frames are unblurred and geometrically calibrated (physical), (2) wearable sampling exceeds biomechanical Nyquist (biomechanical), and (3) semantic labels align with referee interpretation rather than raw contact (refereeing). Failure at any layer vetoes the pipeline output regardless of performance at other layers.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
