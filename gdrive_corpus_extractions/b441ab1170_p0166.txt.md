**1. The Reversionary Operator**

$$ E[X_{S} | X_{F}] = \mu + r(X_{F} - \mu) $$
where $r$ is the reversion coefficient (empirically $r \approx 2/3$ for mid-parent height in Galton's data). In SI-compatible units, if $X$ represents biological mass or optical density, the units remain consistent across generations.

**2. Ancestral Information Cascade**
Extending to multiple generations, let $G_{1}$ represent a grandparent and $G_{2}$ a great-grandparent. The total expected heritage for subject $S$ is a weighted sum of ancestral deviations. Assuming independent contributions (a simplifying assumption later refined by path analysis), the expectation given a set of ancestors $\mathcal{A}$ is:
$$ E[X_{S} | \mathcal{A}] = \mu + \sum_{A \in \mathcal{A}} w_{A}(X_{A} - \mu) $$
where weights $w_{A}$ decay exponentially with generational distance $g$. For direct lineage, $w_{g} = r^{g}$. Thus, a grandparent contributes $r^2 \approx 4/9$ of their deviation relative to the parent's $r^1$.

To apply the operator above, he assigns a numerical index $D_c \in \mathbb{Z}$ to each category. The "amount of peculiarity" $D$ becomes the deviation of the assigned index from the mean index of the population.

$$ P(C_S \leq k | C_F) \approx \Phi \left( \frac{k - E[\text{Index}_S | C_F]}{\sigma_{\text{residual}}} \right) $$
