$$P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal})\ [\text{= location xG}],\quad P(s,\text{shoot},s_{no\_goal})=1-P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal}).$$

Empirical estimators (PAGE 6), with $c^{a}_{s,s'}$ = intended moves $s\!\to\!s'$, $c_{s,s'}$ = successful, $c_{s,shoot}$ = shots, $c_{s,goal}$ = goals, $c_s$ = all actions from $s$:
$$P(s,\text{move\_to}(s'),s')=\frac{c_{s,s'}}{c^{a}_{s,s'}},\quad \pi(\text{move\_to}(s')\mid s)=\frac{c^{a}_{s,s'}}{c_s},$$
$$P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal})=\frac{c_{s,goal}}{c_{s,shoot}},\quad \pi(\text{shoot}\mid s)=\frac{c_{s,shoot}}{c_s}.$$
Breakdown note: $c^{a}_{s,s'}$ is unobservable for failed moves (intended target unknown), so it is imputed by gradient-boosted trees [PAGE 6] — an estimation layer inside the "data."

Value function (PAGE 21), solved by dynamic programming to convergence, $\gamma=1$:
$$V^{\pi}(s)=\sum_{a\in A}\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s'\in S}P(s,a,s')\big(R(s,a,s')+\gamma V^{\pi}(s')\big),\quad R=\mathbb{1}[s'=s_{goal}].$$

Season projection (PAGE 8–9):
$$E[\text{goals}\mid\pi]=\sum_{s}E[\text{shots in }s\mid\pi]\,P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal}),\quad E[\text{shots in }s\mid\pi]=\pi(\text{shoot}\mid s)\,E[\text{visits to }s\mid\pi].$$
$$N=(I-Q)^{-1},\quad Q[i,j]=P(i,\text{move\_to}(j),j)\cdot\pi(\text{move\_to}(j)\mid i),$$
$N[i,j]$ = expected visits to $j$ before absorption from $i$; $I$ is the $f\times f$ identity, $f=375$. Singularity regime: $(I-Q)$ is non-invertible iff the substochastic $Q$ has spectral radius 1, i.e., a non-absorbing closed communicating class (a cycle of move actions with no escape to shoot/loss). The learned $Q$ avoids this because $P(s,\text{move\_to}(s'),s_{loss})>0$ almost everywhere, so mass leaks to absorption and $\rho(Q)<1$.

Quality correction (PAGE 9–10), for marginal shots only:
$$\text{increase: } xG_{\text{marg}}=P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal})-(\mu_s-\mu_{s,\text{below}}),\qquad \text{decrease: } xG=P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal})+(\mu_{s,\text{top}}-\mu_s).$$

Policy perturbation (PAGE 7 worked example; Appendix A.2): increase shoot from $0.2$ by 50% → $0.3$; residual $0.7$ redistributed to moves by original share, e.g. $\pi'(\text{move }A)=\frac{0.3}{0.8}\cdot0.7$. Decrease form (PAGE 22): $\pi'(\text{shoot}\mid s)=(1-x)\pi(\text{shoot}\mid s)$.

Validation (PAGE 10, 21): mean absolute error of state values $\in[0.013,0.015]$ (avg±1 std); mean relative goal error $=11.38\%$.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is explicit and traceable. The optimization substrate is Bellman's dynamic programming (ref [1], 1966), which supplies the fixed-point value equation solved on [PAGE 21]. The application lineage for soccer Markov modeling begins with Rudd (2011, ref [13]), credited on [PAGE 17] as "the first framework for analyzing soccer using Markov Chains," extended by Yam (ref [19]), whose use of the fundamental matrix to "calculate the probability of a sequence resulting in a goal" is the direct ancestor of the $N=(I-Q)^{-1}$ operator here.

The decisive methodological transfer is from basketball: Sandholtz & Bornn (refs [14],[15]). The paper is candid about the delta [PAGE 17]: prior work "only modified the frequency of shooting," whereas this paper "adjust[s] both the frequency and the efficiency," uses the fundamental matrix instead of season simulation, and works from event data rather than tracking data. The xG concept itself — the reward's empirical anchor — sits upstream of the whole enterprise (location-based scoring probability).

A distinct branch is the expected-threat / valuation school: Singh's xT (ref [17]) and Van Roy et al.'s own xT-vs-VAEP comparison (ref [12]), which frame the same "moving increases future scoring probability" question via a different (grid-based) operator. The verification stack (PRISM [7], STORM [5]) is imported from formal methods, which is what makes exact constrained-reachability queries ("move to flank, then move, then shoot") tractable — a genuine novelty claim on [PAGE 17].

Multi-week trap in the genealogy: because this paper sits on the xT/VAEP/MDP tree, there is a persistent temptation to import tracking-data conclusions (defender geometry, pressure) into a framework that only sees event locations. The paper resists this honestly on [PAGE 9] ("purely location-based xG"), but downstream summaries routinely blur the two, overstating what an event-only MDP can assert about, e.g., "open looks." Anyone chaining this work with Cervone et al. (ref [2]) or Routley/Schulte (refs [11],[16]) must keep the event-vs-tracking boundary crisp or double-count spatial information the model never ingested.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The paper contains a genuine, if compact, adversarial-modeling layer. On [PAGE 13] the defensive use case is a second-order belief statement: "a team playing against Everton might want to use a holding defensive midfielder with the aim of clogging the area to the left of the penalty arc in order to force them to move the ball to the flank instead of allowing them to shoot." This is textbook recursive ToM — modeling the opponent's policy $\pi_{opp}$, then choosing a counter-policy that shifts the opponent's reachable state distribution away from high-xG shoot states. The model supports it because the flank-constrained reachability query (Figure 6) shows the opponent's goal probability drops when forced wide, "decreases the chance of generating a goal later on and hence makes shooting more advantageous in many areas" [PAGE 13].

Crisis-habit regression: the model cannot directly represent it, and this is the honest limit. The Markov kernel is memoryless [PAGE 5], so "a player reverting to a habitual low-value long shot under late-game pressure" is outside the state description. Indirectly, the empirical base rates capture the habit: the seven high-volume long-shooters converting "6.5% of their 416 long-distance shots" [PAGE 2] while non-shot touches in the same zone scored only "2.1% of these possessions" — a persistent behavioral signature of shooting despite poor reward. That is revealed habit, but the MDP treats it as a fixed $\pi$, not as a pressure-triggered regression.

Counter-trap vs clock: the "counter-trap" is the flank-clog above. The "clock" (score, minute, remaining time) is explicitly absent — $\gamma=1$ means "all future goals are equally important" [PAGE 21], so there is no time-decay, no game-state-conditional policy. Consequently the framework is strong on spatial counter-traps (where to defend, where to shoot) and silent on temporal ones (game-management, chasing a result). Any ToM deployment that needs the opponent's clock-dependent behavior is extrapolating beyond the learned object.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A "veto" here is a state where the shoot action dominates the move action, i.e., constrained reachability gives $P(\text{goal}\mid\text{shoot}) > P(\text{goal}\mid\text{move-then-shoot})$. The proof obligation is to exhibit zones where this holds robustly.

Biomechanical layer (the one physically grounded): [PAGE 11] identifies the central-front-of-goal band as a shoot-veto zone with a mechanical cause — "when a player attempts a forward central pass from this location, the receiving player almost always needs to turn before being able to shoot," so a pass destroys xG by forcing a body reorientation. A second biomechanical veto mechanism is the inverted-winger cut-in: "These players can cut infield to get the ball on their dominant foot, which facilitates shooting" [PAGE 11], explaining team-specific left-of-center shoot zones (Hazard/Chelsea, Bolasie/Everton). These are the best-supported vetoes because they combine a physical mechanism with the model-checker result. The penalty-box border is a further veto: adding a second required move "increases the odds of losing the ball" [PAGE 12], since each move multiplies a sub-unit success probability — a compounding attrition that is provable directly from $P(s,\text{move},s_{loss})$.

Physical/veto asymmetry: the model shows shoot-preferred payoffs exceed move-preferred payoffs [PAGE 11], so the "always shoot when in doubt near the box" heuristic is a defensible cardinal rule, not just ordinal.

Weather layer — honest boundary: the paper contains no weather variable anywhere. There is no precipitation, wind, or surface term in the state space, transition, or xG. I will not manufacture a "weather veto proof." The correct adjudication is that weather is an unmodeled confounder: it can shift both $P(s,\text{move},s')$ (pass accuracy) and $P(s,\text{shoot},s_{goal})$ (strike quality) simultaneously, and because the model conditions on neither, its per-zone vetoes are marginal over weather. The only rigorous statement is negative: the framework issues no weather-conditional veto, and any such veto imported from elsewhere is exogenous to this source.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable, data-checkable scenarios where the framework's conclusion could be vetoed by verifiable outcomes.

1. Elite long-shooter efficiency (verifiable base rate). Claim: the seven named players left value on the pitch, converting "6.5% of their 416 long-distance shots" while non-shot touches in-zone scored "2.1% of these possessions" [PAGE 2]. Prediction/veto test: the shoot option's expected value should exceed the continuation value for these players in the identified zones; if a re-run showed continuation (move) EV ≥ shoot EV in those specific cells, the "leaving goals on the pitch" claim is vetoed. Caveat the authors flag themselves [PAGE 2, fn 1]: rebounds are excluded, so shot value is understated — biasing toward their conclusion, meaning a true null is even harder to obtain.

2. Uniform vs targeted policy sign flip (verifiable per-team direction). Prediction: uniform +10–20% long-distance shooting raises goals for most teams, but "Newcastle United, Liverpool and Burnley... would see the opposite" because of low conversion [PAGE 15]; targeted increases (only in shoot-preferred zones) raise goals for "all teams," ~+0.5 goals at 10% and ~+1 at 20% [PAGE 16]. Veto test: on a fresh season's event data, re-estimating $\pi$ and re-projecting should preserve these signs; a sign reversal for Chelsea/Everton/Man City (predicted biggest gainers, tied to Hazard/Sigurdsson/De Bruyne) would falsify the mechanism. Note this rides the 11.38% season-error band, so only large, multi-team reversals count as true vetoes — small flips are inside noise.

3. Bournemouth 2019/20 relegation counterfactual (verifiable against ground truth). Claim [PAGE 17]: the model implies Bournemouth "would have scored about one more goal," producing a tie with Aston Villa, and on goal difference (−24 vs −26) Villa would be relegated instead. The bridging empirical law is "every goal scored... equates to roughly one point in the table" [PAGE 16–17], and the stake is "around $255 million" [PAGE 16]. Veto test: this is a retrodiction against a known outcome and known goal/point totals, so it is exactly checkable — the vulnerability is that the "+1 goal" projection inherits the season-error band and the possession-start policy-invariance assumption [PAGE 8]; if a corrected projection (relaxing that assumption, adding rebounds) no longer clears +1 goal, the specific relegation-flip narrative is vetoed while the general "goals ≈ points" law stands independently.

Cross-cutting veto risk: all three depend on the memoryless, location-only MDP. A confounder that correlates with zone but not location (defensive pressure, score state) can silently break scenario 1 and 2 rankings; scenario 3 is the safest because it is anchored to realized totals rather than counterfactual cell values.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
