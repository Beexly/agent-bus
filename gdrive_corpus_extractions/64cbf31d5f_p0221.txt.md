### 1. The Baseline: The S-R Gradient Operator
Standard behaviorist learning (Thorp, Hull, earlier Watson) models behavior as a gradient ascent on a reinforcement potential $V(x)$. 
Let the environment be a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by state space $\mathcal{X}$, action space $\mathcal{U}$, and reward function $R(x,u)$.
An S-R agent learns a policy $\pi(u|x)$ that maximizes expected immediate reinforcement. The update rule follows a stochastic approximation (e.g., Rescorla-Wagner or basic Q-learning):
$$
\Delta \text{Strength}_{SR} = \alpha [R - V(x)]
$$
where $\alpha$ is the learning rate. In this regime, the **Information Unit** is effectively zero regarding global topology; the agent only knows the local value gradient $\nabla V(x)$. The cost of learning is measured in **Joules (J)** of physical energy expended during blind trial-and-error.

### 2. The Tolman Cognitive Operator: $\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{Cog}$
Tolman argues that animals (like the *Cebus* monkeys in Bierens de Haan’s reference or the rats in Gellermann’s double alternation tasks) do not merely associate local cues; they construct a *representation* of the world. 

We define the **Ideational Character** as the Mutual Information $I(O; \mathcal{X})$ between the Organism's internal cognitive state $O$ and the true Environmental State Space $\mathcal{X}$.
$$
I(O; \mathcal{X}) = H(\mathcal{X}) - H(\mathcal{X} | O)
$$
Here, $H(\cdot)$ represents Shannon Entropy in **bits**. 
For a purely S-R agent, $H(\mathcal{X} | O) \approx H(\mathcal{X})$ (the internal state reveals almost nothing about unvisited regions of the maze). For a Tolmanian cognitive agent, $I(O; \mathcal{X}) \to H(\mathcal{X})$. The "cognitive map" is a low-loss compression of the environmental geometry.

### 3. Formalizing "Means-End Capacities"
The **Means-End Capacity** is the ability to select an action $u_t$ based on its efficacy in reaching a distal goal state $G$, rather than its proximal reinforcement. This is formalized as a *model-based* planning operator.
Given a learned transition model $P(x_{t+1} | x_t, u_t)$ (the cognitive map), the agent selects:
$$
u^* = \underset{u \in \mathcal{U}}{\text{argmax}} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \gamma^k \mathbb{E}[R(x_{t+k}) | x_t, u]
$$
Crucially, Tolman notes the **"new or inventive character"** (page 221). This implies the search space includes previously untraversed combinations of actions. Mathematically, this is a *counterfactual rollout* within the cognitive map prior to physical execution. The "inventive" leap reduces the physical trial count $T_{phys}$ exponentially compared to S-R hill climbing.
