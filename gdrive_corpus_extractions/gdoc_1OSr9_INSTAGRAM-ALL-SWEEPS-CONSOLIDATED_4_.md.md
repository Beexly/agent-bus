The drive is a consolidation artifact, not a fresh experiment: “Method: consolidation of the existing record only — no new research.” [PAGE 1] and “Verdicts and numbers are preserved exactly as the source files state them” [PAGE 1]. That changes the audit target. The question is not “which tool is best?” but “what is the provenance closure of each claim, and which claims remain conditional on unread, gated, rate-limited, or provider-lost evidence?” The multi-hop derivation is therefore a closure chain.

Hop 1: the corpus is bounded by provider mechanics. The source records 654 unique DM-share shortcodes attempted, 356 dossiered, and “298 of 654 unique DM-share shortcodes remain UNCLASSIFIED” [PAGE 93]. The cause is explicit: a single HTTP 429 block on the post endpoint, not deletion or privacy. This means the corpus has a measurable non-response region. Any downstream claim that “the archive was fully mined” is false unless it is qualified by that missing mass. The same pattern appears in likes: “4,220 liked posts claimed by the API but never returned” [PAGE 94]. The provider ceiling is a hard observational boundary, not a research failure.

Hop 2: verification is asymmetric. The source repeatedly distinguishes API-verified metadata from creator claims. “posts lie, APIs don’t” [PAGE 8] is the operating rule, but even that is not absolute: stars drift, licenses return NOASSERTION, repos are archived, and demo trajectories are timing-racy. The correct inference is a two-level provenance model: repository existence/license/activity can be verified; claims about accuracy, revenue, speed, and causal effect usually cannot. The source itself models this with labels such as VERIFIED, CLAIM-UNVERIFIED, GATE-LOCKED, STALE, and ACTED-ON.

Hop 3: decision-gate design is the highest-risk mathematical layer. The Laya evaluation is “DESIGNED + ARMED, never run” [PAGE 93]. The source pre-registers a selective-risk bar, calibration constraints, abstention constraints, and kill conditions. It also preserves the warning “schema-valid ≠ correct” [PAGE 12]. This is the central gray zone: a typed decision model can emit a valid schema with a confident probability while choosing the wrong valid option. The gate must therefore be judged by selective risk and calibration, not by schema validity.

Hop 4: orchestration recommendations depend on cost observability and license scope. The source settles n8n as the backbone and Paperclip as a bounded pilot: “n8n as the ARE backbone now, Paperclip as a bounded pilot” [PAGE 23], with the warning “The $20-in-10-minutes burn is the documented default, not an edge case.” [PAGE 23]. The logic is not “which agent company is more autonomous?” but “which system can be warded against unbounded spend and which license permits the intended deployment?” That is a control-plane question, not a model-quality question.

Hop 5: physical, biomechanical, and weather vetoes must be pre-registered before any gate can be trusted. The source cites weather and player-transition evidence, but also labels some working papers as “as cited; not independently opened.” A veto layer that is discovered after evaluation is not a veto; it is post hoc rationalization. The correct architecture is a Boolean veto independent of the model score.

The gray-zone audit is therefore: unread items are not negative evidence; gated payloads are not failures of intelligence but consequences of the “no-messaging rule” [PAGE 5]; provider gaps are not proof of deletion; archived repos are not live tools; creator claims are not measurements; and “nothing gets killed” means cataloged with a closing step, not endorsed. The source’s strongest epistemic property is that it preserves these distinctions instead of collapsing them into a single score.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(N_{\mathrm{ret}}\) be returned items and \(N_{\mathrm{claim}}\) the provider-claimed count. Define coverage
\[
C=\frac{N_{\mathrm{ret}}}{N_{\mathrm{claim}}},\qquad 0\le C\le 1,\ \text{dimensionless}.
\]
Assumption: \(N_{\mathrm{claim}}\) is stable and not itself an estimate. Breakdown regime: if the provider changes the claimed count across pages, \(C\) is not identifiable; if \(N_{\mathrm{claim}}\to 0\), \(C\) is undefined. The source’s likes case is \(N_{\mathrm{ret}}=10{,}778\), \(N_{\mathrm{claim}}=14{,}998\), leaving 4,220 never returned [PAGE 94]. The source’s saved-post case is 267 returned against 310 claimed [PAGE 13].

Let \(\lambda(t)\) be request rate in \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(\mu\) the service rate in \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), and \(\rho=\lambda/\mu\) dimensionless. The backlog in requests is
\[
B(t)=B_0+\int_0^t [\lambda(s)-\mu]_+\,ds .
\]
Assumption: single-server approximation and stationary service during the window. Breakdown regime: \(\rho\to 1^{-}\) produces unbounded waiting time; \(\rho>1\) produces saturation and 429 cascades. This is the formal shape of the Stream A block: 285 of 298 blocked items are attributed to provider HTTP 429 on the post endpoint [PAGE 15]. The operator predicts that retrying without reducing \(\lambda\) or increasing effective \(\mu\) cannot close the gap.

For a selective gate \(g(X)\in\{0,1\}\) and loss \(\ell(f(X),Y)\), define selective risk
\[
R_{\mathrm{sel}}(g,f)=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\ell(f(X),Y)g(X)]}{\mathbb{P}(g(X)=1)} .
\]
Assumption: \(\mathbb{P}(g(X)=1)>0\) and losses are comparable across the evaluated population. Units: probability-weighted loss, dimensionless if \(\ell\) is normalized. Breakdown regime: denominator \(\to 0\) makes \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}\) explode; a gate that abstains almost everywhere is not “safe,” it is undefined. The source’s Laya bar explicitly requires passed-set mean utility, false-pass and false-reject limits, and an abstention ceiling [PAGE 21].

Calibration is measured by
\[
\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{|B_m|}{n}\left|\bar{y}_{B_m}-\bar{p}_{B_m}\right|,
\]
where \(B_m\) are confidence bins, \(\bar{y}\) is empirical frequency, and \(\bar{p}\) is mean predicted probability. Units: dimensionless probability error. Assumption: bins are non-empty and the sample is representative. Breakdown regime: too many bins produce empty or near-empty bins and unstable ECE; too few bins hide overconfidence. The source sets \(\mathrm{ECE}\le 0.07\) and \(\mathrm{Brier}\le 0.24\) as part of the pre-registered bar [PAGE 21].

Information gain from a state \(X\) about outcome \(Y\) is
\[
IG(Y;X)=H(Y)-H(Y\mid X),
\]
with \(H\) in bits when base-2 logarithms are used. Assumption: the state is available before the outcome and contains no leakage. Breakdown regime: if \(H(Y)=0\), the outcome is deterministic and \(IG\) is uninformative; if the state leaks the outcome, \(IG\) is inflated. The source’s leakage rule is explicit: no outcome field in the state; outcome columns are joined after the ruling [PAGE 21].

The veto predicate is
\[
V=\mathbf{1}\!\left[
\mathrm{ECE}>\epsilon_{\mathrm{ECE}}
\lor \mathrm{AUC}<0.50
\lor \mathrm{trunc}=1
\lor \mathrm{schema\ failure}\ge 0.05
\right],
\]
with \(V=1\) meaning “do not seat.” Units: Boolean. Assumption: thresholds are registered before evaluation. Breakdown regime: if thresholds are changed after seeing results, the veto is not a test. The source’s kill conditions are pre-registered and include “any silent-truncation incident” and “schema failure \(\ge 5\%\)” [PAGE 21]. The truncation constraint is also explicit: “small backends silently truncate” [PAGE 18], with a target state budget of \(\le 700\) tokens for Laya and \(\le 400\) for Verdict [PAGE 21].

Finally, token-budget safety is
\[
T_{\mathrm{state}}\le T_{\max},
\]
where \(T_{\mathrm{state}}\) is the tokenized state size. Assumption: the tokenizer is fixed and the truncation behavior is known. Breakdown regime: if \(T_{\mathrm{state}}>T_{\max}\), the backend may silently truncate, turning a valid evaluation into a corrupted one. The source refuses states over 800 tokens rather than sending them [PAGE 21].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression is the tendency, under rate limits and clock pressure, to revert to the earliest available habit: scrape harder, chase the gate, install the unvetted repo, or treat an unread item as negative. The source records exactly these pressure points: S7 is “UNREAD” after a lookup timeout [PAGE 95]; S8 is identified but not dissected; the AM run hit 429 on the fifth call, leaving most dissected posts dependent on stored annotations rather than fresh JSON [PAGE 41]. The recursive model must predict that an operator under pressure will confuse “not returned” with “not real.” The counter-trap is to preserve the distinction: unread is not false; blocked is not deleted; gated is not disproven.

The counter-trap is also social. Creators design comment gates as distribution funnels; the source observes that one creator’s gate carousels pull hundreds or thousands of comments while most videos get near-zero [PAGE 33]. A naive agent reciprocates by commenting, DMing, or triggering the gate. Garrett’s “no-messaging rule” [PAGE 5] is a commitment device: it blocks the reciprocal trap and forces public-source resolution. The cost is real—payloads remain unobtained—but the benefit is that the agent does not become part of the creator’s funnel or risk account actions.

The clock is the third layer. Time-sensitive items create legitimate urgency: the Siri settlement claim carries a creator-stated deadline of 2026-12-21 [PAGE 14]; AirCard is described as patched in iOS 27.2 beta 3+ [PAGE 14]; Kimi K3.1 Pro’s “Full weights due Nov 4” is a dated commitment [PAGE 52]. A recursive theory of mind must distinguish “the clock is real” from “the clock is a pressure tactic.” The correct response is a dated verification step, not an immediate action. The model of the future self is therefore: verify on the official source, record the date, and only then decide.

Finally, the source models the provider’s behavior as data, not instruction: embedded model_response_guidance fields instruct summarization instead of verbatim archiving, and the source treats them as tool data and ignores them [PAGE 18]. That is a theory-of-mind defense: the provider is not a neutral pipe; it has its own preferred output shape. The correct countermeasure is to preserve raw evidence and label the transformation.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: hardware is a hard layer. The source repeatedly marks tools as blocked by the absence of a GPU or a Mac: Openjutsu requires an NVIDIA GPU [PAGE 41], Mesh LLM “pools hardware, doesn’t create it” and has no GPU on either host [PAGE 41], OpenNavo needs a Mac [PAGE 41]. These are not preferences; they are feasibility vetoes. A gate that ignores them produces a false “TEST” verdict.

Biomechanical veto: the source cites player-transition and travel effects. Rosen’s working paper is recorded as finding “starter→backup QB transitions cost ~3.5–7.0 implied points” [PAGE 14], while the source honestly labels it “working paper as cited; not independently opened.” Nichols (2014) is recorded as a “West-to-East afternoon penalty −0.3 to −0.6 pts” that is “fully absorbed by modern closes” [PAGE 14]. Lopez & Bliss (2024) is recorded as a bye/rest edge of about +0.15 points, not significant [PAGE 14]. The adjudication rule is: a biomechanical variable can veto only if it is measured pre-outcome, has a stable sign, and is not already absorbed by the evaluation baseline.

Weather veto: Borghesi (2008) is recorded with “winds >15 mph” associated with a scoring-distribution shift of about 1.8–2.8 points [PAGE 14], and Borghesi (2007) is recorded as a home-team weather advantage [PAGE 14]. The proof obligation is not “weather matters” in general; it is “this weather variable, at this threshold, in this venue, adds information beyond the baseline.” If the baseline already contains venue and forecast, the weather layer must show incremental calibration or it is vetoed as redundant.

The cross-layer adjudication is a conjunction: \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}\lor V_{\mathrm{bio}}\lor V_{\mathrm{weather}}\lor V_{\mathrm{cal}}\lor V_{\mathrm{trunc}}\). A single failed layer blocks seating. This is stricter than a weighted score and is the right shape for a frontier system because it prevents a strong model score from overriding a physical impossibility or a calibration failure. The source’s own pre-registered kill conditions follow the same logic: “Kill = the experiment verdict only” [PAGE 21], not a permanent ban, and “nothing gets killed” in the catalog [PAGE 91].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — rate-limit counterfactual. Intervention: enforce \(\lambda<\mu\) with exponential backoff and a per-endpoint concurrency cap; do not retry the 298 blocked shares until the block clears. Verifiable prediction: blocked count decreases without a new 429 cascade; dossier coverage \(C\) rises; no account-level action is triggered. Falsifier: a new 429 burst appears while \(\lambda<\mu\), or the blocked count stays flat after the block clears. The source’s evidence base is the 298 blocked shares and the 429 signature [PAGE 93].

Scenario 2 — calibration/abstention counterfactual. Intervention: run the pre-registered Laya evaluation and seat the gate only if \(\mathrm{ECE}\le 0.07\), \(\mathrm{Brier}\le 0.24\), \(\mathrm{AUC}\ge 0.58\), false-pass \(\le 0.40\), false-reject \(\le 0.60\), and abstention \(\le 0.25\).
