\]
This is a hard physical veto; no probabilistic ensemble can override a closed pavement.

Biomechanical/human layer: deicing and crew throughput. Let \(N_{\text{crew}}\) be available deicing crews, \(t_{\text{deice}}\) the mean service time per aircraft (seconds), and \(F_{\max}\) the fatigue-limited crew duty factor. The biomechanical throughput bound is
\[
K^{\text{bio}}_t = \frac{N_{\text{crew}} \cdot F_{\max} \cdot 3600}{t_{\text{deice}}} \quad [1/h].
\]
If the ML forecast category \(k_j\) exceeds \(K^{\text{bio}}_t\), the crew layer vetoes and caps capacity at \(K^{\text{bio}}_t\), because human cycle time is a hard constraint not represented in weather ensemble members.

Weather/regulatory layer: approach and operating minima. Define ceiling minima \(H_{\min}\) and visibility minima \(V_{\min}\) for the active approach category, and wind limit \(W_{\max}\). The weather veto operator is
\[
\mathcal{V}_{\text{wx}}(\mathbf{x}_t) = \mathbb{1}[C_t < H_{\min}] \lor \mathbb{1}[V_t < V_{\min}] \lor \mathbb{1}[|\mathbf{w}_t| > W_{\max}].
\]
When \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{wx}} = 1\), operations below minima are prohibited regardless of the ensemble-derived VMC/IMC probability [PAGE 1]. The composite veto is
\[
\mathcal{V}_{\text{total}} = \mathbb{1}[\sum R_t = 0] \lor \mathcal{V}_{\text{wx}}(\mathbf{x}_t) \lor \mathbb{1}[\hat{k}^* > K^{\text{bio}}_t].
\]
If \(\mathcal{V}_{\text{total}} = 1\), the final capacity is forced to \(K_{\text{safe}} = \min(\hat{k}^*, K^{\text{phys}}_t, K^{\text{bio}}_t, K_{\text{reg}})\), where \(K_{\text{reg}}\) is the equipment-limited rate under low-visibility procedures. The exact singularity regime occurs when the ensemble median suggests marginal VMC but the deterministic ceiling/visibility observation is below DA/MDA; then the probabilistic output is non-causal and must be overridden by the physical veto layer.

Scenario 1 — Runway closure veto. Verifiable condition: a NOTAM closes the primary arrival runway during a forecast window in which the ensemble indicates VMC and the ML model outputs the highest capacity category \(k_5\). Prediction: absent the veto, the model would assign non-zero probability to \(k_5\); with the veto, actual arrivals are bounded by \(K_{\text{single}}\) or zero. Falsifiable if tower operations data show arrivals tracking \(k_5\) despite the closure, which would falsify the veto’s necessity.

Scenario 2 — Ceiling/visibility minima veto. Verifiable condition: observed ceiling falls below CAT I decision altitude (e.g., 200 ft) and visibility below RVR minimums while the ensemble median remains in a marginal VMC bin, giving the classifier a non-zero probability for a medium capacity category \(k_3\). Prediction: the weather veto forces operations to a CAT II/III-equipped subset only, reducing effective capacity to \(K_{\text{CAT II/III}} \ll k_3\). Falsifiable if actual IFR arrival rates exceed the equipment-limited rate, indicating the veto was not applied or minima were mis-specified.

Scenario 3 — Convective wind gust veto. Verifiable condition: a gust front or wind shear event with wind speed exceeding \(W_{\max}\) (e.g., >25 kt gusts) is reported by METAR or LLWAS but is not captured by the ensemble mean, which shows a smooth VMC recovery trajectory. Prediction: the unvetoed ensemble scenario would show gradual capacity recovery over \(\tau_m\); the veto imposes a step-function suspension for the gust duration (e.g., 30 min), after which recovery resumes. Falsifiable if tower logs show continuous operations through the reported gust event, falsifying the assumed wind threshold or veto logic. Each scenario produces a concrete, observable divergence between the raw ML ensemble output and veto-corrected reality, satisfying RLVR verifiability.
