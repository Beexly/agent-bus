<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory of mind is encoded in the mutual projection of opponent strategies. Player 1, observing $\mathbb{F}_1$, must assess Player 2's generating process $\zeta^*$ only through its optional projection onto $\mathbb{F}_1$; conversely Player 2 projects $\xi^*$ onto $\mathbb{F}_2$. Definition 2.5 [PAGE 8] names the families $\{\Pi^{*,1}_\theta\}$ and $\{\Pi^{*,2}_\gamma\}$ the players' subjective views precisely because each player reassesses the world conditional on the opponent not having acted—a first-order belief about the opponent's stopping hazard. The equilibrium values $V^{*,1},V^{*,2}$ [PAGE 9–10] are then second-order objects: Player 1 computes the infimum over her own future strategies $\xi$ best-responding to the projected remainder $\zeta^*;\theta$, while Player 2 takes the supremum over $\zeta$ best-responding to $\xi^*;\gamma$. This nesting is exactly the recursive structure: each player's value conditions on a measure that already embeds the opponent's equilibrium strategy.

Crisis habit regression appears near the boundary of the continuation set. Proposition 3.17 [PAGE 32–33] shows $Y^1_t\le0$ and Player 1 acts only when $Y^1_t=0$, i.e. when her value equals the optional projection of her stopping payoff. As $t$ approaches $T$ or as $\zeta^*_{t-}\to1$ (opponent almost surely stopped), the prefactor $\mathbb{E}[1-\zeta^*_{\theta-}\mid\mathcal{F}_1^\theta]$ collapses to zero and the value degenerates to $\mathbb{E}[g_\theta\mid\mathcal{F}_1^\theta]$ [PAGE 9]; a pressured player reverts from mixed randomisation to a pure jump (crisis habit regression), setting $\xi_t=1$ immediately rather than diffusing the generating process.

The counter-trap is the opponent's anticipation of this regression. If Player 1 expects Player 2 to panic-stop near $T$, Player 1 can delay, raising the probability of capturing $g_T$ versus $h_T$; but Player 2 symmetrically anticipates this and may accelerate. The support condition $\int Y^1\,d\xi^*=0$ [PAGE 33] is the equilibrium resolution: the measure associated with $t\mapsto\xi^*_t(\omega)$ is carried only by the zero set $\{Y^1_t=0\}$, so neither player gains by unilaterally shifting mass off the indifference set. The clock is the terminal constraint $\rho_T=1$ and the finite horizon $T\in(0,\infty)$ [PAGE 5]; it converts the recursive belief updating into a deadline-driven stopping game where the value processes must be càdlàg semi-martingales of class (D) [PAGE 13–14, 15], precluding indefinite postponement.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Prediction: no saddle point exists in the class of randomised stopping times; the submartingale system $M_0$ fails because the deviation $\xi\equiv\mathbf{1}\{t\ge\theta\}$ yields $\mathbb{E}[M_0(\theta)]<\mathbb{E}[M_0(0)]$, contradicting Proposition 3.7 [PAGE 15].

Veto Scenario 3 — Simultaneous-jump mass at an intermediate payoff.
