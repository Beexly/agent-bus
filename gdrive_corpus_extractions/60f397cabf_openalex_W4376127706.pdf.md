<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory-of-mind layer arises because the ego vehicle must predict other vehicles, but those other vehicles may also be predicting the ego vehicle. The source makes this explicit by saying surrounding vehicle decisions are hidden variables [PAGE 27]. In a formal belief-state notation, the ego planner maintains a belief over surrounding-vehicle maneuver \(m\in M\):
\[
b_k(m)=\Pr(m\mid I_k),
\]
where \(I_k\) is the information available at time step \(k\). If a new observation \(z_{k+1}\) arrives, a Bayesian update has the formal form  
\[
b_{k+1}(m)=\frac{\Pr(z_{k+1}\mid m,I_k)b_k(m)}{\sum_{m'\in M}\Pr(z_{k+1}\mid m',I_k)b_k(m')}.
\]  
This belief is not directly given as an equation in the source, but it is consistent with the multi-modal density \(p(x(t))=\sum_m p(x(t)\mid m)p(m)\) [PAGE 28]. The units of \(b_k(m)\) and \(p(m)\) are dimensionless probabilities.

Crisis habit regression occurs when a time-constrained planner retreats to a simpler mental model under pressure. For example, in a forced merging scenario, the source warns that overestimating uncertain longitudinal progress can cause the ego vehicle to give up merging and decelerate [PAGE 18]. This is a regression to excessive conservatism. In a cut-in scenario, the source notes that “A fixed goal state can be provided by a higher-level decision maker, but it might be unsafe in the presence of abrupt obstacles, e.g., in a cut-in scenario” [PAGE 33]. If the planner cannot update the target or occupancy quickly enough, it may either brake unnecessarily or fail to react.

The counter-trap is the thesis’s proposed mitigation: interaction-aware moving targets, multi-modal uncertainty awareness, and risk-aware robust MPC. The source describes Paper I as using moving targets updated at each time step, giving “proactive planning ability and can avoid collisions by non-local replanning” [PAGE 37]. Paper II adds a “tunable safety-awareness parameter” to balance performance and robustness [PAGE 37]. Paper IV describes a method that is “less conservative than a conventional robust MPC and more robust than a deterministic MPC” [PAGE 9]. These are counter-traps to both naive nominal planning and excessive worst-case robustness.

The clock is the MPC recursion. The source says an MPC motion planner “essentially solves an OCP at every time step and applies the first element of the optimal control sequence to refresh the reference trajectory of the ego vehicle” [PAGE 30]. The source states the horizon trade-off explicitly: longer horizons improve proactive forecasting but increase computational complexity [PAGE 20].
