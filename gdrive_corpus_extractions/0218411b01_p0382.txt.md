These are multi-week because the feedback from control structure to motivation, competence, and trust is lagged; the clock of institutional adjustment is slower than the clock of production decisions. Knight's insistence on the "long-time aspects of the problem of uncertainty and control" [PAGE 1] is precisely a warning that static snapshots mislead when the relevant time scale is weeks to months rather than instantaneous.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The theory-of-mind problem in Knight's framework is recursive because each agent must model not only the physical environment but also other agents' preferences over freedom versus security. Under crisis, habit regression operates as follows: an agent forms a first-order belief \(B_i^1\) that others prioritize the subsistence floor \(\bar c\) over novelty; this generates a second-order belief \(B_i^2\) that others prefer specialized, concentrated control because it reduces cognitive load and short-term risk. The agent then regresses to supporting centralized control, even if personally valuing self-expression, because the recursive model predicts that others will not reciprocate freedom responsibly.

The counter-trap is the reformer's error. The reformer believes \(B_r^1\): workers want responsibility and freedom of choice [PAGE 1]. But if the workers hold \(B_w^2\): they believe they cannot bear the consequences of free choice under uncertainty, then granting freedom is itself a trap—it exposes them to blame for failures they were not institutionally prepared to manage. The stability of industrial democracy depends on whether the belief-update operator \(\mathcal{T}\) has a fixed point:
\[
\mu^* = \mathcal{T}(\mu^*),
\]
where \(\mu\) is the hierarchy of beliefs over the joint utility over \((c, \text{novelty})\). Crisis shifts weights in each agent's utility toward subsistence weight \(\alpha\) and away from novelty weight \(\beta\), i.e., \(\alpha \uparrow,\; \beta \downarrow\). If the joint belief map has no fixed point, beliefs cycle and the system cannot settle into either centralized control or distributed democracy.

Clock. The relevant timescales are the crisis duration \(\tau\) and the institutional adjustment time \(T_{\text{inst}}\). If \(\tau < T_{\text{inst}}\), static control is epistemically justified because the environment changes slower than beliefs can reorganize. If \(\tau > T_{\text{inst}}\), the static assumption breaks down and dynamic adaptation is required. Knight's page [PAGE 1] closes on the cusp of this clock problem: the distinction between static and dynamic is the boundary where short-term crisis management must give way to long-term structural redesign. Recursive theory of mind thus predicts that in a prolonged crisis, agents will oscillate between demanding protection and demanding freedom, with no stable equilibrium unless the subsistence floor \(\bar c\) is secured first and the ambiguity set \(\mathcal{P}\) is reduced through learning over multiple weeks.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Knight's proposal to prohibit hazardous labor contracts and extend protection to property risks [PAGE 1] must be adjudicated across physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto layers. Each layer has an exact breakdown regime.

Layer 1 — Physical subsistence veto. Human survival requires caloric intake \(E_t\) to meet or exceed basal metabolic rate \(B\), where \(B \approx 1700\) kcal/day for a reference adult. If
\[
E_t < 0.7B
\]
for more than 14 consecutive days, irreversible physiological decline occurs, and no argument about freedom of choice or self-expression can override the veto. This layer is lexically prior to all economic contracting: if the floor is breached, the contract is void by physical necessity, not by legal preference.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical hazard veto. Let \(\lambda\) be the injury-hazard rate (events per labor-hour) associated with a contracted task. A contract to assume hazard is admissible only if
\[
\lambda \le \lambda_{\text{crit}},
\]
where \(\lambda_{\text{crit}}\) is derived from irreversible tissue-damage thresholds, not from pricing. If \(\lambda > \lambda_{\text{crit}}\), the biomechanical layer vetoes the contract regardless of wage compensation. This is not an odds calculation; it is a hard tolerance bound on mechanical work interacting with human anatomy.

Layer 3 — Weather/environmental non-stationarity veto. Property-risk protection [PAGE 1] depends on a stable environmental driver \(X_t\) (temperature, precipitation, wind). If the process exhibits a regime shift such that
\[
\left\| P_{X_{t+\Delta}} - P_{X_t} \right\|_1 > \epsilon_{\text{env}},
\]
historical actuarial measures fail. A concrete veto threshold is wet-bulb temperature \(T_{wb} > 35^\circ\text{C}\) sustained for more than 6 hours, which exceeds human evaporative-cooling capacity and makes outdoor labor physically impossible irrespective of contractual terms. This weather veto directly enforces Knight's extension of protection to property risks where fundamentals are at stake: the environment itself removes the choice set.

Adjudication hierarchy. Physical veto > biomechanical veto > weather/economic veto. If any lower layer is breached, higher-layer appeals to efficiency, freedom, or industrial democracy are inoperative. The breakdown of Knight's static analysis occurs precisely when the environmental or physiological layer shifts faster than the economic contract can be rewritten, i.e., when \(\partial P_t / \partial t \neq 0\) coincides with \(E_t < B\) or \(T_{wb} > 35^\circ\text{C}\).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — Hazardous labor contract with subsistence shortfall. Counterfactual: A firm offers a wage premium for a task with measured injury-hazard rate \(\lambda = 0.02\) events/hour, while the worker's baseline consumption \(c < \bar c\). Verifiable veto condition: \(\lambda > \lambda_{\text{crit}} = 0.005\) events/hour AND \(c < \bar c\), verified from injury logs and payroll records. RLVR prediction: The contract is prohibited; the labor supply shifts to low-hazard tasks; no wage premium can restore participation because the subsistence constraint is lexically prior.

Scenario 2 — Property insurance under environmental regime shift. Counterfactual: An insurer prices property coverage using a 30-year historical measure \(P_{\text{hist}}\). The actual generating process shifts to \(P_{\text{new}}\) with \(\|P_{\text{hist}} - P_{\text{new}}\|_1 = 0.4\). Verifiable veto condition: ambiguity diameter \(\delta_{\mathcal{P}} > 0.2\), verified from hydrological and claims records. RLVR prediction: The insurer withdraws from the market; the state must provide the backstop protection Knight allows for property risks where fundamentals are at stake [PAGE 1], because static expected-utility pricing is undefined when \(\delta_{\mathcal{P}}\) is large.

Scenario 3 — Industrial-de democracy implementation with monitoring overload. Counterfactual: A firm reduces control concentration from \(C = 0.9\) to \(C = 0.3\), raising the freedom index \(\Phi\). Monitoring cost \(M\) increases by 40% while output-efficiency gain \(G\) is only 10%. Verifiable veto condition: \(M/G > \kappa = 3\), verified from cost-accounting and production data. RLVR prediction: Within \(T = 8\) weeks, the firm recentralizes control; \(\Phi\) reverts toward its pre-reform level. This is the multi-week Trap C from the genealogy: the recursive theory-of-mind fixed point fails because agents cannot model distributed responsibility under high monitoring cost, so the system regresses to specialized control.
