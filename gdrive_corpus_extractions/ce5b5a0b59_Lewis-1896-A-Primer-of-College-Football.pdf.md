<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical law overrides tactic in three proofs.

*Weather veto.* [PAGE 189] "How much kicking can be done depends on the direction of the wind more than anything else." With $v_{\text{rel}}=v_{\text{ball}}-w$ and drag $\propto v_{\text{rel}}^2$, a strong headwind makes the vacuum range (O1) unattainable; the text's own remedy is to substitute rushing for kicking. The kicking layer is vetoed, not optimized.

*Biomechanical veto.* A back of $61$–$77\ \mathrm{kg}$ ([PAGE 136] "135 to 170 pounds") driving into a $84$–$91\ \mathrm{kg}$ guard-class defender ([PAGE 108] "185 to 200 pounds") faces a mass disadvantage; the statics of O4/O5 (toppling moment $\propto$ mass and lever arm) veto a pure force confrontation — hence [PAGE 136]'s "Plunging Back" vs "Wriggler" split and the injunction to make holes rather than out-push.

*Scoring-arithmetic veto.* Internal consistency check: [PAGE 99] "two points made or lost after a touch-down" (goal-after = 2) and [PAGE 230] "Better give up two points than four or six" fix the era's values: safety = 2, touchdown = 4, touchdown+goal = 6. Facing a near-certain touchdown ($\ge 4$, i.e. 6 if converted) with forwards "through the line and on him," the full-back should concede the safety (2). This is an arithmetic veto, verifiable from the text alone, and it is exactly what [PAGE 230] prescribes: "he should allow a safety, and not give the other side a touch-down."
