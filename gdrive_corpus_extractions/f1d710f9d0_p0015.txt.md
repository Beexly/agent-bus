Crisis habit regression operates as follows under Knightian uncertainty. When U spikes—because change C accelerates [PAGE xiv]—agents abandon deliberative calculation and regress to crisis heuristics: herd behavior, liquidity hoarding, fire-sale asset liquidation, and refusal to delegate control to entrepreneurs. However, if a sufficient population of agents adopts this counter-trap, it becomes crowded; the contrarian position ceases to be contrarian, and the very act of providing liquidity stabilizes prices so much that the uncertainty premium collapses. The counter-trap therefore contains its own regression trigger: widespread adoption of counter-cyclical courage eliminates the crisis condition that made it profitable.

The clock imposes a finite-horizon backward-induction unraveling on cooperative uncertainty-sharing. As T approaches, no agent wants to be the final bearer of residual uncertainty; each attempts to offload the risk to the next period. In the last period before T, no trade in uncertainty-bearing occurs because there is no future period to absorb the residual. Backward induction unravels the entire intertemporal sharing arrangement: at T−1 no one buys, at T−2 no one sells, and so on. The family problem [PAGE xiv] is the attempted escape from this clock: dynastic control extends the effective horizon beyond individual death, but only if property rights and social norms can enforce post-mortem wishes. Without such enforcement, the clock runs out and the crisis-habit regression becomes the default equilibrium.
