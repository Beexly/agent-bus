<THINK_TRACE>
The paper’s central dynamical claim is that an on-line 3D multi-object tracker can be made robust by combining a Kalman filter with uncertainty-aware data association. The state is an 11-tuple \(s_t=(x,y,z,a,l,w,h,dx,dy,dz,da)^\top\) [PAGE 2]. The first seven entries are center position, yaw, and box dimensions; the last four are “the linear and angular velocity of the object center multiplied by a constant ∆t” [PAGE 2]. Thus \(dx,dy,dz\) are per-frame displacements, not raw m/s unless divided by \(\Delta t\). The process model assumes constant linear and angular velocity and constant dimensions during prediction, with unknown linear and angular accelerations modeled as zero-mean Gaussian variables with covariance \(Q\) [PAGE 2]. In matrix form the prediction is \(\hat{\mu}_{t+1}=A\mu_t\) and \(\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}=A\Sigma_t A^\top+Q\) [PAGE 3]. The observation model is \(H_{7\times 11}=[I\;0]\), so detections measure position, yaw, and dimensions but not velocities; the innovation covariance is \(S_{t+1}=H\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}H^\top+R\) [PAGE 3]. Association uses the Mahalanobis distance \(m=\sqrt{(o_{t+1}-H\hat{\mu}_{t+1})^\top S_{t+1}^{-1}(o_{t+1}-H\hat{\mu}_{t+1})}\) [PAGE 4], followed by greedy bipartite matching with an upper threshold \(T\) [PAGE 4]. The update is the standard Kalman gain update, with orientation correction and a birth-death memory: “we initialize a track after having matches for 3 consecutive frames. And we terminate a track when it does not match any detection for 2 consecutive frames” [PAGE 4].

The multi-hop justification for the Mahalanobis choice is that 3D-IOU is a geometric overlap measure with a hard zero whenever prediction and detection do not overlap. The paper states: “Moreover, the Mahalanobis distance can provide distance measurement even when prediction and detection do not overlap. In this case, the 3D-IOU gives zero which prevents any data association” [PAGE 1]. This matters for “sudden accelerations and for smaller objects such as pedestrians and bicycles” [PAGE 1]. The uncertainty scaling \(S^{-1}\) also makes the distance sensitive to filter confidence, so the same residual can be accepted or rejected depending on predicted covariance. The covariance estimation step closes the loop: process noise is estimated from second differences of training trajectories, \(Q_{xx}=\operatorname{Var}((x_{t+1}-x_t)-(x_t-x_{t-1}))\), and similarly for \(y,z,a\), with velocity-noise entries set equal to the corresponding position/orientation entries [PAGE 5]. Observation noise \(R\) is estimated from detector-to-ground-truth residuals after matching by a 2D center distance criterion of less than 2 meters [PAGE 5]. The paper claims no future or ground-truth leakage: “This also ensures that our experiments on the validation and test set do not use any future or ground-truth information” [PAGE 1].

The gray-zone audit exposes several important stress points. First, there is a dimensional ambiguity: the observation model has \(H_{7\times 11}\) [PAGE 3], so \(R\) is naturally \(7\times7\), but the paper says “We set \(\Sigma_0=R\) as we initialize the multi-object tracker with the initial detection results” [PAGE 5]. Strict equality is not dimensionally well-formed unless \(R\) is embedded into an \(11\times11\) state covariance with additional velocity blocks, or unless the text is shorthand. A faithful implementation must resolve this. Second, the angular-velocity ablation is metric-blind: “excluding the angular velocity from the Kalman Filter state does not decrease the quantitative tracking performance in terms of the AMOTA. That is because the NuScenes tracking evaluation procedure uses 2D center distance as the matching criteria when counting the numbers of the false positives and false negatives” [PAGE 6]. Table 1 confirms nearly identical overall AMOTA, 56.1 for both “Ours w/o angular velocity” and “Ours,” with only tiny category differences [PAGE 6]. Third, the baseline discrepancy is a reproducibility gray zone: AB3DMOT is reported at 17.9 AMOTA in one row and 50.9 in the authors’ reimplementation, and the paper says “Currently, we do not know why the AMOTA numbers are different for the two implementations” [PAGE 6]. Fourth, threshold sensitivity is large: 3D-IOU thresholds 0.01, 0.1, and 0.25 give overall AMOTA 52.7, 49.2, and 43.9 respectively [PAGE 6]. The Mahalanobis threshold \(T\) is therefore not a cosmetic parameter; it is a decision boundary whose calibration controls false association and track loss.

State vector, page 2:
\[
s_t=(x,y,z,a,l,w,h,dx,dy,dz,da)^\top .
\]
Units: \(x,y,z,l,w,h\) in meters; \(a\) in radians; \(dx,dy,dz\) in meters per frame because they are velocity multiplied by \(\Delta t\); \(da\) in radians per frame [PAGE 2]. The paper tracks \(M\) such states, one per object [PAGE 2].

Process model, page 2:
\[
\hat{x}_{t+1}=x_t+dx_t+q^x_t,\qquad
\hat{dx}_{t+1}=dx_t+q^{dx}_t,
\]
and analogously for \(y,z,a\), with dimensions held constant:
\[
\hat{l}_{t+1}=l_t,\quad \hat{w}_{t+1}=w_t,\quad \hat{h}_{t+1}=h_t.
\]
The acceleration terms are zero-mean Gaussian with covariance \(Q\) [PAGE 2]. In matrix form:
\[
\hat{\mu}_{t+1}=A\mu_t,\qquad
\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}=A\Sigma_t A^\top+Q .
\]
Here \(A\) has identity entries on the 11 diagonals and adds each of the four velocity-like entries \(dx,dy,dz,da\) into its corresponding position/yaw entry; all other entries are zero. \(Q\) is diagonal in the paper’s implementation:
\[
Q=\operatorname{diag}(Q_{xx},Q_{yy},Q_{zz},Q_{aa},0,0,0,Q_{dxdx},Q_{dydy},Q_{dzdz},Q_{dada}),
\]
with \(Q_{dxdx}=Q_{xx}\), \(Q_{dydy}=Q_{yy}\), \(Q_{dzdz}=Q_{zz}\), and \(Q_{dada}=Q_{aa}\) [PAGE 5]. Units: \(Q_{xx},Q_{yy},Q_{zz},Q_{dxdx},Q_{dydy},Q_{dzdz}\) in \(\mathrm{m}^2\); \(Q_{aa},Q_{dada}\) in \(\mathrm{rad}^2\). The paper estimates
\[
Q_{xx}=\operatorname{Var}((x_{t+1}-x_t)-(x_t-x_{t-1}))
\]
and analogous equations for \(Q_{yy},Q_{zz},Q_{aa}\) [PAGE 5]. Assumption: acceleration variance is stationary enough across the training set to serve as a deployment covariance model.

Observation model, page 3:
\[
H_{7\times 11}=[I_7\;0],\qquad
\hat{o}_{t+1}=H\hat{\mu}_{t+1},\qquad
S_{t+1}=H\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}H^\top+R .
\]
\(R\) is diagonal with entries \(R_{xx},R_{yy},R_{zz},R_{aa},R_{ll},R_{ww},R_{hh}\), estimated from detector-minus-ground-truth residuals after 2D-center matching within 2 meters [PAGE 5]. Units: position and dimension entries in \(\mathrm{m}^2\); yaw entry in \(\mathrm{rad}^2\). The paper states: “The non-diagonal entries of R are all zero. We set \(\Sigma_0=R\) as we initialize the multi-object tracker with the initial detection results” [PAGE 5]. As noted in the audit, \(\Sigma_0\) is \(11\times11\) while \(R\) is \(7\times7\); implementation requires an embedding or additional initialization convention.

Mahalanobis association, page 4:
\[
m=\sqrt{(o_{t+1}-H\hat{\mu}_{t+1})^\top S_{t+1}^{-1}(o_{t+1}-H\hat{\mu}_{t+1})}.
\]
\(m\) is dimensionless because the quadratic form divides residuals by covariance. The greedy algorithm sorts all prediction-detection pairs by \(m\), then accepts pairs with \(m<T\), rejecting already matched rows/columns; \(T\) is dimensionless [PAGE 4]. Orientation correction rotates the prediction by 180 degrees before distance computation when the angle difference is between 90 and 270 degrees [PAGE 4].

Kalman update, page 4:
\[
K_{t+1}=\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}H^\top S_{t+1}^{-1},
\]
\[
\mu_{t+1}=\hat{\mu}_{t+1}+K_{t+1}(o_{t+1}-H\hat{\mu}_{t+1}),
\]
\[
\Sigma_{t+1}=(I-K_{t+1}H)\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}.
\]
The gain has units that map measurement residuals into state units; for observed position/yaw/dimension blocks this is identity-like, while unobserved velocity blocks receive no direct measurement correction except through covariance coupling.

AMOTA and MOTAR, page 5:
\[
\mathrm{AMOTA}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{r\in\{\frac{1}{n-1},\frac{2}{n-1},\ldots,1\}}\mathrm{MOTAR}_r,
\]
\[
\mathrm{MOTAR}_r=\max\left(0,1-\frac{\mathrm{IDS}_r+\mathrm{FP}_r+\mathrm{FN}_r-(1-r)P}{rP}\right).
\]
Both are unitless. The metric uses 2D center distance for false-positive and false-negative counting, which is why yaw accuracy is not rewarded strongly [PAGE 6].

Singularity and breakdown regimes. The Kalman gain and Mahalanobis distance require \(S_{t+1}=H\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}H^\top+R\) to be positive definite and invertible. If \(R\) is singular in an observed subspace and \(H\hat{\Sigma}_{t+1}H^\top\) has a null direction there, then \(S^{-1}\) is undefined; the distance and gain diverge or become NaN. If \(Q\) has zero variance in an axis and \(\Sigma_0\) has zero variance there, covariance can remain rank-deficient. If \(T\) is too small, all associations are rejected and tracks die after 2 misses [PAGE 4]. If \(T\) is too large, the greedy matcher can accept physically inconsistent pairs. The 3D-IOU alternative has a structural breakdown at zero overlap: “the 3D-IOU gives zero which prevents any data association” [PAGE 1]. Finally, the paper’s metric itself has a breakdown for orientation: AMOTA can stay flat while yaw is qualitatively wrong [PAGE 6].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage runs from Mahalanobis’s generalized distance in 1936 [PAGE 3] and Kalman’s linear filtering in 1960 [PAGE 3], through probabilistic robotics’ use of Mahalanobis gating for outlier detection [PAGE 3], to AB3DMOT’s 3D Kalman filter with 3D-IOU association [PAGE 2], and then to this paper’s NuScenes Tracking Challenge entry using MEGVII detections [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]. The retrocausal point is that later evaluation choices reshape which earlier components appear causally important. NuScenes AMOTA uses 2D center distance for false-positive and false-negative counting [PAGE 6], so angular velocity appears almost useless in Table 1: both “Ours w/o angular velocity” and “Ours” report overall AMOTA 56.1 [PAGE 6]. Yet the qualitative result says including angular velocity produces “better and more realistic qualitative tracking results” [PAGE 6], and Figure 2 shows orientation error without angular velocity [PAGE 7]. Thus the metric’s genealogy retroactively suppresses a real state variable.

Multi-week traps follow directly. First, covariance leakage trap: the paper emphasizes training-only statistics and no future or ground-truth information [PAGE 1], but a reproduction can accidentally tune \(Q\), \(R\), \(\Sigma_0\), or \(T\) on validation and inflate AMOTA. Second, baseline mismatch trap: AB3DMOT is listed at 17.9 AMOTA and at 50.9 in the authors’ reimplementation, with the paper stating “Currently, we do not know why the AMOTA numbers are different for the two implementations” [PAGE 6]. Any claimed gain must therefore specify detector, code path, matching rule, and threshold. Third, metric-blind trap: optimizing only AMOTA can discard angular velocity and reward center-only tracking, even though planning needs orientation. Fourth, geometric-overlap trap: 3D-IOU is structurally zero for non-overlapping but close predictions, which is common for small objects and sharp turns [PAGE 1, PAGE 7]. Fifth, threshold trap: 3D-IOU thresholds 0.01, 0.1, and 0.25 give overall AMOTA 52.7, 49.2, and 43.9 [PAGE 6], so threshold choice is a first-order experimental variable, not a footnote.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In this tracking setting, recursive theory of mind is the coupling of three beliefs: the tracker’s belief about detector noise and object dynamics, the planner’s belief about the tracker’s trajectories, and the tracker’s implicit belief about other agents’ future motion. The paper encodes the last belief as constant linear and angular velocity with Gaussian acceleration noise [PAGE 2]. The crisis habit regression appears when the system falls back to geometric overlap or heuristic identity covariance under stress: AB3DMOT “has difficulties to continue tracking when the object makes a sharp turn” because “the Kalman Filter’s predicted 3D bounding box does not overlap with any detection box when the car is turning sharply” [PAGE 7]. That is a habit regression to 3D-IOU and heuristic covariance exactly when sudden acceleration or rotation breaks the constant-velocity prior.

The counter-trap is the Mahalanobis-plus-data-driven-covariance design: it uses \(S^{-1}\) to weight residuals and it estimates \(Q\) and \(R\) from training statistics [PAGE 1, PAGE 5]. This counter-trap is not unconditional. If \(R\) is estimated in clear weather and deployment has lidar degradation, the observation model is misspecified; if \(Q\) is underestimated, a true maneuver looks like an outlier. The clock is the temporal memory that prevents instantaneous belief flips: birth after 3 consecutive matches and death after 2 consecutive misses [PAGE 4], plus the orientation correction that rotates by 180 degrees when the detector flips facing direction between 90 and 270 degrees [PAGE 4]. These clocks define a belief-update latency. A pedestrian occluded for more than 2 frames can be killed; a sharp turn within one frame can violate the constant-velocity assumption. The recursive failure mode is that the planner treats a dropped or yaw-flipped track as an agent intention rather than a tracker belief. The paper’s own qualitative evidence shows the counter-trap: Mahalanobis tracking continues through sharp turns and pedestrian occlusion noise, while the baseline loses the object [PAGE 7, PAGE 8].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is a hard constraint imposed outside the Gaussian filter that can overrule a low Mahalanobis distance. Sound vetoes must be necessary conditions for physically realizable trajectories. Let \(a_{\max}\) be a calibrated maximum per-frame acceleration magnitude in \(\mathrm{m}/\mathrm{frame}^2\), and let \(\omega_{\max}\) be a calibrated maximum yaw rate in \(\mathrm{rad}/\mathrm{frame}\). For a proposed association, compute the implied increments from the matched state sequence. Veto if
\[
\left\|(d_{t+1}-d_t)\right\| \gt a_{\max}
\]
or
\[
\left|da_{t+1}-da_t\right| \gt \omega_{\max}.
\]
Also veto nonpositive dimensions \(l,w,h\le 0\), since box scale is physically constrained and the filter assumes constant positive dimensions during prediction [PAGE 2]. This veto is sound because any true object trajectory must satisfy some finite acceleration and yaw-rate bound; rejecting an association that violates a calibrated necessary bound cannot reject a physically realizable true track, except when the bound itself is misspecified.

Biomechanical vetoes are category-specific instantiations. A pedestrian association that implies car-like acceleration or yaw rate is suspect; a bicycle or motorcycle has different plausible turn and acceleration envelopes. The paper motivates Mahalanobis specifically for “smaller objects such as pedestrians and bicycles” [PAGE 1] and shows category-level gains for bicycle, motorcycle, and pedestrian [PAGE 6]. A biomechanical gate prevents the association from matching a pedestrian detection to a car track merely because covariance inflation made \(m\) small.

Weather vetoes address observation-model validity. If \(R_{\mathrm{weather}}=R_{\mathrm{clear}}+\Delta R\) with \(\Delta R\) positive semidefinite, then \(S_{\mathrm{weather}}=S_{\mathrm{clear}}+\Delta R\) and, for the same residual \(r\),
\[
r^\top S_{\mathrm{weather}}^{-1}r \le r^\top S_{\mathrm{clear}}^{-1}r.
\]
Thus weather-inflated \(R\) makes the Mahalanobis distance smaller for the same residual, which can make gating more permissive and admit clutter. A weather veto must therefore require temporal support, such as the 3-consecutive-match birth rule [PAGE 4], or an explicit sensor-degradation mode that changes the association policy rather than only inflating \(R\). Conversely, a physically consistent prediction can rescue a large-residual true detection under occlusion, because the filter’s \(S\) encodes prediction uncertainty. The adjudication rule is: use Mahalanobis as a soft Gaussian score, but let physical, biomechanical, and weather-state invariants veto associations that are physically impossible or statistically unidentifiable.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
