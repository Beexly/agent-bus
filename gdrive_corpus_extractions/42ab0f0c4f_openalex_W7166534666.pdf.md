Deep multi-hop derivation and gray-zone audit. The paper’s pipeline is: video frames -> detector -> coarse keypoints \(J=\{j_1,\dots,j_N\}\), with \(j_k=(x_k,y_k,c_k)\) -> STGCN-IS instance separation -> per-instance adaptive spatiotemporal graph convolution -> DSTformer scene/skeleton dual-branch enhancement -> BCQ template/RL refinement. The first multi-hop dependency is: STGCN-IS assumes detection boxes \(B=\{b_1,\dots,b_M\}\) are reliable because candidate keypoints are selected by IOU with \(b_m\) at threshold \(0.7\) [PAGE 6]; only then does spectral clustering on the affinity matrix \(S=[s_{kl}]\) split multi-person skeletons. The second hop is: DSTformer assumes STGCN-IS has produced identity-consistent subgraphs, because the spatial branch aligns \(F_m\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times C_g}\) to scene features via ROI Align [PAGE 7]; if instance separation fails, occlusion completion completes the wrong person’s joint. The third hop is: BCQ assumes DSTformer features are clean enough for category matching \(\hat c=\arg\max_c \operatorname{sim}(F_{dst},F_{T_c})\) [PAGE 9]; if the sport is out of distribution, BCQ applies a template that may be biomechanically wrong. This is a cascade, not three independent modules.

The deepest gray-zone is Eq. (7). The paper states \(w_t=\max(3,\min(11,\gamma\log(v_t+1)+1))\), with \(\gamma=2.5\) [PAGE 8], and then says: “ensuring that smaller windows (w ≈3) are used for fast motions (e.g., sprinting, where vt > 5 pixels/frame) ... and larger windows (w ≈11) are used for slow movements ... (vt < 1 pixel/frame)” [PAGE 8]. This is directionally contradictory. As written, for \(v_t>5\), \(\log(v_t+1)>1.79\), so \(w_t>5.48\); for \(v_t<1\), \(\log(v_t+1)<0.693\), so \(\gamma\log(v_t+1)+1<2.73\), and the outer max sets \(w_t=3\). Thus the equation gives larger windows for faster motion and the minimum window for slow motion, the opposite of the prose. A multi-week implementation trap follows: a team that implements Eq. (7) literally cannot reproduce the claimed fast-motion behavior, and a team that implements the prose cannot reproduce the equation. The claimed MPJPE reduction of \(5.2\)–\(9.2\) pixels in highly dynamic scenarios [PAGE 2] is therefore not attributable to Eq. (7) without a sign inversion or a different velocity definition.

A second gray-zone is dimensional consistency. Eq. (4) writes \(F_m^t=\sigma(A_m\cdot F_m^{t-1}\cdot W_t)\) [PAGE 7], with \(A_m\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}\) and \(W_t\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times C_{in}\times C_{out}}\). If \(W_t\) is genuinely three-dimensional, the matrix product is undefined; if it is a 2D \(C_{in}\times C_{out}\) kernel, the temporal index \(T\) is not represented. Eq. (8) writes temporal attention as \((\hat F_m^t W_q)(\hat F_m^{t' }W_k)^T/\sqrt{d_k}\) [PAGE 8], but \(\hat F_m^t\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times C_{out}}\), so the product is \(K\times K\), not the scalar \(\beta_{t,t'}\) described in the text. The paper says “(F̂_t^m · W_q) ∈ R^dk is the query vector of the t-th frame” [PAGE 8], which requires flattening or pooling over \(K\) joints; that operation is not specified. A third gray-zone is dataset cardinality. Table 1 gives COCO 17 keypoints, MPII 16, and LSP 14 [PAGE 11], but PCK@0.5 says “N is the total number of keypoints (17 keypoints are used in this experiment)” [PAGE 12], and BCQ uses \(K=17\) joints [PAGE 9]. The paper does not define padding, remapping, or masking from 14/16 to 17. This directly threatens the LSP template library, which is built from “professional athlete annotations” and “joint angle information” [PAGE 11] but then used with a 17-joint state/action space.

A fourth gray-zone is the BCQ acronym and reward. “BCQ” in the offline-RL literature usually denotes Batch-Constrained deep Q-learning, not “Behavior Cloning and Q-learning”; the paper defines its own BCQ as a combination of behavior cloning and Q-learning [PAGE 2], so the acronym is overloaded. Eq. (14) defines \(r_2=\prod_{k=1}^K I(\theta_{min,k}\le \pi_\theta(s)[k]+a[k]\le \theta_{max,k})\) [PAGE 9]. This product is zero if any one joint violates its physiological range, making the feasibility reward sparse and non-smooth. Eq. (18) writes \(a^*=\arg\max_a(Q_\psi(s,a)+\epsilon\log\pi_\theta(s,a))\) [PAGE 10] and calls \(\epsilon=0.1\) an “exploration factor” [PAGE 10]. Adding \(+\epsilon\log\pi_\theta\) favors high-probability actions, i.e., exploitation of the initial policy; an entropy/exploration bonus would normally subtract \(\log\pi_\theta\) or add an entropy term. This is another formal inversion.

Finally, the metric definitions have singularities. Eq. These are not cosmetic; they affect whether the reported \(79.5\%\) AP50 and \(88.6\%\) PCK@0.5 are comparable to baselines. The paper’s own detection-box cascade admission is decisive: “When target misses or small target athlete detection boxes are missing, individual initial boundaries are directly lost, causing subsequent skeletal distance clustering to lose its dividing benchmark” [PAGE 28]. That is the physical failure mode behind the gray zone.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source’s core operators, with units, assumptions, and breakdown regimes:

1. Skeletal affinity, Eq. (1) [PAGE 6]:
\[
s_{kl}=\exp\left(-\alpha\|j_k-j_l\|_2^2\right)\cdot \operatorname{sim}(t_k,t_l),
\]
where \(j_k=(x_k,y_k,c_k)\). If the norm uses all three components, it mixes \(\mathrm{pixel}^2\) with dimensionless confidence; if it uses only \((x_k,y_k)\), units are \(\mathrm{pixel}^2\) and \(\alpha\) must be \(\mathrm{pixel}^{-2}\) unless coordinates are normalized. The paper sets \(\alpha=0.1\) [PAGE 6] but does not state whether coordinates are normalized at this stage. Breakdown: \(\alpha\to0\) makes affinity depend only on joint-type similarity; \(\alpha\to\infty\) annihilates all off-diagonal affinities; if the candidate set from IOU \(\ge0.7\) is empty, spectral clustering has no valid graph. The paper states “keypoints with IOU ≥0.7 are selected as candidate points” [PAGE 6].

2. Adaptive adjacency, Eq. (2) [PAGE 7]:
\[
A_m[i][j]=\operatorname{softmax}\left(\operatorname{MLP}(f_i\|f_j)\right),
\]
with \(f_i,f_j\in\mathbb{R}^{256}\), one hidden layer of size \(128\), and row normalization. Units are dimensionless probabilities. Breakdown: if all logits are equal, \(A_m\) is uniform; if logits saturate, softmax becomes near one-hot and graph propagation collapses to a single neighbor. The paper says “ensuring that each row of the adjacency matrix sums to 1” [PAGE 7].

3. Dynamic temporal kernel, Eq. (3) [PAGE 7]:
\[
W_t=W_0+\beta\cdot\operatorname{sigmoid}(v_t)\cdot W_\Delta,
\]
with \(v_t=\|j_k^t-j_k^{t-1}\|_2\) in pixels/frame, \(\beta=0.5\), \(T=5\), \(C_{in}=256\), \(C_{out}=512\). Units: \(v_t\) is pixels/frame; sigmoid is dimensionless; \(W_0,W_\Delta\) carry the same weight units. Breakdown: at \(v_t=0\), \(\operatorname{sigmoid}(0)=0.5\), so \(W_t=W_0+0.25W_\Delta\), not \(W_0\); at high \(v_t\), sigmoid saturates and the dynamic component saturates. The paper’s Eq. (4) [PAGE 7],
\[
F_m^t=\sigma\left(A_m\cdot F_m^{t-1}\cdot W_t\right),
\]
is dimensionally ambiguous because \(W_t\) is declared \(\mathbb{R}^{T\times C_{in}\times C_{out}}\); a 3D tensor cannot be inserted into that matrix product without a specified contraction over \(T\).

4. Occlusion-aware spatial attention, Eqs. (5)–(6) [PAGE 8]:
\[
\alpha_{k,e}=\frac{\exp(\cos(f_k,f_e))}{\sum_{e'}\exp(\cos(f_k,f_{e'}))},\qquad
\hat f_k=f_k+\sum_e\alpha_{k,e}f_e.
\]
Units: cosine similarity is dimensionless; \(\alpha_{k,e}\) is a probability. Assumption: at least one scene entity \(e\) exists and is semantically relevant. Breakdown: if the entity set is empty, the denominator is zero; if all entities are irrelevant, the weighted sum injects noise; if the joint is fully occluded and no torso/adjacent anchor is visible, Eq. (6) has no reliable anchor. The paper admits “When visible information is insufficient, the completion accuracy will drop by 15%-20%” [PAGE 26].

5. Dynamic temporal window, Eq. (7) [PAGE 8]:
\[
w_t=\max(3,\min(11,\gamma\log(v_t+1)+1)),\quad \gamma=2.5.
\]
Units: \(v_t\) in pixels/frame; \(w_t\) intended as frames. Breakdown: the formula is monotone increasing in \(v_t\), but the prose claims the opposite: “smaller windows (w ≈3) are used for fast motions ... and larger windows (w ≈11) are used for slow movements” [PAGE 8]. Also \(w_t\) is real-valued but used as a window boundary, requiring floor/ceil; the paper does not specify rounding. At \(v_t<1\), the formula yields \(w_t=3\), not \(11\); at \(v_t>5\), it yields \(w_t>5.48\), not \(3\).

6. Temporal attention, Eqs. (8)–(9) [PAGE 8]:
\[
\beta_{t,t'}=\operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{(\hat F_m^tW_q)(\hat F_m^{t'}W_k)^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right),\qquad
\tilde F_m^t=\sum_{t'\in[t-w/2,t+w/2]}\beta_{t,t'}\hat F_m^{t'}.
\]
With \(\hat F_m^t\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times C_{out}}\) and \(W_q,W_k\in\mathbb{R}^{C_{out}\times d_k}\), the product is \(K\times K\), not scalar. Breakdown: without joint pooling/flattening, the softmax is over joint pairs rather than time pairs; with \(d_k=64\), scaling is dimensionless.

7. Residual fusion, Eq. (10) [PAGE 8]:
\[
F_{dst}^t=\tilde F_m^t+\operatorname{MLP}(\hat F_m^t),
\]
with \(C_{dst}=768\). This is a residual sum; units are feature amplitudes, not physical units.

8. BCQ category matching and cloning, Eqs. (11)–(12) [PAGE 9]:
\[
\hat c=\arg\max_c \operatorname{sim}(F_{dst},F_{T_c}),\qquad
L_{BC}=\frac{1}{TK}\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{k=1}^K\left(\pi_\theta(F_{dst}^t)[k]-\theta_{\hat c,k}^t\right)^2.
\]
Units: \(\theta\) in radians; loss in \(\mathrm{rad}^2\). Breakdown: if the sport is not in the 8 templates, argmax forces an OOD template; if \(K\) differs between dataset and template, the loss is undefined unless remapping is specified.

9. BCQ rewards, Eqs. (13)–(16) [PAGE 9–10]:
\[
r_1=\exp(-\lambda\|\pi_\theta(s)+a-\theta_{\hat c}^t\|_2^2),\quad
r_2=\prod_{k=1}^K I(\theta_{min,k}\le \pi_\theta(s)[k]+a[k]\le \theta_{max,k}),
\]
\[
r_3=\exp(-\mu\|a-a_{t-1}\|_2^2),\quad
r=\alpha_1r_1+\alpha_2r_2+\alpha_3r_3,
\]
with \(\lambda=0.5\), \(\mu=0.3\), \(\alpha_1=0.4\), \(\alpha_2=0.4\), \(\alpha_3=0.2\). Breakdown: \(r_2\) is zero if any joint violates its range; the feasibility signal is then absent. If the template mean is outside the athlete’s individual range, the optimizer cannot satisfy both \(r_1\) and \(r_2\).

10. TD loss and action selection, Eqs. (17)–(18) [PAGE 10]:
\[
L_Q=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q_{\psi'}(s',a')-Q_\psi(s,a)\right)^2\right],\quad
a^*=\arg\max_a\left(Q_\psi(s,a)+\epsilon\log\pi_\theta(s,a)\right),
\]
with \(\gamma=0.9\), \(\epsilon=0.1\). Breakdown: \(\log\pi_\theta(s,a)\to-\infty\) if \(\pi_\theta(s,a)\to0\); the \(+\epsilon\log\pi_\theta\) term favors high-probability actions and is not an exploration bonus in the usual entropy-regularized sense.

11. Metrics, Eqs. (19)–(23) [PAGE 12–13]:
\[
\mathrm{PCK@0.5}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N I\left(\frac{\|\hat j_k-j_k\|_2}{d_{torso}}\le0.5\right),\quad
\mathrm{MPJPE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N\|\hat j_k-j_k\|_2.
\]
Units: PCK dimensionless; MPJPE pixels. Breakdown: \(d_{torso}=0\) is a singularity; \(N=17\) is stated for all experiments [PAGE 12], while Table 1 gives MPII 16 and LSP 14 [PAGE 11]. The AP50 formula \(\mathrm{AP50}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_m \mathrm{Precision}(m,\mathrm{IOU}=0.5)\) [PAGE 12] is mean precision at a fixed IOU, not the usual area under the precision-recall curve. The recall formulas are fractions of correctly detected joints or instances [PAGE 13].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the system as four agents with recursive beliefs: detector \(D\), graph separator \(G\), temporal enhancer \(T\), and optimizer \(O\). \(G\) believes \(D\)’s boxes are reliable; \(T\) believes \(G\)’s identity labels are correct; \(O\) believes \(T\)’s features and the template category \(\hat c\) are correct. Crisis habit regression occurs under distribution shift. When \(D\) misses a small athlete, \(G\) loses its “dividing benchmark” [PAGE 28] and regresses to over-segmentation or under-segmentation. When \(G\) over-segments, \(T\) regresses to completing joints within the wrong subgraph. When \(T\) sees complete occlusion, \(O\) regresses to the template mean; the paper itself says completion accuracy drops \(15\%\)-\(20\%\) when visible information is insufficient [PAGE 26]. The counter-trap is the corrupted-data ablation: removing DSTformer causes Recall drops of \(12.1\%\), \(13.5\%\), and \(11.2\%\) under Gaussian noise, motion blur, and random occlusion [PAGE 22], while removing STGCN-IS causes AP50 drops of \(11.5\%\), \(12.8\%\), and \(12.2\%\) [PAGE 22]. That is evidence that DSTformer is the counter-trap module for visual corruption and STGCN-IS is the counter-trap module for multi-person aliasing. But the clock is unforgiving: the 3-person inference time is \(37.8\) ms [PAGE 17], leaving only \(12.2\) ms under a \(50\) ms real-time budget; ultra-high-speed motion with single-frame joint movement exceeding \(20\) pixels and stride frequency above \(4\) steps/second still increases MPJPE by \(8\)-\(10\) pixels even at the minimum window [PAGE 26–27]. Recursive theory of mind therefore says: the model’s self-belief “I am robust because I have three modules” is only valid while each upstream agent’s assumption holds. The crisis habit is to trust the detector box; the counter-trap is to corrupt the box and measure the cascade; the clock is the temporal sampling limit that no amount of attention can escape.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
