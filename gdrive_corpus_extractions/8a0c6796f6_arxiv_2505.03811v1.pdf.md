<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The formal operators below are the ones explicitly present in the source. Units are stated where the paper makes them implicit.

**1. Standard GAN min-max objective.** [PAGE 3] states:
\[
\min_{p_g}\max_{D(x)}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_d}[\log D(x)]+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_g}[\log(1-D(x))]
\]
Here \(D(x)\in[0,1]\) is a dimensionless probability. If natural logarithms are used, the expectation is in nats per sample; with \(\log_2\), it is in bits per sample. The optimal discriminator is stated on [PAGE 4] as:
\[
D^*(x)\equiv \frac{p_d}{p_d+p_g}.
\]
Breakdown: this ratio is undefined when \(p_d(x)+p_g(x)=0\), that is, outside the union of the two supports. If both densities are zero at a point, the expression has a \(0/0\) singularity; implementations avoid this by restricting to supported samples, smoothing, or using a different divergence.

**2. Categorical cross-entropy.** [PAGE 4] defines:
\[
\mathrm{CCE}(y_{\rm true},y_{\rm pred})
=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log P_{\rm model}[y_i\in C_{y_i}].
\]
Units: \(P_{\rm model}\) is dimensionless; \(\mathrm{CCE}\) is nats per sample with natural log, or bits per sample with base-2 log. Breakdown: if \(P_{\rm model}[y_i\in C_{y_i}]=0\) for an observed class, then \(\log 0\) diverges to \(+\infty\). This is the exact log-singularity regime. It requires label smoothing, probability clamping, or an explicit floor \(\epsilon_{\rm prob}\gt 0\).

**3. ScarceGAN supervised positive loss.** [PAGE 4] states:
\[
L_D^{S+}=\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in R)+\%\mathrm{Recall}_{+ve}
=-\frac{1}{|p_d^{R+}|}\sum_{i=1}^{|p_d^{R+}|}\log P_{\rm model}[y_i\in R]+\%\mathrm{Recall}_{+ve}.
\]
Units and breakdown: the first term is an information loss; the second is a dimensionless fraction or percent. The sum is only formally well-defined if the recall term is scaled into the same numerical units as CCE or if both are treated as normalized dimensionless terms. If \(|p_d^{R+}|=0\), the division by zero makes the positive loss undefined. If the positive class has no labelled support, the objective cannot train the positive boundary.

**4. ScarceGAN supervised negative loss with leeway.** [PAGE 4] states:
\[
L_D^{S-}=\alpha\,\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in\{D,N,H\})+(1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in U),
\]
with \(\alpha+(1-\alpha)=1.0\). The paper says “We find the setting between 0.7 - 0.6 ideal for 𝛼” [PAGE 4]. Units: \(\alpha\) is dimensionless; both CCE terms are nats or bits per sample. Breakdown: if \(\alpha=1\), the leeway term vanishes and the discriminator is forced to high-confidence exact negative classification; if \(\alpha=0\), known-negative supervision vanishes and only the unknown class remains. If \(\alpha\notin[0,1]\), the expression is not a convex mixture and the “leeway exemption” interpretation fails. The supervised aggregate is:
\[
L_D^{S}=L_D^{S+}+L_D^{S-}.
\]

**5. ScarceGAN unsupervised discriminator loss.** [PAGE 5] states:
\[
L_D^{US_{\rm Real}}=\alpha\,\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in K)+(1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in U),
\]
\[
L_D^{US_{\rm Fake}}=\mathrm{CCE}(x|C_y\in F),
\]
\[
L_D^{US}=0.5*(L_D^{US_{\rm Real}}+L_D^{US_{\rm Fake}}).
\]
Units: CCE in nats or bits per sample; \(0.5\) is a dimensionless averaging weight. Breakdown: the same \(\alpha\)-boundary conditions apply. If the unlabelled real distribution \(p_d\) is shifted away from the labelled prior, the K/U split is not identifiable from the objective alone; the leeway class can absorb arbitrary mass.

**6. ScarceGAN generator “bad generator” objective.** [PAGE 5] gives:
\[
\min_G -H(p_G)+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_g}\log(p(x))\not\parallel[p(x)\gt \epsilon]
+\left\|\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_g}f(x)-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_d}f(x)\right\|^2.
\]
The paper states that “\(\not\parallel[\cdot]\) is the indicator function” and that the confidence threshold “\(\epsilon\) is chosen to be 0.75” [PAGE 5]. Units: \(H(p_G)\) is entropy in nats or bits; the feature-matching term is a squared norm in the activation units of layer \(f\); \(\epsilon\) is a dimensionless confidence threshold. Breakdown: if \(p(x)\) is a continuous density, \(\log p(x)\) is only defined up to an additive constant dependent on the reference measure, so normalized features or discrete bins are required for a unit-consistent implementation. The indicator is discontinuous at \(p(x)=0.75\); exact equality has measure zero in theory but is threshold-sensitive in finite precision.

**7. Longitudinal feature construction.** The paper does not give a single closed-form Prophet equation, but it states that “we define our own set of 10 hyper-parameters from Prophet’s parameter grid, which includes \(1^{st}, 2^{nd}\) and \(3^{rd}\) harmonics of the seasonality periodogram, Laplace smoothing coefficient, mean and standard deviation of the series’ change rate and the growth rate” [PAGE 6]. Forecast reliability is reported as “Mean Percentage Errors of 5-10% on forecasted predictions” [PAGE 6]. Breakdown: if forecast error exceeds the reported 5-10% band, the third-order statistics are outside their validated regime and should veto the feature layer. The observation window is constrained: “The length of observation per sample varied from 15 upto 45 days” [PAGE 6]. Shorter windows are outside the stated support.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is explicit. The GAN line begins with Goodfellow et al. 2014 [PAGE 10], then SSGAN via Odena 2016 and Salimans et al. 2016 [PAGE 3, PAGE 10], then CatGAN via Springenberg 2016 [PAGE 10], then GenPU via Hou et al. 2018 [PAGE 10]. ScarceGAN says it is “inspired from these works” and reformulates semi-supervised GAN by accommodating weakly labelled multi-class negative samples and the available positive samples [PAGE 1, PAGE 3]. The PU-learning line is also explicit: early approaches isolate “Reliable Negatives” from unknown data, and weighted logistic regression treats unknown data as negative with a decayed weight [PAGE 2], citing Liu et al. 2002, Li and Liu 2003, and Lee and Liu 2003 [PAGE 10]. The paper rejects one-class classification because it does not leverage the small positive ground truth [PAGE 2]. The anomaly-detection line includes AnoGAN and ALAD [PAGE 3, PAGE 10], plus AAE prototypes [PAGE 7]. The time-series line includes Prophet [PAGE 6, PAGE 10], with ARIMA and TBATS as rejected alternatives [PAGE 6]. This genealogy matters because ScarceGAN’s novelty is not the GAN itself; it is the leeway label-space expansion and the divided negative focus.

The multi-week traps are where the lineage becomes operationally dangerous. The data are collected across three observation intervals: “the first, the third and then the last quarter of the year 2020” [PAGE 6]. The evaluation sets are “March \(1^{st}\) of 2021, November \(12^{th}\) and August \(14^{th}\) of 2020” [PAGE 7]. A multi-week trap is that simple longitudinal aggregates such as mean, max, and median can erase the trend that defines risk; the paper explicitly says “trend preserving aggregations” are “much more effective than longitudinal aggregates like the mean, max and median values” [PAGE 5]. A second trap is temporal leakage: if the feature window overlaps the label-confirmation window, the model can learn the counsellor’s decision rather than the player’s behavior. The paper’s labels come from business monitoring, rule-engine flags, counselling, throttling, and barring [PAGE 6]. A third trap is label drift across weeks: the flagging precision “fluctuates between 7% - 15% on a monthly basis for the past 6 - 8 months” [PAGE 6], so a prior trained in one quarter may not be weak in the same way in another. A fourth trap is that the positive class is defined by intervention outcome, not by a stable natural label; the paper says “about 85% or higher were declared as false positives post counselling” [PAGE 7]. A fifth trap is the leeway class itself: in the KDD setting, “since prior is strong, the leeway class does not accumulate any samples in the training as well as the test dataset” [PAGE 9]. Thus the leeway mechanism is not a universal component; it is a weak-prior corrective that can become inert when the prior is strong. Any deployment that assumes leeway is always active is retrodictively inconsistent with the source.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive structure is visible in the model and in the business process. In the model, the generator is told to “spy on the intermediate layers of the discriminator” [PAGE 5], so the generator is not merely producing samples; it is modeling the discriminator’s feature statistics. The discriminator, in turn, is modeling the known negative classes, the unknown leeway class, and the scarce positive class [PAGE 4]. This is a two-level recursion: generator models discriminator; discriminator models labels and unlabelled structure. The paper’s “bad generator” is a counter-trap against discriminator overconfidence: it pushes generated samples into low-density regions and increases entropy [PAGE 5]. The leeway class is a second counter-trap: it prevents a confused discriminator from assigning a “not so confident negative sample to a positive sample space” [PAGE 2]. In ablation, removing leeway drops precision to 40.5% and recall to 57%, versus 75.7% and 72.5% with leeway [PAGE 8]. That is the strongest internal evidence that the counter-trap is doing real work.

“Crisis habit regression” appears as the risk that a player’s behavior returns to an over-indulgent pattern after an intervention, or that the intervention itself changes the label distribution. The paper’s business loop is explicit: predicted users are sent for counselling and are then “1) predicted as false positives and are allowed to continue playing as usual, 2) predicted emerging risky and some kind of throttles are placed on their engagement on the platform or 3) predicted highly risky and are barred from playing” [PAGE 6]. This creates a recursive theory-of-mind problem: the model is trained on outcomes that were produced by a policy that used earlier model or rule-engine outputs. If the player responds to throttling by reducing play, the confirmed label is intervention-conditioned; if the player relapses after unblocking, the false-positive label may be a censored future positive. The paper does not resolve this feedback loop. A deployment that treats “unblocked” as clean negative is therefore vulnerable to crisis habit regression.

The “clock” is the longitudinal observation window. The paper uses “at least 15 and at most 45 active play days” [PAGE 6] and three distinct evaluation days [PAGE 7]. The clock trap is that a model trained on 15-45 day windows may not transfer to shorter windows, longer windows, or a different cadence of intervention. The paper’s own feature layer depends on Prophet forecasts with “Mean Percentage Errors of 5-10%” [PAGE 6]; a multi-week drift that pushes error beyond that band invalidates the third-order statistics. The clock also affects the leeway class: a player who is “definitely not risky but not confidently fitting into any of the 3 negative classes” [PAGE 4] may be a transient state. If the U class is estimated over a short window, it can absorb a future positive; if estimated over a long window, it can hide an emerging positive. The counter-trap is to keep U as a diagnostic complement, not as a permanent negative label. The paper hints at this by saying the process “generates complementary labels for samples with unknown labels for future analysis” [PAGE 2], but it does not give a closed-loop update rule. That is the recursive-theory gap.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
