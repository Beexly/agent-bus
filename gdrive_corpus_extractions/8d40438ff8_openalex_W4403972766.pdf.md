<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The article contains an unusually clean theory-of-mind operator and it is not decorative — it is the definition. Cliché criterion 1 is "it is used self-consciously. In other words, it is a cliché used qua cliché" [PAGE 5]. Formally, an utterance $u$ is a cliché iff

$$
\mathcal{B}_{s}\Bigl[\mathcal{B}_{h}\bigl[\mathrm{Register}(u)\bigr]\Bigr]\;\wedge\;s(u)>\theta ,
$$

i.e. the speaker represents that the hearer will recognise $u$ as a register token. This is second-order belief, and it explains at a stroke why *sick as a parrot* can be used by someone with no knowledge of football: the belief is about recognisability, not about truth. The evidence is direct — a Financial Times piece uses "the cult of equities is as sick as the proverbial parrot" [PAGE 6], where the hedge "proverbial" is an explicit second-order marker.

The *Brian* vocative [PAGE 6] raises the order again: the speaker addresses an absent archetypal interviewer, so the structure is $\mathcal{B}_s\mathcal{B}_h\mathcal{B}_h(\text{archetype})$ — third order. Chumbawumba's lyric, which strings five clichés to a nominal addressee, is fourth order: the band models the listener modelling the pundit modelling the interviewer. Under this reading, cliché density is a *signalling* strategy, not a lexical deficiency.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under post-match time pressure the speaker regresses to the most automatised frame available; the narrative present perfect is a candidate because it is high-frequency within the register. The mechanism predicts $RR>1$ (O8), i.e. greater present-perfect density the closer the utterance sits to the final whistle.

**Counter-trap.** The analyst who accepts that prediction without a control commits the ecological fallacy the author himself names: "we are therefore observing it through football, rather than because it was born in football" [PAGE 20]. A vernacular universal, sampled through a heavily broadcast register, will look like a register effect. The counter-trap is to mistake *observability* for *causation*.

**The clock.** The clock is both a real constraint and a confound. Real: broadcast windows truncate turn length and raise cognitive load, plausibly favouring automatised forms. Confound: the same truncation changes the *genre*, so any measured $RR>1$ is equally consistent with a genre effect. Decisively, McKie's observation that the form appears "a whole week later" [PAGE 17] is a standing counter-example to a pure recency account — the regression does not decay on the clock's timescale, which means the clock is not the mechanism, or is not the only one.
