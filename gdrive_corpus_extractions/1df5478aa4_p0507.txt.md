This sets up the crisis-hazard structure analyzed in later sections: expertise is stable only while the cue–movement channel remains encapsulated from meta-cognitive interrogation.
</THINK_TRACE>

Formalization of James’s cue–movement channel:

Let the mental cue be a random variable \(C \in \mathcal{C}\), where each element \(c\) is a memory-image of a distinctive peripheral effect (James’s *Effetbild*) [PAGE 497]. Let the movement be \(M \in \mathcal{M}\). James’s requirement of invariability and exclusivity is:

\[
\forall c \in \mathcal{C},\ \exists ! m \in \mathcal{M} : P(M=m \mid C=c) = 1,
\]
\[
\forall m' \neq m : P(M=m' \mid C=c) = 0. \tag{1}
\]

Equivalently, the conditional entropy vanishes:

\[
H(M \mid C) = 0 \text{ bits}. \tag{2}
\]

The mutual information between cue and movement is then bounded by the entropy of the movement repertoire:

\[
I(C;M) = H(M) - H(M \mid C) = H(M) \leq \log_2 |\mathcal{M}| \text{ bits}. \tag{3}
\]

For \(|\mathcal{M}| = 10^6\) movements (James’s “million” [PAGE 497]), the noiseless channel capacity required is \(\log_2(10^6) \approx 19.93\) bits if every movement is equally likely and uniquely addressable. If cues are not unique or overlap, \(H(M \mid C=c) > 0\), producing cross-talk and violating James’s condition that the cue be “incapable of awakening any other movement” [PAGE 497].

James’s claim that the motor discharge is “devoid of sentience” [PAGE 497] is modeled by setting the sentience variable \(S \approx 0\) bits in the expert regime. In the tyro regime, means-monitoring adds a variable \(K\) (consciousness of gun position, throat, feet, etc.), so the effective channel becomes \(C,K \to M\) with \(H(M \mid C,K) < H(M \mid C)\) but at the cost of increased latency \(\tau\) and metabolic load. The security of movement \(\sigma\) is inversely proportional to supernumerary consciousness:

\[
\sigma \propto \frac{1}{H(K)}. \tag{4}
\]

Biomechanical plant model (for cross-layer adjudication):

\[
\dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t)) + d(t), \quad u(t) = \pi_{\phi}(c(t-\tau_s)), \tag{5}
\]

where \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n\) is the state (limb configuration, vocal-fold tension, pole angle), \(u \in \mathbb{R}^p\) is the motor command derived from cue \(c\) after sensorimotor latency \(\tau_s\) (seconds), and \(d(t)\) is an exogenous disturbance (wind, respiratory pathology, mechanical torque) measured in Newtons or radians per second squared. The fixed-connection assumption breaks down when \(\|d(t)\| > \|u_{\max}\|\) or when \(\tau_s > \tau_{\text{critical}}\), the reafference window within which peripheral feedback can correct the movement. Units: bits for information, seconds for latency, meters/radians for state, Newtons for force.

Breakdown regime: James’s deterministic map (Eq. 1) is singular when (i) cue ambiguity yields \(H(M \mid C) > 0\), (ii) cross-talk yields \(P(M=m' \mid C=c) > 0\) for \(m' \neq m\), or (iii) exogenous veto forces violate the plant’s control authority, rendering the mental cue insufficient regardless of its purity [PAGE 497].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogy of James’s page-497 argument [PAGE 497] runs backward into the German psychophysiological tradition and forward into twentieth-century ideomotor and common-coding theory. The footnote explicitly credits Prof. Harle, whose article “Der Apparat des Willens” in *Fichte’s Zeitschrift für Philosophie*, Bd. 88 (dated 1801 in the OCR, likely a typographical displacement for the 1880s) [PAGE 497], coined *Effetbild* for “memory-images of the movement’s distinctive peripheral effects.” This term anticipates the modern construct of the efference copy and the perceptual consequence of action: the brain does not merely send a motor command; it predicts the sensory consequences and uses that prediction as the cue for execution. James’s strong claim—that no other psychic facts intervene between the peripheral image and the movement [PAGE 497]—places him in the line of William Carpenter’s ideomotor principle (1852), whereby the idea of an action spontaneously excites its own performance, and of later German *Willenspsychologie* that treated the will as ideational rather than energetic.

Forward lineage: the “fixed connection” between mental cue and movement [PAGE 497] prefigures Greenwald’s ideomotor theory (1970s) and the common-coding framework of Hommel, Müsseler, Aschersleben, and Prinz (2001), in which perception and action share a representational domain. James’s “principle of parsimony in consciousness” [PAGE 497] is an ancestor of predictive-coding accounts in which the brain minimizes surprise by suppressing reafferent sensations of self-generated movement, leaving only the goal image in conscious awareness.

The multi-week trap is hidden in the transition from tyro to expert. James describes a gradual “dropping” of supernumerary consciousness [PAGE 497], but he does not specify the consolidation schedule. Automaticity of the cue–movement channel (Eq. 1–2) requires repeated pairing over days to weeks so that the peripheral *Effetbild* becomes a reliable, isolated trigger. If training is interrupted before the channel is encapsulated—e.g., a marksman misses two weeks of practice, a singer suffers a respiratory infection, or a balancer trains on an incompatible rope—reversion occurs. The trap is multi-week because the regression is not immediate; it emerges when the performer attempts to resume at prior intensity and finds that the automatic cue no longer reliably suppresses means-monitoring. The system has unlearned the parsimony but has not yet re-established the tyro’s deliberate control, landing in an unstable intermediate regime where \(0 < H(M \mid C) < H(M)\) and latency \(\tau\) oscillates between automatic and deliberative modes. Historical cases of “choking” under pressure after layoffs illustrate this exact singularity: the expert’s cue is intact, but the inhibitory gating that keeps it “incapable of awakening any other movement” [PAGE 497] has decayed.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
James’s account generates a recursive theory of mind around voluntary action because the agent must model whether their own mental cue is operating automatically or under supervision. In the expert regime, the marksman thinks only of the goal [PAGE 497]; there is no higher-order thought about the motor discharge, which is “devoid of sentience” [PAGE 497]. This is a first-order representational state: cue \(c\) (target position) directly selects movement \(m\). In the tyro regime, a second-order layer appears: the agent thinks of the means (gun position, throat, feet) while thinking of the end [PAGE 497]. The mind is now modeling its own motor control.

Crisis habit regression occurs when stress, novelty, or fatigue forces the system to re-engage the second-order layer. The expert regresses to tyro cognition: the balancer attends to feet on the rope instead of the pole tip; the singer monitors breathing instead of sound [PAGE 497]. Formally, the probability of conscious means-intrusion \(P(K > 0)\) rises as a function of crisis duration \(T_c\) and perceived threat \(\theta\):

\[
P(K > 0 \mid T_c, \theta) = \text{sigmoid}(\alpha T_c + \beta \theta - \gamma A), \tag{6}
\]

where \(A\) is accumulated automaticity (weeks of consolidated cue–movement pairing). As \(T_c\) grows, even highly automatic experts begin to monitor means, degrading performance because Eq. 1 is violated by cross-talk from \(K\) into \(M\).

The counter-trap is the meta-cognitive attempt to prevent regression. If the expert explicitly tells themselves “do not think of your means,” they instantiate the very supernumerary consciousness they seek to avoid—an ironic process analogous to Wegner’s theory of ironic mental control. The counter-trap is therefore not the absence of crisis monitoring but the failure to trust the encapsulated first-order channel. The agent’s theory of their own mind becomes recursive: they believe they must supervise the supervision, which reintroduces \(K\) and collapses security \(\sigma\) (Eq. 4).

The clock variable is the decision time budget \(T\) available before movement initiation. If \(T < \tau_{\text{auto}}\) (the latency of the automatic cue–movement channel), the system must use the first-order path; there is no time for means-monitoring. If \(T > \tau_{\text{auto}}\) and stress is high, the second-order path can intrude, especially if automaticity \(A\) is low. The clock thus determines whether the executive delegates to the automatic channel or reclaims control. In crisis, the clock is compressed by external events (a sudden gust, a missed note, a pole oscillation), forcing a binary: execute automatically or regress. The dangerous gray zone is \(T \approx \tau_{\text{auto}}\), where the system oscillates between regimes, producing the hesitations and “double-clutching” that James associates with insecurity [PAGE 497].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
James’s psychological parsimony [PAGE 497] operates at the psychic layer, but it is vetoed by physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers that lie outside the mental cue. A fixed connection between cue and movement [PAGE 497] is necessary but not sufficient for successful action; the plant must be able to execute the command.

Physical/biomechanical veto: In Eq. 5, the motor command \(u = \pi_{\phi}(c)\) is bounded by \(u_{\max}\), the maximum force or torque the musculoskeletal system can produce. If the required corrective command exceeds \(u_{\max}\)—for example, the balancer’s pole tips beyond the ankle strategy’s torque limit, or the singer’s vocal folds cannot achieve the adduction needed for the imagined pitch—the movement fails regardless of cue purity. The veto condition is:

\[
\exists t : \|u_{\text{required}}(t)\| > \|u_{\max}\| \implies \text{failure}, \tag{7}
\]

even when \(H(M \mid C) = 0\) and \(S = 0\). Expertise does not alter material failure points; a perfectly automatic cue cannot overcome muscle fatigue, tendon strain, or vocal-fold edema.

Weather veto: For the marksman, the mental cue is the exact position of the goal [PAGE 497]. Yet crosswind, temperature, and humidity alter bullet trajectory independently of the cue. The exogenous disturbance \(d(t)\) in Eq. 5 includes aerodynamic drift measured in radians of angular deviation or meters of lateral displacement at the target. If \(\|d(t)\|\) exceeds the sight-adjustment bandwidth or the shooter’s ability to hold the weapon stable, the cue–movement channel produces a precise movement to the wrong physical outcome. James’s parsimony in consciousness cannot compensate for unmodeled weather; the marksman must either adjust the cue (aim off-center) or accept error. This is a veto at the environmental layer that is invisible to the psychic layer.

Sensory reafference veto: James requires that the cue be incapable of awakening other movements [PAGE 497], which depends on rapid peripheral feedback confirming that the movement matches the *Effetbild*. If sensory reafference is delayed or degraded—e.g., a singer cannot hear their own voice clearly due to acoustic masking, or a balancer loses proprioceptive clarity on a moving rope—the cue–movement loop runs open-loop. The veto condition is \(\tau_s > \tau_{\text{critical}}\), where \(\tau_{\text{critical}}\) is the maximum allowable sensorimotor latency for stable control. When this bound is crossed, the system must revert to slower, means-based correction, breaking the expert’s automaticity.

Adjudication hierarchy: the psychological layer (cue purity, sentience absence) is necessary but subordinate. A veto at the biomechanical layer (force limits) or weather layer (disturbance magnitude) overrides the psychic layer regardless of how parsimonious consciousness is. James’s principle describes the optimal psychic organization, not a guarantee of motor success [PAGE 497].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR (reinforcement learning via verifiable rewards) requires falsifiable veto scenarios in which the psychological channel is intact but the action fails due to a higher-layer constraint. Three verifiable scenarios and predictions:

Veto Scenario 1 — Marksman under gusty crosswind.  
Setup: The marksman’s mental cue is the exact goal position [PAGE 497]; cue consistency is verified by pre-shot verbal report and eye-tracking (fixation on target center). A controlled crosswind of known vector \(d_w\) is applied at the moment of release.  
Veto mechanism: Aerodynamic drift exceeds the hold stability of the weapon; the biomechanical layer cannot compensate within the shot latency.  
Prediction: Shot-group dispersion increases linearly with \(\|d_w\|\) while subjective cue confidence and fixation stability remain unchanged. The failure is attributable to \(d(t)\) in Eq. 5, not to \(H(M \mid C)\). Verification: correlate impact error with anemometer readings, not with self-report of mental cue clarity.

Veto Scenario 2 — Singer with acute vocal-fold pathology.  
Setup: The singer has an intact auditory *Effetbild* of the perfect sound [PAGE 497], verified by auditory imagery testing and pitch-matching without phonation. A benign but mechanically limiting condition (e.g., unilateral vocal-fold paresis or edema) is present.  
Veto mechanism: The plant cannot achieve the required glottal configuration; \(u_{\text{required}} > u_{\max}\) for the affected muscle group.  
Prediction: Phonatory onset is delayed, breathy, or breaks despite preserved auditory targeting and unchanged respiratory effort. Laryngoscopy confirms glottal insufficiency. Verification: acoustic measures (jitter, shimmer, harmonic-to-noise ratio) degrade while the singer’s report of the mental image remains stable; the veto is biomechanical, not cognitive.

Veto Scenario 3 — Pole balancer under sudden impulsive torque.  
Setup: The balancer attends only to the point of the pole [PAGE 497]; cue fixation is confirmed by head-mounted gaze tracking on the pole tip. A mechanical actuator applies an impulsive torque \(d_\tau\) to the pole base exceeding the ankle strategy’s corrective authority.  
Veto mechanism: The disturbance exceeds \(u_{\max}\) of the lower-limb corrective torque and arrives faster than \(\tau_{\text{critical}}\) for proprioceptive reafference.  
Prediction: Fall latency and recovery success are independent of years of expertise once \(\|d_\tau\| >\) threshold; automaticity does not shift the material failure point. Expertise improves performance only for sub-threshold torques where the cue–movement channel remains viable. Verification: instrumented pole records torque and angle; expert and novice curves converge at the mechanical singularity.

In all three scenarios, the RLVR reward signal is verifiable failure: the psychological cue is intact (low \(H(M \mid C)\), low \(S\)), but the outcome is vetoed by \(d(t)\), \(u_{\max}\), or \(\tau_s\). This validates James’s boundary: parsimony in consciousness optimizes the psychic layer [PAGE 497], but it cannot override physical layer constraints.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
