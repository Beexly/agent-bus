Sixth, the fixed-percentage commission γ applies to the selling price at checkout; if price changes between click and purchase, the commission basis shifts, creating temporal arbitrage and ambiguity about which price snapshot governs [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. These gray zones are not bugs in the prose; they are structural underspecifications that any operationalization must resolve through explicit business rules, durable storage, and conflict-resolution policies before the system can scale beyond a prototype.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators, LaTeX, units, and breakdown regimes.

Let the associate identifier space be $\mathcal{A} = \{0,1\}^{b_A}$, where each $a_i \in \mathcal{A}$ is a unique associate ID assigned upon enrollment [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. Let the product identifier space be $\mathcal{P} = \{0,1\}^{b_P}$, with $p \in \mathcal{P}$ [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. A referral link is an embedding $L_{i,p} \hookrightarrow (a_i,p) \in \mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{P}$ placed inside a catalog document $D_i$ [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. Upon a customer click, the merchant receives referral information $R = (a_i,p)$ transmitted to the merchant's site [PAGE 2].

Define the shopping-cart state for customer $c$ at time $t$ as
\[
S_c(t) = \{(p_k,\sigma_k)\}_{k=1}^{N_c}, \qquad \sigma_k \in \mathcal{A}\cup\{\varnothing\},
\]
where $\sigma_k$ is the referral source for product $p_k$ and $\varnothing$ denotes no recorded referral [PAGE 2]. Cart validity is governed by a time-to-live predicate
\[
\mathcal{V}(S_c,t) = \mathbb{I}\big[t - t_{\text{last}}(S_c) < \tau\big],
\]
with $\tau \approx 7\text{ days} = 604800\text{ s}$ in the preferred implementation and $t_{\text{last}}$ the timestamp of most recent access [PAGE 2]. Units: $\tau$ in seconds; $b_A,b_P$ in bits; $t$ in seconds since epoch.

At checkout time $t_{\text{out}}$, the attribution and credit operator is
\[
\mathcal{C}(S_c,t_{\text{out}}) = \sum_{\substack{(p,\sigma)\in S_c\\ \sigma\neq\varnothing}} \gamma \cdot \text{Price}(p),
\]
where $\gamma \in [0,1]$ is a dimensionless fixed percentage of the merchant's selling price, and credit is applied to associate $\sigma$'s account and paid electronically on a period $T$ such as one calendar quarter ($T \approx 7.884\times10^{6}\text{ s}$) [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. Price is denominated in currency (USD), so $\mathcal{C}$ has units of currency.

The enrollment text scanner is a Boolean operator $f:\text{FormText}\to\{0,1\}^{F}$ such that
\[
\exists w\in\mathcal{V}_{\text{vulgar}}:\ f(w)=1 \implies \text{route to human review},
\]
where $\mathcal{V}_{\text{vulgar}}$ is the pre-specified vocabulary of rejected words and phrases [PAGE 1].

Singularity and breakdown regimes:
(1) TTL expiry: if $t - t_{\text{last}} \ge \tau$, then $\mathcal{V}=0$, $S_c$ is discarded, and all $\sigma_k$ are lost; attribution probability for referred items drops to zero [PAGE 2].
(2) Referral collision: if $\exists t_1<t_2\le t_{\text{out}}$ with clicks from $a_i\neq a_j$ for identical $p$, the write policy for $\sigma_p$ is undefined in the specification, yielding non-deterministic attribution [PAGE 2].
(3) Ledger reversal: returns or cancellations after checkout are not addressed, so $\mathcal{C}$ has no inverse defined; the commission ledger becomes singular under post-sale order modification [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2].
(4) Scanner saturation: if form submission rate $\Phi$ exceeds human-review capacity $H_{\max}$, enrollment latency $L\to\infty$ and the automated enrollment promise breaks down [PAGE 1].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week traps.

The patent's genealogy reaches backward into pre-digital commerce. The "associate" is a formalization of the independent distributor or commissioned agent familiar from direct-selling organizations (e.g., party-plan and catalog sales) in which a representative earns a percentage on sales generated through personal recommendation. Amazon.com's assignee status situates the invention at the transition from physical retail networks to the World Wide Web, where the merchant lacked resources to review millions of titles and needed low-cost customer acquisition [PAGE 1]. Scientifically, the system belongs to the lineage of marketing attribution and information-processing for electronic commerce: unique IDs as database pointers, referral links as labeled hyperedges in a bipartite associate-product graph, and the shopping cart as a persistent, item-level attribution ledger. This precedes and anticipates modern multi-touch attribution (MTA), cookie-based tracking, and affiliate-network platforms, replacing broadcast advertising expense with pay-for-performance contingent on a verified purchase event [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2].

Multi-week traps arise directly from the persistence architecture. Because each shopping cart persists for approximately one week after the most recent access [PAGE 2], a referral link clicked in week one can generate a commissionable sale in week two if the customer returns within the window. This long-tail attribution creates a multi-week trap for the merchant: marketing influence is decoupled from the checkout event by up to $\tau$, complicating return-on-investment measurement and inflating the apparent efficacy of associate content whose causal role decays over time. A second trap is enrollment latency: the text-scanning and human-review pipeline introduces a variable delay between application and ID assignment [PAGE 1], during which potential associates are inactive and network effects are suppressed. A third trap is the quarterly payment cadence $T$ [PAGE 1], which creates a cash-flow lag for associates and delays feedback; associates cannot verify attribution in real time, so they optimize for click volume rather than verified purchase quality, inviting link stuffing and low-intent traffic. A fourth trap is stale-cart attribution: if price or availability changes between the referral click and checkout within the $\tau$ window, the commission basis $\gamma\cdot\text{Price}(p)$ may no longer reflect the economics at the time of recommendation, producing disputes that surface weeks after the initial touch [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock alignment.

Model the recursive reasoning of four actors: merchant, associate, customer, and adversarial referrer. Under crisis conditions—spiking fraud, chargebacks, or cart-collision disputes—the merchant exhibits crisis habit regression: shortening $\tau$, suspending auto-enrollment in favor of manual vetting, reducing $\gamma$, or imposing stricter text-scanner thresholds. Associates, observing reduced commission windows and higher rejection rates, regress to link-spamming, incentivized clicks, and cloaked referrals to preserve revenue. Customers, sensing aggressive attribution or privacy-invasive tracking, regress to cart abandonment, incognito browsing, and cookie clearance, which silently reset $\sigma_k$ to $\varnothing$ and veto attribution [PAGE 2].

The merchant's counter-trap is the automated enrollment scanner plus persistent per-product source tracking: it attempts to admit only legitimate associates while preserving referral provenance across sessions [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. The second-order trap is that the counter-trap becomes its own bottleneck: human review of flagged applications limits associate throughput [PAGE 1], and long cart persistence credits associates for purchases they did not directly influence (e.g., item added via referral, bought after independent research), over-attributing marketing value.

The system runs on multiple clocks with potential misalignment: (i) the session clock $\tau \approx 1$ week [PAGE 2]; (ii) the enrollment clock, governed by scanner latency and human-review queue depth [PAGE 1]; (iii) the payment clock $T \approx 1$ quarter [PAGE 1]; and (iv) the catalog/price clock, during which selling prices may change [PAGE 1]. When these clocks drift, recursive instability results: a customer referred on day 1 who checks out on day 8 sees attribution vetoed by the session clock even though the merchant's quarterly payment clock has not yet settled the associate's account [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. An associate who generates a click on day 1 receives no verified signal until up to 90 days later under the payment clock, sustaining trust through delay but disabling real-time counterfactual learning. Crisis habit regression thus interacts with the clock: if the merchant shortens $\tau$ in response to fraud, associates lose incentive to produce durable long-form content, regressing to shallow links whose conversion decays before checkout; if the merchant lengthens $\tau$, the merchant bears stale-attribution liability across the quarterly settlement boundary [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto conditions.

A valid attribution requires an unbroken causal chain: click $\to$ referral information $R=(a_i,p)$ received [PAGE 2] $\to$ cart inscription $\sigma_p=a_i$ [PAGE 2] $\to$ cart persistence $\mathcal{V}=1$ [PAGE 2] $\to$ checkout credit $\mathcal{C}>0$ to associate $a_i$ [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. A veto at any layer severs this chain.

Physical layer. Network partition or merchant server downtime between the click and cart write prevents $R$ from being recorded, so $\sigma_p=\varnothing$ and attribution is vetoed [PAGE 2]. If the shopping cart is held in volatile storage and a power outage occurs before durable commit, the one-week persistence $\tau$ is irrelevant because $S_c$ is lost [PAGE 2]. Client-side disabling of cookies, JavaScript, or referral-header transmission similarly prevents the merchant site from associating the click with the session cart [PAGE 2].

Biomechanical layer. Human motor behavior introduces false attribution: a misclick on a referral link followed by immediate exit can still inscribe $\sigma_p=a_i$ if the item is later added, crediting an associate for no purchase intent [PAGE 2]. Conversely, a customer who reads an associate's review for an extended period without clicking generates no $R$ at all; the system records zero attribution despite demonstrated influence [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. Reading latency itself does not break the clock because $t_{\text{last}}$ resets on access, but motor-error clicks produce spurious entries in $S_c$ [PAGE 2].

Weather and environmental layer. Severe weather disrupting last-mile ISP infrastructure increases packet loss and latency; referral clicks or checkout submissions may time out, probabilistically vetoing attribution even when the user intent is clear [PAGE 2]. Regional power outages at the merchant's data center invalidate cart persistence regardless of $\tau$ unless geographically replicated durable storage exists, which the specification does not describe [PAGE 2].

Adjudication rule. A veto is sustained if any layer breaks the chain and no redundant attribution path (e.g., offline coupon matching, post-hoc email verification) is specified in the patent. Because the disclosure contains no fallback for physical-layer outage, no biomechanical intent disambiguation, and no weather-hardened replication, environmental and biomechanical failures are unmitigated singularities that reduce observed conversion and commission accuracy below the theoretical ideal [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable condition and prediction.

Scenario 1: Cart TTL expiry veto. Counterfactual: a customer clicks associate $a_i$'s referral link for product $p$ at $t_0$, adding $p$ to the cart, and returns to check out at $t_{\text{out}} = t_0 + \tau + \Delta t$ with $\Delta t>0$. Verifiable condition: server logs show $t_{\text{out}} - t_{\text{last\_access}} > \tau$, where $\tau\approx 604800\text{ s}$ [PAGE 2]. Veto: cart validity $\mathcal{V}=0$, $S_c$ discarded, $\sigma_p$ reset to $\varnothing$; $\mathcal{C}$ assigns zero to $a_i$ [PAGE 1]-[PAGE 2]. Prediction: despite a verifiable click and a completed purchase of $p$, the commission ledger records zero credit for $a_i$ for that session. This is detectable by a sharp drop in attributed revenue at the $\tau$ boundary in click-to-checkout timestamp data.

Scenario 2: Enrollment scanner false-negative veto. Counterfactual: an applicant's form contains a prohibited vulgarity that the text scanner misses, so auto-enrollment proceeds and a unique associate ID $a_i$ is assigned [PAGE 1]. Verifiable condition: a post-enrollment audit finds the prohibited term in the associate's public catalog or original form text, violating the online business agreement [PAGE 1]. Veto: associate account terminated; all pending commissions within the open quarterly payment period $T$ are withheld or clawed back [PAGE 1]. Prediction: future referrals from $a_i$ yield $\sigma_p=\varnothing$ and zero credit; historical commissions accrued before detection but not yet paid under the quarterly cycle are suspended. Verifiable via account-status flag and commission-ledger entries showing a transition from positive to zero payout for $a_i$ [PAGE 1].

Scenario 3: Duplicate-referral collision veto. Counterfactual: within one session, a customer clicks $a_i$'s link for product $p$ at $t_1$ and $a_j$'s link for the same $p$ at $t_2>t_1$ before checkout. Verifiable condition: click logs show two distinct referral events for identical $p$ in one customer session, but the cart record holds only one $\sigma_p$ [PAGE 2]. Veto: because the specification does not define first-write vs. last-write resolution [PAGE 2], the system either attributes to one associate and vetoes the other (loss of $\gamma\cdot\text{Price}(p)$ for the loser) or flags the session for manual review, withholding commission until resolved. Prediction: exactly one of $\{a_i,a_j\}$ receives credit; the losing associate's session conversion rate is zero despite a verifiable click. Detectable by mismatch between referral-click logs (two clicks) and the commission ledger (one payment), exposing the attribution singularity at the collision point [PAGE 2].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
