Three clocks interact: the developmental clock τ (critical period for imprinting, wariness, or running acclimation), the generational clock g (epigenetic decay λ of acquired marks), and the crisis clock t_c (regression rate γ). A veto is triggered whenever g > 1/λ (acquired mark erased) or τ > τ_crit (plasticity window closed), because James's transgenerational habit hypothesis then lacks a mechanistic carrier.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof. Define the veto operator 𝒱: a behavioral claim C is rejected if there exists a physical constraint φ such that φ(C) = 0 (false).

Layer 1 — Atmospheric physics (greyhounds, rarefied air) [PAGE 691]. On the high plateau of Mexico, parents "could not at first run well, on account of rarefied air." The physical constraint is pO₂ < pO₂,crit and air density ρ below the operational band for sustained sprinting. Because v_max scales with (pO₂ ⋅ ṀO₂,max)^(1/2), no behavioral "instinct" can compensate below the metabolic floor. The whelps' adjustment can only be vetoed as inherited instinct unless developmental changes in hematocrit, capillary density, or lung morphometry are measured; otherwise the claim is vetoed by the physics layer.

Layer 2 — Biomechanics (sporting dogs) [PAGE 691]. Pointing and retrieving are "partly ... due to training, but which in well-bred stock are all but innate." The biomechanical veto states: training cannot generate pointing behavior in a conformation q ∉ Q_viable (e.g., absent shoulder angulation, olfactory posture, or stifle structure). Therefore C = "training alone produces pointing" is false whenever q lies outside the viable morphological manifold. James's caveat that it is "bad for a litter ... if its sire or dam have not been trained in the field" conflates genetic conformation with parental training history; the veto separates them by requiring conformation measurement independent of training exposure.

Layer 3 — Weather and ecology (woodcock and large-versus-small birds) [PAGE 691]. Knight's sixty-year woodcock shyness trend and Darwin's large-bird shyness are confounded by ΔE_weather, Δhabitat, and Δhunting pressure. The weather veto rejects instinct-evolution attribution unless Δshyness remains significant after covarying for annual temperature, precipitation, and hunter effort. Breakdown: the veto fails when historical records lack these covariates (σ_φ > effect size), which is precisely the condition of James's anecdotal list.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions.

Scenario 1 — Common-garden greyhound altitude test. Counterfactual: whelps of high-plateau parents are raised at sea level and later challenged at altitude, compared with lowland controls. Prediction under James's inherited-acclimation view: sea-level-raised whelps should outperform lowland controls at altitude due to inherited adjustment. RLVR veto: reject the inherited-instinct claim if v(whelp | sea-level raised, altitude) ≈ v(control, altitude) within ±5% and p > 0.10. Expected outcome if veto holds: performance difference is explained by developmental acclimation (τ-layer), not transgenerational inheritance (g-layer).

Scenario 2 — Cross-fostered wild-duck imprinting test. Counterfactual: wild-duck eggs are fostered to tame ducks and to wild ducks in identical enclosures; all other conditions held constant. James on [PAGE 691] says wild ducks "latched out by tame ones, fly off." Prediction under species-instinct dominance: tame-fostered ducklings show significantly higher flight-off propensity within 48 hours than wild-fostered controls. RLVR veto: reject the instinct hypothesis if P(flight off | tame foster) < 0.05 or if the difference between foster conditions is not statistically significant (p > 0.10). Expected outcome if veto holds: imprinting or maternal effects dominate over any inherited "tameness" instinct.

Scenario 3 — Terrier begging acquisition-rate test. Counterfactual: litters from beg-trained parents and from untrained parents are exposed to an identical operant contingency (food delivered contingent on the begging posture), with experimenters blind to lineage. James cites the trained-parent terrier pup on [PAGE 691] as evidence for inherited begging. Prediction under inherited-begging view: trained-lineage pups reach criterion in fewer trials. RLVR veto: reject inherited begging if mean trials-to-criterion for trained lineage minus untrained lineage lies within [−2, +2] trials and the two-sample test yields p > 0.10. Expected outcome if veto holds: "begging" is a general operant response, not a transgenerationally transmitted habit.
