Historical and scientific lineage. AGC-Bench descends directly from Spearman’s \(g\) and the Cattell–Horn–Carroll (CHC) framework [McGrew, 2005], in which intelligence comprises general ability \(g\), fluid reasoning \(G_f\), and crystallized knowledge \(G_c\) [PAGE 3–4]. The paper adopts the human-creativity position that performance requires both novelty and appropriateness [Amabile, 1996; Runco & Jaeger, 2012] and partitions creativity into the six domains used in major self-report inventories: Carson et al. (2005) CAQ, Kaufman (2012) K-DOCS, Diedrich et al. (2018) ICAA [PAGE 3]. Process-level roots run through Guilfordian divergent thinking—Alternate Uses Task, Torrance Tests, Divergent Association Task—reported in Stevenson et al. [2022], Guzik et al. [2023], Hubert et al. [2024], Bellemare-Pepin et al. [2026] [PAGE 3].

The psychometric instrument layer draws on Myszkowski and Storme’s Judge Response Theory (JRT) [2019], a Bayesian graded-response calibration of judge leniency/severity, applied here to a three-frontier-vendor panel under a planned-missing 2-of-3 design [PAGE 2, PAGE 18]. The curation layer follows PRISMA 2020 [Page et al., 2021] and the HELM evaluation paradigm [Liang et al., 2023] [PAGE 2]. The pure-\(G_f\) discriminant probe is the Lewis–Mitchell counterfactual letter-string analogy task [2025], whose permuted alphabets block surface-pattern shortcuts, paralleling Chollet’s Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus [2019] [PAGE 3–4].

Closest prior consolidation is CreativityPrism [Hou et al., 2025]: nine tasks across divergent thinking, creative writing, and logical reasoning, seventeen models, one LLM judge, reporting within-domain > across-domain correlation [PAGE 3]. AGC-Bench extends on scale (67 text-only benchmarks vs nine; 83 frontier models vs seventeen), scoring (3-judge planned-missing Bayesian rater calibration vs single judge), and psychometric grounding (formal factor analysis with parallel analysis on an 83 × 6 matrix vs per-pair correlations on 17 × 9) [PAGE 3]. Six of CreativityPrism’s nine tasks appear among AGC-Bench’s 67 benchmarks [PAGE 3].

Multi-week traps. The literature review cutoff is December 2025, yet the model cohort spans releases through 2026 (claude-opus-4.7, gpt-5.4/5.5, glm-5, gemma-4), so the leaderboard has a short half-life and post-cutoff benchmarks are absent from the catalog [PAGE 1–2, PAGE 15]. Lu et al. [2026] catalog confounds—prompt sensitivity, model inconsistency, data contamination—that can invalidate single-benchmark scores [PAGE 3]. Judge severity (\(\beta\)) is estimated over a fixed evaluation window; if a vendor updates a judge mid-deployment, \(\theta\) estimates drift and rank preservation (\(\rho = +0.95\)) may not replicate. The composite has no theoretical ceiling (unlike Chatbot Arena ELO) because it is scored against frontier peers, so longitudinal comparability requires re-calibration rather than direct comparison [PAGE 9].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock. Under evaluation pressure, both models and judges exhibit habit regression: models fall back on high-probability stylistic registers, and LLM judges develop stable leniency/severity habits captured by JRT severity \(\beta\) [PAGE 19]. JRT assumes these habits are stationary across the scoring window; if judge severity drifts, per-unit ability \(\theta_p\) is biased even when rank order is preserved.

Counter-trap. The recursive loop is explicit: AGC-Judge is fine-tuned exclusively on LLM-produced responses paired with frontier-LLM ratings (48,299 JRT-corrected rows; no human responses or human ratings in training) [PAGE 19, PAGE 24]. When it first encounters human-produced creative responses on AGC-Human and the Orwig corpus, a source-preference effect emerges. Appendix L’s counterfactual style-flip experiment (50 cells; Grok-4.1-fast paraphrases preserving ideas verbatim while swapping register—humans rewritten in polished LLM style, LLMs rewritten in casual human style) yields near-zero mean score deltas (\(\Delta_{\text{polish}} = +0.40\), \(d = 0.29\); \(\Delta_{\text{humanize}} = +0.52\), \(d = 0.15\) on the 10–50 scale) [PAGE 24]. This indicates the cross-group bias operates at the content/idea level, not purely at stylistic register, though the 50-cell probe cannot fully rule out register effects. The fairness-aware prompt (source disclosure, focus on underlying idea) reduces the LLM–human gap from +11.4 to +7.4 points on the 10–50 scale (~35% reduction) while preserving cell-level rank ordering (\(r = +0.91\)) and improving convergent validity with the formal AGC-Human composite (\(\rho = +0.53 \to +0.60\)) [PAGE 24]. Bias is attenuated, not eliminated, consistent with Liu et al. [2025] and Jang & Silavong [2025].

Clock. The evaluation has a fixed temporal boundary: models released 2024–2026, review cutoff December 2025, judge versions pinned to gemini-3-flash-preview, grok-4.1-fast, gpt-4.1-mini [PAGE 6, PAGE 18]. Measurement-clock sufficiency is demonstrated by rank-stability bootstrapping: Spearman \(\rho = 0.99\) between \(k=10\) and \(k=50\) items, Top-10 Jaccard = 1.0 by \(k=50\) [PAGE 6]. Yet the construct-sensitivity clock is shorter: within-model interventions show the benchmark responds four times more strongly to a creativity-targeted prompt (\(d_z = +1.40\)) than to a reasoning-toggle (\(d_z = +0.34\)) [PAGE 8], so the instrument’s validity window closes if future models are optimized to game the “be creative” instruction without underlying generative flexibility.

This is a biomechanical veto: non-interpretable or garbled outputs are excluded before psychometric aggregation.

Weather/environmental layer (external drift). Environmental vetoes include data contamination and prompt sensitivity cataloged by Lu et al. [2026] [PAGE 3]; English-dominance and the absence of audio/video modalities [PAGE 9]; and the unassessed construct of transformational creativity (reshaping conceptual space rather than recombining within it) [Schapiro et al., 2025, cited P9]. Cross-source bias on the Orwig corpus shows AGC-Judge is significantly more aligned with the LLM-judge gold (\(\rho = +0.80\)) than the human gold (\(\rho = +0.65\); Williams \(t = -9.42\), \(p < 0.0001\)) [PAGE 20], so any cross-group claim using the standard prompt is environmentally vetoed unless the fairness-aware prompt is applied.

Scoring-class invariance veto. If the general creativity factor C were an artifact of one scoring family, splitting by canonical metric would destroy unidimensionality. It does not: formula-based subset eigenvalue 3.68 (\(\alpha = 0.91\), 73.6% variance); LLM-judge subset eigenvalue 4.28 (\(\alpha = 0.96\), 85.5% variance) [PAGE 22]. The veto condition—“C exists only within one scoring class”—is falsified.
