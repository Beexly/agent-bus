Preceding it, the British associationists and Herbert Spencer treated pleasure and pain as tags attached to ideas by contiguity and frequency; James reframes the tag as contingent on the *discharge dynamics* of the impulse itself, a move that anticipates Freud’s pleasure principle and, more critically, *Beyond the Pleasure Principle* (1920), where repetition compulsion is understood as an attempt to master an interrupted discharge by re-enacting it. The Skinnerian operant tradition (1938) externalizes this into reinforcement contingencies, but strips away James’s introspective distinction between pursued and incidental pleasure, collapsing both into “reinforcing consequence.” The Rescorla–Wagner model (1972) formalizes prediction error, yet still treats relief as a scalar outcome value rather than a derivative of discharge-continuity. Modern active inference and the free-energy principle (Friston, 2010s) recover James’s insight: relief is the reduction of interoceptive surprise when a constraint on sampled sensory flow is removed, which maps directly onto the asthma/dyspnœa example at [PAGE 556].

A delayed credit-assignment trap compounds this: relief arrives minutes after discharge, while the pursued pleasure (e.g., long-term health from escaping danger) arrives weeks later. Temporal discounting \(\gamma^{t}\) with \(\gamma<1\) overweights immediate relief, so the agent may preferentially select actions whose discharge structure is easily completed, even when the pursued pleasure is smaller. James’s “pursued pleasure” versus “incidental pleasure” fork thus predicts a systematic preference for low-barrier discharge over high-value delayed ends during the consolidation window—a trap that persists across multiple weeks of habit formation.
