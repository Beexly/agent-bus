Thus \(S\) abandons the effortful analytic part-whole sequence and defaults to an egocentric, body-centered spatial code. If \(O\) still expects \(S\) to use an extrinsic or allocentric frame, \(O\)’s model of \(S\) becomes misaligned: \(O\) predicts superposition-based precision, but \(S\) delivers body-relative categorical judgments.

Counter-trap: \(O\) can attempt to force \(S\) back into analytic processing by imposing congruity (making \(\Gamma(A,B)=1\)) and demanding superposition, thereby raising \(D(A,B,\alpha)\) [PAGE 151]. The trap is that this counter-trap is effortful and collapses exactly when resources are lowest—during crisis—so \(O\) cannot rely on it under load.

Clock: the temporal variable is the attentional dwell time required to notice “first a part, and then the whole” [PAGE 151]. Let \(t_{\text{part}}\) and \(t_{\text{whole}}\) be the fixation/saccadic dwell times on the part and the whole; superposition additionally requires a motor comparison interval \(t_{\text{super}}\). The total clock is \(t_{\text{total}} = t_{\text{part}} + t_{\text{whole}} + t_{\text{super}}\). If the task deadline \(T_{\text{deadline}} < t_{\text{total}}\), the exact-measurement condition is vetoed and \(S\) regresses to the body-relative habit [PAGE 151]. Thus the recursion closes: \(O\) models \(S\)’s clock, \(S\) models \(O\)’s deadline pressure, and under crisis both collapse to the body-centered default James defends against Kant [PAGE 151].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The exact sensation of more via superposition depends on three independent layers. If any layer fails, the measurement condition James calls “indispensable” [PAGE 151] is vetoed.

Physical layer (optics and photometry): James compares “a feebler light to a stronger” and “a paler blue to a richer” [PAGE 151]. The physical precondition \(P_{\text{phys}}\) requires that spatial endpoints and intensity gradients be resolvable. Veto if luminance contrast falls below contrast threshold, fog or scatter corrupts line endpoints, or occlusion hides one figure so that \(\mathcal{P}(A,B)=0\). Units: luminance in \(\text{cd/m}^2\), contrast dimensionless (Michelson), visibility in meters.

Biomechanical layer (body schema and peripersonal space): James’s rebuttal to Kant makes the body the sufficient frame [PAGE 151]. The precondition \(P_{\text{bio}}\) requires an intact body schema, available effectors, and congruence between peripersonal reach space and the figures being compared. Veto if a limb is immobilized, vestibular mismatch distorts egocentric coordinates, or peripersonal/extrapersonal boundaries prevent the motor act of superposition. Units: joint angles in radians, reach distance in meters, efference-copy prediction error dimensionless.

Weather/environmental layer: “a feebler light to a stronger” [PAGE 151] implies environmental luminance matters. The precondition \(P_{\text{env}}\) requires ambient illumination and atmospheric transparency sufficient to see spots, lines, and colors. Veto if precipitation, fog, or low ambient light reduces the retinal image below threshold, or if temperature-induced sensorimotor slowing lengthens \(t_{\text{super}}\) beyond the clock deadline. Units: illuminance in lux, precipitation rate in \(\text{mm/h}\), ambient temperature in K or °C.

Adjudication rule: the composite veto is

\[
V = \neg\bigl(P_{\text{phys}} \land P_{\text{bio}} \land P_{\text{env}}\bigr).
\]

If \(V = 1\), exact superposition is impossible, \(D\) falls below \(\theta_D\), and the “sensation of more” may persist only as an inexact, non-measurable feeling [PAGE 151]. Proof: James’s superposition condition [PAGE 151] presupposes an intact body (biomechanical), clear optics (physical), and adequate environmental illumination (weather); therefore any single layer failure logically blocks the exact measurement of the more, independent of the subject’s verbal report.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios, each tied to [PAGE 151], with explicit predictions:

Scenario 1 — Non-congruous superposition veto. Present a small square and a large circle so that no rigid superposition is possible (\(\Gamma=0\), \(S=0\)), while matching their subjective “more” magnitude. Prediction: subjects report a sensation of more but cannot perform exact measurement; two-alternative forced-choice thresholds increase by more than \(2\times\) relative to congruous controls; the RLVR reward signal is invalidated because no verifiable overlap measure \(\mathcal{P}(A,B)\) exists. Verifiable via staircase psychophysics and overlap-error recording [PAGE 151].

Scenario 2 — Body-schema disruption veto. Induce temporary body-schema disruption (e.g., limb anesthesia, tendon vibration, or prism adaptation) and test right/left figure contrasts. Prediction: James’s claim that “Relation to our own body is enough” [PAGE 151] fails; right/left judgments collapse to chance or shift to extrinsic environmental cues; pointing and line-bisection errors show systematic egocentric shifts. Verifiable via perimetry and visuospatial pointing tasks with pre/post disruption baselines [PAGE 151].

Scenario 3 — Cross-modal invariance veto. Equate physical Weber ratios (\(\Delta I/I\)) across line length, light intensity, and blue saturation, but vary congruity \(\Gamma\). Prediction: if James’s trans-modal identity axiom holds [PAGE 151], incongruent and congruent pairs yield matched magnitude estimates; if it fails, incongruent pairs produce modality-specific sensations and mismatch-negativity (MMN) deviations. Verifiable via magnitude estimation and EEG/MMN, with the RLVR episode terminated when cross-modal variance exceeds a pre-registered bound [PAGE 151].
