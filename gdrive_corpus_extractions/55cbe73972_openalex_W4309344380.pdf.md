<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**(A) Two-factor mean-reverting patent value** [PAGE 18–19]. Units: $V$ in USD; $\alpha_t,\sigma_1,\eta$ in $\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$; $\sigma$'s dimensionless-per-$\sqrt{\mathrm{yr}}$.
$$\frac{dV_t}{V_t}=\alpha_t\,dt+\sigma_1\,dz_1,\qquad \frac{d\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}=\eta(\bar\alpha-\alpha_t)\,dt+\sigma_2\,dz_2.$$
Itô-discrete form (as printed, OCR-corrected):
$$V_t=V_{t-1}\exp\!\Big[(\alpha_{t-1}-\tfrac12\sigma^2)\Delta t+\sigma_1\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta t}\Big],$$
$$\alpha_t=\alpha_{t-1}e^{-\eta\Delta t}+(1-e^{-\eta\Delta t})\bar\alpha+\tfrac{r-\rho}{\eta}+\sigma_2\sqrt{\tfrac{1-e^{-2\eta\Delta t}}{2\eta}}\,\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta t}.$$
Breakdown: as $\eta\to0$ the OU drift degenerates to arithmetic Brownian motion (mean reversion vanishes); the $(r-\rho)/\eta$ term diverges unless $r=\rho$. Parameter set [PAGE 20]: $V_0=\$1\mathrm{M}$, $\alpha=0.10\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$, $\sigma=0.08\,\mathrm{yr^{-1/2}}$, $\eta=0.02\,\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$, $\bar\mu=\$50\mathrm{M}$, $T=20\,\mathrm{yr}$.

**(B) Dividend-adjusted real option** [PAGE 21].
$$d_1=\frac{\ln(V/K)+(\,r-\delta_f+\tfrac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T},\quad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt T,\quad C=V e^{-\delta_f T}N(d_1)-K e^{-rT}N(d_2).$$
With $V=3422,\ K=2875,\ r=0.0675,\ \delta_f=0.0579,\ \sigma^2=0.224,\ T=17$: $\sigma\sqrt T=1.951$, $N(d_1)=0.872$, $N(d_2)=0.2076$, $C\approx\$907\mathrm{M}$ versus $\mathrm{NPV}=3422-2875=\$547\mathrm{M}$. **Source-integrity caveat:** printed denominator "9.4377" is OCR corruption; the chapter is retracted [PAGE 132].

**(C) Piecewise-deterministic differential game** [PAGE 29–33]. Sales/profit per mode $m\in\{1,2,3\}$:
$$f_I^{1}=a-b_Ip_I+\theta A_I,\quad \pi_I(p_I,A_I,1)=(p_I-c_I)f_{I,1}-lA_I^2;$$
$$f_G^{2}=a-b_Gp_G+\theta A_I,\quad \pi_G=(p_G-c_G-R)f_G^{2},\quad \pi_I(\cdot,2)=(p_I-c_I)f_{I,2}-lA_I^2+Rf_G^{2}.$$
Endogenous hazards (units $\mathrm{yr^{-1}}$):
$$\lambda_{1,2}=\frac{\delta_{1,2}}{x_I},\qquad \lambda_{1,3}=\delta_{1,3}\,x_I,\qquad \lambda_{2,3}=\delta_{2,3}.$$
Feedback HJB (Eq. 14):
$$rV_i(x,m)=\max_{p_i}\Big\{\pi_i(p_i,m)+\partial_{x_I}V_I f_I+\partial_{x_G}V_G f_G+\!\!\sum_{n\ne m}\lambda_{n,m}\big(V_i(x,n)-V_i(x,m)\big)\Big\}.$$
Semi-Lagrangian jump probability $P^h_{x,i,j}=1-e^{-h\lambda_{i,j}(x,q)}$, discount $\beta=e^{-\omega h}$, operator (Eq. 20) $N_iV^h(x)=\max_q\{h\pi_i+\beta[P^h_{x,i}V^h(x+hG)+\sum_{j\ne i}P^h_{x,i,j}V^h(x,j)]\}$.
Breakdown: $\lambda_{1,2}=\delta_{1,2}/x_I\to\infty$ as $x_I\to0^+$ (hazard singularity); HJB admits no closed form [PAGE 33].

**(D) First-price structural inversion (GPV)** [PAGE 69–71]. Risk-neutral value recovery and its bid-space form:
$$v=b+\frac{F(\phi(b))}{f(\phi(b))\phi'(b)(N-1)}=b+\frac{G(b)}{(N-1)\,g(b)},\qquad v_i=\xi(b_i,G,I)=b_i+\frac{1}{I-1}\frac{G(b_i)}{g(b_i)}.$$
Quantile form $Q_v(\alpha)=Q_b(\alpha)+\frac{1}{I-1}\frac{\alpha}{g(Q_b(\alpha))}$. Kernel estimates $\tilde g(b)=\frac{1}{ILh_g}\sum\sum K_g(\frac{b-B_{pl}}{h_g})$.
Breakdown: $g(b)\to0$ (thin bid support) makes $\hat v$ explode; small $N$ destabilizes the estimate [PAGE 62].

**(E) Bellman / A3C for MEIO** [PAGE 80–89].
$$V(s)=\mathbb E_\pi\Big[\sum_{k=0}^\infty\gamma^kR_{t+k+1}\mid S_t=s\Big],\quad V(s)=\sum_a\pi(a|s)\sum_{s',r}p(s',r|s,a)[r+\gamma v_\pi(s')].$$
TD target/error: $G_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma G_{t+1}$; update $V(S_t)\leftarrow V(S_t)+\alpha[R_{t+1}+\gamma V(S_{t+1})-V(S_t)]$. REINFORCE $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\alpha G_t\frac{\nabla_\theta\pi(A_t|S_t,\theta_t)}{\pi(A_t|S_t,\theta_t)}$; reparam $g_\theta=\mu_\theta+\sigma_\theta\epsilon$; advantage $A(s,a)=Q(s,a)-V(s)$. Cost $c_{\text{total}}=\sum_i(c_{i,\text{shortage}}+c_{i,\text{reordering}}+c_{i,\text{holding}})$. Specification [PAGE 88]: 12-dim state, 13-dim action, $\gamma$ (unspecified in OCR), Adam, lr $=10^{-4}$, converges below $10\mathrm{M\,CHF}$ but "variance … remains on a high level" [PAGE 90].

**(F) SDE integrator + PK/PD** [PAGE 101–105].
$$dV_t=\alpha(t,V_t)\,dt+\sigma(t,V_t)\,dW,\qquad V_{n+1}=V_n+\alpha(t_n,V_n)h+\sigma(t_n,V_n)\Delta W_n,$$
with $\Delta W_n\sim\mathcal N(0,h)$; strong order $p=1/2$, weak order $q=1$. $\vartheta$-family blends explicit/implicit. Stochastic Gompertz PD: $dN_t=(r-b\log N_t-kC_t)N_t\,dt+\gamma N_t\,dW$, $C_t=\frac{Dk_a}{V(k_a-k_e)}(e^{-k_et}-e^{-k_at})$.
Breakdown: Pardoux–Peng uniqueness [PAGE 104] needs $|\alpha(t,x)-\alpha(t,y)|+|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)|\le C|x-y|$ and linear growth; the drift $bN\log N$ is **not** globally Lipschitz → EM strong divergence possible.

**(G) Hawkes–Cox intensity and moments** [PAGE 117–122].
$$\lambda_t=a+(\lambda_0-a)e^{-\delta t}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_t}Y_ie^{-\delta(t-T_i)}+\sum_{i=1}^{J_t}X_ie^{-\delta(t-S_i)},\quad k:=\delta-\mu_1^G.$$
Generator $\mathcal A f=-\delta(\lambda-a)\partial_\lambda f+\lambda\!\int\![f(\lambda+y,n{+}1)-f]\,dG+\rho\!\int\![f(\lambda+c,n)-f]\,dH.$
Mean (Prop. 1): $\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb E[N_{t+\tau}-N_t\mid\lambda_0]=\frac{a\delta}{\delta-\mu_1^G}\tau=:h_1^\tau$; variance $h_2^\tau$ (Prop. 2) scales with $\frac{\theta}{k}+\frac{2}{k^2}(\cdots)+\frac{\rho\mu_2^H}{2k}$; covariance (Prop. 3) $\propto\big(\frac{1-e^{-k\tau}}{k}\big)^2e^{-k\tilde\tau}$.
Breakdown: stationarity **fails** as $\mu_1^G\to\delta^-$ ($k\to0^+$), so $h_1^\tau,h_2^\tau\to\infty$ (critical branching).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Scientific lineage of the operators. The Wiener process (named for Norbert Wiener, per [PAGE 98]) formalizes Robert Brown's 1827 pollen motion; Kiyosi Itô gave the stochastic integral a rigorous meaning ("the notion of Ito's integral gives a definition for the integral $\int_a^b f\,dW$" [PAGE 99]), and Itô's lemma yields the closed GBM solution $V_t=V_0e^{(\alpha-\sigma^2/2)t+\sigma W_t}$ [PAGE 100]. The mean-reversion operator in (A) is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (L. S. Ornstein & G. E. Uhlenbeck, 1930), the same physics used for velocity of a Brownian particle, transplanted here to drift dynamics. The valuation operator (B) descends from Black–Scholes–Merton (1973) with a continuous dividend yield; the "patent-as-option" reading follows Myers (1977 real options), Dixit–Pindyck (1994, *Investment under Uncertainty*), Schwartz (2004, "Patents and R&D as real options"), Kellogg–Charnes (2000), and ultimately Wu & Wu (2011) — the exact paper whose overlap triggered the retraction [PAGE 132].

The control layer (C) sits in the differential-games tradition of Dockner, Jørgensen, Van Long & Sorger (2000, Ch. 8, cited [PAGE 29]) and piecewise-deterministic Markov processes (Davis 1984). The auction layer (D) is the Guerre–Perrigne–Vuong (2000) GPV inversion, refined by Marmer–Shneyerov (2012) quantile methods and Luo–Wan (2017) tuning-free estimators. The RL layer (E) traces to Bellman (1957), Sutton & Barto (2018), and the deep-RL breakthrough Mnih et al. (2013, DQN) plus Mnih et al. (2016, A3C); multi-echelon theory goes back to Clark & Scarf (1960), whose proof "the reorder policy of an individual warehouse can only be optimal if the reorder policy is optimal for the entire network" [PAGE 82] is the theoretical anchor. The point-process layer (G) is Hawkes (1971) self-excitation fused with Cox–Isham shot noise, generalized by Brémaud–Massoulié (2002) and Dassios–Zhao (2011).

Multi-week traps embedded in the genealogy. Trap 1 — *variance vs volatility*: [PAGE 20] gives "average variance … 0.224," but Eq. (B) requires $\sigma=\sqrt{0.224}=0.473$; plugging $0.224$ as if it were $\sigma$ shifts $d_1$ by $\sim0.3$ and corrupts every downstream $N(d)$. Trap 2 — *retraction contamination*: because (B) re-states Wu & Wu (2011), any "independent confirmation" of the $907$ figure is circular. Trap 3 — *stationarity misuse*: the branching ratio $k=\delta-\mu_1^G$ must stay positive; borrowing the long-run formulas $h_1^\tau,h_2^\tau$ outside that regime silently produces negative or infinite variances.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The only genuine multi-agent belief structure in the volume is the licensing game (C), and it maps cleanly onto the crisis/counter-trap/clock frame if we read "crisis habit regression" as the patent-holder's pricing behavior under the *threat* of a compulsory license, "counter-trap" as the regulator's conditional CL trigger, and "clock" as patent expiry.

Layer 1 — Innovator's belief about an unobserved target. The regulator's sales target $\tilde S$ is private; the innovator holds a log-logistic belief $F_{\tilde S}(x)=\frac{1}{1+(x/a)^{-b}}$ with $a=b=2$ [PAGE 27], so $\Pr(\text{CL issued}\mid S_{H1})=F_{\tilde S}(S_{H1})$. The innovator therefore prices $p_I$ not against a known constraint but against a *distribution of triggers* — a recursive belief over the regulator's type.

Layer 2 — Counter-trap encoded in the hazard. The regulator's "trap" is state-contingent: $\lambda_{1,2}=\delta_{1,2}/x_I$ rises precisely when the innovator under-serves the market (low cumulative sales), while $\lambda_{1,3}=\delta_{1,3}x_I$ rewards high penetration by pushing straight to expiry. The innovator's best response is thus to keep $x_I$ high enough that $\lambda_{1,2}$ stays small — a regression toward aggressive early pricing to avoid the CL trap. This is textbook higher-order belief: the innovator acts on the regulator's action, which itself is a function of the innovator's action.

Layer 3 — The clock dominates at expiry. [PAGE 32]: "the expiration of the patent is independent on the sales of the product, we just assume a constant hazard function," $\lambda_{2,3}=\delta_{2,3}$. Once in mode 2 or 3, beliefs about the regulator become irrelevant; the constant hazard is a memoryless countdown. The authors also flag the modeling choice [PAGE 31]: "as the patent expires the innovator stops research in product innovation," i.e. $A_I\to0$, so the $\theta A_I$ demand boost and the $lA_I^2$ cost both switch off — the innovator's strategic "habit" collapses to static Bertrand/price competition (mode-3 profits (10)–(11)).

Counter-trap check: the design is self-consistent only if a royalty $R$ exists s.t. the innovator prefers licensing (mode 2) to the CL-litigation lottery, which is exactly the Sarmah et al. (2020) result the authors build on [PAGE 27]. The "clock" ($e^{-rs}$ discounting in $J_G,J_I$, [PAGE 32]) ensures neither player over-invests in a phase that will be truncated — the recursive loop closes because both players discount the same countdown.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
