Given the subsequent reversion, the phrase most likely refers to the eventual collapse back to baseline, or it is an editorial slip. I flag it rather than resolve it by fiat.

Multi-hop derivation: (Hop 1) Days 1–8: no food sign at goal; expectancy P(F|goal) ≈ 0; error probability ε(t) high. (Hop 2) Prior conditioning makes S a sign for food; inserting S at the goal transfers that expectancy, producing approach behavior as if F were present; hence the day-9 to day-13 improvement matching Group III. (Hop 3) Repeated non-reinforced exposure to S updates sign-value downward; the animals “discover that here at the end of the maze the discrimination-box was not a real sign”; expectancy collapses; by day 23 performance regresses to the pre-S baseline.

Assumptions: motivational deprivation state is constant across the 23 days; the discrimination-box stimulus is physically and perceptually invariant between the discrimination context and the maze context; Group III is a valid yoked control receiving S+F; no retroactive interference from extramaze cues. Breakdown (singularity) regimes: learning rate α = 0 produces no transfer; partial or ambiguous reinforcement (λ stochastic) prevents asymptotic extinction and stabilizes an intermediate performance level; if S is not discriminable from background cues, the sign-gestalt readiness R(S) remains near zero and no improvement occurs.

Gray-zone audit: The text does not specify how many discrimination trials preceded the maze phase, nor the exact inter-trial interval, food-deprivation hour, or whether “curves” plot errors, time, or a composite. Without these parameters, a quantitative fit is underdetermined. I therefore treat the narrative as qualitative but formalizable with the operators in the next section.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All empirical claims below are drawn from [PAGE 151].

Let t ∈ N index day/trial, measured in days. Let n_err(t) be the count of errors and n_tot(t) the total choices. The error probability is
ε(t) = n_err(t) / n_tot(t), ε(t) ∈ [0,1],
dimensionless. Latency to goal is T(t) ∈ R⁺, measured in seconds.

Introduce sign stimulus S (discrimination-box) and primary reinforcer F (food). Define binary reinforcement λ(t) ∈ {0,1}, where λ=1 denotes food present. The sign-value or expectancy strength is V_S(t) ∈ R. Using a Rescorla–Wagner/Tolmanian update formalism:
V_S(t+1) = V_S(t) + α [ λ(t) − V_S(t) ],
with learning rate α ∈ (0,1), unit day⁻¹. Under non-reinforcement (λ=0) for k consecutive days:
V_S(k) = V_S(0)(1−α)^k,
showing geometric decay toward zero.

Sign-gestalt readiness is a bounded propensity:
R(S,t) = σ( V_S(t) ) = 1 / (1 + e^(−V_S(t))), R ∈ [0,1],
dimensionless probability. A minimal performance map is
ε(t) = ε_0 exp[ −β R(S,t) ] + η(t),
β > 0, η(t) noise, where ε_0 is the baseline error rate without sign expectancy.

Page citations: [PAGE 151] for “Group I was the experimental group”; [PAGE 151] for “From the ninth day on, however, they were suddenly provided with the discrimination-box at the end of the maze, though now with no food in it”; [PAGE 151] for “on the thirteenth day their curves had got down to the level of Group III”; [PAGE 151] for “by the twenty-third day they were making almost as many errors and taking as much time as they had before”; [PAGE 151] for “the discrimination-box had become a sign for food”; [PAGE 151] for “Hence the drop in their maze performance.”

Breakdown regimes: (i) α = 0 yields no sign acquisition and no day-9 improvement; (ii) λ stochastically equal to 1 with probability p ∈ (0,1) yields partial reinforcement, preventing full extinction and producing a stable intermediate ε rather than reversion; (iii) β = 0 severs the sign-performance link, so S can be valued without affecting maze errors; (iv) if food-deprivation state drifts, λ is confounded with motivational state.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men [PAGE 151] occupies a historical hinge between strict Watsonian stimulus-response psychology and later cognitive accounts. The explicit appeal to “Dr. Williams” and the claim that the discrimination-box became “a sign for food” invoke a sign-theoretic tradition descending from Gestalt psychology (Koffka, Köhler) and from Kurt Lewin’s valence/expectancy formulations, in which objects are not merely stimuli but signs with “to-be-demanded character.” Tolman’s own subsequent work on latent learning and cognitive maps generalizes exactly this sign-gestalt mechanism: rats build expectations about goal objects independent of immediate reinforcement.

The multi-week trap in this passage is temporal and inferential. Because the improvement appears within days 9–13, an experimenter might conclude that a sign substitute has permanently replaced food as a goal object. The 23-day arc refutes that: without primary reinforcement, the sign’s valence decays and performance regresses. A second trap is to treat Group III (S + food) as merely a “reward” control; it is in fact a yoked reinforcement control that isolates the contribution of the sign itself. The historical lesson is that expectancy-based performance is valid only over a limited reinforcement horizon; extending the horizon without updating the contingency produces extinction, not stable learning.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
From the animal’s perspective, the crisis is a substitution at the top of the goal hierarchy. The habitual expectancy is F at maze end. On day 9, S is inserted as a higher-order sign for F. Initially the animal resolves the crisis by treating S as if it were F: “to-be-demanded character” transfers, errors fall, and running becomes efficient. This is a habit regression to a food-expectant policy under a new sign cue.

The counter-trap is the experimenter’s assumption that the sign–food equivalence is transitive and permanent. It is transitive only while non-reinforced exposures remain few; once the animal accumulates enough disconfirming trials, the second-order expectancy collapses. The recursive structure is: expect F → see S → infer F → run well → S delivered without F → infer S is not a real sign → revert to pre-S poor policy.

The clock is the count of non-reinforced S-exposures at the maze goal. Improvement is measurable by day 13 (roughly 4–5 exposures), but full reversion requires approximately 14–15 additional non-reinforced days to reach day 23. This asymmetry—rapid sign-mediated improvement, slower extinction—implies that the sign’s initial affective charge outlasts its epistemic validity.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The maze apparatus and discrimination-box are held constant across groups and across days 1–23. There is no evidence in [PAGE 151] of apparatus alteration. Veto condition: if the box’s dimensions, texture, or location changed between day 13 and day 23, the reversion could be perceptual rather than cognitive. No such change is reported.

Biomechanical layer: Running speed and error rate can dissociate under motor fatigue. However, Group I shows low latency and few errors on day 13, demonstrating intact motor capacity. The day-23 reversion therefore cannot be explained by cumulative biomechanical fatigue alone, because performance was recently fluent under the same apparatus. Veto condition: if day-23 rats show reduced speed on a separate, non-maze motor task, a fatigue confound would require re-evaluation.

Weather layer: The experiment is conducted indoors; ambient temperature and humidity are not mentioned in [PAGE 151] and are assumed controlled. If vivarium temperature or food-deprivation hours varied across the 23 days, motivational strength λ could drift independently of sign-value. Veto condition: if deprivation duration increased over time, the reversion might reflect reduced hunger rather than sign extinction.

Veto adjudication: The within-subject recovery (day 13) followed by collapse (day 23) under constant apparatus and presumably constant motivation constitutes a veto proof against purely physical or biomechanical accounts. The reversion is best localized to the cognitive sign–expectancy layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios:

Scenario 1 (Re-reinforcement): Beginning on day 14, food is again placed inside the discrimination-box at the maze end. Prediction: ε(t) remains at the low Group III level through day 23; no reversion to day-23 baseline. Veto: If performance reverts to poor levels despite food delivery, the sign-value extinction account is falsified.

Scenario 2 (Novel neutral object): On day 9, replace S with an object N that has never been paired with food. Prediction: No day-9 to day-13 improvement; ε_I(t) remains high and statistically indistinguishable from the pre-day-9 baseline through day 23. Veto: If improvement occurs with N, the explanation is neophilia or general novelty, not a specific sign-for-food expectancy.

Scenario 3 (Cross-modal sign degradation): Present the discrimination-box at the maze end but alter its visual/tactile pattern so it is physically present yet not recognized as the trained sign. Prediction: Transfer is delayed or absent; the improvement curve shifts right relative to day 9–13. Veto: If immediate full transfer occurs despite pattern alteration, sign-gestalt specificity is absent and the “sign for food” interpretation is too weak.

Each scenario is verifiable by standard error-rate and latency recording and isolates a distinct assumption of the Tolmanian sign account.
