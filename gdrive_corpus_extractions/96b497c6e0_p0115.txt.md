Natural selection strongly favors animals with high sensitivity to prediction error (low resistance to extinction) to rapidly pivot to new sources, a trait completely antithetical to the maximized habit strength described by Hull's fitted curve. Thus, the elegant mathematical formalism derived from albino rats in boxes acts as a genealogical blind spot, trapping subsequent researchers (and modern machine learning practitioners designing reinforcement learning agents) into building systems prone to catastrophic inertia and poor transfer learning.
