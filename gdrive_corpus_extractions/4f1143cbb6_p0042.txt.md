Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each names a manipulation of a [PAGE 1] constraint, states the physical mechanism, and gives a prediction about measurable outcomes (film-reviewable or chartable), not a betting line.

Scenario 1 — Violation of farthest-arm shielding on a wide slant under wet conditions.
Manipulation: On a wide slant or wide run, the ball carrier carries the ball in the arm nearest the scrimmage line (near arm) rather than the far arm, with light rain on the ball.
Mechanism: The near arm is pinned by the first defender, reducing available \(N_h\) and eliminating the free arm’s fending moment. Rain lowers \(\mu_s\), so the single available contact approaches the slip singularity \( \mu_s N_h < F_{t,\text{perturbation}} \).
Prediction: On film-reviewed plays meeting this description, the first contact after the catch results in a fumble or forced loose ball at a rate significantly higher than wide-slant plays where the far-arm rule is observed. Verifiable by charting arm position (near vs. far) at the point of first defender contact and correlating with fumble events.

Scenario 2 — Quarter fails belly placement on a close plunge.
Manipulation: On a close slant or plunge, the quarter does not place the ball firmly on the back’s belly and does not insert his hand between the back’s hands; the back receives the ball with hands only, and the long axis is not parallel to the ground.
Mechanism: The handoff impulse \(\mathbf{J}\) is applied with alignment error \(\epsilon_\theta > 0\), producing asymmetric normal loading and a contact deficit \(n_c = 2\) (back’s hands only) without the quarter’s stabilizing third contact at the instant of transfer. The stability margin \(S\) crosses below zero at the line of scrimmage.
Prediction: The back muffs or fumbles on the first step or at first contact with the defensive line. Verifiable by measuring snap-to-fumble interval on charted plunge plays; the interval should cluster near \(t \le 0.8\) s (first step/contact) relative to baseline plunge plays with proper quarter placement.

Scenario 3 — Ordinary-play arm switch under fourth-quarter fatigue.
Manipulation: In the fourth quarter, a back switches the ball from one arm to the other on an ordinary play (not running free in the open), after a sequence of high-exertion snaps.
Mechanism: During the transfer, contact points drop from \(n_c = 2\) to \(n_c = 1\) or \(0\) for \(\Delta t_{\text{switch}} > 0\). Fatigue reduces maximum sustainable \(N_i\) and slows the reactivation of the second contact. The hazard integral accumulates a spike during \(\Delta t_{\text{switch}}\).
Prediction: Fumble probability on ordinary-play arm-switch snaps in the fourth quarter is higher than on ordinary-play non-switch snaps by the same back in the same game. Verifiable by play-by-play charting of arm-switch events on ordinary downs versus baseline carries, stratified by quarter and snap count since last substitution.
