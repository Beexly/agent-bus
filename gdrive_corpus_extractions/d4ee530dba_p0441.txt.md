Let $G = \{g_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ denote the set of $N$ independent genetic factors and $T = \{t_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ the set of $M$ learned habit units. Following Tolman [PAGE 410], a behavioral capacity $C_k$ (for $k \in \{\text{d, m, me, r, ci, cr}\}$ corresponding to discriminanda, manipulanda, means-end, retentivity, consciousness/ideation, creativity) is not atomic but an emergent functional formed by weighted sums over subsets of $G \cup T$.

Define indicator variables $I_{ik}(x)$ which equal 1 if factor $g_i$ contributes to capacity $C_k$ given stimulus context $x$, and 0 otherwise. Similarly $J_{jk}(x)$ for $t_j$. The total contribution to capacity $C_k$ in context $x$ is:
\[
C_k(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{ik} I_{ik}(x) + \sum_{j=1}^{M} v_{jk} J_{jk}(x)
\]
where $w_{ik}, v_{jk}$ are efficacy weights. Crucially, $I_{ik}(x)$ and $J_{jk}(x)$ depend on $x$, meaning the composition of $C_k$ varies with context. Tolman states that each response taps "slightly different arrays," implying that for two contexts $x$ and $x'$, the sets $\{i : I_{ik}(x)=1\}$ and $\{i : I_{ik}(x')=1\}$ differ.

The footnote asserts that the correlation between two measurements of $C_k$ on successive occasions is less than unity. Let $C_k^{(1)} = C_k(x_1)$ and $C_k^{(2)} = C_k(x_2)$. Then:
\[
\rho(C_k^{(1)}, C_k^{(2)}) = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(C_k^{(1)}, C_k^{(2)})}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(C_k^{(1)})\mathrm{Var}(C_k^{(2)})}} < 1
\]
This inequality holds because the random variation in the active subsets induces noise in the measurement of $C_k$. Assuming independence of factors conditional on context, we can model the variance decomposition:
\[
\mathrm{Var}(C_k(x)) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_x[\mathrm{Var}(C_k(x) | x)]}_{\text{noise from sampling }} + \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_x[\mathbb{E}[C_k(x) | x]]}_{\text{signal from true ability}}
\]
The first term captures the variability due to different arrays being tapped even for the same nominal task, explaining why test-retest reliability is imperfect. This equation formalizes Tolman's claim that capacities are only "approximately identical" across instances.

Units: $C_k$ is dimensionless (scaled to represent proficiency). The correlation coefficient is unitless. Assumptions: Factors combine additively; weights are static within a context but the membership indicators are stochastic and context-dependent. Breakdown regime: When $N, M \to \infty$ and overlaps become negligible, $C_k(x)$ converges to a Gaussian process, and capacity assessment becomes purely probabilistic.
