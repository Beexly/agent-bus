Crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock.

Crisis habit regression. When an automated strike zone is introduced, catchers and pitchers face a sudden removal of the framing feedback loop. The recursive hypothesis is that under uncertainty about the machine's calibration, catchers regress to pre-ABS receiving habits out of distrust of the algorithm, while pitchers regress to pre-ABS sequencing because they no longer trust the catcher's target. This is a second-order belief: Agent A models Agent B's belief about the machine. If both agents expect the other to regress, the observed PA-outcome distribution remains near the historical mean even though the underlying zone rule changed—consistent with the reported effect ≈ 0 [PAGE 1]. The regression is a crisis habit: under regime change, actors revert to the last stable equilibrium rather than computing the new Nash response to the height-scaled zone.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is overfitting to the algorithmic zone. Teams may train batters to take pitches at the exact nominal boundaries of Z(h), expecting zero human variance. But sensor latency, calibration drift, and the machine's own error function η_machine introduce a residual band around the boundary. If a team overcommits to the nominal edge, the machine's false-positive/false-negative calls create a systematic exploitation opportunity for the opponent. The trap is recursive: the league anticipates teams overfitting to the machine, so the machine's zone may be deliberately softened near edges, inducing teams to under-adjust—an iterated elimination of dominated strategies that collapses back toward the human-zone distribution.

Clock. Three temporal layers operate simultaneously. (1) The data-engineering clock: Polars 2.0.0 (Oct 6) and PyArrow 26 (Oct 9) impose dependency breakages before the Carnegie Mellon conference (Oct 23–24) [PAGE 1]. Pipelines must be migrated before conference deadlines or they run on stale, incompatible stacks. (2) The sports-season clock: sportsdataverse-py's most_recent_nfl_season returns the previous season from Sep 1 to the Wednesday after Labor Day (#791) [PAGE 1]. A pipeline querying in early September silently trains on the prior year's rosters, a temporal off-by-one that contaminates preseason models. (3) The literature clock: the roundup window (Oct 14–21) captures preprints from Sep 27–Oct 20, but package releases are from Aug–Sep [PAGE 1]. The clock mismatch means the most recent methods are not yet in the most recent code.

Recursive equilibrium. Analysts know other analysts are regressing to crisis habits, so they preemptively adjust; but if everyone preempts, the aggregate distribution reverts again. The stable point is not the human zone nor the pure machine zone, but a hybrid where behavioral hysteresis offsets algorithmic precision. The only way to break the recursion is exogenous instrument variation—such as comparing games with and without ABS challenges—which requires the challenge-flag exposure currently missing from the Statcast surface [PAGE 1].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather veto proof.

A veto at any layer invalidates downstream causal claims about automated umpiring or credit assignment.

Physical layer — pitch trajectory and sensor geometry. A pitched baseball follows Magnus-affected flight under gravity; its crossing position (x,z) at the plate is determined by release velocity, spin axis, and atmospheric density. The automated zone \(\mathcal{Z}(h)\) depends on batter height h [m], which is itself a biomechanical variable: stance adjusts effective height by several centimeters. If the sensor system (e.g., Hawk-Eye/TrackMan) samples at finite frame rate, the observed crossing point is interpolated, introducing a spatial quantization error δ_z [m]. Veto condition: if δ_z exceeds half the vertical band of \(\mathcal{Z}(h)\) at the boundary, calls within δ_z of the edge are non-identifiable, and any estimate of "bias removed" is vetoed by sensor resolution.

Biomechanical layer — catcher framing removal. The KBO and MLB studies state robot umpires remove framing [PAGE 1]. Framing is a motor skill with latency on the order of human visual-motor response (~150–250 ms). Removing it eliminates a degree of freedom in the catcher's action space, but it does not remove the pitcher's ability to target corners. If pitchers adapt by exploiting the machine's systematic zone errors (e.g., consistent vertical offset for left-handed batters), the biomechanical equilibrium shifts without a change in PA outcomes. Veto condition: without pitch-level catcher-glove position data synchronized to the machine's call, the framing-removal channel is unobserved, and the zero PA-effect claim is confounded by unmeasured pitcher–catcher substitution.

Weather layer — unobserved environmental confounding. [PAGE 1] contains no weather data. This absence is itself a veto risk: outdoor automated zones depend on sensor calibration that drifts with temperature and humidity. If air density ρ [kg/m³] varies with weather, the Magnus deflection changes, shifting the pitch's actual crossing point relative to the sensor's modeled trajectory. If the machine's zone model does not condition on ρ, calls become weather-correlated. Veto condition: if the residual call error ε in the strike-probability equation is correlated with temperature or humidity, the estimated effect of the automated zone is biased by an omitted atmospheric variable. Because [PAGE 1] provides no meteorological covariates, any cross-game comparison of ABS effects must be treated as vetoed until sensor-level calibration logs are joined to the play-level data.

Cross-layer synthesis. The decision layer (umpire call) rests on the sensor layer (trajectory estimate), which rests on the physical layer (ball flight), which is modulated by the weather layer. The behavioral layer (batter/pitcher adjustment) responds to calls with a lag. A single veto—e.g., unquantified sensor quantization δ_z near zone boundaries, or unobserved weather-correlated calibration drift—breaks the identification chain from "automated zone" to "PA outcome ≈ 0." Therefore the roundup's null-effect finding is provisionally accepted only under the assumption of sensor exogeneity and weather invariance, neither of which is established in [PAGE 1].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
