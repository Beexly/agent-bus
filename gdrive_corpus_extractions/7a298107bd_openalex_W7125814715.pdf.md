Under temporal crisis—the 2 s visualization window bracketed by a 1 s configuration phase [PAGE 6]—athletes are forced into recursive habit regression. Professionals regress to a perceptual–cognitive habit: rapid detection of relevant game-state variables and commitment to a decision (high RC, fast VMRS). University players regress to a motor habit: once a decision is made, they execute the short displacement more quickly (faster DS), likely reflecting younger athletes’ conditioning bias toward explosive, change-of-direction actions [PAGE 7]. This dissociation predicts that under acute time pressure, expertise manifests in the pre-movement interval (VMRS, RC) rather than in the movement interval itself (DS) for very short displacements.

The counter-trap is the university players’ DS advantage. At face value it reads as superior motor speed, but it is context-locked to ≈2 m frontal, lateral, and diagonal bursts with brief accelerations and decelerations, not sustained match-speed running [PAGE 5, PAGE 7]. Thus the DS result is a trap for practitioners who might interpret it as general physical superiority; it is instead a narrow, task-specific agility profile that does not transfer to longer sprints or repeated high-intensity efforts. Professionals’ lower DS may reflect greater body mass (75.34 ± 7.58 kg vs. 73.21 ± 8.00 kg), different movement economies, or tactical hesitation under uncertainty, none of which are captured by a scalar displacement time [PAGE 2].

The clock imposes a hard boundary on the entire measurement chain. The 30 fps video sampling produces a fundamental temporal quantum of 33.3 ms; the reaction-start uncertainty of ≈4 frames (≈0.133 s) consumes roughly 6.7% of the 2 s visualization window and nearly 70% of the observed between-group VMRS difference (0.19 s) [PAGE 5, P
AGE 6, PAGE 7]. Consequently, any experimental manipulation that shortens the visualization window below the algorithmic uncertainty floor would collapse VMRS discriminability, whereas lengthening it would dilute the time pressure that generates the professional advantage in the first place. The recursive loop is therefore: stimulus duration sets cognitive load, which determines VMRS; VMRS plus DS determines CMS; but because CMS is an unweighted sum, the clock’s resolution limits whether the composite can ever resolve group differences when component effects oppose each other.

Physical layer. The apparatus comprises a computer, HDMI cable, 50″ smart TV, webcam, Internet access, and a clean flat 5 m × 5 m surface with nine circular markers (15 cm diameter); peripheral points sit 2 m from the center with 45° inter-point angles [PAGE 2]. Only outfield players (defenders, midfielders, forwards) were admitted; goalkeepers were excluded because of distinct perceptual–motor demands and specialized training [PAGE 2]. Participants were required to be actively competing and free of documented post-concussion syndrome or uncorrected sensory deficits [PAGE 2].

Biomechanical layer. Each session followed a standardized warm-up: 4 min moderate continuous running, 4 min joint mobility, five 10 m sprints (two straight, three change-of-direction), and 5 min stretching [PAGE 5]. Between the three repetitions, a standardized 6 min rest was mandated as sufficient to restore performance after explosive movements [PAGE 5]. Players reported session RPE on a 1–10 scale, and the next repetition began only when RPE ≤ 2 [PAGE 5]. These constraints bound the protocol to brief, low-fatigue, repeated explosive efforts rather than sustained match load.

Environmental/weather layer. Calibration was routinely successful on indoor flooring, natural grass, and synthetic turf [PAGE 5]. A hard veto condition exists: “reflective floors or highly non-uniform lighting may require the semiautomatic fallback” [PAGE 5]. Because the study was conducted in situ and not under variable outdoor weather, rain, solar glare, wet turf, or uneven illumination are unadjudicated vetoes that could break the contrast-based polygon detection and force manual intervention [PAGE 5]. Under such conditions, the stated measurement error (≈0.133 s reaction, ≈0.053 s arrival) would likely increase, threatening the already narrow VMRS signal-to-noise ratio.

Veto adjudication summary. No single layer invalidates the reported group differences under the tested indoor conditions. However, the conjunction of (i) reflective or non-uniform lighting, (ii) goalkeepers or mixed positions, (iii) extended fatigue protocols without RPE gating, and (iv) outdoor weather would violate the system’s stated operating envelope and void the reproducibility claims [PAGE 2, PAGE 5, PAGE 7].
