Recursive theory of mind here models the rat as a system whose behavior under crisis (extinction) regresses to previously reinforced habits. This is a habit-regression cascade: the primary reinforcer is removed, the secondary reinforcer (click) briefly preserves the response at superthreshold levels [PAGE 88], and only after repeated non-reinforced evocations does the stimulus lose its capacity to evoke the reaction—the definition of experimental extinction given on [PAGE 88].

The counter-trap is to assume that the magazine click is a conditioned reinforcer when it may instead function as a discriminative stimulus or an occasion setter signaling the broader reinforcement context. If the click merely occasions bar pressing without strengthening it, then the >30% difference in pressures reflects stimulus control rather than secondary reinforcement value. The text supports the reinforcement interpretation ("power of a stimulus ... to contribute to the maintenance ... at a superthreshold level") [PAGE 88], but it does not rule out the occasion-setter alternative because Skinner's missing control experiment would have been required to isolate the click's specific role [PAGE 88].

Clock dynamics. The "clock" is the extinction time course. Hull notes that extinction is gradual: "the stimulus gradually loses its capacity to evoke the reaction" [PAGE 88]. The 30% count advantage is therefore a temporal integral:
\[
\Delta P = \int_{t_0}^{t_{\text{ext}}} \left[ r_c(t) - r_{nc}(t) \right] dt > 0,
\]
where \(r_c(t)\) and \(r_{nc}(t)\) are press rates under click and no-click conditions. This integral accumulates over the extinction clock; it is not an instantaneous strength measurement. A recursive analyst must therefore distinguish between rate suppression (immediate) and persistence over time (delayed). The clock trap is to read a total-count difference as evidence of greater habit strength when it may only reflect slower decay, i.e., a longer time constant \(\tau\) in an exponential extinction model \(r(t) = r_0 e^{-t/\tau}\), with \(\tau_c > \tau_{nc}\).
