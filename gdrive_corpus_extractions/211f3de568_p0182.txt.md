Biomechanical layer. Three successive climbs to food incur cumulative work W = 3 m g h (1 + η⁻¹), where η is muscular efficiency. If the delay period does not include adequate rest, fatigue degrades subsequent descent and floor locomotion, producing a performance floor unrelated to mnemonization [PAGE 1].

Chemosensory veto. Food odor residue O_t on the correct stand, if above the rat’s detection threshold O_min after Δt, supplies a non-mnemonic cue. Veto proof: swab all ringstands and paths with a neutral solvent between acquisition and test; if P(Y_t = C_t | Δt) remains significantly above 1/3, the olfactory veto is falsified and a genuine sign-gestalt or expectancy trace is implicated [PAGE 1].

Weather/laboratory climate. The setting is an indoor lab, so meteorological weather is not a direct variable, but ambient temperature T_a relative to the rat’s thermoneutral zone (~26–30 °C) acts as a physiological veto. If T_a < 20 °C during the delay, metabolic rate and arousal shift; if T_a > 32 °C, lethargy reduces path exploration. Light–dark phase must be held constant because testing during the normal sleep phase confounds retention with circadian sleep state.

Adjudication rule. The inference that the rat “delay[s] successfully” [PAGE 1] survives only if: (1) the motor veto is inactive (θ < arctan μ and W does not induce fatigue), (2) the olfactory veto is falsified by cleaning, (3) circadian phase is controlled or modeled as a covariate, and (4) P(Y_t = C_t | Δt) exceeds 1/3 with I(G ; Y | Δt) > 0 after the information decay model is applied.

Scenario 1 — Olfactory scrub. Intervention: between the three acquisition runs and the test, clean every ringstand and path with 70 % ethanol, replace substrate, and air-dry before imposing Δt. Veto condition: if P(Y_t = C_t | Δt ≥ 600 s) falls to the chance baseline 1/3, the original “minutes or even hours” success [PAGE 1] was odor-mediated rather than mnemonic. Prediction: cleaned condition P(Y_t = C_t | 86400 s) ≈ 0.33; uncleaned condition P(Y_t = C_t | 86400 s) > 0.50, rejecting the null of chance by binomial test (α = 0.05).

Scenario 2 — Allocentric rotation. Intervention: rotate the entire apparatus and distal room cues 180° during the delay so the floor stands no longer occupy their acquisition vectors; keep odor cues constant by not cleaning. Veto condition: if performance collapses only under rotation and not under cleaning, the rat depended on spatial sign gestalt, not abstract delay memory. Prediction: P(Y_t = C_t | rotated, Δt = 3600 s) → 1/3; P(Y_t = C_t | unrotated, Δt = 3600 s) > 1/3.

Scenario 3 — Interpolated interference. Intervention: during Δt, expose the rat to the two previously correct (now incorrect) stands paired with food, creating retroactive interference, while a control group rests undisturbed. Veto condition: if retention decays faster with interference than without, the apparently “surprisingly long period” retention [PAGE 1] was due to low interference, not robust memory. Prediction: baseline I(Δt = 86400 s) ≥ 0.5 bits; with interference, I(Δt = 3600 s) ≤ 0.2 bits, implying γ_interference ≫ γ_baseline in I(Δt) = I₀ exp(−γ Δt). All three scenarios are verifiable by comparing choice frequencies against 1/3 across n ≥ 30 trials per condition.
