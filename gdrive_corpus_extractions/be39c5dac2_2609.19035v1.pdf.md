<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage. CPIT descends from the conformal prediction tradition of Vovk et al. (2005) and Shafer & Vovk (2008), extended to distribution-free regression by Lei et al. (2018), and to predictive distributions via conformal predictive systems and conformal calibrators (Vovk et al., 2017, 2020) ([PAGE 3]). It explicitly distinguishes itself from distributional conformal prediction (Chernozhukov et al., 2021), which assumes an evaluable conditional CDF \hat F_x(y), whereas CPIT works only with generated samples ([PAGE 3]). It also differs from probabilistic conformal prediction using conditional random samples (Wang et al., 2023), which targets set-valued coverage at a single level rather than a full calibrated predictive CDF ([PAGE 3]). The impossibility of exact distribution-free conditional coverage (Barber et al., 2021) motivates the finite-sample marginal target ([PAGE 3]). Proper scoring via CRPS and Cramér–von Mises calibration diagnostics trace to Gneiting & Raftery (2007) ([PAGE 3]); kernel CDF smoothing defaults to Lopez-de Ullibarri (2015) ([PAGE 11]); GAM spline penalties follow standard additive model practice ([PAGE 7]).

Multi-week traps embedded in the experimental design. WeatherBench 2 covers 2018–2022 with twice-daily initialization (00 and 12 UTC) and a fixed 24-hour lead ([PAGE 22]); day of year is modeled with a cyclic P-spline to capture annual periodicity ([PAGE 24]). This temporal structure creates three recursive failure modes over multi-week deployment: (i) covariate shift between the calibration window and deployment weeks, which violates the exchangeability condition conditioning on D_{\rm tr},D_{\rm bias} ([PAGE 15]) and invalidates the finite-sample marginal guarantee absent weighted conformal adaptation ([PAGE 31]); (ii) ensemble member redundancy or collapse over extended re-forecasts, reducing effective m below 50 and degrading tail support for high quantiles ([PAGE 31]); (iii) seasonal regime changes (e.g., Mediterranean winter cyclones vs. summer convection) that make a single global affine correction inadequate, requiring the covariate-dependent GAM ([PAGE 7]) and localized diagnostics ([PAGE 20]). The Taiwan experiment exacerbates these traps because individual 1.5° grid cells expose land–sea contrast and unresolved orography that spatial averaging in the Europe experiment suppresses ([PAGE 27]). Rare-event horizons are especially fragile: the 80 mm threshold arises only ~4–7 times per cell in 1,241 test cases ([PAGE 28]), so any multi-week tail-skill claim is statistically underpowered before considering temporal drift.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis-habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock. Under operational stress, forecasters exhibit crisis-habit regression: they revert to the raw ensemble cloud or to a single fixed-level interval, discarding skewness, heteroskedasticity, and multimodality ([PAGE 2]). This is the compression trap—turning sample-based generators into point summaries before applying standard conformal regression—which destroys the very distributional information needed for threshold probabilities and tail expectations ([PAGE 2]). CPIT is the counter-trap: it preserves the full weighted empirical law \bar P_x=\sum_j w_j\delta_{\hat Y_{{\rm adj},(j)}(x)} ([PAGE 10], Eq. 13) so that exceedance probabilities, quantiles, HDIs, tail expectations, and calibrated resamples all derive from one coherent CDF rather than from disjoint, level-specific interval constructions ([PAGE 29]).

The clock layer concerns split timing and temporal causality. In simulations the data are partitioned n_{\rm bias}=1000,\ n_{\rm cal}=1000,\ n_{\rm test}=5000 ([PAGE 20]); in WeatherBench 2, n_{\rm bias}=1204,\ n_{\rm cal}=1204,\ n_{\rm test}=1241 ([PAGE 22]). The conformal guarantees are conditional on the fitted generator and bias-correction rule and require exchangeability across calibration and test cases ([PAGE 15]). Because forecasts are initialized twice daily at a 24-hour lead over five years, adjacent calibration cases share meteorological regimes; if temporal blocking is not respected, the exchangeability assumption leaks and the finite-sample coverage bound becomes optimistic ([PAGE 31]). Recursive theory of mind enters at the decision interface: the downstream decision model assumes it receives a calibrated predictive law, while an interval-only baseline provides only a set at one nominal \alpha. If the operator queries P(Y>c|x) at multiple thresholds c, an interval-only system cannot return monotone, threshold-coherent probabilities, whereas CPIT guarantees \hat\pi_i(10\,{\rm mm})\ge\hat\pi_i(20\,{\rm mm})\ge\hat\pi_i(40\,{\rm mm})\ge\hat\pi_i(80\,{\rm mm}) automatically from a single CDF ([PAGE 29]). A misaligned operator who treats CPIT’s equal-tail interval as a guaranteed fixed-level set without the PIT-centrality wrapper will experience coverage drift: central 90% coverage ranges 0.879–0.892 unsmoothed across Europe variants ([PAGE 26]) and rises to 0.919–0.928 only after kernel smoothing ([PAGE 27], Table 4). The clock therefore demands that the interval-calibration split I_int be held out and refreshed as the deployment calendar advances, or the nominal coverage guarantee expires with the season.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, weather, and mathematical veto proof.

Physical support veto. Precipitation is nonnegative and zero-inflated. The analysis retains meters internally but fits the affine correction on the log-millimeter scale g(y)=\log(y+0.1) ([PAGE 24]). This transformation respects the physical lower bound at zero while reducing right skew; applying an untransformed affine correction to raw millimeters would violate support constraints and produce negative corrected draws after inverse transformation.

Spatial aggregation veto. The Europe experiment uses area weights a_s so each grid cell contributes according to physical area rather than equal weight on a latitude–longitude grid ([PAGE 23]). The Taiwan experiment intentionally uses singleton 1.5° cells ([PAGE 24]) because spatial averaging would mask displacement error, land–sea contrast, and unresolved orographic effects ([PAGE 27]). Any method that aggregates without area weighting or that smooths over orographic boundaries fails the physical-consistency veto.

Calibration-map singularity veto. The finite-support map \hat C_m normalizes by \hat C\{m/(m+1)\} ([PAGE 9], Eq. 10). Theorem 3 explicitly assumes C^\star\{m/(m+1)\}>0 ([PAGE 18]). If calibration PITs concentrate at the top rank cell—systematic under-prediction of the observation relative to the ensemble—the denominator approaches zero and rank-cell weights explode. This is a hard veto: no finite-support CPIT CDF can be constructed without redistribution or isotonic regularization.

Global-affine breakdown veto. When bias is covariate-dependent, a global constant location-scale correction is insufficient. In Simulation 2, BC(Global) leaves CvM=3.8295 and central 90% coverage 0.801 ([PAGE 21]); in the Taiwan NW cell, BC(Global) worsens CvM from 7.4014 to 10.2603 ([PAGE 28]). The veto rule is: if a covariate-shift diagnostic or binwise PIT histogram shows residual structure after global correction, the GAM affine correction is mandatory, and CPIT(GAM) is preferred over CPIT(Global).

Tail-support and smoothing veto. With m=50 ensemble members, the empirical CDF has 51 rank cells; tail quantiles beyond the (m)/(m+1) grid require extrapolation. Proposition 1 bounds the W_1 perturbation from Gaussian smoothing by \tau_x\sqrt{2/\pi} and any Lipschitz summary error by L\tau_x\sqrt{2/\pi} ([PAGE 17], Eq. 30). Veto rule: if the target quantile level q satisfies q>m/(m+1) or the expected event count is below ~10 cases (as for 80 mm in Taiwan, ~4–7 events; [PAGE 28]), the unsmoothed empirical tail is vetoed; the smoothed law is required, and Brier skill at that threshold is labeled a sensitivity diagnostic, not stable operational skill ([PAGE 28]).

Exceedance-monotonicity veto. Because \bar F_x is a proper CDF, predicted exceedance probabilities \hat\pi_i(c)=1-\bar F_x^{(\tau)}(c) are automatically monotone decreasing in c ([PAGE 29]). Any post-processing layer that produces non-monotone threshold probabilities across c\in\{10,20,40,80\}\,mm fails the physical-coherence veto and must be recalibrated.

W_1 bandwidth veto. Smoothing is numerical regularization, not a substitute for ensemble diversity ([PAGE 31]). If \tau_x is large relative to the physical scale of precipitation gradients, Lipschitz summaries (e.g., expected exceedance severity E[(Y-c)_+|x]) are biased by up to L\tau_x\sqrt{2/\pi}. The veto triggers when \tau_x\sqrt{2/\pi} exceeds a pre-specified decision tolerance in mm.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reward-leveraged verification and rejection) counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 — Exchangeability/covariate-shift rejection. Verifiable condition: run a two-sample distributional test between calibration covariates \{X_i\}_{i\in I_{\rm cal}} and deployment covariates \{X_j\}_{\rm deploy} (e.g., kernel maximum mean discrepancy or a classifier two-sample test). Veto if the null of identical covariate distributions is rejected at p<0.01. Rationale: Theorems 1 and 2 condition on D_{\rm tr},D_{\rm bias} and require exchangeability of forecast–response objects ([PAGE 15–16]); covariate shift breaks the finite-sample rank-uniformity and marginal coverage guarantees ([PAGE 31]). Prediction: if the veto is ignored, central 90% interval coverage will drift below nominal by at least 5 percentage points within four weeks of deployment under gradual seasonal drift, and the Cramér–von Mises PIT statistic will exceed the simulation baseline (e.g., >1.0 for shape-misspecified regimes; [PAGE 21]).

Veto scenario 2 — Tail-support/effective-ensemble-size rejection. Verifiable condition: compute the effective number of unique adjusted order statistics carrying non-negligible weight, m_{\rm eff}=1/\sum_j w_j^2. Veto unsmoothed tail quantile/HDR output for any target level q>0.95 if m_{\rm eff}<20. Rationale: The empirical CDF is supported on m atoms; the normalized rank-cell weights w_j derive from \hat C_m whose grid granularity is 1/(m+1) ([PAGE 9–10]). With m=50 in WeatherBench 2, the upper tail is sparse ([PAGE 31]). Prediction: if ignored, the unsmoothed 99th percentile will exhibit Monte Carlo standard error exceeding 20% of the interquartile range, and exceedance probabilities at rare thresholds will become non-robust to single-member perturbations. The smoothed alternative is mandated, with W_1 perturbation capped at \tau_x\sqrt{2/\pi} ([PAGE 17], Eq. 29).

Veto scenario 3 — Calibration-map denominator singularity rejection. Verifiable condition: evaluate \hat C\{m/(m+1)\} from Eq. (10) on the held-out calibration split. Veto CPIT CDF construction if \hat C\{m/(m+1)\}<10^{-3} (numerically indistinguishable from zero). Rationale: Theorem 3 requires C^\star\{m/(m+1)\}>0 ([PAGE 18]); Eq. (10) divides by \hat C\{m/(m+1)\} ([PAGE 9]). Near-zero values occur when calibration PITs concentrate at the top rank cell, indicating systematic under-dispersion or under-prediction. Prediction: if ignored, division by a near-zero denominator concentrates >90% of weighted predictive mass on a single adjusted order statistic, producing degenerate exceedance probabilities that violate threshold monotonicity \hat\pi_i(c_1)\ge\hat\pi_i(c_2) for c_1<c_2 ([PAGE 29]) and invalidating all downstream risk summaries.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
