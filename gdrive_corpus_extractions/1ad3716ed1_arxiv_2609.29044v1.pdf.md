The second pillar is the orchestrator: on [PAGE 1], “The orchestrator tracks the per-realization oracle to within 1.07 dB and improves the normalized mean-square error (NMSE) over the best fixed strategy by up to 3.6 dB at high SNR.” The multi-hop derivation is: pilot LS observations are noisy CFR samples; each estimator imposes a different prior or denoiser; the best prior changes with SNR and spatial correlation; therefore a condition-adaptive selector can beat any fixed prior. The validation split selects \(a^\star(c)\) by validation NMSE, then reports test NMSE, so the selector is genie-free but still condition-aware. The residual gap to the per-realization oracle measures selector headroom, not achieved oracle accuracy.

Gray-zone audit. First, the orchestrator is not magic: it uses condition labels such as channel model, numerology, SNR, and antenna configuration. If those labels are unavailable, stale, or misclassified, the argmin selector can be a counter-trap. The paper’s own per-realization oracle gap of 1.07 dB bounds the damage only under the tested split. Second, the concurrency claim is conditional. On [PAGE 2], the single-realization limit is explicit: data-parallel sharding is impossible for \(N=1\), and by-algorithm partitioning is Amdahl-limited by the CNN. The paper states “for our bank s ≈0.97, so concurrency yields only ≈1.03× over sequential execution.” That is not a contradiction of the up-to-6.9× data-parallel result; it is a different axis. Third, the Doppler unit statement on [PAGE 2], “sweeping the maximum Doppler fd from 0 to 1400 Hz (equivalently 0–120 km/h at 3.5 GHz),” is dimensionally inconsistent. Using \(f_{d,\max}=v f_c/c\), at \(f_c=3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\), \(120\,\mathrm{km/h}=33.33\,\mathrm{m/s}\) gives about \(389.2\,\mathrm{Hz}\), while \(1400\,\mathrm{Hz}\) gives about \(431.7\,\mathrm{km/h}\). This is a physical-layer veto on that equivalence, not on the whole mobility study. Fourth, the LMMSE formulas are safe only under positive noise loading and valid covariance assumptions. If \(\sigma^2\to 0\) and the pilot covariance is rank-deficient, the inverse becomes ill-conditioned; rank-truncated SVD and robust covariance assumptions are mitigations, not universal cures. Fifth, the CNN saturation and DFT floor are model-class breakdowns: DFT floors near \(-12.6\,\mathrm{dB}\) because of off-grid spectral leakage [PAGE 3], and the CNN saturates near \(-16\,\mathrm{dB}\) at high SNR because its training loss is dominated by the noisy regime [PAGE 3]. Sixth, cross-layer claims about weather or biomechanics are not supported by the source. Large-scale pathloss and shadowing are disabled and links are power-normalized [PAGE 2], so weather cannot be inferred from these experiments. Biomechanical variables are absent entirely and have no units or model in the paper; any such claim is unfalsifiable here. The audit therefore accepts the estimator-ranking and orchestration claims within the simulated 3GPP/Sionna grid, but rejects overgeneralization to live deployments, unlabeled conditions, weather causation, or single-realization latency scaling.

Core signal model on [PAGE 1]:
\[
\mathbf y=\mathbf H\mathbf x+\mathbf n,\qquad \mathbf n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma^2\mathbf I),
\]
with \(\mathbf H=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf h)\). After power normalization, \(\mathbf h\) is the normalized CFR, \(\mathbf x\) carries unit-modulus pilots, and \(\sigma^2\) is normalized noise power. The paper defines \(\mathrm{SNR}=1/\sigma^2\), dimensionless. The pilot LS observation is
\[
\hat{\mathbf h}^{LS}_{\mathcal P}=\mathbf h_{\mathcal P}+\tilde{\mathbf n}_{\mathcal P},
\]
where \(\mathcal P\subset\{0,\dots,N-1\}\) is the comb pilot set, \(|\mathcal P|=N_p\). The paper uses \(N=128\), 5G-NR SCS \(30\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(f_c=3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\) with comb-4 pilots \(N_p=32\), and LTE SCS \(15\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(f_c=2.1\,\mathrm{GHz}\) with comb-8 pilots \(N_p=16\) [PAGE 2].

The NMSE metric on [PAGE 2] is
\[
\mathrm{NMSE}
=
\frac{\mathbb E\!\left[\|\hat{\mathbf h}-\mathbf h\|_2^2\right]}
{\mathbb E\!\left[\|\mathbf h\|_2^2\right]},
\]
dimensionless; reported as \(10\log_{10}(\mathrm{NMSE})\) in dB. The LMMSE operator on [PAGE 2] is
\[
\hat{\mathbf h}
=
\mathbf R_{a\mathcal P}
\left(\mathbf R_{\mathcal P\mathcal P}+\sigma^2\mathbf I\right)^{-1}
\hat{\mathbf h}^{LS}_{\mathcal P},
\]
where \(\mathbf R_{\mathcal P\mathcal P}\) is the pilot covariance and \(\mathbf R_{a\mathcal P}\) is the cross-covariance between target CFR and pilots. Units are normalized power for covariance entries. The inverse is well-defined when \(\sigma^2>0\) and \(\mathbf R_{\mathcal P\mathcal P}\) is positive semidefinite; it becomes ill-conditioned as \(\sigma^2\to 0\) with rank-deficient pilot covariance. SVD-LMMSE restricts to the dominant rank \(r\); full Freq-LMMSE estimates \(\mathbf R=\mathbb E[\mathbf h\mathbf h^H]\); SpaceFreq-LMMSE stacks \(L=N_rN_t=16\) spatial links into a length-\(LN\) vector and uses a joint covariance \(\mathbf R\in\mathbb C^{LN\times LN}\) [PAGE 2]. The joint covariance estimate can be rank-limited by the number of snapshots; that is exactly the high-SNR mismatch mechanism the paper invokes.

The orchestrator selection on [PAGE 2] is
\[
a^\star(c)=\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A}\mathrm{NMSE}_{\mathrm{val}}(a,c),
\]
with \(|\mathcal A|=8\), and the reported score is \(\mathrm{NMSE}_{\mathrm{test}}(a^\star(c),c)\). The selector uses no genie knowledge, only the SNR and scenario label available at the receiver. A per-realization oracle selects the best agent for each drop; the reported gap between orchestrator and oracle is \(1.07\,\mathrm{dB}\) [PAGE 3]. The Amdahl operator on [PAGE 2] is
\[
S_K=\frac{1}{s+(1-s)/K},\qquad S_\infty=\frac{1}{s},
\]
where \(s=t_{\mathrm{CNN}}/\sum_{a}t_a\) is dimensionless and \(S_K\) is dimensionless speedup. With \(s\approx0.97\), \(S_\infty\approx1.031\). This is the exact breakdown regime for by-algorithm partitioning when the CNN dominates. The data-parallel result is a different partition: [PAGE 4] reports “data-parallel partitioning scales from 152 s to 22 s at K = 8 (6.9×, near the ideal 1/K).” Wall-clock units are seconds; speedup is dimensionless.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

[PAGE 1, PAGE 6]; DFT denoising descends from Zhao and Huang [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]; robust LMMSE from Li, Cimini, and Sollenberger [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]; SVD-LMMSE and frequency-domain LMMSE from Edfors et al. [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]; joint space-frequency LMMSE for MIMO from Barhumi, Leus, and Moonen and from Biguesh and Gershman [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]; the CNN denoiser from Soltani et al. [PAGE 1, PAGE 6]. The simulation substrate is Sionna [PAGE 1, PAGE 6] over 3GPP TR 38.901 UMa, UMi, and RMa [PAGE 2, PAGE 6]. Retrocausally, the modern deployment target — 5G-NR and LTE coexistence, 8×2 MIMO, low-latency receivers, and heterogeneous propagation — reaches backward and revalues older estimators. LS is no longer merely a low-complexity baseline; it is a moderate-SNR competitor within 1–2 dB of LMMSE in some regimes [PAGE 3]. DFT denoising is no longer a safe default because its spectral-leakage floor is visible [PAGE 3]. The CNN is no longer an automatic high-SNR winner because it saturates [PAGE 3]. The older statistical estimators become regime specialists under a modern orchestrator.

Multi-week traps in this genealogy are reproducible. Trap one: treating the historical “best estimator” as stationary across SNR, numerology, and antenna geometry; the win-count table refutes that [PAGE 3]. Trap two: validation leakage. If the same realizations choose \(a^\star(c)\) and report NMSE, the 1.07 dB oracle gap is meaningless; the paper’s split is validation half and test half [PAGE 2]. Trap three: covariance-model mismatch. A robust-LMMSE tuned to a uniform PDP can degrade under mismatch, and a joint MIMO covariance can lose at high SNR once noise is small [PAGE 3]. Trap four: latency mismeasurement. By-algorithm partitioning looks parallel but is Amdahl-locked by the CNN, so a multi-week benchmark that only reports worker count can hide the single-realization ceiling [PAGE 2, PAGE 4]. Trap five: unit drift. The Doppler equivalence on [PAGE 2] is a concrete example where a plausible phrase can propagate for weeks unless checked against \(f_{d,\max}=v f_c/c\). Trap six: over-extending simulation. The source disables large-scale pathloss and shadowing and power-normalizes each link [PAGE 2], so weather or deployment-loss claims cannot be retrofitted without a new model.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind here means the receiver must model not only the channel but the competence of each estimator agent under the current condition, and must model its own selector’s uncertainty. The paper’s argmin selector is a first-order policy: it believes the validation-best agent is test-best for condition \(c\). The per-realization oracle is the counterfactual “what would have been best for this drop,” and the 1.07 dB gap is the price of not knowing the drop-specific winner [PAGE 3].

Crisis habit regression: under stress, a receiver defaults to a fixed habit. The paper’s clean example is a system frozen on the low-SNR champion SpaceFreq-LMMSE. At high SNR, “a system frozen on the low-SNR champion SpaceFreq-LMMSE loses up to 3.6 dB (on RMa)” [PAGE 3]. The habit is rational in the low-SNR regime but regressive in the high-SNR regime. The orchestrator avoids the habit by following the winner, but it can only do so if the condition label and validation statistics remain representative.

Counter-trap: the opposite failure is meta-overfitting the selector. If the validation half is small, nonstationary, or mislabeled, \(a^\star(c)\) can be a validation artifact. The selector then “knows” the wrong agent. The paper’s genie-free design is necessary but not sufficient; it upper-bounds any fixed strategy only by construction on the validation split, and the test report is the check. A counter-trap also appears in concurrency: optimizing for best-of-eight accuracy can obscure the single-realization latency floor. The honest policy must distinguish multi-realization data-parallel throughput from one-drop makespan.

Clock: there are two clocks. The propagation clock is Doppler. As \(f_d\) grows, inter-carrier interference from intra-symbol time variation dominates, and all estimators converge to poor NMSE, about \(+2\,\mathrm{dB}\) at \(f_d=1400\,\mathrm{Hz}\) [PAGE 3]. The compute clock is wall-clock. A single agent runs all eight estimators sequentially in 145 s, with the CNN alone at 140 s [PAGE 4]. In the single-realization limit, data-parallel sharding is impossible, and by-algorithm partitioning is Amdahl-limited with \(s\approx0.97\), yielding only about \(1.03\times\) [PAGE 2]. The recursive policy should therefore be: if many drops are available, use data-parallel orchestration for throughput; if one drop is available, orchestration buys accuracy at max-agent latency, not speed. The clock veto overrides any claim that worker count alone solves single-drop latency.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto: active. The paper’s physical model is OFDM with \(N=128\), 5G-NR SCS \(30\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\), LTE SCS \(15\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(2.1\,\mathrm{GHz}\), and 8×2 MIMO with \(L=16\) links [PAGE 2]. Any claimed estimator ranking that violates these constraints is vetoed. The Doppler equivalence on [PAGE 2] is the clearest physical veto: “sweeping the maximum Doppler fd from 0 to 1400 Hz (equivalently 0–120 km/h at 3.5 GHz).” The physical relation is
\[
f_{d,\max}=\frac{v f_c}{c}.
\]
At \(f_c=3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\), \(v=120\,\mathrm{km/h}=33.33\,\mathrm{m/s}\) gives \(f_{d,\max}\approx389.2\,\mathrm{Hz}\). Conversely, \(1400\,\mathrm{Hz}\) at \(3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\) gives \(v\approx431.7\,\mathrm{km/h}\). Therefore the parenthetical equivalence is physically inconsistent by roughly a factor of 3.6. The mobility sweep itself can still be valid as a Doppler sweep; the “120 km/h” label cannot be attached to \(1400\,\mathrm{Hz}\) at \(3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\) without correction. This veto does not invalidate the NMSE-versus-Doppler trend; it invalidates the unit conversion.

Biomechanical veto: null and rejected. The source contains no biomechanical variables, sensors, subject data, gait, load, fatigue, or tissue mechanics. There is no unit system for biomechanics in the paper. Any biomechanical explanation of estimator selection would be unfalsifiable with this source and is vetoed as unsupported. The receiver is a signal-processing block, not a biomechanical system.

Weather veto: indirect and unsupported. Weather can affect wireless propagation through rain attenuation, atmospheric absorption, wind-induced motion, or shadowing, but this simulation disables large-scale pathloss and shadowing and power-normalizes each link. The exact constraint on [PAGE 2] is: “Large-scale pathloss and shadowing are disabled and each link is power-normalized so that the SNR = 1/σ2 is well defined.” Given that, weather cannot be inferred as a causal layer in these experiments. Wind could only enter via Doppler or time variation if explicitly mapped into \(f_d\), but the paper’s mobility study uses a Doppler sweep, not weather measurements. A weather claim would need a new channel model with weather parameters and a new validation split. Until then, weather is vetoed as an explanatory variable.

Cross-layer adjudication summary: the physical layer veto is active and specific; the biomechanical layer is absent and cannot veto or explain; the weather layer is not simulated and cannot be used to rescale or reinterpret the reported NMSE. The valid claim space is the 3GPP/Sionna simulation grid, with the Doppler label corrected.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1: Doppler-equivalence invariant. A run or report claims that \(f_d=1400\,\mathrm{Hz}\) at \(f_c=3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\) is equivalent to \(120\,\mathrm{km/h}\). Verifiable check: compute \(v=f_d c/f_c\). Prediction: the correct speed is about \(431.7\,\mathrm{km/h}\), not \(120\,\mathrm{km/h}\); the correct Doppler for \(120\,\mathrm{km/h}\) at \(3.5\,\mathrm{GHz}\) is about \(389.2\,\mathrm{Hz}\). Veto the equivalence, retain the Doppler sweep. This is directly traceable to [PAGE 2] and the physical relation \(f_{d,\max}=v f_c/c\). If a code path uses a speed-to-Doppler conversion, the test should fail unless the carrier frequency or speed is changed.

Veto scenario 2: fixed-strategy superiority claim at high SNR. A report claims a single fixed agent, especially the low-SNR champion SpaceFreq-LMMSE, is best across the whole MIMO grid. Verifiable check: inspect the high-SNR slice \(\ge 15\,\mathrm{dB}\) and the RMa condition. Prediction: the orchestrator should follow Freq-LMMSE at high SNR and beat the frozen SpaceFreq-LMMSE by up to \(3.6\,\mathrm{dB}\) on RMa, while the whole-grid average is dominated by low-SNR magnitudes [PAGE 3]. If the fixed agent still wins on the high-SNR slice, check for validation/test leakage, condition-label mismatch, or a different SNR grid. This veto is grounded in the paper’s explicit regime-switch statement on [PAGE 3] and the win-count split on [PAGE 3].

Veto scenario 3: single-realization concurrency claim. A report claims data-parallel orchestration gives near-\(1/K\) speedup for one channel realization, or claims by-algorithm partitioning gives large speedup for the estimator bank. Verifiable check: set \(N=1\), then run by-algorithm and data-parallel partitions. Prediction: data-parallel sharding is impossible for \(N=1\); by-algorithm partitioning is Amdahl-limited by the CNN with \(s\approx0.97\), so speedup is at most about \(1.031\times\). The paper’s numbers are consistent: SISO sequential \(89.8\,\mathrm{s}\), by-algorithm \(90.3\,\mathrm{s}\) at \(K=7\) [PAGE 3]; MIMO sequential \(145\,\mathrm{s}\), by-algorithm about \(150\,\mathrm{s}\) at all \(K\), dominated by CNN \(140\,\mathrm{s}\) [PAGE 4]. Veto any single-drop claim of \(6.9\times\); that number belongs to data-parallel multi-realization throughput, not single-realization latency. This is a verifiable RLVR counterfactual because the partition, \(N\), \(K\), and wall-clock are directly checkable.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
