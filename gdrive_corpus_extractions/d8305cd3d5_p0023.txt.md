<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when search quality metrics decline after debiasing is deployed. Engineers may respond by regressing to the pre-debiasing habit of trusting raw position and raw IR scores, effectively setting the bias adjustment to zero and restoring the original confounded ranking. This is a habit regression because it abandons the counterfactual correction in favor of the familiar, biased equilibrium.

The counter-trap is that debiasing itself changes user behavior. If the engine successfully demotes high-bias, low-relevance results and promotes relevant results previously suppressed by position effects, users learn to examine lower positions more carefully. The examination function implicit in $M_b$ [PAGE 21, Claim 12] was estimated under the old user policy; under the new policy, users click differently at the same rank. The model’s presentation-bias parameters become stale, and the ratio adjustment over-corrects or under-corrects. The system thus creates a trap in which successful debiasing invalidates the bias model that enabled it.

The clock variable is the retraining cadence relative to the behavioral equilibration timescale. If the model is retrained weekly but user examination habits adapt within days, the system operates on a stale bias surface for most of each cycle. If retraining is faster than behavioral adaptation, the ratio updates may chase transient click noise. The claims specify no retraining interval, no drift threshold, and no clock synchronization between model updates and user-policy shifts, leaving the recursion unstable at unspecified timescales.
