<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The “crisis habit regression” is the context-only CRPS model’s fallback under high uncertainty: it keeps reducing training loss while validation nCRPS rises at 3-day and 5-day horizons [PAGE 4]. The paper’s diagnosis is that context-only training “can become harder as forecast uncertainty grows” [PAGE 1] and that “context-only CRPS training can struggle at longer forecast horizons” [PAGE 4]. The habit is endpoint-only learning: the model learns only \(p_\theta(X\mid c)\), with no auxiliary view of the same future. The regression shows up as overfitting and as SSR moving away from 1; the paper notes that for CRPS and endpoint-only training, validation nCRPS increases after an initial improvement “while SSR declines substantially below 1” [PAGE 20]. This is a recursive failure: the model’s own training signal reinforces a representation that does not generalize when its information state is impoverished.

The “counter-trap” is the auxiliary variable itself. If the model is trained with \(X_t\) at \(t<1\), it receives partial future information. If that information were also available at inference, the model would become an oracle and the one-step forecasting claim would be invalid. The paper’s defense is structural: inference is at the fully corrupted endpoint \(t=1\), where \(X_1=\varepsilon\perp(c,X)\), so “the corrupted future becomes uninformative at the fully corrupted endpoint” [PAGE 2]. The endpoint-only ablation confirms that \(t_{\min}=1\) closely matches CRPS [PAGE 20]. The gray-zone is that if the learned predictive distribution depends on \(\varepsilon\), the endpoint-only and standard CRPS objectives “need not coincide” [PAGE 15]; population marginals still coincide, but finite-sample behavior can differ. A counter-trap test is therefore to shuffle or refresh \(\varepsilon\) at \(t=1\): if predictions change systematically, the model has learned an illicit dependence on corruption noise, and the one-step interpretation is vetoed.

The “clock” is the pair \((t,\tau)\): \(t\in[0,1]\) is the corruption clock, and \(\tau\) is forecast lead time. Training integrates over \(t\); inference fixes \(t=1\). NFE is a third clock: one forward evaluation at \(t=1\) for the main results, with additional NFE used only as a refinement test [PAGE 7, PAGE 15]. The paper observes that gains from additional NFE are present only early in the 12 h task but persist in the 3 d task, “where learning an accurate one-step predictor may be more difficult and iterative refinement may improve the approximation” [PAGE 8]. Recursive theory of mind here means the predictor must represent its own information state: during training it sees \((c,X_t,t)\); during inference it sees only \((c,\varepsilon,1,\zeta)\). The shared parameters must transfer the context–future relation without confusing the training-time partial future with the inference-time absence of future information. That is the correct counter-trap discipline, and it is what the endpoint-only and shuffle tests verify.

The paper does this: training uses \(M=2\), evaluation uses \(M=50\), and the high-dimensional study uses 84 forecast channels [PAGE 19, PAGE 25]. The fair estimator’s \(M(M-1)\) denominator is a hard singularity at \(M=1\); any reported “fair CRPS” with \(M=1\) is invalid. The normalization convention must also be declared, because training and validation nCRPS can use different normalizations [PAGE 19]. Under these conditions the statistical evidence passes: MOSAIC-DDM wins 787/840 channel–lead pairs (93.7%), U-Cast-DDM wins 82.0%, and SSR shifts toward 1 [PAGE 9].

**Weather layer.** The weather-layer vetoes are distribution shift, calibration, and extreme-event track. On ERA5→HRES-fc0 shift, CRPS-trained MOSAIC degrades by \(+3.10\%\) nCRPS and DDM by \(+2.60\%\); SSR deviation is \(+0.017\) versus \(+0.007\) [PAGE 10, Table 4]. The paper reports: “DDM shows smaller average increases in nCRPS, nRMSE, and mean SSR deviation” [PAGE 10]. For Hurricane Ian, DDM places more members near the IBTrACS center at 200 km: MOSAIC 47/50 (94%) versus 31/50 (62%), and U-Cast 23/50 (46%) versus 18/50 (36%) [PAGE 32, Table 18]. But Hurricane Michael is a validation-period case [PAGE 31], so it cannot serve as an out-of-sample extreme-weather proof. The weather-layer verdict is therefore conditional: the distribution-shift and Ian evidence support robustness; the Michael evidence is illustrative only.

**Biomechanical layer.** There is no biomechanical variable, model, or measurement in the source. No biomechanical claim can be admitted. If a downstream claim tries to transfer the DDM result to biomechanics, it is vetoed by absence of evidence, absence of units, and absence of a validated forward operator. The cross-layer proof is thus: statistical layer passes under matched fair-CRPS conditions; physical layer passes only as a statistical forecast claim, not as a conservation-law claim; weather layer passes conditionally with the stated distribution-shift, calibration, and cyclone caveats; biomechanical layer is non-applicable and therefore vetoed for overreach.
