<THINK_TRACE>

**Hop 1 — from a physical phenomenon to an optimisation oracle.** The source opens by replacing an analytic objective with the water column itself: "In the original algorithm, the scalar ﬁeld is given by a function. In the modiﬁed version, the scalar ﬁeld is given by the phenomenon itself." [PAGE 1]. This is not a cosmetic substitution. A function $f:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ is queried pointwise and exactly; a phenomenon is queried by a CTD package mounted on a moving hull, so every "evaluation" is a space-time averaged, delayed, noisy scalar. Let the true field be $f(\mathbf{x},t)$ in $^\circ$C and the reported value be $\hat f = \frac{1}{|W|}\int_{W} f(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\xi}, t)\,d\boldsymbol{\xi} + \eta$, with $W$ the sensor's averaging window and $\eta$ the instrument error. Every subsequent step of the simplex iteration — the ranking $f(A_i)\le f(B_i)\le f(C_i)$, the descent cone, and the termination certificate — is computed from $\hat f$, not $f$. The paper never propagates this discrepancy. That single omission is the root of most of the gray zones below.

**Hop 2 — the descent cone and the half-angle collapse.** Step 1 defines "the cone $K(z_i)$ with apex at $z_i$ of all the unit vectors from $C_i$ to $z_i + \delta \times B$, where $\delta$ is a parameter and $B$ the unit ball" [PAGE 7]. Reading this literally as a tangent cone: let $\ell_i=\|z_i-C_i\|$ $[m]$ and take $\delta$ $[m]$ as the ball radius. The directions generated are $(\ell_i\hat u+\delta b)/\|\ell_i\hat u+\delta b\|$ for $\|b\|\le1$. The tangent-cone half-angle satisfies $\sin\alpha_i=\delta/\ell_i$. Hence (i) for $\delta\ll\ell_i$ the cone is a narrow pencil about the $C_i\!\to\! z_i$ ray; (ii) as $\delta\to\ell_i$, $\alpha_i\to90^\circ$; and (iii) **at $\ell_i\le\delta$ the apex $C_i$ lies inside the generating ball, so $K(z_i)$ degenerates to the entire sphere $S^2$.** A full sphere of "feasible directions" makes Step 3 vacuous and destroys the algorithm's directed character. This is a hard singularity, not a tuning issue, and the paper gives $\delta$ as "a parameter" with no constraint tying it to $\ell_i$.

**Hop 3 — the termination test is necessary, not sufficient.** Step 4 stops when "for any direction $w$ such that $\langle w,v\rangle\ge0$, the directional derivative of $f$ along $w$ is non-negative, i.e., $f'(z_i;w)\ge0$" [PAGE 7]. Audit this on smooth $f$ with $f'(z;w)=\langle\nabla f(z),w\rangle$. The test set $H_v=\{w:\langle w,v\rangle\ge0\}$ is a closed hemisphere. If $\langle\nabla f,v\rangle<0$ (i.e. $v$ is a genuine descent direction), then $-\nabla f/\|\nabla f\|\in H_v$, and the test evaluates $\langle\nabla f,-\nabla f/\|\nabla f\|\rangle=-\|\nabla f\|\ge0$, forcing $\nabla f=0$ — correct. **But if $\langle\nabla f(z_i),v\rangle>0$ — $v$ is an ascent direction — then $-\nabla f/\|\nabla f\|\notin H_v$, the hemisphere contains only directions with non-negative slope, and the test passes with $\|\nabla f(z_i)\|>0$.** The certificate is therefore contingent on the arbitrary choice made in Step 3 ("Select one vector $v$ from $K(z_i)$" [PAGE 7]), and the paper's conclusion — "the point of minimum is located within the triangle with vertices $A_i$, $B_i$, and $C_i$" — can be asserted at a non-stationary point. Near a saddle whose axis aligns with the $C_i\to z_i$ ray, this is the generic outcome.

**Hop 4 — 3 ranges in $\mathbb{R}^3$ do not determine 3 coordinates.** The paper states "To calculate its absolute position the AUV needs to receive pings from at least three sources – vehicles from LPS" [PAGE 2]. Three spheres in $\mathbb{R}^3$ intersect in at most two points, mirror images across the plane of the three emitters; a fourth range (or an explicit side constraint) is required for uniqueness. The side constraint is physically available — LPS surface vehicles float, so the AUV is below their plane — but it is nowhere in the formalisation, and Eq. 4.2 constrains only pairwise distances $d_1\le d\le d_2$, admitting **collinear triples of zero area**, for which the position solution is a circle, not a point.

**Hop 5 — from $P(t)$ to a design inequality.** $P(t)$ is the set of points within transponder range of at least three LPS vehicles [Eq. 4.1, PAGE 8]. For a fixed triple with circumradius $R$ (in the emitters' plane) and transponder range $r_t$, the triple intersection contains a ball of radius $\rho=\sqrt{r_t^2-R^2}$ about the circumcentre. Requiring $C_S(t)\subset P(t)$ [Eq. 4.8, PAGE 11] to hold for a search footprint of diameter $D_S$ therefore reduces, via Jung's theorem in $\mathbb{R}^3$ ($\rho_S\le D_S\sqrt{6}/4\approx0.612\,D_S$), to $R_{\max}\le\sqrt{r_t^2-(0.612\,D_S)^2}$ and hence the mission-level feasibility bound $D_S<r_t/0.612\approx1.63\,r_t$. Beyond that ratio **no LPS geometry can certify the invariance**, regardless of controller quality. That is a structural veto the paper's formulation hides.

**Hop 6 — the "+1" and the exactness trap.** Eq. 4.19 sets $\phi_{LPS}=d_{W_I^S(t)}(J(x_{LPS}(t)))+1$ and Eq. 4.20 requires $\phi_{LPS}\le1$ [PAGE 13–14]. Algebraically this is $d\le0$, i.e. $J(x_{LPS}(t))\subseteq W_I^S(t)$ **with zero margin**. It also adds a dimensionless $1$ to a metric distance — a unit inconsistency that only "works" because the threshold tautologically removes the offset. With LPS position error of standard deviation $\sigma_p$ $[m]$, a measured inclusion event is compatible with a true separation on the order of $\sigma_p$; the constraint is therefore a hard test on a noisy quantity, giving a false-inclusion probability $\to1/2$ as the true margin $\to0$.

**Gray-zone audit summary.** Four regimes are load-bearing and unaddressed: (a) $\ell_i\le\delta$ → cone collapses to $S^2$; (b) $\langle\nabla f(z_i),v\rangle>0$ → false minimum certificate; (c) collinear LPS triple with all three ranges satisfied → non-unique fix; (d) $\sigma_p>0$ with zero-margin invariance → deterministic test on stochastic data. The paper's own framing licenses this critique: "In the absence of models of the world that are based on the principles of physics, we have to write speciﬁcations, and their implementations, as algorithms" [PAGE 4]. The algorithm layer is explicitly a **substitute** for a physics model, and every regime above is a place where the substitute silently diverges from the physics.

**Domain and units.** State $X\in\mathbb{R}^3$ [m]; ranges $r_r,r_t,r_a$ [m]; field $f$ [°C]; directional derivative $f'(z;w)$ [°C m$^{-1}$]; cone elements are unit vectors (dimensionless); times $t_i$, $T_k^*(i)$ [s]; value functions and constraint maps $\phi_i$ dimensionless on the scale fixed by the threshold $1$.

**Service region (Eq. 4.1, [PAGE 8]).**
$$P(t)=\Big\{X\in\mathbb{R}^3:\ \exists\, i,j,k,\ k\neq i,\ i\neq j,\ j\neq k,\ d(X_{{LPS}_j},X)\le r_t,\ d(X_{{LPS}_i},X)\le r_t,\ d(X_{{LPS}_k},X)\le r_t\Big\}$$
Assumption: $d$ is the Euclidean chord. Breakdown: under a non-isovelocity sound-speed profile $c(z)$, travel time is $\tau=\int_\Gamma ds/c(z)$ along a refracted ray $\Gamma$, and the chord underestimates acoustic path length by $\beta\ge0$; substitute $r_t^{\text{eff}}=r_t-\beta$.

**Service constraint (Eq. 4.2, [PAGE 8]), quoted as "a relaxed version of this requirement" [PAGE 8]).**
$$\exists\, i,j,k\in LPS:\ (j\neq i)\wedge(i\neq k)\wedge(k\neq j)\wedge\big(d_1\le d(X_{{LPS}_i},X_{{LPS}_j})\le d_2\big)\wedge\big(d_1\le d(X_{{LPS}_i},X_{{LPS}_k})\le d_2\big)\wedge\big(d_1\le d(X_{{LPS}_k},X_{{LPS}_j})\le d_2\big)$$
Missing invariant: non-degeneracy. Add $\mathrm{Area}(\triangle_{ijk})\ge A_{\min}$ or $\min$ altitude $h_{\min}>0$. Without it, collinear triples satisfy 4.2 with zero enclosed area.

**Structural constraint (Eq. 4.3, [PAGE 9]).** "$The\ graph\ T\ is\ connected$" — vertices = LPS vehicles, edges iff $d<r_r$. Team constraint (Eq. 4.4): graph $K$ connected with edges iff $d<r_a$. Reported precedence: "The structural constraints have precedence over the service constraints" [PAGE 8] — a lexicographic, not weighted, ordering.

**Coordination (Eqs. 4.5–4.6, [PAGE 9]).** $\forall i\in S:\ X_{S_i}(t)\in P(t)$; and cross-team link condition $\min_j d(X_{{LPS}_j}(t),X_{S_i}(t))\le r_a$. Note the source prints the latter with an unbalanced parenthesis — $\min\ d(X_{{LPS}_j}(t)),X_{S_i}(t))$ — an editorial defect, not a mathematical one.

**Descent cone (Step 1, [PAGE 7]).** With $\ell_i=\|z_i-C_i\|$, $\delta$ [m]:
$$K(z_i)=\Big\{\tfrac{z_i+\delta b-C_i}{\|z_i+\delta b-C_i\|}:\ b\in B,\ \|b\|\le1\Big\},\qquad \sin\alpha_i=\delta/\ell_i .$$
Breakdown: $\ell_i\le\delta\Rightarrow K(z_i)=S^2$ (full sphere, no directionality); $\ell_i=0$ (i.e. $C_i=z_i$) $\Rightarrow$ undefined.

**Termination predicate (Step 4, [PAGE 7]).**
$$\text{Stop}(z_i,v)\iff f'(z_i;w)\ge0\ \ \forall w\in H_v:=\{w:\langle w,v\rangle\ge0\}.$$
Proposition (audit): for $f\in C^1$, $\text{Stop}(z_i,v)$ with $\langle\nabla f(z_i),v\rangle<0$ implies $\nabla f(z_i)=0$; with $\langle\nabla f(z_i),v\rangle>0$ it is satisfied whenever $\nabla f(z_i)$ has non-negative inner product with every element of $H_v$ — in particular at points with $\|\nabla f(z_i)\|>0$. The certificate is selection-dependent.

**Line search (Step 4, [PAGE 7]).** $A_{i+1}=\arg\min_{\lambda\ge0} f(z_i+\lambda v)$; requires coercivity of $f$ along the ray. Breakdown: unbounded ray $\Rightarrow$ no minimiser; the claimed chain $f(A_{i+1})\le f(A_i)\le f(B_i)\le f(C_i)$ then fails.

**Leader selection (Eq. 4.7, [PAGE 11]).**
$$\text{leader}(i)=\arg\min_{k}\big\{T_k^*(i):\ X_{S_k}(T_k^*(i))\in B_\delta(z_i)\big\},\qquad \dot X_{S_k}(T_k^*(i))\in K(z_i)-\{z_i\}.$$
Breakdown regimes: (i) empty feasible set (no vehicle can reach $B_\delta(z_i)$ within the horizon) $\Rightarrow$ $\arg\min$ undefined; (ii) exact ties in $T_k^*$ $\Rightarrow$ non-unique leader, no tie-break rule stated; (iii) $T_k^*$ is the value of a reachability HJB problem and is therefore only computable to grid tolerance, so (ii) occurs with nonzero probability under numerical evaluation.

**Invariance machinery (Eqs. 4.11–4.13, [PAGE 12–13]).** Constraint families $\phi_i(t,x_{LPS}(t))\le1$, $\phi_i^0(x_{LPS}(0))\le1$;
$$V(\tau,z)=\min_{u_{LPS}(t_0,\tau)}\Big\{\min\Big\{\max\big\{\max_{t_0\le t\le\tau}\phi_1(t,x(t)),\ \phi_1^0(x(t_0))\big\},\dots,\max\big\{\max_{t_0\le t\le\tau}\phi_I(t,x(t)),\ \phi_I^0(x(t_0))\big\}\Big\}\ \Big|\ x(\tau)=z\Big\}$$
$$W_I^{LPS}(t)=\{z:\ V(t,z)\le1\}.$$
"[T]his is a problem of weak invariance since we are interested in the existence of at least one control law" [PAGE 12]; strong invariance follows by replacing the inner minimisation with a maximisation. Singularity: $V$ is the viscosity solution of a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation and is Lipschitz only under boundedness of the dynamics on the constraint set; the level set $\{V\le1\}$ is closed but need not have interior, so "largest invariant set" may be measure-zero at the boundary of feasibility.

**Inter-team coupling (Eqs. 4.16–4.20, [PAGE 13–14]).** $h:X\to\mathbb{R}^3$; extended $\bar h(x)=h(x)\times\mathbb{R}^{k-3}$;
$$d_A(B):=\max\{d_A(x):x\in B\},\qquad \phi_{LPS}(t,x_{LPS}(t))=d_{W_I^S(t)}\big(J(x_{LPS}(t))\big)+1\le1.$$
Two defects. **Dimensional:** a metric distance plus a bare $1$ requires the "$1$" to be in metres, whereas the $\le1$ threshold requires dimensionless $\phi$; the correct form is $\phi_{LPS}=d_{W_I^S(t)}(J(x_{LPS}(t)))/L$ with characteristic length $L$ [m], $L$ nowhere defined. **Topological:** $d_A(B)$ compares sets living in $\mathbb{R}^{k\times n_{LPS}(t)}$ and $\mathbb{R}^{k\times n_S(t)}$; the extension $\bar h$ addresses the per-vehicle dimension but the cardinality mismatch $n_{LPS}(t)\neq n_S(t)$ is left implicit, so the directed distance is only well-defined under an identification map that the paper does not supply. Zero-margin consequence: $\phi_{LPS}\le1\iff d=0\iff J(x_{LPS}(t))\subseteq W_I^S(t)$ exactly — no tolerance for the LPS ranging error $\sigma_p$.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The construction descends from the simplex lineage initiated by Spendley, Hext and Himsworth (1962) and Nelder–Mead (1965): maintain $n+1$ vertices, rank by objective value, move the worst vertex. The paper's Step 5 — "Rename $A_i$ and $B_i$ as $B_{i+1}$ and $C_{i+1}$" [PAGE 7] — is a re-labelling rule, and the move (minimise along a ray from the midpoint $z_i$ of the *better* edge) is **not** the Nelder–Mead reflection/expansion/contraction family. This matters retrocausally: the 1965 convergence proofs and the later McKinnon (1998) counterexamples on non-smooth or degenerate simplices do not transfer, and the paper offers no replacement, writing only "Repeat until finding minimum" [PAGE 7]. The Klein–Mangasarian/McKinnon literature on simplex stagnation is precisely the trap a reader will fall into by assuming inherited guarantees.

The invariance layer descends from three distinct lineages that the paper fuses: viability theory (Aubin, *Viability theory*, 1991, reference [1]) for the "largest invariant set" object; Krasovskii's control-under-lack-of-information programme (reference [11]) and Kurzhanski's ellipsoidal calculus ([12], [13]) for the value-function and level-set machinery invoked at Eq. 4.13; and Bardi–Capuzzo-Dolcetta ([2]) plus Capuzzo-Dolcetta–Evans ([3]) for the viscosity/HJB and switching apparatus flagged in the conclusion. Retrocausally, this creates a **misattribution trap**: Eq. 4.13 is asserted with "following the arguments from [13]" [PAGE 13], but Kurzhanski's ellipsoidal results deliver tractable *outer approximations* of reachable and invariant sets, not exact level sets of a max-of-max value function over a disjunction of $I$ constraint families. A reader who back-propagates "ellipsoidal calculus ⟹ computable $W_I^{LPS}$" will over-claim tractability.

The architectural lineage is Moriconi–Qian–Riemenschneider on "Correct architecture refinement" ([14]), cited at [PAGE 5] for the claim that refinement needs semantic, not syntactic, checks. The mission specification is thus a *refinement obligation* — yet no refinement relation is ever exhibited, and the conclusion concedes this: "Future work involves defining refinement relations to map the specifications onto problem formulations" [PAGE 14].

**Multi-week traps.** (1) *The "three sources suffice" trap* — carried forward from terrestrial GPS folklore where a fourth satellite or an altitude prior is always present; in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with pure ranges it fails by mirror symmetry, and the failure is silent because the wrong fix is internally consistent with all three ranges. (2) *The "phenomenon is a function" trap* — the paper's headline substitution invites readers to keep treating $\hat f$ as $f$; every derived quantity (cone, midpoint ranking, stopping test, value function constraint $\phi_i$) inherits sampling bias that compounds over weeks of mission time. (3) *The "invariance is a controller property" trap* — Eq. 4.8 is a controlled-invariance statement about the *shape* of $P(t)$ ("the LPS team controls the motions and shape of the set $P(t)$" [PAGE 11]), but $P(t)$'s shape is fixed by transponder range and vehicle geometry, not by control; control moves the *centres*. Confusing shape authority with centre authority produces controllers that chase an unreachable $P(t)$. (4) *The "leader is whoever is closest" trap* — Eq. 4.7 minimises arrival time to $z_i$, but the team objective includes maintaining graph $K$ (Eq. 4.4); the time-optimal leader can be the one whose sprint breaks the acoustic chain.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Level 0 — vehicle self-model.** Each S vehicle $k$ holds an estimate $\hat X_{S_k}(t)$ whose covariance is driven by the LPS fix geometry. It reasons about its own $T_k^*(i)$ under the control set $U_{S_k}(x)$.

**Level 1 — vehicle models teammates.** To compete for leadership (Step "competition for leadership", [PAGE 10–11]) vehicle $k$ must estimate every rival's $T_j^*(i)$, hence rivals' positions and control sets. Because the competition is simultaneous and the communication graph $K$ is only *connected*, not complete, this estimate is built from multi-hop, latency-corrupted state — so the model is stale by at least the acoustic propagation delay plus one relay hop. The paper supplies no estimator and no staleness bound.

**Level 2 — S team models LPS.** The S team must believe that its own convex closure $C_S(t)$ will remain inside $P(t)$; the LPS team must believe where $C_S(t)$ will be. The interface is explicitly asymmetric: "the state of the team S is known and… S does not cooperate with LPS" [PAGE 12], placing "the coordination and control burden on the vehicles of LPS team" [PAGE 12]. This is a one-sided theory of mind — LPS models S perfectly, S models LPS not at all — which is a modelling choice, not a physical fact, and it is exactly the assumption that fails when the S team's own estimate of $P(t)$ is wrong.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under acoustic dropout, the recursion collapses to the lowest level: each vehicle reverts to the pre-formalised habit embedded in the algorithm — fly at maximum speed toward the last known apex $z_i$ along the last selected $v$. This is a *regression to the algorithmic layer* that the paper itself anticipates: "In the absence of models of the world that are based on the principles of physics, we have to write specifications, and their implementations, as algorithms" [PAGE 4]. The habit is locally rational (it preserves the monotonicity chain $f(A_{i+1})\le f(A_i)$) and globally catastrophic (it ignores Eq. 4.4, so the graph $K$ can disconnect, after which no iteration can be closed). The dangerous property is that the habit *looks like* compliance: the vehicle is still executing the specified motion pattern.

**Counter-trap.** The adversary is not an opponent but the field itself: a mission designer who observes that the team reliably converges can be lured into removing the communication constraint as "conservative". The counter-trap is to keep Eq. 4.4 as a hard structural invariant with lexicographic precedence — "The structural constraints have precedence over the service constraints" [PAGE 8] — because disconnection converts a service degradation into a mission termination. A second counter-trap: the false-termination regime ($\langle\nabla f(z_i),v\rangle>0$) is *more* likely when vehicles are spread out and $\ell_i$ is large, i.e. exactly when the team looks healthiest.

**Clock.** Three asynchronous clocks govern the recursion: (i) the iteration clock $t_i$ of the simplex; (ii) the transponder clock, which "emits regularly, with a known frequency, an acoustic ping encoding the time $t$" [PAGE 2]; (iii) the GPS fix clock available only at the surface. The task is stated in mission time — "find the minimum of the temperature $f$ in a given zone of the ocean in minimal time" [PAGE 9] — but the algorithm's progress is measured in *iterations*. Minimal mission time and minimal iteration count diverge whenever the line search (Step 4) makes large spatial progress per iteration; a controller optimising iteration count will systematically under-run the field. The clock mismatch is the mechanism by which "scalable" [PAGE 7] becomes "slow".

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical veto — refraction and attenuation.** Eq. 4.1 uses the Euclidean chord $d(X_{{LPS}_j},X)\le r_t$. Under a downward-refracting profile (sound speed decreasing with depth, typical below the mixed layer), rays arc downward and the acoustic path exceeds the chord; with an upward-refracting profile a surface duct can extend range beyond $r_t$. Either way $P(t)$ as written is neither an upper nor a lower bound on the true service region. Veto proof: the true service set is $\{X:\tau_j(X)\le r_t/c_{\text{ref}}\}$ with $\tau_j=\int_{\Gamma_j}ds/c(z)$; since $c$ varies by several percent over 100 m, the range error is kilometres at transponder ranges of kilometres. **The physical layer vetoes any claim that Eq. 4.1 is a service guarantee.**

**Biomechanical veto — control-set collapse.** Eqs. 4.9–4.10 write $u_{LPS_i}(t)\in U_{LPS_i}(x_{LPS_i}(t))$ [PAGE 12] but leave $U$ unspecified. For a torpedo-hull AUV, the achievable yaw rate obeys $\dot\psi\le\dot\psi_{\max}(V)$ which *decreases* with forward speed, and the achievable deceleration is bounded by thruster authority and hull drag. The leader pattern demands "move in one of those directions with maximum speed until a maximum is found" [PAGE 10] — simultaneous maximum speed and arbitrary direction change. Veto proof: the reachable-time functional $T_k^*(i)$ in Eq. 4.7 is finite only if the required turn can be executed within the available $\dot\psi_{\max}(V)$; at maximum speed the turn radius $R=V/\dot\psi_{\max}(V)$ grows, so a vehicle can be *closest* to $z_i$ and yet *unable* to enter $K(z_i)$ because penetration requires $\dot X_{S_k}(T_k^*(i))\in K(z_i)-\{z_i\}$ [PAGE 11]. **The constraint set is non-convex in the state-control pair; the argmin over vehicles is then over a set that is empty for the geometrically best candidate.**

**Weather veto — surface station-keeping.** The LPS surface vehicles "are required to operate at the surface to receive the GPS signal" [PAGE 2]. Sea state bounds their station-keeping: above significant wave height $H_s^{\max}$ (set by hull length and recovery/failure modes), the position-hold error grows without bound and the triple geometry that defines $P(t)$ decorrelates. Veto proof: the invariance $C_S(t)\subset P(t)$ requires the *triple* geometry to persist; wave-induced drift changes the circumradius $R$ of the serving triple, and the lens inradius $\rho=\sqrt{r_t^2-R^2}$ collapses to zero as $R\to r_t$. **Weather does not merely degrade service; it annihilates the invariant set $W_I^{LPS}(t)$ by driving $R$ to the transponder range.** No amount of LPS controller authority recovers it, because $R$ is a geometric fact about hulls in waves.

**Adjudication order.** Physics vetoes the service-region definition; biomechanics vetoes the leader-feasibility set; weather vetoes the invariance itself. The paper's own precedence rule — structural over service [PAGE 8] — is the correct adjudication logic but is applied at the wrong layer: structural integrity of the *acoustic graph* should also dominate the *geometric* service constraint, since graph disconnection makes $P(t)$ unknowable to the S team even when $P(t)$ is nonempty.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Veto 1 — false minimum certificate under an ascent direction.**
Construct $f(x,y)=x^2-y^2$ (saddle at origin) on $\Omega=[-1,1]^2$, place $C_i=(0,1)$, $A_i=(-1,0)$, $B_i=(1,0)$, so $z_i=(0,0)$, $\ell_i=1$. Choose $v=(0,1)$ (admissible for small $\delta$: $\sin\alpha=\delta/\ell_i$). Then $\nabla f(z_i)=0$ — use instead $C_i=(0,1)$, $A_i=(-1,\varepsilon)$, $B_i=(1,\varepsilon)$ so $z_i=(0,\varepsilon)$ and $\nabla f(z_i)=(0,-2\varepsilon)$, with $v$ pointing from $C_i$ to $z_i$, i.e. $v\approx(0,-1)$: $\langle\nabla f,v\rangle=2\varepsilon>0$, an ascent direction. Test set $H_v=\{w:\langle w,v\rangle\ge0\}=\{w:w_y\le0\}$; for all such $w$, $f'(z_i;w)=\langle(0,-2\varepsilon),w\rangle=2\varepsilon w_y\le0$… sign-checked: take $\nabla f=(0,+2\varepsilon)$ with $v\approx(0,-1)$ gives $\langle\nabla f,v\rangle=-2\varepsilon<0$ (descent, test correctly fires only at $\varepsilon=0$). The failing configuration is $\nabla f=(0,-2\varepsilon)$, $v\approx(0,-1)$: then $f'(z_i;w)=-2\varepsilon w_y\ge0$ for all $w_y\le0$, so **Stop fires with $\|\nabla f\|=2\varepsilon>0$.** *Prediction:* an RLVR harness that seeds the simplex with this configuration reports termination with a residual gradient norm scaling linearly in $\varepsilon$ and independent of iteration count — falsifiable by a single forward evaluation of $\nabla f$ at the reported terminus.

**Veto 2 — collinear LPS triple yields a circle of position solutions.**
Take three surface vehicles at $X_1=(0,0,0)$, $X_2=(200,0,0)$, $X_3=(400,0,0)$ m, all pairwise distances in $[d_1,d_2]$, so Eq. 4.2 is satisfied; take $r_t=1000$ m so Eq. 4.1 is satisfied for points near the line. The trilateration linear system has rank 2, and the solution set is a circle of radius $\sqrt{r_t^2-y^2-z^2}$-type about the line. *Prediction:* with ranging noise $\sigma_\rho=1$ m, the horizontal position error along the line-of-sight degenerates and the RMS 3-D fix error grows as $\sigma_\rho/\sin\theta$ where $\theta$ is the elevation angle subtended by the triple, diverging as $\theta\to0$; the harness will observe fix error exceeding $10\sigma_\rho$ while Eqs. 4.2 and 4.1 both report "satisfied". Falsifiable by Monte-Carlo trilateration with the reported geometry.

**Veto 3 — cone collapse at $\ell_i\le\delta$ destroys monotonicity.**
Set $\delta=50$ m and construct an iteration with $\|z_i-C_i\|=30$ m. Then $C_i$ lies strictly inside the generating ball $B(z_i,\delta)$, so $K(z_i)=S^2$ and the leader constraint $\dot X_{S_k}(T_k^*)\in K(z_i)-\{z_i\}$ [PAGE 11] is vacuous. *Prediction:* with $f$ having a local maximum in the half-space behind $C_i$, a run with $\delta=50$ m admits a leader motion that increases $f$, violating the asserted chain $f(A_{i+1})\le f(A_i)\le f(B_i)\le f(C_i)$ [PAGE 7], whereas the same seed with $\delta=5$ m preserves it. This is a two-arm counterfactual differing only in $\delta$, and the outcome is a strict inequality on the objective sequence — directly checkable from the logged iteration table, with no appeal to convergence theory.
