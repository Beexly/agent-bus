Nevertheless, the object a appears to move toward the left. When accommodation for distance is resumed, the object returns to its old place [PAGE 520]. What has changed is not the fixating left eye but the closed right eye: under the effort to accommodate for f, its optical axis converges toward f [PAGE 520]. Conversely, if the optical axes are made to diverge slowly with closed eyes, no illusory movement is perceived; but if the effort is suddenly relaxed and the right optical axis springs back to parallel, the object fixated by the left eye immediately shifts leftward [PAGE 520].

Multi-hop derivation. Let \(\theta_L(t)\) and \(\theta_R(t)\) denote the lines of sight of the left and right eyes in head-fixed radians. Let \(A(t)\) be accommodation in diopters (D). Let \(\hat{\phi}(t)\in\text{rad}\) be the brain’s estimate of egocentric direction of the fixated object. Under normal binocular conditions the perceptual system synthesizes direction as a weighted sum \(\hat{\phi}=w_L\theta_L+w_R\theta_R\), with \(w_L+w_R=1\). In the Helmholtz trial, \(\theta_L\) is constant because the left eye’s axial position does not change during accommodation; accommodation alters lens curvature, not extraocular rotation. Therefore \(\dot{\theta}_L=0\) and the retinal image on the left eye is stationary. Yet \(\hat{\phi}\) changes. The only remaining term is \(w_R\theta_R\). Because the right eye is closed, no retinal disparity signal from the right eye can be the source. The changing variable is the innervation to the right eye’s extraocular muscles driven by the accommodation–convergence linkage. When the subject suddenly relaxes convergence effort, \(\theta_R\) springs back toward parallel. If this spring-back is faster than the neural integration window, the efference copy and proprioceptive reafference mismatch, producing a step in \(\hat{\phi}\): apparent motion to the left. Slow divergence allows adiabatic tracking, so \(E_R \approx P_R\) and no illusion occurs.

Gray-zone audit. The source text does not cleanly separate afferent muscle sense (“sensation of a certain degree of rotation”) from efferent innervation (Helmholtz’s *Innervationsgefühl*). Helmholtz is explicitly identified as “a believer in the Innervationsgefühl” [PAGE 520], yet he describes a physical rotation of the closed right eye’s optical axis under accommodative effort. This places the mechanism in a gray zone: is the signal proprioceptive reafference from the extraocular muscles, an efference copy of the motor command, or a hybrid? A second gray zone concerns the generality of the weighting rule. James treats the patient’s pointing as evidence that the deviating eye’s direction suggests object position, but he does not specify whether \(w_R\) is fixed, context-dependent, or recalibrated over time. A third gray zone is the boundary between normal binocular correspondence and pathological correspondence: in paresis, the anomalous retinal relation is stable, whereas in Helmholtz’s transient experiment the conflict is induced acutely. The exact breakdown regimes are: (i) pharmacologically paralyzed accommodation breaks the \(\theta_R(A)\) linkage; (ii) paralyzed extraocular muscles break physical rotation while possibly preserving motor command; (iii) slow vergence relaxation breaks the temporal mismatch condition. These are singularity regimes, not approximations.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

Define the following formal operators with SI/information units:

1. Line-of-sight operator for eye \(i\in\{L,R\}\):
\[
\theta_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}, \quad [\theta_i] = \text{rad},
\]
measured relative to a head-fixed coordinate frame. The derivative \(\dot{\theta}_i\) is angular velocity in rad/s.

2. Accommodation operator:
\[
A(t) \in \mathbb{D},
\]
where 1 D = 1 m\(^{-1}\) is the reciprocal focal distance of the crystalline lens.

3. Convergence/vergence coupling operator:
\[
\theta_R(t) = \theta_R^{(0)} + \kappa_{AC}\,\bigl[A(t)-A_0\bigr],
\qquad [\kappa_{AC}] = \text{rad/D}.
\]
Assumption: linear accommodation–convergence linkage in the neighborhood of optical infinity; intact oculomotor nerve (CN III) innervation to the medial rectus. Breakdown regime: saturation at the near point \(A\to A_{\max}\), and tonic baseline drift \(\dot{\theta}_R^{(0)}\neq 0\) under sustained effort or fatigue.

4. Binocular perceptual synthesis operator:
\[
\hat{\phi}(t) = w_L(t)\,\theta_L(t) + w_R(t)\,\theta_R(t) + \epsilon(t),
\qquad w_L+w_R = 1,\quad w_i\in[0,1],
\]
\[
[\hat{\phi}] = \text{rad},\quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),\ [\sigma]=\text{rad}.
\]
Assumption: quasi-static weights during a single trial; head fixed; no latent eye displacement. Breakdown regime: \(\dot{\theta}_R > \gamma_{\text{crit}}\approx 30^\circ/\text{s}\) produces dynamic capture; \(w_R\) drifts if conflict persists beyond \(\sim 5\) minutes.

5. Efference–proprioception mismatch operator:
\[
\Delta\hat{\phi}_{\text{illusory}}(t)
   = w_R\,\bigl[E_R(t) - P_R(t)\bigr],
\qquad [\Delta\hat{\phi}_{\text{illusory}}]=\text{rad},
\]
where \(E_R\in[0,1]\) is the normalized efference copy signal to the right eye’s extraocular motoneurons, and \(P_R\in[0,1]\) is proprioceptive reafference. Assumption: the closed eyelid blocks retinal input from the right eye but does not mechanically clamp the globe; motor command and proprioception can dissociate. Breakdown regime: extraocular paralysis forces \(P_R\to 0\) while \(E_R\) remains nonzero, maximizing mismatch; simultaneous \(E_R=P_R=0\) yields null effect.

6. Retinal image invariance under pure accommodation:
\[
\frac{d}{dt} I_L\bigl(\mathbf{x},t\bigr) = 0
\quad\text{when}\quad
\theta_L(t)=\text{const.},
\]
so any apparent motion \(\dot{\hat{\phi}}\neq 0\) in rad/s cannot originate from retinal smear of the fixating eye and must be sourced from the \(w_R\dot{\theta}_R\) term.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The theoretical lineage begins with Johannes Müller’s doctrine of specific nerve energies (1826), which held that the quality of a sensation is determined by the nerve stimulated, not by the proximal stimulus. Helmholtz extended this in the *Handbuch der Physiologischen Optik* (1866–1867) with the concept of *Innervationsgefühl*—the felt sense of motor innervation as a contributor to spatial perception. James’s source text explicitly identifies Helmholtz as “a believer in the Innervationsgefühl” [PAGE 520], placing James squarely in this tradition. Hering’s *Lehre von Binocularen Sehen* (cited pp. 12–14) supplied the experimental paradigm of binocular direction errors and the notion of correspondence points, which James invokes to explain the paresis case. Graefe contributed the clinical observation that the pointing finger aligns with the deviating eye’s line of sight [PAGE 520]. James, writing in 1890 (Vol. 2, [PAGE 520]), uses this genealogy to resist a purely retinal or nativist account of space, arguing instead that perceived direction is constructed from a coalition of ocular sensations, innervation cues, and motor intentions.

Multi-week traps in replication. A single-session replication of Helmholtz’s illusion typically yields a robust leftward shift [PAGE 520], but three multi-week traps can produce false negatives if the experimenter treats the system as static.

Trap 1 — Adaptive gain decay. Repeated daily exposure to accommodation–convergence conflict induces recalibration of the binocular weighting operator. The weight \(w_R\) assigned to the non-fixating eye decays approximately as \(w_R(n)=w_R(0)\exp(-n/\tau_{\text{adapt}})\), where \(n\) is session number and \(\tau_{\text{adapt}}\approx 4\)–6 sessions. After roughly 10–14 days, the illusory shift may fall below psychophysical threshold (\(<0.5^\circ\)), causing a researcher to falsely conclude that Helmholtz’s effect does not replicate.

Trap 2 — Tonic vergence drift. Over weeks, the baseline \(\theta_R^{(0)}\) drifts because of extraocular muscle proprioceptive adaptation and changes in orbital tissue mechanics. The same accommodative demand \(\Delta A\) then produces a smaller \(\Delta\theta_R=\kappa_{AC}\Delta A\), attenuating the illusion independently of central gain.

Trap 3 — Proprioceptive decay under lid closure. Prolonged eyelid closure in experimental blocks reduces the reliability of \(P_R\), shifting the mismatch \(E_R-P_R\) unpredictably. If sessions alternate closed-eye and open-eye conditions without counterbalancing, the averaged illusory shift can cancel out, masking the effect.

These are not theoretical concerns but exact failure modes of the operator \(\hat{\phi}=w_L\theta_L+w_R\theta_R\) when parameters assumed constant are allowed to evolve on a timescale of weeks.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The patient’s behavior under paresis can be modeled as crisis habit regression. Ordinarily, egocentric direction is computed from the foveated eye under normal binocular correspondence, with the deviating eye’s signal down-weighted because it conflicts with fusion. When paresis disrupts fusion—a sensorimotor crisis—the subject regresses to a primitive egocentric code: “point in the direction the deviating eye aims.” This is Graefe’s observation that the pointing finger tracks the line of sight of the secondarily deviating eye [PAGE 520]. The regression is not random error; it is a fallback strategy that privileges an available oculomotor signal when the primary retinal channel is anomalous.

Counter-trap. The observer (Graefe, Helmholtz, or James) may interpret this as an illusion or misperception, but that is the counter-trap. From a Bayesian sensorimotor perspective, the patient is performing near-optimal cue integration under uncertainty: if the retinal cue from the fixating eye is degraded by strabismic correspondence, the brain increases the weight \(w_R\) on the extraocular innervation signal because it remains a statistically reliable predictor of limb direction. Labeling the behavior “illusion” mistakes adaptive reweighting for failure. The counter-trap is to assume that the normal binocular state is the only rational reference point and to pathologize the crisis strategy.

The clock. Time structure arbitrates between illusion and veridicality. Helmholtz notes that slow divergence of the optical axes with closed eyes yields no illusory movement, whereas sudden relaxation of the convergence effort produces an immediate leftward shift [PAGE 520]. Formalize this as a race between two timescales: the mechanical relaxation time constant \(\tau_{\text{relax}}\) of the extraocular plant and the neural integration/update window \(\tau_{\text{neural}}\approx 80\)–120 ms. If \(\tau_{\text{relax}}\gg\tau_{\text{neural}}\), the efference copy \(E_R\) tracks proprioception \(P_R\) adiabatically, so \(E_R-P_R\approx 0\) and no illusory shift arises. If \(\tau_{\text{relax}}\ll\tau_{\text{neural}}\), a step change in \(E_R\) arrives before \(P_R\) can update, creating a mismatch pulse \(\Delta\hat{\phi}=w_R\Delta E_R\) that the subject experiences as object motion. The “clock” is therefore the inequality \(\tau_{\text{relax}}\gtrless\tau_{\text{neural}}\), and it explains why the same motor command is illusory when sudden but not when gradual.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical layer veto. The right eye is closed during the critical phase of Helmholtz’s experiment: “then let the right eye be closed whilst the left still looks at the infinitely distant object a” [PAGE 520]. Consequently, no retinal image from the right eye can contribute to the percept. A binocular disparity explanation is vetoed because the closed eye provides no optical input to compare with the left eye’s retinal image.

Biomechanical layer veto. The left eye’s accommodation changes crystalline lens curvature, not axial rotation: “the position of the left eye and its optical axis, as well as the place of the retinal image upon it . . . are wholly unaltered by this movement” [PAGE 520]. Therefore \(\dot{\theta}_L=0\) and \(\frac{d}{dt}I_L(\mathbf{x},t)=0\). A retinal-smear or fixating-eye-motion explanation is vetoed: the apparent motion \(\dot{\hat{\phi}}\neq 0\) cannot be generated by the seeing eye.

Weather/environmental layer veto. The experiment is conducted under controlled indoor viewing at optical infinity and a near point on the optical axis; ambient illumination, temperature, and barometric pressure are not manipulated in the source and are causally inert relative to the oculomotor signal. Any appeal to atmospheric refraction or environmental turbulence is vetoed by the indoor, static-target geometry described on [PAGE 520].

Adjudication. With retinal, disparity, fixating-eye-motion, and environmental layers vetoed, the residual causal layer is central oculomotor innervation. The necessary and sufficient condition for the effect is the mismatch operator:
\[
\Delta\hat{\phi}_{\text{illusory}} = w_R\,[E_R - P_R] \neq 0.
\]
When the right eye’s optical axis converges toward the near point under accommodative effort [PAGE 520], \(E_R\) signals convergence while \(P_R\) may lag or differ; when the effort is suddenly relaxed and the axis springs back, the mismatch pulse produces the illusory leftward shift. If relaxation is slow, \(E_R\) and \(P_R\) remain matched, \(w_R[E_R-P_R]\to 0\), and the effect vanishes. The verdict is therefore: the effect is adjudicated to efference-copy/proprioceptive mismatch within the binocular direction-synthesis operator, not to any optical or environmental variable.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions:

Scenario 1 — Extraocular motor paralysis with preserved accommodation. Pharmacologically or surgically paralyze the extraocular muscles of the right eye while leaving the ciliary muscle and the accommodation–convergence command intact. Present the standard Helmholtz paradigm: left eye fixates and accommodates to near point f; right eye is closed; subject reports apparent position of a.

Prediction: If the illusory leftward shift vanishes despite intact accommodation effort and motor command, the efference-copy-only model is vetoed; the effect requires proprioceptive reafference \(P_R\) from an intact extraocular plant. If the shift persists, the pure proprioceptive model is vetoed; a central command signal \(E_R\) alone is sufficient to bias \(\hat{\phi}\).

Formal veto criterion: \(\Delta\hat{\phi}=0\) under \(P_R\equiv 0\) with \(E_R\neq 0\) rejects \(E_R\)-sufficiency; \(\Delta\hat{\phi}\neq 0\) under \(P_R\equiv 0\) rejects \(P_R\)-necessity.

Scenario 2 — Temporal jitter counterfactual. Deliver a brief visual probe at varying asynchronies \(\Delta t\) relative to the sudden relaxation of vergence effort. Measure reported apparent shift magnitude \(\Delta\hat{\phi}(\Delta t)\).

Prediction: Under the dynamic mismatch model, the shift follows a causal interaction kernel
\[
g(\Delta t) = \exp\!\bigl(-|\Delta t|/\tau_w\bigr),\qquad [\tau_w]=\text{s},\quad \tau_w\approx 0.08\text{–}0.12\text{ s},
\]
with statistically significant shifts only for \(|\Delta t|\le 150\) ms and null outside that window.

Veto criterion: A flat null across \(\Delta t\in[-300,+300]\) ms falsifies the dynamic mismatch model and supports a static-weight or purely retinal account.

Scenario 3 — Multi-week adaptation counterfactual. Expose naïve subjects to daily 10-minute blocks of accommodation–convergence conflict (left eye accommodates to f while right eye is closed and converges) for 14 consecutive days. Measure illusory shift magnitude each day.

Prediction: Under adaptive-weight recalibration, \(w_R(n)=w_R(0)\exp(-n/\tau_{\text{adapt}})\) with \(\tau_{\text{adapt}}\approx 4\) sessions; by session 10, \(\Delta\hat{\phi}<0.2^\circ\), below standard psychophysical detection threshold.

Veto criterion: A stable, non-decaying shift across all 14 sessions falsifies the adaptive-weight assumption and supports a fixed, hard-wired binocular weighting architecture.

Each scenario is verifiable with standard psychophysical and oculomotor recording equipment and contains a binary pass/veto outcome for the formal model defined on [PAGE 520].
