The paired Recruiter-to-Manager contrast is −11.28 ± 2.28 points (n = 32), localizing the regression to the Recruiter–Manager boundary rather than recruitment access itself [PAGE 6]. Across that boundary, GPT-5.6’s skipped-decision rate rises from 1.1% to 57.9%, bid rate falls from 12.6% to 2.7%, bids per episode fall from 5.91 to 1.31, and invalid actions remain at 0% [PAGE 6]. Among bids with explicit outcomes, acceptance does not fall (35.0% vs 38.7%) [PAGE 6]. The authors conclude the regression has "a behavioral signature of passivity rather than malformed or invalid actions" [PAGE 7]. Passivity extends to shared duties: matchday skip rate rises from 0.2% as Recruiter to 58.0% as Manager across 1,216 shared matchday decision points [PAGE 27].

Gray-zone audit. (1) Determinism covers only the simulator; hosted-model inference is explicitly excluded and "may vary across repeated calls" [PAGE 4], so bit-identical replay is conditional on inference stability, which is not guaranteed. (2) The Ours scaffold is stateless: "observe, then take one action, with no session memory" [PAGE 4], so long-horizon credit assignment across ten seasons cannot be supported by internal scaffold memory; the 10Y Claude Code arm uses a separate no-memory configuration [PAGE 8]. (3) Cost normalization is fragile: Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 were billed at 0.29 of official list prices at the snapshot, and the gateway "may omit billed reasoning tokens from its returned usage objects," so costs are recovered as measured bill divided by 0.29 [PAGE 21]. (4) Single-season outcomes remain noisy at eight evaluation seeds per cell [PAGE 11], and SEs are clustered by seed [PAGE 20], limiting inference power for narrow contrasts such as Pro-minus-Flash (−0.08 to +0.09 Z). (5) Results are operational measurements inside a procedurally generated, anonymized simulator; they are "neither psychological claims about a model nor evidence that any evaluated system is suitable for real organizational, employment, sporting, or financial decisions" [PAGE 11].

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal operators, units, and breakdown regimes.

Paired difference (dimensionless raw outcome units, e.g., league points, £M):
Δs = y_agent^(s) − y_ref^(s), s ∈ {1,…,n} [PAGE 5].
Mean paired difference: 
\[\bar{\Delta} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_s \Delta_s\]
with uncertainty stated as mean ± SE unless noted; SEs are seed-clustered [PAGE 5].

Reference-normalized Z-score (dimensionless):
\[Z = \frac{\bar{y}^{\text{eval}}_{\text{agent}} - \mu^{\text{cal}}_{\text{ref}}}{\sigma^{\text{cal}}_{\text{ref}}}\]
where μ_ref^cal and σ_ref^cal are frozen over 100 calibration seeds per scenario and responsibility scope [PAGE 5].

Composition Gap (dimensionless Z):
\[G = Z_{\text{Coach}} - Z_{\text{Manager}}\]
Positive G denotes a decrease in reference-normalized advantage after management responsibilities are added [PAGE 6].

Greedy reference utility (dimensionless score units):
\[U = \text{ovr} + \text{role\_fit} - 0.5(100 - \text{condition}) - 0.2(100 - \text{fitness})\]
role_fit subtracts 8 for out-of-position fielding; injured players are excluded [PAGE 18].

Breakdown and singularity regimes:
1. Z is undefined if σ_ref^cal = 0; the frozen calibration must retain dispersion across the 100 seeds [PAGE 5].
2. Paired SE inference breaks down when n = 8 seeds per cell yields SE comparable to or larger than the effect; e.g., match-control estimates −3.38 ± 3.03 (Flash) and −9.63 ± 3.75 (Pro) points at n = 8 pairs [PAGE 8], so sign is not reliably detected.
3. Determinism guarantee excludes hosted inference: repeated model calls may diverge, breaking bit-identical replay across model providers [PAGE 4].
4. Squad-layer singularity: if fewer than eight requested players are available, the engine discards the lineup request and selects an automatic best-fit eleven; no minimum-squad rule and no error is raised, so a squad unable to field eleven degrades silently [PAGE 18].
5. Ten-year attrition singularity: squad size falls from 22.6 in year 2 to 12.3 in year 5 under Claude Code+Pro, driven by 25.3 contract expiries versus 16.3 arrivals over nine seasons [PAGE 28], approaching a regime where sporting outcomes collapse despite financial recovery.

SI/information/probability units used in the source: league points (count), £M (10^6 GBP), £k (10^3 GBP), simulated days (day), decision points (count), tokens (dimensionless count), wall-clock time (seconds), skipped-decision rate (probability, %), bid rate per step (probability), acceptance rate (probability).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage plus multi-week traps.

FromPitch2Board inherits from a lineage of long-horizon agent benchmarks that never jointly vary model, scaffold, responsibility scope, match-control granularity, and horizon in one environment [PAGE 3]. WebArena and OSWorld test realistic computer use but remain bounded around a specified task [PAGE 3]. SWE-bench tests repository-scale issue resolution [PAGE 3]. τ-bench and ToolSandbox evaluate stateful tool use and user interaction [PAGE 3]. Planning benchmarks test long action sequences; ultra-horizon suites extend them to persistent settings; memory benchmarks isolate retention over extended interactions [PAGE 3]. Game environments provide partial observability and sequential consequences but generally expose a fixed player role [PAGE 3]. Long-horizon business simulations introduce accumulated resources and delayed objectives: Vending-Bench studies operational coherence, while CEO-Bench and YC-Bench evaluate organizational decision making over simulated time [PAGE 3]. FM-Bench is the closest concurrent work, evaluating full-control football managers over as many as twenty seasons with deterministic replay, scripted and human baselines, and a privileged-information oracle [PAGE 3, 10]. FromPitch2Board differs by fixing neither the management role nor the horizon, instead varying responsibility scope, match-control granularity, and horizon within the same world to locate where performance changes [PAGE 3]. Attribution literature (SWE-agent, Continual Harness, AgentScope, Agent Lightning, PokéAgent, Harness-Bench, The Scaffold Effect) establishes that agent outcomes reflect both foundation model and surrounding interface [PAGE 3].

Multi-week traps exposed by the design. An endpoint score conceals where performance changes and how degradation appears in behavior [PAGE 2]. The 3Y cohort shows a directional reversal in mean ranking between years one and three: Claude Code+Pro leads year one by 2.8 ± 2.5 points over Ours+Flash, whereas by year three Ours+Flash finishes first (79.0 vs 73.8, 72.9, 72.8) [PAGE 8]. The ten-year case study peaks early: Claude Code+Pro reaches 85.4 season points in year three, then falls to 47.1 by year six and remains far below that peak thereafter [PAGE 9]. Across ten seasons the agent accumulates 602.4 points versus 388.7 for the greedy reference, so the decline is relative to its own early peak, not a cumulative deficit; the reference shows no comparable early peak [PAGE 9]. This delayed-feedback pattern is invisible in one-season scores: contract expiries drive the sharp squad contraction in years 3–5 (6.9 expiries in year 3 alone), and net value later recovers without league performance recovering [PAGE 28]. Any benchmark that stops at year one or reports only cumulative totals would miss the responsibility-boundary regression and the temporal persistence collapse.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression. Under responsibility expansion, GPT-5.6 does not produce malformed actions; it stops deciding. The skipped-decision rate rises from 1.1% (Recruiter) to 57.9% (Manager), bid rate collapses from 12.6% to 2.7%, and matchday skip rate rises from 0.2% to 58.0% across the same 1,216 matchday decision points [PAGE 6, 27]. Invalid actions remain at 0%, and bid acceptance holds steady (35.0% vs 38.7%), so the failure is not a competence boundary in action generation but a participation boundary: a crisis habit of passivity under expanded scope [PAGE 6, 7].

Counter-trap. The trap is not recruitment itself: GPT-5.6 improves from Coach (46.1) to Recruiter (58.1), a +12.0 raw-point gain [PAGE 6]. The regression is localized to the Recruiter–Manager boundary, where the bundle of incoming-offer negotiation, transfer listing, and wage/transfer budget responsibility triggers full-management passivity [PAGE 6]. Session continuity is a counter-trap: a contemporaneous Flash ablation finds Coach scores effectively unchanged (+0.60 vs +0.61 Z) but Manager scores directionally worse with continuity (+0.81 vs +1.08 Z; paired difference −3.56 ± 2.28 points) [PAGE 7, 24]. Thus adding memory to the scaffold does not rescue the Manager scope and may harm it.

Clock. Temporal persistence is governed by a seasonal clock, not merely episode length. The 3Y cohort reverses ranking from year one to year three [PAGE 8]. The 10Y clock peaks at year three (85.4 points) then collapses to 47.1 by year six, coinciding with substantial squad attrition: squad size falls from 22.6 (year 2) to 12.3 (year 5), with 25.3 players leaving on contract expiry versus 16.3 arrivals over nine seasons [PAGE 9, 28]. The agent’s internal model of the world must therefore project contract expiry, squad aging, and wage-budget accumulation across a horizon where early sporting success is decoupled from long-term roster health. A stateless scaffold cannot maintain this model; even a stateful no-memory Claude Code+Pro configuration peaks early and regresses, showing that the clock exposes behavior changes a single headline score conceals [PAGE 2, 9].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather veto proof.

Physical layer (player condition, fitness, injury, morale). The observation exposes squad-level physical attributes, and the greedy reference utility penalizes low condition and fitness: U = ovr + role_fit − 0.5(100 − condition) − 0.2(100 − fitness); injured players are excluded [PAGE 18]. This is a physical veto on tactical execution: even if the manager selects a starting eleven, low-condition or injured players are scored down or removed, and the engine fills empty slots with the best available player [PAGE 18].

Biomechanical layer (substitution density, in-match control). Match-control granularity adds checkpoints at 30′, half-time, 60′, 75′ where agents may substitute or change formation and play style [PAGE 4]. DS Pro substitutes 38% more often than DS Flash (1.62 vs 1.17 substitutions per match), yet this yields no detectable season-level benefit: control-minus-default is −3.38 ± 3.03 points for DS Flash and −9.63 ± 3.75 points for DS Pro at n = 8 [PAGE 8]. The biomechanical veto is that minute-level intervention is actively used but does not improve outcomes under the current evaluation.

Weather layer. No weather variable is modeled in the Openfoot Manager fork; the simulator covers world generation, matches, and turn-level simulation with keyed random streams partitioned into days, matches, and scouting sub-streams [PAGE 4]. Absence of weather means there is no weather veto to adjudicate; this is a structural limitation, not a measured factor.

Cross-layer adjudication. Management-layer financial decisions (wage budget, transfer listing, offer negotiation) are vetoed by squad-layer constraints: the 22–26 player squad-size target, automatic departure on contract expiry, and the silent-degradation rule when fewer than eight requested starters are available [PAGE 18, 22]. Under GPT-5.6, full management causes financial retrenchment rather than uniform collapse: cash rises by £5.79M but net value does not improve (−£0.83M ± £2.07M SE), squad value falls by £6.61M, and the agent spends £5.73M less while reducing wages by £33.1k [PAGE 7]. The cross-layer veto is therefore consistent: responsibility expansion triggers passivity at the decision layer, which cascades into squad-value attrition and financial retrenchment at the roster layer, while invalid actions never fire as a veto because the agent simply declines to act.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
