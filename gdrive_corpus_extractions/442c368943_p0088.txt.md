The clock enters through Hamilton’s delayed incentive [PAGE 88]. Delay introduces a temporal discounting operator \(\delta(t)\) such that the effective value at decision time is \(u_{\mathrm{eff}} = u_0\,\delta(t)\). The organism must run a clock from choice to reward; if the clock is noisy or the delay is long, the credit assignment to the preceding maze response degrades. Recursively, the experimenter models the rat’s clock, while the rat’s behavior reveals whether its internal clock matches the external contingency. Breakdown occurs when \(|\mu_E - \mu_R| > \epsilon\): the experimenter’s model of the rat’s incentive model diverges beyond tolerance, and the observed “habit regression” is actually a model-mismatch artifact rather than a motivational collapse.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three veto layers must be satisfied before any inference about “Demands for Goal-Objects” from [PAGE 88] can be deemed physically valid.

Physical layer: The obstruction method, invoked by Holden [PAGE 88], Jenkins M. [PAGE 88], and Nissen [PAGE 88], requires a physical barrier of specified dimensions. Veto condition \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}=1\) if barrier height is below the rat’s jumping threshold, width is insufficient to force a detour, or barrier permeability \(\kappa\) exceeds a critical value \(\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}\). If the animal can climb over or squeeze through without latency cost, the obstruction score is invalidated as a measure of goal-demand.

Biomechanical layer: Sharp’s continuous-running paradigm [PAGE 88] subjects the rat to sustained locomotor output. Veto condition \(V_{\mathrm{bio}}=1\) if running duration \(t_{\mathrm{run}}\) exceeds the species-specific endurance limit \(T_{\max}\), or if fatigue-induced gait degradation alters maze-running kinematics independently of incentive value. Disintegration under these conditions is a biomechanical failure, not a motivational one.

Weather/environmental layer: Although [PAGE 88] does not report ambient meteorology, Holden’s starvation [PAGE 88] and McGillivray and Stone’s escape-from-water incentive [PAGE 88] are thermally coupled processes. Ambient temperature \(T_{\mathrm{amb}}\) outside the rat’s thermoneutral zone alters metabolic heat production and hence the hunger input rate \(h(t)\) in the drive equation. Veto condition \(V_{\mathrm{env}}=1\) if \(T_{\mathrm{amb}}\notin [T_{\min},T_{\max}]\), because thermal stress confounds the drive state.

The experiment is admissible only if \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}=0\land V_{\mathrm{bio}}=0\land V_{\mathrm{env}}=0\). The weather veto is an external physical constraint required for replication validity; it is not textually asserted in [PAGE 88] but is a necessary boundary condition for any laboratory attempting to reproduce the cited starvation or water-escape incentives.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a formal prediction and a falsification rule.

Scenario 1 (incentive removal after continuous running, Sharp [PAGE 88]): Run rats to asymptotic maze performance with food at the goal, then remove the food incentive while retaining the maze structure. Prediction: the entropy of error turns increases significantly, \(H(E) = -\sum_e p(e)\log_2 p(e)\) measured in bits, and wrong-turn frequency rises above the pre-removal baseline by a margin exceeding the session-to-session reliability bound reported by Stone and Nyswander [PAGE 88]. Veto: if \(\Delta H(E)\approx 0\) and wrong-turn frequency does not increase, the claimed “disintegrative effect” of incentive removal is falsified for this replication.

Scenario 2 (obstruction barrier permeability, Holden [PAGE 88]; Jenkins M. [PAGE 88]; Nissen [PAGE 88]): Systematically lower or remove the obstruction barrier so that \(\kappa>\kappa_{\mathrm{crit}}\). Prediction: time-to-goal \(T\) decreases toward the unobstructed minimum, and the variance in path choice collapses because the detour option is eliminated. Veto: if \(T\) does not change significantly when the barrier is rendered permeable, the obstruction method is invalidated as an operationalizer of goal-demand, and all citations relying on it lose construct validity.

Scenario 3 (delayed versus immediate incentive, Hamilton [PAGE 88]): Contrast a delayed food reward (delay \(t_d>0\)) with an immediate food reward matched for magnitude. Prediction: the acquisition curve under delay exhibits a lower asymptotic learning rate and an effective value \(u_{\mathrm{eff}}=u_0/(1+kt_d)\) that decays with \(t_d\); the discount parameter \(k\) should be strictly positive. Veto: if immediate and delayed conditions yield statistically indistinguishable acquisition rates, the delayed-incentive effect on the hunger drive is falsified, and the incentive-demand model fails at the temporal credit-assignment layer.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
