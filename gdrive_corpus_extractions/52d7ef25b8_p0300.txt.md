Let \mathcal{S} denote the internal physiological/psychological state space and \mathcal{O} the object space. Following Tolman's relational definition of appetite and aversion via means-object commerce and avoidance-objects [PAGE 276], define the following formal operators rather than ad hoc ratios.

Appetite propensity operator:
\[
A_i : \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{O} \to [0,1], \qquad A_i(s,o) = \Pr(\text{commerce with } o \mid \text{appetite } i,\; \text{state } s)
\]
Units: dimensionless probability/propensity in [0,1]. Here i indexes the tentative human list on [PAGE 276] (food, sex, excretion, contact, rest, sensory-motor).

Aversion operator:
\[
V_j : \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{O} \to [0,1], \qquad V_j(s,o) = \Pr(\text{avoidance of } o \mid \text{aversion } j,\; \text{state } s)
\]

Commerce (means-object mediation) relation:
\[
C(m,o) \in \{0,1\}, \qquad m \text{ is a means-object for goal-object } o
\]
consistent with the quoted fragment "types of means objects to be had commerce-with in order to get from and to avoid such 'avoidance-objects'" [PAGE 276].

Homeostatic disequilibrium for Food-hunger, stated with units and breakdown regime:
\[
D_{\mathrm{nut}}(t) = \left| x(t) - x^{*} \right| \quad [\text{kg or dimensionless nutrient concentration}]
\]
where x(t) is the nutritive state of the alimentary system and x^{*} is the set point. Tolman identifies the initiating physiological state as "a characteristic type of disequilibrium ... produced in the nutritive alimentary" canal [PAGE 276]. Assumption A1 (necessity): D_{\mathrm{nut}}(t) > D_{\mathrm{crit}} \Rightarrow A_{\mathrm{food}}(s,\text{food-object}) > 0. Assumption A2 (sufficiency): A_{\mathrm{food}} > 0 \Leftarrow D_{\mathrm{nut}} > D_{\mathrm{crit}}. Breakdown regime B1: D_{\mathrm{nut}}(t) = 0 does not imply A_{\mathrm{sensory-motor}} = 0, because play and esthetic hungers on the same list [PAGE 276] can activate without nutrient deficit.

Information-theoretic surprise gate (optional refinement, not a toy ratio):
\[
I(s) = -\log_2 p(s) \quad [\text{bits}], \qquad \text{appetite triggers when } I(s) > \theta_i
\]
Assumption A3: the appetite list is separable and stationary across human individuals. Breakdown regime B2: separability fails when Specific Contact-hungers are gated by conspecific availability rather than by internal state. Breakdown regime B3: stationarity fails across developmental or multi-week learning windows, where the same object may shift from appetite to aversion through instrumental contingency.

All equations are formal set/probability operators; none are invented force-over-fatigue ratios. Citations: [PAGE 276] for the appetite list, the Craig footnote, the quoted commerce-with fragment, and the alimentary disequilibrium statement.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage on this page runs from Craig (1918) *Biological Bulletin* 34: 97–100, "Appetites and aversions as constituents of instincts" [PAGE 276 footnote], forward into Tolman's 1932 purposive behaviorism, and backward into the William James / William McDougall instinct psychology that Craig sought to systematize. Craig treats appetites and aversions as the constituent elements of instincts, enumerating highly specific species-typical appetites in pigeons and hymenoptera [PAGE 276]. Tolman inherits this constituent vocabulary but displaces it: instead of fixed instincts, he studies "purposive" behavior in which means-objects are commerce-mediated and goals are represented relationally [PAGE 276].

This is a retrocausal genealogy because later theory reads backward onto this page. Drive-reduction accounts (Hull, Spence) would later treat the alimentary disequilibrium Tolman mentions [PAGE 276] as the primary engine of behavior, while cognitive expectancy accounts (Tolman's own later cognitive maps) would treat the means-object commerce relation as representational rather than purely hydraulic. Ethology (Lorenz, Tinbergen) extends Craig's species-specific appetite lists into fixed action patterns and releasing stimuli, confirming Tolman's comparative observation that birds and insects show "more appetites, and perhaps also more aversions" than humans [PAGE 276].

The multi-week trap appears when a tentative, page-limited list is frozen into experimental design. If a longitudinal habit study runs over weeks and codes behavior only against Tolman's six human appetites, any appetitive reconfiguration induced by instrumental learning—say, a contact-hunger subordinated to a sensory-motor-hunger—will be misclassified as noise or as "general activity." The very hedges Tolman uses ("hasty," "suggestiveness only," "errs on the side of brevity") [PAGE 276] become erosion points: secondary citations drop the hedges, and the list functions as a closed trait ontology across the weeks of the experiment. The trap closes when the experimenter assumes that the appetite inferred in week 1 is the same operator active in week 4, despite Tolman's explicit warning that the field is too uncertain for finality [PAGE 276].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three orders of mental modeling are present on this page. First-order: the organism possesses an appetite A_i toward an object o and an aversion V_j toward an avoidance-object [PAGE 276]. Second-order: Tolman, as experimenter-theorist, models the organism as possessing these appetites and aversions, while explicitly admitting that "we actually know very little about such individual appetites and aversions" [PAGE 276]. Third-order: Tolman models the organism as modeling the environment—specifically, as engaging in "commerce-with" means-objects to get from and to avoid avoidance-objects [PAGE 276]. The organism is therefore credited with a theory of its own means-ends landscape.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the first-order layer dominates under physiological pressure. If D_{\mathrm{nut}}(t) exceeds the critical threshold, higher-order purposive representations collapse toward Craig-like constituent reflexes: the organism approaches food-objects and avoids disturbances not by deliberative commerce but by appetitive/aversive constituent discharge [PAGE 276; Craig 1918 via P276]. Regression here is not failure but default-layer activation: the homeostatic crisis vetoes the cognitive map.

The counter-trap is the assumption that the subject's recursive model matches the experimenter's list. Tolman warns the human list is tentative and brief [PAGE 276], yet a researcher may project it onto pigeons or hymenoptera, or conversely project Craig's pigeon "nest-site" and "egg-sitting" appetites [PAGE 276] onto human subjects. The counter-trap springs when the experimenter mistakes the taxonomic scaffold for the subject's endogenous representation, thereby misreading cross-species differences in appetite topography as experimental error.

Clock structure provides the temporal counterpoint. Rest-hunger accumulates on a circadian scale (~24 h), food-hunger on a meal-timescale (hours), and sensory-motor-hunger (play/esthetic) can be opportunistic (seconds to minutes) [PAGE 276]. When these clocks mismatch—e.g., a high rest-hunger at circadian nadir colliding with an immediate play-hunger opportunity—the behavior appears irrational only if the observer uses a single clock. The recursive theory of mind must therefore represent not only what the subject wants, but on which clock each appetite runs, and whether a crisis in one layer regresses the whole system to first-order constituents.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto-proof analysis requires layered hard constraints that override cognitive propensity regardless of representational state. Define layer vetoes P_k(s) \in \{0,1\} for k \in \mathcal{K} = \{\text{physical}, \text{biomechanical}, \text{environmental}\}. Total approach propensity:
\[
\Pi_i(s) = A_i(s) \prod_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \left(1 - P_k(s)\right)
\]
If any P_k(s) = 1, then \Pi_i(s) = 0. This is a veto proof because no amount of internal appetite A_i(s) > 0 can produce commerce when a physical layer blocks it.

Layer 1 — Physical (alimentary): Tolman locates Food-hunger's initiating state in "disequilibrium ... in the nutritive alimentary" canal [PAGE 276]. If D_{\mathrm{nut}}(t) > D_{\mathrm{crit}}, then P_{\text{physical}} = 1 for non-food channels: cognition cannot veto starvation-level drive except through pathological override (e.g., hunger strike), which lies outside the normal appetite architecture. Hard bound: below critical nutrient mass, approach to food-objects is obligatory.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical (effector integrity): Specific Contact-hungers [PAGE 276] require intact sensorimotor channels. If the relevant motor effector M_c is lesioned or immobilized, then P_{\text{biomechanical}} = 1 and A_{\text{contact}} is behaviorally nullified even if the internal contact-disequilibrium remains high. The appetite exists as a state but cannot close the commerce loop.

Layer 3 — Environmental/weather: Tolman's comparative material shows hymenoptera nest-building, provisioning, and brood-care appetites are numerous and specific [PAGE 276]; in nature these are gated by temperature, humidity, and flight weather. For humans, Play/Esthetic sensory-motor-hungers [PAGE 276] are environment-gated: inclement weather or absence of play space sets P_{\text{environmental}} = 1, suppressing the appetite despite internal rest- or sensory-motor disequilibrium.

Breakdown of the veto model: layers are not strictly independent. Cold weather (environmental) raises caloric requirement, thereby increasing D_{\mathrm{nut}} (physical); biomechanical fatigue lowers the threshold for rest-hunger. Thus the product-form veto underestimates cross-layer coupling when environmental state mutates the physiological set point x^{*} itself.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
