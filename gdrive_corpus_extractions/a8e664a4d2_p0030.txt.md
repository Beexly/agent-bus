**Multi-Week Trap #1: The "Molar" vs. "Molecular" Confusion**  
Early behaviorists often conflated muscular movements (molecular) with organized activity (molar). Tolman insists only molar acts have purpose; molecular components do not. This false dichotomy persists today in debates over neural correlates vs. psychological explanations.  

**Multi-Week Trap #2: The Circular Inference Dilemma**  
Tolman’s risk: Inferring purpose from goal-directed behavior, then using that purpose to explain the same behavior. Historical example: Thorndike’s cats were said to learn via "trial-and-error," but the maze structure implied a goal (escape). Tolman’s innovation was to make the goal *publicly observable* (e.g., food cup) and measure *alternative route selection*, not just escape latency. The trap remains whenever researchers interpret complex behavior (e.g., tool use) as evidence of foresight without ruling out simpler reinforcement histories.

Tolman’s response (foreshadowed here) would be that even such behavior is organized toward a *perceptual* goal (e.g., seeking stimulation), but the debate exposes the fragility of inferring cognition from overt acts alone.
