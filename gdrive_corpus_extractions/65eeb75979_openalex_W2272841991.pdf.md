The first hop is definitional. Parker splits the rule set of a video game into "fixed rules" — "fixed rules which cannot be broken from the player position" [PAGE 1] — and "implied rules which are not enforced by the computer program" [PAGE 1], and then adds a third, player-supplied stratum he calls "imposed rules" [PAGE 3]. The natural carrier for this triple is a transition system: an engine is a set of trajectories, and a rule set is a subset of that set. Under that reading, "fixed" means *constitutive of the ambient trajectory set* (a violation has probability zero, not merely low probability), "implied" means a distinguished subset of that set with no enforcement mechanism attached, and "imposed" means a further subset declared by the players. The second hop is the compatibility condition. Parker asks rhetorically whether imposed rules could conflict with fixed rules — "None of these alternative, imposed rules run counter to the fixed rules of the game— indeed, how could they?" [PAGE 4] — and later asserts that "rules imposed on video games cannot be in conflict with the foundational fixed rules of the computer program, which suggests to me a distinct kind of engagement" [PAGE 4]. That "how could they?" is not rhetorical decoration; it is a satisfiability theorem. Imposed rules are defined as a subset of the engine's trajectory set, so their intersection with it is nonempty by construction. The instant the engine changes, however, the theorem evaporates — which is why patch notes are an existential threat to player-created games, not merely an inconvenience. The third hop is the operator algebra. Parker separates expansive gameplay from modding precisely by which stratum the operator touches: "a modder leaves the player position: the modder is no longer playing the game when he creates his mod, he is using the game software as a tool" [PAGE 3], whereas expansive gameplay "involves players dictating additional or alternative rules from completely within the confines of the existing game rules" [PAGE 3]. Formally, modding moves the fixed stratum; expansive gameplay moves only the implied/imposed strata. The fourth hop is the load-bearing word "expansive." Parker asserts that "rules are inherently expansive, in that the imposition of limitations creates a specific range of possibilities and outcomes" [PAGE 2]. Taken naively this is false — a constraint shrinks the trajectory set. It becomes true only under a different measure: constraints can raise the *discriminability* of outcomes with respect to player skill even as they lower the *volume* of admissible play. That is the gray zone I audit hardest below, because the relation is not monotone: constraints that strip skill-expressive variance (Parker's own example of "completing the game without scoring a single point" [PAGE 7]) drive discriminability to zero exactly. The fifth hop is multiplayer. Parker repeatedly locates enforcement outside the code — "the rules of Jeep Tag exist in the collective agreement of the players to abide by them" [PAGE 2] — and describes the sanction as being "forcibly removed from the server or ostracized by other players" [PAGE 8]. That is a costly peer-punishment public good, and it has *two* opposing size effects: detection improves with group size, monitoring incentive decays with group size. The interior optimum, and the observed institutional response (posted server rules, admin kicks), fall out without any extra assumption. The audit caveat I hold throughout: Parker explicitly disclaims a taxonomy [PAGE 5] and disclaims social-scientific method [PAGE 2] — "this is not intended to be a social scientific analysis or survey of the phenomenon." <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**1. Base object.** Let an engine be a transition system $M=(S,A,T,s_0)$ with $T:S\times A\to\Delta(S)$. Let $\mathcal{T}\subseteq (S\times A)^{\omega}$ be the set of $T$-feasible trajectories. *Fixed rules* $F\equiv T$; the defining property is that for any player policy $\pi$, $\Pr_\pi[\tau\notin\mathcal{T}]=0$. Parker's "rules that the player cannot refuse" [PAGE 3] is exactly this zero-probability statement, not a high-cost statement.

**2. Strata.** *Implied rules*: $I\subseteq\mathcal{T}$, unenforced, e.g. "the player is supposed to use power-ups, collect coins, kill the bad guys by jumping on their heads, and complete each level" [PAGE 4]. *Imposed rules*: $P\subseteq\mathcal{T}$ declared by players. Parker's definition — "all that which is assumed but not necessarily enforced about how a game should be played" [PAGE 4] — is the $I$ stratum verbatim.

**3. Feasibility predicate (the "how could they?" theorem).**
$$\Phi(P)\;=\;\mathbf{1}\!\left[\,P\cap\mathcal{T}\neq\varnothing\,\right],\qquad P\subseteq\mathcal{T}\;\Rightarrow\;\Phi(P)=1 .$$
Units: dimensionless indicator. Breakdown regime: an engine revision $T\mapsto T'$ with $P\cap\mathcal{T}'=\varnothing$ gives $\Phi(P)=0$; the imposed game is not harder, it is **undefined**. This is the exact singularity behind "rules imposed on video games cannot be in conflict with the foundational fixed rules of the computer program" [PAGE 4].

**4. Operator algebra.** With $\mathcal{R}=(F,I,P)$ and $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{R})$ the rule-revision group:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{expansive: } & g\in\mathrm{Stab}(F),\quad g\cdot P=P'\supseteq P,\\
\text{modding: } & g\notin\mathrm{Stab}(F),\quad g\cdot F=F'\neq F,\\
\text{canonization: } & (F,I,P)\mapsto(F\cup P,\;I,\;\varnothing).
\end{aligned}
$$
Canonization is the operator the paper observes empirically: "both 'Zombie' and 'Cat and Mouse' have been officially integrated into subsequent versions of Halo and Project Gotham Racing due to their overwhelming popularity" [PAGE 8]. Modding's domain exclusion is verbatim: "the modder is no longer playing the game when he creates his mod" [PAGE 3].

**5. Inertness lemma (constraint entropy budget).** Let $X$ be a $\mathcal{T}$-valued trajectory under a reference policy and $\mathbf{1}_A$ the compliance indicator of one binary imposed rule with $\mu(A)=p\in(0,1)$. Since $\mathbf{1}_A$ is a deterministic function of $X$, $H(X,\mathbf 1_A)=H(X)$ and
$$H(X)-H(X\mid \mathbf 1_A)=H_b(p)=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\ \text{[bits]}\;\le\;1\ \text{bit}.$$
Consequences: $k$ independent binary rules remove at most $k$ bits; marginal bit-efficiency peaks at $p=\tfrac12$; rules with $p\to 0$ (null) or $p\to 1$ (inert habit) contribute $0$ bits exactly. Assumption: discrete trajectory alphabet, finite horizon.

**6. Skill discriminability ("expansive" made non-vacuous).** Let $\Theta$ be a policy/skill parameter with prior $\pi$, $O:\mathcal{T}\to\Omega$ an outcome functional (score, win indicator, completion time in seconds, Warthog apex height in metres),
$$\mathcal{D}(P)=I(\Theta;O\mid P)=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{o}\pi(\theta)\,p_P(o\mid\theta)\log_2\frac{p_P(o\mid\theta)}{p_P(o)}\ \text{[bits]}.$$
Gaussian special case with $O=g(\theta)+Z$, $g\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_g^2)$, $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_Z^2)$ independent:
$$\mathcal{D}=\tfrac12\log_2\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma_g^2}{\sigma_Z^2}\right)\ \text{[bits per outcome]}.$$
Two exact singularities: (i) $\sigma_g^2\to 0$ (rule removes the skill-expressive channel, e.g. "completing the game without scoring a single point" [PAGE 7]) $\Rightarrow \mathcal{D}=0$ — pure ritual; (ii) $\sigma_Z^2\to 0$ $\Rightarrow \mathcal{D}\to\infty$ but ex-ante uncertainty vanishes — the solved-game degeneration. Non-monotonicity in constraint strength is therefore structural, not an artifact. This is the precise content of "the imposition of limitations creates a specific range of possibilities and outcomes" [PAGE 2], and it is why "it is rules and competition that make games fun, not random, free-form play" [PAGE 2] is a claim about $\mathcal{D}$, not about $|\mathrm{supp}\,\mu|$.

**7. Enforcement viability in multiplayer.** $N$ players, private gain from violation $b$, fine $L>b$, per-observer detection probability $q$. Mutual compliance is a Nash equilibrium iff
$$\left[1-(1-q)^{N-1}\right]L\;\ge\;b \;\Longrightarrow\; N\ge N_{\min}=1+\left\lceil \frac{\ln(1-b/L)}{\ln(1-q)}\right\rceil .$$
Second-order free riding supplies the opposing bound: each punisher captures $\approx v/(N-1)$ of the regime value $v$ at cost $c$, so punishing is rational only while $N\le N_{\max}$. Viable band $N_\min\le N\le N_\max$; outside it, codification substitutes — matching "posted server rules" and being "forcibly removed from the server" [PAGE 7–8]. Parker's own setting (basements and living rooms [PAGE 2]) sits inside the band; 64-player public servers do not.

**8. Common-knowledge existence condition.** Fixed rules exist at belief level $0$ (they execute). Imposed rules exist only as a common-knowledge fixed point $C P=\bigcap_{k\ge 0}B^{k}P$, consistent with "the rules of Jeep Tag exist in the collective agreement of the players" [PAGE 2]. Truncation at level $k$ is a breakdown regime with a distinctive signature: mutual violation without sanction.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The imposed stratum has no execution semantics, only belief semantics, so its stability is a theory-of-mind problem. Three regimes interact. **Crisis habit regression:** under load, the effective policy solves $\pi^\star=\arg\min_\pi\{\mathbb E_\pi[-u]+\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,\pi_0)\}$ [nats], with $\pi_0$ the low-description-length default and $\beta$ the inverse cognitive budget. As $\beta\to\infty$, $\pi^\star\to\pi_0$: the player reverts to unconstrained deathmatch behaviour. Jeep Tag rule 5 — "The players who are not 'it' must not attack one another" [PAGE 2] — is precisely the constraint requiring suppression of $\pi_0$, hence predicted to carry the highest violation density; rule 6's pre-commitment (sticky grenades "expressly forbidden" [PAGE 2]) carries the lowest. **Counter-trap:** an opponent engineers a state where compliance is locally costly; compliance survives iff $g\le \delta\rho/(1-\delta)$, with $g$ the one-shot defection gain, $\rho$ the reputation asset, $\delta$ the continuation probability — the formal shadow of being "ostracized by other players" [PAGE 8]. **Clock:** the timer is a boundary object. In rule 7 the "pre-defined time period" [PAGE 2] is externally instrumented and functions as a fixed rule, yet its trustworthiness rests on implied norms (proof videos, screen captures [PAGE 7]) — a verification layer that is itself unenforced, hence forgeable. Depth matters too: common knowledge $C P$ requires $\omega$ levels; truncation at $k=1$ yields players who know the rules but doubt that others know, producing unsanctioned violation cascades — the predicted signature of pickup groups versus the deep mutual knowledge of "myself and a group of high school friends" [PAGE 2].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Physical/engine layer (dominant veto).** Jeep Tag exists only because the engine's impulse model throws the Warthog — the paper celebrates "the gloriously unrealistic physics engine powering the game, which allowed the Warthog to soar impressively high into the air when struck with explosive weapons" [PAGE 2]. A physics patch alters $T$; if $P\cap\mathcal{T}'=\varnothing$ then $\Phi(P)=0$ and the game is annihilated, not weakened. This layer vetoes with probability one and admits no appeal. **Biomechanical layer.** Rule 4 grants "a head start of roughly two seconds" [PAGE 1]. Take simple visual reaction time $\approx 2.0\text{–}2.5\times10^{2}\,\text{ms}$ plus motor transit and re-entry animation $t_{\text{transit}}$; the constant must satisfy $t_{\text{hs}}\ge t_{\text{react}}+t_{\text{transit}}+\varepsilon$, so $\sim 2.0\times10^{3}\,\text{ms}$ is roughly eight reaction times — comfortably above the ergonomic floor, which is why the rule reads as *fair* rather than arbitrary. Reduce it below $\sim 4\times10^{2}\,\text{ms}$ and the rule becomes unsatisfiable in practice regardless of intent. **Environmental/network layer.** Let round-trip jitter be $\sigma$ and the decision window that distinguishes an accidental from a deliberate grenade be $\Delta t$. When $\sigma\gtrsim\Delta t$ the referee's discrimination error approaches $\tfrac12$, peer punishment becomes noise, and informal enforcement collapses toward the $N_\max$ regime — which is exactly why communities migrate to "posted server rules" and administrative removal [PAGE 7–8]. **Adjudication order:** engine veto $\succ$ biomechanical veto $\succ$ network veto $\succ$ social sanction. Lower layers cannot override a higher-layer veto; they can only re-parameterize within its feasible set.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
