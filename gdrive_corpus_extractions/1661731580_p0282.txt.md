Recursive theory of mind enters because the dog's response is a function of its model of the experimenter (is food coming? is this a trial?), while the experimenter's behavior (entering, talking, staying two minutes) is a function of his model of the dog (has it extinguished?). Each perturbation updates both models recursively. The recursion terminates when the extra-stimulus trace falls below the detection threshold; at that point, the dog's model reverts to "no food expected" and the experimenter's model reads "extinction complete." The five-minute null result \([PAGE 1]\) marks the fixed point of this recursive loop.

Physical layer: Salivary glands are finite-capacity exocrine organs. The submaxillary gland produces mixed serous-mucous secretion; the parotid is predominantly serous \([PAGE 1]\) table headers. They have distinct refractory recovery periods and maximal output rates. Because [PAGE 1] does not cleanly resolve which numerical entries belong to which gland (OCR column collapse), a physical veto is available: if submaxillary and parotid responses to the same disinhibitory probe diverge in sign or magnitude, the claim of a unitary "disinhibition" is physically untenable.

Biomechanical layer: Tactile stimulation of the skin and auditory knocks are exteroceptive inputs that can trigger generalized autonomic arousal independent of the conditioned oral stimulus. The text states these stimuli alone produced no secretory effect \([PAGE 1]\), but a biomechanical veto exists if tactile stimulation, when administered during the inter-trial interval, elevates baseline salivary tone in the next minute via sympathetic spillover. That would confound the attribution of post-stimulus saliva to disinhibition of the conditioned response rather than to non-specific arousal.

Weather/environmental layer: Room temperature, humidity, and experimenter proximity alter cutaneous and autonomic state. [PAGE 1] reports no environmental controls. A weather veto triggers if ambient conditions drift between 1:53 P.M. and 2:28 P.M. such that basal salivation changes independently of conditioning; under that condition, the 11-drop baseline and the later 5-drop probe are not commensurate.

Adjudication: The strongest veto is temporal and internal to [PAGE 1]: "Five minutes later, however, the same stimulus produced no reaction." Any physical, biomechanical, or environmental model that predicts persistent disinhibition beyond approximately five minutes after the extra stimulus is falsified. The permitted regime is strictly bounded: simultaneous disinhibition during the extra stimulus and perseveration for roughly one to five minutes after, with amplitude strictly less than the pre-extinction 11-drop baseline \([PAGE 1]\).
