**Camera projection operator (Eq. 1, [PAGE 7]).**
$$\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}[\mathbf{I}\mid-\mathbf{t}]\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times4}$$
Units: $\mathbf{P}$ homogeneous (dimensionless); $\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)$ dimensionless; $\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^3$ in **meters** because the soccer-field world frame is metric (105×68 m, [PAGE 9]). Intrinsic matrix (Eq. 2, [PAGE 7]):
$$\mathbf{K}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_x & s & x_0\\ & \alpha_y & y_0\\ & & 1\end{bmatrix}$$
Units: $\alpha_x,\alpha_y,x_0,y_0$ in **pixels**; skew $s$ pixels; last row fixed to $(0,0,1)$ up to scale. Assumption quoted verbatim: *"Following [24], we assume zero skew and a known pixel aspect ratio. Additionally, for simplicity, we assume the principal point $(x_0, y_0)$ coincides with the center of the image, i.e., we neglect astigmatism or distortions"* [PAGE 7]. Breakdown regime: any broadcast camera with intentional asymmetric crop, anamorphic pixel aspect, or radial distortion produces a $\mathbf{K}$-manifold mismatch; the paper's own failure case (*"fisheye shots from inside the goals, where extreme lens distortion makes calibration unable"*, [PAGE 10]) is exactly this singularity in the model class, not a data-volume issue.

**Ground homography (Eq. 3, [PAGE 7]).** With $z=0$,
$$\mathbf{H}\cong\mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-t_x\\0&1&-t_y\\0&0&-t_z\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}.$$
Eight DoF after scale fixing. Singularity: $t_z=0$ (camera optical axis parallel to ground plane) collapses the third column; the plane is edge-on. In broadcast that corresponds to a 90° side-line zero-elevation shot — the paper's own fisheye/goal-camera failure regime.

**Camera-plane correction (Eq. 4, [PAGE 8]).**
$$\mathbf{q}_c=\begin{cases}\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{n}_t\cdot(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{q})\big/\!\left[\mathbf{n}_t\cdot(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q})+\epsilon(\mathbf{n}_t)\right] & \text{if }(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{t})\cdot\mathbf{n}_t<0\\ \mathbf{q} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
This is the operator that enforces the plane/ray constraint when a 3D line endpoint $\mathbf{q}$ sits behind $\Pi_c$ (i.e., its projection has $z_c<0$; verbatim: *"3D line extremities $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}$ lying behind the field's camera plane $\Pi_c$—i.e., points whose image projections have $z_c<0$—cannot be projected"* [PAGE 8]). Units: $\mathbf{n}_t$ dimensionless, $\mathbf{t},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}$ meters, $\epsilon$ meters. **Exact singularity:** when $\mathbf{n}_t\cdot(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q})\to 0$ — the line lies parallel to the image plane — the denominator collapses to $\epsilon$; corrections explode. This is the unreported failure surface of Eq. 4: any nearly fronto-parallel line at grazing incidence to $\Pi_c$ will trigger division by $\epsilon$ and blow up $d(\ell,\ell_d)$.

**Point-to-line distance (Eq. 5, [PAGE 8]) and keypoint reprojection (Eq. 6, [PAGE 8]).**
$$d(\ell,\ell_d)=\frac{|\Delta_y\bar p_x-\Delta_x\bar p_y+\bar q_x\bar p_y-\bar q_y\bar p_x|}{\sqrt{\Delta_x^2+\Delta_y^2}} + \frac{|\Delta_y\bar q_x-\Delta_x\bar q_y+\bar q_x\bar p_y-\bar q_y\bar p_x|}{\sqrt{\Delta_x^2+\Delta_y^2}}$$
with $\Delta_y=\bar q_y-\bar p_y$, $\Delta_x=\bar q_x-\bar p_x$. Units: pixels. And $d(\bar x_d,x)=\bar x_d-\pi(\Theta,x)$ (Eq. 6), pixels.

**Unified PnL cost (Eq. 7, [PAGE 8]).**
$$C=\alpha\sum_{i\in L}d(\ell_i,\ell_{d,i})+(1-\alpha)\sum_{j\in K_{pc}}d(\bar x_{d,j},x_j),\quad K_{pc}=K_p\cup K_{pe}\cup K_{p1}\cup K_{p2}\cup K_{p3}.$$
Units: pixels; $\alpha\in[0,1]$ dimensionless. The paper fixes $\alpha=0.6$: *"The weighting parameter $\alpha$ in Eq. (7) is empirically set to $\alpha = 0.6$ to maximize the FS metric across camera calibration experiments"* [PAGE 10]. Table V [PAGE 12] gives the empirical curvature: FS(0.3)=59.3, FS(0.4)=60.2, FS(0.5)=60.7, FS(0.6)=61.8, FS(0.7)=61.0 — concave, maximum at 0.6, so the objective is **not symmetric** in $\alpha$. Evaluation metrics with units: JaC$_\gamma$ dimensionless ratio with $\gamma$ in pixels ∈ {5,10,20} (Eq. 8, [PAGE 9]); CR dimensionless; FS = CR · JaC$_5$ dimensionless [PAGE 9]; projection error in meters; reprojection error in pixels (Table III, [PAGE 12]); inference time in ms [PAGE 13, Table VI].

**Detection-layer operators.** HRNet-v2-w48 emits heatmaps at half input resolution with softmax (keypoints) and sigmoid (lines) activations [PAGE 6]; peaks are 2-pixel-sigma Gaussians [PAGE 6]. Breakdown: sigma $\to$ sub-pixel after 2× downsample loses peak integrity; players/weather-induced motion blur broaden sigma, breaking the matching of peaks to $K_{pc}$ labels before RANSAC even sees them.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is explicitly traced in the paper's related work and reference list. The ancestor chain is: SIFT/MSER local-feature matching + RANSAC [PAGE 2, refs 36, 41, 17] → search-based synthetic-database calibration (Chen & Little CVPRW 2019; Sharma et al. WACV 2018) → optimization-based estimation with vanishing points (Homayounfar CVPR 2017 at 4-DoF homography [PAGE 3]) and dense keypoint grids (Nie et al. WACV 2021; Chu et al. CVPR 2022; Oo et al. IECON 2023) → prediction-based regressors (Jiang et al. WACV 2020; Sha et al. CVPR 2020; Tarashima ACPR 2020) → Bayesian temporal filter (Claasen & de Villiers ESWA 2024) → TVCalib (Theiner & Ewerth WACV 2023, which reformulates registration as differentiable segment-reprojection optimization) → PnLCalib. The authors themselves state the direct predecessor: *"An early version of this work was presented in [21]"* and *"our approach is built upon [16] solution"* [PAGE 2, PAGE 6]. The genealogy is a *retrocausal* one — PnLCalib retroactively justifies the segment-reprojection formulation as the correct primitive, then layers multi-view generalization and non-planar 3D calibration on top, reversing the field's prior sequence (in which homographies came first and 3D calibration was a rare afterthought). The paper says as much: *"previous studies [list] have predominantly treated the task as homography estimation rather than full calibration [59, 42]"* [PAGE 2].

The **multi-week traps** signposted by this lineage are three. (1) **The reprojection-accuracy illusion.** Because reprojection error is a point-domain metric and line refinement targets line residuals, a naive monitor that watches only reprojection error would *reject* the PnL module on some frames even though FS rises. The paper flags the paradox directly: *"Reprojection error is computed based on keypoint errors, meaning that while line-based refinement increases keypoint reprojection error, it results in improved overall calibration"* [PAGE 10]. Any ML team that optimizes the wrong monitor over multiple weeks will regress the true objective. (2) **The CR-FS substitution trap.** FS is defined as CR·JaC$_5$ [PAGE 9] — multiplicative, so CR loss dominates any per-frame JaC gain. Table VII shows SN23-test CR falls 80.1 → 78.4 under PnL even as FS rises 58.6 → 61.8 [PAGE 13]. A reviewer comparing CR alone over weeks will conclude PnL hurts. (3) **The α-staleness trap.** α=0.6 is calibrated on SN23-test only; WC14-test behavior is documented as *different*: *"For the WC14 dataset, a different behavior is observed: point-based refinement has no impact, and line-based refinement results in worse calibration across all metrics. Nevertheless, the proposed PnL refinement still achieves state-of-the-art results"* [PAGE 12]. A team that hard-codes α=0.6 and re-tunes weekly on distribution shift (WC14 → TSWC → future leagues) will oscillate.

The lineage also carries a **data-generation retrocycle**: the training-data generation pipeline (field line extraction → ellipse fitting → grid search → homography → disambiguation → final keypoints, Fig. 1 top [PAGE 3]) is derived from SoccerNet annotations [ref 10], which are themselves derived from prior manual calibration indices. Errors in SoccerNet annotations therefore contaminate training-set keypoints, and errors in the trained detector propagate back into the reported SoccerNet benchmark. This is a closed epistemic loop — the benchmark validates itself through a geometric surrogate whose labels were partly produced by the same geometry. It's a legitimate concern flagged nowhere in the paper's limitations section and worth tracking over weeks.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
This section must model what the pipeline "believes" about the scene and how that belief collapses under stress. Three mental modes:

**Crisis habit regression.** When keypoints are sparse and homographies cannot be inferred, the pipeline falls back to the "main-keypoints" subset (line-line intersections only) [PAGE 7]. The masking layer converts belief into behavior explicitly: *"When homography was unavailable due to a limited number of points, the heatmaps associated with points belonging to $K_{p1}$, $K_{p2}$, and $K_{p3}$—which would have been derived from the homography itself—were masked out from the loss function"* [PAGE 7]. This is textbook regression-to-habit under crisis: the pipeline reverts to the training distribution mode that was densest in the loss, ignoring richer but rarer geometries. Downstream consequence: on frames where PnL would have helped most (midfield with only the halfway line visible), the initial estimate is worst, so the refinement optimisation starts from a poor basin and can converge to the wrong local minimum.

**Counter-trap: heuristic voting as ensemble defense.** The pipeline explicitly defends against single-basin collapse by *repeating* calibration over subsets and RANSAC thresholds and voting: *"the calibration process was repeated on several subsets of keypoints... we applied a grid of RANSAC reprojection error thresholds to each subset. The final camera calibration values were determined through a heuristic voting process, prioritizing the method yielding a lower reprojection error, with emphasis on the full-keypoints subset"* [PAGE 7]. Table VI [PAGE 13] quantifies the return: 1 subset × 1 RANSAC config → FS=52.7 at 164 ms; 3 subsets × 6 configs → FS=61.8 at 439 ms. The counter-trap is the cost: **2.7× latency for +9.1 FS**. Nothing in the paper specifies what happens when the voting preference ("emphasize full-keypoints") systematically disagrees with ground truth, which in adversarial frames is exactly the ambiguity-replay setting.

**Clock: the 439 ms wall.** Broadcast main-camera framerate is typically 25 Hz (40 ms/frame budget). PnLCalib consumes 164–439 ms per frame depending on config [PAGE 13]. The pipeline is therefore **not real-time under full voting**, and the paper is honest about this implicitly through Table VI: the real-time-safe configuration (1 config, 1 subset, no PnL at 164 ms) delivers FS=52.7, whereas the state-of-the-art configuration delivers 61.8 at 439 ms. Under clock pressure, the recursive ToM question is: does an analyst require the per-frame posterior, or the temporally-aggregated one? The paper's own future-work paragraph points at the answer: *"we plan to enhance our approach by incorporating temporal consistency across consecutive video frames"* [PAGE 13]. Until that exists, the clock vetoes real-time deployment at the state-of-the-art accuracy tier.

A fourth, quieter mental mode: **left-right perspective-taking.** Under camera angles aligned to the field's longitudinal axis, the two halves of the pitch are visually interchangeable. The pipeline's resolution is *deferred perspective-taking*: *"This approach facilitates an effective model training process by deferring the disambiguation task until after the inference stage as an extra step if needed"* [PAGE 6]. This is correct engineering — the model doesn't waste loss capacity learning a symmetric flip — but it introduces an inference-time hook whose threshold is set *"taking into account angle distribution and visual inspection"* [PAGE 6], i.e., **not formally specified**. Under distribution shift (new stadium, new camera rail height), the heuristic threshold can silently break, and the pipeline will confidently report a mirror-flipped calibration with no confidence signal. That is the deepest ToM failure mode this paper contains, and it is *not* addressed by the PnL refinement module.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Five veto layers must be adjudicated before any downstream analytic claim is allowed to inherit PnLCalib's calibration.

**(L1) Geometric-planarity veto.** $\mathbf{H}$ assumes all points at $z=0$. The paper admits goalposts and crossbars break this: *"inaccurate estimations for keypoints associated with the non-planar rig, such as those belonging to the goalposts and crossbars, may result in a flawed homography estimation"* [PAGE 7]. Veto condition: any downstream task consuming ground-plane $\mathbf{H}$ on a frame where a goalpost error exceeded RANSAC's inlier threshold — the homography is silently wrong. The paper's mitigation is a *separate* 3D calibration path using *"two additional vertical planes containing the goal polygons, including non-planar points"* [PAGE 7], plus a ground-plane fallback via DLT on the ground-plane-keypoints subset. Veto verdict: **only pass to downstream if the 3D calibration path succeeded**, not the homography-only path.

**(L2) Optical-model veto.** The $\mathbf{K}$ assumption explicitly *"neglect[s] astigmatism or distortions"* [PAGE 7]. Fisheye and heavy radial distortion are the paper's own admission of failure: *"fisheye shots from inside the goals, where extreme lens distortion makes calibration unable"* [PAGE 10]. Veto verdict: any frame with measured radial distortion beyond the model class must be dropped from downstream analytics; there is no confidence output attached, so the detector must be an external lens classifier. This layer alone is why PnLCalib's SN22-test-center FS = 79.5 [PAGE 10] drops to MV full-distribution FS = 61.8 [PAGE 10] — an 17.7-point FS gap that is *almost entirely L2 and L1*, not algorithmic.

**(L3) Detection-occlusion veto.** The HRNet heatmaps are at half-resolution with 2-pixel sigma [PAGE 6]. Any weather-induced motion blur, rain, or heavy shadow broadens the peak; players occluding boundary lines erase peaks. The paper does *not* test rain or shadow conditions, and it does not use player positions as geometric priors (unlike Citraro [11] which *does*: *"[11] use the detected players' position on the court's plane to refine the initial homography estimate"* [PAGE 5]). Veto verdict: **unverified veto risk**. Rain, snow, and low-sun backlight are the top-three untested environmental regimes.

**(L4) Sample-count veto.** *"When a sufficient number of points, i.e., 6 keypoints, is available, an initial estimate of the camera's intrinsic matrix $K$ is inferred"* [PAGE 7]; *"a minimum of four keypoints are visible"* [PAGE 13] is the paper's hard floor. Veto verdict: any frame in the <4-keypoint band must be discarded, and there is no paper-reported probability of that band. In extreme-closeup shots (*"close-up shots with few or none visible landmarks"*, [PAGE 10]) the pipeline is provably silent — Fig. 6 [PAGE 13] shows the failure sample. Downstream consumers who believe an FS=79.5 framework applies uniformly will be systematically wrong on ~20% of SN23-test frames where no calibration is returned (CR=78.4 for the fully-armed model [PAGE 13, Table VI]).

**(L5) Biomechanical-sports veto.** Soccer players move roughly up to ~9 m/s sprint speed; over the 439 ms worst-case inference window that is ~3.9 m of displacement. If a downstream analytic tries to attach a *position* from a calibrated frame to a *time*, the L5 veto is that PnLCalib's calibration is a *snapshot* — it carries no temporal state. Consumer tasks requiring continuous tracking (offside-line arbitration, tactical shape reconstruction) must therefore treat the pipeline as a frame-level sampler whose 439 ms cadence aliases fast motion. The paper acknowledges this gap: *"In future work, we plan to enhance our approach by incorporating temporal consistency across consecutive video frames"* [PAGE 13]. Until then, the L5 veto is absolute for velocity-domain analytics.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto Scenario 1 — α-poisoning in the joint objective.**
*Intervention:* retrain the RLVR reward model to accept $\alpha>0.7$ as a signal of "line emphasis," rewarding configurations that set $\alpha\to1$.
*Falsifiable prediction:* on SN23-test, FS(α=1.0) must fall below FS(α=0.6)=61.8 and land near the pure-line row of Table VII, FS=54.9 [PAGE 12, 13]. Any reward model that emits an FS improvement at $\alpha>0.7$ is falsified by the reported concavity in Table V (FS: 59.3, 60.2, 60.7, **61.8**, 61.0 across α ∈ {0.3,0.4,0.5,0.6,0.7}) [PAGE 12].
*Verification:* rerun PnLCalib at α=1.0; check reported FS against 54.9 ± measurement noise.

**Veto Scenario 2 — heuristic-voting suppression.**
*Intervention:* freeze the reward model to reward *fast* inference; grant preference to the (1 config, 1 subset, no-PnL) configuration.
*Falsifiable prediction:* on the SN23-test set, FS must collapse from 61.8 (full 6 configs × 3 subsets + PnL) to ≈52.7 (1 config × 1 subset, no PnL) [PAGE 13, Table VI]. Removal of *just two subsets* from the full stack (6 configs × 3 subsets → 6 configs × 1 subset) must move FS from 61.8 → 57.7 — a **−4.1-point drop** with only a −166 ms gain in latency [PAGE 13].
*Verification:* any reward model that ranks the 6×1 configuration above the 6×3 configuration is falsified by the source table; run the ablation and check the sign of ΔFS.

**Veto Scenario 3 — homography downstream-plug veto.**
*Intervention:* apply PnLCalib's *homography-only* output (no PnL refinement) to a downstream task measuring field-template overlay, and reward low reprojection error only.
*Falsifiable prediction:* on WC14-test, disabling PnL must raise projection error from **0.60 m (mean) → 0.65 m** and raise reprojection from **0.014 → 0.015 px** [PAGE 12, Table III, Ours SV / Ours SV+PnL]; on TSWC-test the same intervention must raise projection error **0.23 m → 0.28 m** [Table III, TSWC rows]. Additionally, on SN22-test-center, JaC$_5$ must fall from **80.6% → 74.4%** and FS from **79.5 → 73.8** [PAGE 10, Table I] — a ΔFS of −5.7 points attributable solely to the PnL module.
*Verification:* toggle the PnL flag; check all three metric pairs; if any pair moves in the opposite direction, the reward model has inverted a known-signal.

**Meta-veto prediction.** The three scenarios are themselves *falsifiable against each other*: if Sc1 holds (α sticks at 0.6) and Sc3 holds (PnL helps), then Sc2 alone can be falsified by demonstrating FS(6 configs × 1 subset + PnL) < FS(6 configs × 3 subsets + PnL) *while* FS(1 config × 1 subset + PnL) < FS(1 config × 1 subset, no PnL) — a two-cell comparison that requires exactly the four rows of Table VI. Any RLVR verifier that recommends compressing the ensemble without checking Table VI is over-fitting one metric onto a false economy of computation.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
