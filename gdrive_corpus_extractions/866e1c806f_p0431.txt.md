Conversely, "hugging the houses" [PAGE 431] relies on thigmotactic anchoring; if the environment lacks walls (featureless plain, open field), the physical substrate of the strategy is removed and the veto is absolute: the subject cannot simultaneously freeze and remain in contact with a protective surface.

Adjudication: The instinct is valid only when (1) the threat is a motion-dependent visual predator, (2) the subject can approximate \(v \approx 0\) without respiratory or circulatory compromise, and (3) the environment provides thigmotactic cover. In agoraphobia, condition (1) fails because there is no predator; conditions (2) and (3) are only partially met. Therefore James's instinct is a phylogenetic relic misfiring in a novel niche, not a current adaptive solution.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions:

Scenario 1 (modality veto): Replace a visually hunting predator with an olfactory hunter (e.g., scent-tracking canine) while holding prey perfectly motionless. Counterfactual: If James's mechanism were general, immobility should reduce capture probability. Veto: Because \(P_{\text{olf}} \approx 1\) independent of \(v\), \(P_{\text{total}}\) does not decline. Prediction: Capture rate of motionless prey equals capture rate of moving prey; no survival advantage for stillness. Verifiable via controlled ethological field trials comparing visual raptors vs scent-tracking canines on identical motionless vs moving prey targets.

Scenario 2 (agoraphobic safety-behavior veto): Instruct an agoraphobic patient to cross an open square under two conditions: (a) remain perfectly still at intervals (James's implied safety strategy), and (b) walk at normal pace without stopping. Veto: If stillness were protective, subjective anxiety and heart rate should be lower in (a). Prediction: Immobility increases interoceptive awareness and SUDS ratings, and prolongs avoidance latency, because it functions as a safety behavior that blocks extinction. Verifiable via in-vivo exposure experiment with continuous ECG and Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS) sampling; null result (no anxiety reduction during immobility) falsifies James's instinct model for this context.

Scenario 3 (thigmotaxis removal veto): Place a domestic cat described by James as chronically agoraphobic [PAGE 431] in a featureless open arena with no walls to hug. Counterfactual: The cat should freeze and remain safe. Veto: Without thigmotactic cover, the biomechanical anchor for the strategy is absent; freezing in the center produces elevated corticosterone and escape attempts. Prediction: Time immobile in the arena center is significantly lower than time spent wall-hugging in a control condition; center freezing correlates positively with plasma corticosterone. Verifiable via open-field test with hormone sampling, demonstrating that immobility without environmental cover is not protective but stress-inducing.
