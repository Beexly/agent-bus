<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The source does not formalize recursive theory of mind, but its family-system claims require it. Let \(B_i^{(n)}(t)\) be person \(i\)’s probability belief about person \(j\)’s belief at recursion depth \(n\):
\[
B_i^{(n)}(t)=\Pr_i\!\left(\theta_j^{(n-1)}\mid o_i(t),a_j(t)\right),
\]
with units of probability. Depth \(n=0\) is a first-order belief about the other’s state; \(n=1\) is “I believe that you believe”; higher \(n\) is nested. Assumption: observable actions \(a_j(t)\) and observations \(o_i(t)\) update beliefs. Breakdown: as \(n\to\infty\), the recursion is not identifiable from finite behavioral data; in crisis, beliefs collapse toward pre-injury habits rather than updating. The source supports this collapse: “If there were difficulties in a relationship prior to the injury, these problems will most likely fester and magnify after the trauma” [PAGE 42]. That is **crisis habit regression**: the family and survivor revert to old role patterns under stress.

The **counter-trap** is clinician overreach. The source warns that the risk of not entering the client’s world is “imposing our values onto another and losing that critical therapeutic rapport” [PAGE 42]. It also says “we do not heal, but rather that we facilitate the healing process” [PAGE 47]. A recursive-ToM counter-trap occurs when the clinician believes they know the family’s model better than the family does, or when the clinician’s model treats the four-week schedule as the family’s recovery clock. The correct counter-trap is to externalize the model: ask what the survivor believes about family expectations, what the family believes about the survivor’s capacity, and what each believes the clinician expects.

The **clock** is the time-scale separator. Habit regression can occur in minutes to days; the four-week time-management system is an administrative cycle [PAGE 9]; residual stabilization is 4–6 years [PAGE 41]; learning continues over the lifetime [PAGE 41]. A plan that uses the four-week clock to judge the 4–6 year process will misclassify normal fluctuation as failure or false recovery. The recursive model should therefore track three clocks: \(\tau_{\mathrm{habit}}\) in days, \(\tau_{\mathrm{mgmt}}\) in four-week blocks, and \(\tau_{\mathrm{rehab}}\) in years. Breakdown occurs when these clocks are conflated: a family crisis in week 2 is not evidence against a multi-year rehabilitation trajectory, and a good week 4 is not evidence of residual stabilization.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
