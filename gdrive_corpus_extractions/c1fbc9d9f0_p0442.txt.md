\[
P \propto \mathbb{E}_{Obs}\left[ I(S; X | Obs) \right] = \mathbb{E}_{Obs}\left[ H(S|Obs) - H(S|X, Obs) \right]
\]
where `H` is Shannon entropy (units: bits). High `P` occurs when observation significantly reduces uncertainty about `S` (i.e., `H(S|X, Obs)` is low). This assumes `S` contains socially sensitive information.

A "blind" secretive agent follows a modified policy:
\[
A^*_{secret} = \text{argmin}_{A \in \mathcal{A}} D_{KL}\left( P(S|O_A) \parallel P(S) \right) + \lambda \cdot \mathbb{E}[U(A, S, E)]
\]
Here, `D_KL` is the Kullback-Leibler divergence (nats), measuring how much observing `O_A` changes belief about `S`. The term `\lambda` modulates the strength of the blind secrecy drive versus calculated utility. When `\lambda \rightarrow 0`, behavior is purely secretive; when `\lambda \rightarrow \infty`, it is purely calculated. This equation formalizes the tension James identifies.
