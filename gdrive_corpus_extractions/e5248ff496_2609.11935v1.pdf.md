**Definitions and units.** Spatial coordinate \(x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d\); \(D\) a differential operator; \(u|_{\partial\Omega}=g\); solution operator \(S:k\mapsto u\); neural operator \(f_\theta\); residual \(R(x)=D[f](x)\). The paper states this verbatim: "The PDE residual at a prediction is \(R(x)=D[f](x)\)" [PAGE 3]. Significance level \(\alpha\in(0,1)\); calibration quantile \(\hat q\).

**PI-CP score (Eq. 2, [PAGE 3]):**
\[
s(x,y)=\frac{|y-f(x)|}{1+\lambda|R(x)|},\qquad \lambda\ge 0 .
\]
Units: \([s]=[u]\) (output-field units — K for thermal, m for Darcy head, m for displacement, s⁻¹ for vorticity). Dimensional consistency of \(1+\lambda|R|\) forces \([\lambda]=[R]^{-1}\), i.e. m³·W⁻¹ (thermal), s·m⁻¹ (Darcy), m³·N⁻¹ (elasticity), s² (vorticity). The constant \(\lambda=1.0\) used across all scenarios [PAGE 13] is therefore dimensionally non-transferable absent an unstated nondimensionalization.

**Interval inversion (Eq. 3, [PAGE 4]):**
\[
C(x)=\big[f(x)-\hat q\,(1+\lambda|R(x)|),\;\; f(x)+\hat q\,(1+\lambda|R(x)|)\big].
\]
Width operator \(W(x)=2\hat q\,(1+\lambda|R(x)|)\).

**Quantile operator.** \(\hat q=\lceil(1-\alpha)(n+1)\rceil\)-th order statistic of \(\{s_i\}_{i=1}^n\) [PAGE 3, Alg. 1 line 10, PAGE 5].

**Proposition 1 (coverage), [PAGE 4].** Under exchangeability of \(\{(x_i,y_i)\}\) and fixed \(f\): \(\Pr\{y\in C(x)\}\ge 1-\alpha\). Assumptions required and stated: (a) exchangeability, (b) \(f\), \(\lambda\), \(D\) fixed at inference, (c) identical residual discretization on calibration and test. Assumption (c) is *not* stated but is necessary: if \(R\) is computed by finite differences on calibration and by finite volume on test, the scores are not exchangeable.

**Proposition 2 (width), [PAGE 4].** Spatial adaptivity \(\mathrm{CV}=\mathrm{std}(|R|)/\mathrm{mean}(|R|)\), scale-free and independent of \(\hat q\); overhead
\[
\frac{\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{PI}}]}{\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{std}}]}=\frac{\hat q_{\mathrm{PI}}}{\hat q_{\mathrm{std}}}\big(1+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}|R|\big)\le 1+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}|R|,
\]
the inequality resting on \(\hat q_{\mathrm{PI}}\le\hat q_{\mathrm{std}}\), which follows pointwise from \(s_{\mathrm{PI}}\le s_{\mathrm{std}}\) since \((1+\lambda|R|)\ge1\).

**Proposition 3 (spectral equivariance), [PAGE 5].** With \((\tau_\delta f)(x):=f(x-\delta)\) and \(\widehat{\tau_\delta f}(\kappa)=e^{-2\pi i\langle\kappa,\delta\rangle}\hat f(\kappa)\):
\[
\mathrm{SpectralConv}(\tau_\delta f)=\tau_\delta\,\mathrm{SpectralConv}(f).
\]
Validity regime: torus \(\mathbb{T}^d\) / periodic padding. Breakdown regime: zero-padding or bounded domain — the discrete FFT performs circular convolution, so the identity holds only modulo wrap-around.

**Theorem 1 (barrier), [PAGE 5].**
\[
\sup_{k}\|F(k)-S(k)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\ \ge\ C\,\|g-\bar g\|_{L^2(\partial\Omega)} .
\]
Two formal defects: the left norm is over \(\Omega\) while the right is over \(\partial\Omega\) (no trace-inequality constant is supplied), and \(C>0\) is existence-only — "the exact constant \(C\) depends on the PDE operator and domain geometry" [PAGE 12]. The proof's engine is the constant-input argument: "Take \(k_0\equiv1\) (constant). Then \(\tau_\delta k_0=k_0\), so equivariance requires \(S(k_0)=\tau_\delta S(k_0)\)" [PAGE 5].

**Theorem 2 (resolution), [PAGE 6].** \(\tilde k=[k,x_1,x_2]\) breaks equivariance, so \(\tilde F(\tau^k_\delta\tilde k)\ne\tau_\delta\tilde F(\tilde k)\) in general. Group-theoretic gloss: "Dirichlet BCs reduce the problem's symmetry to the trivial group \(\{e\}\)" [PAGE 6].

**Singularity / breakdown regimes.**
1. \(\lambda\to\infty\). With \(e_i=|y_i-f(x_i)|\), \(r_i=|R(x_i)|\), \(s_i(\lambda)=e_i/(1+\lambda r_i)\). If \(\#\{i:r_i=0\}\ge\lceil(1-\alpha)(n+1)\rceil\), then \(\hat q(\lambda)\to q_\infty>0\) and \(W(x,\lambda)\to\infty\) for all \(x\) with \(r(x)>0\). If instead \(\hat q(\lambda)\sim c/\lambda\), then \(W(x)\to 2c\,r(x)\), a zero-width interval wherever \(r(x)=0\). The "fully physics-adaptive" limit [PAGE 3] is thus singular, not smooth.
2. Discretization-noise floor. Finite-difference residuals carry truncation error \(\mathcal{O}(h^p)\) that acts as a floor \(r_{\mathrm{noise}}\). Where \(|R_{\mathrm{true}}|\lesssim r_{\mathrm{noise}}\), \(\rho\to0\) or negative — the thermal 2D case, \(\rho=-0.14\) [PAGE 10].
3. Spectral shock failure. Viscous Burgers at \(\nu\in[0.001,0.02]\) gives \(>100\%\) rel_l2 [PAGE 10]; "without an accurate base model, the PDE residual is not a meaningful error indicator" [PAGE 12]. In this regime \(R\) measures Gibbs ringing, not model error.
4. Exchangeability violation under drift — acknowledged and deferred [PAGE 12].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The statistical lineage runs Vovk–Gammerman–Shafer (2005) split conformal → Lei–G'Sell–Rinaldo–Tibshirani–Wasserman (2018) normalized scores \(s=|y-f|/\sigma(x)\) → Romano–Patterson–Candès (2019) CQR → Bates–Angelopoulos–Lei–Malik–Jordan (2021) risk control → Barber–Candès–Ramdas–Tibshirani (2023) beyond exchangeability → Gibbs–Candès (2021) adaptive CI. The paper is explicit that its algebra is not new: "PI-CP shares the mathematical structure of normalized scores (Lei et al., 2018)" [PAGE 2]. The *genealogical* novelty is the substitution of the normalizer: model-internal \(\sigma(x)\) replaced by external \(D[f](x)\). That substitution is genuinely interesting because \(\sigma(x)\) is trained to predict error and \(R(x)\) is not — it is a physical constraint violation that happens to correlate with error. The lineage trap is to assume correlation is structural rather than contingent.

The operator-learning lineage runs Li et al. (2020) GNO → Li et al. (2021) FNO → Lu et al. (2021) DeepONet → Kovachki et al. (2023) JMLR survey. The critical ancestral footnote is that FNO's authors themselves flagged grid channels as an afterthought: Li et al. "noted that 'grid features' can be optionally added without analysis" [PAGE 2]. The paper's contribution is to convert an engineering footnote into a theorem. The PINN lineage (Raissi et al., 2019) supplies the residual-as-signal idea; the paper correctly positions itself as complementary, using residuals *post hoc* rather than in the loss.

**Multi-week traps.**
*Trap 1 — algebraic isomorphism.* Because \(s=|y-f|/(1+\lambda|R|)\) looks like \(s=|y-f|/\sigma(x)\), reviewers will transfer intuitions wholesale. The paper guards against this ("the information source is fundamentally different" [PAGE 2]) but the guard is verbal, not formal. A three-week effort spent porting Lei-et-al. conditional-coverage bounds would be spent in vain: those bounds need \(\sigma(x)\approx\) true conditional scale, a property \(D[f]\) has no reason to possess.

*Trap 2 — CV as a quality metric.* Width CV measures *heterogeneity*, not *correctness*. A method could achieve CV \(=0.6\) with widths anti-aligned to error. The paper's own diagnostic, \(\rho\), is the right metric, and the paper's own Table 7 shows the dissociation: thermal 2D has the *highest* CV (1.062) and the *worst* \(\rho\) (−0.14) [PAGE 10]. Anyone optimizing CV will select the pathological scenario.

*Trap 3 — marginal coverage as safety certificate.* The paper's framing sentence — "a point prediction without a calibrated confidence interval is of limited value when lives are at stake" [PAGE 1] — invites treating 90.1% as a certification. It is not; it is an average over the test distribution, and the redistribution mechanism is precisely designed to move coverage *away* from uniform.

*Trap 4 — the equivariance theorem as universal.* Theorem 1 is stated for Dirichlet BCs. The paper concedes "Neumann/Robin BCs have similar symmetry-breaking but require separate analysis" [PAGE 12]. Extrapolating the 63× figure to mixed BCs is unsupported; indeed the mixed cantilever case gives only 2.3× in 2D [PAGE 7].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under deadline pressure in a certification workflow, the regression path is: (1) keep the ensemble already in the stack because "Deep Ensembles from 82.1% to 97.8%" [PAGE 11] *sounds* conservative; (2) fall back to standard CP because Algorithm 1 collapses to three lines at \(\lambda=0\); (3) drop the coordinate channel because it violates a house "no engineered features" policy. Each regression has a documented failure signature in this paper. Regression (1) is refuted by the 82.2% MC Dropout floor on Darcy [PAGE 10] — under-coverage in exactly the safety-critical direction. Regression (3) is refuted by 76.57% rel_l2 on cantilever 3D without coordinates [PAGE 7] — at that error level the UQ question is moot.

**Counter-trap.** The sophisticated failure is over-trusting adaptivity: seeing "PI-CP (λ=1) achieves width CV=0.221 (spatially adaptive) with only 4% width increase" [PAGE 7] and concluding the intervals are now *conditionally* calibrated. They are not; Proposition 1 is marginal by construction. The correct mental model is that PI-CP is a *budget reallocator*, and the budget is only well-spent when \(\rho>0\). The paper's own decision rule is the honest one: "if \(\rho>0\), PI-CP will tighten intervals where physics is well-satisfied; if \(\rho\le0\), PI-CP may over-widen" [PAGE 11]. Operating without computing \(\rho\) first is the counter-trap.

**Clock.** Three cadences. *Fast clock* (per-batch): recompute \(R_{\mathrm{test}}\) with the identical discretization used at calibration; a mismatch silently voids exchangeability. *Medium clock* (per-validation-cycle): recompute \(\rho=\mathrm{corr}(|R|,|y-f|)\); the paper's numbers range from \(-0.14\) to strong positive across scenarios, so \(\rho\) is not a constant of the method. *Slow clock* (per-deployment-quarter): re-verify exchangeability. Under geometric drift the guarantee lapses and the paper defers to Barber et al. and Gibbs–Candès [PAGE 12]. The psychological failure mode on the slow clock is that coverage looks fine *because* PI-CP over-widens — a drifting model can pass a coverage audit while its point predictions degrade, since width inflation masks error growth.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Physical layer (elliptic well-posedness).** Theorem 1's proof sketch routes a boundary defect to the interior "via the PDE's elliptic regularity" [PAGE 5]. Veto condition: if the operator's ellipticity constant degenerates (high-contrast permeability \(k\) in Darcy, near-incompressible elasticity), the interior propagation of the boundary mismatch is not uniform and the constant \(C\) in \(\sup_k\|F(k)-S(k)\|_{L^2}\ge C\|g-\bar g\|_{L^2(\partial\Omega)}\) can approach zero, making the barrier vacuous even though the empirical 63× persists. Verdict: the *empirical* ablation stands; the *theoretical* lower bound is not load-bearing and should not be cited as proof of necessity.

**Biomechanical layer (structural mechanics, mixed BC).** The cantilever 2D result is the honest stress test: 2.23% → 0.96%, a 2.3× improvement [PAGE 7], versus 63.4× for strong Dirichlet. Mixed BCs only partially break the symmetry, so the barrier is only partially active — consistent with Theorem 1 being a Dirichlet statement. But note the UQ side: cantilever 2D yields PI-CP CV \(=0.001\), "near-zero residual-error correlation" [PAGE 10]. Physical veto: the FEM residual for a mixed-BC elastic problem concentrates at the load application and at element-level stress discontinuities, which are *not* where displacement error concentrates; there is no mechanical reason to expect \(\rho>0\). This is a scenario where physics itself predicts PI-CP will not help, and the data confirms it.

**Environmental/thermal layer.** Heat conduction with spatially varying conductivity shows the inverse pathology: near-uniform BC gives 1.2× coordinate improvement (0.08% → 0.07% [PAGE 7]) — i.e. no barrier — yet thermal 2D produces the worst PI-CP behaviour (CV \(=1.062\), \(\rho=-0.14\) [PAGE 10]). Mechanism: the finite-difference Laplacian residual is dominated by truncation error at grid-scale conductivity jumps, so \(|R|\) tracks discretization noise, not model error. Veto: any thermal deployment where the conductivity contrast is unresolved at the grid scale must not use PI-CP without first checking that the residual spectrum is not noise-dominated.

**Cross-layer conjunction.** The three layers veto in different directions: physical (theory weak, empirics strong), biomechanical (both weak, correctly predicted), thermal (empirics strong on accuracy, weak on UQ). No single layer's veto invalidates the method; the conjunction does invalidate any claim that PI-CP is *uniformly* applicable. The operating envelope is: strong Dirichlet + smooth solution + residual resolved above the discretization floor + \(\rho>0\).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
**Veto 1 — Nondimensionalization audit.** *Verifiable check:* for each of the four UQ scenarios, report \(\mathbb{E}|R|\) in SI units alongside the width ratio. *Counterfactual:* re-express Darcy's finite-volume residual in nondimensional head units and recompute \(\lambda\) such that the multiplier \(1+\lambda\mathbb{E}|R|\) matches the reported 1.04×. *Prediction:* the required \(\lambda\) differs across thermal/Darcy/NS by more than an order of magnitude, so the single "\(\lambda=1.0\) for all PI-CP results" [PAGE 13] cannot be simultaneously optimal. Falsified if the four implied \(\lambda\) values fall within a factor of 2, which would indicate the fields were nondimensionalized and the fact was omitted from §5.1.

**Veto 2 — Conditional-coverage decile test.** *Verifiable check:* bin the 300 test points by \(|R(x)|\) into deciles and report empirical coverage per bin for PI-CP at \(\lambda=1\). *Counterfactual:* on thermal 2D (\(\rho=-0.14\)), the bottom-\(|R|\) decile should show coverage materially below nominal, because the multiplier \(1+\lambda|R|\) is smallest exactly where the anti-correlation places the largest errors, while the inflated \(\hat q\) keeps the *marginal* figure at 0.912 [PAGE 10]. *Prediction:* bottom-decile coverage in \(0.70\)–\(0.85\) with marginal in \(0.90\)–\(0.92\). This is a decisive test because Proposition 1 explicitly does not preclude it: "Proposition 1 guarantees only marginal coverage" [PAGE 4]. Falsified if every decile lies within \(\pm3\%\) of 0.90.

**Veto 3 — Equivariance fair-test with periodic padding.** *Verifiable check:* retrain the no-coordinate FNO on Darcy with (a) the paper's padding and (b) periodic padding with the Dirichlet data imposed only through the boundary-adjacent region, holding parameters and epochs fixed. *Counterfactual:* Proposition 3's identity requires the Fourier shift property on \(\mathbb{T}^d\); on a bounded grid the FFT computes circular convolution, so part of the 63.4× gap [PAGE 7] is wrap-around contamination rather than symmetry mismatch. *Prediction:* under periodic padding the no-coordinate error falls from 63% to the \(5\)–\(15\%\) band, shrinking the coordinate-channel "improvement" to roughly \(5\)–\(15\times\) rather than \(63\times\), while the *with-coordinate* number stays near 1%. Secondary prediction: on the periodic-domain Navier–Stokes Taylor–Green case the coordinate-channel gain should be near unity, since no boundary exists to break the symmetry — consistent with the paper never running that ablation on NS.

**Meta-veto.** Any of the three can be executed from released code and a single V100 in under a week; all three target load-bearing claims rather than peripheral ones. The paper's own honest limitations section — "without an accurate base model, the PDE residual is not a meaningful error indicator" [PAGE 12] — already concedes the mechanism that Veto 2 exploits, which is why Veto 2 is the highest-value check: it converts an acknowledged caveat into a quantified conditional-coverage deficit.
