(2) Recursive Bayesian estimation, originating with Kalman (1960) and extended to multiplicative extended Kalman filters (MEKF) for attitude, supplies the fusion operator that corrects inertial drift with intermittent external position fixes. (3) Magnetic Attitude and Heading Reference Systems (AHRS) assume a homogeneous local geomagnetic vector; the patent explicitly rejects this assumption by citing steel beams, furniture, and speakers as distortion sources [PAGE 1], thereby acknowledging that compass-based heading is not a universally valid prior. (4) Optical motion capture (Vicon, Optotrak, PhaseSpace) and ultrasonic indoor positioning (e.g., Cricket, MIT, 2000s) provide the external position frames but introduce the data-association problem when passive markers lack unique IDs.

Multi-week traps arise because none of the failure modes is stationary over deployment timescales. Magnetic distortion maps are environment-specific and change weekly as ferrous furniture, HVAC equipment, or structural steel are relocated; a factory-calibrated magnetometer bias model becomes stale within days. Optical marker occlusion patterns are activity-dependent and vary across weeks as subject behavior, clothing, and camera arrangements change. Soft-tissue artifact between an IMU mounted on skin and an optical marker mounted on a bone or rigid shell evolves with fatigue, hydration, and skin compliance, meaning the rigid-body transform \((\mathbf{R}_{IO},\mathbf{t}_{IO})\) estimated in week one is invalid by week three. Finally, drift is cumulative: a ten-minute laboratory validation (the typical duration of the walking trials in FIGs. 2, 4, 5) cannot reveal the cubic position-error growth \(t^{3}\) that dominates a multi-hour or multi-week field deployment. The patent's figures show short walking trials; extrapolating those results to chronic use without re-estimating the association and rigid transform is a retrospective trap.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the system as a hierarchy of beliefs about beliefs. Level 0: the inertial unit believes it is undergoing specific force \(\mathbf{a}\) and angular rate \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\), and therefore integrates its own trajectory. Level 1: the optical positioning system believes a particular passive marker belongs to a particular limb and emits \(\mathbf{z}_{opt}\). Level 2: the fusion algorithm believes the association \(a_{ij}=1\) and fuses \(\mathbf{z}_{opt}\) into IMU \(j\)'s state. Level 3: the meta-cognitive layer must ask, "What if the optical system is wrong about which marker belongs to which limb, and what if I am wrong about the optical system being wrong?" This recursive structure is where crisis habit regression enters.

Crisis habit regression: under nominal conditions the filter habits rely on external aiding to bound drift. When a crisis occurs—magnetic distortion [PAGE 1] or marker occlusion (FIG. 5, occasional occlusion of the right-foot marker among four candidates on feet and hands) [PAGE 2]—the system regresses to its prior habit: pure inertial dead-reckoning. Because dead-reckoning uncertainty grows as \(t^{3}\) [derived above], the regression is itself a trap; the longer the system stays in IMU-only mode, the larger the uncertainty ellipsoid, and the more catastrophic the eventual correction when association is restored.

Counter-trap: FIG. 7's brute-force association statistic tests all permutations [PAGE 2], but this creates a second-order trap. If two limbs have similar trajectories (e.g., feet and hands during symmetric motion), multiple permutations yield low residuals over short windows, and the test statistic may lock onto a wrong permutation that appears locally optimal. The counter-trap is therefore temporal consistency and rigidity: a wrong association produces a rigid transform \((\mathbf{R}_{IO},\mathbf{t}_{IO})\) that cannot persist across the full kinematic chain without violating segment-length constraints or the rigid-body residual bound from FIG. 6 [PAGE 2].

Clock: the drift clock is physical time since last valid association. Let \(T_{aid}\) be the interval between optical updates. Position covariance scales as \(\mathbf{P}_{r}(T_{aid})\approx\mathbf{P}_{r}(0)+\mathbf{Q}_{a}T_{aid}^{3}/3\) plus attitude-bias-coupled terms. The system must schedule re-association before \(\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{P}_{r})\) exceeds a veto threshold. Recursive regression means that as the clock ticks down toward the veto threshold, the filter should increasingly distrust its own integrated trajectory and increasingly demand association evidence, rather than passively waiting for the next optical packet.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: The magnetometer heading model assumes \(\mathbf{B}_{local}=\mathbf{B}_{earth}\) uniformly. The Background rejects this: "sources of magnetic distortion (caused by steel beams in floors/ceiling, furniture, speakers, etc.)" make the field inhomogeneous [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: \(\|\nabla\mathbf{B}\|_{2}>\beta_{th}\) over the capture volume, or magnetometer residual innovation \(\|\mathbf{z}_{m}-\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{m}\|_{\mathbf{R}_{m}^{-1}}>\chi^{2}_{th}\). When vetoed, absolute yaw is unobservable; the system must switch to optical-aided yaw or hold the last valid heading with inflated covariance.

Biomechanical layer veto: The rigid-body transform in FIG. 6 [PAGE 2] presumes \(\mathbf{p}_{j\to m}\) is constant. Soft-tissue artifact, IMU strap slippage, or joint skin movement violates this. Veto condition: residual \(\|\mathbf{r}_{pred}-\mathbf{r}_{opt}\|_{2}>3\sigma_{rigid}\) sustained for \(>\Delta t_{veto}\), or non-rigid relative motion \(\|\dot{\mathbf{p}}_{j\to m}\|_{2}>v_{tissue}\). Under veto, the association is suspect and the rigid transform must be re-estimated or the limb declared un-aided.

Weather/environmental layer veto: For acoustic/ultrasonic aiding (noted in [PAGE 1]), speed of sound \(c=\sqrt{\gamma R T}\) [m/s] varies with temperature \(T\) [K]; a gradient \(\Delta T\) induces ranging error \(\Delta r\approx c\,\Delta t\,\tfrac{\Delta T}{2T}\). Optical aiding is vetoed by ambient sunlight, specular reflection, and marker occlusion (FIG. 5). Veto condition: measured SNR or triangulation residual below threshold, or occlusion duration \(>T_{occ,max}\).

Adjudication hierarchy: (1) Optical measurement + verified association + rigid-residual within bound \(\Rightarrow\) high-confidence fuse, reset drift. (2) Optical + association ambiguity \(\Rightarrow\) run brute-force statistic (FIG. 7 [PAGE 2]) but hold update until permutation confidence \(>1-\alpha\). (3) Magnetic field homogeneous + magnetometer innovation clean \(\Rightarrow\) use magnetometer for yaw only. (4) Any veto active \(\Rightarrow\) regress to inertial-only propagation with covariance inflation; flag drift growth and request re-aiding.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Magnetic-distortion veto. Counterfactual: introduce a localized steel beam or speaker array producing \(\nabla\mathbf{B}\neq\mathbf{0}\) inside the capture volume while the system nominally relies on magnetometer-aided yaw. Prediction: magnetometer heading bias exceeds \(15^{\circ}\) within 30 s; the physical-layer veto triggers and the system must switch to optical-aided yaw or pure inertial hold. Verifiable metric: heading RMSE against optical ground truth; veto latency \(<\Delta t_{veto}\); post-veto yaw drift rate bounded by gyro instability rather than magnetic anomaly.

Scenario 2 — Passive-marker association-veto under occlusion. Counterfactual: four candidate optical markers on feet and hands (as in FIG. 5 [PAGE 2]); occlude the right-foot marker for a sustained interval while the feet cross paths, making trajectories temporally correlated. Prediction: the brute-force test statistic in FIG. 7 [PAGE 2] selects an incorrect permutation with probability \(P_{err}>0.5\); once locked, the wrong rigid transform injects position error into the wrong kinematic chain, causing trajectory RMSE to grow as \(\mathcal{O}(t^{2})\) to \(\mathcal{O}(t^{3})\) until the rigidity residual exceeds \(3\sigma_{rigid}\) and forces re-association. Verifiable metric: association accuracy (fraction of correct \(a_{ij}\)) and time-to-detect-wrong-lock \(<\Delta t_{veto}\).

Scenario 3 — Biomechanical rigid-body violation (IMU slippage). Counterfactual: during a running trial, the inertial unit rotates relative to its optical marker by \(\delta\theta_{slip}>10^{\circ}\) due to soft-tissue artifact, violating the constant-\(\mathbf{p}_{j\to m}\) assumption of FIG. 6 [PAGE 2]. Prediction: the residual \(\|\mathbf{r}_{pred}-\mathbf{r}_{opt}\|_{2}\) exceeds \(3\sigma_{rigid}\) within 1 s; the biomechanical-layer veto fires, suppressing the optical update and regressing to inertial-only mode. Verifiable metric: residual-norm time series, veto true-positive rate \(>0.95\), and false-positive rate \(<0.05\) during nominal rigid motion.
