The system therefore mistakes identity continuity for location stability, and the counter-trap is that the very optimization intended to save compute becomes a source of state corruption.

The clock is absolute: T_frame = 1/fps. For 30 Hz video, T_frame ≈ 33.3 ms; for 60 Hz, ≈ 16.7 ms. A deadline-monotonic schedulability test requires T_loc + T_σ + T_γ + T_δ ≤ T_frame for every frame. Crisis points are predictable: frames with high object count raise C_loc; frames with rapid articulation raise ω and threaten the ROI-exit regime; frames with poor lighting raise σ_n and force δ to iterate more to converge. When any of these push the sum above T_frame, the recursive habit regression triggers, and the disclosed “substantially real time” claim fails exactly when the scene is most complex [PAGE 9 / PAGE 10].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto conditions with adjudication hierarchy.

Physical-layer veto. The sensor imposes a diffraction-limited spot size and a pixel pitch p (meters). Cropping cannot recover spatial information beyond the Nyquist limit of the original sensor. If the required keypoint precision σ_req (meters on the object plane) satisfies σ_req < p/(2·√N_photons), then even a perfect ROI lacks sufficient photons per output pixel to support sub-pixel detection. Veto: physical information deficit. The different image is smaller but not information-richer.

Biomechanical-layer veto. Human or animal articulation changes apparent object width w_i rapidly (e.g., a thrown limb approaching the camera). Self-occlusion removes parts of the object from view. Motion blur arises when exposure time τ_e and angular velocity ω satisfy ω·τ_e > p, smearing keypoints across multiple pixels and invalidating the point-detection model δ. Veto: biomechanical motion exceeds the spatiotemporal sampling of the pipeline.

Weather/atmospheric-layer veto. Rain, fog, or snow on the lens modifies the modulation transfer function: MTF_weather(f) = exp(−σ_s·f), where σ_s is the scattering coefficient (m⁻¹) and f is spatial frequency (cycles/m). Droplets add multiplicative speckle noise; fog reduces scene contrast C_roi = (I_max − I_min)/(I_max + I_min). Keypoint detectors typically require C_roi > C_min (empirically ~0.2–0.3 for gradient-based detectors). Veto: atmospheric contrast degradation.

Adjudication hierarchy. Vetoes are evaluated in descending order of fundamentality: (1) physical sensor/photon limit, (2) weather/atmospheric contrast, (3) biomechanical motion blur, (4) computational budget T_frame. If any higher-priority veto fires, lower-priority “optimization” arguments are moot: no amount of algorithmic retuning can extract keypoints from information that was never captured. The system must then fallback to full-frame processing with reduced confidence, raise an alert, or skip output for that frame [PAGE 9 / PAGE 10].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
