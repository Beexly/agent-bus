The paper derives a 2D pose prior for sports pose refinement by extending Neural Distance Fields (NDF) from angular-only representations to a polar coordinate formulation that explicitly carries joint connection lengths. The representation on page 3 encodes each of J=15 joint vectors as v_i=(θ_i^(1), θ_i^(2), r_i) ∈ R^3 with ||(θ_i^(1), θ_i^(2))||=1, so orientation lives on the unit circle and r_i carries the radial limb length. This is a multi-hop departure from POST [21], which the paper cites as using only cosine/sine orientation and neglecting length, thereby permitting poses that are angularly valid but biomechanically implausible when lengths are distorted (page 2-3).

The training operator stack consists of three loss terms. Equation 1 on page 4 supervises distance prediction on real poses x_gt and synthetic fake poses x_fk via L_2 regression. Equation 2 adds a gradient regularization ||∇f^ndf(x_gt)||_2 to force the field to collapse to zero on the real manifold. Equation 3 sums them. Fake poses in Equation 4 are constructed as x_fk^(i)=u·ε^(j)+x_gt^(k)+λ·T_polar(ξ), where u∼U(0,1), ε is drawn from observed HPE detection errors, and ξ is Cartesian Gaussian noise transformed to polar coordinates. This ensures the model learns near-manifold regions that match actual detection noise rather than arbitrary off-manifold space.

The distance metric is the critical architectural choice. Equation 5 gives the geodesic baseline weighted by joint weights w_j, mixing radial and angular terms inside a square root. Equation 6 proposes the arc-radius distance: Σ w_j [ r_{j,1}·arccos(θ_{j,1}·θ_{j,2}) + |r_{j,1}-r_{j,2}| ], which separates angular discrepancy (arc length on the unit circle scaled by radius) from pure radial length error. The correction loss in Equation 9 treats the Cartesian pose estimate as learnable while freezing NDF parameters, minimizing f^ndf(T_polar(x_fk^cart)) until the distance falls below a validation-set threshold.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical veto: The polar representation explicitly models limb length r_i, so the NDF veto rejects poses with anatomically impossible segment lengths off the learned manifold. However, page 3 notes 2D projected joint lengths vary continuously from zero to full projected length depending on viewpoint, meaning the prior encodes projection-dependent length, not absolute bone length. It cannot veto a pose with correct segment lengths but impossible temporal joint velocity, because no temporal kinematic constraint is imposed beyond the per-pose prior.

Weather/environmental layer: The source addresses motion blur, dynamic lighting, and multi-person occlusion as HPE failure modes (page 1), but the NDF prior itself is invariant to image appearance because it operates only on normalized 2D pose coordinates (page 6: unit height, hips at origin). Therefore rain, wind, or stadium lighting do not directly invalidate the prior, but they alter HPE detection errors ε fed into Equation 4; if ε distribution shifts, the synthetic fake poses no longer cover the true near-manifold error region, and the veto surface drifts.

Adjudication: The veto is strong for static structural implausibility (e.g., crossed limbs, inverted joint orientations) but weak for extremity dynamic errors—wrists and ankles show minimal correction at PCK@0.2 (page 7). Consequently, the system should be hard-vetoed for any downstream metric that depends on ankle take-off angles or wrist release timing in long jump, unless augmented with temporal continuity constraints (which the paper explicitly says are insufficient alone, page 1).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
