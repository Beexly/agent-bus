<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s intellectual lineage begins with Lévy-walk theory: finite-speed persistent runs with broad duration or length scales producing superdiffusion over intermediate times [1, 2] [PAGE 18], and the caution that finite samples make truncated power laws, log-normals, and other broad forms difficult to distinguish [3] [PAGE 18]. It then inherits the collective-foraging and swarm literature: Lévy-like movement in microorganisms, insects, marine predators, birds, and human mobility [1–3] [PAGE 18]; swarm dynamics generating Lévy flights without a prescribed search programme [4] [PAGE 18]; and emergence of Lévy walks in systems of interacting individuals [5] [PAGE 18]. The termination mechanism draws directly on random-death and ageing frameworks: persistent random walks involving anomalous effects and random death [6] [PAGE 18], and ageing in mortal superdiffusive Lévy walkers [7] [PAGE 18], which motivate the inverse-age baseline and late-age tempering in Eq. (5)–(6) [PAGE 7].

Within football specifically, the paper descends from work on team centroids, occupied areas, formations, pairwise coordination, and transitions among collective states [8–12] [PAGE 18–19], including oscillations of centroid position [8] [PAGE 18], methodological considerations for trajectory coordination [9] [PAGE 18], spectral analysis of Brazilian football tactics [10] [PAGE 18], collective states and transitions [11] [PAGE 19], and coordination via collective movement analysis [12] [PAGE 19]. Stochastic interaction models of football collective dynamics [13] [PAGE 19] precede the present data-driven hazard approach. The most immediate antecedent is the report of broad run lengths and short-lag superdiffusion in both individual players and team centroids, motivating a “football as foraging” interpretation [14] [PAGE 19], which the authors reframe as a survival-and-reorganisation problem rather than evidence of an ex ante search programme.

The continuous-age formulation invokes the McKendrick–von Foerster age-structured population balance [15, 16] [PAGE 19], and the survivor-selection logic echoes classical frailty models in which aggregate mortality declines because vulnerable individuals are removed earlier [17] [PAGE 19]; here heterogeneity is partly observed through collective order and is not fixed at birth.

Multi-week traps: (i) the cohort spans two seasons (2020 and 2021) [PAGE 14], so tactical regimes may drift across the leave-one-match-date-out folds and stationarity of birth distributions and transition matrices is assumed rather than proven; (ii) head-to-head fixtures contributing two records on one date and separate fixtures on fifteen other dates are grouped for validation [PAGE 14], risking date-level dependence leakage; (iii) low-, mid-, and high-order terciles are estimated from training dates and applied unchanged [PAGE 5], meaning the state definition itself is retrospective and may not transfer to a new competition; (iv) run counts and duration scales remain sensitive to segmentation [PAGE 9], so a different turn threshold could shift the supported age range and invalidate cross-week comparisons; (v) active players are reconstructed without official substitution logs [PAGE 14, 19], so participation-state errors could accumulate across a match week and masquerade as collective-order effects.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The paper models collective order as an internal state of a translating team centroid, but it does not model the opposing team’s beliefs about that state. A full recursive theory of mind would treat the centroid run as a partially observed stochastic game: Team A’s persistence in a high-order translational state depends on Team B’s failure to recognize and disrupt the heading coherence; Team B’s pressing or defensive shift depends on its inference about Team A’s current order state; and Team A’s subsequent switching depends on its model of Team B’s pressure. This recursion is invisible in the GPS-only data because ball possession, opponent pressure, and event data are incomplete [PAGE 19].

Crisis habit regression maps onto the state multipliers m_L = 1.31, m_M = 1.00, m_H = 0.67 [PAGE 8]. Under pressure or match crisis, a team may regress from high order to low order; low-order intervals carry roughly 1.31 times the baseline termination multiplier of mid-order intervals, so regression mechanically shortens the run. The counter-trap is the opponent’s inference problem: if Team B can estimate Team A’s polarisation from centroid heading stability, it can time a pressure event to force the >30° heading change that terminates the run [PAGE 3]. The paper shows preceding directional stability is a diagnostic adjustment [PAGE 6, 23], but does not measure opponent-initiated disruption, so it cannot distinguish whether high order protects translation or merely records a mode the opponent has not yet attacked.

The clock introduces hard finite horizons: match windows are kickoff to +45 min and +60 to +105 min [PAGE 14], and pitch geometry from OpenStreetMap imposes spatial boundaries [PAGE 14, 22]. The late-age hazard term rising after t_c ≈ 20 s and τ_q ≈ 5 s [PAGE 23] may partly reflect boundary proximity or match-phase clock pressure rather than purely internal fatigue. Because primary inference is restricted to 0–35 s due to sparse older risk sets [PAGE 8], the clock’s absolute limit (end of half or match) remains outside the supported hazard range. The surviving population’s composition thus reflects which collective modes evaded both internal termination and external counter-traps long enough to project onto player displacement; what the trajectory records is the outcome of this recursive, clock-bounded survival process.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Pitch geometry was obtained from OpenStreetMap and converted to pitch-aligned Cartesian coordinates [PAGE 14, 22], and runs cannot exceed finite pitch dimensions or match-window duration. If the late-age termination rise after t_c ≈ 20 s [PAGE 23] is primarily a boundary artifact—runs ending because the centroid approaches the sideline or goal area—then the q term is vetoed as a universal intrinsic property of collective modes and reclassified as a finite-pitch censoring effect. The authors acknowledge the late rise should be interpreted as finite-range tempering rather than an asymptotic law [PAGE 13], which partially concedes this veto.

Biomechanical layer veto: Native positions were recorded at 10 Hz with STATSports APEX multi-GNSS units and reduced to 1 Hz [PAGE 14]. The active set is capped at 11 and requires at least seven players for a centroid frame [PAGE 15, 21]; turns are segmented at θ_c = 30° [PAGE 3], with low-speed turn guards at 0.25 and 0.50 m s⁻¹ [PAGE 22, 27]. Sensitivity tests show the rolling median preserves the primary curve while low-speed guards produce fewer, longer runs and shifted absolute termination probabilities, yet preserve the onset peak, decline, and later flattening [PAGE 9]. Removing velocity coherence from active-player selection barely moves the polarisation coefficient (0.802 → 0.814) [PAGE 28]. Therefore the biomechanical reconstruction is robust for the onset–decline signature, but a veto would trigger if an alternative velocity-threshold regime eliminated the early-onset hazard peak entirely.

Weather layer veto: No precipitation, wind, temperature, or pitch-condition covariates appear in the data or models [PAGE 14, 19]. Outdoor Norwegian Toppserien matches imply unmeasured weather effects on pitch friction, ball roll, and player coordination. Because weather is absent, a weather veto cannot be adjudicated from the current data; it remains the largest untested external-validity hole. A replication in a different climate showing systematic shifts in polarisation or termination hazard would veto the claim that the age–order hazard structure is climate-invariant.

Adjudication: Physical and biomechanical vetoes are partially tested and largely survived within the observed operational range; the weather veto is untested and constitutes the principal barrier to external generalization. The inference that collective order adds limited predictive information beyond kinematics [PAGE 6, 8] holds only within the observed biomechanical and physical envelope.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
