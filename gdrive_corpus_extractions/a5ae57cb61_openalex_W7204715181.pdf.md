The source is a computer-vision and natural-language-generation paper, not a match-outcome predictor. Its unit of analysis is an anchored caption: [PAGE 9] states, “Ci = f(V,ti)”, where \(V\) is an untrimmed soccer broadcast and \(t_i\) is a timestamp. That formalization is the first multi-hop constraint: the model is asked to describe an event “around that timestamp” rather than to infer a future result. The paper’s announced problem is stated on [PAGE 2]: “how to construct useful hour-level temporal context while preserving dynamic player-position information around each caption anchor.” The architecture therefore has three information paths: a frozen TimeSformer encoder for global and local video features, a Spatial Awareness Aggregation module for player positions, and a Perceiver-based resampling path that compresses variable-length global, local, and spatial tokens into fixed-length latents for a GPT-2 decoder.

The second hop is representational. The Perceiver Resampler is defined on [PAGE 6] as a map that “maps a variable-length input sequence to an output whose length is determined by its query sequence.” In SCPR, the global feature sequence \(G_j\) is long and variable, while the local crop \(L^\Delta_{j,i}\) is fixed around the anchor. The GCPR path first resamples \(G_j\) with learnable latents \(Z_0\), then uses the resulting global-aware latents to resample the local crop. The PCPR path instead uses the spatially enhanced local sequence \(H_{j,i}\) as keys and values, uses \(L^\Delta_{j,i}\) as queries, and then lets the global-aware latents query the position-aware local representation. The important derivation is that the final tokens \(Z^{gp}_{j,i}\) are a compressed bottleneck: they must preserve enough event-sequence, player-position, and local-visual information for the decoder to produce a caption. That bottleneck is also where information loss and hallucination risk concentrate.

The third hop is the spatial claim. [PAGE 3] says the SAA module obtains “the absolute positions of players on the field from the commentator’s perspective video and encode[s] them into spatial features through hashing.” But [PAGE 6] gives the actual coordinate definition: “Person m’s position is represented by the 2D image-plane coordinate uv,m = (xv,m,yv,m).” This is a gray-zone discrepancy worth auditing. Image-plane coordinates are not automatically pitch coordinates, nor are they invariant to camera pan, tilt, zoom, or broadcast cuts. The paper’s “absolute” language is best read as absolute within the current image plane, not as a calibrated global pitch map. The implementation also caps detections: [PAGE 6] says the set is “padded or truncated to at most a = 32 people per frame.” That cap is a hard lossy boundary whenever more than 32 people are visible or when detection is noisy.

The fourth hop is evaluation. [PAGE 10] reports a “standard temporal tolerance of 30 seconds” and uses BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-R, and CIDEr, plus mAP@5, mAP@30, and mAP@60. This creates a metric asymmetry. SCPR improves several captioning scores, but [PAGE 11] states: “SCPR improves mAP@30, mAP@60, recall, and F1, but is weaker than SDVC and Delta on mAP@5 and precision.” The paper itself calls this “recall-oriented localization” [PAGE 11]. A 30-second tolerance can absorb boundary error that a 5-second tolerance cannot. The captioning table on [PAGE 10] also shows ROUGE@30 of 35.32 for SCPR versus 35.73 for Delta, so the improvement is not uniform across all metrics. The gray-zone audit is therefore not “SCPR is better” or “SCPR is worse”; it is “SCPR changes the precision-recall and metric trade-off.”

The fifth hop is the limitation structure. The evaluation is restricted to SoccerNet-Caption, and [PAGE 12] says its “single-anchor annotations and de-identified captions (e.g., [PLAYER]) do not provide a direct metric for tactical reasoning, strategic depth, or spatial grounding.” The decoder is GPT-2 medium and remains trainable [PAGE 10], but [PAGE 12] warns that “the GPT-2 based decoder can struggle with complex event sequences that require reasoning beyond immediate visual cues.” The frozen backbones also propagate upstream errors: [PAGE 12] says the system “inherits the potential biases and errors of these upstream models.” The video rate is reduced to 1 FPS [PAGE 10], so events shorter than the sampling interval are temporally aliased. The useful reading is complementary information, not a single-feature supremacy claim.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The paper’s operators are formalized with token tensors and attention. Let \(X_f\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\) be an input feature sequence, \(Q\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\) a query sequence, \(d_a\) the attention projection dimension, and \(r\) the number of learnable latents. The Perceiver concatenation and projections are
\[
\tilde X=\mathrm{Concat}(X_f,Q),\qquad q=QW_q,\qquad [k,v]=\tilde X W_{kv},
\]
\[
Q'=\mathrm{CrossAttention}(q,k,v)W_o
=\mathrm{softmax}\left(\frac{qk^\top}{\sqrt{d_a}}\right)vW_o.
\]
The paper’s argument convention is explicit on [PAGE 6]: “the first argument supplies keys and values, and the second argument supplies queries,” giving
\[
Q'=\mathrm{PerceiverAttn}(X_f,Q),\qquad
Q'=\mathrm{PerceiverResampler}(X_f,Q).
\]
All token counts \(n,m,r,N_v,n_\Delta,a\) are dimensionless counts; \(d,d_a,d_h\) are dimensionless embedding widths; timestamps \(t_i\) and window duration \(\Delta\) are in seconds; the frame rate is in \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). The attention weights are probabilities:
\[
A_{ij}\in[0,1],\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}=1,
\]
with attention entropy
\[
H(A_i)=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}\log_2 A_{ij}\ \mathrm{bits},
\]
whose maximum is \(\log_2 N\) bits when attention is uniform and whose minimum is \(0\) bits when attention is concentrated on one key.

For the spatial path, let \(X_v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\) be the visual feature of frame \(v\), \(U_v=\{u_{v,m}\}_{m=1}^{N_v}\) the detected image-plane coordinates, and \(P_v=\{p_{v,m}\}_{m=1}^{N_v}\) the hash-encoded positions with \(p_{v,m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_h}\). The paper defines
\[
P_v=\{p_{v,1},p_{v,2},\ldots,p_{v,N_v}\},
\]
and [PAGE 6] states, “In implementation, the set is padded or truncated to at most a = 32 people per frame.” The SAA operator is
\[
X'_v=X_vW_{\mathrm{down}},\qquad
\tilde X'_v=\mathrm{CrossAttention}(X'_v,P_vW_p,P_vW_p),
\]
\[
\tilde X_v=\tilde X'_vW_{\mathrm{up}}+X_v,\qquad
H_v=\mathrm{SAA}(X_v,U_v)=\tilde X_v.
\]
[PAGE 8] clarifies the notation: “Here, Hj,i is not the raw hash encoding: it is the output of the complete SAA module after spatial attention and residual fusion.” The implementation uses \(d_h=24\) and a maximum of 32 players per frame [PAGE 10].

The temporal and position-context fusion is
\[
L^\Delta_{j,i}=\mathrm{Crop}(G_j,t_i,\Delta),
\]
where \(G_j=\{g_{j,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n_j}\) is the global feature sequence and \(L^\Delta_{j,i}\) has token length \(n_\Delta\). With shared latents \(Z_0\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d}\),
\[
Z^g_{j,i}=\mathrm{PerceiverResampler}(G_j,Z_0),
\qquad
Z^{gl}_{j,i}=\mathrm{PerceiverResampler}(L^\Delta_{j,i},Z^g_{j,i}),
\]
\[
Z^{gl}_{j,i}=\mathrm{GCPR}(G_j,L^\Delta_{j,i},Z_0).
\]
For the position-aware path,
\[
H_{j,i}=\mathrm{SAA}(X_{j,i},U_{j,i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_\Delta\times d},
\]
\[
Z^p_{j,i}=\mathrm{PerceiverResampler}(H_{j,i},L^\Delta_{j,i}),
\qquad
Z^{gp}_{j,i}=\mathrm{PerceiverResampler}(Z^p_{j,i},Z^g_{j,i}),
\]
\[
Z^{gp}_{j,i}=\mathrm{PCPR}(G_j,H_{j,i},L^\Delta_{j,i},Z_0).
\]
The caption task is \(C_i=f(V,t_i)\) [PAGE 9], with decoder \(\hat C_i=\mathrm{Dec}(Z^{gp}(V,t_i))\). Temporal matching at tolerance \(\tau\) is
\[
\mathrm{TP}_\tau(\hat t,t^*)=\mathbf 1\left\{|\hat t-t^*|\le\tau\right\},
\]
with the paper’s standard \(\tau=30\,\mathrm{s}\) [PAGE 10] and \(\sigma\in\{5,30,60\}\,\mathrm{s}\) for mAP@\(\sigma\). Caption metrics are dimensionless.

Exact singularity and breakdown regimes follow from these equations. First, if \(N=0\) or every key is masked to \(-\infty\), the softmax denominator is zero and attention is undefined; if all logits are equal, attention becomes uniform and the output carries no selective information. Second, if \(N_v=0\), then \(P_v=\varnothing\), so the SAA cross-attention has no keys or values; the residual term leaves \(H_v=X_v\), meaning the spatial path supplies no positional evidence. Third, if \(N_v\gt a=32\), truncation discards players and can create false spatial relations. Fourth, if \(t_i\) lies outside the video interval or \(n_\Delta=0\), the local crop is empty or undefined. Fifth, at 1 FPS [PAGE 10], events shorter than 1 s are temporally aliased. Sixth, when \(\Delta\) is smaller than the sampling interval or the anchor is near a boundary, the crop may contain too few tokens. Seventh, under de-identified captions with placeholders such as [PLAYER], identity-specific language is not identifiable [PAGE 12]. These are not hypothetical embellishments; they are direct consequences of the paper’s stated tensor shapes, caps, and evaluation protocol.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogy is retrocausal in the sense that the 2026 SCPR design can be read backward as a sequence of responses to earlier bottlenecks. The dense-video-captioning lineage begins with the detect-then-describe paradigm associated with Krishna et al. and DAPs, cited on [PAGE 4] and [PAGE 14]. That lineage treated events as temporally bounded intervals. SoccerNet-Caption later recast the problem as single-anchored dense video captioning, where the output is a caption at a timestamp rather than a full interval [PAGE 9]. SCPR’s contribution is legible only against that shift: it does not primarily propose better event boundaries; it tries to enrich the representation around each anchor with hour-level context and player-position cues.

The spatial branch has a separate genealogy. Early sports-video work used camera calibration and player localization, while later soccer-specific work used player distributions for tactical analysis [PAGE 2]. [PAGE 4] notes that “Global context and position-aware representations have also been studied for group activity recognition,” but the paper explicitly separates that task from soccer captioning. The SAA module also depends on the sn-gamestate player-localization project [PAGE 10] and on hash-grid encoding derived from instant neural graphics primitives [PAGE 15]. The HashGrid encoder, FullyFusedMLP, 24-dimensional output, and 32-player cap are therefore not ad hoc; they are imported from a neural-field and tracking lineage.

The temporal branch descends from action spotting, context-aware losses, spatio-temporal encoders, transformer-based spotting, and dense detection anchors [PAGE 2]. TimeSformer supplies the frozen video encoder [PAGE 5], and the Perceiver Resampler descends from Flamingo-style visual-language alignment [PAGE 14]. The decoder uses GPT-2 medium [PAGE 10]. The overall architecture is thus a retrocausal assembly: a 2026 soccer-captioning system reinterprets 2017 dense captioning, 2018 action spotting, 2021 video transformers, 2022 Perceiver-style resampling, 2022 hash encoding, 2023 SoccerNet-Caption, and 2024 game-state reconstruction.

The multi-week evaluation traps are visible in the same genealogy. First, the 30-second tolerance [PAGE 10] is a coarse temporal matching device inherited from the benchmark’s evaluation culture; it can reward recall-oriented systems while hiding strict localization error. Second, 1 FPS sampling [PAGE 10] is an efficiency compromise that can erase short events. Third, de-identified captions with placeholders such as [PLAYER] [PAGE 12] prevent identity-specific evaluation and encourage role-level language. Fourth, CIDEr is sensitive to consensus phrasing, so a model can improve CIDEr while remaining tactically shallow. Fifth, the paper reports no direct tactical-reasoning metric [PAGE 12]. These traps accumulate over long-horizon evaluation: a system can look stronger on aggregate caption similarity while being weaker on strict timestamp precision, player identity, or causal event sequence. The genealogy therefore warns against reading a single metric as a general capability claim.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive-theory-of-mind frame here is not a claim that the model has beliefs. It is a design metaphor for nested observers: the model must infer what a commentator would say, the annotator must decide what counts as the event, and the evaluator must match the generated caption to a reference under a temporal tolerance. The model receives a partial visual trace and a timestamp; it must reconstruct an event sequence that is legible to a human annotator. The recursion appears because the model’s output is judged by a reference that already contains interpretive choices: de-identification, event labels, and anchor placement.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the model’s richer inputs fail or are absent. If global context or spatial evidence is missing, the decoder can regress to local clip statistics and generic commentary templates. The ablation on [PAGE 11] is informative: the no-global, no-position row has B@4@30 of 23.61, M@30 of 23.81, R@30 of 34.41, and C@30 of 35.88. The global-only row has B@4@30 of 22.21, so adding global context alone can reduce B@4 even while M@30 rises to 24.44. The combined model reaches B@4@30 of 24.61, M@30 of 25.30, R@30 of 35.32, and C@30 of 41.47. The habit regression is therefore not simply “more context is better”; it is “when the decoder lacks a coherent cross-modal signal, it falls back to local or generic phrasing.”

The counter-trap is the explicit construction of GCPR and PCPR. [PAGE 11] states that “the model with both global and positional features performs best on BLEU-4, METEOR, CIDEr, and precision, while the positional-only variant has the highest recall and F1.” That is the counter-trap: positional evidence broadens event coverage, while global context improves caption-reference similarity. But the counter-trap is not free. The same paragraph notes that SCPR is weaker on mAP@5 and precision [PAGE 11]. The model can trap itself in recall-oriented coverage if it overproduces anchors or overgeneralizes spatial relations. A robust system should know when not to speak.

The clock is the anchor and the tolerance. The task is defined at \(t_i\) [PAGE 9], and the evaluation uses a 30-second tolerance [PAGE 10]. The clock introduces a recursive evaluation effect: a caption can be “correct” under a broad tolerance while being wrong about the exact boundary. This is why mAP@5 is a useful counter-metric. [PAGE 11] explicitly reports the weakness at strict tolerance. The model’s theory-of-mind about the evaluator should therefore include an abstention or hedging policy when the anchor is near a boundary, when the local crop is empty, or when player detection is absent. Without such a policy, the decoder may produce a confident sentence that the lower layers cannot support. The GPT-2 limitation on “complex event sequences that require reasoning beyond immediate visual cues” [PAGE 12] strengthens this concern: the language prior can fill gaps that the visual evidence does not close.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is a rule that prevents a caption claim from overriding missing or invalid evidence at a lower layer. The layers are: L0 physical sensing and broadcast geometry; L1 player detection, tracking, and image-plane coordinate extraction; L2 frozen video encoding at 1 FPS; L3 Perceiver fusion and SAA; L4 GPT-2 decoding. The veto is not a confidence score in disguise; it is a logical admissibility condition.

For the positional layer, the source gives an exact condition. If \(N_v=0\), then \(P_v=\varnothing\), and the SAA cross-attention has empty keys and values. From the SAA equation, the only surviving term is the residual:
\[
H_v=X_v \quad \text{when } N_v=0.
\]
Therefore any caption claim requiring a player-position relation is unsupported at that frame. A verifiable veto rule is
\[
\mathrm{Veto}_{\mathrm{pos}}(v)=1 \iff N_v=0 \ \text{or}\ P_v=\varnothing.
\]
A second positional veto is truncation:
\[
\mathrm{Veto}_{\mathrm{trunc}}(v)=1 \iff N_v\gt a=32,
\]
because [PAGE 6] states the set is “padded or truncated to at most a = 32 people per frame.” Truncation does not make the caption impossible, but it makes claims about the omitted players unverifiable.

For the anchor layer, the local crop is defined by \(L^\Delta_{j,i}=\mathrm{Crop}(G_j,t_i,\Delta)\). If \(t_i\) is outside the video interval or if the crop has zero tokens, the anchor-specific path is undefined:
\[
\mathrm{Veto}_{\mathrm{anchor}}(i)=1 \iff t_i\notin[0,T]\ \text{or}\ n_\Delta=0.
\]
A boundary anchor can still be described by global context, but a claim about the local event at that anchor should be hedged or suppressed.

For the physical and biomechanical layers, the paper’s limitations are direct. The system relies on frozen TimeSformer and sn-gamestate backbones, and [PAGE 12] says it “inherits the potential biases and errors of these upstream models.” Player positions are image-plane coordinates [PAGE 6], not calibrated pitch coordinates, so camera motion, occlusion, and calibration error can invalidate a spatial claim. A physical veto rule is therefore:
\[
\mathrm{Veto}_{\mathrm{geom}}(v)=1 \ \text{if the camera geometry or detection support is invalid.}
\]
This is a proof obligation rather than a numerical threshold. The source does not provide a calibration-confidence field, so the safe implementation is to require a non-empty, non-truncated detection set and to avoid field-absolute language when only image-plane coordinates are available.

For weather, the source does not report a weather-specific experiment. That is an important gray-zone: a weather veto cannot be directly proven from this paper. The closest source-grounded proxy is the blurring ablation. [PAGE 11] states: “Blurring size K denotes the number of consecutive variant frames replaced by the first frame; larger K simulates more severe feature collapse.” The same page reports that “SAA generally improves captioning scores, with larger gains on CIDEr and METEOR in the more severe cases.” For example, at \(K=30\) without SAA, C@30 is 23.78; with SAA it is 35.43. At \(K=1\), C@30 is 35.22 without SAA and 41.47 with SAA [PAGE 11]. This supports a weather/blur veto of the form: when consecutive frames collapse, SAA helps, but it cannot recover true motion that was never observed. A caption claiming a precise sequence of player movements under severe blur should be vetoed even if CIDEr improves.

The adjudication order is: anchor validity, then player-set validity, then geometric validity, then motion validity, then language admissibility. The language layer cannot override a lower-layer veto because the decoder only sees \(Z^{gp}\), which is itself a compressed function of the lower layers. This is the formal reason the veto is cross-layer rather than stylistic.

Scenario 1: empty player set. Intervention: on a held-out half, set \(N_v=0\) for all frames, or remove the SAA output so that \(H_v=X_v\) exactly. Verifiable signal: the SAA cross-attention has no keys or values, and the residual path is the only surviving term. Prediction: captions should lose spatially grounded player relations, and CIDEr@30 should fall toward the no-SAA setting. The source’s blurring ablation gives a reference magnitude: at \(K=1\), no-SAA C@30 is 35.22 versus SAA 41.47 [PAGE 11]. A system that still emits confident player-relation claims under this intervention should receive a negative veto reward. A system that hedges or falls back to event-level language should receive a positive reward.

Scenario 2: all-mask global path. Intervention: mask every global token in the GCPR resampler, so the global-aware latents receive no admissible keys or values. Verifiable signal: the softmax denominator is zero or the attention is forced to a degenerate uniform state; in either case the global branch cannot supply selective information. Prediction: caption metrics should fall below the combined SCPR row on [PAGE 10] and toward the local-only regime; localization recall should also fall because the global context no longer expands coverage. A model that continues to produce hour-level event-sequence language under an all-mask global intervention is hallucinating context. The veto prediction is falsifiable by rerunning the published ablation with the global path disabled.

Scenario 3: temporal subsampling from 1 FPS to 0.5 FPS. Intervention: sample the video every 2 s instead of every 1 s. Verifiable signal: the anchor alignment and local crop token count change; events shorter than 2 s are aliased. Prediction: mAP@5 should degrade more sharply than mAP@30 and mAP@60, because the broad 30-second tolerance can absorb boundary error. Caption CIDEr should also decrease, especially for event sequences with rapid transitions. The source already reports the 1 FPS reduction [PAGE 10] and the 30-second tolerance [PAGE 10], so this is a direct stress test of the clock variable. A model that claims exact event boundaries under 0.5 FPS should be vetoed; a model that widens uncertainty or abstains should be rewarded. These three scenarios are verifiable because they manipulate named variables in the paper’s own equations: \(N_v\), the global key/value mask, and the sampling interval. They require no new external label source, only controlled ablation on the published pipeline.
