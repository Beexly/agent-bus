If the physical transmission layer is not conditioned on, the observed double hump may reflect exposure dose (high-dose severe disease vs. low-dose latency or immunity) rather than inherited susceptibility.

Biomechanical layer: Cough mechanics determine droplet generation; alveolar deposition and granulomatous containment determine whether infection remains latent or progresses to severe cavitary disease. The “severe disease” hump in Galton’s description may map to biomechanical failure of containment under high inoculum, not to a dominant hereditary allele.

Weather layer: Seasonal variation in sunlight (UVB-driven vitamin D synthesis), indoor crowding in cold months, and humidity affect both transmission probability and immune competence.
