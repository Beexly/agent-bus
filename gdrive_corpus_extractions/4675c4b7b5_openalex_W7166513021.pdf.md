**Notation and unit conventions.** Entropy quantities \(H(\cdot)\) are Shannon entropies in **bits** (base-2 logarithm); differential-privacy parameters are **dimensionless** (natural-log convention unless stated); probabilities are dimensionless on \([0,1]\); times are in **days (d)**; signal-to-noise ratios are given in **dB**; sample sizes are **dimensionless counts**.

**Operator 1 — Personal-data predicate \(\mathsf{PD}\).**
Grounded in Art. 4(1) as quoted at [PAGE 13]: "'personal data' means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person."

\[
\mathsf{PD}(x) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; H\!\left(I \mid x, K\right) < H(I), \qquad [\text{bits}]
\]

where \(I\) is the identity variable over the population \(\mathcal{P}\), and \(K\) is the auxiliary information reasonably available to a motivated adversary. \(\mathsf{PD}\) is true when observing \(x\) together with \(K\) reduces identity uncertainty at all.
*Breakdown regime:* as \(H(I \mid x,K) \to H(I)\) (the datum carries zero identity information), \(\mathsf{PD}\) fails and the GDPR does not attach. Conversely, when \(H(I \mid x,K) \to 0\) the datum is a direct identifier.

---

**Operator 2 — Special-category predicate \(\mathsf{SC}\).**
From the Art. 9(1) text quoted verbatim at [PAGE 17]: "Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, and the processing of genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, data concerning health or data concerning a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation shall be prohibited."

\[
\Pi(x) = \mathbf{1}\!\left[x \in \mathcal{S}\right], \qquad
\text{processing lawful} \iff \Pi(x)=0 \;\vee\; \bigvee_{j \in \{a,\dots,j\}} E_j(x)
\]

with \(E_j\) the Art. 9(2) derogations. Two of these matter here: \(E_a\) (explicit consent) and \(E_e\) (manifestly made public).
*Breakdown regime — the sharp technical boundary:* biometric data enters \(\mathcal{S}\) only "for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person." Define biometric template entropy \(H(B)\) in bits over a gallery of size \(N\). When \(H(B) \to \log_2 N\), templates approach uniformity and lose discriminative power; the unique-identification purpose fails and the Art. 9(1) trigger *narrows*. When \(H(B)\) is small relative to \(\log_2 N\) — the collision regime — false-match rates rise and the system certifies identity it cannot actually resolve. The legal category and the technical capability are coupled through a single entropy term, and the paper never states this coupling.

**Operator 3 — Manifest-public operator \(\mathsf{PUB}(x,t)\).**
The paper's rendering at [PAGE 17] is that "the restriction on processing sensitive data does not apply where the data in question has been manifestly made public by the data subject, in accordance with Article 9(2)(e)."

\[
\mathsf{PUB}(x,t) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; x \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{pub}}(t) \;\wedge\; \text{volitional disclosure by the data subject}
\]

*Breakdown regime:* \(\mathsf{PUB}\) is **not monotone in time under aggregation**. Define the inference gap

\[
\Delta H(t) \;=\; H\!\left(S \mid \mathcal{D}_{\text{pub}}(t)\right) \;-\; H\!\left(S \mid \mathcal{D}_{\text{priv}}(t)\right) \quad [\text{bits}]
\]

where \(S\) is a sensitive attribute (injury status, load intolerance, undisclosed condition). As \(t\) grows and \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{pub}}\) accumulates tracking and event records, \(\Delta H(t) \to 0\): the public corpus becomes a substitute for the private corpus. At \(\Delta H = 0\) the Art. 9(2)(e) defence is doing the work of Art. 9(2)(a) without the consent. The paper's premise at [PAGE 16–17] that public data "generally do not harm the players concerned" is exactly the assertion \(\Delta H > 0\) for all \(t\), and it is stated without proof.

**Operator 4 — Consent-validity conjunction \(\mathsf{CV}\).**
Grounded in the paper's discussion of duress at [PAGE 15] and withdrawal at [PAGE 16].

\[
\mathsf{CV}(c) \;=\; F \wedge I \wedge Sp \wedge G
\]

with \(F\) = freely given (no detriment on refusal), \(I\) = informed, \(Sp\) = specific as to purpose, \(G\) = granular. This is a Boolean conjunction, deliberately **not** a weighted score: no arithmetic trade-off between "informed" and "freely given" is admissible, because a coerced-but-well-explained consent is void, not partially valid.
*Breakdown regime:* \(F = 0\) whenever refusal is exclusionary. The paper localises this at [PAGE 15]: "a player who refuses to provide consent may be unable to remain with a team or club, or may be excluded from participation in sports competitions." That is the precise condition under which \(\mathsf{CV} \equiv 0\) regardless of disclosure quality.

---

**Operator 5 — Consent-withdrawal survival.**
Art. 7(3) is unconditional; the paper flags "players may withdraw their consent at any time" [PAGE 16].

\[
S(t) = \Pr[T > t], \qquad h(t) = \frac{f_T(t)}{S(t)}, \qquad t_{1/2}: \; S(t_{1/2}) = \tfrac{1}{2}
\]

with \(T\) the withdrawal time in days. The regulatory response deadline under Art. 12(3) is one month, \(\tau = 30\ \text{d}\).
*Breakdown regime:* when \(t_{1/2} < \tau\), a consent-only architecture cannot guarantee continuity of any integrity-critical processing function. This is the quantitative form of Russell's objection reported at [PAGE 16].

---

**Operator 6 — Legitimate-interests triple.**
The EDPB conditions are quoted at [PAGE 16]: "(1) 'the pursuit of a legitimate interest by the controller or by a third party'; (2) 'the need to process personal data for the purposes of the legitimate interest(s) pursued'; and (3) 'the interests or fundamental freedoms and rights of the concerned data subjects does not take precedence…'"

\[
\mathsf{LI} \;=\; L \wedge N \wedge \neg P
\]

*Breakdown regime:* \(\neg P\) is decided by a **lexicographic** ordering, not a ratio. Any construction that scores controller benefit against subject detriment on a common scale is illegitimate under the EDPB formulation, because fundamental rights are not commensurable with commercial convenience. The paper asserts the three conditions and then never tests condition (3) against the Fig. 2 private-data class.

---

**Operator 7 — Sensor-integrity gate (cross-layer precondition).**
The technologies described at [PAGE 4–6] — WHOOP recovery/strain, Catapult GPS apparel, Rapsodo computer vision and radar, Hawk-Eye line calling, semi-automated offside, goal-line technology — are all measurement instruments. Their output is

\[
y = f(\theta) + \eta, \qquad \mathrm{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}\!\frac{\|f(\theta)\|}{\|\eta\|}
\]

where \(\eta\) aggregates motion artifact, multipath, occlusion and thermal drift.
*Breakdown regime:* the paper's legitimate-interest argument for performance processing rests on "objective assessments of athletes' performances" [PAGE 3]. That premise requires \(\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{dB}} \ge \mathrm{SNR}_{\min}\), where \(\mathrm{SNR}_{\min}\) is the level at which the instrument resolves the construct it claims to measure. Below \(\mathrm{SNR}_{\min}\), the "objective assessment" purpose is not merely weak — it is unavailable, and the \(\mathsf{LI}\) conjunction fails at condition \(N\).

---

**Operator 8 — Audit power.**
For a re-identification audit estimating a rate \(p\) to within \(\pm\delta\) at confidence \(z\),

\[
n \;\ge\; \frac{z^{2}\,p(1-p)}{\delta^{2}} \quad [\text{records}]
\]

*Breakdown regime:* for rare inferences (\(p \to 0\)) the required \(n\) diverges as \(1/p\), so absence of demonstrated re-identification in a small sample is uninformative — a point the paper's public/private argument silently depends on.

---

**Operator 9 — Whereabouts entropy reduction.**
WADC Art. 5.5 requires a daily 60-minute availability window, listed at [PAGE 12]. The schedule reduces the adversary's search space:

\[
\Delta H_{\text{where}} = \log_2\!\frac{N_{\text{prior}}}{N_{\text{post}}} \quad [\text{bits}]
\]

With a 60-minute daily window out of 1440 minutes, the coarse bound is \(\Delta H_{\text{where}} \le \log_2 24 \approx 4.58\) bits per day before address, activity-location and competition-schedule fields are added.
*Breakdown regime:* accumulated daily, this term grows without bound while the Art. 5(1)(e) storage-limitation clock runs — the FNASS litigation [PAGE 12–13] turned precisely on whether Art. 8(2) ECHR justification survives that accumulation.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The article's doctrinal present is produced by a chain of decisions whose causal arrow runs backwards relative to how the paper narrates them.

**Strand one — the anti-doping line.** *Fédération Nationale des Syndicats Sportifs (FNASS) and Others v. France* (apps. 48151/11, 77769/13) is reported at [PAGE 12–13]: "The Court accepted that the rules constituted an interference with rights under Article 8(1), but concluded that such interference could be justified in the public interest under Article 8(2) ECHR." That holding pre-authorises a surveillance architecture — whereabouts, 60-minute windows, missed-test sanctions — and the paper then imports it into the player-data analysis as an established baseline. The genealogy matters because Art. 8(2) proportionality was decided on a *doping-integrity* rationale; the same architecture later supports commercial load-monitoring, and the justification does not travel automatically.

**Strand two — the sex/gender line.** *Semenya v. Switzerland* (no. 10934/21, Judgment of 10 July 2025) is invoked twice — at [PAGE 12] for the classification of sex and gender identity as sensitive data, and at [PAGE 15] for the proposition that there exists a "structural imbalance of power between sports governing bodies and athletes" (para. 200). The CAS awards (2018/O/5794 and 2018/O/5798) precede the ECtHR judgment by roughly seven years. This is the corpus's clearest **multi-year trap**: the CAS reasoning was authoritative for most of a decade before Strasbourg reframed the power asymmetry, and any analysis drafted on the pre-2025 CAS footing is now stale on precisely the coercion question that decides \(\mathsf{CV}\).

**Strand three — the gambling-liberalisation line.** The paper is explicit at [PAGE 8]: *Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association*, 584 U.S. 138 S. Ct. 1461 (2018), "legalised sports betting in the United States by striking down the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act (PASPA)," after which "individual states were permitted to regulate sports betting independently, creating significant commercial opportunities linked to player data and sports gambling." This is the retrocausal hinge. The *future* commercial value created by *Murphy* determined the *content* of the 2022–2026 MLB CBA's protective clause. The instrument at [PAGE 8] reads: "Unless otherwise authorized under the Basic Agreement, the Office of the Commissioner and Clubs will not license or disclose to any Sports Gaming Company … any confidential personal medical information, personal biometric data, or non-public data used to evaluate Player performance in practices or training sessions." A prohibition is drafted *because* a market appears; the legal form is downstream of the commercial forecast.

**Strand four — the union-instrument line.** WPA's Player Data Protection Principles (adopted 2018, revised 2021) → FIFA/FIFPRO joint statement of 21 November 2019 → FIFPRO Player Data Policy of 3 July 2020 → FIFPRO Charter of Player Data Rights of 19 September 2022 → FIFPRO Technologies partnership with Sports Data Labs in 2025 "to launch a global technology platform for players that will enable them to collect, control, and monetise their data collected both on and off the pitch" [PAGE 8]. Read in sequence, the instruments migrate from *protective* (limitation, due diligence, access) to *proprietary* (monetisation, single repository, player-controlled licensing). The normative centre of gravity shifts in six years.

**Strand five — Project Red Card.** Announced in 2020 with "more than 850 current and former professional footballers" [PAGE 3], still described at [PAGE 9] as "at the pre-litigation stage" with "no formal proceedings … yet commenced." By 2023 the model had propagated to cricket and rugby [PAGE 9, n.1]. **Multi-week/multi-year trap:** a pre-litigation claim generates no ratio, no holding, and no estoppel. Any downstream reasoning that treats Project Red Card as authority is reasoning on a null corpus. The trap compounds because the claim's success would retroactively validate six years of accumulated practitioner commentary.

**Strand six — the Brussels-effect superstructure.** Bradford's *Brussels Effect* (2020) and Tamim (2024) are cited at [PAGE 13] to explain why non-EU enterprises comply: "this European framework has a 'Brussels effect' … meaning that it binds them to respect the EU GDPR to maintain a business transaction with business enterprises located in one of the EU member states." This is the mechanism by which a 2016 European regulation governs a 2022 American collective agreement governing a Swiss data intermediary. The genealogy is not jurisdictional but *contractual* — the Regulation propagates through supply-chain terms, not through extraterritorial enforcement.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The belief hierarchy.** Five agents occupy the ecosystem: the player (data subject), the club/league (controller), the commercial processor (the Sportradar-type intermediary described at [PAGE 3]), the regulator (EDPB and national authorities), and the union (MLBPA, FIFPRO, WPA). Represent the player's operative belief as \(B_p\big(B_c(B_p(\cdot))\big)\) — the player's belief about the club's belief about the player's own understanding.

*Level 0 (naive):* the player treats the signature on the authorization as the protection. This is precisely the instrument described at [PAGE 8]: under Art. XIII.G(1), each player must execute the "Authorization for the Use and/or Disclosure of Major League Player Health Information."

*Level 1 (controller):* the club knows the authorization is executed under squad-availability pressure. The paper's own analysis at [PAGE 15] supplies the mechanism.

*Level 2 (player):* the player knows the club knows, and discounts the document accordingly — without any change in behaviour, because the alternative to signing is exclusion.

*Level 3 (union):* the union anticipates the entire stack and interposes itself. This is the paper's prescription at [PAGE 19]: "Where players experience difficulties in understanding the data protection framework, players' unions—such as the MLBPA or FIFPRO—should assume responsibility for managing and overseeing the consent process."

**Crisis habit regression.** Under time compression — transfer-window medicals, pre-season screening, contract renegotiation — all five agents regress to the lowest-effort behaviour: boilerplate consent. The regression is not a failure of knowledge but of *bandwidth*; the paper's diagnosis at [PAGE 15] is structural, not cognitive. The counter-trap is the three-part disclosure discipline: players "must be clearly informed of: (1) who will collect and process their data; (2) the purposes for which the data will be collected and used; and (3) the methods by which the data will be collected and processed" [PAGE 18]. That triad functions as a forcing function: it cannot be satisfied by a boilerplate clause, because each element is content-specific and verifiable.

**The clock.** Three clocks run simultaneously and out of phase. (i) The *withdrawal clock* — \(t_{1/2}\) against the 30-day Art. 12(3) response window; a consent architecture with a short half-life is structurally unstable for integrity functions. (ii) The *storage clock* — Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation, stated at [PAGE 13] among the seven principles. (iii) The *inference clock* — the term \(\Delta H(t)\) in Operator 3, which shrinks monotonically as the public corpus accumulates. The recursion is that clock (iii) is invisible to all five agents at collection time: the player at \(t_0\) cannot price a disclosure whose significance only materialises at \(t_1\). This is the temporal inference ratchet, and it is why single-moment consent is structurally inadequate irrespective of how well it is explained.

**Counter-trap design.** The paper's reclassification is itself a counter-trap against the union-stewardship trap: by separating Sensitive Player Data from Stats Data, and then re-separating private from public, it forces the negotiating party to specify *which* category is being licensed rather than licensing "data" wholesale. The residual risk — named in the THINK_TRACE — is that union stewardship relocates rather than dissolves the power asymmetry, since the union sits inside the same governance ecology as the league.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A legal conclusion in this domain is vetoed whenever the physical measurement layer cannot support the purpose that the legal basis invokes. The veto is not interpretive; it is empirical, and it binds the controller irrespective of drafting quality.

**Veto 1 — the motion-artifact veto on "objective performance assessment."** The paper's legitimate-interest argument for performance processing rests on providing "objective assessments of athletes' performances" [PAGE 3]. Photoplethysmographic and inertial sensors worn during competition are dominated by motion artifact; the effective \(\mathrm{SNR}_{\text{dB}}\) falls below \(\mathrm{SNR}_{\min}\) for all but coarse kinematic outputs. Where that happens, condition \(N\) of the \(\mathsf{LI}\) triple fails: processing is not *necessary* for the stated purpose because the stated purpose is not achievable by the instrument. **Proof structure:** if the instrument's error exceeds the construct's effect size, the output is not evidence; a purpose that cannot be evidenced cannot ground a necessity claim.

**Veto 2 — the biomechanical-construct veto on "injury prevention."** The paper cites wearable use "to analyse throwing motions, preventing injuries, and supporting recovery" [PAGE 4, citing Lizzio 2020 and Cage et al. 2022]. Kinematic load proxies are not injury outcomes. Unless the surrogate has established construct validity against a measured injury endpoint, "injury prevention" is a *hoped-for* benefit, not a demonstrated one, and a legitimate-interest claim built on it is a claim about an unvalidated instrument. The paper's own safety framing — "improving players' health and safety, as it enables accurate diagnoses and appropriate treatment" [PAGE 12] — correctly reserves diagnostic authority to licensed practitioners, which is a tacit concession that the wearable stream is not diagnostic.

**Veto 3 — the surface and weather veto on optical tracking.** The article reports at [PAGE 5] that "Hawk-Eye itself has occasionally produced incorrect line-call decisions." The error model is surface-dependent: clay retains a physical mark that permits post-hoc adjudication, grass does not, and the reference list at [PAGE 21] includes Eccleshare (2025) on electronic line calling on clay creating "a ball-mark monster." Indoor stadia introduce multipath for GPS-based apparel [PAGE 5, Catapult], and thermal/humidity variation perturbs sweat-rate, hydration and core-temperature surrogates enumerated in the CBA biometric definition at [PAGE 11–12] ("perspiration rate, internal or external body temperature, … hydration levels"). **Veto proof:** where the environmental term of \(\eta\) dominates, two recordings of the same athlete under different conditions are not comparable, and any downstream commercial valuation or selection decision built on them is arbitrary with respect to the athlete's actual state. Arbitrariness is the precise defect that condition \(\neg P\) of the legitimate-interests test is designed to catch.

**Veto 4 — the occlusion veto on facial recognition.** The deployments at [PAGE 4] (Citi Field), [PAGE 5] (Brøndby IF, FacePRO) and [PAGE 6] (2024 Australian Open express entry) all operate under variable illumination, crowd occlusion and accessory interference. In the high-error regime, false rejects concentrate on exactly the populations least able to absorb them — a discrimination channel that converts a security rationale into an Art. 9(1) equality exposure. The cross-layer veto: a system whose error is demographically correlated cannot be justified by a security interest that is demographically neutral.

**Adjudication rule.** Where any of Veto 1–4 fires, the controller must fall back to Art. 6(1)(a) with a valid \(\mathsf{CV}\) conjunction, or abandon the processing. There is no intermediate "mostly objective" basis, because \(\mathsf{LI}\) is a conjunction, not a scalar.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario is stated as a counterfactual with a falsifiable prediction and an explicit kill-condition.

**RLVR-1 — Consent-withdrawal stress test.**
*Counterfactual:* a club relies exclusively on Art. 6(1)(a) for load-monitoring, and a player invokes Art. 7(3) mid-season.
*Prediction:* the club cannot sustain the processing past the Art. 12(3) one-month window without re-papering onto Art. 6(1)(f) or an Art. 9(2) derogation; the observable signature is a **document diff** in the next contract or CBA edition introducing a legitimate-interests or integrity clause alongside the consent clause. The 2022–2026 MLB CBA's Art. XIII.G(1) authorization requirement [PAGE 8] and Attachment 61 Clause A(5) [PAGE 8] are the template such a diff would follow.
*Kill-condition:* if a subsequent instrument retains consent as the *sole* basis with no fallback, RLVR-1 is falsified — the structural argument in the paper at [PAGE 16] would then be empirically inert.

**RLVR-2 — The manifest-public inference attack.**
*Counterfactual:* an adversary uses only \(\mathcal{D}_{\text{pub}}\) — broadcast event data and published tracking aggregates — to infer a sensitive attribute.
*Prediction:* with sufficient \(t\), an inference attack recovers injury or availability status at accuracy materially above the base rate, driving \(\Delta H(t)\) toward zero. If sustained, the Art. 9(2)(e) defence stated at [PAGE 17] fails for match-derived data, and the paper's premise at [PAGE 16–17] that public data "generally do not harm the players concerned" is empirically false.
*Kill-condition:* if repeated attacks at the audit power specified by Operator 8 fail to beat the base rate, the paper's public/private separation survives intact. Note the asymmetry: a *failed* attack at small \(n\) is not evidence of safety.

**RLVR-3 — The Project Red Card pleading test.**
*Counterfactual:* formal proceedings are issued in the Project Red Card matter, currently described at [PAGE 9] as "at the pre-litigation stage."
*Prediction:* the pleaded claim heads will enumerate UK GDPR Arts. 5, 6 and 9, with Art. 6(1)(f) as the contested battleground, and the defence will raise a manifest-public argument for match-derived statistics while conceding the sensitive category for biometric and health streams. The verifiable artefact is the statement of case.
*Kill-condition:* if the claim is pleaded purely in contract or unjust enrichment with no data-protection head, the article's framing at [PAGE 9] — that the central issue is "whether clubs, leagues, and companies can lawfully process player data on the basis of legitimate interests" — is falsified.

**RLVR-4 — The union-stewardship relapse test.**
*Counterfactual:* a union-operated consent platform is deployed, of the kind announced by FIFPRO Technologies with Sports Data Labs [PAGE 8].
*Prediction:* within one governance cycle, the platform's licence terms will be shaped by the same commercial counterparties the union was designed to counterbalance, observable as revenue-share or exclusivity provisions in published platform terms. This tests the internal tension identified in the THINK_TRACE between [PAGE 15] (coercion diagnosis) and [PAGE 19] (union remedy).
*Kill-condition:* if platform terms contain no exclusivity and permit unilateral player exit with data portability — one of the eight FIFPRO Charter rights listed at [PAGE 7] — the relocation objection is falsified and union stewardship is a genuine cure.

**Aggregate prediction.** Across all four, the highest-information observable is the *document diff*: consent architecture is revealed by what each successive instrument adds as a fallback basis, not by what it declares as its principle.
