Biomechanical layer: rat turning radius, center-of-mass trajectory, and limb stride frequency impose centripetal acceleration constraints a = v²/r at each vertex [PAGE 142]. The inscribed path has more vertices but shallower deflection angles; the circumscribed triangle has fewer vertices but sharper turns. A veto experiment would equate path length and total turn angle while varying vertex count. If rats still prefer the path with fewer sharp turns regardless of inscribed/circumscribed status, the explanation is biomechanical optimization, not goal-direction expectancy.

Weather/environment layer: although the experiments are indoors, laboratory temperature and humidity modulate rat locomotor activity and exploratory behavior [PAGE 142]. A veto condition would run identical mazes across a controlled temperature gradient (e.g., 18 °C vs 26 °C) while holding geometry constant. If inscribed-path preference covaries with ambient temperature rather than with path topology, the original result is an environmental confound.

Adjudication rule: the expectancy hypothesis survives only if preference for the goal-directed path persists after (a) path length and total turn angle are equated, (b) forced-run order is counterbalanced or removed [PAGE 142], (c) running speed is matched across conditions, and (d) ambient temperature is held constant. Failure of any one condition vetoes the cognitive interpretation in favor of a lower-layer account.

Veto Scenario 1 — Path-length equating. Prediction under expectancy: rats prefer the path whose early segments point more directly at the goal. Prediction under distance/energy minimization: no systematic preference, because length and effort are matched. Veto condition: if choices are at chance (mean ≈ 30 of 60, CR ≈ 0) [PAGE 142], the original effect is not explained by goal-direction expectancy alone.

Prediction under expectancy: rats should reorient to the new goal direction within one to two trials, rapidly shifting inscribed-path choice to match the new geometry. Prediction under habit/crisis regression: rats should persist in the previously reinforced inscribed-path selection for many trials, showing slow retraining. Veto condition: if rats continue choosing the old inscribed path at above-chance rates for more than five post-relocation trials, the “expectancy” construct is falsified in favor of cached habit.
