<THINK_TRACE>
The drive source is a pre-analysis plan, not a completed causal estimate. That distinction drives the entire audit. The target is an aggregate causal effect of high-school football participation on later-life cognition and depression, but the source only randomizes nothing; it proposes matching on measured 1957 covariates, full matching with a propensity-score caliper, regression adjustment on matched-set indicators, an ordered testing gate, and a sensitivity analysis indexed by a dimensionless bound Γ. The first multi-hop derivation is therefore: treatment assignment \(A_i\) is not exchangeable with control assignment; observed differences may reflect baseline selection; the protocol attempts to restore conditional exchangeability \(\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp A_i\mid X_i\) and positivity \(0<P(A_i=1\mid X_i)<1\); matching approximates this by forming sets with similar \(X_i\); regression adjusts residual imbalance; ordered testing controls the family-wise error rate; sensitivity analysis quantifies departures from conditional exchangeability. The source acknowledges the core problem: “treated and control groups are generally not comparable prior to treatment” [PAGE 3]. That single sentence is the gray-zone center.

The second hop concerns the exposure. The source contrasts 1,153 varsity football players with 2,751 males who did not play football, of whom “1,951 of the control subjects did not play any sport and the remaining 800 controls played a non-contact sport” [PAGE 1]. This is not a clean treated-versus-untreated contrast because the control pool mixes non-sport controls, who may differ in temperament and fitness, with other-sport controls, who are closer on athletic participation but may still have repetitive head exposure. The protocol’s ordered testing is a response to that tradeoff: test against all controls first, then separately against non-sport and other-sport controls, then compare the two control groups, stopping when a gate fails [PAGE 7]. The gray-zone risk is that the first gate is the most powerful but also the most confounded; the later gates are cleaner but less powerful. A reviewer should not treat a failed later gate as evidence of no effect, nor a passed first gate as proof of a football-specific effect.

The third hop concerns the outcome construction. The primary cognitive outcome is built by “averaging the z-scores for LF and DWR” [PAGE 5], with the available z-score used when one component is missing. That makes the estimand dimensionless but also changes the metric across subjects. If missingness differs by exposure and is outcome-dependent, the composite is not a single linear functional of the same two tests. The protocol checks whether “football playing status was not a significant predictor of whether a subject will drop out” [PAGE 5], which is useful but not equivalent to missing-at-random conditional on outcomes. A null exposure coefficient in a missingness model does not rule out outcome-dependent missingness within exposure strata.

The fourth hop concerns temporality. Exposure is recorded from senior-year yearbooks around 1957; outcomes are measured at ages approximately 54, 65, and 72 [PAGE 4]. The primary analysis focuses on the 2003–05 wave because it was “the first to report results from the majority of cognition tests” [PAGE 4]. That is a defensible clock, but it also means the primary outcome is not the earliest or latest wave. A post-treatment mediator such as concussion, military service, or later education must not be inserted casually into the adjustment set; the protocol instead uses military service and post-secondary education as negative-control-like checks and reports “no significant association” after adjustment [PAGE 6]. The gray-zone audit must distinguish pre-exposure covariates from post-exposure consequences.

The fifth hop concerns dose and biomechanics. Football participation is binary, but the protocol says “Football playing status is an admittedly coarse measure of potential exposure to repetitive head trauma” [PAGE 8] and proposes years of participation as a dose [PAGE 9]. The footnote is decisive: “Finer still would be to use the number of practices or games played but this data is not recorded” [PAGE 9]. Therefore the source cannot identify a biomechanical threshold, cumulative acceleration, or practice-intensity effect. It can identify, under assumptions, an aggregate association for a coarse exposure. Any claim that the study proves a specific physical dose-response law is vetoed by the source’s own measurement limits.

The final hop is the sensitivity breakdown. The protocol will report “the Γ at which the result is sensitive” [PAGE 9], and states “Larger values of Γ provide greater evidence for the studys causal conclusions” [PAGE 9]. Formally, \(\Gamma^*=\inf\{\Gamma\ge1:p_\Gamma>\alpha\}\). If \(\Gamma^*\) is near 1, a small unmeasured imbalance could explain the result; if \(\Gamma^*\) is large, the result is more robust, though never proof of no unmeasured confounding. Exact singularity regimes include positivity failure at \(e(X)\in\{0,1\}\), empty matched sets, rank-deficient regression design, logistic separation in missingness models, and the Γ threshold itself. The source is a protocol; the correct audit is conditional: it specifies what would count as evidence and what would break it, not what the evidence already is.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Define the aggregate causal estimand for a binary exposure \(A_i\in\{0,1\}\) and outcome \(Y_i\) as
\[
\tau=\mathbb{E}[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)],
\]
in z-score units for cognitive and depression composites. The z-scores are dimensionless; exposure duration \(D_i\) and age are in years (yr; \(1\ \mathrm{yr}=3.15576\times10^7\ \mathrm{s}\) if SI conversion is required). Identification requires conditional exchangeability and positivity:
\[
\{Y_i(0),Y_i(1)\}\perp A_i\mid X_i,\qquad 0<P(A_i=1\mid X_i)<1.
\]
The source motivates matching because “treated and control groups are generally not comparable prior to treatment” [PAGE 3]. The propensity score is
\[
e(X_i)=P(A_i=1\mid X_i),
\]
and the protocol uses “full matching with a propensity score caliper” [PAGE 6]. A caliper imposes \(|e(X_i)-e(X_j)|\le c\), with \(c\) dimensionless. Full matching partitions subjects into sets \(S_k\) with
\[
n_{1k}=\sum_{i\in S_k}A_i\ge1,\qquad n_{0k}=\sum_{i\in S_k}(1-A_i)\ge1,
\]
so each set contains at least one treated and one control subject. Balance is measured by the standardized difference
\[
d_j=\frac{\bar x_{j,1}-\bar x_{j,0}}{\sqrt{(s_{j,1}^2+s_{j,0}^2)/2}},
\]
which is dimensionless; \(s_{j,a}^2\) is the within-arm variance of covariate \(j\). The source uses Love plots and standardized differences to assess “covariate balance” [PAGE 6].

The primary regression model is
\[
Y_i=\beta A_i+\sum_k \eta_k \mathbf{1}\{i\in S_k\}+X_i^\top\gamma+\varepsilon_i,
\]
where \(\beta\) is in z-score units, \(\eta_k\) are matched-set intercepts, and \(\gamma\) is a covariate-adjustment vector. The source says “we regress each primary outcome on matched set indicators, covariates, and a treatment indicator” [PAGE 7]. The null \(H_0:\beta=0\) is tested with OLS p-values. For two primary outcomes, the protocol uses \(\alpha=0.025\) with a Holm-Bonferroni correction [PAGE 7].

The ordered testing procedure defines, for each primary outcome, a sequence:
\[
H_{01}:\tau=0\ \text{versus all controls},\quad
H_{02}:\tau=0\ \text{versus non-sport controls},\quad
H_{03}:\tau=0\ \text{versus other-sport controls},
\]
followed by an equivalence comparison between control groups. The stopping rule is explicit: “If at any stage of the ordered testing procedure we do not reject, we stop the procedure” [PAGE 7]. This is what “controls the family wise error rate (FWER)” [PAGE 7]. Formally,
\[
\Pr(\text{at least one false rejection among the ordered family})\le\alpha.
\]
The FWER bound is a probability, dimensionless. The equivalence stage is not a no-difference test; the source states that the alternative is that “the two groups differ less from each other than either group differs from the treated group” [PAGE 7]. Without a prespecified equivalence margin, that stage is not falsifiable in a quantitative sense; that is a breakdown regime.

For binary secondary outcomes, the protocol uses “conditional logistic regression” [PAGE 8] with matched sets as strata:
\[
\operatorname{logit}P(Y_i=1\mid A_i,X_i,S_k)=\beta A_i+X_i^\top\gamma+\eta_k,
\]
and for binary sensitivity it uses a Mantel-Haenszel test [PAGE 6, PAGE 9]. The sensitivity parameter \(\Gamma\ge1\) is a dimensionless bound on treatment-assignment imbalance among matched subjects. The protocol will report “the Γ at which the result is sensitive” [PAGE 9]. Define
\[
\Gamma^*=\inf\{\Gamma\ge1: p_\Gamma>\alpha\}.
\]
At \(\Gamma^*\), the inference changes from rejection to non-rejection. Larger \(\Gamma^*\) means greater robustness; the source states “Larger values of Γ provide greater evidence for the studys causal conclusions” [PAGE 9]. This is not proof of no unmeasured confounding.

The dose analysis uses “the number of years of football participation (as recorded by a students yearbook) as a dose” [PAGE 9]:
\[
Y_i=\beta_D D_i+\sum_k \eta_k \mathbf{1}\{i\in S_k\}+X_i^\top\gamma+\varepsilon_i,
\]
with \(\beta_D\) in z-score units per year. This is coarse because “Finer still would be to use the number of practices or games played but this data is not recorded” [PAGE 9]. The missingness check is a logistic model
\[
\operatorname{logit}P(M_i=1)=\alpha_0+\alpha_A A_i+X_i^\top\alpha,
\]
where \(M_i=1\) if the outcome is observed. The source reports that “football playing status was not a significant predictor of whether a subject will drop out” [PAGE 5]. This is a necessary but not sufficient check for missing-at-random.

The information matrix for the regression is \(I(\theta)=Z^\top W Z\), where \(Z=[A,D,X]\) and \(W\) is a weight matrix. It has units of inverse variance, \(\mathrm{z}^{-2}\) for z-score outcomes. Exact singularity and breakdown regimes are:
1. Positivity failure: \(e(X_i)\in\{0,1\}\); no comparable subject exists, so matching cannot identify \(\tau\) without extrapolation.
2. Empty matched set: \(n_{1k}=0\) or \(n_{0k}=0\); the set contributes no information, and full matching can fail if the caliper leaves no controls.
3. Rank deficiency: \(\operatorname{rank}(Z)<p\); matched-set indicators can be collinear with treatment, or a covariate can be constant within all sets, making OLS coefficients non-unique.
4. Separation in missingness or binary models: perfect prediction drives logistic coefficients to infinity; no finite estimate exists.
5. Sensitivity breakdown: \(\Gamma^*\) is reached; at that point the p-value crosses \(\alpha\), and the causal claim is no longer robust to the stated bound.
6. Composite-metric breakdown: if LF or DWR is missing, \(C_i=\frac12(z_{LF,i}+z_{DWR,i})\) becomes \(C_i=z_{\text{available}}\), so the outcome is a different linear combination; differential missingness by arm mixes metrics.
7. Ordered-testing breakdown: if analysts test downstream hypotheses after a failed gate, FWER control is lost.
8. Equivalence breakdown: if no equivalence margin is fixed before analysis, the control-group equivalence stage cannot be interpreted quantitatively.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The protocol sits at the intersection of two genealogies. The first is the CTE and sports-concussion genealogy. The source states: “Chronic Trauma Encephalopathy (CTE) is a neurodegenerative disease that is the result of repetitive brain trauma” [PAGE 1]. It cites professional-football evidence that “87 of 91 have been found to have chronic trauma encephalopathy (CTE)” among donated brains [PAGE 2] and that “Professional football players are 3 times more likely to die of a neurodegenerative disease than the general U.S. population” [PAGE 2]. Those claims are genealogically important but selected: brain donation is not a random sample, and professional exposure is not high-school exposure. The protocol’s historical contribution is to move the question to the Wisconsin Longitudinal Study of 1957 high-school graduates, using “1,153 high school males who played varsity football” and “2,751 male students who did not” [PAGE 1]. The retrocausal trap is transporting a professional-level, convenience-sample signal backward into a 1957 high-school cohort as if the exposure dose and selection mechanism were constant. The source itself warns that high-school players have fewer years of exposure and that positive developmental effects could counterbalance harm [PAGE 2].

The second genealogy is the Rosenbaum-Rubin observational-study lineage. The protocol uses full matching as “the optimal sub-classification for an observational study (Rosenbaum, 1991)” [PAGE 6], covariance adjustment in randomized experiments and observational studies (Rosenbaum, 2002a; Hansen, 2004) [PAGE 7], testing in order (Rosenbaum, 2008) [PAGE 7], and sensitivity analysis (Rosenbaum, 2002b) [PAGE 9]. This lineage is explicitly anti-naive: it treats matching as design, regression as residual adjustment, ordered testing as multiplicity control, and Γ as a quantitative fragility audit. The genealogical trap is treating any one of these tools as a substitute for the others. Matching alone does not handle unmeasured confounding; regression alone does not fix poor overlap; a significant p-value alone does not survive multiplicity; a large Γ alone does not prove causality.

Multi-week and long-horizon traps include: (i) the professional-to-high-school transport trap; (ii) the brain-donation selection trap; (iii) the survivor and missing-outcome trap, because Table 2 shows many subjects with no primary outcome [PAGE 5]; (iv) the control-contamination trap, because other-sport controls may have repetitive head exposure and non-sport controls may differ in temperament and fitness [PAGE 7]; (v) the yearbook-misclassification trap, because complex schools had a football-playing rate “half that in non-complex schools” and were dropped [PAGE 3 footnote 3]; (vi) the multiplicity and ordered-gate trap; (vii) the coarse-dose trap, since practices and games are unrecorded [PAGE 9]; (viii) the post-treatment-adjustment trap, which would turn a covariate into a mediator; and (ix) the equivalence-as-no-effect trap. These are not one-week coding issues; they are design commitments that determine what any later result can mean.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when uncertainty produces a fallback to the simplest comparison: football players versus non-football players, unadjusted, with a significant p-value treated as proof. The source blocks that habit with matching, regression adjustment, ordered testing, and sensitivity analysis. But the counter-trap is also real: a pre-analysis plan can become a ritual. If the first ordered gate fails, the protocol stops [PAGE 7]; that protects FWER but can suppress informative control-specific estimates. If the other-sport control group is already balanced before matching, as the source notes for Match 3 [PAGE 8], matching may buy little and cost power. If the non-sport control group is large but confounded by temperament and fitness, the first gate may be the most powerful and the most biased. A recursive theory of mind must model the skeptical reviewer at each gate: after a failed first gate, the reviewer will ask why later gates were not tested; after a passed first gate, the reviewer will ask whether the effect is football-specific or sport-general; after a passed other-sport comparison, the reviewer will ask whether the other sport was truly non-contact; after a failed equivalence test, the reviewer will ask whether the control groups were powered to be equivalent.

The clock is the protocol’s strongest defense. Exposure is recorded in 1957; outcomes are measured at approximately ages 54, 65, and 72 [PAGE 4]. The primary wave is 2003–05 because it was “the first to report results from the majority of cognition tests” [PAGE 4]. That clock prevents reverse causation from later-life cognition to high-school football. It also exposes a different vulnerability: the 2011–13 wave has more attrition, and the 1993 wave lacks most cognitive tests. The investigator must resist the crisis habit of switching the primary outcome to whichever wave is convenient. The counter-trap is to prespecify the age-65 composite and then report age-72 and all available ages as secondary, with multiplicity control [PAGE 5, PAGE 8]. The recursive mind of the participant and family is also relevant: a null result may be read as “football is safe,” while a positive result may be read as “football causes CTE.” The protocol cannot support either overread. It estimates an aggregate association under assumptions for a coarse exposure; it does not measure concussion, CTE pathology, or biomechanical dose. The clock also applies to covariates: most are 1957, but some are marked as 1975-recorded in Appendix A [PAGE 12–13]. Any 1975 variable that could be affected by high-school football must be treated as post-exposure, not as a baseline confounder, unless its referent is clearly retrospective.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer is supported: football participation is a real exposure, and the source treats it as a coarse marker of “potential exposure to repetitive head trauma” [PAGE 8]. But the physical layer does not license a biomechanical claim. The source does not measure helmet acceleration, hit count, practice intensity, position, or concussion history. It says directly: “Finer still would be to use the number of practices or games played but this data is not recorded” [PAGE 9]. Therefore a biomechanical veto is triggered for any claim of the form “the study shows that \(N\) hits or \(g\)-force causes later cognitive decline.” The study can estimate \(\tau\) for binary football participation and, more weakly, a years-of-participation dose. It cannot identify a threshold or a force-response function. A formal cross-layer proof is simple: let \(H_i\) be unmeasured repetitive head trauma. The observed exposure \(A_i\) affects \(Y_i\) through \(H_i\) and possibly other pathways. Without \(H_i\), the aggregate effect \(\tau=\mathbb{E}[Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)]\) may be identifiable under ignorability, but the controlled direct effect or biomechanical dose effect is not. Conditioning on a post-treatment \(H_i\) would induce selection and break the aggregate interpretation.

The weather layer has no source support. There is no weather variable, no climate covariate, no meteorological exposure, and no seasonal outcome model in the 15 pages. Any weather-based explanation or weather-adjusted estimate is therefore vetoed as unsupported by the drive source. If a reviewer introduces weather as a confounder, it becomes an unmeasured confounder and must be handled by the Γ sensitivity bound, not by assertion. The physical/biomechanical/weather adjudication is thus: physical exposure allowed as a coarse binary or years dose; biomechanical dose vetoed unless new measured data are added; weather vetoed because absent. The breakdown regime is when an analyst substitutes a measured proxy for an unmeasured mechanism and then uses the proxy as if it were the mechanism. The protocol avoids that error by keeping the exposure coarse and the sensitivity analysis explicit.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
