Maze geometry can be formalized as a graph \(\mathcal G=(V,E)\) with turn sequence \(\tau=(v_1,\dots,v_L)\) and path length \(d(\tau)=\sum_i w(v_i,v_{i+1})\) in meters. Hamill’s contrast on [PAGE 83] between “sequence of turns” and “distances” is represented by the comparison between symbolic sequence complexity, ideally \(K(\tau)\) in bits, and metric length \(d(\tau)\). The shortcut singularity occurs when a graph edge exists such that \(d_G(v_{\text{start}},v_{\text{goal}})<d(\tau)\); an agent locked into the trained turn sequence is then systematically suboptimal, and the error is representational, not motivational. Physical units used throughout: seconds for latency, meters for path length, bits/nats for information, dimensionless probabilities. All specific effect sizes, apparatus dimensions, and environmental conditions are absent from [PAGE 83] and must be drawn from the cited originals, not inferred from this reference list.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage traceable from [PAGE 83] begins with Watson’s Behavior, an Introduction to Comparative Psychology (1914) [PAGE 83], which frames behavior in stimulus-response terms and resists cognitive purpose. Tolman’s chapter “Expectations of Goal- and Means-Objects” [PAGE 83] inverts that priority: the organism is treated as expecting particular outcomes and selecting means accordingly. The cited studies form the experimental boundary conditions for that inversion. Ewert’s inverted retinal stimulation work [PAGE 83] and Stratton’s vision-without-inversion experiments [PAGE 83] belong to the perceptual-adaptation lineage that later intersects with sensorimotor recalibration and, much later, with active-inference accounts of forward models. Macfarlane’s kinesthesis study [PAGE 83] anchors the proprioceptive side of maze learning; Hamill’s turn-sequence-versus-distance paper [PAGE 83] anchors the symbolic-versus-metric debate; Miles’s elevated narrow-path and alley-maze comparisons [PAGE 83] introduce affective and biomechanical confounds into transfer; Warden and Warner’s standardized maze [PAGE 83] attempts to remove apparatus as a confound; and Tinklepaugh’s monkey study [PAGE 83] extends representational expectancy beyond rodents.

External to [PAGE 83], but explicitly flagged as not present on this page, are Tolman and Honzik’s latent-learning demonstrations, O’Keefe and Nadel’s hippocampal cognitive-map theory, and modern expected-free-energy formulations. The multi-week trap is inherent in this lineage: maze experiments cited here span 1896–1931 [PAGE 83] and typically required days or weeks of training. A short probe after week 1 may measure performance, whereas a probe after week 4 may measure retention, strategy transfer, or sensory adaptation. Because Stratton’s inversion adaptation and maze kinesthetic learning both unfold over extended exposure [PAGE 83, Stratton; Macfarlane], a multi-week design risks non-stationarity: motivation, circadian phase, and sensory precision can drift while the nominal contingency remains constant. The counter-trap is to assume that a stable asymptotic curve proves cognitive expectancy; it may instead reflect a cached kinesthetic habit [PAGE 83, Macfarlane] that is insensitive to goal devaluation. The genealogical danger, therefore, is retrospective teleology: reading Tolman’s purpose language back into Watsonian references, or reading modern place-cell findings back into 1932 reference titles, without trial-level data from [PAGE 83] or the cited originals.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind applies at two levels: the animal modeling the experimenter’s contingency, and the experimenter modeling the animal’s model. Crisis habit regression occurs when threat or biomechanical constraint shifts control from expectancy to cached response. On [PAGE 83], Miles’s narrow-path elevated maze provides the trigger: elevation and narrowness raise the stakes of an error, so under stress the rat may replay the previously reinforced turn sequence rather than recompute the means-object path to the goal object. Formalize two traces over trials \(t\): habit \(H_{t+1}=H_t+\alpha_h\delta_t\) and expectancy \(E_{t+1}=E_t+\alpha_e\delta_t\), with \(\alpha_h,\alpha_e\in[0,1]\) and prediction error \(\delta_t\) dimensionless. Assumptions: \(\alpha_h\) accumulates slowly with overtraining, while \(\alpha_e\) tracks contingency changes more rapidly. Breakdown or “habit singularity”: after extensive training, behavior becomes governed by \(H\) even when the goal is devalued, so sensitivity to the means-goal relation collapses despite intact expectancy representations.

The counter-trap is for the experimenter to interpret regression as “no learning” and stop probing. A valid counter-probe is start-box reversal or goal devaluation: if the animal still emits the trained turn sequence when the outcome is devalued, habit regression is confirmed; if it switches to a shorter path, expectancy was present and the earlier regression was a precision-weighting artifact under threat, not an absence of representation. The clock variable is session time and inter-trial interval. Across weeks, a confound \(C(t)=C_0+\gamma t\) can accumulate through fatigue, circadian drift, or sensory adaptation [PAGE 83, Stratton adaptation timescale; Macfarlane kinesthetic training]. If \(\gamma\neq0\), within-session comparisons across days are invalid unless counterbalanced. [PAGE 83] contains no \(t\), \(ITI\), or \(\gamma\), so any clock model is external and must be vetoed unless the original cited papers supply trial timestamps.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: maze path width. Miles’s narrow-path elevated maze [PAGE 83] imposes width \(w_{\text{path}}\). Let rat body width be \(w_r\) and whisker clearance margin be \(\delta\). If \(w_{\text{path}}<w_r+2\delta\), head sweeps and normal exploratory sampling are mechanically truncated; the result is a biomechanical veto, not a psychological failure of expectancy. Exact \(w_r\) and \(\delta\) are not printed on [PAGE 83] and must be taken from Miles 1927/1930; using invented values would violate the no-fake-ratio invariant.

Biomechanical veto: sensory inversion. Ewert [PAGE 83] and Stratton [PAGE 83] rotate or invert the retinal image. A pi-rotation can be represented by \(R=\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1)\) in the eye-head-body transform. The sensorimotor Jacobian \(J=\partial s/\partial p\) must be recalibrated; without visual feedback, the forward model cannot converge and spatial behavior should collapse. If an experiment claims stable spatially coordinated behavior under sudden inversion with zero recalibration trials, the claim is vetoed by the Jacobian sign inconsistency.

Weather/HVAC veto: [PAGE 83] reports no temperature, humidity, barometric pressure, or airflow. Laboratory maze studies implicitly assume HVAC control, but the reference page itself provides zero atmospheric evidence. If odor trails or thermal cues serve as means-objects [PAGE 83 heading], then uncontrolled \(T\) or relative humidity \(\phi\) can alter plume dispersion and invalidate cross-lab comparison with Warden and Warner’s standardized maze [PAGE 83]. Because no weather data appear on [PAGE 83], any assertion of weather invariance is not supported and should be treated as missing-data veto until the original methods are inspected.

Adjudication rule: a behavioral result survives only if (i) path width passes the physical clearance test, (ii) sensory transform is either uninverted or explicitly recalibrated over logged trials, and (iii) atmospheric covariates are recorded or shown to be irrelevant. Failure at any layer vetoes the psychological interpretation at that layer, independent of statistical significance, because no statistical test appears on [PAGE 83] to rescue a physically confounded design.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios. Each has a checkable predicate from logged video/inertial/maze data and an explicit prediction; no spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds are used.

Scenario 1 — Inverted-retina sensorimotor veto [PAGE 83, Ewert; Stratton]. Predicate: from camera and inertial logs, compute the signed angle \(\theta_t\) between visual heading and locomotor heading. Veto if \(|\theta_t-\pi|<5^\circ\) is maintained and no recalibration block occurs after \(N\) trials. Prediction: binary-turn choice accuracy starts at chance \(A_{\text{chance}}=0.5\), then recovers as \(A_t=A_{\text{chance}}+(A_{\text{pre}}-A_{\text{chance}})\exp(-t/\tau_{\text{adapt}})\), with \(t\) in trials and \(\tau_{\text{adapt}}\) estimated from data. If accuracy does not exceed \(0.55\) within 200 inversion trials, veto the claim that spatial behavior is purely visually driven without proprioceptive recalibration. Breakdown: if the logged animal never receives visual feedback, the recovery term is undefined and the scenario is not applicable.

Scenario 2 — Kinesthetic versus distance control veto [PAGE 83, Macfarlane; Hamill]. Predicate: automate door openings to expose a graph shortcut with \(d_G(v_{\text{start}},v_{\text{goal}})<d(\tau_{\text{trained}})\). Record error rate \(e_t\in[0,1]\) across shortcut exposures. Veto if the animal is physically able to take the shortcut but consistently replays the trained turn sequence. Prediction: under response/habit control, \(e_t=e_0\exp(-t/\tau_s)+e_\infty\) with \(e_\infty\) significantly above 0 for the first \(m\) exposures; under place/expectancy control, \(e_t\) should decay toward 0 as the graph-distance contingency is learned. If \(e_\infty\) is not statistically separable from 0 after 40 shortcut trials, veto the habit-only account. Units: errors are dimensionless probabilities; \(t\) is trials.

Scenario 3 — Elevated narrow-path physical veto [PAGE 83, Miles; Warden & Warner]. Predicate: measure path width \(w\) and height \(h\); compare the same maze pattern in alley versus elevated form. Veto if \(w<w_r+2\delta\) or if freezing latency \(L_{\text{elevated}}\) exceeds alley latency by \(\Delta L>5\) s. Prediction: under a pure pattern-learning account, turn-sequence entropy should be unchanged by elevation; under the physical/anxiety confound, latency increases and head-sweep rate \(\lambda_{\text{sweep}}\) in sweeps/min decreases. If \(\lambda_{\text{sweep}}\) is unchanged but errors rise, the psychological account survives; if \(\lambda_{\text{sweep}}\) drops and latency rises, veto the claim that the elevated-maze difference reflects maze-pattern learning rather than biomechanical threat. All times in seconds; rates in min\(^{-1}\).
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
