Veto layers are adjudicated in descending physical priority. If any layer fires, p₁ is suppressed or reverted to p₀ with an uncertainty flag; no betting odds or wagering implications are inferred.

Physical layer: The court manifold Ω is bounded. If x₁ ∉ Ω or x₂ ∉ Ω, the tracking solution is invalid and p₁ is vetoed. Ball kinematics must satisfy projectile bounds; for example, serve speed v_ball cannot exceed roughly 60 m/s in elite tennis. A feature trajectory τ(s) implying v_ball > v_max triggers a physical veto because it violates the medium’s energy budget.

Biomechanical layer: Human reaction latency τ_react ≥ 150 ms and sprint acceleration bounds ‖a_i‖₂ ≤ a_max (≈ 6–8 m/s² for sustained efforts, higher for brief bursts) constrain observable v_i and a_i. If [PAGE 26] velocity/acceleration features report ‖a_i‖₂ beyond physiological limits, the sensor stream is vetoed as noise or artifact. Save attempts are also bounded by reach: if the time from ball strike to target arrival is less than τ_react plus the defender’s displacement time over d_line, the save probability is physically forced toward zero regardless of ML output.

Weather layer: Although [PAGE 26] lists court surface type s but not ambient weather, cross-layer adjudication must include wind velocity v_wind [m/s]. If ‖v_wind‖ exceeds a threshold that materially deforms τ(s) beyond the model’s training envelope, the trajectory feature is vetoed unless the model is explicitly conditioned on v_wind.

Adjudication order: Physical → Biomechanical → Weather → ML output. The fallback after veto is p₀ (initial position-only probability) with inflated epistemic uncertainty, ensuring that p₁ never claims precision the input geometry cannot support.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-via-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios, each with a counterfactual and a testable prediction.

Scenario 1 — Second-player occlusion. Counterfactual: had x₂ been observed within proximity r_prox of L, d_line would be < r_prox and I_prox = 1. Actual: tracker loses x₂, so x₂ = ∅. Veto: the d_line and I_prox features are undefined; p₁ reverts to p₀ conditioned only on D₀ = {x₁, x_T}. Prediction: until track reacquisition, the expected calibration error (ECE) increases by at least the posterior standard deviation σ_{p₀}, and the log-loss gap between p₁ and p₀ converges to zero.

Scenario 2 — Fatigue overload. Counterfactual: a rested player at shot count n₀ exhibits baseline velocity v_i(n₀) and acceleration a_i(n₀). Actual: current shot count n > n_fatigue (e.g., n > 25 high-intensity shots in a single tennis set without rest). Veto: the velocity and acceleration terms are excluded from F; p₁ is computed from positional features {x₁, x₂, x_T, d_line, φ} only. Prediction: p₁ regresses toward p₀, and the Brier score improves relative to the non-vetoed model because overconfident kinematic extrapolation is removed.

Scenario 3 — Surface-label mismatch. Counterfactual: if court surface s_obs matched the training surface s_train, the friction-dependent decay of v_i along τ(s) would follow the GAN’s learned monotonic weights λ_k(s_train). Actual: s_obs ≠ s_train (e.g., model trained on clay, deployed on hard court). Veto: surface-conditioned monotonic weights λ_k(s_train) are disabled; a surface-agnostic baseline is used. Prediction: expected calibration error spikes above 0.05 unless domain adaptation is applied, and the updated probability p₁ shows systematic overprediction of winner probability on the low-friction surface because sliding increases effective d_line beyond the clay-trained constraint manifold.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
