High passes (with XP): $\Delta R = 0.830-0.575 = 0.255$, confirming the "over 25%" claim ([PAGE 13]).

**3. AUC as a ranking probability** (standard Mann–Whitney identity; an interpretation, *not* an equation stated in the paper):

$$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr\!\big(s(\text{success}) > s(\text{fail})\big)$$

Reported AUCs: pass 0.755, high 0.683, low 0.776, ground 0.751 (Fig. 11 mislabeled "low," [PAGE 15–17]). The high-pass AUC (0.683) is the weakest ranker, consistent with its low precision 0.561.

**4. Exponential-decay weight.** The paper lists parameters $\{1,0.9,\dots,0.01,0.001\}$ and states "Lower decay parameters (near 0) preserved the influence of older events, while higher parameters increasingly prioritized recent interactions" ([PAGE 5]). The form consistent with this phrasing is $w(\Delta t)=e^{-\lambda \Delta t}$, $\lambda\in\{1,\dots,10^{-3}\}$. Breakdown/units ambiguity: the exponent's time unit is never specified. If $\Delta t$ is in days and $\lambda=1$, $e^{-30}\approx 10^{-13}$ (only same-day events survive) — implausibly aggressive; the intended normalization (per week, per month, or window-normalized) is unstated. Because all $\lambda$ yielded statistically indistinguishable F1 ([PAGE 17]), the conclusion is robust to this ambiguity, but the operator is not reproducible from the text alone.

**5. Composite features** ([PAGE 5], verbatim): "three composite shared experience features were derived by multiplying the number of shared matches with the number of missed passes, the total number of passes, and the number of successful passes" — i.e., $M\cdot(1-p_{\text{miss}})$ type products; the success-gating is the operative signal.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Here "retro-genealogy" means tracing the *retrospective-analysis* lineage of the constructs, not any physical retrocausality. The macro operator is directly inherited from Saulière et al.'s rugby-union "cumulative shared selection" (ref. 16, [PAGE 2]), transplanted to football as CSS/ISS. The core hypothesis — that *prior shared success*, not mere shared time, drives future outcomes — is Mukherjee et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* 2019, "Prior shared success predicts victory in team competitions" (ref. 8, [PAGE 2]). The present study is explicitly positioned *against* that result at the macro scale ([PAGE 2]: "In contrast with Mukherjee et al. [8], the current study is designed to disentangle and isolate the effect of shared experience from confounding variables such as team strength or FIFA rankings") while *reinstating* it at the micro scale.

Lineage branch on familiarity: Grund (ref. 3) — higher passing rates when players know each other; Mclean et al. (ref. 7) — implicit coordination in possession vs. increased verbal communication when defending; Grossman et al. (ref. 6) — task cohesion over social bonds; Leo et al. (ref. 5) — early-season cohesion → collective efficacy → results. Turnover contra-lineage: Mehta et al. (ref. 9), Kolodziejczyk et al. (ref. 10), Davis et al. NFL (ref. 11), Barreira et al. (ref. 12) — squad stability benefits, motivating the lineup-similarity feature.

Multi-week traps in the temporal design:
1. **Window-trough trap.** The bimodal F1 (20-day and 6/12-month peaks, mid-range trough, [PAGE 14]) is easily misread as "chemistry decays then rebuilds." The more defensible reading: mid-range windows straddle neither transient form nor structural style and thus capture a low-signal mixture ([PAGE 5]).
2. **Averaging trap (macro).** Whole-squad CSS/ISS averaging cancels dyadic signal — the authors concede this ([PAGE 12]: "When individual player relationships are averaged across an entire team, important high impact team cohesion can get lost").
3. **Decay-family trap.** Only exponential decay was tested; the authors themselves flag that "memory decay models other than an exponential decay" and "situational moderators" are needed ([PAGE 14]). A non-monotone or power-law kernel could behave differently — the negative decay result is family-specific.
4. **Dataset-era trap.** Macro spans 2012–2023 (excluding 2019, [PAGE 3]); micro spans 2004–2023 across only La Liga and Ligue 1 ([PAGE 4]). Cross-era tactical drift confounds any pooled estimate.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Modeling the analyst/decision-maker as an agent subject to habit regression under constraint:

**Crisis-habit regression.** Under pressure to "explain winning," the default habit is to reach for legible, high-variance aggregates — FIFA ranking, FM ratings. The decision tree enacts this regression literally: root split on FIFA-rank difference ([PAGE 7]). The agent's prior (strength explains results) is strong, and the model confirms it because strength genuinely has the largest marginal information gain at the match level. The trap is that this confirmation is *right about the macro question and wrong about the micro question*, and a habit-bound analyst would generalize the macro null to "cohesion doesn't matter" — which the micro results refute.

**Counter-trap.** The disciplined counter-move is to *condition* rather than *average*: replace the aggregate exposure feature with exposure × success (the composite features, [PAGE 5]). This converts a cancelled signal into an informative one. The paper's whole contribution is this counter-trap: "shared experience's value lies in the quality of successful coordination rather than mere familiarity" ([PAGE 1]).

**Clock.** The temporal clock is the third agent variable. The analyst must decide *how far back to look*. The paper's clock is bimodal; the rational agent facing a bimodal response would not pick a single window but *combine* short and long horizons — which the authors explicitly defer to future work ([PAGE 14]: "future work should consider combining short and long term shared experience features within a single model"). A regression-prone agent picks one window (habit: "recent = best") and misses the long-horizon structural signal; a counter-trap agent uses both.

**Recursive layer.** Anticipating a reviewer's theory-of-mind: a skeptical reviewer models the authors as having "explained away" a desired positive (they wanted shared experience to matter, it didn't at macro, so they pivot to micro). The rebuttal embedded in the text is the pre-registered asymmetry — they state the macro isolation test *before* observing it null ([PAGE 7]), and the micro gain for high passes (recall 0.575→0.830) is large and mechanistically motivated (aerial timing), not a post-hoc salvage.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A conclusion survives only if it does not contradict physical/biomechanical/weather constraints.

**Biomechanical layer — PASS.** The 5 m threshold root split is physically coherent. Sub-5 m passes ([PAGE 8]: "Passes of 5 m or less ... exhibit very low success rates") occur in high defender-density zones where the receiver's control radius overlaps an opponent's interception radius; the time-of-flight $t\approx d/v$ is too short for the receiver to create separation. No veto — the statistical split aligns with the interception-geometry constraint.

**Biomechanical layer — HIGH PASS.** The strongest shared-XP effect (recall +25%, [PAGE 13]) passes biomechanical scrutiny: aerial passes have longer flight time, larger interception exposure, and require tighter passer–receiver run-timing. The cited mechanism — "receivers adjust their runs by approximately 7 % to optimize separation" (ref. 25, [PAGE 13]) — is a small kinematic adjustment, and the effect's concentration in high passes (not ground/low) is exactly what a timing-sensitivity model predicts. No veto.

**Positional layer.** "Passes to attackers were generally less effective" ([PAGE 13]) is consistent with tighter marking and lower receiver free-space in the final third — a spatial-density veto *supports* rather than contradicts the finding.

**Weather layer — the unadjudicated confounder.** The paper controls for pass length, angle, height, pressure, switch, and type ([PAGE 4]), but *not* for weather (wind, precipitation, surface). Wind materially alters aerial-pass success and the very high-pass model that shows the largest shared-XP gain. This is a genuine cross-layer exposure: the high-pass result could be partially weather-confounded, and no weather feature appears in the feature list. **Conditional veto flag:** the high-pass shared-XP effect is *not* refuted, but its magnitude is not weather-clean and cannot be fully adjudicated from the supplied pages.

**Aggregation layer (macro).** The macro null passes the physical veto only trivially — the veto here is *statistical-mechanical*, not physical: averaging $n(n-1)$ dyadic terms with heterogeneous tenure is a low-pass filter that attenuates high-frequency dyadic signal. The null is therefore an artifact-consistent result, and physical reasoning cannot promote it to "cohesion is irrelevant."

Net adjudication: micro findings pass biomechanical and positional vetoes; the macro null passes only as an aggregation artifact; the high-pass magnitude carries an unresolved weather-confound flag.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three verifiable (RLVR-style, checkable against reconstructable StatsBomb/FM data) counterfactuals that would veto the paper's claims, each with a falsifiable prediction:

**Veto 1 — Success-gating is spurious (feature-leakage test).** Claim under test: shared XP × success predicts pass outcome. Counterfactual: recompute the ">10 successful passes" feature using *only* information strictly prior to the event, then re-evaluate. Prediction: if the effect is causal coordination and not leakage of same-match outcomes, high-pass recall gain (0.575→0.830) persists (within ±0.03). Veto condition: if the gain collapses to <0.02 under strict temporal hold-out, the result is leakage and is vetoed.

**Veto 2 — Weather confound on the high-pass model.** Claim under test: shared XP drives aerial completion. Counterfactual: stratify the 1,602-match set by wind/precipitation proxies (or restrict to matches with wind below a threshold). Prediction: if the shared-XP high-pass advantage holds across weather strata (effect sign invariant), the finding survives; veto condition: if the advantage appears only in high-wind strata, it is a weather artifact and is vetoed.

**Veto 3 — Macro null is power, not truth.** Claim under test: shared experience has no independent macro effect. Counterfactual: assemble a matched cohort of near-equal FIFA-rank fixtures (the authors state their sample "was not of sufficient size to yield robust results," [PAGE 14]) large enough for adequate power, and re-estimate. Prediction: if the CSS/ISS coefficient remains statistically indistinguishable from zero with tight confidence intervals, the null is confirmed; veto condition: if a powered matched design yields a significant CSS/ISS effect, the macro null is vetoed as an underpowered/aggregation artifact.

**Robustness prediction (non-veto).** Because all exponential-decay parameters were statistically indistinguishable ([PAGE 17]), any future memory kernel (power-law, log) is predicted to yield no significant F1 improvement on the mid-range trough — the trough is structural, so kernel choice should not move it. If a non-exponential kernel *does* fill the trough, the "structural mixture" explanation (and the decay-family reasoning) is falsified.

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
