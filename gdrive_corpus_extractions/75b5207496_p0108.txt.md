Let \(F_{\text{ax}}\) be the compressive neck load; injury or reflexive inhibition occurs when \(F_{\text{ax}}>F_{\text{fail}}\), assumed here to be on the order of \(3\text{--}4\,\text{kN}\) for an adult cervical spine under axial compression. Because the source specifies contact with the throat [PAGE 108], the technique carries a biological veto independent of its tactical logic.

Environmental (weather/turf) veto. The outside-step technique depends on static friction. From Section 2, \(|F_{\text{lat}}|\le \mu_s m_D g\cos\phi\). Rain, snow, or mud reduce \(\mu_s\) from roughly \(0.7\text{--}0.9\) on dry turf to below \(0.3\) on saturated surfaces. Critical breakdown occurs when \(\mu_s g < a_{\text{lat,req}}\); the plant foot slips, the lateral blow never lands, and the defender's charge becomes uncontrolled. At \(\mu_s\to0\) (ice), all charge techniques are physically nullified.

Adjudication hierarchy: Physical veto > Biomechanical veto > Environmental veto. A single layer's failure invalidates the play prescription. Exact singularities: \(\mu_s=0\) (frictionless surface), \(\theta_{\text{knee}}\to0\) (locked leg, no low position), and \(m_O/m_D\to\infty\) (immovable offensive lineman relative to defender).
