Breakdown regime: when reward is stochastic p=0.5, δ variance is maximized, explaining [PAGE 41]: "the uncertainty of getting a reward... excited Dopamine."

**Eq. 2: H-index**
h = max{n ∈ ℕ : |{p ∈ P : c(p) ≥ n}| ≥ n}
Units: h is dimensionless count; |·| cardinality; c(p) citation count in ℕ.
Assumptions: citation counts are stationary, no self-citation adjustment.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

1. **Dopamine reward prediction error (Schultz TD model).** For each timestep t,
$$\delta(t) \;=\; r(t) + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t),$$
with $\delta, r, V$ in arbitrary utility units (dimensionless reward scale), discount factor $\gamma\in[0,1]$ dimensionless, and $V(s)$ the learned state value. Assumptions: Markov decision process, TD(0) learning, stationary reward distribution. **Singularity/breakdown:** $\delta(t)\to 0$ exactly when $r(t)+\gamma V(s_{t+1})=V(s_t)$ — the fully predictable regime — which is exactly the extinction reported at [PAGE 37–38]: “after a while, their Dopamine production completely stopped.” Variance of $\delta$ is maximized at reward probability $p^\star = 0.5$, matching [PAGE 41]. Breakdown regime: when $p\to\{0,1\}$, the signal carries zero information and the “prediction molecule” is functionally silent.

2. **Hirsch index.** For a publication set $P$ with citation counts $c:P\to\mathbb{N}$,
$$h \;=\; \max\bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N} \;:\; \bigl|\{p\in P : c(p)\ge n\}\bigr| \ge n\bigr\}.$$
Assumption: $c(p)$ is stationary and self-citations are unadjusted. **Breakdown:** by construction $h\le|P|$, so hyper-prolific low-impact authors score arbitrarily high; this is exactly Raoult’s 2,053 papers / 72,847 citations / 25% self-citations vs. Einstein’s 147 / 28,404 / 1.7% [PAGE 131]. Singularity: as self-citation fraction $\to 1$, $h$ measures reflexivity, not impact.

3. **Brier score (implicit scoring rule for Tetlock).** For $N$ forecasts $f_i\in[0,1]$ and outcomes $o_i\in\{0,1\}$,
$$\mathrm{BS} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f_i - o_i)^2 \quad \text{(dimensionless)}.$$
Assumption: proper scoring; $f_i$ conditionally independent given information set. Reference baseline for a fair coin: $\mathrm{BS}_{\text{coin}}=0.25$. Snow’s empirical result that chimp-dart accuracy dominated expert forecasts [PAGE 15] implies expert $\mathrm{BS}\ge 0.25$ over the 82,361-question battery.

4. **Wisdom of crowds aggregation (Galton).**
$$\hat{\mu} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i, \quad x_i \text{ in pounds (ox weight)}.$$
Assumptions (from [PAGE 101]): independence, diversity, decentralization, and a bias-free aggregation rule. Empirical anchor: $|\hat{\mu}-x_{\text{true}}|=|1197-1198|=1$ lb over $N\approx 800$ — [PAGE 99]. **Breakdown:** if independence fails (correlated guesses under contagion), $\hat{\mu}$ collapses toward the modal estimate; this is the regime the Challenger case [PAGE 99–101] shows the market got *right* because trade heterogeneity restored near-independence.

5. **Henrich Genius–Butterfly transmission model.** Let $p_G=10^{-1}$, $p_B=10^{-3}$ (independent invention probability per lifetime), $k_G=1$, $k_B=10$ (friend count), $\tau=0.5$ (transmission fidelity per [PAGE 93]). With $q_j$ the population fraction with innovation at generation $j$,
$$q_{j+1} = 1 - (1-p)\bigl[1-\tau q_j\bigr]^{k}.$$
Steady-state results [PAGE 94]: $q_G^\ast\approx 0.18$, $q_B^\ast \approx 0.999$. **Breakdown:** if $\tau\to 0$, Butterflies collapse toward $p_B=10^{-3}$ and lose to Geniuses; the entire result is a function of $\tau$ and $k$.

6. **Spacing effect (Ebbinghaus / Bahrick–Phelphs).** Retention $R(t)\in[0,1]$ at delay $t$ (years) with spacing interval $s$:
$$R(t) = A\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_0\,[1+\alpha\log s]}\right),$$
where $\tau_0$ is the cramming time constant and $\alpha\ge 0$ the spacing gain. Empirical anchor: “the ones with the spaced-out review sessions - remembered 250% more word-pairs than the first group” [PAGE 157]. Cross-species anchor from the fruit-fly odor-shock paradigm: 10 back-to-back pairings → ~3-day avoidance; spaced pairings → “a week a more” [PAGE 156–157]. **Breakdown:** as $s\to\infty$ with unlimited total study budget, cost explodes; optimum $s^\star$ solves $\mathrm{d}R/\mathrm{d}s$ against study-time cost, i.e., there is a finite-cost regime in which spacing stops paying.

7. **Moravec capability inversion.** For task class $T$ requiring bounded retrieval and search:
$$C_M(T) \gg C_H(T) \quad\text{if } T\in\text{Kind (chess, arithmetic),}$$
$$C_H(T) \gg C_M(T) \quad\text{if } T\in\text{Wicked (perception, abstraction).}$$
Empirical anchors: Deep Blue evaluated “200 million possible moves on the chessboard per second” [PAGE 119]; yet ZackS with three home PCs beat 32-processor Hydra [PAGE 122–123]. Breakdown: as $T$ moves from Wicked to Kind via better formalization, the sign of the inequality flips.

8. **McNamara divergence.** Let $G$ be the true goal utility and $M$ a measurable proxy. The fallacy occurs when
$$\arg\max_{x} M(x) \;\neq\; \arg\max_{x} G(x).$$
Vietnam operationalization: $M=\text{enemy body count}$, $G=\text{Vietnamese welfare}$ [PAGE 136–137]; the divergence produced a *decrease* in $G$ as civilian casualties drove recruitment toward the Viet Cong.

9. **Attention-bandwidth constraint.** With $B_{\max}\approx 120$ bits/s [PAGE 112] and conversation demand $B_{\text{conv}}\approx 60$ bits/s, mutual simultaneous comprehension of two full conversations requires $2B_{\text{conv}} = 120 = B_{\max}$ — zero slack for any additional channel. Information available: $I(2020)=64.2\times10^{21}$ bytes $\approx 5.1\times 10^{23}$ bits; $I(2025)$ projected $\approx 1.4\times10^{24}$ bits [PAGE 109–110]. **Breakdown:** whenever $\int_0^{T_{\text{life}}} B_{\max}\,dt < I(t)$, the informational environment is decision-theoretically supersaturated and selection, not acquisition, becomes the binding constraint.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The book’s operators are informational, but they sit *inside* a physical/biomechanical/weather envelope that can veto them and that cannot itself be vetoed by purely cognitive intervention.

**Physical (energetic) veto.** [PAGE 24]: “Your brain is 2 percent of your body’s weight but devours 20 percent of the energy you get from your food.” Together with the raw-food constraint that “animals that live on a raw food diet, like Chimps, need to expend a ton of energy externally and internally for every meal” and “spend a lot of time eating - sometimes, for up to 12 hours a day” [PAGE 26–27], this establishes a hard energetic precondition: encephalization is only feasible if cooking fire unlocks a caloric surplus. Any theory of prophecy that ignores caloric budget violates conservation of energy. The Promethean claim “the invention of cooking food with fire... enabled the development of their brains” [PAGE 27] is therefore protected by a physical veto, not a cultural one.

**Biomechanical (sensory-transduction) veto.** The Doppler effect, invoked for Brian May’s stomp-stomp-clap recording [PAGE 67–68], is a constraint of classical wave propagation and phase relationships. May’s decision to record repeats at prime-number delay intervals exploits this: if the delays are commensurable, the repeats phase-align and produce audible periodicity; prime intervals de-correlate the delay train. Any “psychology-only” account of the recording’s perceptual realism must cede to this acoustic layer; the prime spacing is not a preference but a constraint.

**Biomechanical (attentional) veto.** [PAGE 112]: “The amount of processing power needed to have a meaningful conversation with another human is 60 bits per second. Our brains can do a maximum of 120 bits per second.” The Zoom + Tinder + driving example [PAGE 111] is vetoed by this constraint regardless of task design or user motivation. No pedagogical or organizational redesign can make a human process two full conversations plus a driving task simultaneously; only offloading, delegation, or temporal serialization can.

**Weather veto (Honda Point).** [PAGE 174]: “the weather had been treacherous, and a blanket of fog lay heavily atop the waters.” The Delphy relied on dead-reckoning because visibility was nil; the radio-direction station offered a corrective fix and the commander dismissed it: “Given how successful he had been so far with his dead-reckoning navigation, the commander decided to ignore the new data” [PAGE 175]. The weather directly configured the information environment (fog → no visual fixes → error accumulation over transit). No amount of individual expertise could substitute for the missing signal; the eleventh ship, the *Kennedy*, heard its deck officers and broke formation [PAGE 175–176]. Weather was a hard gate on prediction, not a soft modifier.

**Weather→agriculture cascade.** The Holocene stabilization [PAGE 104] made “predicting weather patterns six months out” [PAGE 105] culturally rewarded. Without that climatic regime there is no agricultural surplus, no Dojima futures, no Information Age, and therefore no “problem of prophecy” to solve. Physical and climatic layers set the *regime* in which predictive cognition has adaptive payoff; the cognitive layer operates within, not above, that regime.

**Adjudication conclusion.** Veto directionality is one-way: physical/biomechanical/weather constraints can invalidate a cognitive-prediction strategy (e.g., no cooking → no caloric surplus → no large brain → no executive function → no prophecy), but no cognitive-prediction strategy can invalidate them. Daydreaming cannot cure a caloric deficit; insight cannot pierce opaque fog; analogical transfer cannot exceed 120 bits/s of channel capacity.

**Veto 3 — The spacing-effect long-horizon veto (Bahrick–Phelphs replication).**
