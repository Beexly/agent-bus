The closest antecedent is the domain splitting method of [8] (Buchholz and Hochbruck, Math. The localized implicit prediction replaces that explicit leapfrog step with an idea inspired by [20] (Gallistl and Maier, SIAM J. Numer. Multi-week traps arise in the temporal accumulation of the splitting error. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression. Under a stability crisis—large time step τ relative to mesh size h, or high polynomial degree k—a practitioner's default habit is to regress to an explicit integrator such as leapfrog with mass lumping, as discussed on [PAGE 2] and demonstrated in Figures 4 and 7 on [PAGE 27, 30]. That habit carries a CFL-type restriction coupling τ to h and, for higher-order elements, an even more restrictive admissible step size noted on [PAGE 28]. The regression is attractive because each step is cheap, but it sacrifices the unconditional stability of the implicit CN reference.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is the domain splitting method with localized implicit prediction (DSLP). Instead of regressing to explicitness, DSLP performs one local implicit CN step per overlapping subdomain and one localized implicit prediction per interface, as described on [PAGE 7–10]. The counter-trap neutralizes the CFL habit by proving that no step-size restriction of the form τ ≤ ch is needed; stability holds for all tested time steps on [PAGE 2, 27–30]. The trap is sprung when the prediction overlap parameters ℓp,1, ℓp,2 and the subdomain overlap ℓo are chosen to satisfy the overlap condition ϑ ≤ τ p+1 from Theorem 4.1 on [PAGE 13]. If that condition is violated, the counter-trap fails: the method remains stable but the splitting error no longer decays as O(τ p), and the advantage over CN evaporates.

Clock. The clock is the discrete time index n and the accumulated error recursion. Equation (40) on [PAGE 15] is a first-order non-autonomous recurrence En ≤ (1+ϑ)En−1 + ϑCdata with E0 = 0. Its solution (41) is governed by the compounding clock factor exp(nϑ). Since nτ ≤ T, the requirement ϑ ≤ τ p+1 ensures that over the whole interval [0, T] the accumulated error stays bounded by τ p Cdata T exp(T τ p) as in (32) on [PAGE 13]. The clock therefore imposes a joint constraint on the overlap widths and the final time: for fixed overlaps, longer simulations demand smaller τ or larger overlaps to keep nϑ small. In the numerical study on [PAGE 26], the condition nT ϑ ≪ 1 is identified as the practical accuracy criterion on a fixed time interval.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
