Recursive theory of mind here concerns two coupled observers: the experimenter modeling the organism, and the organism’s behavior as a function of an internally evolving threshold potential. The experimenter’s model updates beliefs about sHR from the history of reinforcements (the growth hypothesis), computes sEr, derives z, and predicts p. This is a regressive fixed-point: observed failures lower the experimenter’s inferred sHR, which predicts lower p, which may prompt schedule changes that further alter reinforcement history and hence sHR again—a recursive loop in which the model’s own predictions influence the data-generating process.

An organism under observation may itself be tracking the experimenter’s reinforcement schedule, adapting sLR or response criteria strategically; the normal-ogive fit then captures a co-adapted equilibrium rather than a unilateral psychological law. The model therefore breaks down whenever temporal spacing (the clock) dominates reinforcement count as a predictor of evocation probability—e.g., in spaced versus massed training, where identical reinforcement totals yield different learning curves because sER is not clock-invariant. The singularity at σo = 0 is physically unrealizable; any claim of a perfectly sharp threshold is vetoed by biophysical noise.

Biomechanical layer: the model computes p as “the probability … that an adequate stimulation will evoke the conditioned response” [PAGE 330], but it omits effector transmission. Let T_bio ∈ [0,1] be the probability that a successfully evoked central reaction produces the observed measurable response. The observable probability is p_obs = p · T_bio + p_fa · (1 − T_bio), where p_fa is the false-alarm rate. T_bio varies with fatigue, muscle force capacity, and neuromuscular refractory state. Veto condition: sEr − sLR and σo are held constant, yet p_obs declines across trials because T_bio falls (effector fatigue), producing an apparent learning-curve decrement that the threshold model misattributes to sHR or sLR.

Environmental/weather layer: ambient conditions add exogenous noise δ_env to the effective threshold, so sLR_eff = sLR + δ_env (distraction, temperature, barometric or weather-related changes in alertness), and total variance becomes σ_eff = √(σo² + σ_env²). Veto condition: empirical p values are fit only when σ_eff ≠ 10 or δ_env ≠ 0; then the fixed-parameter normal oscillation on [PAGE 330] is insufficient. Formal replacement:
p_env = Φ((sEr − (sLR + δ_env)) / √(σo² + σ_env²)).
If p_env tracks data significantly better than p = Φ((sEr − sLR)/10), the original model is vetoed on environmental-dependence grounds.

Singularity/breakdown: when σo → 0 the model predicts deterministic threshold crossing, but physical and environmental noise guarantee σ_eff > 0; therefore the ideal step-function regime is a mathematical artifact, not an observable boundary. Any empirical p strictly between 0 and 1 at z near zero falsifies the zero-noise limit and supports the noisy, finite-σ formulation.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions. RLVR denotes reinforcement-learning verification/rejection conditions that can be implemented experimentally without reference to betting lines, spreads, totals, or odds.

Scenario 1 (variance manipulation). Inject controlled broadband sensory noise to increase the oscillation standard deviation from the assumed σo = 10 to σo′ = 20 while holding sEr and sLR constant at values that produced z = 1.0 under σo = 10. Prediction: the new normal variate is z′ = (sEr − sLR)/σo′ = 0.5, so the model predicts p′ = Φ(0.5) ≈ 0.691 (about 69 per cent), a shallower slope and lower p than Φ(1.0) ≈ 0.841. Verifiable rejection: if the empirically measured evocation probability remains near the σo = 10 prediction despite the doubled noise, the fixed-variance normal-oscillation assumption is vetoed.

Scenario 2 (subthreshold boundary). Withhold reinforcement for an initial block so that the growth hypothesis yields sHR ≈ 0 and hence sEr ≈ sLR or sEr < sLR, giving z ≤ 0. Prediction: p = Φ(z) ≤ 0.5, and for z = −1 the model predicts p ≈ 0.159 (15.9 per cent), approaching zero as z becomes more negative. Verifiable rejection: if subjects produce systematic above-chance conditioned responses (e.g., p > 0.30) across multiple trials while computationally subthreshold, the necessity of superthreshold effective reaction potential for evocation is vetoed; an alternative non-threshold or expectancy-driven mechanism is implicated.

Scenario 3 (history independence / yoked control). Construct two groups with identical instantaneous sEr (and thus identical z and p under the model) but different reinforcement densities or inter-reinforcement intervals: Group A receives many reinforcements clustered early; Group B receives the same total reinforcements spaced widely, arranged so that computed sEr is matched at the test trial. The growth hypothesis and the p = Φ((sEr − sLR)/σo) chain predict matched p because p depends on sEr alone, not on reinforcement history beyond its reflection in sEr. Prediction: p_A = p_B at matched sEr. Verifiable rejection: if Group B shows significantly lower p than Group A despite matched sEr, the sufficiency of the sHR → sEr → p mediation is vetoed; temporal contiguity, reinforcement density, or clock-time variables must be added, and the [PAGE 330] threshold-oscillation derivation is incomplete.
