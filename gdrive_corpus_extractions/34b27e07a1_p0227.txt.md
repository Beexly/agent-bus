*Interpretation:* Average remaining uncertainty about brother’s height after knowing man’s height. Lower values indicate stronger informational coupling.

*Units:* Joules/individual.
*Assumptions:* Energy dissipation scales linearly with information processed during development. This is a heuristic extension, not a proven law for morphogenesis.

**5. Regression Gradient Operator (Phenotype Space Velocity):**
Define deviation vector `Δh = H_m - μ_H`, where `μ_H` is population mean. Expected brother deviation:
`E[ΔH_b | ΔH_m] = r · ΔH_m`.
This defines a flow field in phenotype space: `d(ΔH)/dt = -λ · ΔH`, with solution `ΔH(t) = ΔH_0 · exp(-λ t)`. Here `r = exp(-λ τ)` for generation time `τ`. Thus `λ = -ln(r)/τ`.
*Units:* `λ` has dimensions [Time]⁻¹. `r` is dimensionless.
*Singularity:* As `r → 1`, `λ → 0` (no relaxation, critical slowing down). As `r → 0`, `λ → ∞` (instantaneous collapse to mean, unstable to noise).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s analysis emerges from the post-Darwinian crisis in heredity theory. Pre-Galton, blending inheritance (Pangenesis) was dominant, implying子女的 traits were averages of parents, leading to rapid loss of variation—a problem Darwin himself acknowledged. Galton’s discovery of regression directly refuted blending: extremes do not disappear but persist at reduced magnitude across generations, preserving variation. This was a retrocausal leap: he used offspring data to infer the mechanism of parental contribution.

Scientifically, this line connects to:
- **Fisher’s Fundamental Theorem (1930):** Rate of increase in fitness equals additive genetic variance. Regression coefficient `r` estimates additive genetic variance relative to phenotypic variance.
- **Quantitative Genetics (Lush, 1937):** Formalized breeding values using covariance matrices like Galton’s.
- **Modern Genomics:** GWAS identifies SNPs contributing to height heritability, confirming Galton’s statistical intuition at molecular resolution.

**Multi-Week Trap Analysis:**
In short-term prediction (e.g., next generation), using only parental height yields biased estimates because it ignores:
1.  **Secular Trends:** Height increased ~10 cm globally in 20th century due to nutrition. Applying Galton’s `r` today without adjusting for cohort effects would underestimate future heights.
2.  **Assortative Mating Feedback:** Positive correlation between mates increases offspring variance, potentially accelerating divergence from population mean over multiple generations—a destabilizing force absent in Galton’s original model.
3.  **Gene-Environment Correlation (rGE):** Active rGE (tall individuals seeking taller partners) creates spurious genetic correlations that inflate `r` and distort long-term projections.

Thus, while Galton’s local regression is robust, extrapolating it causally across decades fails due to unmodeled systemic feedbacks. His table captures a snapshot of equilibrium dynamics in Victorian England, not a universal invariant.

Galton’s purely statistical model faces hard vetoes from lower-level realities:

**1. Physical/Biomechanical Veto:**
Height is bounded by skeletal strength and cardiovascular efficiency. Towering skeletons require disproportionate bone mass (scaling with cube of height vs. cross-sectional area scaling with square), increasing fracture risk. Cardiac output must scale to perfuse greater tissue volume. These physical constraints impose nonlinear penalties on extreme heights, causing the regression slope to flatten further at tails—not captured by Galton’s linear model. Empirical evidence shows diminishing returns in athletic performance beyond optimal height ranges, supporting biomechanical veto.

**2. Metabolic/Energetic Veto:**
Basal metabolic rate scales with mass^0.75 (Kleiber’s law). Taller individuals have higher absolute energy demands. In resource-limited settings, this creates selective pressure against excessive height. During famines, taller children suffer disproportionately, truncating the right tail of the distribution. Galton’s era had relatively stable nutrition; modern global inequalities introduce dynamic veto pressures.

**3. Weather/Environmental Stochasticity Veto:**
Droughts reduce crop yields, lowering childhood protein intake, stunting growth regardless of genetics. A severe multi-year drought acts as a veto override: even genetically predisposed tall stature collapses to environmentally determined minima. This introduces fat-tailed risk into the system—rare events reset trajectories abruptly. Climate models project increased frequency of such events, threatening the stability of height distributions assumed by static regression coefficients.

**Proof of Adjudication:**
These factors demonstrate that Galton’s operator `E[H_b|H_m]` is insufficient alone. A complete model must include constraint functions:
