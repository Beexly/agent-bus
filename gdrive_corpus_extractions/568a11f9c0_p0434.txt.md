**Operator 2 (Eduction of Relations):**
Given two fundaments $F_1, F_2 \in \mathcal{F}$, the system derives a relation $R \in \mathcal{R}$.
Define the eduction operator $\mathcal{E}_{rel}: \mathcal{F} \times \mathcal{F} \rightarrow \mathcal{R}$.

$$ I(F_1; F_2 | R) > I(F_1; F_2) $$
The relation $R$ maximizes the reduction in uncertainty about the co-occurrence of $F_1$ and $F_2$. If we treat $F_i$ as random variables, $R$ minimizes the conditional entropy $H(F_1, F_2 | R)$.

**Operator 3 (Eduction of Correlates):**
Given a character (fundament) $F_c$ and a relation $R$, the system educates a correlative fundament $F_{corr}$.
Define $\mathcal{E}_{corr}: \mathcal{F} \times \mathcal{R} \rightarrow \mathcal{F}$.
Modeled as a Bayesian inference:
$$ P(F_{corr} | F_c, R) = \frac{P(F_c, F_{corr} | R) P(R)}{P(F_c | R)} $$
Assuming a prior over valid correlations, the system outputs the most probable $F_{corr}$.
Example from [PAGE 1]: If $F_c =$ 'good', $R =$ 'opposite to', then $F_{corr} =$ 'bad'.

*   Eduction: Information units (bits/shannons). The reduction in entropy $\Delta H$ measures the efficiency of the eductive process.

**Singularity/Breakdown Regimes:**
The eduction operators fail if the input distribution lacks statistical regularity. Specifically, if the mutual information $I(F_1; F_2) \approx 0$, no stable relation $R$ can be inferred. In extreme cases, high-dimensional sensory noise leads to combinatorial explosion in $\mathcal{E}_{corr}$, causing hallucinations (spurious correlates) or agnosia (failure to apprehend).

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The validity of any high-level eduction is subject to veto by lower physical and biomechanical layers.
1.  **Physical Layer Veto:** Even if the brain successfully educes the correlation "lever A opens door B," fatigue or metabolic depletion (e.g., hypoglycemia) may render the organism physically incapable of executing the movement. The motor command is vetoed before reaching the muscles.
2.  **Biomechanical/Anatomical Veto:** Structural limitations impose hard constraints. An organism cannot educo-correlate a plan to fly if the skeletal-muscular apparatus lacks wings. The "product of eduction" might exist mentally, but behavioral output is null.
3.  **Weather/Environmental Veto:** External conditions can invalidate an internally consistent map. If the path to a food source is blocked by sudden flooding, the previously valid relation $C$ is rendered obsolete. The behaviorist principle dictates that the environment ultimately adjudicates the success of the purpose; internal cognition is provisional until tested against external reality.
