James’s own clock-hand example functions as a recursive probe of the theorist’s stance: staring at the second-hand yields direct motion; looking away yields inferred displacement [PAGE 181]. The theorist who “looks away” from the subject’s immediate experience toward a position-difference model replicates the subject’s gaze displacement from the watch. The recursion breaks down only when latencies are compared: direct cutaneous motion signalling has afferent-plus-cortical latencies on the order of tens to roughly one hundred milliseconds, whereas position comparison across a working-memory buffer requires recurrent activation (>300 ms) and is vulnerable to decay. Under crisis conditions—high speed, brief stimulus, divided attention—the buffer fails while the fast afferent motion channel may still report d′ₘₒₜ > 0; that is the regime where regression to inference is most seductive and most likely false.

Veto logic: the motion/position dissociation is accepted as autonomous only if no single physical, biomechanical, or environmental layer can reduce d′ₘₒₜ(ℓₘ) > 0 while d′ₚₒₛ(10ℓₘ) ≈ 0 [PAGE 181] to a trivial artifact. Physical layer: the pencil tip exerts normal force F_N and lateral shear on skin; if the contact radius a satisfies 2a > Δx₂ₚₜ, two points merge mechanically before neural transduction. However, mechanical spatial merging cannot by itself explain detection of a 2.54 mm line when 25.4 mm point pairs are fused, unless a dynamic strain operator ∂ε/∂t (fast-adapting afferents) signals motion independently of static spatial sampling. Veto condition: ¬[ (2a > Δx₂ₚₜ) → (d′ₘₒₜ ⇒ d′ₚₒₛ) ]; the inequality fails empirically [PAGE 181], so mechanics alone is vetoed. Biomechanical layer: skin is viscoelastic, exhibiting creep and stress relaxation with time constants τᵥ ≈ O(0.1–10 s). A slow trace may be filtered out while a faster trace is detected, but such filtering applies similarly to static position judgments and cannot selectively abolish two-point discrimination at ten times the trace scale while preserving motion detection. Veto condition: viscoelastic transfer functions are common to position and motion; they do not generate the observed ratio Δx₂ₚₜ/ℓₘ ≈ 10. Weather/environmental layer: ambient temperature T and relative humidity H shift skin friction coefficient μₛ(T,H) and mechanoreceptor baseline firing λ₀(T,H). These are nuisance parameters that move absolute thresholds but, because James’s dissociation is ratio-based (ten-fold spatial disparity), the inequality d′ₘₒₜ(ℓₘ) >> d′ₚₒₛ(10ℓₘ) is expected to persist across physiologically tolerable T and H ranges. Veto condition: environmental modulation does not collapse the dissociation. Adjudication: all three layers fail to entail position-from-motion, so the formal veto V = ¬[ (Physical ∧ Biomechanical ∧ Environmental) → (d′ₘₒₜ → d′ₚₒₛ) ] holds; an autonomous spatiotemporal motion operator 𝓜 is required by the data on [PAGE 181].

Scenario 1 — Selective fast-afferent adaptation. Apply 30 Hz vibrotactile adaptation (amplitude 0.5 mm, duration 60 s) to James’s test site to suppress rapidly adapting (FA-I/FA-II) afferents while sparing slowly adapting position channels. Prediction: motion-detection threshold ℓₘ increases significantly (d′ₘₒₜ → 0), while two-point threshold Δx₂ₚₜ remains ≈ 25.4 mm [PAGE 181]. Verification: simultaneous microneurography and psychophysics; a selective FA block should abolish the direct motion report. Veto: if motion perception persists unchanged after FA adaptation, James’s “direct sensation” is not FA-mediated and the autonomous-operator claim is weakened.

Scenario 2 — Tactile apparent-motion ISI sweep. Deliver two taps separated by Δx = 5 mm with inter-stimulus interval ISI swept from 0 to 500 ms. Prediction: for ISI < ~100 ms, subjects report continuous travel or a felt “jump” (direct motion, d′ₘₒₜ > 0); for ISI > ~200 ms, they report two static positions and infer motion post hoc. Verification: forced-choice direction and confidence ratings; the transition point indexes the limit of the direct operator versus the inferential buffer. Veto: if motion is reported identically at all ISIs, the position-inference precondition is falsified; if motion is never reported at any ISI, the claim of direct cutaneous motion sensation is falsified.

Scenario 3 — Cognitive-load dual task. Subjects perform arm skin-motion detection while maintaining a five-item auditory working-memory load. Prediction: position-inference judgments requiring explicit terminus-a-quo/terminus-ad quem comparison degrade under load, whereas direct motion detection remains intact because it is pre-categorical and does not require holding x(t₁) in memory while sampling x(t₂). Verification: compare d′ₘₒₜ and d′ₚₒₛ under load versus no-load. Veto: if both degrade equally, motion perception depends on working-memory inference, contradicting James’s claim that motion is given directly rather than derived from known separate positions [PAGE 181].
