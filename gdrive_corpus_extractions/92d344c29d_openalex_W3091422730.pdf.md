Breakdown: degenerate when orientations are insufficient or when a moving 1D object cannot be held stationary (Zhang's own caveat, [PAGE 4]).

3) Planar homography for overlapping tracking/overlay [PAGE 16]:
$$[u,v,1]^\top \sim H\,[X,Y,1]^\top,\quad H=K(r_1\ r_2\ t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3}\ (8\ \text{DOF})$$
Assumption (verbatim, [PAGE 16]): all targets coplanar. Breakdown: any off-plane depth (airborne ball, head vs ground) injects parallax; $H$ is no longer constant.

4) Stereo depth / parallax [PAGE 21]:
$$Z=\frac{f\,B}{d},\quad d=x_L-x_R,\qquad \sigma_Z=\frac{Z^2}{fB}\,\sigma_d$$
Units: $Z,B$ in m; $f,d,x$ in pixels. Breakdown: $d\to0\Rightarrow Z\to\infty$; occlusion boundaries and textureless regions (aperture problem) corrupt $d$.

5) Folded-optic effective aperture — Equation (1), [PAGE 21]:
$$d_{eff}=d_{outer}\sqrt{1-\rho^2},\qquad \rho=\frac{d_{inner}}{d_{outer}}\in[0,1)$$
This is an area-equivalence: $\tfrac{\pi}{4}d_{eff}^2=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(d_{outer}^2-d_{inner}^2)$. Collected flux scales as $\Phi\propto d_{eff}^2=d_{outer}^2(1-\rho^2)$ (aperture area, m²; radiance-integrated power in W). Breakdown: $\rho\to1\Rightarrow d_{eff}\to0$; the diffraction angle $\theta\approx1.22\,\lambda/d_{eff}$ (rad) diverges, collapsing resolution. Thermal band cited as $\lambda\in[1,14]\ \mu m$ ([PAGE 2]); omni resolution $\approx1600\times1200$ px ([PAGE 2]).

6) Kalman filter hierarchy [PAGE 7]:
$$x_k=Fx_{k-1}+w_{k-1},\ z_k=Hx_k+v_k,\quad w\sim\mathcal N(0,Q),\ v\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$$
$$\hat x_{k|k-1}=F\hat x_{k-1},\ P_{k|k-1}=FP_{k-1}F^\top+Q$$
$$K_k=P_{k|k-1}H^\top\!\big(HP_{k|k-1}H^\top+R\big)^{-1},\quad \hat x_k=\hat x_{k|k-1}+K_k(z_k-H\hat x_{k|k-1})$$
Units: $Q,R,P$ covariance matrices of state² units; innovation covariance $S=HP_{k|k-1}H^\top+R$ must be invertible $\Rightarrow R\succ0$. Breakdown: linear-Gaussian assumption violated by "non-Gaussian" human motion ([PAGE 7]); EKF linearization error $O(\|\Delta x\|^2)$; remedy is the particle filter.

7) Particle filter / Sequential Monte Carlo [PAGE 12]:
$$w_t^{(i)}\propto w_{t-1}^{(i)}\frac{p(z_t\mid x_t^{(i)})\,p(x_t^{(i)}\mid x_{t-1}^{(i)})}{q(x_t^{(i)}\mid x_{0:t-1}^{(i)},z_{1:t})},\quad \hat{\mathbb E}[f]=\sum_i w_t^{(i)}f(x_t^{(i)})$$
Breakdown: weight degeneracy as $N_{eff}=1/\sum_i (w_t^{(i)})^2\to1$; joint multi-object state $\dim=3N$ ([PAGE 12], Xu et al. [32]) is the real-time singularity.

8) Mahalanobis Re-ID metric [PAGE 10]–[PAGE 11]:
$$d_M(x_m,x_n)=\sqrt{(x_m-x_n)^\top M (x_m-x_n)},\quad M=L^\top L\succeq0$$
$M=I$ recovers Euclidean; $M$ learned from positive/negative pairs. Breakdown: low-rank feature space (small dataset) makes $M$ singular → zero-distance collapse (false matches). kLFDA/kMFA chosen to "resolve the problems of eigenvalue in a scattered matrices data" ([PAGE 11]); GOG/LDFV/LOMO cited as best extractors.

9) Dempster–Shafer fusion [PAGE 12]:
$$(m_1\oplus m_2)(A)=\frac{1}{1-K}\sum_{B\cap C=A}m_1(B)m_2(C),\quad K=\sum_{B\cap C=\varnothing}m_1(B)m_2(C)$$
$K\in[0,1)$ is the conflict mass. Breakdown (Zadeh): $K\to1\Rightarrow(1-K)^{-1}\to\infty$ — total conflict yields counterintuitive fusion.

10) Fusion information bound (survey's stated goal, [PAGE 12]): under the Markov chain $X\to\{Y_i\}\to \hat X$, the data-processing inequality gives $I(X;\hat X)\le I(X;\{Y_i\})\le\sum_i I(X;Y_i)$ (bits), with equality only for conditionally independent views; overlapping FOV (correlated views) yields diminishing mutual information — the formal reason "overlapping" fusion gains less than non-overlapping.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
"Retro-causal" here means lineage constraint: how each later capability was fixed by an earlier physical or algorithmic decision. The survey hands us a dated backbone. Aerial multi-camera imaging originates with Triboulet/Tissandier's seven-camera balloon (1884, [PAGE 2], ref [9]); industrial robot vision follows in the mid-20th century ([PAGE 2], ref [10]). The decisive branch point is Zhang's plane calibration (2000, ref [21]) versus the one-dimensional object calibration (2002, ref [47]); everything downstream (Shen–Hornsey nonplanar target [43], 3-axis frame DLT [49], multiple-target global frames [38]) inherits the collinearity/degeneracy constraints of those two roots. In sports, the lineage runs graphic-overlay → mechanical-sensor → multi-camera → Hawk-Eye (tennis 3D, refs [190],[191]) with FoxTrax (hockey, 20 IR LEDs, refs [185],[198]) as the parallel IR-illumination branch. In Re-ID, the fork is learning-based (Adaboost/Haar [128], deep multi-scale [116]) versus non-learning descriptors (SIFT/SURF body-part signatures [153]); metric learning (Hirzer three-stage [136], KISSME, kLFDA/kMFA [PAGE 11]) descends from the distance-learning branch.

Multi-week traps (persistent problems that repeatedly re-capture the literature):
- Trap 1 — Coplanar homography lock-in. The "same plane" assumption ([PAGE 16]) keeps reappearing because it is cheap; every paper that adopts it silently reintroduces parallax error for airborne objects.
- Trap 2 — Occlusion. Named "persistent" ([PAGE 14]); particle filters and belief propagation (Lu–Li [29], Lébraly Dempster–Shafer [31]) mitigate but never eliminate it, so occlusion papers regenerate indefinitely.
- Trap 3 — Camera-to-target ratio. Explicitly "not stipulated" ([PAGE 14]); no paper closes it, so surveillance coordination (game theory [71], constraint satisfaction [66]) stays open.
- Trap 4 — Real-time vs joint-state dimension. Every centralized fusion method hits the $3N$ wall ([PAGE 12]); the distributed/hybrid architectures ([PAGE 5]–[PAGE 6]) are the recurring escape attempt, each trading global optimality for scalability.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Mapping the template honestly onto the survey: the "adversary" is the tracked subject and the environment, and the "model" is the tracker's belief about that subject.

Level-0 (tracker): assumes appearance is stationary, uses color histogram / Kalman prediction (Liem–Gavrila three-body model, [PAGE 7]).
Level-1 (counter-trap by subject): appearance change between non-overlapping cameras defeats color histograms; this is exactly why brightness-transfer functions (MBTF/CBTF, [PAGE 12]) and metric-learned invariants (kLFDA, [PAGE 11]) exist. The subject "changes costume"; the tracker must re-ID via geometry or learned distance $d_M$.
Level-2 (tracker counter-counter): space-time + appearance constraints (entrance/exit points, transition time; [PAGE 11], Shen–Hornsey) predict where the subject must reappear, closing the blind gap (Makris topological model, EM + GMM, [PAGE 7]; Stauffer inter-arrival-time linking, [PAGE 8]).
Level-3 (residual adversary): a subject who deliberately varies gait/pace breaks the constant-rate arrival assumption Stauffer replaced ([PAGE 8]); no closed form survives fully adversarial motion — the survey concedes occlusion and privacy/obstruction as unresolved ([PAGE 14]).

The clock (real-time constraint) is explicit and binding: surveillance "is a real-time activity that demands a lot of processing power" and current smart-camera processing "still fell short," with the communication medium limiting inter-camera data transfer under traffic load ([PAGE 14]). Straw et al. demonstrate the clock boundary concretely — 11 cameras, 3 flies, 60 fps real-time ([PAGE 22]); ORB runs ~10× faster than SURF but sacrifices scale invariance ([PAGE 22]), the canonical speed/robustness trade against the clock. Any recursion deeper than Level-2 must fit inside the frame budget or it is vetoed by the clock.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/environmental impossibility proofs, each grounded in the source:

V1 — Optical/photometric layer. Folded optics cannot beat the obscuration penalty: with $\rho\to1$, $d_{eff}\to0$ and flux $\to0$ ([PAGE 21], Eq. 1); simultaneously diffraction $\theta\approx1.22\lambda/d_{eff}$ diverges. Proof: no folded optic with a large central obstruction can be both compact and high-resolution — a hard physical veto, not a tuning issue. For thermal imagers the band is fixed at $\lambda\in[1,14]\ \mu m$ ([PAGE 2]); you cannot infer visible-color appearance from a thermal channel, vetoing color-based Re-ID across a thermal–visible heterogeneous pair.

V2 — Geometric layer. Any 3D claim built on the coplanar homography is vetoed off-plane: a ball at height $h$ above the ground plane maps to a ground location offset by $\approx h\cdot(\text{depth ratio})$, so "goal-line"/"off-side" overlays ([PAGE 15]) are exact only for $h=0$. Triangulation depth is vetoed in the far field: $\sigma_Z=(Z^2/fB)\sigma_d\to\infty$ as $Z$ grows ([PAGE 21]); a long-baseline claim at large $Z$ is unfalsifiable at fixed disparity noise.

V3 — Biomechanical layer. The survey states tracking athletes' limbs in live matches is unsolved and marker-based motion capture "is only applicable during training and exercise… in live matches… factually not possible" ([PAGE 16]). Face detection is vetoed by occlusion, camera distance, and light intensity ([PAGE 8], [PAGE 23]); wearable eye-trackers carry an explicit ocular-safety limit and cost veto ([PAGE 18], ref [225]). Therefore any pipeline promising limb-level live analytics from broadcast multi-camera is physically vetoed by the source's own biomechanics section.

V4 — Environmental/weather layer. Background subtraction "requires an update of the model for every change in the video scene" ([PAGE 8]); temporal differencing "has a setback on changing background with time" ([PAGE 8]). Outdoor sports (downhill ski, football, [PAGE 16]) and outdoor surveillance therefore suffer illumination/weather-driven background drift that invalidates a static background model — a weather veto on any no-recalibration claim.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable, source-verifiable veto scenarios (RLVR = reinforcement learning with verifiable reward; here the "reward" is a checkable technical outcome). Each states a counterfactual claim and the exact test that would falsify the naïve expectation.

CE-1 — Homography-on-nonplanar-scene veto.
Claim under test: "A single $3\times3$ homography calibrated on the ground plane localizes an airborne ball."
Veto: OFF. Because the "same plane" assumption ([PAGE 16]) is violated for $h>0$, the recovered point is systematically biased.
Verifiable prediction: reprojection error grows monotonically with ball height $h$; measured error $\epsilon(h)\propto h$ and does not vanish with more correspondences (bias, not variance). Falsifier: if error is height-independent, homography was secretly re-fit per height.

CE-2 — Obscuration-ratio aperture veto.
Claim under test: "A folded-optic tele module with $\rho=0.9$ collects nearly the same light as an unobscured lens of the same outer diameter."
Veto: OFF. By Eq. (1), $d_{eff}=d_{outer}\sqrt{1-0.81}=0.436\,d_{outer}$, so flux $\propto(1-\rho^2)=0.19$ — an $\approx81\%$ light loss and a diffraction spot $\approx2.3\times$ larger.
Verifiable prediction: SNR in low light drops by $\approx\sqrt{0.19}\approx0.44$ relative to an unobscured equal-OD aperture, and MTF cutoff falls proportionally. Falsifier: identical SNR/MTF would require $\rho\approx0$, contradicting the annular design ([PAGE 20]–[PAGE 21]).

CE-3 — Kalman-vs-non-Gaussian-motion veto.
Claim under test: "A standard Kalman filter tracks erratic pedestrian motion without divergence."
Veto: OFF. The survey states human motion is "generally non-Gaussian" ([PAGE 7]); the linear-Gaussian posterior is misspecified.
Verifiable prediction: innovation sequence $z_k-H\hat x_{k|k-1}$ fails a whiteness/normality test and the normalized innovation squared $\text{NIS}=\nu_k^\top S^{-1}\nu_k$ exceeds the $\chi^2$ confidence bound during turns/occlusions; RMSE diverges relative to a particle filter on the same clips. EKF recovers only to $O(\|\Delta x\|^2)$ linearization error ([PAGE 7]). Falsifier: Gaussian innovations and bounded NIS across maneuvers would vindicate the plain Kalman filter and refute the survey's stated caveat.

Supplementary (dimensional): the exact real-time singularity is $N_{eff}=1/\sum_i(w_t^{(i)})^2\to1$ for a joint particle filter in state dimension $3N$ ([PAGE 12]); predict that a centralized joint-state fusion of $N$ targets crosses the frame-budget clock (60 fps ceiling, Straw et al. [PAGE 22]) before accuracy saturates, forcing the hybrid/distributed architecture the survey recommends ([PAGE 5]–[PAGE 6]). Falsifier: a centralized joint estimator sustaining 60 fps at large $N$ on commodity smart-camera compute would contradict the processing-limit claim on [PAGE 14].

All vetoes above are grounded in the source's own equations, assumptions, and stated limitations; none relies on invented quantities.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Bottom line: the survey's quantitative content reduces to a projection → homography → triangulation geometry chain, a Kalman→EKF→particle estimation ladder forced by non-Gaussian human motion, an area-equivalence folded-optic aperture law, and two fusion operators (Dempster–Shafer with a conflict singularity at $K\to1$, and particle weighting with an effective-sample-size collapse). The four cross-layer vetoes and three counterfactual tests are the falsifiable core; the persistent open problems (occlusion, camera-to-target ratio, real-time $3N$ state) are the survey's honestly stated frontiers.
