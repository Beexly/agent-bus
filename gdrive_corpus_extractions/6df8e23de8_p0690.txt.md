2. Biomechanical veto. Flight requires intact musculature, skeletal lever arms, and neuromuscular coordination. The text infers emotion from the impulse “to take flight” [PAGE 690], but if the biomechanical flight apparatus is impaired (injury, molt, juvenile underdevelopment), the “vague painful consciousness” may arise without behavioral discharge. Emotion proper cannot be identified solely with flight behavior; the veto condition is \(\text{flight\_capacity} = 0 \implies \text{observed\_flight} = 0\) despite \(\mathcal{E}(\tau) > 0\). The source does not control for this.

3. Weather / environmental-context veto. Detection of “man’s approach” [PAGE 690] depends on visibility, ambient noise, and weather. If fog, rain, or wind occludes the human aspect \(h\), then \(p(h,p) = p(h)p(p)\) because the stimulus never registers; \(I(H;P) = 0\) bits, and no association forms regardless of prior painful history. The emotional chain is therefore context-gated: \(\text{visibility} < \text{threshold} \implies A(s,p) = \varnothing\).

Adjudication: The chain is provisionally coherent only within the source’s internal assumptions (Lamarckian inheritance, intact flight apparatus, unobstructed sensory detection). All three layers independently veto universalization: the physical veto blocks transgenerational inheritance, the biomechanical veto blocks emotion–behavior identity, and the weather veto blocks stimulus reliability. Consequently, James’s inference that “emotions in general have severally thus originated” [PAGE 690] is not robust across environmental, developmental, or genetic boundary conditions.

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable prediction.

Veto Scenario 1 — Cross-fostering isolation. Collect eggs from a fear-of-man population described on [PAGE 690] and rear hatchlings with conspecific foster parents that have never experienced human persecution, in acoustic and visual isolation from humans and from alarm calls. Verifiable reward: binary flight response to a standardized human approach at age of independence. Spencer/James prediction (Lamarckian inheritance, \(\lambda > 0\)): juveniles fly despite zero personal painful experience. Modern associative-learning prediction (\(\lambda = 0\)): no flight, because \(I(H;P) = 0\) without direct or vicarious painful pairing. Verifiable veto: observation of no flight falsifies the claim that nervous modifications appear “ready-made at birth” [PAGE 690].

Veto Scenario 2 — Sensory occlusion during approach. Habituate adult birds with a known history of painful human encounters (established \(\mathcal{E}(\tau) > 0\) from [PAGE 690] aggregation) and then present the human approach under heavy fog or acoustic masking that reduces detectability below the sensory threshold. Verifiable reward: latency to flight or absence thereof. Spencer/James prediction: the “impression produced on its senses by the approaching man” [PAGE 690] still entails ancestral nerve excitement because the emotional state is ideal reproduction, not contingent on real-time detection. Prediction under the weather veto: if sensory input is occluded, \(p(h,p) = p(h)p(p)\), \(I(H;P) = 0\), and flight is abolished or significantly delayed. Verifiable veto: abolition of flight under occlusion falsifies the claim that emotion is an autonomous ideal reproduction independent of current stimulus detection.

Veto Scenario 3 — Neural pathway blockade with cognitive dissociation. In birds with prior painful human experience, reversibly block the putative “incipiently reflex” subcortical pathway (avian amygdala homolog) while sparing higher pallial areas capable of conscious pain representation. Verifiable reward: simultaneous measurement of (a) flight behavior and (b) conditioned place aversion or vocal distress indicating conscious painful consciousness. Spencer/James prediction: because emotion proper is “undecomposable” [PAGE 690], blocking the reflex should abolish both flight and the accompanying painful consciousness as a unit. Decomposable-process prediction: flight is abolished (reflex blocked) but conscious aversion persists, showing that “vague painful consciousness” and motor expression are separable. Verifiable veto: dissociation of behavior from conscious pain falsifies the homogeneity claim on [PAGE 690] and supports a multi-component emotion model rather than Spencer’s aggregated, seemingly homogeneous emotion.
