The passage on [PAGE 215] sits inside a specific genealogy. Hull's 1943 Principles of Behavior attempts to formalize the conditioned reflex tradition descending from Pavlov (1927), in which a neutral stimulus gains excitatory power through contingency with an unconditioned stimulus. Hull adds Thorndike's Law of Effect (1911) via the law of primary reinforcement cited at [PAGE 80], converting association into a quantifiable habit-strength increment △sHR. The sHR notation itself—excitatory habit strength—represents Hull's effort to make learning algebraic and cumulative.

The "retrocausal" structure is explicit in the text: the reinforcer arrives after the response, yet the law assigns strength backward to every afferent element active at the time of the to-be-conditioned reaction ([PAGE 215]). This is a temporal credit-assignment loop running from outcome (reinforcement) to antecedent stimuli (the entire compound S). Hull's perseverative-trace clause extends this loop further into the past: stimuli whose energy is no longer present still acquire strength because their traces are "active" at the moment of reaction ([PAGE 215]). The later Rescorla-Wagner model (1972) directly corrected Hull's "every component" problem by replacing universal increment with a signed prediction error, δ = λ − ΣV, such that only surprising outcomes drive learning and blocked or redundant stimuli receive little or no credit. Modern temporal-difference learning (Sutton & Barto) further replaces Hull's fixed trace with a learned eligibility trace, decaying at a rate tuned to the actual contingency delay.

Multi-week traps arise from the interaction of perseverative traces and sparse reinforcement. If τ_c,i is on the order of hours or days, then a stimulus component present in week 1 can still be in trace when reinforcement arrives in week 2, producing spontaneous recovery or retroactive interference across sessions. Hull's system has no mechanism to tag a component with its trial of origin; thus, after weeks of training, the habit strength sHR_i is an entangled sum of contributions from many contexts. The renewal effect (Bouton) and context-dependent relapse are direct consequences: when the animal is returned to the original laboratory context, the olfactory, visual, and proprioceptive elements enumerated on [PAGE 215] reactivate their stored traces, reinstating behavior that appeared extinguished in a different room. Without a context-gating operator, Hull's model predicts that multi-week training produces a single undifferentiated sHR mass rather than separable episodic memories—a gray zone the text does not address.

In reality, Hull's rule selects the component with the highest total sHR, which may be an old, salient, non-causal element (e.g., a persistent proprioceptive posture) rather than the true predictive cue. The organism regresses not to the most rational cue but to the most entrenched one.

The clock is the perseverative time constant τ_c,i. It sets the credit-assignment window: only stimuli within roughly τ_c,i of the reinforcement receive △sHR ([PAGE 215]). This mismatch between the biological clock (τ_c,i) and the environmental contingency clock is a fundamental source of mislearning in Hull's system, and it explains why crisis regression can lock in superstitions: the clock has already mislabeled causality before the crisis ever occurs.
