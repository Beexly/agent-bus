*Trap 3: The diameter-vs-oscillation trade is a genuine two-regime switch.* Theorem 7 and Theorem 8 are not ordered pointwise: for Δ_op near LD (i.e. observables oscillating at scale of the whole state space), Theorem 8 gives v² ~ 3200/π² · L²D²κ^{-1}(1 + 0) ≈ 3200/192 ≈ 16.7× larger than Theorem 7's 192/π² · L²D²κ^{-1}, exactly the counterintuitive regime in which the “improved” bound is worse. Failing to check this will misattribute Theorem 8 as uniformly dominating Theorem 7.

*Trap 4: The spectral-gap notion is not the Markov-chain spectral gap.* Definition 9 [PAGE 8] defines σ_t as the one-step singular value of the *transition operator between different measures* µ_t → µ_{t−1}, not the second eigenvalue of a fixed kernel. Attempting to verify Assumption 6 with a single stationary-measure spectral gap (as in the classical Dobrushin/Diaconis–Stroock line) will under-verify: the inhomogeneous product Π σ_ℓ must satisfy the reciprocal bound in Eq. (8), which is precisely the quantity the merging-time literature [28] studies.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The three-way framing of “crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock” maps cleanly onto the paper's own combinatorial triad of (curvature, tightness construction, burn-in).

**“Crisis habit regression” = the chain's rate of forgetting its initial state.** Lemma 12 [PAGE 11] gives the forgetting lemma: for any probability µ, a centred matrix map H̃ := H − µ(H) satisfies Lip(H̃)_op = Lip(H)_op and ||H̃||_∞ ≤ D Lip(H)_op — the initial state's memory is *compressed* into the diameter, not eliminated. The chain's curvature κ_t then generates geometric decay, and Assumption 1's sum identity 1 + Σ_{k=1}^t Π_{ℓ=k}^t (1 − κ_ℓ) ≤ 1/κ is the exact statement of “the memory of the initial state regresses at an average rate κ.” In the Elo setting, Lemma 27 [PAGE 31] instantiates this with ||E(X_t − ρ_t)||_2² ≤ (1−κ)^{t−1}||X_0 − ρ_1||_2² + Δ/κ + 2η²/κ — the “habit term” is the (1−κ)^{t−1} initial-state penalty, the “crisis” is the transient regime t ≲ κ^{-1}, and the fixed-point of the recursion is the asymptotic tracking error. The recursive operator that the analyst must model is precisely this three-term sum: geometric memory, drift floor Δ/κ, noise floor 2η²/κ. If the analyst collapses any one of the three into a single “rating quality” scalar, the result will fail whenever the drift Δ and the step size η are not jointly tuned.

**“Counter-trap” = the tightness construction of Section 4.3.1.** The chain X_{t+1} = X_t/2 + (D/2)B_{t+1} is a deliberate counterexample to the reader's optimistic hypothesis that sub-Gaussian concentration at variance proxy Δ²κ^{-1} suffices. Because F_t(X_t) = (Δ/2)cos(2π X_0/D) depends only on X_0 for all t ≤ ℓ, the sum S_ℓ = (Δ/2)ℓ cos(2π X_0/D) is deterministically correlated in the worst possible way: the ℓ terms do not diversify. The larger point is that the trap *is not adversarial to a chain, only to a chain that forgets slowly relative to the observable's frequency*. The observable family 2π2^t/D is designed to oscillate at the forgetting rate, so the chain's fastest-mixing scales are precisely those on which F_t is most sensitive. In audit language: a “counter-trap” is any situation where the observable's feature scale is locked to the chain's spectral edge — the exact opposite of the ergodic-averaging regime in which concentration bounds are comfortable.

**“Clock” = the burn-in parameter.** Three clocks coexist, and they are not exchangeable. (i) The concentration clock: n, the number of samples. (ii) The tracking clock for Elo Theorem 28 [PAGE 31]: t ≥ C κ^{-1} log(nM ε^{-1} η^{-1}), an explicit burn-in, which couples n, M, ε, η — the four free parameters of the problem. (iii) The averaging clock for Theorem 29 [PAGE 32]: T ≥ C ε^{-2} η^{-2} M² n log(n/δ). The averaging clock is *quadratically worse* in 1/η than the tracking clock is linearly worse in 1/η. The counter-trap is to believe that “averaging over longer windows” is always safer; Theorem 29 says that if you insist on averaging and want a distribution-free (granularity-free) bound, you pay T ∝ η^{-2}. Conversely, Theorem 28 gives a pointwise bound under granularity assumptions B = 2√2 η + 2h_ρ + 4√2 h_q. The recursive-theory-of-mind point is that the *analyst's* error mode mirrors the *chain's*: both substitute “more data” for “more curvature,” and both fail in exactly the regime κ → 0 (or λ → 0).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The paper's “layers” are (L1) the observable layer — Lipschitz/bounded sup-norm; (L2) the geometry layer — Wasserstein/Banach curvature κ_t; (L3) the spectral layer — one-step singular value σ_t or uniform κ; (L4) the granularity layer — σ_∞ := sup_{t,x} diam Supp P_t(x,·); (L5) the environment layer in Elo — drift Δ and curvature ν of (ρ^t, q_t). A cross-layer veto is any pair of layers whose joint failure produces a bound that is formally true but vacuous. Four vetoes are decisive.

**Veto 1 (L1 × L2).** If Lip(F_t)_op > 0 but Assumption 1 has κ = 0, Theorem 7's bound is exactly exp(0)=1 and carries no information. The mechanism is the tilt recursion in Lemma 14: with κ = 0, r = e^0 = 1 in Lemma 15, and the “summability” 1 + Σ_i Π (1 − 0) = 1 + n grows linearly. The veto is sufficient: a κ = 0 chain with D < ∞ and L < ∞ still yields v² = ∞.

**Veto 2 (L2 × L4).** Lemma 13 [PAGE 11] bounds ||W_j||_∞ ≤ e^{sLD/2}; if D = ∞ (unbounded state space), the only escape is to switch to Regime (B) and Assumption 5's Δ_op, Δ_F < ∞ [PAGE 8]. But Regime (B) requires λ > 0 (Veto 3), so the pair (D = ∞, λ = 0) is a genuine dead zone in the paper: no theorem applies.

**Veto 3 (L3 × L5).** In Elo, λ̲ is the Fiedler value of the comparison graph with weights q_{i,j}. If some pair is never matched (q_{i,j} = 0 for some {i,j}), then the associated Laplacian disconnects, λ̲ = 0, and κ = (1/8)ηe^{−4M}λ̲ = 0. The joint chain of Lemma 26 then has κ_joint = 0, and both Theorems 28 and 29 collapse. There is no η rescuing this: κ is proportional to ηλ̲, so η → 0 does not increase κ. The biomechanical analogue is a competition graph in which one player never plays anyone — a structural impossibility, not a statistical one.

**Veto 4 (L2 × L5, the “weather” veto).** The drift Δ ≥ 0 on [PAGE 30] appears inside sqrt(Δ/κ) in both Theorems 28 and 29. Locally in time, an abnormally abrupt environment shock — “weather” in the sports-analytics framing — inflates Δ. But the theorem's expectation E[‖ρ^{t+1} − ρ^t‖_2² + 4M‖ρ^{t+1} − ρ^t‖_1 | F_t] ≤ Δ is averaged, so a *single* extreme shock is not vetoed by the theorem; only a sustained shift in the mean drift is. The cross-layer subtlety: a shock also raises h_ρ (granularity), and Theorem 28's B = 2√2 η + 2h_ρ + 4√2 h_q inflates the confidence-band radius CεB/√κ linearly in h_ρ. The correct adjudication is therefore: a shock is a *hard* veto only when it shifts the mean of the drift over the averaging window; a transient shock is absorbed into the h_ρ factor and is reflected only in the confidence radius, not in the bias.

**The hard veto condition.** All four vetoes reduce to a single meta-condition: the intersection of (κ > 0) ∧ (D < ∞ or Δ_op < ∞ with λ > 0) ∧ (σ_∞ < ∞ if using Theorem 36/37) must be non-empty. If any conjunct fails, no quantity of the paper's machinery produces a finite-probability, finite-radius claim. This is a stronger statement than any single theorem's hypothesis — it is the whole paper's satisfiability condition, and the analysts' habit of quoting only the rates (v² ∝ L²D²/κ) without checking the conjuncts is the single most dangerous cross-layer error.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable, distributional counterfactual experiments follow. Each directly exercises a different layer and each has a *numerically checkable prediction*. All three are stated purely in the paper's own formal quantities; no external data are required to state them, so each is a genuine RLVR-style verifiable veto (pass/fail against a pre-registered prediction, not a narrative retelling).

**RLVR-1 — The log-diameter lower-bound contravariance test.** Construct the Section 4.3.1 chain on Ω = [0, D] with X_{t+1} = X_t/2 + (D/2)B_{t+1}, B_t iid Bern(½), κ ≥ ½; observables F_t(x) = (Δ/2)cos(2π2^t x/D) for t = 1,…,ℓ with ℓ the largest integer satisfying πΔ2^ℓ ≤ LD; take the chain started uniformly on [0,D] so µ_0 = Unif([0,D]) is stationary (verbatim [PAGE 21]: “Let the chain start from the uniform distribution on Ω, which is the stationary distribution of the chain”). Set ε = Δ/4. *Prediction (falsifiable):* P(S_ℓ > Δℓ/4) = 1/3 exactly, from the elementary computation on [PAGE 22]: “P(cos(2πX_0/D) ≥ ½) = 1/3.” *Veto consequence:* any claimed sub-Gaussian bound with variance proxy v² must satisfy v² ≥ ℓε²/(2 log 3) = Ω(Δ² log(LD/Δ)/κ^{-1}). Run the test across κ ∈ {0.5, 0.9} and Δ ∈ {0.05, 0.2}: the predicted coefficient ℓε²/(2 log 3) must scale linearly in ℓ and quadratically in ε, contradicting any v² that is independent of Δ. A v² that passes for Δ = 0.2 but fails at Δ = 0.05 vetoes the claimed bound.

**RLVR-2 — The κ^{-2} versus κ^{-1} separation.** Compare two bounds on the *same* chain: (i) Theorem 37 [PAGE 39] with granularity σ_∞ and proxy ∝ L²σ_∞²κ^{-2}, and (ii) Theorem 7 or 10 on the same chain. Pick a chain where κ ≪ σ_∞/D (i.e. mixing is slow relative to the diameter), which is the “much slower than the diameter” regime explicitly named [PAGE 3]: “This can be much worse than our bounds whenever mixing is much slower than the diameter of the chain.” *Prediction:* the ratio (σ_∞²κ^{-2}) / (D²κ^{-1}) = σ_∞²/(D²κ) grows like κ^{-1} and should be observed to exceed 10² for κ = 10^{-3}, σ_∞/D = 10^{-1}. *Veto consequence:* if a numerical or simulation experiment finds the two bounds coinciding (ratio bounded by a constant) on such a chain, the claim that Regime (A)'s κ^{-1} improvement is genuine is falsified — the theorem would still be true, but the paper's stated advantage over Pillai–Smith would be vacuous.

**RLVR-3 — The Elo joint-chain curvature under drift zero vs drift positive.** Run the dynamic-BTL Elo chain with n = 4 players, comparison graph a fixed star (so λ̲ ≤ ½, computable exactly from the Laplacian spectrum), step size η = 0.1, M = 3 (so κ = (1/8)·0.1·e^{−12}·λ̲ ≈ 1.1 × 10^{-6}·λ̲). Freeze ρ^t for all t (Δ = 0) versus a small rotation of ρ^t with drift Δ = 10^{-4}·κ holding λ̲ fixed. *Predictions:* (a) With Δ = 0, Theorem 28's tracking bias is exactly (1+ε)√(2η²/κ) + CεB/√κ; the drift term sqrt(Δ/κ) contributes zero and must be unmeasurable in the empirical time-average error (the √κ denominator makes the noise floor ≈ √2η = 0.14). (b) With Δ = 10^{-4}·κ, the bias term sqrt(Δ/κ) = sqrt(10^{-4}) = 10^{-2}, which is ≈ 7% of the noise floor: detectable at n = 10^4 samples with ε = 0.1 confidence. *Veto consequence:* if the empirically measured time-average error does not increase by ≈ 10^{-2} under positive drift but by the right amount, the joint-chain curvature Lemma 26 or the decomposition of Lemma 27 is falsified. A secondary veto: the optimal η from the balance of noise floor 2η²/κ and lag Δ/κ is predicted at η* = Θ(√Δ). At Δ = 10^{-4}κ, this predicts η* ≈ 0.01·√κ ≈ 1.1 × 10^{-5} — well below the setup's η = 0.1. Any η-sweep showing a monotone improvement as η decreases past this predicted minimum is inconsistent with the paper's bias-variance crossover claim [PAGE 32]: “the bound in Theorem 28 is minimised when η = Θ(√Δ).”
