Veto proof: The instinct explanation is veto-proof only if the predicted behavior survives all three layers simultaneously. Tolman's milder claim on [PAGE 303] makes the response explicitly dependent on E, which means it is inherently vulnerable to ambient and weather-layer vetoes. That vulnerability is a feature, not a bug: it renders the interactionist model falsifiable in a way that the strong "sole inheritance" doctrine is not. The breakdown regime occurs when all three layers are held constant but behavior still fails to appear; in that case the missing variable must be sought in the maturation clock M(t) or in unmeasured H components, not in instinct as a primitive.

Verifiable prediction under the strong instinct doctrine [PAGE 303]: the behavior appears with P(A | H, E=∅) = 1 because it is "dependent solely upon inheritance." Scenario 2 — Heredity swap with constant environment: Cross-foster embryos between two species with distinct response characters, holding post-natal E identical. Verifiable prediction under sole inheritance: the expressed behavior follows the donor heredity H_donor, not the rearing environment. Veto condition: if behavior tracks the rearing environment E instead of H_donor, heredity-only determination is vetoed and the interactionist claim on [PAGE 303] is supported.

Scenario 3 — Maturation clock arrest: Genetically or pharmacologically arrest M(t) at a pre-onset state while H and E remain intact and supportive. Verifiable prediction under a purely hereditary model: the behavior should appear immediately because H alone suffices [PAGE 303]. Veto condition: if onset remains absent until M(t) reaches a critical threshold θ, then heredity is insufficient and the clock layer is causally necessary. This veto falsifies any model that omits M(t) from the composition f(H, E, M).
