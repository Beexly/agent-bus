Scenario 1 (novel-object sensory isolation): An adult views an object with zero prior reproductive trace (\(R_{<t} = \emptyset\)), receives no name, and is permitted only a single static view. Counterfactual veto condition: if object constancy were innate or directly given, the subject would immediately report hidden properties (back, squareness, mass). Measurable veto trigger: the subject reports only local proximal qualities and performs at chance on forced-choice questions about hidden properties until exploratory experience accumulates. Prediction: \(P(o_t \mid s_t, R_{<t}=\emptyset)\) remains diffuse; constancy does not emerge without reproductive trace. Anchor: “Every perception is an acquired perception.” [PAGE 78]

Scenario 2 (prism-induced sensory conflict): A subject wears wedge prisms that optically displace the visual field and attempts to reach toward the table. Counterfactual veto condition: if perception directly delivered the undistorted table, reach endpoints would be accurate from trial 1. Measurable veto trigger: systematic initial reach error in the direction of the optical shift, followed by gradual recalibration across repeated trials. Prediction: endpoint error decreases monotonically with trial number; visual-haptic constancy is re-acquired rather than given. No rate constant is asserted; only the directional trend is predicted.

Scenario 3 (infant versus adult auditory startle): A sudden loud sound is presented to an infant and to an adult habituated to the same sound class. Counterfactual veto condition: if sudden sounds were equally salient independent of habit, startle magnitude would be matched across ages. Measurable veto trigger: infant startle amplitude significantly exceeds adult startle amplitude, and adult startle attenuates after repeated familiarization. Prediction: startle amplitude is inversely related to the strength of the reproductive trace for that sound class. Anchor: “all sudden sounds seem alarming to babies” [PAGE 78 footnote]; “Nature … is frugal … experience and habit will soon produce.” [PAGE 78]
