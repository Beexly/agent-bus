A deterministic LSTM trained with an L1/L2-style residual (Eq 8) is a conditional-mean predictor; it *does* regress toward the mean of plausible futures and blurs high-entropy motion. The claim is only strictly true for stochastic/generative predictors. (b) The Eq 7 "log-linear" generative model carries an intractable partition term $\log Z(h_t)$ over a $\sim$$14{\times}14{\times}512\approx100{,}352$-dimensional output; the paper never specifies its computation, and training actually optimizes Eq 8, so Eq 7 is an interpretive scaffold, not the fitted likelihood. (c) Static-scene degeneracy: if $\hat m_t\!\to\!0$ everywhere, then $E(t)\!\to\!0$ and Eq 9's numerator and denominator both collapse, so the attention is undefined/uniform — the method cannot localize a "frozen" action. (d) The paper's own recurring-action caveat (Page 2, "can be recurring") conflicts with a residual that vanishes once a periodic action is learned: steady-state walking produces near-zero residual and thus low attention. (e) Page 14 admits the residual "is often not at the center of the region of interest," directly degrading Eq 10's $\phi$ term. (f) Source anomaly: Page 13 states supervised DFG "achieve 10.6 and 88.3 for AUC and AAE" — an AUC of 10.6 is outside the valid $[0,1]$ range, so this is almost certainly a transcription error in the source (likely 0.6); I flag rather than trust it.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All equations below are transcribed from the paper; units and breakdown regimes are annotated.

LSTM cell (Page 5, Eqs 1–6):
$$i_t=\sigma(W_i x_t+W_{hi}h_{t-1}+b_i),\quad f_t=\sigma(W_f x_t+W_{hf}h_{t-1}+b_f)$$
$$o_t=\sigma(W_o x_t+W_{ho}h_{t-1}+b_o),\quad g_t=\varphi(W_g x_t+W_{hg}h_{t-1}+b_g)$$
$$m_t=f_t\cdot m_{t-1}+i_t\cdot g_t,\qquad h_t=o_t\cdot\varphi(m_t)$$
Units: $x_t\in\mathbb R^{d}$ dimensionless activations; $\sigma:\mathbb R\to(0,1)$, $\varphi=\tanh:\mathbb R\to(-1,1)$; $(\cdot)$ is element-wise product. Hierarchical coupling (Page 5): $(h^l_t,m^l_t)=\mathrm{LSTM}(h^l_{t-1},h^{l-1}_t,m^{l-1}_t)$ — higher layers see the *current* lower-layer output, "as opposed to that from the previous time step." Breakdown: standard vanishing/exploding gradients over long horizons contradict the Page 5 claim that LSTMs are "progressively better … especially for actions of longer duration"; with only "1 epoch" of training the cell weights barely adapt.

Generative prediction (Page 6, Eq 7):
$$\log p(\hat f^{s}_{t+1}\mid h^{l}_t)=\sum_{n=1}^{t} f(W_e,f^{S}_t)+\log Z(h_t)$$
$Z(h_t)$ is a normalization constant (partition function). Breakdown/singularity: $Z$ over the full feature tensor is intractable; not computed anywhere in the text. The formula is a formal energy-based statement; optimization uses Eq 8 instead.

Prediction error / objective (Page 6, Eq 8):
$$E(t)=\frac{1}{n_f}\sum_{i=1}^{w_f}\sum_{j=1}^{h_f}\hat m_t(i,j)\odot\big\lVert f^{S}_{t+1}(i,j)-\hat f^{S}_{t+1}(i,j)\big\rVert^{2}$$
Units: squared activation difference $[\text{activation}^2]$, scaled by count $1/n_f$ (dimensionless) and masked by $\hat m_t$ (dimensionless zero-order-hold indicator). With $w_f{=}h_f{=}14$, $d_f{=}512$. Singular regime: $\hat m_t\to0 \Rightarrow E(t)\to0$ (static scene); after a periodic action is learned, residual $\to 0$ (under-attention to recurring motion).

Error-based attention (Page 6, Eq 9):
$$\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{k=1}^{w_k}\sum_{n=1}^{h_k}\exp(e_{mn})}$$
Units: dimensionless probability mass, $\sum_{ij}\alpha_{ij}=1$. Fixed temperature $=1$ (non-parametric). Breakdown: $e_{ij}\ge0$ unbounded $\Rightarrow$ $\exp(e_{ij})$ overflow for abrupt scene cuts; uniform error $\Rightarrow$ uniform $\alpha$; single spike $\Rightarrow$ one-hot (arg-max) attention. Derived (mine, not the paper's) spatial concentration measure: Shannon entropy $H_t=-\sum_{ij}\alpha_{ij}\ln\alpha_{ij}\in[0,\ln(w_f h_f)]=[0,\ln 196]\approx[0,5.278]$ nats — low $H_t$ = confident localization, high $H_t$ = diffuse failure.

Tube energy (Page 7, Eq 10):
$$E(B^{t}_i)=w_\alpha\,\phi(\alpha_{ij},B^{t}_i)+w_t\,\delta\big(B^{t}_i,\{B^{t-1}_j\}\big)$$
$\phi$ = characteristic distance between box center and max-error location; $\delta$ = minimum spatial distance to previous-step boxes (pixels). "we set $k=10,\ w_\alpha=0.75$." Breakdown: dimensional heterogeneity — a softmax-derived dimensionless $\phi$ is summed with a pixel-distance $\delta$ with no stated normalization, so the weighting is only meaningful if $\delta$ is implicitly scaled; and fixed $k=10$ mis-ranks scenes with $>10$ or $<10$ true actors.

Metrics (Page 8-9): IoU $=\dfrac{\text{Area}(\cap)}{\text{Area}(\cup)}\in[0,1]$; mAP = mean of per-class average precision $\in[0,1]$; AUC over overlap thresholds $\in[0,1]$; AAE in angular degrees. Reported operating points (Page 11-13): JHMDB recall $77.8\%@\sigma{=}0.5$; THUMOS'13 recall $0.47@\sigma{=}0.4$, $0.33@\sigma{=}0.5$; homogeneity $74.56\%\,(k_{gt})$, $78.97\%\,(k_{opt})$.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage. The idea descends from Zacks, Tversky & Iyer's event-segmentation theory [49] and Horstmann & Herwig's surprise/novelty attention results [13,14], which the authors cite for "longer-term, temporal surprise … correlate with event boundary detection" and "short-term spatial surprise … stronger effect on human attention" (Page 2). Architecturally it fuses Hochreiter–Schmidhuber LSTM [12], Bahdanau attention [4], Vondrick et al.'s feature-anticipation from unlabeled video [46,47], VGG-16 [32] and SSD [27]. Most proximally it is the localization sibling of the authors' own CVPR 2019 "perceptual prediction framework for self supervised event segmentation" [2], from which the adaptive learning schedule ($\Delta^-_t,\Delta^+_t$) is imported.

Multi-week replication traps. (1) The learning-rate schedule (Page 7: initial LR $1\times10^{-8}$, $\Delta^-_t=1\times10^{-2}$, $\Delta^+_t=1\times10^{-3}$) is stated but the adaptation rule from [2] is not reproduced here — a re-implementer will spend days recovering it. (2) The cluster-count protocol ($k_{gt}$ vs elbow-derived $k_{opt}$, with "$k_{opt}$ is typically three times the number of ground truth classes," Page 9) is a decisive, easily-mis-set knob: mAP swings sharply between the two settings across all three datasets (Page 10-11). (3) "Class-agnostic SSD … considering bounding box proposals at all detection thresholds" (Page 12) is underspecified and materially changes recall. Any of these three, mis-set, will produce results that fail to reproduce the reported numbers.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Interpretation for this domain: "recursive loop" = the self-supervised predict→error→update feedback; "crisis habit regression" = collapse into a degenerate fixed predictor; "counter-trap" = design choices that avert it; "clock" = the streaming/causal temporal constraint.

The system is a closed loop: the event model $W_e$ predicts $\hat f^S_{t+1}$; the residual (Eq 8) is the reward; gradients update $W_e$; the sharper model changes the next residual. Because supervision is generated by the model's own error, it is a fixed-point iteration seeking $W_e^\star$ where residual equals irreducible aleatoric motion. Crisis/habit regression: if the model learns the *background* well (predictable clutter) and the *actor* poorly, the residual concentrates on the actor — this is the intended attractor. The failure attractor is the opposite collapse: a static-scene or fully-learned-periodic regime drives $\hat m_t\to0$ and residual $\to0$, so attention (Eq 9) degenerates and the loop receives no gradient signal (a silent lock, not a diverging one).

Counter-traps embedded in the design: (i) class-agnostic proposals (Page 4) prevent the model from learning "background = non-object" shortcuts; (ii) the $\hat m_t$ mask (Eq 8) re-injects changing-region weighting even when absolute residual is small; (iii) hierarchical decoding (Page 5) keeps lower-level spatial residuals visible to higher layers so error propagates upward rather than being averaged away.

Clock: the model is strictly causal — "output … in a streaming fashion, requiring only a single pass" (Page 1) and Eq 10's $\delta$ term ties box $t$ to box $t{-}1$. This forbids bidirectional/temporal-smoothing tricks (e.g., Viterbi linking as in [7]); the temporal consistency must emerge from LSTM memory, which the authors admit is why $\delta$ is "optional" (Page 7). Under adversarial clock conditions (large camera motion, JHMDB, Page 10) the residual fires everywhere, and the recursive loop mis-attributes scene motion to actor motion — a habit regression the causal constraint cannot correct.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/vision veto proof (adapting the template's "physical/biomechanical/weather veto" to the paper's actual physical failure modes; there is no weather or biomechanics in this source).

Veto 1 — Static-action veto (formal). If action $A$ occupies cells $S$ with $f^S_{t+1}(i,j)=f^S_t(i,j)$ for $(i,j)\in S$, then $\hat m_t(i,j)=0$, so Eq 8 contributes zero over $S$ and Eq 9 gives $\alpha_{ij}\to$ uniform on $S$. Proof of failure: a perfectly still actor with moving background yields peak error *off* the actor; Eq 10's $\phi$ then ranks background boxes lower-energy than the actor box → the true tube is not in the top-$k$. The method's premise ("unpredictable factors … point to the actor") is physically vetoed whenever the discriminative cue is absence of motion.

Veto 2 — Global-motion / camera-pan veto. Under camera motion $g_t$, $f^S_{t+1}(i,j)\approx \mathcal T_{g_t}f^S_t(i,j)$; if the predictor has not factored $g_t$, $\hat m_t>0$ over the whole frame, so Eq 8 error is spatially flat and Eq 9 entropy $H_t\to\ln 196$ (maximal, 5.278 nats). The paper confirms this empirically on JHMDB: "large camera motion and intra-class variations have a significant impact" and mAP suffers at $k_{gt}$ (Page 10). Veto holds: diffuse error cannot localize.

Veto 3 — Occlusion / appearance-transform veto. The design explicitly anticipates actors that "transform their physical appearance" (Page 4), which spikes $e_{ij}$ and, via $\exp(\cdot)$ in Eq 9, can numerically dominate (one-hot) or overflow. Page 14's own observation — error "often not at the center of the region of interest" — is the empirical signature of this veto: high recall but degraded IoU/localization quality.

Net adjudication: the physical layer does not veto the method in its intended regime (motion-bearing actors, moderate camera stability), but rigorously vetoes it in three regimes — static actors, global motion, and heavy appearance change/occlusion — all consistent with the reported per-class variance (Page 13, Fig 3a-b: 7/10 UCF-Sports classes exceed 80% recall @0.5, the other 3 do not).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual vetoes (scenarios that would falsify the paper's claims) with concrete, checkable predictions.

Veto A — Static-scene reward collapse. Setup: freeze the actor, keep background still (residual $\to0$ everywhere). Verifiable prediction: Eq 8 loss $\to 0$; Eq 9 attention $\to$ uniform ($H_t\to\ln196\approx5.28$ nats); Eq 10 top-$k$ boxes become arbitrary → localization IoU $\to$ chance. Falsifier for the paper's core claim if a truly static action class scores near zero mAP while a moving class scores high — testable by constructing a static control on UCF Sports "Lifting" holds.

Veto B — Camera-motion saliency inversion. Setup: pure translational pan, no actor motion. Prediction: error is spatially uniform, $\alpha_{ij}\approx1/196$; the model should localize *no* specific region (max $\alpha_{ij}\approx$ baseline $0.0051$). The paper's JHMDB result (Page 10, mAP drops at $k_{gt}$) is the weak-form confirmation. Falsifiable prediction: on a synthetic pan set, per-frame max attention stays within a few percent of $1/196$ — if instead a spurious hotspot forms, that refutes the "predictability = attention" mapping.

Veto C — Recurring-action under-attention (tests the Page 2 "surprise ≠ rarity … can be recurring" caveat). Setup: train to convergence on a periodic action (e.g., walking cycle). Prediction: once the LSTM predicts the cycle, steady-state $\hat m_t\to0$ on the actor's core and residual shifts to cycle transitions only; homogeneity of the actor cluster should stay high but spatial attention should oscillate rather than persist. Verifiable metric: $H_t$ should be periodic (peaks at gait transitions, troughs mid-stride). If attention instead stays persistently peaked on the actor across the whole cycle, the "residual = surprise, and recurring actions become predictable" claim is falsified.

Corroborating numbers to check against (all from the source): UCF-Sports class-agnostic mAP $55.7$ at $\sim10$ proposals vs APT $63.7$ at $\sim1{,}500$ (Table 1, Page 10); THUMOS'13 recall $0.84/0.72/0.58/0.47/0.33$ and mAP@0.2 $=0.59$ vs VideoLSTM $0.37$ and Actor-Supervision $\sim0.46$ (Table 2, Page 12); GTEA gaze AUC $0.861$/AAE $13.6^\circ$ (Table 3, Page 13). Each is a direct target for the three vetoes above.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Summary: the paper is internally coherent — its strength (motion-residual-as-attention) and its failure modes (static, global-motion, and appearance-transform regimes) are the same mechanism viewed from two sides, and the source is candid about several of them (Page 14 error mis-centering, Page 10 camera motion). The main audit flags are the overstated "no averaging" LSTM claim (Page 5), the uncomputed $Z(h_t)$ in Eq 7, the dimensional heterogeneity of Eq 10, and the out-of-range DFG AUC "10.6" (Page 13), which appears to be a transcription error.
