Let a run be represented by true position \(\gamma(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\) for \(t\in[0,T]\), with \(\gamma\) measured in metres and \(t\) in seconds. The true path length is
\[
L=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\right\|_{2}\,dt \quad [\mathrm{m}].
\]
A watch records discrete fixes \(r_i\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\) at times \(t_i\), with sampling interval \(\Delta t_i=t_{i+1}-t_i\) [s]. The reported cumulative distance is
\[
D=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\|r_{i+1}-r_i\right\|_{2}\quad [\mathrm{m}],
\]
where \(N\) is the number of fixes. If \(r_i=\gamma(t_i)\) exactly, then \(D\le L\) for a curved path, with equality only in the zero-curvature or zero-step limits. The paper describes this as treating the run “as a collection of straight-line segments rather than a continuous activity” [PAGE 3].

With position error \(\varepsilon_i\), write \(r_i=\gamma(t_i)+\varepsilon_i\). Then
\[
D=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left\|\Delta\gamma_i+\Delta\varepsilon_i\right\|_{2},
\quad \Delta\gamma_i=\gamma(t_{i+1})-\gamma(t_i),
\quad \Delta\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_{i+1}-\varepsilon_i.
\]
The expected measurement bias is
\[
B_m=\mathbb{E}[D\mid m]-L \quad [\mathrm{m}],
\]
and precision is
\[
\sigma_m=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(D\mid m)} \quad [\mathrm{m}].
\]
A compact accuracy functional is
\[
\operatorname{RMSE}_m=\sqrt{B_m^{2}+\sigma_m^{2}}\quad [\mathrm{m}],
\]
which matches the paper’s ISO 5725-style distinction between trueness and precision: “trueness is indicated by how close the average (mean) of the distance measurements reported by a device is to the actual distance covered” and “a small standard deviation of the distance values indicates high precision” [PAGE 5].

For a Gaussian mean model \(D_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_D,\sigma_D^{2})\), the Fisher information about \(\mu_D\) from \(n\) observations is
\[
I(\mu_D)=\frac{n}{\sigma_D^{2}}\quad [\mathrm{m}^{-2}],
\qquad
\operatorname{SE}(\hat{\mu}_D)=\frac{\sigma_D}{\sqrt{n}}\quad [\mathrm{m}].
\]
Using the paper’s reported variances, approximate standard errors are \(4.01\ \mathrm{m}\) for the 205, \(1.96\ \mathrm{m}\) for the 220, and \(3.86\ \mathrm{m}\) for the 45S. The corresponding Fisher informations are approximately \(0.062\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}\), \(0.261\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}\), and \(0.067\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}\). These are not “toy ratios”; they are inverse-variance information quantities in SI units.

The watch-versus-watch tests use Welch’s unequal-variance statistic:
\[
t=\frac{\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B}}
{\sqrt{s_{A}^{2}/n_{A}+s_{B}^{2}/n_{B}}},
\]
with Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom
\[
\nu=
\frac{\left(s_{A}^{2}/n_{A}+s_{B}^{2}/n_{B}\right)^{2}}
{\frac{\left(s_{A}^{2}/n_{A}\right)^{2}}{n_{A}-1}
+\frac{\left(s_{B}^{2}/n_{B}\right)^{2}}{n_{B}-1}}.
\]
The \(t\)-statistic is dimensionless; \(p\)-values are dimensionless probabilities. The source reports:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{205 vs 220: } t=0.3798947,\quad p_{\text{one-tail}}=0.3535546,\\
&\text{205 vs 45S: } t=-12.85147,\quad p_{\text{one-tail}}=9.107\times10^{-18},\\
&\text{220 vs 45S: } t=-16.92756,\quad p_{\text{one-tail}}=1.440\times10^{-29},
\end{aligned}
\]
from [PAGE 6]. As surprisal information, those two small \(p\)-values correspond roughly to \(56.6\ \mathrm{bits}\) and \(95.8\ \mathrm{bits}\), respectively.

One directionality audit: in Table 3 the reported one-tailed \(p=0.3535546\) corresponds to the positive \(205-220\) contrast; for H1 as written (“Forerunner 220 distance > Forerunner 205 distance”), the directional complement would be about \(0.6464\). The conclusion “H1 is not supported” [PAGE 6] is unchanged, but the sign convention is a gray-zone detail.

Against the GIS nominal \(L_{\mathrm{GIS}}=5685\ \mathrm{m}\), the one-sample tests give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{205: } t=-4.1126919,\quad p_{\text{two-tail}}=0.0007265,\\
&\text{220: } t=-9.2906966,\quad p_{\text{two-tail}}=6.831\times10^{-14},\\
&\text{45S: } t=14.2669884,\quad p_{\text{two-tail}}=1.262\times10^{-20},
\end{aligned}
\]
from [PAGE 7]. The mean deviations are approximately \(-16.5\ \mathrm{m}\) (\(-0.29\%\)), \(-18.2\ \mathrm{m}\) (\(-0.32\%\)), and \(+55.0\ \mathrm{m}\) (\(+0.97\%\)) from GIS. The 45S standard deviation is 29.627 m, and the paper states that it “was 74% greater than that of the Forerunner 205 and 78% greater vs. the Forerunner 220” [PAGE 8].

Breakdown and singularity regimes:
1. As \(\Delta t\to0\) with independent per-fix noise, the chord term vanishes but the noise term does not. If \(\|\Delta\gamma_i\|\ll\|\Delta\varepsilon_i\|\), the norm \(\|\Delta\gamma_i+\Delta\varepsilon_i\|_2\) is dominated by noise, and without filtering \(D\) can grow like \(O(N\sigma_\varepsilon)\), not like \(L\). This is the fast-sampling noise-floor breakdown. Real watches use filtering and correlated fixes, which bound the divergence.
2. At a near-stationary fix, \(\|\Delta\gamma_i\|\to0\), the Euclidean norm is non-differentiable at zero. Tiny position noise then produces a positive length contribution with no corresponding true displacement. This is the cusp singularity of chord-length accumulation.
3. The GIS baseline is itself an estimator. If \(|L_{\mathrm{GIS}}-L_{\mathrm{true}}|\) is comparable to \(|B_m|\), absolute trueness tests break. The three GIS values span about 10 m, and Gmap-pedometer is cited as “accurate to within 0.1 percent” [PAGE 4], i.e. about 5.7 m at 5685 m. The older watches’ deviations are roughly three times that, but still within the same order as plausible GIS and route-plotting error.
4. The t-tests assume independent observations within each watch. Repeated runs on one route over years are not fully independent: satellite geometry, weather, firmware, and the runner’s habits are serially correlated. If the effective sample size is smaller than \(n\), the reported \(p\)-values are optimistic even if the means are correct.

There is the sampling clock: \(\Delta t\) controls chord error and battery tradeoff. There is the generational clock: 2006, 2013, and 2019 devices are worn in different calendar blocks. The recursive model is therefore: the runner believes the route is constant; the watch designer believes battery life and size are salient to buyers; the analyst believes the GIS trace is close enough to truth; and the statistician believes the runs are independent enough for t-tests. Each belief can be locally reasonable while jointly producing an overconfident causal story. The counter-trap is to replace one overconfidence with another—either “newer is worse” or “old is gold”—without a simultaneous-device, calibrated-route, firmware-controlled test.

The 45S’s \(+55\) m GIS bias and 29.6 m standard deviation are larger than the 16 m path-deviation estimate, so a pure “runner wandered” explanation is insufficient for the 45S. But the physical layer still vetoes a clean hardware-only attribution because satellite geometry and multipath were not logged per run.

Biomechanical layer: The runner’s path deviations are estimated as “almost always less than 0.01 mile (52.8 feet or approximately 16 m)” [PAGE 6]. That is a real uncertainty floor. It cannot explain the 45S’s roughly 55 m overread against GIS, but it is close to the older watches’ roughly 16.5–18.2 m short readings. Therefore the biomechanical layer vetoes strong absolute claims that the 205 and 220 “measure short” in a metrological sense; their deviations are near the author’s own path-variability envelope. The relative 45S-versus-older difference of about 71–73 m is less vulnerable, but still depends on the GIS baseline.

Weather layer: The source provides no weather covariates, no temperature, humidity, ionospheric state, or precipitation records. Because watch eras are calendar blocks, weather is confounded with model. The weather layer therefore vetoes the leap from “45S is worse in this dataset” to “Garmin changed design priorities.” A controlled study would need simultaneous wear of all three models on the same runs, with the same route, same weather, same satellite geometry, and randomized order.

Veto result: The physical and weather layers do not erase the statistical model difference, but they veto the causal design-tradeoff conclusion as proven. The biomechanical layer additionally weakens the older watches’ absolute short-reading claim. The surviving claim is narrower: under this runner, this route, and this GIS baseline, the 45S produced a larger mean and larger dispersion than the 205 and 220, with very small reported p-values. That is a valid relative accuracy finding, not a proven manufacturer-intent finding.
