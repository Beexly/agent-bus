$$\theta_{l+1} = \theta_l - \alpha\,\nabla E(\theta_l) + \mu(\theta_l - \theta_{l-1})$$
Units: θ dimensionless; α learning rate (α > 0); μ momentum coefficient; l iteration index. Breakdown: divergence/oscillation for α above the local curvature stability bound; μ → 1 risks overshooting. Reported values: α = 0.01 (new layers), α = 0.00005 (retained) [PAGE 4]; note conflict with "0.0005" [PAGE 9].

Intersection over Union / Jaccard (Eq. 7, [PAGE 5]), verbatim:
$$\text{IOU} = \frac{|B_{BA} \cap B_{BB}|}{|B_{BA} \cup B_{BB}|} = \frac{|B_{BA} \cap B_{BB}|}{|B_{BA}| + |B_{BB}| - |B_{BA} \cap B_{BB}|}$$
Units: dimensionless, range [0,1]; areas |·| in pixels². Singularity: denominator = 0 iff |B_BA| = |B_BB| = 0 (two zero-area boxes) → 0/0 undefined; one empty box gives IOU = 0.

</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper positions itself as the terminus of a lineage it narrates explicitly. "Research work on tracking of ball in sports began in late 90′s" and prioritized 3D position (refs [3–5]) over detection [PAGE 1]. The operator genealogy is recoverable from that page: Kalman Filter (refs [8,9]) — optimal only under linear-Gaussian dynamics; the paper states "unsuitability of KF for tracking non-linearly and dynamically moving objects" [PAGE 1] as the causal driver of its decline. That failure motivates Particle Filters (refs [12–15]), which "handle non-linearity" but whose "track of ball was lost due to slow updation of number of particles" [PAGE 1]. Template Matching (refs [16–18]) followed, effective on "merged and missing ball" yet generating "false positives" from "ball-like objects in the background" [PAGE 1]. Offline Data Association and graph-based methods (refs [21–26]) handle occlusion "for a very small duration" only [PAGE 2]. Energy-function optimization (refs [27–29]) modeled ball–player interaction but "processing time of this approach increases for bigger fields like soccer" [PAGE 2] — a direct complexity objection this work answers with the IOU cascade.

The immediate ancestors are Reno et al.'s CNN for tennis (ref [31]) and Takahashi et al.'s supervised framework (ref [30]); the authors explicitly differentiate on task hardness: "Ball tracking in team sports is much more complex" [PAGE 2]. The representational substrate descends from LeCun–Bengio–Hinton (ref [32]), backpropagation from Rumelhart (ref [34]), and the backbone from Chatfield et al.'s VGG-M (ref [46]). IOU itself is repurposed from Jaccard's 1901 ecological similarity coefficient (ref [51]) — an operator migrated from set-similarity measurement to a tracking objective, which the paper flags as non-standard: "IOU has so far been used on randomly selected image patches [47] for tracking. But mainly it is a performance measure" [PAGE 5].

Multi-week traps for anyone building on this: (1) the 0.00005-vs-0.0005 retained-learning-rate discrepancy [PAGE 4] vs [PAGE 9]; (2) the CL2 input ambiguity (25×25 pooled vs 51×51 raw) that changes the terminal conv map from 3×3×512 to 10×10×512 and hence the flattened FC input size; (3) the assumption that the pre-trained-on-prior-frames claim ("not a single frame from the current recording is used for training," [PAGE 9]) survives the dataset being cropped "from online available and our own soccer videos" [PAGE 4] that may share broadcast provenance with ISSIA — a leakage risk the genealogy does not exclude.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The tracker maintains a one-level model of an adversarial occluder. When ball blobs vanish, "the control is passed to the player-tracking module assuming that the ball is occluded" [PAGE 6]; the system then binds a box on "all the near-by players suspicious of occluding the ball" and "assigns ball position to a player only if he occupies or occludes the ball" [PAGE 2]. That is a proxy belief: the ball's state is imputed to whichever player most plausibly hides it, and "During occlusion, status flag (SFi) is set for players" [PAGE 7]. The recursive step is one level deep and explicitly bounded — "it is difficult to identify which player(s) is(are) possessing the ball" [PAGE 6] — so the model does not recurse over competing player intents; it defers to grids.

The clock is encoded as frame-window counters, not wall time: M consecutive frames for candidate validation ("a ball candidate blob ... in M consecutive frames ... may be a true ball," [PAGE 5]), PAT1 frames of player-only presence before FGRID activation ("If only a player(s) is detected for PAT1 previous frames ... FGRID is activated," [PAGE 7]), and PAT2 frames of sustained true-ball presence before FGRID deactivation and BGRID activation ("until true ball is found in PAT2 frames," [PAGE 7]). These windows are the temporal memory horizon; their numeric values are not reported in the supplied text, which is an operational gray zone.

Counter-trap behavior (crisis regression): when occlusion outlasts the model's patience, the policy degrades to exhaustive search — "If neither ball nor player positions are found ... the control passes to the grid module" [PAGE 7]. FGRID spreads over "full frame" and rejects stationary hits ("If it is stationary, it is not a true ball position," [PAGE 7]); BGRID then patrols boundaries under the "ball out of frame" hypothesis [PAGE 7]. This is a graceful-fallback automaton, not a learned belief revision, so under genuinely multi-player scrums the theory-of-mind layer is silent and only the geometric grid operates.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three physical/operational vetoes constrain the system, each traceable to the text.

Veto 1 — camera stationarity. "This background modelling technique is applicable only to fixed camera videos" [PAGE 4]. The 2-stage median model over SIZE1×SIZE2 = 18×18 frames [PAGE 9] assumes a static scene; any pan/zoom/tilt reclassifies the whole frame as foreground, saturating the blob stage. ISSIA's "six stationary cameras" [PAGE 9] satisfy this; broadcast feeds do not. This is a hard veto on the moving-camera regime.

Veto 2 — kinematic coverage of IOU. Overlap survives only if SWELLBY (20 px [PAGE 9]) ≥ projected inter-frame displacement. With v_max = 129 km/h = 35.83 m/s [PAGE 6] and capture at 25 FPS [PAGE 9], Δx = 1.43 m/frame (or 3.58 m at the 10 FPS processing rate). Whether 20 px ≥ 1.43 m is entirely a function of the field-location pixels-per-meter scale, which the paper leaves unquantified — so on long fast balls near the far touchline the veto is plausibly triggered and tracking falls back to FGRID/BGRID. The elevated PE on filmrole1 (128.10) and filmrole2 (70.68) [PAGE 10] is consistent with this failure mode.

Veto 3 — photometric collision. "When the ball blob and a blob of a player wearing similar color uniform, with similar sizes, touch each other, the algorithm fails momentarily" [PAGE 9]. Under low light or shadow ("shadowing" listed as a challenge [PAGE 1]) the bi-threshold at 0.07 [PAGE 5] on the three channels OR-merges ball and uniform, and the classifier cannot separate them. The masked stands ("we masked the stands region, thereby losing track of the ball when it goes in or flies through the masked region," [PAGE 9]) is a coverage veto by construction.

Adjudication: none of these are violated on ISSIA (fixed cameras, masked regions, mostly on-field play), so the reported numbers stand within-domain; all three are violated in the general broadcast setting, which the paper does not evaluate.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactuals with falsifiable predictions, each grounded in a testable quantity from the paper.

CF-1 (moving camera). Perturbation: replace ISSIA fixed cameras with a panning broadcast feed. Prediction: the 2-stage median background [PAGE 4] yields near-total foreground, blob count explodes, CNN classification cost per frame rises, and effective FPS falls below the reported 10 [PAGE 9]; PE diverges toward the MIL/KCF weighted averages (479.63 / 560.43) or worse. Verifiable by running Version 1/2 on any single panning clip and measuring blob-count and PE.

CF-2 (speed vs inflation). Perturbation: inject shots near 129 km/h and vary SWELLBY. Prediction: for SWELLBY below the projected Δx (1.43 m at 25 FPS), IOU (Eq. 7) between consecutive inflated boxes → 0, forcing "ball track lost" and FGRID activation [PAGE 7], with a measurable PE spike; above the threshold, IOU > 0 and tracking continues. Falsifiable by sweeping SWELLBY and plotting the PE-vs-SWELLBY knee against the kinematic Δx.

CF-3 (color collision). Perturbation: recolor the ball to match a player's uniform and force blob contact. Prediction: momentary failure exactly as stated [PAGE 9], quantified as a bounded PE transient (the paper attributes filmrole2's low accuracy to "ball color matching the player uniform," [PAGE 10]), self-correcting once "the ball blob assumes its actual shape and size and gets isolated" [PAGE 9]. Verifiable on filmrole2 by measuring PE during contact vs post-separation frames.

Supporting anchors for all three: Table 2 average PE 36.71 / IOU 0.52 [PAGE 10]; "30% have IOU greater than 0.7" and "maximum IOU of 0.99" [PAGE 10]; event metrics ball-detected precision 93.25% / recall 73.25% and ball-out-of-frame precision 89.08% / recall 97.38% [PAGE 11].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
