Define the inertial sensing-unit state at time t as
x_s(t) = [p_s(t); v_s(t); q_s(t)] ,
with p_s in R^3 [m], v_s in R^3 [m/s], and q_s in SO(3) [dimensionless attitude]. The SDI mechanization over a prediction horizon T is

v_s(t+T) = v_s(t) + integral_t^(t+T) a_s(tau) dtau ,
p_s(t+T) = p_s(t) + integral_t^(t+T) v_s(tau) dtau ,

where a_s is the free acceleration [m/s^2]. Under a locally constant-acceleration approximation,

p_hat_s(t+T) = p_s(t) + v_s(t) T + (1/2) a_bar_s T^2 .

The positional-unit prediction for sensing unit i uses the lever arm l_ps^a in R^3 [m], denoted v_ps^a in the source [PAGE 8], and the rotation matrix R_Ps(t+T) in SO(3) [dimensionless]:

p_hat_p,i(t+T) = p_hat_s(t+T) + R_Ps(t+T) l_ps^a .

The association hypothesis test is

norm_2( p_p,j^meas(t+T) - p_hat_p,i(t+T) ) <= e ,

where p_p,j^meas is the j-th positional-unit measurement [m] and e in R_{>=0} [m] is the maximum admissible error [PAGE 8]. The quantity e may be a fixed design parameter or a function of the estimated statistical uncertainties of the positional-unit measurement and the inertial dead-reckoning prediction [PAGE 8].

Page citations: SDI compatibility, bandwidth reduction, lossy-link tolerance, and the four-stage tracking process appear on [PAGE 5]. The lever-arm definition, rotation matrix, dead-reckoning mechanization with v(t)_s and a(t)_s, unlabeled-marker association, norm-based hypothesis test, and threshold e appear on [PAGE 8].

Breakdown and singularity regimes: As T grows, SDI position error scales approximately with T^2 under acceleration bias, so p_hat_s diverges and the association test fails. If e is smaller than the combined measurement and prediction standard deviation, true associations are rejected; if e exceeds the minimum inter-marker distance, false associations are accepted. Optical occlusion renders p_p,j^meas undefined, removing aiding entirely. A time-varying lever arm caused by soft-tissue artifact violates the constant-l_ps^a assumption and biases every prediction. Rotation-matrix error or gyro heading drift misaligns R_Ps, producing frame misalignment even when positions are locally correct.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage traces from strap-down inertial navigation developed at MIT Draper Laboratory and used in Apollo guidance, through the Kalman filter (1960) and classic inertial-aided optical tracking of the 1980s and 1990s. Dead reckoning itself is centuries old, but its MEMS-based implementation for body segments is modern. Optical motion capture (e.g., Vicon-style marker systems) established the expensive, line-of-sight benchmark that the patent explicitly seeks to undercut in cost and complexity [PAGE 5]. The listed alternative aiding modalities—acoustic, ultra-wideband radio, GPS/GNSS, WiFi, magnetic field map, cellular, computer vision—place the invention within the broader multi-sensor fusion tradition of vision-inertial odometry and radio-frequency positioning.

Multi-week traps emerge from the assumptions embedded in [PAGE 5] and [PAGE 8]. MEMS gyro bias instability and thermal drift cause slow heading divergence; because the patent claims heading alignment without magnetometers when optical aiding is present [PAGE 8], any extended optical outage removes the only heading reference and lets uncorrected drift accumulate over days or weeks. Lever-arm calibration degrades through strap slippage, sensor-mount creep, or soft-tissue compression, invalidating the constant l_ps^a required for association [PAGE 8]. Battery aging raises sensor noise density, increasing SDI integration error and shrinking the valid short-interval window. Wireless congestion increases packet loss; the SDI robustness to lossy transmission [PAGE 5] degrades if gaps exceed the interval over which dead reckoning remains accurate. Optical marker reflectivity fades and camera auto-exposure drifts, threatening the requirement that a clear view of at least one marker with sufficient cameras be preserved at each time [PAGE 8]. In longitudinal studies, intermittent aiding therefore risks a slow reversion to pure dead-reckoning drift rather than graceful degradation.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression occurs when optical aiding is lost through occlusion or system failure. The operator or software stack regresses to pure dead reckoning, a mode the patent identifies as drift-prone [PAGE 5]. Because the user has no external position reference during the outage, confidence in the output decays as inertial integration error accumulates, leading to repeated manual interventions such as recalibration, marker re-illumination, or system restart. This is not a designed fallback but a recurring crisis behavior.

The counter-trap is association lock-in during ambiguous frames. When multiple unlabeled markers on adjacent segments pass close together, the hypothesis test may accept a wrong pairing because the residual falls below e [PAGE 8]. Once a false association is accepted at time t, the dead-reckoning prediction at t+T uses the wrong marker as pseudo-ground truth, reinforcing the incorrect ID mapping. This confirmation-bias loop means the system can “remember” the wrong association and propagate it until a residual outlier test or nearest-neighbor check forces re-association.

The clock constraint binds the entire loop. The association must be re-solved every frame within a short interval T [PAGE 8]. Processing latency, clock skew between the inertial monitor and the positioning system, and the prediction horizon T form a hard real-time chain. If latency exceeds T, the predicted position p_hat_s(t+T) is compared against stale measurements, invalidating the norm test and producing either false rejections or false acceptances. The system therefore depends on a bounded compute budget and tightly synchronized timestamps; without them, the recursive theory of mind collapses into open-loop dead reckoning.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto: MEMS accelerometer noise density above roughly 100 micro-g per square-root-hertz or gyro bias instability above roughly 10 degrees per hour causes SDI position error to exceed the association threshold e within seconds [PAGE 5, PAGE 8]. When this bound is crossed, the veto is triggered: fusion output is frozen at the last aided pose and no new association is attempted until sensor noise falls back below specification.

Biomechanical-layer veto: The lever arm l_ps^a is assumed constant and rigidly fixed between the positional unit and the inertial sensing unit [PAGE 8]. Soft-tissue artifact, skin movement, or strap loosening makes the optical marker or other positional unit move relative to the inertial sensor, violating this assumption. Stage 305 joint-center equalization [PAGE 5] corrects inter-segment kinematic drift but cannot repair intra-unit non-rigidity. If the estimated residual between marker and predicted sensor position exceeds a biomechanical tolerance on the order of 10 to 20 millimeters, the veto invalidates the frame and requests a recalibration pose.

Weather and environmental veto: Optical markers require preserved line of sight with sufficient cameras [PAGE 8]. Rain, fog, smoke, or direct sunlight saturates or obscures cameras and breaks visibility. Radio-based aiding suffers multipath in indoor or urban canyon environments; GPS and GNSS are denied indoors; acoustic aiding is perturbed by temperature- and humidity-dependent sound speed; magnetic field maps are distorted by ferrous structures. Any of these conditions removes external position aiding, forcing the system into unaided SDI dead reckoning [PAGE 5] and triggering a veto until the environmental disturbance clears or aiding is restored.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Total optical occlusion counterfactual. If all positional units are occluded for T_occ exceeding 2 seconds while the subject undergoes peak acceleration of 2 meters per second squared, and accelerometer noise density is 0.05 meters per second squared, the unaided SDI position error bound grows as approximately one-half sigma_a T_occ squared. At T_occ equals 2 seconds the error is approximately 0.1 meter; at approximately 4.2 seconds it reaches approximately 0.45 meter. Prediction: the veto triggers at T_occ equals 2 seconds; output is frozen at the last aided pose; re-acquisition requires a fresh association search because heading drift during occlusion invalidates the previous inertial-to-positional ID mapping.

Scenario 2: Marker-swap counterfactual. If two unlabeled markers on adjacent segments pass within a separation d_min less than e, and the association test accepts the swapped pairing because the inter-marker distance d_swap is 150 millimeters while e is 200 millimeters, the test passes erroneously. Prediction: nearest-neighbor or residual outlier rejection flags a post-fit residual exceeding 3 sigma; the veto invalidates the frame and freezes association until the markers separate by more than e.

Scenario 3: Lever-arm drift counterfactual. If the lever arm l_ps^a is miscalibrated by delta ell of 30 millimeters while e is 20 millimeters, every association test carries a systematic offset whose norm exceeds e. Prediction: the hypothesis test rejects essentially all true associations, producing a miss rate approaching 100 percent; the veto triggers an automatic recalibration routine requiring a static calibration pose with known marker positions to re-estimate l_ps^a before tracking resumes.
