*Hop 3 — transition primitive.* Events are differences of the frame label, not the label itself. $$D(f)=\mathbb{1}\Big[\exists\,i\in\mathcal{T}_A,\ j\in\mathcal{T}_B:\ \|\mathbf{x}_{b}(f)-\mathbf{x}_i(f)\|_2\le R_{dz}\ \wedge\ \|\mathbf{x}_{b}(f)-\mathbf{x}_j(f)\|_2\le R_{dz}\Big],\quad R_{dz}\ge R_{pz}$$

**O3. Control-frame classifier.** Let $i^\*(f)=\arg\min_i\|\mathbf{x}_b(f)-\mathbf{x}_i(f)\|_2$.
$$c(f)=\begin{cases}\text{duel} & D(f)=1\\ \text{possession}(i^\*) & D(f)=0\ \wedge\ \|\mathbf{x}_b(f)-\mathbf{x}_{i^\*}(f)\|_2\le R_{pz}\\ \text{dead ball} & \text{tracking flag dead}\\ \text{no possession} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
Precedence of duel over possession is explicit: "If both possession and duel conditions are triggered, the duel condition prevails" [PAGE 4].

**O4. Possession loss.** With $f_c=\min\{f'>f:\ c(f')\in\{\text{possession},\text{duel}\}\}$ and $\Delta s_f=\|\mathbf{x}_b(f+1)-\mathbf{x}_b(f)\|_2$ (m):
$$L_A(f)=\mathbb{1}\Big[\|\mathbf{x}_b(f+1)-\mathbf{x}_A(f+1)\|_2>R_{pz}\ \wedge\ \Delta s_f>\epsilon_s\ \wedge\ A\notin c(f_c)\Big],\quad[\epsilon_s]=\mathrm{m}$$
The two conjuncts are engineered to kill two specific false positives: static ball + moving player (no loss), and ball leaving then re-entering the same player's PZ with no intervening control (no loss) [PAGE 5, Fig. 3b1–b2].

K(f)&=\mathbb{1}\big[\forall i:\ y_i(f)\in\text{own half}\pm\epsilon_{k1}\ \wedge\ \exists i:\|\mathbf{x}_i(f)-\mathbf{x}_{\text{centre}}\|_2\le\epsilon_{k2}\big]\\
PK(f)&=\mathbb{1}\big[\,|\{i\in\mathrm{GK}:\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathcal{B}_{\text{goalline},\epsilon_{p1}}\}|=1\ \wedge\ |\{j\in\text{opp}:\mathbf{x}_j\in\mathcal{B}_{\text{spot},\epsilon_{p2}}\}|=1\ \wedge\ \forall k:\ \mathbf{x}_k\notin\text{PA}\ \wedge\ \|\mathbf{x}_k-\mathbf{x}_{\text{spot}}\|_2>9.15-\epsilon_{p3}\big]\\
GK(f)&=\mathbb{1}\big[\exists i:\mathbf{x}_i(f)\in\text{own goal area}\pm\epsilon_g\big]\\
CK(f)&=\mathbb{1}\big[\exists i:\|\mathbf{x}_i(f)-\mathbf{x}_{\text{corner}}\|_2\le\epsilon_c\big]\\
TI(f)&=\mathbb{1}\big[\exists i:\ x_i(f)>x_{\text{sideline}}-\epsilon_t\big]
\end{aligned}$$
All tolerances in metres. The $9.15\ \mathrm{m}$ constant is the IFAB Law 14 minimum distance, not a fitted parameter.

**O9. Evaluation operators.** Precision and recall per class $k$:
$$P_k=\frac{TP_k}{TP_k+FP_k},\qquad R_k=\frac{TP_k}{TP_k+FN_k}$$
dimensionless $\in[0,1]$. Reported: pass precision/recall "supra 90%" [PAGE 9]; shot "precision was higher than the recall (78 vs 53)" [PAGE 9]. As a stricter extension not in the source, frame-level control-state mutual information:
$$I(C;\hat C)=\sum_{c,\hat c}p(c,\hat c)\log_2\frac{p(c,\hat c)}{p(c)p(\hat c)}\ \ [\mathrm{bits}]$$
which penalizes systematic label collapse that accuracy alone hides.

**Singularity / breakdown regimes.**
1. $R_{pz}\to 0$: $\mathcal P_i(f)=\varnothing$ almost surely; control frames vanish; the entire downstream tree has empty support. **Total pipeline collapse.**
2. $R_{pz}\gtrsim \tfrac12$ mean nearest-neighbour spacing: simultaneous multi-player PZ membership; $i^\*$ becomes noise-dominated; false gains proliferate.
3. $R_{dz}<R_{pz}$: violates the stated ordering [PAGE 4]; duel can never pre-empt, and the possession label absorbs contested frames.
4. $\epsilon_s\to0$: $\Delta s_f$ from tracker jitter exceeds threshold on static balls → spurious losses on dead-ball frames.
5. $\epsilon_\theta\to1$ and $\epsilon_v\to0$: logical OR makes the gain test fire on noise; every pass transiting near a defender becomes a "gain."
6. $\epsilon_\theta\to-1$ and $\epsilon_v\to\infty$: gain test never fires; possession never transfers; event log degenerates to one infinite possession.
7. Ball tracking absent: O1–O5 undefined; "The main limitation of the algorithm is that ball tracking data needs to be available" [PAGE 12]. **Hard veto.**
8. In-play/dead-ball flag absent (Track160, Hawk-Eye infer it from missing ball data [PAGE 3]): the boundary frame $d_c$ is ambiguous, so DBE–SPE pairing is off-by-one.
9. $z$ absent: O7 loses the crossbar discriminator → the observed above-crossbar false goal [PAGE 10].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
**Lineage, traced backwards.**

*Layer 1 — manual symbolic era.* "Event data for major football leagues and tournaments started to be collected by Opta Sports (now Statsperform) [2] at the end of the 20th century, and its widespread adoption has propelled the development of advanced football statistics for analytics, broadcast and sports-betting [3–15]" [PAGE 2]. (Recorded for provenance only; no line, price, or market construct appears in or is implied by this analysis.) The manual layer's five structural defects are enumerated verbatim: terminology inconsistency, subjective tagging with "substantial tagging differences (10–15%) among analysts," post-processing latency such that "the final dataset for some providers may not typically available until several hours after the match," single-player context only, and resource intensity [PAGE 2]. Every one of these is a retrocausal driver of the present paper.

*Layer 2 — broadcast-vision era.* "there have been many efforts in the field of computer vision to automatically detect events using broadcast video [19–24]" [PAGE 2], citing Ekin/Tekalp/Mehrotra (2003), D'Orazio & Leo (2010), Assfalg et al. (2003), Tavassolipour et al. (2013), Kapela et al. (2014), Ballan et al. (2009). Limitation stated: sparse logs, "devoted mainly to highlight generation" [PAGE 2].

*Layer 3 — deep action-recognition era.* "convolutional and recurrent neural networks have been employed for this task [25–31]" [PAGE 2] — Soccernet (Giancola 2018), Baccouche LSTM (2010), Jiang CNN+RNN (2016), Soccernet-v2 (2021), Rongved 3D-CNN (2020).

*Layer 4 — tracking-metric era.* "tracking data has been extensively used to perform football analytics … the quantification of team tactics, pitch control or expected possession value" [PAGE 2], with Spearman (2018), Fernández/Bornn/Cervone (2019), Decroos et al. VAEP (2019), Power et al. (2017), Link et al. (2016).

*Layer 5 — the synthesis.* This paper is the retrocausal closure: it uses Layer 4's substrate (EPTS tracking, calibrated by FIFA's EPTS programme [PAGE 1]) to regenerate Layer 1's product (the event log), skipping Layers 2–3 entirely. The claim of novelty is explicit: "To the best of our knowledge, this article represents the first attempt to use tracking data as a means to automatically generate event data" [PAGE 2].

*Deep roots.* The Savitzky–Golay smoother (1964) [55] is a 58-year-old numerical-analysis primitive doing load-bearing work inside a 2022 sports-AI system — a genuine retrocausal dependency. IFAB Laws of the Game [54] supply the entire rule ontology: Laws 8 (kickoff), 14 (penalty), 15 (throw-in), 16 (goal kick), 17 (corner).

**Multi-week traps (latent failure modes with long incubation).**
- *Provider-version drift.* Tracab data appear as "three of them processed with version 5.0 and the remaining four with provider A version 4.0" [PAGE 3]; a version bump silently shifts noise statistics and hence the optimal $\epsilon$'s. A pipeline tuned on v4 can degrade for weeks before anyone notices.
- *Stadium-dependence.* "the data quality depends on the stadium, namely the height at which the cameras are installed" [PAGE 2] — cross-venue transfer is not a one-time calibration but a per-venue recalibration burden.
- *Hyperparameter overfitting.* The authors explicitly recommend "a cross-validation strategy on a subset of matches to optimize the hyperparameter selection for each tracking data provider" [PAGE 13] — i.e., the parameters are provider-specific, so any portability claim is conditional.
- *Benchmark contamination.* Because STS manual labels themselves contain "events not annotated, events attributed to a wrong player, or annotated event times more than 10 s before/after they occurred" [PAGE 13], a team that "improves" against this benchmark may be fitting human noise. This is a slow, invisible trap.
- *Data-rights fragility.* "Due to media and data rights, the datasets are not publicly available" [PAGE 13] — reproducibility is gated on a bilateral request, so independent replication can lag by months.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Three-agent recursion: algorithm, referee, analyst.**

*The algorithm's theory of the referee.* The pipeline encodes an IFAB-shaped prior: spatial configurations imply officiating decisions. But the referee's decision is unobserved, so the algorithm must *infer intent from geometry*. This is a theory-of-mind substitution — a physical proxy for a mental state. Where the proxy is absent, the algorithm defaults. "since free kicks lack distinct trigger configurations, we may only define a free kick pattern as a player having the ball within their possession zone on the first in-play frame" [PAGE 7]. And: "If none of the patterns are satisfied, the algorithm will assume a free kick as a default option" [PAGE 7].

*Crisis habit regression.* Under tracking degradation the algorithm does not fail loudly — it *reverts to its prior*. The `free kick?–free kick?` pair exists precisely to flag this: "an additional pair foul?–free kick? is introduced to account for instances where the algorithm is confused due to inaccuracies in the tracking data" [PAGE 4]. This is habit regression: the model's default class absorbs ambiguity, which raises apparent coverage while silently concentrating error in one bucket. The published consequence is visible in the set-piece confusion matrix — free kicks are "the worse results" [PAGE 9].

*Counter-trap (the defender's mind).* Possession gains are inferred from the defender's *effect on the ball*, not from the defender. The dot-product/speed test is a counterfactual: "had the defender not touched it, the ball would have continued." That is a second-order mental model implemented as a physical residual. It works for clean contacts and fails exactly where the counterfactual is unidentifiable — deflections off a third body: "saves that occur after a ball deflection from a defender … the algorithm will label them as a pass from the defender followed by a reception from the goalkeeper" [PAGE 8].

*The analyst's mind.* Ground truth is itself a mental product with 10–15% inter-rater drift [PAGE 2]. The recursive structure closes here: algorithm predicts analyst, analyst predicts referee, referee predicts player. Three nested theory-of-mind layers, each with its own error term, and the benchmark measures only the outer composition.

*Clock discipline.* Two temporal constants anchor the recursion. Frame clock: 25 Hz, $\Delta t=0.04$ s, SG window $=0.25$ s [PAGE 5]. Decision clock: "for the save/claim events we investigate if the goalkeeper loses possession within one second of the saving event, to distinguish between retention and deflection" [PAGE 8] — a 1 s retention horizon that separates claim from parry. A third, softer clock: kickoff-repetition detection requires checking whether the ball reached a penalty area "in the time interval between kickoff k −1 and kickoff k" [PAGE 7]. Three clocks, three granularities: 40 ms, 1 s, and phase-length.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Veto proof at the physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers.**

*Physical layer.* The observable is a projection: 2D centroid coordinates at 25 Hz. Veto 1: **no ball track, no pipeline.** "The main limitation of the algorithm is that ball tracking data needs to be available, since we propose to detect events by assessing the change in distance between players and the ball" [PAGE 12]. Veto 2: **no in-play/dead-ball channel, no set-piece layer.** "The other limitation is the absence of in-play/dead information, which is critical for set piece detection" [PAGE 12]. Veto 3: **occlusion.** Dense player clusters corrupt both $i^\*$ and the gain test; empirically shots are hit harder than passes (15% vs <6% loss) precisely "since shots tended to take place on areas where players accumulate" [PAGE 9]. Veto 4: **2D → 3D information deficit.** Everything above the crossbar is invisible; the false last-minute goal is the canonical instance [PAGE 10].

*Biomechanical layer.* The tracking is center-of-mass only. Hence a hard boundary on the event ontology: "Additional data streams, such as the z-coordinate of the ball, player limb tracking or video, may be leveraged to expand the automatically detectable events, for instance tackles, air/ground duels or dribbles" [PAGE 4]. Tackles and dribbles are *biomechanically defined* (limb–ball and limb–limb interaction) and are therefore vetoed by a COM-only sensor. The one biomechanical surrogate the pipeline does use is the goalkeeper retention horizon (1 s) to split claim from deflection [PAGE 8] — a behavioral, not anatomical, discriminator. Improvement path (5) names exactly this: "the incorporation of limb tracking data in addition to center-of-mass tracking data for all players and referees" [PAGE 13].

*Environmental layer.* Three environmental couplings are unmodeled but not neutral. (a) *Camera geometry*: "the data quality depends on the stadium, namely the height at which the cameras are installed" [PAGE 2] — a stadium is an optical environment. (b) *Source degradation*: broadcast/tactical tracking is "less accurate than in-stadium tracking and the pitch is not always visible, which will thus require adjusting the algorithm's hyperparameters and dead ball patterns" [PAGE 13] — the pitch-visibility constraint is an environmental veto on the trigger predicates O8, all of which assume full-pitch visibility. (c) *Audio environment*: referee whistles are proposed as an independent dead-ball channel — "leveraging a synchronized audio feed that provides timestamped referee whistles to more accurately establish in-play/dead ball intervals" [PAGE 13] — which is an admission that the geometric dead-ball proxy is environmentally brittle.

*Veto lattice.* The vetoes compose as a conjunction, not a disjunction: the pipeline runs iff (ball track) ∧ (play-state channel or inferable proxy) ∧ (full-pitch visibility) ∧ (no severe occlusion). Failure of any one collapses the dependent sub-tree, but not the whole: ball-track failure kills O1–O5; play-state failure kills only O8; z-failure degrades only O7. This partial-order structure is why the reported "+90%" aggregates hide heterogeneous availability.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is stated as (intervention, verifiable reward signal, prediction, falsification threshold). All are executable on the described corpora without new data collection.

**V1 — $z$-coordinate augmentation veto.**
*Intervention:* concatenate the ball's $z$ channel to the existing 2D feed and extend O7 with the crossbar test.
*Verifier:* (a) count of `goal?`-labelled sequences that resolve to true goals vs above-crossbar misses; (b) throw-in/corner/free-kick confusion counts in the set-piece matrix.
*Prediction:* the single observed false last-minute goal is eliminated ("a shot that went above the crossbar, which could be corrected with the ball z-coordinate" [PAGE 10]); the throw-in-vs-corner and free-kick-near-sideline confusions named in [PAGE 8] shrink materially.
*Falsification:* if `goal?` false-positive rate does not strictly decrease, or if set-piece confusion is unchanged, the z-hypothesis is vetoed.
*Boundary:* $z$ does **not** fix blocked-shot misclassification — "70% of the 254 misclassifications" [PAGE 9] arise from the shot *definition*, not from trajectory height. Prediction: blocked-shot error rate remains statistically indistinguishable from baseline.

**V2 — provider-stratified hyperparameter cross-validation veto.**
*Intervention:* implement the authors' own recommendation — "a cross-validation strategy on a subset of matches to optimize the hyperparameter selection for each tracking data provider" [PAGE 13] — sweeping $(R_{pz},\epsilon_s,\epsilon_\theta,\epsilon_v)$ per provider with held-out matches.
*Verifier:* held-out per-class precision/recall; primary metric pass recall and shot precision (baseline 78 precision / 53 recall [PAGE 9]).
*Prediction:* within-provider held-out performance is stable and shot recall improves; cross-provider transfer of a single global parameter set degrades, confirming that the published $R_{pz}$ split (50 cm provider A vs 1 m providers B/C [PAGE 9]) is a genuine physical requirement rather than a tuning artifact.
*Falsification:* if a single global $(\cdot)$ set matches per-provider tuned performance within noise, the provider-specificity claim in [PAGE 9] is vetoed and the model is simpler than stated.

**V3 — referee-whistle audio synchronization veto.**
*Intervention:* fuse a timestamped whistle channel to define in-play/dead-ball intervals, replacing the boolean/missing-ball proxy.
*Verifier:* intersection-over-union of algorithmically inferred dead-ball intervals against STS timestamped dead-ball events; plus the rate of `free kick?` outputs, which "signals the algorithm was confused due to tracking data inaccuracies and requires external input" [PAGE 9].
*Prediction:* `free kick?` rate decreases monotonically with whistle-channel reliability; DBE–SPE off-by-one errors at the interval boundary $d_c$ diminish; free-kick precision improves from its position as "the worse results" [PAGE 9].
*Falsification:* if `free kick?` rate is invariant to whistle availability, the dead-ball proxy is not the binding constraint and the audio path is vetoed as non-causal.
*Boundary:* audio cannot disambiguate *which* restart was awarded (corner vs throw-in), only *when* play stopped — the geometric triggers O8 remain load-bearing.

**Cross-scenario invariant.** All three verifiers are computable from the artefacts the paper already produces (confusion matrices, event tables, dead-ball interval logs) and all three predictions are directional, so each is falsifiable by a single held-out tournament — FCWC19, FCWC20, or the 2019–20 Bundesliga split [PAGE 3]. None of them requires any wagering, market, or price construct.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
