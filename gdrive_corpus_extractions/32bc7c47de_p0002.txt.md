1. **Relevance Operator ($R$):** 
   Maps a document $d_i$ and query $q$ to a scalar probability in $[0,1]$.
   $$ R(d_i | q) : D \times Q \rightarrow [0,1] $$

2. **Semantic Similarity / Redundancy Operator ($Sim$):** 
   Measures the overlap between two documents within a high-dimensional vector space derived from the affinity graph topology.
   $$ Sim(d_i, d_j) : D \times D \rightarrow [0,1] $$
   *Assumption:* $Sim(d_i, d_j)$ is symmetric and $Sim(d_i, d_i) = 1$.

3. **Subset Utility Function ($U$):**
   The SDR algorithm seeks to maximize a utility function that balances marginal relevance against cumulative redundancy. Using a submodular optimization approach (a standard technique for such diversity problems):
   $$ U(S) = \sum_{d_i \in S} R(d_i | q) - \lambda \sum_{d_i, d_j \in S, i < j} Sim(d_i, d_j) $$
   Where $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is a tunable hyperparameter controlling the strictness of the diversity penalty. 

4. **Units and Information Metric:**
   The output of $U(S)$ is measured in **effective bits (ebits)**, representing the net reduction in uncertainty regarding the query $q$ after consuming the subset $S$, penalized by the informational overlap (redundancy) between elements.
