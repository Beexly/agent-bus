The paper’s pipeline is a multi-hop set-selection and classification chain. First, a tracklet is represented as \(T=\{F_i:F_i\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}\}_{i=1}^{t}\), where \(H,W\) are pixel dimensions and the final 3 is the RGB channel count [PAGE 3]. The Keyframe Identification module is explicitly a set-subtraction operator: “KfID(T ) = T \{Fn1, Fn2, ..., Fnk} , where Fn1, Fn2, ..., Fnk are noisy frames with diminutive digit features” [PAGE 3]. Thus the first hop is frame-level visibility: JNL localizes candidate digits, RoI and Local Histogram Correlation filter local detections, and Global Histogram Correlation filters across the tracklet. The paper states these as \(\det(T)=\{\det_i\}_{i=1}^{t}=\{\mathrm{JNL}(F_i)\}_{i=1}^{t}\), \(f_{\mathrm{local}}(T)=\{f_{\mathrm{local}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{t}=\{\mathrm{LHC}(\mathrm{RoI}(\det_i))\}_{i=1}^{t}\), and \(f_{\mathrm{global}}(T)=\mathrm{GHC}(f_{\mathrm{local}}(T))\) [PAGE 3]. The second hop is sequence extraction: a fixed sequence length is sampled, with the constraint that “any 2 frames sampled are at least d frames apart from each other” [PAGE 5]. The third hop is spatial encoding: ResNet-18 produces 512-dimensional features \(F_s\in\mathbb{R}^{512}\) [PAGE 5]. The fourth hop is temporal encoding: a bi-LSTM produces \(F_t\in\mathbb{R}^{256}\) [PAGE 5]. The fifth hop is digit-wise classification under the multi-task loss \(L_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.5L_{d_1}+0.5L_{d_2}\), with \(L_{d_1}=-\sum_{i=0}^{10}d_i^1\log p_i^1\) and \(L_{d_2}=-\sum_{j=0}^{10}d_j^2\log p_j^2\) [PAGE 5]. The causal claim is that removing noisy frames prevents the temporal network from extracting “spurious features” caused by the “absence of jersey numbers in the majority of frames” [PAGE 1].

The audit conclusion is that the paper demonstrates a robust empirical selection effect on SoccerNet, but it does not prove all-weather, all-camera, or all-occlusion robustness.

The formal operators and units are as follows. The tracklet is
\[
T=\{F_i:F_i\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}\}_{i=1}^{t},
\]
with \(H,W\) in pixels and 3 RGB channels dimensionless [PAGE 3]. KfID is
\[
KfID(T)=T\setminus\{F_{n_1},F_{n_2},\ldots,F_{n_k}\},
\]
where \(n_1,\ldots,n_k\) index noisy frames [PAGE 3]. Assumption: the frame indices are finite and distinct. Breakdown regime: if \(k=t\), the keyframe set is empty; if \(k=0\), KfID is an identity operation and the spurious-frame problem remains.

JNL and local/global filtering are
\[
\det(T)=\{\det_i\}_{i=1}^{t}=\{\mathrm{JNL}(F_i)\}_{i=1}^{t},
\]
\[
f_{\mathrm{local}}(T)=\{f_{\mathrm{local}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{t}
=\{\mathrm{LHC}(\mathrm{RoI}(\det_i))\}_{i=1}^{t},
\]
\[
f_{\mathrm{global}}(T)=\mathrm{GHC}(f_{\mathrm{local}}(T)).
\]
These are set-valued detections and filtered detections, not probability units [PAGE 3]. The RoI intersection metric is
\[
I^*=\frac{A(R_1\cap R_2)}{\min(A(R_1),A(R_2))+\epsilon},
\]
with “eps is a small constant (10^{-7})” [PAGE 4]. If \(A(\cdot)\) is measured in square pixels, then \(A\) has units \(\mathrm{px}^2\) and \(I^*\) is dimensionless. Assumption: \(\epsilon=10^{-7}\) is in the same area unit as \(A\). Breakdown regimes: if \(R_1\) or \(R_2\) is degenerate, the intersection geometry is undefined or zero; if both areas are zero, \(I^*=0\); if the detection area is extremely small relative to \(\epsilon\), the denominator is dominated by \(\epsilon\); and the decision threshold for accepting a detection is not stated on the supplied pages, so threshold sensitivity is an unresolved gray zone.

The spatial and temporal features are
\[
F_s\in\mathbb{R}^{512},\qquad F_t\in\mathbb{R}^{256},
\]
where 512 and 256 are dimensionless feature dimensions [PAGE 5]. The multi-task loss is
\[
L_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.5L_{d_1}+0.5L_{d_2},
\]
\[
L_{d_1}=-\sum_{i=0}^{10}d_i^1\log p_i^1,\qquad
L_{d_2}=-\sum_{j=0}^{10}d_j^2\log p_j^2.
\]
The paper defines \(d_1,d_2\in\mathbb{R}^{11}\) and \(p_1,p_2\in\mathbb{R}^{11}\) [PAGE 5]. Unit assumption: \(L\) is in nats if the logarithm is natural and in bits if base-2; the supplied text does not specify the base. Breakdown regime: if \(p_i^1=0\) while \(d_i^1=1\), the cross-entropy is infinite without clipping or label smoothing. The 11-class digit vector is also a gray zone because the supplied pages do not enumerate the 11 classes or state the maximum jersey-number length; they only state digit-wise classification. The paper’s empirical results are reported as percent accuracy, e.g. LSTM with KfId at 68.53 test and 73.77 challenge [PAGE 7]. Sequence length is measured in frames: length 40 gives 68.53 test accuracy, length 60 gives 65.80, and the paper states that beyond 40 “the accuracy of the model begins to decline” [PAGE 8]. Dataset counts are dimensionless: 2,052,306 images and 253,350 keyframes [PAGE 5].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage runs from static-image jersey recognition to tracklet-level temporal recognition. The paper describes earlier work as CNN-based static-image recognition, Spatial Transformer Network localization, pose-guided multitask RCNN, and multi-task digit classification [PAGE 2]. It then describes tracklet methods using transformers and LSTMs, including “a transformer-based architecture to recognize jersey numbers from ice-hockey player tracklets” and an LSTM-based method that extracts temporal characteristics [PAGE 2]. The dataset lineage is also explicit: Table 2 compares static-image datasets with temporal datasets and lists SoccerNet as 2,052,306 images [PAGE 6]. Retrocausally, the paper reinterprets the earlier static-image success as a visibility-selection problem: the old models were evaluated where digits were already present, while real broadcast tracklets contain many frames where “jersey numbers are not visible in most of the frames” [PAGE 1]. That reinterpretation is the genealogical shift from “better digit classifier” to “better frame selector.”

The multi-week traps are reproducibility and attribution traps. First, a team could reproduce the temporal network without KfID and see the no-KfID LSTM at 30.89 test and 36.07 challenge [PAGE 7], then incorrectly conclude that temporal modeling alone is sufficient. Second, a team could over-filter and produce empty or near-empty keyframe sets; the paper’s 87.65% frame reduction [PAGE 5] is a warning that selection is aggressive, but the supplied pages do not give a minimum-keyframe guard. Third, a team could tune sequence length by intuition; the paper’s ablation shows 40 is best, 60 falls to 65.80, and below 30 underfits [PAGE 8]. Fourth, a team could assume the HSV histogram and GHC cluster generalize across lighting and jersey colors; the paper motivates HSV by “different disruptive conditions” [PAGE 4], but the supplied pages do not report weather or illumination stress tests. Fifth, a team could miss that the RoI is manually preset: “we manually preset the RoI for every image based on its dimensions” [PAGE 6]. That is a dataset-specific design choice and a domain-gap trap. Sixth, a team could add standard augmentations and degrade performance, because the paper reports that “Applying standard data augmentations such as ColorJitter, HorizontalFlip and normalization decreases the performance” [PAGE 6]. The genealogical lesson is that the paper’s advance is not a generic temporal-model upgrade; it is a visibility-gated pipeline whose assumptions must be reproduced with the same selection, sampling, and preprocessing discipline.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In this pipeline, “crisis habit regression” is the tendency to fall back to the older static-image or holistic-label habit when visibility collapses. The paper gives direct evidence: holistic jersey-number prediction scores 55.71, while digit-wise prediction scores 68.53 [PAGE 7]. The habit is to predict the whole number as one class even though the paper states that “there a lot of jersey numbers in the test and challenge set that are absent in the training set” [PAGE 7]. Digit-wise prediction is the counter-habit because it avoids unseen whole-number classes. The second failure mode is the counter-trap: after observing that KfID helps, an operator may over-filter, treating every low-confidence detection as noise. That can produce \(KfID(T)=\emptyset\) or a GHC majority cluster that is actually the opposition player. The paper’s own example shows the need for this caution: the JNL module can detect “a player’s leg” as a jersey number [PAGE 4], so filtering is necessary, but the GHC module’s majority-cluster rule is not guaranteed to preserve the target if opposition detections dominate. The recursive theory-of-mind point is that the system’s “belief” about visibility is only the detector output; the operator’s belief about the system can be wrong in both directions, trusting spurious detections or deleting true ones.

The “clock” is the temporal budget. The paper fixes sequence length and samples frames at least \(d\) apart [PAGE 5]. The ablation shows a clock optimum: length 40 gives 68.53, length 60 gives 65.80, and the text says lengths beyond 40 extract “redundant and unnecessary features” while lengths below 30 underfit [PAGE 8]. Training also has a clock: “20,000 iterations,” batch size 32, AdamW for ViT, Adam for the others, and a scheduler that reduces learning rate every 2000 iterations for the first 6000 [PAGE 6]. The recursive audit is therefore: do not ask only “does KfID help?” Ask whether the operator has a guard against empty selection, whether the GHC cluster has a target-preservation check, and whether the sequence clock is fixed at the validated regime rather than tuned to the test set. The paper supplies strong empirical evidence for the KfID benefit, but the recursive failure analysis remains necessary because the supplied pages do not give keyframe-count guards or cluster-confidence diagnostics.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical-layer veto is resolution and motion blur. The paper’s abstract names “motion blur, low resolution, distortions, and occlusions” [PAGE 1]. If the point-spread blur width exceeds the digit stroke width, or if the digit’s projected area is below the detector’s resolvable area, JNL cannot produce a reliable detection; then RoI, LHC, and GHC have nothing valid to filter. The veto proof is conditional but strong: if no frame in \(T\) yields a digit detection that passes the RoI and histogram filters, then \(KfID(T)=\emptyset\), and the downstream classifier has no defined input. The correct system behavior is abstention or tracklet rejection, not a forced jersey-number prediction. The paper’s Figure 3 outlier case, where “a player’s leg is incorrectly detected for the presence of a jersey number” [PAGE 4], shows that the physical layer can also create false positives, so the veto must be two-sided: reject missing digits and reject non-digit detections.

The biomechanical-layer veto is body orientation and occlusion. The paper states that jersey numbers are “typically located on the back of the player’s jersey” [PAGE 1]. If the player is facing the camera, if the back is occluded by another player, or if the torso is rotated away from the camera, the digit is physically absent from the image regardless of model capacity. The paper’s related work mentions pose-guided methods [PAGE 2], but the proposed pipeline does not use pose as an explicit visibility gate; it uses JNL, RoI, LHC, and GHC. Therefore the biomechanical veto is an external validity limit: the method can only classify when the back-facing digit is visible and localized. The weather/illumination-layer veto is color-histogram instability. The paper converts frames to HSV because this “allows us to separate the color information of each component from the intensity of light” and helps “in various disruptive conditions” [PAGE 4]. However, the supplied pages do not report rain, snow, shadow, or floodlight stress tests. If hue distributions shift across a tracklet, LHC and GHC correlation scores can become unreliable, and the majority cluster can misassign the target. The adjudication is therefore: physical and biomechanical visibility vetoes are hard constraints supported by the paper’s own problem statement; weather/illumination is a plausible but experimentally unproven veto in the supplied pages. Any claim of universal robustness is vetoed unless those cross-layer conditions are explicitly tested.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: KfID ablation with the same LSTM, sequence length, and loss. The paper already supplies the counterfactual: LSTM without KfID scores 30.89 test and 36.07 challenge, while LSTM with KfID scores 68.53 and 73.77 [PAGE 7]. Prediction: removing KfID returns accuracy to the no-KfID regime, approximately 30.89 and 36.07. Veto condition: if the no-KfID rerun matches the KfID accuracy within run-to-run variance, the claimed KfID causal effect is falsified for that setup.

Scenario 2: GHC replacement or disablement. Replace the GHC majority-cluster selection with random cluster selection or with no GHC filtering, holding JNL, RoI, LHC, ResNet-18, bi-LSTM, and digit-wise loss fixed. Prediction: challenge accuracy falls below the full-pipeline 73.77 [PAGE 7], and the degradation should be larger on tracklets containing opposition-player digits, because the paper introduces GHC specifically to address “jersey numbers present on opposition player jerseys” [PAGE 5]. Veto condition: if the no-GHC variant is statistically indistinguishable from 73.77 on opposition-heavy tracklets, the GHC mechanism is not causally supported by the supplied evidence.

Scenario 3: Sequence-length clock stress. The paper reports length 40 at 68.53 and length 60 at 65.80, with overfitting beyond 40 and underfitting below 30 [PAGE 8]. Prediction: a length-60 rerun will score below the length-40 rerun on the same test split, and a length-10 rerun will score near the reported 62.82 [PAGE 8]. Veto condition: if length 60 equals or exceeds length 40 under the same seed and preprocessing, the paper’s overfitting interpretation is falsified. A fourth optional stress test would inject synthetic occlusion or motion blur beyond the KfID training distribution; prediction: KfID recall and downstream accuracy degrade, and the system should abstain rather than predict. Veto condition: if severe blur leaves accuracy unchanged, the physical-layer veto is falsified for that perturbation.
