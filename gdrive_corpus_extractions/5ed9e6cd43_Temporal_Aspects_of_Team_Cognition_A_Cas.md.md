dimensionless. This is a formal index only; it does not replace the source’s local-sharing and compatibility analysis.

As an analytic extension, recursive theory of mind can be layered onto the source without claiming that the authors measured it. Let \(M_i^{(0)}\) be player \(i\)’s own concern, \(M_i^{(1)}\) be \(i\)’s model of teammate \(j\)’s concern, and \(M_i^{(2)}\) be \(i\)’s model of \(j\)’s model of \(i\). The source’s simultaneous mode depends on shared recognition of a “situation-type” and associated team routines [PAGE 234]; that is a high-consensus, low-recursion mode: all players recognize the same configuration and enact trained roles. The progressive mode depends on perceiving a teammate’s behavior, inferring a compatible interpretation, and acting so that another teammate can infer the same [PAGE 234-235]; that is a recursive, dyadic-adjustment mode.

Crisis habit regression can be formalized as
\[
\Pr(\text{habit}\mid T_{\mathrm{avail}})=\sigma(\alpha-\beta T_{\mathrm{avail}}),\qquad \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},
\]
where \(T_{\mathrm{avail}}\) is available decision time in seconds, \(\beta\) has units \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(\alpha\) is dimensionless, and the probability is dimensionless. Assumption: habit is a logistic function of available time. Breakdown: as \(T_{\mathrm{avail}}\to 0\), the probability tends to \(\sigma(\alpha)\); if \(\alpha\) is large, habit dominates. This matches the source’s observation that non-sharedness occurred when “the clock was stopped” and there was “a drop in the time pressure” [PAGE 233], while simultaneous routine use occurred when players recognized a familiar fast-play situation-type [PAGE 234].

A counter-trap occurs when the opponent induces a familiar situation-type but violates the expected outcome. Let \(R_i(t)\) be recognition strength for prototype \(s\), and let \(D_i(t)\) be a trap-inconsistency penalty. Players enact the routine when \(R_i(t)-D_i(t)\geq \theta_i\). If the opponent maximizes \(R_i\) while increasing \(D_i\) after the routine begins, the team’s recursive model is shallow: it predicts teammates’ routine actions but not the opponent’s counter to the routine. The clock is the third variable: high time pressure favors habit and simultaneous routine; stopped-clock or low-threat moments permit non-shared concerns, as in the inbound and slow-advance examples [PAGE 233]. Prediction: raising time pressure should increase simultaneous routine convergence and increase vulnerability to a prepared counter-trap; stopping the clock should increase concern dispersion.

The source provides no force-plate, jump-load, sprint-distance, heart-rate, injury, court-surface, venue, travel, or weather measurements. A formal veto criterion is conditional mutual information:
\[
I(E_{\mathrm{eff}};S\mid L)=0\quad \text{bits},
\]
where \(E_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is team effectiveness, \(S\) is sharedness or sharing, and \(L\) is the cross-layer vector of load, biomechanical, and environmental variables. If this mutual information is zero, sharedness adds no information beyond \(L\), and the cognitive causal claim is vetoed. In a linear approximation, if the true model is
\[
E_{\mathrm{eff}}=\beta_S S+\gamma_L L+\varepsilon,
\]
but \(L\) is omitted, then
\[
\operatorname{plim}\hat\beta_S=\beta_S+\gamma_L\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(S,L)}{\operatorname{Var}(S)}.
\]
The bias term has the same units as \(\beta_S\). If \(\operatorname{Var}(S)=0\), the estimand is undefined; if \(S\) and \(L\) are collinear, the design is rank deficient; if \(\gamma_L\operatorname{Cov}(S,L)\neq 0\), the observed sharedness coefficient is confounded. A biomechanical veto therefore requires showing that load or movement constraints co-vary with both concern convergence and effectiveness. A weather veto is weak indoors but not zero if venue, travel, or environmental stress affects movement; the source does not measure these. The defensible adjudication is: the source supports local, reportable cognitive coordination patterns, but it does not prove that sharedness survives a cross-layer veto. Any stronger claim must add load and environmental covariates or an intervention that holds them fixed.
