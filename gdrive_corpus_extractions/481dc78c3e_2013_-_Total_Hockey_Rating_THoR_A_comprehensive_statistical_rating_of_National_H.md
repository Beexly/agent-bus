\hat{\theta} = \arg\min_{\theta} \big\{ \| y - X\theta \|_2^2 + \lambda \|\theta\|_2^2 \big\}.
\]
Ridge shrinks small-sample players toward zero and mitigates multicollinearity among co- deployed players [PAGE 5].

THoR scaling:
\[
\text{THoR}_j = \hat{\theta}_j \times 80 \times \frac{82}{6},
\]
units: wins per season relative to average; 80 approximate even-strength events per game per player, 82 games, 6 goals per win (Vollman) [PAGE 6, PAGE 8].

Reliability metric:
\[
\Gamma = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{T} (\hat{\theta}_{k,1} - \hat{\theta}_{k,2})^2 / n_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{P} (\hat{\theta}_j - \bar{\theta})^2 / n_j} = 0.12,
\]
where traded players are treated as distinct team-specific entities [PAGE 5].

Estimated effects: home ice \(\mu \approx 0.32\) goals per game; zone-start effect \(\approx 0.53\) goals per game for a player starting every shift in the offensive zone, or \(\approx 5.4\) goals over 82 games for 10 additional offensive-zone starts per game [PAGE 7].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The method descends from Macdonald's adjusted plus-minus for NHL players using ridge regression with goals, shots, Fenwick, and Corsi [PAGE 1]; Ferrari's shot-direction concepts [PAGE 1]; Awad's Goals Versus Threshold (GVT) framework [PAGE 1]; Ryder's product-recall work on shot-quality rink bias [PAGE 2]; Desjardins's analyses of giveaway/takeaway scorer bias and shooting-percentage regression [PAGE 1]; Fischer's blocked-shot scorer-bias documentation at New Jersey [PAGE 1]; Schuckers's Defense Independent Goalie Rating via spatially smoothed save-percentage maps [PAGE 2]; Charron's and Calloway's zone-start analyses [PAGE 3]; Sagarin's team-level home-ice ratings [PAGE 7]; Buttrey et al.'s NHL scoring-rate estimation [PAGE 7]; and Vollman's goals-versus-salary conversion (6 goals \(\approx\) 1 win) [PAGE 8].

Multi-week traps: (1) Small-sample ridge shrinkage systematically deflates players with fewer events, which explains why Sidney Crosby—ranked 15th at 4.92 wins despite being viewed as elite—was suppressed by limited sample size [PAGE 8]. (2) Treating traded players as separate entities creates a team-contingency trap: cross-team rating stability is the validation target, but the model cannot disentangle system effects from individual talent without out-of-sample trade data [PAGE 5]. (3) Conventional zone-start percentage collapses defensive and neutral starts into a single ratio, masking raw volume effects that THoR's \(\{-1,0,1\}\) coding preserves [PAGE 3]. (4) Restricting estimation to even strength excludes power-play and penalty-kill contexts where defensemen may carry disproportionate value, a position-context trap the authors flag for future extension [PAGE 9].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis-habit regression: During losing streaks, coaches alter deployment—checking lines receive more defensive-zone starts, top lines more offensive starts. THoR models \(ZS\) with a constant \(\gamma\), but crisis-driven deployment changes may interact with score state and opponent quality, violating stationarity of the zone-start coefficient [PAGE 4, PAGE 7].

Counter-trap: Opponents respond to high-THoR players by matching elite defensive pairings and altering on-ice composition (\(x_{ij}\)). Ridge regression stabilizes collinear predictors such as the Sedin twins but cannot attribute value when strategic shadowing redesigns the exposure matrix mid-season; the model then risks attributing teammate-driven suppression or inflation to the focal player [PAGE 5].

Clock: NP20's 20-second hazard window assumes goal probability is stationary after an event. Endgame clock situations—goalie pulled, trailing by one, 30 seconds remaining—produce non-stationary goal hazards that the even-strength-only fit does not capture; an offensive-zone faceoff late in a close game is not equivalent to one at 10:00 of period one [PAGE 3]. Because the model excludes non-even-strength events, evacuated-net and endgame scenarios are either omitted or misvalued, creating a temporal boundary breakdown.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: NHL rinks are standardized at \(200 \times 85\) feet; shot coordinates are recorded in feet and adjusted via CDF matching [PAGE 2, PAGE 10]. Venues are indoor, climate-controlled, so temperature, wind, and precipitation exert negligible exogenous variance. Weather veto: NULL (not applicable).

Biomechanical layer: Shot value depends on shot type and 54-grid location; player fatigue, shift length, and within-shift biomechanical decay are not modeled—the on-ice roster is treated as a static set per event, so fatigue gradients across a shift are unobserved [PAGE 3, P
AGE 4].

Breakdown regime: The shot-coordinate adjustment becomes singular when \(F_R\) and \(F_A\) lack overlapping support (e.g., new rink construction or scorekeeper recalibration), forcing \(x'\) into extrapolation. MSG was explicitly identified as a rink with a significantly different shot distribution [PAGE 2].

Veto proof: Because climate is controlled, the sole physical veto is rink-level scorer bias, which Appendix I addresses through the away-team CDF transport operator [PAGE 10]. No weather or outdoor-biomechanical veto applies to NHL indoor ice.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 (sample-size ridge bias): A player such as Sidney Crosby, injury-limited below the 4,000-event two-season floor, is ranked 15th at 4.92 wins [PAGE 8]. Verifiable veto: if an ordinary-least-squares (unshrunk) estimate on full-season data places Crosby above the top-5 threshold, THoR's ranking is vetoed for that player. Prediction: ridge shrinkage pulls small-sample \(\theta_j\) toward zero; with a full 82-game sample Crosby's unshrunk rating would enter the top 5.

Veto scenario 2 (rink-coordinate non-stationarity): If the Madison Square Garden scorekeeper protocol changes, \(F_{RA}(x) - F_A(x)\) shifts, altering adjusted coordinates \(x'\) for Rangers home games [PAGE 2, PAGE 10]. Verifiable veto: Rangers skaters' THoR would fluctuate season-to-season without corresponding changes in on-ice goal differential or Corsi. Prediction: cross-validation against expected-goals models would show systematic Rangers-specific residuals uncorrelated with shot volume.

Veto scenario 3 (zone-start confounding): A coach adds 10 defensive-zone starts per game to a shutdown defenseman. THoR's \(\gamma \approx -0.53\) goals per start implies a \(-5.4\)-goal season differential (10 starts \(\times\) 82 games \(\times\) \(-0.53\) / 10, equivalently the paper's stated 5.4 goals for 10 additional offensive starts) independent of player skill [PAGE 7]. Verifiable veto: if the player's on-ice goal differential does not decline by roughly 5.4 goals over 82 games, \(\gamma\) is confounded by teammate/opponent quality and the model is vetoed for that deployment context.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
