These are payout multipliers, not probability units; \(m_i\) is dimensionless, \(s\) is USD, and \(p_i\) is a probability.

The realized payout is
\[
\Pi(\hat y)=s\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[m_i(\hat y_i)\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\}-\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i\ne y_i\}\right]\quad \text{USD}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\Pi(\hat y)=s\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[(m_i(\hat y_i)+1)\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\}-1\right]\quad \text{USD}.
\]
This identity is the source of the accuracy/pay-out divergence. Accuracy is
\[
\mathrm{Acc}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\}\quad \text{dimensionless},
\]
while payout weights each correct prediction by \(w_i=m_i(\hat y_i)+1\). Thus two models with identical accuracy can have different \(\Pi\) if their correct predictions concentrate on different \(w_i\). The source exhibits this directly: NB has lower accuracy than KP but positive pay-out, while KP loses money [PAGE 5]. \]

\[
\mathbb{E}[\Pi_i\mid p_i,\hat y_i=0]
=s\left[(1-p_i)m_i(0)-p_i\right]
=s\left[(1-p_i)(m_i(0)+1)-1\right]\quad \text{USD}.
\]

\[
\hat y_i=1 \iff p_i(m_i(1)+1)>(1-p_i)(m_i(0)+1).
\]

p_i^{*,0}=\frac{m_i(0)}{m_i(0)+1}.

This is a stopping problem under adversarial observation: if many bettors wait for the same recovery signal, the quoted prices adjust and the recovery disappears; if many exit at the same peak, the peak shifts earlier. The source’s safest reported model is NB: “In all cases, the safest model seems to be a Na¨ıve Bayes predictor” [PAGE 9]. That is not a claim that NB is the best outcome model; it is a claim that a simpler, less overconfident value-weighted policy is more robust to the recursive trap of overfitting match-type and timing. The theory-of-mind conclusion is: do not ask “who wins?” in isolation; ask “who wins relative to what the quoted price already implies, at what point in the season, and with what lower-layer information missing?”
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto question is whether a physical, biomechanical, or weather state \(Z_i\) can invalidate a decision made from the source’s feature vector \(X_i\). The source’s basketball features are season-level Adjusted Efficiencies and related statistics [PAGE 4]; the football features are box-score averages normalized for 65 possessions, opponent averages, adjusted averages, and SRS [PAGE 7]. These are upper-layer aggregates. They do not encode in-game injury severity, player availability, travel load, field surface, wind, temperature, or down-level biomechanical efficiency. The source acknowledges that new information such as “injuries, player arrests, coaches’ announcements” arrives over time [PAGE 3], and it explicitly forgoes play-by-play measures such as DVOA and DYAR [PAGE 7]. Therefore a lower-layer state can enter after the feature snapshot and change the true probability \(p_i^Z\) while the model’s \(p_i\) remains unchanged.

Formal veto condition. Let \(D_i(p_i)\in\{\text{bet side 1},\text{bet side 0},\text{abstain}\}\) be the decision from the expected-payout rule in the previous section. Let \(p_i^Z=\Pr(y_i=1\mid X_i,Z_i)\). Define the veto set
\[
\mathcal{V}_i=\{Z_i:\ D_i(p_i)\ne D_i(p_i^Z)\ \text{or}\ \mathbb{E}[\Pi_i\mid p_i^Z,D_i(p_i)]<0\}.
\]
A policy is layer-robust at level \(\alpha\) only if \(\Pr(\mathcal{V}_i)\le\alpha\) under the match distribution. The source cannot establish layer robustness because \(Z_i\) is unmeasured in the reported tables. Proof sketch by contradiction: suppose the source model were robust to every \(Z_i\). Then its decision would be invariant to the omission of \(Z_i\). But the source’s own football section says it forwent play-by-play DVOA/DYAR and used box-score averages [PAGE 7], and its betting discussion says quoted prices move on injuries and announcements [PAGE 3]. There exist lower-layer states with nonzero effect on \(p_i^Z\); for example, a late injury to a high-usage player can move \(p_i^Z\) across the break-even threshold \(p_i^{*,a}\). Since the model’s \(X_i\) does not contain that state, \(p_i\) and \(p_i^Z\) can lie on opposite sides of the threshold. Then \(D_i(p_i)\) and \(D_i(p_i^Z)\) differ, so the model is not robust. The only valid adjudication is conditional: abstain when a known lower-layer event is absent from \(X_i\), or when \(p_i\) is within an uncertainty band of \(p_i^{*,a}\) and \(Z_i\) has nonzero plausible effect. Otherwise the decision is an upper-layer projection masquerading as a physical guarantee.

Biomechanically, the football problem is especially exposed: “individual events are Downs and their outcome is mainly measured in yards gained (or lost)” [PAGE 7], but the source’s Basic Averages normalize box-score totals for 65 possessions [PAGE 7]. That abstracts away down distance, field position, fatigue, and opponent adjustment. Weather is likewise absent from the reported feature list; outdoor football games can change passing efficiency, kicking, and turnover risk. The veto proof is not that the model is always wrong; it is that the model cannot certify non-veto when the lower layer is omitted. The correct cross-layer rule is hard abstention on unmodeled physical/weather states, not retroactive explanation after the payout is known.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: accuracy-reward versus payout-reward. Counterfactual: train two otherwise identical classifiers on the same features, one with reward \(R_{\text{acc}}=\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\}\), the other with \(R_{\text{pay}}=m_i(\hat y_i)\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i=y_i\}-\mathbf{1}\{\hat y_i\ne y_i\}\). Prediction: the payout-reward model will have equal or lower accuracy but higher cumulative USD pay-out, and its advantage will concentrate in underdog and Pick ’em correct predictions. This is directly motivated by the source’s NB-versus-KP inversion: NB has accuracy 0.6865 and pay-out 293.52, while KP has accuracy 0.7014 and pay-out -231.34 [PAGE 5], with NB getting five underdog wins and KP getting zero [PAGE 5]. Verification: bootstrap matches, report \(\Delta\mathrm{Acc}\), \(\Delta\Pi\), and counts by favorite/underdog/Pick ’em. Veto if \(\Delta\Pi\le 0\) out-of-sample or if the gain is entirely explained by one match.

Scenario 2: forced action versus abstention. Counterfactual: replace the source’s “bet every match” policy [PAGE 3] with abstention when neither side has positive expected payout under the model’s \(p_i\). Prediction: abstention will improve mean payout per bet and reduce maximum drawdown on NBA and NCAAB, while preserving most of NFL NB’s positive result. The source’s NBA baseline already shows that following the favorite side without Pick ’ems yields accuracy 0.7121 but pay-out -2374.16 [PAGE 4]; a forced-action policy can be accurate and still negative. The source’s NCAAB Pick ’em variance [PAGE 4] predicts that abstention will help most where the model’s probability is near the break-even threshold. Verification: recompute cumulative payout with a no-bet band, report number of abstentions, mean payout per bet, and drawdown. Veto if abstention lowers risk-adjusted payout or if it merely avoids the winning tail.

Scenario 3: causal stopping clock versus hold-to-end. Counterfactual: impose a walk-forward stopping rule, such as “stop after cumulative payout falls by \(\delta\) from its running maximum” or “stop after \(k\) weeks,” with no future information. Prediction: the causal rule will beat hold-to-end on the NFL because the source says “each predictor reaches a high point that comes before the end of the season” [PAGE 8]; on the NBA, a causal entry rule will matter because models recover from troughs and “one could win money if one could determine when to start betting” [PAGE 6]. Verification: walk-forward backtest over multiple \(\delta\) and \(k\), report final payout, maximum drawdown, and turnover. Veto if no causal rule beats hold-to-end out-of-sample or if the apparent gain disappears after the first season. A fourth-layer ablation, injury/weather flags as hard vetoes, would test the cross-layer proof: prediction is that decisions within the break-even uncertainty band flip most often and that abstention on unmodeled lower-layer states improves calibration-adjusted payout.
