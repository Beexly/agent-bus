1. Physical/environmental layer (absolute veto): The scaling \dot{X}_{sup}/\dot{X}_{req} \propto L^{-1} with L_c = \sigma/\kappa [PAGE 66] sets a hard survival boundary. Atmospheric pO₂ enters through \sigma; Wiener’s footnote notes Carboniferous atmosphere differed, shifting L_c and permitting large fossil insects, while modern insect maxima remain mouse-sized [PAGE 67 footnote]. If L > L_c(pO₂,T), no behavioral strategy can prevent asphyxiation. Quote: efficiency “falls below the point of survival for an animal of any considerable size” [PAGE 66].

2. Biomechanical layer (structural veto): Conserved neuron diameter d_n bounds N \le V_b/d_n^3 and C_{\text{stored}} \le \alpha N [bits]. Because insect V_b is small, there is “no room … for a nervous system of great complexity, nor for a large stored memory” [PAGE 67]. This veto cannot be overridden by motivation or experience.

3. Behavioral layer (downstream, vetoed rather than vetoing): Instinct-versus-intelligence and the computing-machine “tape” analogy [PAGE 67] are consequences of layers 1–2. Wiener states the physical strait-jacket is “directly responsible” for the mental strait-jacket [PAGE 67].

Adjudication order: Evaluate the physical/environmental veto first (can the animal survive at this L under current pO₂?). If passed, evaluate the biomechanical veto (is there neural volume for the proposed memory/complexity?). Only then consider the behavioral tape constraint. Weather is the control parameter: modern pO₂ ≈ 21 kPa fixes the insect ceiling; altering pO₂ changes L_c but does not eliminate the scaling singularity. Forced ventilation (lungs, fans, building HVAC) is the engineering bypass that extends L beyond passive L_c, but its shutdown recreates the minutes-to-failure regime [PAGE 67].

Scenario 1: Skyscraper forced-ventilation shutdown. Seal mechanical ventilation in interior rooms of a large building and log CO₂, O₂, and occupant-reported air quality. Prediction: intolerable foulness or physiological distress within minutes, matching “a shut-down … will be followed in a very few minutes by an intolerable foulness” [PAGE 67]; occupants cannot learn or adapt their way out of the air-quality collapse without restoring mechanical flow. Veto condition: if passive leakage maintains acceptable air quality for >30 minutes, the building envelope is leak-dominated rather than skyscraper-like, and Wiener’s scaling analogy fails for that specific architecture.

Scenario 2: Hyperoxic insect rearing. Rear locusts or Drosophila at elevated pO₂ (e.g., 30–35 kPa) versus normoxic 21 kPa across generations; measure maximum adult body mass, tracheal volume, neuron counts, and maze-learning flexibility. Prediction: maximum mass increases somewhat but remains orders of magnitude below vertebrate scaling; neuron count and stored memory remain low; behavior remains dominated by fixed action patterns rather than flexible learning. Veto condition: if hyperoxia yields insects with mass ≥ 0.5 kg and vertebrate-equivalent brain complexity plus open-ended learning, Wiener’s absolute-size claim and Carboniferous-atmosphere footnote are falsified [PAGE 67].

Scenario 3: Metamorphosis-blocked ant learning. Use juvenile-hormone manipulation to remove or blur the juvenile–adult insulation in ants, then expose individuals to repeated novel spatial-navigation tasks with retention tested over days. Prediction: minimal learning gain relative to metamorphosing controls because the ceiling is structural (few neurons, small C_stored) rather than solely life-stage insulation. Veto condition: if blocked ants show flexible, long-term retention comparable to small vertebrates, Wiener’s claim that the physical strait-jacket alone causes the mental strait-jacket via neuron/memory limits is incomplete; distributed or external memory must be added to the model [PAGE 67].
