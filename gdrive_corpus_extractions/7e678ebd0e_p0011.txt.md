If any runway required for the forecasted capacity category \hat{k}_t is closed, the realized capacity is capped by the subset of available runways: \text{Cap}_t^{\text{realized}} \le \sum_{r: O_{t,r}=1} \text{rate}_r. This veto is independent of P(R_t = IMC) and of the binary classifier outputs. A forecast of normal capacity under VMC is vetoed to zero for the affected runway configuration.

Demand veto: If scheduled flight demand D_t (flights/h) is below the forecasted capacity category \hat{k}_t, the airport is demand-constrained, not capacity-constrained. The capacity reduction scenario is operationally moot; throughput equals min(D_t, \hat{k}_t) = D_t. Staffing, deicing, and snow-removal decisions predicated on capacity reduction are over-provisioned relative to actual demand. Units: D_t and \hat{k}_t in flights/h or mov/h.

Biomechanical/human-factors layer: ATC workload, fatigue, and decision-making under stress are not weather parameters [PACE 4], yet they modulate effective sector and runway acceptance rates. There is no biomechanical operator in the disclosed equations. The veto condition is: if controller staffing falls below the minimum required for the forecasted configuration, or if duty-time limits are approached, \hat{k}_t is reduced by a human-performance factor not present in the model. This is a breakdown regime because the source attributes capacity to “historical behaviors by air traffic controllers” without modeling the physiological or cognitive constraints that bound those behaviors during extreme weather and IR-ROP.

Adjudication hierarchy: When multiple vetoes apply, the binding constraint is the minimum of weather-imposed, infrastructure-imposed, demand-imposed, and human-imposed capacities. The ML output is admissible only in the interior of the feasible set where none of the vetoes bind; otherwise the categorical posterior is decision-irrelevant.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios are specified below. Each includes an observable, non-betting prediction metric.

Veto Scenario 1 — Runway closure during forecasted VMC.
Counterfactual: The ensemble forecasts VMC with high probability, and the binary classifiers output a high-capacity category \hat{k}_t (e.g., the maximum arrival rate). Simultaneously, an operational limitation O_t places a runway in closed status. Verifiable veto: the system must override \hat{k}_t and recompute capacity using only available runways. Prediction: realized arrival throughput will fall by at least the fractional capacity of the closed runway relative to the full configuration (e.g., if one of two parallel runways closes, throughput decreases by approximately the runway’s share of declared capacity, measured in mov/h), regardless of the VMC likelihood output. Observation: compare post-hoc ATADS or ASOS/DATIS runway-in-use logs against the forecasted category; a sustained discrepancy exceeding the closed-runway capacity share confirms the veto.

Veto Scenario 2 — Ceiling/visibility hovering near VMC/IMC threshold with rapid oscillation.
Counterfactual: The probabilistic forecast yields P(R_t = IMC) near 0.5 over a rolling time horizon, causing the five binary classifiers to flip categories between consecutive time steps. The postprocessing MAP rule generates high-frequency “chattering” in \hat{k}_t, producing unstable timings, magnitudes, and durations of capacity reduction [PAGE 4]. Verifiable veto: impose a dwell-time or hysteresis constraint such that a category change is registered only after the posterior exceeds a persistence threshold (e.g., P(R_t = IMC) > 0.6 for at least \Delta t minutes). Prediction: without the veto, the scenario consolidation will split a single physical event into multiple spurious weather analytical scenarios, inflating S and distorting \pi_s. Observation: high-frequency category transitions in the model output correlated with ceiling/visibility sensor noise near decision minima, without corresponding changes in actual runway acceptance rates.

Veto Scenario 3 — Demand below minimum forecasted capacity category.
Counterfactual: The model forecasts a capacity reduction to category k_2 (the second-lowest of five), but scheduled demand D_t is below even k_2. Verifiable veto: because the airport is demand-constrained, the capacity category is operationally irrelevant; the system should not trigger snow-removal, deicing staffing, or passenger re-accommodation protocols predicated on capacity shortfall [PAGE 4]. Prediction: realized throughput will equal D_t, and the capacity-reduction scenario will have zero operational consequence. Observation: compare flight-schedule demand (scheduled arrivals per hour) against the forecasted capacity category during low-demand periods (e.g., overnight or off-peak); if deicing/snow-removal resources are deployed but queues remain empty, the demand veto has been violated by the system’s failure to condition on D_t.
