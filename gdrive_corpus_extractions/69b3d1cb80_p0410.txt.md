Tolman then observes that subjects “without any motivation on their part” still connect pairs into “sign-gestalt wholes,” such that the first member of a pair leads the subject to expect the second. Fourth, this occurs even under a null motivational state, which challenges drive-reduction accounts that require arousal or need as a precondition for learning [PAGE 410].

Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

The subject does not merely register \(S \to X\). The subject models the experimenter’s pairing rule. A recursive theory-of-mind operator can be written as:

\[
R_n = \mathbb{E}\!\left[ X_n \,\middle|\, S_n,\; B_n,\; \mathbb{E}_{\text{exp}}\!\left[\text{pair} \mid \text{context}\right],\; R_{n-1} \right]
\]

where \(R_n\) is the subject’s nth-order expectation of the significate, conditioned on the sign, belonging, the subject’s model of the experimenter’s contingency, and the previous expectation state.

Crisis habit regression: under stress, novelty, or high response conflict, the subject may regress from the sign-gestalt (cognitive expectancy) to a fixed Thorndikian reflexive habit—stamping-in of the most recently reinforced response rather than flexible expectancy of \(X\). This is a collapse from \(B=1\) organizational learning to \(B=0\) stimulus-response chaining.

Counter-trap: the subject may anticipate that the experimenter will violate the pairing (an oddball or reversal), generating a second-order prediction error. The counter-trap is the subject’s model that “belonging” is provisional, not stable.

Clock: temporal contiguity provides the clock \(\tau_c\). If \(X\) arrives outside the contiguity window—\(\Delta t \gg \tau_c\)—the sign-gestalt is not formed, even when \(B=1\) is instructed. The clock and the belonging operator are independent veto points: belonging without timely contiguity yields \(W=0\); contiguity without belonging yields \(W=0\) [PAGE 410].

The recursive system is therefore vulnerable at three nodes: (1) the clock (\(\tau_c\) exceeded), (2) the belonging operator (regression to habit under crisis), and (3) the counter-model (expectation of contingency violation).
