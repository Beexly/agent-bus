**Three regimes, one clock.**

*(a) Crisis habit regression.* Under relegation pressure a team's behaviour is not a smooth function of its λ; it regresses to a low-variance habit — deeper block, more fouls, more cards, longer ball. In model terms the *variance* of the scoring process contracts before the *mean* does, so a model fitted on mean λ will systematically over-predict the goal count for a team in crisis while under-predicting its red-card rate. The paper's own data hint at this: "If we look at the 2020 season we can see that for 'Crystal Palace' their 'LamHome' is lower than the 'LamAway'" [PAGE 22] — the home-effect sign itself inverts for at least one club, which is a habit/boundary-condition effect, not a home-advantage effect. The corrective operator is a state-conditional λ(θ) with θ = pressure index (points above the drop line, matches remaining), not a season constant.

*(b) Counter-trap.* The moment a Poisson-λ construction becomes the consensus, its residuals become the only remaining information. This is a second-order recursion: your model's output feeds the aggregate forecast, which feeds the observable, which feeds back into your model. The fixed point is a rational-expectations equilibrium in which the naive λ margin carries zero residual signal. Detection: track the out-of-sample decay of the λ-margin's discriminative power across successive forecast cycles; a monotonically shrinking edge with a stable in-sample R² is the signature. The paper's own architecture is vulnerable here — it reports R² of 0.98 in-sample [PAGE 28] with no out-of-sample margin at all, and the one genuine out-of-sample number it produces (0.85 on 20 rows [PAGE 39]) sits inside the sampling noise of a no-skill baseline.

*(c) The clock.* Two different clocks are being conflated. The **match clock** (0–90′) is where red cards act; the **season clock** (rounds 1–38) is where λ drifts and relegation pressure accrues; the **data clock** (the 16/05/2021 snapshot [PAGE 39]) is where the label is censored. A model that fits λ on season-clock aggregates but predicts match-clock events is mis-specified in the time domain. The disciplined formulation makes λ a function of the round index t: λ(t) = λ̄ · exp(γ(t − t̄) + δ·1[pressure]) with the season-clock trend γ identifiable only if t is retained as a feature — which the paper does not do.

**Recursive audit of the paper's own stance.** The paper's epistemic posture is single-loop: fit → score → report.
