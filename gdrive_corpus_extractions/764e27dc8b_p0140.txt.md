<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The debate over the locus of hallucination generation traces back to ancient philosophical dualisms but crystallized in the late 19th century with the advent of experimental psychology. Binet’s reliance on peripheral intensity was heavily influenced by Helmholtzian physiology, which treated the nervous system largely as a passive conduit and processor of external mechanical/optical energies. In this lineage, perception is a reconstruction based strictly on incoming data; thus, a false reconstruction (hallucination) requires false data (peripheral noise or optical tricks like prisms).

James, writing in 1890, acts as a pivot point, absorbing the emerging neuro-pathology of Charcot and the psychical research of the Society for Psychical Research (SPR)—notably the work of Edmund Gurney cited in the text [PAGE 1]. Gurney’s investigation into "Phantasms of the Living" forced a genealogical shift: if dying individuals are seen by distant relatives with accurate details, the "noise" cannot originate from the observer's retina. This forced the theoretical migration from the periphery (the eye) to the center (the cortex/thalamus) and eventually to non-local domains (telepathy/retrocausality).

From a multi-week trap perspective, researchers falling into the "Binet Trap" dismiss any hallucination failing prism duplication as mere imagination or dream-like states, entirely ignoring the clinical reality of fully exteriorized, non-responding hallucinations in sane subjects. Conversely, those falling into the "Psychical Trap" over-index on veridical hallucinations, inventing elaborate spiritualist metaphysics while ignoring the robust biomechanical explanations for central cortical hyperexcitability (e.g., migraine auras, fever-induced delirium) that James also acknowledges. The true genealogical trajectory points toward Predictive Processing theories (e.g., Karl Friston, Andy Clark), where the brain is an active prediction engine, and Binet's "peripheral intensity" is reinterpreted merely as a strong prediction error signal that forces model updating.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The manifestation of a hallucination, particularly the "sporadic... extraordinarily complete" type mentioned by James [PAGE 1], represents a severe failure in the brain's recursive reality-monitoring loop. Normally, the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) tags internally generated imagery with a "self-generated" label, preventing it from entering the stream of exteroceptive perception.

During periods of crisis habit regression—such as profound grief following a sudden death or extreme sleep deprivation—the brain's regulatory systems weaken. The individual enters a state of high emotional arousal and cognitive vulnerability. To cope with the unbearable prediction error of a loved one's absence, the limbic system hijacks the generative modeling network. It actively constructs a highly detailed simulation of the deceased.

This is a counter-trap mechanism: the mind traps itself in a simulated reality to avoid the psychological agony of the actual timeline (the "clock" of irreversible loss). The recursive loop involves:
1. Emotional trigger (Loss/Grief).
2. Generative model activation (Summoning the image/voice of the lost).
3. Reality monitoring failure (The DLPFC fails to apply the 'imagined' tag due to fatigue or overwhelming amygdala drive).
4. Externization (The image is projected into egocentric space, satisfying the craving for contact).

If the hallucination is veridical—containing information about the circumstances of the death—it suggests that the generative model is not drawing solely from memory, but is somehow synchronizing with external data streams, implying a breakdown in the boundary between the self-model and the environment.

*Physical/Biomechanical Veto:* A hallucination cannot violate basic geometric optics if it interacts with the physical environment. If a hallucination remains fixed in space despite prism displacement, it passes the physical veto for peripheral origin, strongly supporting central cortical generation. However, if the subject claims the hallucination *moved* with the prism, it indicates either a highly sophisticated top-down predictive correction or that the stimulus actually had a peripheral component (e.g., a faint phosphene).

*Weather/Environmental Veto:* Sporadic hallucinations often occur at dawn or dusk, or in dimly lit rooms. Low ambient light reduces peripheral $I_{periph}$, dropping the signal-to-noise ratio. This creates a biomechanical vacuum where $S_{central}$ can dominate without being overwritten by strong afferent input. If a reported hallucination occurs under bright, direct sunlight with high visual contrast, a physical veto is triggered: the immense peripheral drive should mathematically swamp any pathological central firing (unless the pathology is a full-blown epileptic storm). Thus, veridical hallucinations occurring in broad daylight demand extraordinary scrutiny or non-local explanations.

*Sensory Coherence Veto:* As derived earlier, cross-modal hallucinations must exhibit precise temporal and semantic alignment. If a subject sees a face and hears a voice, the audio must match the lip movements and identity. A failure in this coherence (e.g., hearing a voice unrelated to the visual figure) breaks the illusion of externality, causing the central projection to collapse back into an internal fantasy.
