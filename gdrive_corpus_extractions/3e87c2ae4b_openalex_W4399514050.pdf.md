**Notation.** Let K ≥ 2 alternatives, forecast vector \(p=(p_1,\dots,p_K)\) with \(p_k \ge 0\), \(\sum_k p_k = 1\), realization \(y \in \{1,\dots,K\}\), one-hot \(o_k = \mathbf{1}\{y=k\}\), and \(N\) forecast rows. All scores below are dimensionless; log scores are reported in nats (natural log). The manual's default orientation is "smaller scores imply better forecasts" [PAGE 2, PAGE 5].

**1. Brier (quadratic) score — two alternatives.** The manual's own hand computation is
\[
S_B(p,y) = (p - a)^2,\qquad a \in \{0,1\},
\]
quoted verbatim as `scores.man <- with(WorldEvents, (forecast - answer)^2)` [PAGE 6]. Range \([0,1]\). Multi-category form, licensed by "The Brier score is obtained for gamma=2" [PAGE 6]:
\[
S_B(p,y) = \sum_{k=1}^{K}(p_k - o_k)^2 = \lVert p - o\rVert_2^2,\qquad \text{range } [0,2].
\]

**2. Logarithmic score.** With natural log,
\[
S_L(p,y) = -\ln p_y,\qquad \text{range } [0,\infty).
\]
The manual fixes the range exactly: "## Raw log scores (0 best, infinity worst)" [PAGE 9] and "the upper bound of the logarithmic score is infinity" [PAGE 8]. **Exact singularity:** the operator diverges as \(p_y \to 0^+\), i.e. \(S_L \to +\infty\) at the boundary \(p_y = 0\). This is not a numerical artifact; it is a boundary blow-up that makes the score non-robust to a single confidently-wrong forecast.

**3. Spherical score.** With \(\lVert p\rVert_2 = (\sum_k p_k^2)^{1/2}\),
\[
S_S(p,y) = \frac{p_y}{\lVert p\rVert_2},\qquad \text{range } \left[\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{K}},\,1\right].
\]
Licensed by "The spherical score is obtained for gamma=2" [PAGE 6]. Unlike the log score, the denominator is bounded away from zero for any probability vector with \(K\) finite, so \(S_S\) has **no interior singularity** — the only excluded point is the zero vector, which is not in the simplex.

**4. Power and pseudospherical families with baseline.** The manual states only the parametric skeleton: `param` supplies "first the family parameter gamma and optionally NCOL(forecast) baseline parameters" [PAGE 4], and the families are "described by Jose et al. (2009)" [PAGE 6]. The verified anchor points are: \(\gamma=2\) with equal baselines \(\to\) Brier, and \(\gamma=2\) with equal baselines \(\to\) spherical for the pseudospherical family [PAGE 6]. **Breakdown regime (removable singularity):**
\[
\lim_{\gamma \to 1} S_\gamma = S_L,\quad\text{yet } S_{\gamma=1} \text{ is undefined,}
\]
verbatim: "The family approaches the log score as gamma goes to 1 (but the family is undefined for gamma=1)" [PAGE 6]. The manual's ε-regularization is `param=1.001` [PAGE 7, PAGE 8], and it offers **no error bound** on \(|S_{1.001} - S_L|\). Any claim of the form "we used the power family to compute log scores" is false at exactly \(\gamma=1\) and approximate at \(\gamma = 1.001\).

**5. Beta family.** Parameterized by \((\alpha,\beta)\) with the two canonical identifications [PAGE 6]: \((\alpha,\beta)=(0,0) \Rightarrow S_L\); \((\alpha,\beta)=(1,1) \Rightarrow S_B\). Provenance: "The beta family was originally proposed by Buja et al. (2005)" [PAGE 6]. The manual prints no closed form; only the two endpoints and the reference.

**6. Strict propriety and the induced divergence.** The defining property, asserted by the package title "Proper Scoring Rules" [PAGE 1] and by the cited "Merkle, E. C. & Steyvers, M. (in press). Choosing a strictly proper scoring rule" [PAGE 6], is: for forecast distributions \(P, Q\) on the same \(K\)-simplex,
\[
\Delta(P,Q) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim Q}\!\left[S(P,Y)\right] - \mathbb{E}_{Y\sim Q}\!\left[S(Q,Y)\right] \;\ge\; 0,
\]
with equality iff \(P = Q\). The divergence is closed-form for the three canonical rules:
\[
\Delta_B(P,Q) = \sum_{k}(p_k - q_k)^2 = \lVert P-Q\rVert_2^2 \quad [\text{probability}^2],
\]
\[
\Delta_L(P,Q) = \mathrm{KL}(Q\,\Vert\,P) = \sum_k q_k \ln\frac{q_k}{p_k}\quad [\text{nats}],
\]
\[
\Delta_S(P,Q) = \lVert Q\rVert_2 - \frac{\langle Q,P\rangle}{\lVert P\rVert_2} \;\ge\; 0 \quad\text{by Cauchy–Schwarz.}
\]
These identities are derived, not quoted; they follow from the range and special-case statements above and are the correct formal content of "strict propriety" for this source.

**7. Weighted aggregation.** With weight vector \(w\) on the row index set,
\[
S_w = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\,(p_i - o_i)^2,\qquad \sum_i w_i = 1,
\]
defaulting to \(w_i = 1/N\) [PAGE 2, PAGE 3]. Units: probability². The weights are a probability measure, so changing \(w\) is a re-weighting of the evaluation population, not a change of the forecast.

**8. Murphy-type decomposition (two alternatives, \(B\) bins).** The manual exposes this as `decomp=TRUE` with `roundto` defaulting to `.1` [PAGE 2, PAGE 3], citing Murphy (1973), Yates (1982), and Young (2010) [PAGE 3]. For bin \(b\) with \(n_b\) members, mean forecast \(\bar p_b\), and observed frequency \(\bar o_b\), and overall base rate \(\bar o\):
\[
\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_b n_b(\bar p_b - \bar o_b)^2}_{\text{REL}} \;-\; \underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_b n_b(\bar o_b - \bar o)^2}_{\text{RES}} \;+\; \underbrace{\bar o(1-\bar o)}_{\text{UNC}} .
\]
All three terms are in probability². **Breakdown regime:** with `roundto = .1` the binary forecast axis supports at most 11 bins, so for a panel of order \(3\times 7 = 21\) rows [PAGE 14: "three world events provided by seven Mechanical Turk users"] the expected bin occupancy is \(\approx 2\), and REL/RES carry \(O(n_b^{-1/2})\) noise that swamps the signal. The decomposition is defined but statistically unstable in exactly the regime the packaged examples use.

**9. Affine bounds operator.** For target bounds \((b_1,b_2)\),
\[
T(S) = b_1 + (b_2-b_1)\,\frac{S - S_{\min}}{S_{\max}-S_{\min}},
\]
**defined only if \(S_{\max} < \infty\).** For the log score \(S_{\max} = \infty\), so \(T\) is undefined — verbatim: "This scaling cannot be accomplished when the scoring rule allows scores of infinity (the log score is the most common case here)" [PAGE 5]. **10. Ordered rules.** "an 'ordered' scoring rule is obtained using the strategy proposed by Jose, Nau, & Winkler (2009). **11. Simplex repair operators.** When rows violate \(\sum_k p_k = 1\), the manual offers `binstyle`: "If equal to 1 (default), the smallest forecast is one minus the sum of the other forecasts.
