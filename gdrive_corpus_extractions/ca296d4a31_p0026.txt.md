<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In Hull’s behaviorist economy, the organism maintains a hierarchy of habits, and under crisis it regresses to earlier, lower-level response patterns when higher-order expectancies fail [PAGE 26]. A recursive theory of mind must model two agents simultaneously: the scientist (or organism) \(A\) who treats the environment as an urn with unknown bias \(\theta_E\), and the environment (or experimental system) \(E\) which, insofar as it contains other adaptive observers, models \(A\)’s deduction rules \(L_A\). When \(A\) observes a streak of empirical agreements, \(A\) recursively updates \(\mathrm{Conf}(L_A)\) and \(\mathrm{Conf}(P_A)\) [PAGE 26]. If a disagreement arrives—a black marble—the recursive loop faces a crisis: regress \(L_A\) to primitive, overlearned inference rules, or revise \(P_A\) to accommodate the anomaly.

The counter-trap operates at the meta-level. After a long run of white marbles, \(A\) may suffer inductive overconfidence, treating \(\frac{\alpha+n}{\alpha+\beta+n}\) as practically indistinguishable from 1 and thereby neglecting the finite-\(n\) residual that Hull insists always remains [PAGE 26]. The observer’s own “dynamic situation” [PAGE 26] is thus subject to the same confirmation dynamics as the theory under test. The clock is the draw index \(n\): each comparison is a tick. On a finite clock horizon \(N\), the maximum achievable confidence is bounded by \(\frac{\alpha+N}{\alpha+\beta+N}\), strictly less than 1. Crisis habit regression in \(A\) is therefore not merely a behavioral reflex but an epistemic failure mode: when the clock shows many confirmations, \(A\) may suppress the revision of \(P_A\) and instead double down on \(L_A\), mistaking streak length for truth. The recursive trap closes when \(E\) models \(A\) as overconfident and deliberately introduces counter-examples, forcing \(A\) into regression precisely when \(A\)’s own model predicts stability.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Theory confirmation in Hull’s architecture [PAGE 26] is adjudicated across five layers, any one of which can veto an update to confidence. 

**Logical layer (\(L\)):** Rules of deduction must be syntactically valid. Veto condition: \(L\) admits an invalid inference rule or is inconsistent (\(\vdash \bot\)). Under explosion, every theorem is derivable, so empirical confirmation carries zero information [PAGE 26].

**Empirical postulate layer (\(P\)):** Dynamic postulates about the environment must be physically possible. Veto condition: \(P\) asserts stationarity of \(\theta\) while environmental sensors register non-stationarity (e.g., drift in reinforcement magnitude across the “long succession” [PAGE 26]).

**Physical layer (urn and marbles):** The drawing mechanism must realize exchangeability. Veto condition: imperfect mixing, marble wear, or electrostatic clustering makes draws path-dependent. High humidity or temperature variation alters marble surface properties and urn interior friction, making \(\theta = \theta(t)\) rather than constant; this is a weather-dependent physical veto because atmospheric conditions change the mechanical randomization Hull assumes when he describes theorems as “thoroughly mixed” and drawn “one at a time” [PAGE 26].

**Biomechanical layer (the drawer):** A human experimenter’s hand is not a perfect randomizer. Motor noise, grip variation, and visual tracking bias introduce serial correlation in the sequence of drawn marbles. Veto condition: a runs test on the draw sequence rejects independence at the pre-specified verification threshold. Fatigue-induced motor drift over a multi-hour session violates the iid assumption required for the Bayesian update above.

**Observational layer:** The act of comparing theorem to empirical fact must be noise-free. Veto condition: measurement error in classifying agreement vs. disagreement (false positive/negative rates) contaminates the likelihood.

Adjudication rule: a theorem \(t\) updates confidence only if \(V(t) = \bigwedge_{\lambda} \mathrm{Pass}_\lambda(t) = \mathrm{true}\). If any layer vetoes, \(\Delta \mathrm{Conf}(L) = \Delta \mathrm{Conf}(P) = 0\) regardless of the length of the agreement streak. This enforces Hull’s caution that probability “frequently becomes very high indeed” but never complete [PAGE 26]; physical, biomechanical, and weather constraints guarantee that the urn is never perfectly closed and the drawer never perfectly impartial, so the theoretical “truth” remains bounded below certainty.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR (Reinforcement Learning via Verifiable Rewards) is instantiated here as an update rule that modifies \(\mathrm{Conf}(L,P)\) only on a reward signal \(r\) independently verifiable against an external observation channel. Three veto scenarios:

**Scenario 1: Non-stationary bias from weather/physical drift.** A sealed reference urn with known \(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is run in parallel with the primary urn. Verifiable condition: the primary urn’s predictive probability \(\frac{\alpha+n}{\alpha+\beta+n}\) diverges from the reference frequency by more than a verification threshold \(\epsilon\). Prediction: after an undetected change point (e.g., humidity shift altering marble adhesion), the Bayesian posterior overestimates \(P(X_{n+1}=1)\), the verified reward signal shows a spike in prediction error, and the RLVR update is vetoed. Confidence remains frozen until physical stationarity is re-established.

**Scenario 2: Logical explosion from inconsistent \(L\).** A syntactic contradiction is injected into the deduction rules \(L\). Verifiable condition: arbitrary, semantically unrelated theorems are derived and empirically “confirmed,” causing \(\mathrm{Conf}(P)\) to rise without empirical discrimination. Prediction: confidence in postulates becomes uncalibrated, approaching 1 despite lacking sound derivations, directly violating Hull’s principle that theoretical truth “must always lack something of being complete” [PAGE 26]. The RLVR reward is withheld because the verified outcome (arbitrary theorem confirmation) contradicts the soundness predicate.

**Scenario 3: Biomechanical draw bias.** Human hand-draws are compared against an automated random-number-driven draw mechanism. Verifiable condition: a runs test on the human-draw sequence rejects serial independence (\(p < \text{verification threshold}\)). Prediction: posterior updates conditioned on human draws will be biased toward recent streaks, overestimating next-agreement probability after runs of white marbles. Because the verified automated sequence shows no such streak dependence, the RLVR reward signal detects a mismatch and vetoes the confidence increment, preserving the finite-\(n\) uncertainty gap that Hull describes [PAGE 26].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
