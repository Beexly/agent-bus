<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of the architecture in [PAGE 1] extends backward through several converging scientific and engineering traditions, while the HRV-with-sleep-detection application (suggested by the source path) pulls those traditions forward into a longitudinal consumer-health context.

Historical/scientific lineage. Distributed computer system 2300 descends from the ARPANET / Internet architectural tradition (Cerf & Kahn, 1974) and the client-server and cluster-computing models of the 1980s–1990s. The patent’s protocol list—TCP/IP, UDP, HTTP, FTP, SNMP, REST, SOAP, CORBA, RMI, DCOM, Web Services [PAGE 1]—is a near-complete inventory of distributed-systems middleware from the 1990s through the 2010s. The inclusion of both legacy (token ring, SS7) and web-native (JSON, REST, SOAP) protocols [PAGE 1] indicates a design written during a transitional era, likely the mid-2010s, when wearable biosensors were migrating from proprietary dongles to cloud-mediated platforms.

HRV science traces to Hon & Lee (1963), who demonstrated fetal distress precedents in beat-to-beat variability, and to the 1996 Task Force of the European Society of Cardiology and North American Society of Pacing and Electrophysiology, which standardized time-domain (SDNN, RMSSD) and frequency-domain HRV metrics. Sleep-detection science descends from Rechtschaffen & Kales (1968) and the AASM manual (2007, updated 2017), which define 30-second epochs and the stage set \{W, N1, N2, N3, R\}. The patent’s generic distributed-computer claim therefore acts as a retrocausal container: a network architecture conceived for general data exchange [PAGE 1] is later inhabited by physiological algorithms whose temporal requirements (bounded jitter, epoch alignment) were not part of the original computer-science specification.

Multi-week traps. Deploying HRV-with-sleep detection over weeks exposes failure modes invisible in single-epoch validation. (1) Baseline nonstationarity: resting HRV exhibits circadian, circadian-amplitude, and week-to-week drift; if p_{w}(X) denotes the feature distribution in week w and p_{w+2}(X) in week w+2, a domain-shift trigger D_{KL}(p_{w} || p_{w+2}) > \epsilon_{\mathrm{recal}} forces recalibration, yet the patent’s “any number of computer systems” [PAGE 1] contains no version-control or model-drift-monitoring clause. (2) Illness and stress confounds: an acute immune challenge suppresses RMSSD for 5–14 days; a system trained only on healthy baseline weeks mislabels recovery as pathology. (3) Firmware and sensor drift: LED current degradation in PPG modules, accelerometer bias instability, and Bluetooth-stack updates alter f_s and noise spectra across weeks; because processor 2310 and interface 2316 are generically specified [PAGE 1], there is no locked sensor chain. (4) Protocol obsolescence: deprecated security (“TSL” likely TLS) or legacy Bluetooth versions in \mathcal{P} [PAGE 1] may lose OS support, breaking the network 2308 path between computer systems 2302/2304/2306 [PAGE 1]. (5) Storage-format rot: “non-transitory storage media” [PAGE 1] persists bits, but not necessarily interpretable semantics; JSON/XML schemas from [PAGE 1] may become unparsable after OS migrations (Windows, macOS, Linux, Solaris listed [PAGE 1]). The multi-week trap is therefore a composite of physiological nonstationarity, hardware aging, and software-dependency rot, all of which sit outside the patent’s abstracted computer-system boundary.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the operator O and the distributed system S as recursively coupled agents. O maintains a mental model M_t of S’s reliability; S maintains an operational threshold \theta_t for alert generation based on observed operator behavior. Under nominal conditions, O trusts automated sleep-stage and HRV outputs. Under crisis—defined as a missed sleep-detection alert, an RMSSD anomaly, or a system outage—O exhibits crisis habit regression: a reversion to manual, habitual verification (e.g., inspecting raw PPG traces, checking the wearable physically, reverting to paper sleep diaries).

Formalization. Let h_t \in [0,1] be habit strength at time t, updated by
h_{t+1} = (1 - \lambda_h) h_t + \lambda_h \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{crisis}_t], \quad \lambda_h \in (0,1),
where \mathbb{I}[\cdot] is the indicator function. As h_t approaches 1, O’s reliance on manual regression increases and trust in S’s automated output decreases. Operator alert sensitivity \alpha_t \in [0,1] decays under sustained crisis:
\alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t - \lambda_{\alpha} h_t + \eta_{\alpha}, \quad \lambda_{\alpha} > 0,
bounded below by \alpha_{\min}. If \alpha_t < \alpha^{\ast} (a critical sensitivity threshold), true physiological anomalies are missed because O dismisses automated alerts as habitual false alarms.

Counter-trap. S may counter O’s regression by raising its own alert threshold \theta_t to reduce false positives during periods of high manual override:
\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t + \lambda_{\theta} \cdot \mathbb{I}[\text{manual override}_t], \quad \lambda_{\theta} > 0.
This is the counter-trap: by suppressing alerts to accommodate operator distrust, S increases the false-negative rate. The recursion closes when S observes O’s increased manual checking as evidence that its own alerts are unreliable, further raising \theta_t, while O observes fewer alerts as evidence of system unreliability, further increasing h_t. A fixed point of this coupled map can reside in a high-habit, high-threshold, low-sensitivity regime where genuine HRV depression or sleep fragmentation goes undetected.

Clock. The physical clock layer mediates the recursion. Let t_{\mathrm{edge}} and t_{\mathrm{cloud}} be timestamps generated by computer system 2302 (edge) and 2306 (cloud) [PAGE 1], with skew \Delta_t = |t_{\mathrm{edge}} - t_{\mathrm{cloud}}|. Sleep-epoch integrity requires \Delta_t \le T_{epoch} = 30 s. If network 2308 latency or OS scheduler jitter (Windows, Linux, macOS, Solaris [PAGE 1]) drives \Delta_t > 30 s, the hypnogram is assigned to the wrong physiological window. O’s crisis habit regression is then triggered not by physiology but by clock desynchronization, and S’s counter-trap misattributes the resulting manual overrides to algorithmic instability rather than timestamp skew. The recursive fixed point is therefore clock-conditioned: stable only when \Delta_t \ll T_{epoch} and \tau_{e2e} < T_{stationarity}.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The output of any HRV-with-sleep-detection pipeline built on the distributed computer system of [PAGE 1] is subject to cross-layer veto. Define three veto layers and a composite veto signal:
V = v_{\mathrm{phys}} \lor v_{\mathrm{bio}} \lor v_{\mathrm{weather}}, \quad V \in \{0,1\}.
If V = 1, the emitted sleep stage \hat{s}_t and HRV metric \hat{m}_t are set to \perp (undefined); no inference is released.

Layer 1 — Physical (sensor-skin and electro-optical). Let Z_c [ohms] be contact impedance between the wearable and skin, and let \sigma_{\mathrm{acc}}^{2} [m^{2} \cdot s^{-4}] be the variance of the triaxial accelerometer signal over an epoch, used as a motion-artifact proxy. Veto conditions:
v_{\mathrm{phys}} = 1 \iff (Z_c > Z_{\max}) \lor (\sigma_{\mathrm{acc}}^{2} > \sigma_{\max}^{2}).
Typical Z_{\max} is device-dependent (order 10^{5}–10^{6} \Omega for dry electrodes); \sigma_{\max}^{2} is set by motion-artifact calibration. Breakdown: during vigorous nocturnal movement, \sigma_{\mathrm{acc}}^{2} spikes, forcing V=1 and blanking the epoch.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical (peripheral perfusion and tissue state). Let PI \in [0,100] be the peripheral perfusion index (%), and T_{\mathrm{skin}} [^\circ C] the skin temperature at the sensing site. Veto condition:
v_{\mathrm{bio}} = 1 \iff (PI < PI_{\min}) \lor (T_{\mathrm{skin}} < T_{\min}).
Vasoconstriction reduces PPG AC/DC ratio, increasing RR-interval extraction error. If PI < PI_{\min} (commonly < 0.5–1.0% in cold extremities), the optical signal-to-noise ratio collapses and \tau_i estimates become biased.

Layer 3 — Weather / ambient environment. Let T_{\mathrm{amb}} [^\circ C] be ambient temperature and RH \in [0,100] relative humidity. Veto condition:
v_{\mathrm{weather}} = 1 \iff (T_{\mathrm{amb}} < T_{\mathrm{amb,min}}) \lor (RH > RH_{\max}) \lor (\text{precipitation} = 1).
Cold ambient temperature drives peripheral vasoconstriction (coupling into Layer 2); high humidity degrades ECG electrode adhesion and optical encapsulation; precipitation (rain, swim, shower) invalidates optical coupling and risks shorting interface devices 2316 [PAGE 1].

Adjudication proof. The veto composition is a monotone Boolean OR: any single layer failure suffices to invalidate the output. This is conservative but necessary because HRV metrics are multiplicative in signal quality: RMSSD \approx f(\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{PPG}}, \Delta t_{\mathrm{jitter}}), and if either SNR or timing is compromised, the statistic is physically meaningless. The patent’s generic interface devices 2316 [PAGE 1] accept input from “external sources” but impose no environmental operating envelope; therefore the veto logic must be supplied externally. Breakdown regime for the veto itself: if P(V=1) over a 7-day window exceeds \eta (e.g., \eta = 0.30), the system has insufficient valid epochs to compute a weekly HRV baseline, and the longitudinal output is vetoed in aggregate.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each scenario specifies a verifiable condition, a counterfactual antecedent, and a formal prediction.

Scenario 1 — Network partition between edge gateway and cloud scorer.
Verifiable condition: network 2308 logs show zero TCP/UDP packets between computer systems 2302 and 2306 for a contiguous interval \Delta t_{\mathrm{part}} [s] [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: had the partition not occurred (\Delta t_{\mathrm{part}} = 0), the sleep epoch at time t would have been transmitted, scored by the distributed system, and stored in storage system 2318 [PAGE 1].
Prediction: if \Delta t_{\mathrm{part}} > T_{epoch} = 30 s, then \hat{s}_t = \perp and N_{\mathrm{eff}} for that hour is reduced by one epoch; the system must veto real-time HRV for the affected window rather than impute. Verifiability: compare packet captures against hypnogram gaps; a mismatch (hypnogram present despite partition) falsifies the veto logic.

Scenario 2 — Processor thermal-throttling-induced deadline miss.
Verifiable condition: processor 2310 (e.g., ARM Cortex or Snapdragon [PAGE 1]) frequency logs show a sustained frequency reduction f_{\mathrm{CPU}}(t) < 0.5 f_{\mathrm{CPU,nominal}} for duration exceeding one QRS-processing cycle [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: had f_{\mathrm{CPU}} remained at nominal, the QRS detector would have completed within the inter-beat interval budget \tau_{\mathrm{IBI}} [s].
Prediction: instruction latency \Phi_i^{-1} increases such that \tau_{\mathrm{proc}} > \tau_{\mathrm{IBI}} - \tau_{\mathrm{sense}} - \tau_{\mathrm{net}}, causing dropped beats, N_{\mathrm{eff}} to fall below N_{\min}, and RMSSD to enter the singularity regime (N \le 1). The system vetoes HRV output for the throttled interval. Verifiability: correlate CPU frequency telemetry with RR-interval gaps in stored storage-system 2318 media [PAGE 1].

Scenario 3 — Storage write failure on non-volatile media.
Verifiable condition: storage system 2318 returns I/O error codes or SMART reallocated-sector events during the write-ahead logging of an encoded sleep epoch [PAGE 1].
Counterfactual: had the write succeeded, the epoch’s RR series \{\tau_i\} and stage label \hat{s}_t would be persistently stored as “encoded signals” on non-transitory media [PAGE 1] for later weekly baseline computation.
Prediction: the missing epoch introduces a gap in the longitudinal time series; if the weekly missing fraction f_{\mathrm{miss}} > 0.05, the weekly SDNN/RMSSD baseline is vetoed (set to \perp) because the sample is not missing at random—storage failures cluster during thermal or power events. Verifiability: cross-reference storage error logs with weekly baseline availability; baselines published despite f_{\mathrm{miss}} > 0.05 falsify the veto rule.
