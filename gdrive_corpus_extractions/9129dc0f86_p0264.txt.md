For a rectangular cross aligned to retinal axes, foreshortening along the slant axis converts the legs into an oblique-legged cross. Units: coordinates in rad or visual-degree units; \(\alpha\) in rad; \(D_w\) in m. Breakdown: if \(D_w\) is unknown or the surface is not planar (e.g., a curved mask), \(H\) cannot be uniquely estimated, and the percept reverts to the retinal shape or to a random distortion. This formalizes James’s claim that “Even an imaginarily inclined wall, in a picture, will, if an after-image be thrown upon it, distort the shape thereof” [PAGE 254].

(3) Bayesian depth inversion for intaglio / hollow-face stimuli. Let \(C \in \{\text{convex},\text{concave}\}\) and let \(\mathbf{y}\) be the cue vector (shading, occlusion, disparity, motion parallax):
\[
P(C\mid\mathbf{y}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{y}\mid C)\,P(C)}
{\sum_{C'\in\{\text{convex},\text{concave}\}} P(\mathbf{y}\mid C')\,P(C')}.
\]
For faces, ecological priors make \(P(\text{convex}) \gg P(\text{concave})\). Even when \(\mathbf{y}\) specifies concave (intaglio profile, hollow mask), the posterior can favor convex unless the likelihood ratio is extreme. Information content of the percept is \(I(C\mid\mathbf{y}) = -\log_2 P(C\mid\mathbf{y})\), unit bits. Breakdown: dynamic cues that change with observer motion can drive \(P(\mathbf{y}\mid\text{concave}) \gg P(\mathbf{y}\mid\text{convex})\), flipping the posterior; the singularity occurs at deterministic cue conflict or when \(P(C)=0\). This captures James’s “irresistibly as a basrelief” and the mask that “looks convex instead of hollow” [PAGE 254].

Source anchor: all verbatim claims above are drawn from [PAGE 254], including the footnote reference to A. W. Volkmann, *Physiologische Untersuchungen im Gebiete der Optik*, v. [PAGE 254]. Cross-references to pp. 231–2 and page 232 appear in the text on [PAGE 254] but those pages are not provided in this source set.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of these phenomena runs from 19th-century German physiological optics into contemporary computational perception. James explicitly credits A. W. Volkmann’s *Physiologische Untersuchungen im Gebiete der Optik*, v. [PAGE 254], placing the after-image distortion and size-distance judgments within the Fechner-Helmholtz-Hering tradition of “unconscious inference.” Helmholtz treated perception as an inference from the retinal image to the most probable distal object; James’s observation that a man approaching does not “sensibly … grow” [PAGE 254] is the phenomenological expression of that inference under the size-distance invariance constraint. The intaglio-to-bas-relief and hollow-mask reversals anticipate Gregory’s top-down, hypothesis-testing model of perception and the modern Bayesian brain: an object-specific prior (faces are convex) can override local depth cues so strongly that the inversion is “irresistible” [PAGE 254].

Historical trap: reading James’s constancy as a timeless, immutable law rather than a calibrated operating point. Multi-week adaptation studies (prism goggles, reversing lenses, distorted environments) show that the calibration constant \(k_s\) and the prior \(P(C)\) are plastic over timescales from minutes to weeks. If one assumes constancy is fixed, one misses the recalibration that follows chronic exposure to altered visual statistics. Counter-trap: concluding from cue-degradation breakdowns (fog, darkness, monocular viewing) that the perceptual system has no geometric commitment at all. The correct intermediate position is that constancy is a default mode with precisely definable failure regimes (Section SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS), not an all-or-none property. James’s text gives the stable endpoint but not the learning curve; that gap is filled by later work on perceptual adaptation across days and weeks.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The observer does not merely perceive the mask or the approaching man; the observer models the perception of others and recursively models that model. In a crisis condition—sensory degradation, time pressure, or conflict between cues—the hierarchical model regresses to habitual priors. For the hollow face, the first-order model says “the shading indicates concave”; the second-order model says “faces are convex, so the first-order model is likely wrong”; the third-order model says “I know you are biased toward convexity, so I will discount your report.” Under crisis, this recursion collapses: the perceiver reverts to the zero-order habit (convex face; constant size) because the computational cost of maintaining higher-order corrections exceeds the available evidence.

Counter-trap: assuming that because recursion collapses under pressure, the system is purely modular and encapsulation-proof. The empirical pattern is instead state-dependent: with abundant cues and time, the observer can sustain recursive corrections (“I know it is a mask, but it looks convex”); with degraded cues or brief exposure, the correction is dropped and the habit regresses. Clock parameter: the time constant of this regression varies by layer. After-image projection onto an inclined surface shifts within seconds as surface slant is re-estimated; size constancy under changing distance operates over hundreds of milliseconds to seconds as \(\hat{D}\) is updated; prior-dominated depth inversion can persist for the duration of steady viewing (“soon appears” [PAGE 254]) but flips rapidly when motion provides disambiguating disparity. Thus the “clock” of perceptual decision-making has multiple hands, and a crisis in one layer does not necessarily reset the others.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers must jointly pass for James’s described constancy and inversion phenomena to hold; failure in any layer triggers a veto.

Physical/optical layer: Retinal irradiance is governed by
\[
L_r = L_o \, T(\lambda,D) \, \cos\beta,
\]
where \(L_o\) is source radiance (unit \(\text{W}\,\text{m}^{-2}\,\text{sr}^{-1}\)), \(T\) is atmospheric transmittance (dimensionless), \(D\) is distance (m), and \(\beta\) is the angle of incidence (rad). Weather (fog, haze, glare) reduces \(T\) and contrast \(C = (L_{\max}-L_{\min})/(L_{\max}+L_{\min})\), dimensionless. When \(C\) falls below the contrast threshold (\(C_{th} \approx 10^{-2}\) to \(5\times10^{-2}\)), distance cues become unreliable and \(\hat{D}\) is vetoed.

Biomechanical layer: Binocular distance from disparity uses interpupillary baseline \(b \approx 0.062\) m:
\[
D = \frac{b}{2\tan(\phi/2)},
\]
where \(\phi\) is the vergence angle (rad). Extraocular muscle dynamics and accommodation set the allowable \(\phi\) range. Strabismus, paralysis, or monocular viewing removes disparity, forcing reliance on monocular cues; if those are ambiguous (a flat picture, a featureless wall), the homography \(H\) for after-image projection and the size-distance equation both lose their distance anchor.

Weather veto proof: Fog does not merely add noise; it selectively attenuates high spatial frequencies and reduces the effective information rate about \(D\). If the physical layer cannot deliver sufficient contrast to estimate \(\hat{D}\), the perceptual system cannot compute \(\hat{s}=k_s\theta\hat{D}\) and reverts to angular size \(\theta\). Likewise, if atmospheric scattering washes out the shading gradient on an intaglio profile, the likelihood \(P(\mathbf{y}\mid\text{concave})\) weakens, allowing the convex-face prior to dominate unopposed. Thus a veto in the weather or physical layer cascades into the perceptual layer: constancy fails, depth inversion strengthens, and after-image distortion becomes unanchored. James’s phenomena therefore hold only within the intersection of viable optical transmission, intact oculomotor geometry, and sufficient cue information; outside that intersection, the equations hit their singularities and the illusions either break or default to retinal-report mode.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-via-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios. A model is vetoed if its counterfactual prediction fails the stated observable condition.

Scenario 1 — Hollow-face rotation under dynamic disparity. Setup: A concave mask of physical depth \(d=0.05\) m rotates at \(\omega = 30^\circ/\text{s}\) at distance \(D=1.0\) m; binocular disparity and motion parallax are available. Veto condition: any model predicting that the convexity prior holds for the entire rotation interval is vetoed. Prediction: the percept flips to concave within \(\Delta t \in [0.5,2.0]\) s after rotation begins, measurable by continuous button-press or perceptual report. The timing depends on the rate at which dynamic disparity likelihood \(P(\mathbf{y}_t\mid\text{concave})\) accumulates relative to the static prior \(P(\text{convex})\). If a model reports no flip, it fails RLVR.

Scenario 2 — After-image homography on a slanted, edgeless surface. Setup: Fixate a cross for 60 s to induce an after-image of retinal size \(\theta_r = 2.0^\circ\); project onto a matte wall slanted by \(\alpha = 30^\circ\) that fills the visual field with no border or texture edges. Veto condition: a model predicting preservation of the retinal rectangular shape is vetoed. Prediction: the after-image appears as an oblique-legged cross with foreshortening consistent with \(H(\alpha,D_w)\), adjustable via a matching task to aspect ratio \(r \approx \cos\alpha\) (modulo perceived \(D_w\)). Verifiable by having the subject adjust a comparison cross until it matches the after-image. If the model predicts no shear or oblique legs, it is vetoed.

Scenario 3 — Size constancy under atmospheric scattering. Setup: A sphere of physical diameter \(s_0=0.30\) m is viewed through fog with transmittance \(T=e^{-\sigma D}\), \(\sigma=0.1\,\text{m}^{-1}\), at distances \(D\in\{5,10,20\}\) m. Veto condition: any model predicting \(\hat{s}=s_0\) independent of \(\sigma D\) is vetoed. Prediction: because contrast loss degrades the estimate \(\hat{D}\), perceived diameter will compress approximately as \(\hat{s}(D) \approx k_s\,\theta(D)\,\hat{D}(D)\) with \(\hat{D}\) under-estimated as \(T\) falls; magnitude estimation yields \(\hat{s}<s_0\) with error increasing monotonically in optical depth \(\sigma D\). Verifiable via direct magnitude estimation or size-matching against a reference at the same distance in clear air. If the model outputs perfect constancy under fog, it fails RLVR.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
