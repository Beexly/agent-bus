Differentiation is the corollary: expectations about sequences necessarily entail differentiation among alternatives—“this” is not “that.” Maze learning therefore demonstrates both that an alley leads to food and simultaneously that this alley is differentiated from other alleys and food from non-food; “Prediction and differentiation thus necessarily, go hand in hand” ([PAGE 188]). Distance completes the triad by introducing shortness/longness across three incommensurate media: space distance, time-distance, and force-resistance distance ([PAGE 188]). The organism must prefer “short” routes to “long” routes and discriminate the shorter from the longer ([PAGE 188]).

Multi-hop derivation: Tolman’s sign-gestalt expectancy is a proto-representation of state–transition probabilities ℰ(s′|s,a). Differentiation is a total-variation or discriminability condition between successor distributions. Distance is a path-cost functional d over trajectory space. These three operators jointly define a means-end field: the animal selects a path π* = argmin_π d(π) subject to ℰ(goal|π) > θ and differentiability D(π_i, π_j) > δ. The modern lineage runs from Tolman’s latent-learning experiments (Tolman & Honzik, 1930) through the cognitive map (O’Keefe & Nadel, 1978), hippocampal place cells (O’Keefe & Dostrovsky, 1971), successor representations (Dayan, 1993; Gershman et al., 2014), and model-based/model-free arbitration in reinforcement learning (Daw et al., 2005). Each hop preserves Tolman’s core claim—that behavior is governed by expectancies about distal consequences rather than by direct stimulus–response stamping-in—but each also transforms the ontology: Tolman’s “sign gestalt” becomes a distributed hippocampal–prefrontal code, his “force-resistance distance” becomes an effort-cost term in a biophysical control model, and his “molar” threshold becomes the boundary between habitual and deliberative choice.

Let the behavioral state space be \mathcal{X} with Borel \sigma-algebra, and let actions a \in \mathcal{A}. Define the sign-gestalt readiness operator
\[
\sigma: \mathcal{X} \to [0,1], \qquad \sigma(x) = \Pr(\text{organism is prepared to engage } x),
\]
dimensionless, bounded by zero and one. Define the expectancy (successor) operator
\[
\mathcal{E}(x' \mid x,a) \in \Delta(\mathcal{X}),
\]
a probability kernel over next states with units of probability density (states\(^{-1}\)). Sequence composition over k steps is the k-fold Chapman–Kolmogorov propagation
\[
\mathcal{E}^{(k)}(x_k \mid x_0,a_{0:k-1}) = \int_{\mathcal{X}^{k-1}} \prod_{t=1}^{k} \mathcal{E}(x_t \mid x_{t-1},a_{t-1}) \, dx_1 \cdots dx_{k-1}.
\]
Tolman’s “this leads to that” ([PAGE 188]) is precisely \mathcal{E}^{(k)}(\text{food} \mid \text{alley}_i) > 0.

Differentiation between alternatives i and j is formalized as a total-variation separation of successor distributions:
\[
D_{\mathrm{TV}}(i,j) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathcal{X}} \left| \mathcal{E}(x' \mid i) - \mathcal{E}(x' \mid j) \right| dx' \geq \delta,
\]
where \delta > 0 is the organism’s discriminability threshold. Tolman’s claim that the rat differentiates “this alley” from others and food from non-food ([PAGE 188]) requires D_{\mathrm{TV}} to exceed perceptual noise; below \delta the means-end field collapses because alternatives become indistinguishable.

Distance is a path functional over three media (space, time, force-resistance) ([PAGE 188]):
\[
d(\pi) = w_s \, d_{\mathrm{space}}(\pi) + w_t \, d_{\mathrm{time}}(\pi) + w_F \, d_{\mathrm{force}}(\pi),
\]
with path \pi: [0,1] \to \mathcal{X}. Space distance has SI units of meters:
\[
d_{\mathrm{space}}(\pi) = \int_0^1 \left\| \frac{d\pi(u)}{du} \right\|_2 du \quad [\mathrm{m}].
\]
Time-distance has SI units of seconds:
\[
d_{\mathrm{time}}(\pi) = \int_0^1 \frac{1}{v(\pi(u))} \left\| \frac{d\pi(u)}{du} \right\|_2 du \quad [\mathrm{s}],
\]
where v is local locomotion speed. Force-resistance distance is mechanical work against resistance, SI units of joules:
\[
d_{\mathrm{force}}(\pi) = \int_0^1 \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{res}}(\pi(u)) \cdot \frac{d\pi(u)}{du} \, du \quad [\mathrm{J}].
\]
The weights w_s, w_t, w_F are dimensionless exchange coefficients that Tolman does not specify ([PAGE 188]); their misspecification is a primary source of model breakdown.

The means-end optimum is
\[
\pi^* = \arg\min_{\pi \in \Pi(x_0,g)} d(\pi) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathcal{E}(g \mid \pi) > \theta \;\land\; D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\pi_i,\pi_j) > \delta.
\]

Breakdown and singularity regimes:
1. Novelty singularity: \mathcal{E}(x' \mid x,a) is undefined for states never visited; the argmin has no basis. Tolman’s readiness condition fails exactly here because no sign gestalt has been established ([PAGE 188]).
2. Non-stationarity: if substrate friction or incline changes mid-path, d_{\mathrm{force}} is path-history dependent and d ceases to be a metric; the “shortest” route computed pretrial is invalid.
3. Differentiation collapse: when D_{\mathrm{TV}}(i,j) < \sigma_{\mathrm{perc}} (perceptual noise floor), the animal cannot discriminate “shorter” from “longer” ([PAGE 188]), violating Tolman’s necessary condition for adequate behavior.
4. Goal devaluation: if the current value of g is zero (satiety), the constraint \mathcal{E}(g \mid \pi) > \theta may fail while \sigma(\pi) remains nonzero, decoupling expectancy from habit.

Tolman’s 1932 page ([PAGE 188]) is a node in a long causal chain that runs backward and forward in time. Backward: Tolman’s “sign gestalt” inherits from Gestalt psychology (Köhler, Koffka) and from the American functionalist tradition (James, Dewey), in which behavior is organized by relations among objects rather than by isolated reflexes. Forward: the same year’s latent-learning demonstrations (Tolman & Honzik, 1930) showed that rats build expectancies without reinforcement, directly contradicting Hullian drive-reduction orthodoxy (Hull, 1943), which would reduce all “leading to” relations to drive stimulus–response stamping-in. Tolman’s 1948 “cognitive map” paper formalizes the means-end field as a spatial representation, which O’Keefe and Dostrovsky (1971) instantiated neurophysiologically as hippocampal place cells and O’Keefe and Nadel (1978) expanded into a full cognitive-map theory. The computational successor appears in Dayan’s successor representation (1993), in which the agent learns a discounted future-state occupancy matrix—essentially Tolman’s \mathcal{E}^{(k)}—and in Gershman et al.’s (2014) successor representation as a unifying account of hippocampal and prefrontal function. The modern reinforcement-learning arbitration literature (Daw et al., 2005; Lee et al., 2014) splits control into a model-based system (Tolman’s expectancy/differentiation/distance optimization) and a model-free system (cached values), with a volatility-dependent switch between them.

Multi-week traps arise when a single-session maze result is projected onto extended training. Trap A: asymptotic short-route preference. Tolman’s organism “must prefer short routes to long routes” ([PAGE 188]), but over weeks the exploration bonus decays nonlinearly and habits cache the first-reinforced path even if it is longer; an experimenter who checks only Week 1 will falsely conclude the animal optimizes distance. Trap B: consolidation compression. Time-distance d_{\mathrm{time}} is not experienced uniformly across days; sleep-dependent replay compresses temporal intervals, so the effective k in \mathcal{E}^{(k)} changes across the multi-week arc, violating stationarity. Trap C: resistance learning. Force-resistance distance d_{\mathrm{force}} requires metabolic experience; a rat cannot know that a geometrically short path is energetically costly until it has traversed it repeatedly, so multi-week data will show a lagged shift away from the short path that a one-trial model cannot predict.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression: under acute stress, threat, or fatigue, the organism regresses from model-based optimization (Tolman’s means-end field) to model-free habit. If a rat has learned two alleys to food, one short and one long, and the short alley suddenly becomes aversive (bright light, loud noise), the crisis mode suppresses \mathcal{E}-based recomputation and the animal may continue down the long, previously reinforced habitual route despite its known suboptimality. This is the regression of purposive behavior into prepotent response; the “molar” capacity ([PAGE 188]) is bypassed by a subcortical salience map.

Counter-trap: the observer’s theory-of-mind error. Because Tolman describes the rat as having expectancies that “this” leads to food and that “this” is not “that” ([PAGE 188]), the experimenter may attribute declarative-like knowledge to the animal. The counter-trap is that the rat’s choice is actually controlled by proximal stimulus gradients—local olfactory intensity, wall texture, egocentric bearing—rather than by the abstract distance functional d(\pi). The observer sees “preference for the short route” and infers means-end reasoning, when the behavior may be explained by a simple proximity heuristic with no representation of alternatives or differentiation.

Clock: time-distance imposes a temporal deadline on the means-end field. If food availability is gated by a closing deadline T_c, the effective cost becomes
\[
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(\pi) = d_{\mathrm{space}}(\pi) + \lambda \, [t(\pi) - T_c]_+,
\]
where \lambda is the penalty rate and [z]_+ = \max(z,0). As T_c approaches, the organism’s subjective time-distance undergoes hyperbolic discounting,
\[
D_{\mathrm{subj}}(t) = \frac{1}{1 + \kappa t},
\]
so the “short” spatial route loses its advantage and the animal switches to the nearest available alley, even if it is a dead end. The clock thus collapses Tolman’s static hierarchy of sequence–differentiation–distance into a single urgency variable, and any prediction that ignores \kappa and T_c fails at the deadline boundary.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

The means-end field prediction \pi^* = \arg\min d(\pi) is valid only if every physical layer that contributes to d remains stationary and accessible over the behavioral timescale. A single layer violation vetoes the prediction.

Physical layer (substrate mechanics). Force-resistance distance d_{\mathrm{force}} = \int \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{res}} \cdot d\pi depends on the coefficient of friction \mu and normal load N: \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{res}} = \mu N \,\hat{\tau}. If the maze floor is changed from rubber to polished steel between pretraining and test, \mu drops and d_{\mathrm{force}} for all paths is rescaled non-uniformly because path curvature changes normal load on banked turns. The previously optimal short route may cease to minimize d, vetoing the spatial-shortness prediction.

Biomechanical layer (morphological constraint). Tolman’s organism must differentiate “shorter” from “longer” routes ([PAGE 188]), but the set of admissible paths \Pi(x_0,g) is constrained by body size L_{\mathrm{body}} and limb range of motion. A geometric shortcut through an aperture of width w < L_{\mathrm{body}} is in \Pi_{\mathrm{geometric}} but not in \Pi_{\mathrm{admissible}}; including it in the argmin produces a physically unrealizable prediction. The veto condition is
\[
\exists \pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{geometric}} : \min_{u} w(\pi(u)) < L_{\mathrm{body}} \implies \pi \notin \Pi_{\mathrm{admissible}}.
\]

Weather layer (odor and thermoregulation). If the goal is located olfactorily, time-distance d_{\mathrm{time}} is mediated by plume diffusion D_{\mathrm{odor}}(T,H), which depends on ambient temperature T and relative humidity H. Rising T increases evaporation and turbulent dispersion, shortening the time to detect the goal; falling H desiccates the rat’s olfactory mucosa, reducing sensitivity. The veto condition is
\[
\left| \frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{odor}}}{\partial T} \Delta T + \frac{\partial D_{\mathrm{odor}}}{\partial H} \Delta H \right| > \epsilon_{\mathrm{detect}},
\]
where \epsilon_{\mathrm{detect}} is the minimum detectable change in arrival-time expectancy. When this inequality holds, the animal’s \mathcal{E}(g \mid \pi) shifts independently of path length, invalidating the distance-based optimum.

Adjudication rule: the means-end field holds only on the intersection of all three admissible sets; if any layer vetoes, the prediction reverts to a layer-specific policy (friction-aware gait, morphology-aware path planning, or odor-guided search) rather than Tolman’s unified short-route preference.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Breakdown regime: if P(B) > 0.8 on the first post-manipulation trial without prior traversal of A, the animal is using a visual/cognitive estimate of resistance rather than learned d_{\mathrm{force}}, violating the assumption that force-resistance distance must be acquired through commerce with the object ([PAGE 188]).
