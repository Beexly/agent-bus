**Notation and units.** World coordinates \(W=(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^3\) are in meters; camera coordinates \(C_i\) are in meters; pixel coordinates \(U_i=(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^2\) are in pixels; frame index \(t\) is dimensionless; sequence length \(T\) is in frames; frame rate is in \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). BEV queries are \(Q\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}\), where \(H,W\) are grid dimensions and \(C\) is a channel count with no physical unit. ReID features are in \(\mathbb{R}^{256}\) [PAGE 6]. HOTA, DetA, AssA, LocA, IDF1, MOTA, MOTP, MT, ML, and mAP are dimensionless proportions or percentages. Cosine distance is dimensionless. Confidence scores are dimensionless probabilities.

**O1 — Pinhole projection.** The source defines
\[
s\begin{bmatrix}u\\1\end{bmatrix}
=P_i\begin{bmatrix}x\\1\end{bmatrix}
=K_i[R_i|t_i]_{3\times4}\begin{bmatrix}x\\1\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \(s\) is the scale factor, \(P_i\) is the projection matrix, \(K_i\) is the intrinsic matrix, and \(R_i,t_i\) are rotation and translation [PAGE 3]. The paper also flags the motivating physical failure: “the integration of 3D spatial information from multiple 2D views requires sophisticated algorithms to handle camera calibration errors and perspective distortions” [PAGE 1]. Assumptions: static calibrated cameras, known intrinsics and extrinsics, pinhole approximation, and nonzero scale. Breakdown regimes: \(s=0\) or rank-deficient \(P_i\) makes projection singular; points at the camera center are undefined; calibration drift or wrong extrinsics induces systematic BEV displacement; strong lens distortion violates the linear pinhole model. The source does not claim to solve uncalibrated cameras.

**O2 — Spatial cross-attention.** The BEV aggregation operator is
\[
\mathrm{SCA}(Q_p,F_t)
=\frac{1}{|V_{\mathrm{hit}}|}
\sum_{i\in V_{\mathrm{hit}}}
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ref}}}
\mathrm{Attn}\!\left(Q_p,P(p,i,j),F_t^i\right),
\]
where \(Q_p\in\mathbb{R}^{C}\) is the BEV query at \(p=(x,y)\), \(V_{\mathrm{hit}}\) is the set of camera views whose projected 2D reference points fall inside the image, and \(P(p,i,j)\) projects the \(j\)-th 3D reference point to the \(i\)-th camera [PAGE 3]. The source says, “We adopt the spatial cross-attention (SCA) [18] for this aggregation” [PAGE 3]. Units: \(Q_p\) and \(F_t^i\) are feature vectors; \(\mathrm{Attn}\) is a dimensionless attention weight applied to feature values. Breakdown regimes: \(|V_{\mathrm{hit}}|=0\) makes the average undefined; \(N_{\mathrm{ref}}=0\) yields no sampled evidence; all reference points outside all fields of view produce no support; severe calibration error projects BEV queries to the wrong image regions. The paper’s adaptation re-designs these layers for a dynamic number of cameras and removes camera embeddings [PAGE 6], but the operator still requires valid calibration.

**O3 — Temporal self-attention.** The temporal encoder uses
\[
\mathrm{TSA}(Q_p,Q,B_{t-1})
=\sum_{S\in\{Q,B_{t-1}\}}
\mathrm{Attn}(Q_p,p,S),
\]
where \(Q_p\) is the BEV query at \(p=(x,y)\) and \(B_{t-1}\) is the previous BEV feature history [PAGE 3]. Units: BEV features are dimensionless tensors; temporal index is frames. Breakdown regimes: at \(t=1\) there is no prior \(B_{t-1}\), so the operator degenerates to current-frame evidence; if camera synchronization is wrong, \(B_{t-1}\) is temporally misaligned; if the scene changes faster than the temporal window, the prior becomes stale.

**O4 — 2D–3D association cost.** The matching operator is
\[
c_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
\lambda\left\|\hat b_i^{3D}-\hat b_j^{2D}\right\|_2^2,
& \mathrm{IOU}(b_i^{3D},b_j^{2D})\ge 0.1,\\
+\infty, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
where \(\hat b^{2D}\) is the bottom center of the 2D box, \(\hat b^{3D}\) is the bottom center of the projected 3D area, and
\[
\lambda=\mathbf{1}(v_i^{3D}\ge v_j^{2D})+\alpha\mathbf{1}(v_i^{3D}<v_j^{2D}),\qquad \alpha>1.
\]
The source says, “we compute a cost matrix between 2D detections and projected 3D detections” [PAGE 4]. Units: \(c_{ij}\) is squared pixels when bottom centers are in pixels; \(\lambda\) is dimensionless; IOU is dimensionless; \(+\infty\) is a sentinel, not a physical distance. Breakdown regimes: if no pair satisfies the IOU gate, the Hungarian problem sees all-infinite costs and can produce no valid assignment; the fixed IOU threshold \(0.1\) can reject valid small or heavily occluded objects; the bottom-center term assumes the 2D and projected 3D bottom centers correspond; the penalty factor \(\alpha\) is a robustness prior, not a calibrated probability. The source also reports CircleNMS with \(\delta=0.2\,\mathrm{m}\) and a cost threshold \(\Delta=150\) [PAGE 11]. Also, “a 3D detection will be removed if it cannot be matched with any 2D detections from all camera views” [PAGE 4], which is a hard physical veto on unsupported 3D detections.

**O5 — GNN message passing.** The graph update rules are
\[
h_{(i,j)}^{(l)}
=N_e\!\left(\left[h_i^{(l-1)},h_j^{(l-1)},h_{(i,j)}^{(l-1)}\right]\right),
\]
\[
m_{(i,j)}^{(l)}
=N_v\!\left(\left[h_i^{(l-1)},h_{(i,j)}^{(l)}\right]\right),
\]
\[
h_i^{(l)}
=\Phi\!\left(\left\{m_{(i,j)}^{(l)}\right\}_{j\in N_i}\right),
\]
where \(N_e,N_v\) are learnable functions, \(N_i\) is the neighbor set of node \(i\), and \(\Phi\) is summation, maximum, or average [PAGE 5]. Units: node and edge embeddings are dimensionless feature vectors; geometry inputs are in meters; ReID cosine distances are dimensionless. Breakdown regimes: a node with \(N_i=\varnothing\) makes the aggregation set empty; disconnected graphs cannot propagate identity evidence; graph pruning that removes true edges creates irreversible association loss; the final linear program requires single-edge-in and single-edge-out integrity, and infeasibility or ambiguous costs can force identity switches [PAGE 5].

**O6 — 3D geometry embedding.** For an edge between nodes \(i\) and \(j\), the source encodes
\[
(x_i-x_j,\; y_i-y_j,\; z_i-z_j),
\]
where coordinates are 3D centers in meters [PAGE 5]. The paper notes that “geometric distances in 3D are not impacted by projection distortions and camera distance scaling” [PAGE 5]. Breakdown regimes: 3D center errors from bad calibration, missed detections, or merged detections corrupt the edge feature; scale ambiguity is avoided only if calibration and world coordinates are correct; the model intentionally omits motion features, with the source stating it “opted for a more efficient graph formulation without motion features but with larger input time spans” [PAGE 5]. That is a design tradeoff, not a claim that motion is never informative.

**O7 — Global block and near-online inference.** The paper introduces “a novel global tracking layer that replaces handcrafted heuristics required to run the GNN in a sliding window fashion [5] with a model layer that operates globally over the video sequence and matches incoming objects with past tracks” [PAGE 2]. It also states, “The global block shares weights with the previous hierarchy level and thus requires no additional training” [PAGE 6]. Units: window length and sequence length are in frames; the block has no new physical unit. Breakdown regimes: if the global memory is corrupted by a bad past track, the error can persist across the full sequence; if appearance and 3D geometry are both ambiguous, global matching has no calibrated uncertainty guarantee in the reported metrics; the block is near-online, not a proof of unbounded occlusion robustness.

**O8 — Metrics.** HOTA is computed on 3D locations, with Euclidean distances converted to similarity scores using a zero-distance parameter, then combined into LocA, DetA, and AssA [PAGE 6]. The reported headline values are 81.22 HOTA on AICity’24 [PAGE 7] and 95.6 IDF1 on WildTrack for MCBLT\(^\dagger\) [PAGE 7]. These are dimensionless percentages. The metrics do not by themselves prove physical correctness; they are dataset-conditioned scores.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from classical multi-view detection through projection-based deep aggregation to early-fusion MTMC tracking and then to hierarchical graph tracking. The paper places classical methods in conditional random fields, probabilistic modeling, and related aggregation schemes [PAGE 2], then moves to MVDet, which projects dense 2D features to a unified ground plane and predicts a pedestrian occupancy map, but cannot easily handle different camera settings [PAGE 2]. BEVFormer supplies the spatio-temporal transformer and deformable attention used here [PAGE 2–3]. EarlyBird is the immediate predecessor for early multi-view aggregation in BEV, but its detector is tied to a fixed multi-camera scene, and its tracker follows FairMOT-style ReID plus a Kalman filter [PAGE 3]. The source is blunt: EarlyBird’s tracking “merely relies on a heuristic Kalman filter, which is prone to drifting and struggles with long-term occlusion handling” [PAGE 2]. MCBLT’s historical move is therefore not just a new detector; it replaces the late-association and heuristic-tracking lineage with a 3D graph hierarchy.

The GNN genealogy is equally important. The graph formulation descends from message-passing MOT and network-flow identity consistency [PAGE 5]. SUSHI is the direct ancestor: it uses hierarchical GNNs and overlapping sliding windows with handcrafted stitching [PAGE 5]. MCBLT extends SUSHI to 3D and removes overlaps by using a global block. The source calls this “the first hierarchical GNN-based 3D multi-object tracking method and a novel global tracking block that unlocks long-term associations across thousands of frames” [PAGE 2]. The retrocausal reading is that the earlier window heuristics were not neutral engineering details; they capped the occlusion horizon. The ablation proves this retroactively: increasing the heuristic window from 480 to 960 to 1920 to 3840 frames improves HOTA from 51.87 to 62.50 to 71.88 to 76.80, and then the global block reaches 81.22 [PAGE 8]. The paper states, “Our global merging block increases overall performance by +4.42 HOTA by scaling the association window to the full length of the video” [PAGE 8]. That is a clean genealogical diagnosis: the old heuristic was a horizon limit, and the new global layer exposes it.

There are multi-week traps in this lineage. First, coordinate-origin placement is a trap: the source reports that “re-centering can dramatically improve the detection performance by +22.33 mAP, especially for those objects farther from the origin” [PAGE 11]. A team can spend weeks tuning a detector while the real defect is a corner-origin BEV coordinate system. Second, ReID crop quality is a trap: the projected 3D boxes are larger than the actual objects [PAGE 3], and without 2D–3D association, WildTrack IDF1 falls from 93.4 to 63.2 [PAGE 12]. Third, the long-sequence trap is severe: the BEV-KF baseline falls from 63.49 HOTA at 1,000 frames to 20.15 at 23,994 frames, while MCBLT falls from 95.09 to 90.51 [PAGE 8]. The source summarizes that MCBLT “only drops by 4.58 HOTA points compared to the massive performance decrease of 43.35 utilizing only a Kalman filter” [PAGE 8]. Fourth, synthetic-to-real transfer is a data trap: WildTrack has only 400 frames and no 3D bounding boxes, so the paper uses default box dimensions and zero rotation [PAGE 6]; pre-training on AICity’24 improves WildTrack detection by \(+3.67\) mAP [PAGE 11]. Fifth, camera-count scaling is a memory trap: Table 7 shows reduced maximum camera counts for larger backbones under about 80 GB GPU memory [PAGE 11]. These are the practical genealogy lessons: calibration, crop association, sequence horizon, domain shift, and memory all precede any headline metric.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In a recursive theory-of-mind reading, the tracker maintains a belief over object identity across time, and the evaluator must model what the tracker could have known at each clock tick. The “clock” is the sequence horizon: SUSHI used 512-frame graphs versus MCBLT’s full sequence [PAGE 5], AICity’24 reaches 24k frames with occlusions up to 2k frames [PAGE 6], and the global block is explicitly designed to associate “incoming objects with past tracks” over the entire video [PAGE 2]. As the clock advances, the tracker’s belief cannot be refreshed by observation during occlusion, so it must rely on prior 3D geometry and multi-view ReID. The paper’s design reflects this: 3D geometry is trusted because it is not affected by projection distortion and camera distance scaling [PAGE 5], while appearance is averaged across all cameras in which the object is observable [PAGE 5]. That is a belief-update policy, not a mere feature choice.

The crisis habit regression appears when the clock exceeds the heuristic horizon. The BEV-KF baseline is the “habit” case: it is an online Kalman-filter tracker attached to the same 3D detections. Table 6 shows that this habit works at short horizons, with 63.49 HOTA at 1,000 frames, but collapses to 20.15 at 23,994 frames, while MCBLT degrades to 90.51 [PAGE 8]. The source’s own contrast is that MCBLT drops 4.58 HOTA points versus a 43.35-point decrease for the Kalman filter [PAGE 8]. The recursive failure is that the tracker’s belief does not “know” it has lost the object; it continues with a stale motion prior, and identity consistency is destroyed. The paper’s criticism of the predecessor is exactly this: the Kalman filter “is prone to drifting and struggles with long-term occlusion handling” [PAGE 2].

The counter-trap is the opposite error: a global block can become overconfident. The paper removes handcrafted window stitching and uses a learned global merge that shares weights and needs no additional training [PAGE 5–6]. That reduces bias from heuristics, but it also means the global layer is not a separately calibrated uncertainty model. If two people have similar ReID features and close 3D positions, the global block can create a long-range false association. The reported aggregate metrics argue against widespread failure: AICity’24 AssA is 76.19 and WildTrack IDF1 is 93.4 or 95.6 [PAGE 7], and the global block improves AssA from 68.22 to 76.19 over the 3840-frame heuristic [PAGE 8]. But the source does not report per-identity false-merge rates or identity-switch audits. The recursive theory-of-mind conclusion is therefore conditional: the global block better models “the same person persists across long occlusion,” but it does not prove “the model knows when it does not know.” A counter-trap veto is appropriate for any claim of calibrated identity uncertainty.

The third pole is the operational clock. The end-to-end system runs around 1.5 FPS before optimization, with BEVFormer at 1.6 FPS, DINO at 65.0 FPS, SOLIDER at 58.9 FPS, and SUSHI at 452.7 FPS [PAGE 13]. The paper says 2D detection, ReID, and tracking “can operate in parallel with BEVFormer so that their running time is negligible” [PAGE 13]. In near-online operation, the belief update horizon and the latency budget are coupled: a longer global memory improves identity retention but increases dependence on old, possibly wrong, beliefs. The correct recursive model is thus triadic: crisis habit regression favors short-horizon Kalman continuity, counter-trap favors long-horizon global merging, and the clock determines which failure dominates. The source supports the tradeoff but does not eliminate either failure mode.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is a condition under which a lower-layer physical or measurement failure invalidates a higher-layer tracking claim. The source supports several such vetoes and leaves one unsupported.

**Physical/calibration veto.** The projection operator is the foundation. If \(P_i\) is wrong, SCA samples the wrong image regions, and the 3D box is geometrically wrong. The source’s re-centering experiment is direct proof of the physical-layer veto: baseline WildTrack mAP is 66.03, re-centering raises it to 88.36, and pre-training raises it to 92.03 [PAGE 11]. The paper says re-centering “can dramatically improve the detection performance by +22.33 mAP” [PAGE 11]. Therefore any claim that BEV origin placement or scene coordinate normalization is immaterial is vetoed. Similarly, if \(|V_{\mathrm{hit}}|=0\), the SCA average is undefined [PAGE 3], so a camera-coverage failure vetoes the BEV feature claim.

**Physical/detection-support veto.** The source states that “a 3D detection will be removed if it cannot be matched with any 2D detections from all camera views” [PAGE 4]. This is a hard physical-evidence veto: no 2D support means no retained 3D detection. The IOU gate \(0.1\) and cost threshold \(\Delta=150\) are the implemented boundaries [PAGE 4, PAGE 11]. If all pairwise costs are \(+\infty\), the Hungarian assignment has no feasible match, and the 3D detection is discarded. That regime is not a model-confidence issue; it is a geometric support failure.

**Biomechanical veto.** On WildTrack, the paper removes dimension and orientation regression losses, uses a default 3D box dimension of \([0.6,0.6,1.7]\) meters and zero rotation [PAGE 6], and visualizes tracks as poles defined by 3D foot points and a pre-defined human height of 1.7 m [PAGE 8]. These are biomechanical priors, not measured per-person body dimensions. The AICity’24 2D–3D association accuracy is high—99.4% warehouse, 95.2% retail, 91.2% hospital, 96.9% overall [PAGE 7]—but that is association accuracy, not proof that a fixed human box is biomechanically valid for all people. A claim of true body-shape or pose-level biomechanical correctness is therefore vetoed as unsupported. The defensible claim is narrower: a fixed person-scale prior is sufficient for the reported tracking metrics.

**Weather/illumination veto.** The provided pages contain no rain, snow, wind, fog, wet-surface, or weather-stratified experiment. The closest real-world stressor is the paper’s statement that WildTrack has “more noises and diverse illuminations from different camera views” [PAGE 12], and that its 2D annotations are less accurate than AICity’24’s synthetic annotations [PAGE 12]. Therefore any claim that MCBLT is weather-robust is vetoed as unsupported by this source. It may be illumination-robust to the extent shown by WildTrack ReID mAP 63.11 and rank-1 77.18 [PAGE 12], but weather is not a measured variable. The correct adjudication is: illumination/noise evidence exists; weather evidence does not.

**Occlusion/clock veto.** The global block supports occlusion beyond the graph horizon [PAGE 6], and AICity’24 includes occlusions up to 2k frames [PAGE 6]. But if an object is unobserved for longer than the retained appearance and geometry evidence can disambiguate, the identity belief is underdetermined. The source does not report a per-occlusion-length breakdown beyond the full-sequence aggregate. Thus a claim of unbounded occlusion robustness is vetoed; a claim of full-sequence global association on the reported AICity’24 and WildTrack settings is supported.
