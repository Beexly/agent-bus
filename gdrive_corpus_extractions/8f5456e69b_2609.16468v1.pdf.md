<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The formal operators in DSQ are cross-attention pooling, state-specific affinity heads, a sigmoid MWC gate, an occupancy-weighted expectation, and four loss terms. The state-probing operator is
\[
Z_s=\mathrm{MHA}(Q_s,\tilde H_P,\tilde H_P),\qquad s\in\{\mathrm{act},\mathrm{inact}\},
\]
where \(Q_s\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}\), \(\tilde H_P\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\), and \(Z_s\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}\). The paper describes this as “cross-attention pooling” and states that “the contribution is not a new attention primitive, but the use of two state-specific probe sets whose outputs are coupled to an MWC-inspired thermodynamic gate” [PAGE 3]. Units: \(K\) is a count of latent probes, \(N\) is residue count, and \(d\) is a dimensionless embedding width. Assumption: \(K\) probes per state are not \(K\) thermodynamic conformational states; they are latent attention channels.

The state-affinity operators are
\[
\hat y_{\mathrm{act}}=\mathrm{MLP}_{\mathrm{act}}(\mathrm{Interaction}(H_L,Z_{\mathrm{act}})),\qquad
\hat y_{\mathrm{inact}}=\mathrm{MLP}_{\mathrm{inact}}(\mathrm{Interaction}(H_L,Z_{\mathrm{inact}})).
\]
The outputs \(\hat y_s\) are in pX-like regression units, which the paper defines by “pX = 9 −log10X” with \(X\) in nM [PAGE 5]. They are therefore dimensionless in the paper’s convention, not joules. The interaction operator uses ligand tokens as queries and state representations as keys and values; the two heads have “identical architectures but independent parameters” [PAGE 4].

The MWC gate is
\[
P(R^{*}|p,l)=\sigma\!\left(\frac{(\hat y_{\mathrm{act}}-\hat y_{\mathrm{inact}})-E_{\mathrm{barrier}}(p)}{\tau}\right),
\]
with \(E_{\mathrm{barrier}}(p)\) produced by a projection head on the pooled protein embedding and \(\tau\) initialized to 1.0 [PAGE 4]. The Boltzmann derivation is
\[
P(R^{*})=\frac{e^{-\beta G_{\mathrm{act}}}}{e^{-\beta G_{\mathrm{act}}}+e^{-\beta G_{\mathrm{inact}}}}
=\sigma[\beta(G_{\mathrm{inact}}-G_{\mathrm{act}})],\qquad \beta=\frac{1}{k_B T}.
\]
Here \(\beta\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\), \(G\) has units \(\mathrm{J}\), and \(\beta G\) is dimensionless. The neural \(\tau\), however, is a learnable scale in pX units; it is not \(k_B T\) in kelvin. The affinity bridge is stated as “\(y \propto -\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}} = k_B T \ln K_a\)” [PAGE 4], but a dimensionally complete form requires a standard-state concentration \(C^\circ\) and a logarithmic base conversion, e.g. \(\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^\circ=-RT\ln(K_a C^\circ)\) and \(y=\mathrm{pX}\) proportional to \(-\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^\circ/(RT\ln 10)\) up to an offset. The paper’s Eq. 6 therefore should be read as an ordinal proportionality, not a calibrated energy equation.

The final bioactivity operator is
\[
\hat y_{\mathrm{final}}=P(R^{*}|p,l)\hat y_{\mathrm{act}}+[1-P(R^{*}|p,l)]\hat y_{\mathrm{inact}},
\]
which the paper calls “the weighted expectation of the state affinities” [PAGE 4]. This is an expectation over a two-state occupancy model; it is not the same as a single experimentally measured pKi for a ligand that may stabilize multiple conformations or bind with kinetic complexity.

The loss operators are:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}=\mathbb E\left[\frac{|Z_{\mathrm{act}}\cdot Z_{\mathrm{inact}}|}{\|Z_{\mathrm{act}}\|_2\|Z_{\mathrm{inact}}\|_2}\right],
\]
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}}=\max(0,-r(\hat y_{\mathrm{act}}-\hat y_{\mathrm{inact}})+m),\qquad r=2y_{\mathrm{label}}-1,
\]
and
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{total}}=\lambda_1\mathcal L_{\mathrm{reg}}+\lambda_2\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gate}}+\lambda_3\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}+\lambda_4\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rank}},
\]
with \(\lambda_1=1.0\), \(\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\lambda_4=0.5\), and \(m=1.0\) [PAGE 5]. The orthogonality term is dimensionless but singular when either \(\|Z_s\|_2=0\); the ranking margin \(m\) is in pX units and is not calibrated to an experimental energy margin. Gate accuracy is defined only as “accuracy of classifying agonist vs. antagonist” [PAGE 8], not as calibration, likelihood, or expected free-energy error.

Exact singularity and breakdown regimes:
1. \(T\to 0\) gives \(\beta\to\infty\); the sigmoid becomes a step function, numerically saturates, and gradients vanish except near exact equality. The equilibrium assumption also becomes physically inappropriate at \(T=0\).
2. \(\tau\to 0\) makes Eq. 8 singular at \(\tau=0\); for small \(\tau\), \(\partial P/\partial(\Delta \hat y)\approx P(1-P)/\tau\), so the gate can become numerically unstable and produce near-hard efficacy decisions. The learned value is reported as \(\tau=0.2627\), not zero [PAGE 7], but the singularity remains a formal breakdown.
3. \(\|Z_s\|_2\to 0\) makes \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}\) undefined; cosine similarity has no direction at the origin. This is a mode-collapse risk, and the paper reports that removing orthogonality degrades RMSE to 1.141 and gate accuracy to 0.672 [PAGE 7].
4. \((\hat y_{\mathrm{act}}-\hat y_{\mathrm{inact}})-E_{\mathrm{barrier}}(p)=0\) gives \(P=0.5\), the maximum-gate-gradient regime; small changes flip the predicted efficacy class.
5. Low-homology receptors are a generalization breakdown: DSQ low-homology overall \(R^2=-0.081\), agonist \(R^2=-0.095\), antagonist \(R^2=-0.093\) [PAGE 7]. This is not a numerical singularity but an out-of-distribution failure.
6. Mixed assay labels are a semantic breakdown: “All bioactivity values (IC50, EC50, Ki) were standardized to the negative logarithmic scale with nM unit (pX = 9 −log10X)” [PAGE 5]. IC50, EC50, and Ki are not interchangeable thermodynamic quantities, so cross-assay free-energy interpretation is invalid without assay metadata.
7. The model uses frozen ESM-2 and MolFormer encoders and only 3.96M trainable parameters [PAGE 5]; “DSQ relies on frozen ESM-2 and MolFormer encoders, which may limit adaptation to novel receptor families or chemical scaffolds” [PAGE 12]. This is a representation bottleneck, not an MWC derivation.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer veto is conditional but strong. The Boltzmann form is formally correct for a two-state equilibrium, but the paper’s bridge from pX to \(\Delta G\) is incomplete: Eq. 6 states \(y\propto-\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}=k_B T\ln K_a\) [PAGE 4] while \(y\) is a pX-like dimensionless number [PAGE 5]. A dimensionally complete mapping needs \(RT\ln 10\), a standard-state concentration, and an assay-temperature specification. The learned \(\tau\) is not \(k_B T\); it is a dimensionless scale initialized at 1.0 and learned to 0.2627 [PAGE 7]. Therefore the physical veto adjudication is: the claim “DSQ is a quantitative thermodynamic model” is vetoed; the claim “DSQ uses an MWC-inspired thermodynamic inductive bias” survives. The paper’s own wording supports the weaker claim: it “provides useful thermodynamic inductive bias” [PAGE 1].

The biomechanical/conformational layer veto is also conditional. A two-state model cannot represent biased signaling, pathway-specific efficacy, or multiple active conformations; the paper explicitly says the current implementation “cannot explicitly model biased signaling, pathway-specific efficacy, or multiple active conformations” [PAGE 12]. The low-homology results are the empirical veto: overall \(R^2=-0.081\), agonist \(-0.095\), antagonist \(-0.093\) [PAGE 7]. Thus “general GPCR bioactivity profiling across evolutionary distances” is vetoed, while “high-homology agonist-enriched benchmark improvement” survives. The antagonist result is a secondary veto against a universal-efficacy claim because “DSQ does not uniformly outperform the baselines on the antagonist subset” [PAGE 6].

The weather/environment layer is the assay and membrane context: temperature, pH, buffer, cell background, receptor construct, and membrane mimetic all change apparent IC50/EC50/Ki. The paper standardizes “IC50, EC50, Ki” to pX [PAGE 5] but does not model these environmental covariates. This is a cross-layer veto for any claim that the learned gate is a universal free-energy switch. A pX value measured in one assay weather is not the same thermodynamic object as a pX value measured in another. The correct adjudication is a layered verdict: benchmark regression survives; agonist-specific gate discrimination survives as an empirical pattern; motif attention survives as a hypothesis-generating signal; quantitative free-energy mechanism and cold-target generalization are vetoed unless temperature, assay type, standard state, and receptor-disjoint validation are supplied.
