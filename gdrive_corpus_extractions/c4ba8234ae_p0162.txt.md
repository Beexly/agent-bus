<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Knight’s 1921 taxonomy did not emerge in isolation; it is a direct retrocausal response to the marginalist synthesis of the late 19th century, which struggled to explain persistent profits in competitive equilibria. By isolating "uncertainty" as distinct from "risk" (statistical variability), Knight rescued the concept of the entrepreneur from the static model. The historical lineage traces back to Carl Menger’s emphasis on subjective valuation and Joseph Schumpeter’s later work on innovation, but Knight provided the formal epistemological gap.

However, a multi-week trap exists in contemporary applications of this genealogy. Modern financial engineering often misinterprets Knight’s "known law" to include any stochastic process with a calibrated distribution. This leads to the dangerous assumption that tail risks (black swans) are merely part of a fat-tailed distribution (risk) rather than true uncertainty (unknown unknowns). The trap manifests when institutions rely on Value-at-Risk (VaR) models that assume stationarity ($\mathcal{L}$) while ignoring regime shifts ($\mathcal{U}$). Historically, the 2008 crisis exemplified this: housing defaults were modeled as correlated risks within a known framework, failing to account for the uncertainty of systemic structural breakdown. Recognizing Knight’s original intent—that many factors like "tastes and dispositions" cannot be quantified without destroying their essence—is crucial to avoiding this anachronistic error.
