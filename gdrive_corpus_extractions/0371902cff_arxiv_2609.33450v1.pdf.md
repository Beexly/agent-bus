The paper’s central move is not “a larger model reads better.” It is an architectural relocation of association into the decoder’s grammar. I trace that move in five hops.

**Hop 1: the failure being attacked is association, not OCR.** The paper separates three axes: “syntac-tic validity (does the output parse into the schema on every frame?), visual grounding (is every attribute anchored to a real, non-degenerate region?), and semantic association (is every attribute bound to the correct person?)” [PAGE 2]. Cascades fail mainly the third axis because the post-hoc IoU stitch can attach a correctly read number to the wrong athlete under occlusion [PAGE 2]. The abstract states the intended invariant: “Output is therefore schema-valid on every frame by construction, and no post-hoc binding step exists to attach a correctly read number to the wrong player: residual misassociation is pure perception error, ≈4× rarer than zero-shot-prompted frontier APIs’ (0.057 vs. 0.21–0.24)” [PAGE 1]. So the claimed gain is structural: in-block emission makes binding readable from the sequence.

**Hop 2: grammar enforces both validity and association.** The serialization puts each player’s OCR string inside that player’s block: “every OCR string (and, when emitted, its coordinates) is generated inside exactly one &lt;player&gt;. . . &lt;/player&gt; block, so text–player binding is read off the sequence rather than reconstructed by a post-hoc IoU stitch” [PAGE 5]. A prefix-allowed-tokens function admits only legal continuations, so “every decoded sequence parses into the schema—validity 1.0 on every frame” [PAGE 5]. The grammar is load-bearing: with it disabled, “the decoder never emits a content token, hits the 1024-token cap, and yields zero parseable persons” [PAGE 10]. This is a decoding-stability result, not a perception-accuracy result; the paper says so explicitly [PAGE 11].

**Hop 3: confidence is a replay operator, not a new head.** After constrained generation, one teacher-forced fp32 replay recovers per-token log-probabilities. For a field \(F=(y_{t_1},\ldots,y_{t_k})\), confidence is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities, Eq. (1): \(c(F)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log P(y_{t_i}\mid y_{<t_i},X)\right)\) [PAGE 5]. This yields a reject option: “jersey precision 0.71 at full coverage, 0.87 at the top 70%, and 0.96 at the top half” [PAGE 11]. <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**1. Grammar-validity operator.** Let \(s\) be a decoded sequence and \(\mathrm{parse}_G(\cdot)\) the finite-state grammar parser. Define
\[
V(s)=\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathrm{parse}_G(s)=\text{success}\right],\qquad P(V=1)=1
\]
by prefix-constrained decoding: “a prefix-allowed-tokens function admits only grammar-legal continua-tions at each step, so every decoded sequence parses into the schema—validity 1.0 on every frame” [PAGE 5]. Units: \(V\) is dimensionless, Bernoulli-valued. Assumption: the grammar is enabled at every decode step. Breakdown: with the grammar disabled, the same checkpoint loops on the start token, hits the 1024-token cap, and yields zero parseable persons [PAGE 10]. The implemented grammar bounds 12 players and 17 OCR items per player [PAGE 5]; if a true frame exceeds those caps, the grammar cannot represent it, though the paper says the 12-person cap is non-binding on its data [PAGE 14].

**2. Native-association error rate.** Let \(C_{\mathrm{read}}\) be the set of correctly read jersey-digit items and \(C_{\mathrm{bound}}\subseteq C_{\mathrm{read}}\) those bound to the true owner. Then
\[
\mathrm{AER}=\frac{|C_{\mathrm{read}}\setminus C_{\mathrm{bound}}|}{|C_{\mathrm{read}}|}
\]
dimensionless. This is the paper’s definition: “AER is the fraction of correctly-read jersey digits not bound to their true owner” [PAGE 7]. Assumptions: same matcher for every system; digit-only rate because team text is owner-ambiguous [PAGE 7]. Reported values: 0.057 single-pass, 0.068 after crop re-query, 0.242 Gemini-2.5-pro, 0.214 Gemini-3.1-pro [PAGE 9]. Breakdown: for oracle-boxed cascades the denominator spans only the 32–37% of numbers they read [PAGE 9 footnote], so AER is not directly comparable across coverage regimes. Crop re-query raises AER because “a crop that straddles a pile-up can bind a neighbour’s number” [PAGE 6].

**3. Detection F1.** Let \(G\) be ground-truth boxes, \(\hat G\) predicted boxes, and \(M\) the Hungarian assignment at \(\mathrm{IoU}\geq 0.5\). Then
\[
P=\frac{|M|}{|\hat G|},\qquad R=\frac{|M|}{|G|},\qquad F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}
\]
dimensionless. The paper’s protocol is “Hungarian assignment at IoU ≥0.5 (P/R/F1)” [PAGE 7]. Reported: 0.950 for the proposed single pass, 0.652 and 0.745 for the APIs [PAGE 9]. Breakdown: small-player detection is the failure point; tiny-bucket recall is 0.85 for the proposed model versus 0.56 for Gemini-3.1 [PAGE 10].

**4. Per-field confidence, Eq. (1).** For contiguous field \(F=(y_{t_1},\ldots,y_{t_k})\),
\[
c(F)=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log P\!\left(y_{t_i}\mid y_{<t_i},X\right)\right)
\]
[PAGE 5]. Units: \(P\in[0,1]\) is dimensionless; \(\log P\) is in nats if natural log is used; the average is nats per token; the exponential returns a dimensionless probability-like score. Assumptions: teacher-forced fp32 replay over generated tokens; unmasked full-vocabulary probabilities; contiguous field; \(k>0\). Singularity/breakdown: if any \(P(y_{t_i}\mid\cdot)=0\), then \(\log 0\to-\infty\) and \(c(F)=0\); if \(k=0\), the expression is undefined, which is why the paper uses non-empty digit-only fields. Calibration breakdown: raw likelihood is overconfident, ECE 0.12–0.15 on sports [PAGE 11], but calibrated on WIDER-Attribute after fusion, ECE 0.028 [PAGE 13].

**5. Coordinate quantization.** Continuous coordinates are quantized to 1000 bins:
\[
q_x=\mathrm{round}\!\left(1000\frac{x}{W}\right),\qquad q_y=\mathrm{round}\!\left(1000\frac{y}{H}\right),\qquad q\in\{0,\ldots,1000\}
\]
[PAGE 5]. Units: normalized width/height fraction. The derived quantization floor is at most \(1/(2\cdot1000)=5\times10^{-4}\) in normalized coordinate, before model error. Breakdown: small players and tiny text are bounded by legible pixels; “the 1000-bin coordinate quantization and finite decoder resolution limit tiny-text reading” [PAGE 14].

**6. Degenerate-box veto.** For a normalized predicted box \(b=(x,y,w,h)\),
\[
\mathrm{valid}(b)=1 \iff w>0.002 \ \land\ h>0.002
\]
The paper defines a degenerate box as “Zero width or height after normalization (w ≤0.002 or h ≤0.002); such a box has IoU = 0 with any ground-truth player and can be matched to no one” [PAGE 11 footnote]. Breakdown: Gemini-3.1-pro emits a degenerate box on 20.3% of predicted players, at least one on 22.2% of frames [PAGE 11]; 90–97% of API misbindings are such orphaned reads, whereas the proposed model’s residual errors are “contact-occlusion swaps between overlapping players” [PAGE 12].

**7. Latency operator.** Let \(\Delta t_j\) be wall-clock inference time on frame \(j\):
\[
L=\frac{1}{N_f}\sum_{j=1}^{N_f}\Delta t_j\quad[\mathrm{ms\,frame^{-1}}]
\]
Reported: 1224 ms/frame single-pass, 1274 after re-query, 26009 and 8426 for APIs [PAGE 9]. The grammar ablation reduces wall-clock by 7,580 ms/frame, −85.6% [PAGE 11]. Breakdown: the oracle-cascade 89 ms and 708 ms cells are reading/team/stitch only with oracle boxes; no detector was run, so they are not end-to-end [PAGE 9 footnote].

**8. Structural ablations and noise floor.** Quad supervision is net-negative for reading: dropping quads gives “+3.8 jersey-F1” under the any-OCR rule, “+1.6 OCR-text-F1,” “+3.7 team purity,” and a 28% latency reduction, against −0.4 detection-F1 [PAGE 10]. The LoRA ladder is null: “no adapter variant ... moves the aggregate by more than +0.0011” [PAGE 10], below the +0.005 floor [PAGE 6]. WIDER-Attribute: 93.1 mAP given-box, 84.5 mAP end-to-end [PAGE 12]; mAP and mA are explicitly not comparable across blocks [PAGE 13].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s lineage is retrocausal because the final grammar makes earlier components look like partial ancestors. Autoregressive spatial perception—Pix2Seq, Kosmos-2, Florence-2—already emitted boxes or grounded phrases as tokens [PAGE 3]. Constrained decoding already existed for entity linking, text-to-SQL, and grammar-constrained LLMs; PICARD and Willard–Louf are cited [PAGE 3, 15–16]. DETR-style object queries bind attributes to instances but require trained heads per attribute and give no schema-validity guarantee [PAGE 3]. Grounded multimodal NER emits entity–region associations in one sequence but over image–text pairs with detector-proposed regions and no validity guarantee [PAGE 3]. The paper’s claimed novelty is the composition: “the grammar carries association, not merely well-formedness” [PAGE 3]. That is a retrocausal reframing: JSON-schema prompting, IoU stitching, OCR confidence, and calibration all become incomplete ancestors of a single grammar-constrained record.

**Multi-week trap 1: schema-valid is not association-valid.** A team can spend weeks tuning a JSON-mode API and see 100% parseability, then discover degenerate geometry: Gemini-3.1-pro emits a zero-area box on 20.3% of predicted players [PAGE 11]. The paper’s three-axis separation [PAGE 2] is the antidote: monitor parse success, non-degenerate grounding, and AER separately.

**Multi-week trap 2: metric asymmetry.** The all-text AER can favor oracle-boxed cascades because team text is owner-ambiguous; “under the all-text rate the oracle-boxed cascades attain lower AER than our single pass (0.057–0.070 vs. 0.085)” [PAGE 7–8]. A team that switches metrics after deployment can reverse the ranking. The digit-only ranking is specific to that metric [PAGE 8].

**Multi-week trap 3: oracle assistance.** Oracle-boxed cascades are “generous to the cascades; their AER spans only the 32–37% of numbers they read” [PAGE 9 footnote]. A weekly report that compares full-coverage AER to oracle-boxed AER will mislead.

**Multi-week trap 4: tuning noise.** The LoRA ladder is a null under a pre-registered noise floor [PAGE 10]. Without that floor, repeated weekly tuning can chase +0.001 deltas that are below noise. The paper’s structural ablation is larger: dropping quads is worth +0.008 aggregate, roughly 7× the best tuning delta [PAGE 10].

**Multi-week trap 5: WIDER annotation sparsity.** The end-to-end WIDER number is a lower bound because the detector fires on background people the annotators skipped; unmatched predictions have median box area 0.023 and 69.5% arise on images with more predictions than labels [PAGE 12]. Treating that gap as a model failure would be a category error.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under operational pressure, teams regress to the familiar disjoint cascade: detector, OCR, classifier, then IoU stitch. The paper says that even with ground-truth boxes and one crop per athlete, cascades “still misbind 15–18% of the jersey digits they read” [PAGE 2]. The recursive failure is epistemic: the model emits schema-valid output, the operator sees validity 1.0, and both over-attribute correctness to the schema. The abstract’s “residual misassociation is pure perception error” [PAGE 1] is the correct mental model, but a crisis habit treats “parses” as “true.” Countermeasure: display AER and confidence rank, not just parse success. The confidence signal is useful for ranking but not absolute probability; raw likelihood is overconfident, ECE 0.12–0.15 [PAGE 11].

**Counter-trap.** The reject option is a precision lever: jersey precision rises from 0.71 at full coverage to 0.87 at top 70% and 0.96 at top half [PAGE 11]. The counter-trap is to optimize the displayed precision by silently shrinking coverage. The paper explicitly makes the reject option “opt-in—our headline numbers are at full coverage, so the reject option adds precision without quietly dropping recall” [PAGE 11]. A recursive operator model must ask: if the dashboard shows 0.96 precision at half coverage, what happened to the abstained half? Those are likely small or occluded players; the crop re-query targets exactly that bucket, lifting tiny-bucket jersey F1 from 0.61 to 0.75 [PAGE 10] but raising AER from 0.057 to 0.068 [PAGE 6, 9]. The counter-trap is to treat a high-precision low-coverage operating point as a full-coverage system.

**Clock.** The clock trap is counter-intuitive: disabling the grammar to “speed up” decoding collapses generation into a start-token loop, hits the 1024-token cap, and is 7× slower [PAGE 10–11]; on sports it costs 7,580 ms/frame, −85.6% when the grammar is on [PAGE 11]. The operational clock also constrains re-query: single-pass is 1224 ms/frame, re-query 1274 ms/frame at 1.93 passes/image [PAGE 9]. A live operator who enables re-query for every frame buys small-player recall at a small latency cost and a real AER cost. The recursive theory-of-mind point is that the model’s “fast” path and the operator’s “fast” path are not the same: the grammar is the fast path because it terminates; the unconstrained path is the slow path because it loops.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
I treat deployment as a conjunction of layer vetoes. A layer veto is a hard block, not a soft score. The source supports two layers strongly and leaves one unadjudicated.

**Physical layer: supported veto.** The physical layer is pixel resolution, scale, blur, quantization, and decoder resolution. The paper’s own limitation is “Accuracy on small players is bounded by legible pixels: the 1000-bin coordinate quantization and finite decoder resolution limit tiny-text reading” [PAGE 14]. The size-stratified analysis shows tiny-bucket detection recall 0.85 versus Gemini-3.1’s 0.56 [PAGE 10], and crop re-query lifts tiny-bucket jersey F1 0.61 → 0.75 [PAGE 10]. Veto predicate: if a player is in the bottom area quartile and the re-read confidence remains low, do not treat the jersey read as deployable; abstain or flag. Proof status: supported by the reported size-bucket results and the quantization limit [PAGE 5, 10, 14]. The veto is not absolute because re-query mitigates it, but the paper says it is “mitigated but not eliminated” [PAGE 14].

**Biomechanical layer: supported veto.** The biomechanical layer is body contact, pose overlap, and pile-ups. The paper’s residual association errors are “contact-occlusion swaps between overlapping players” [PAGE 12], and crop re-query can fail because “a crop that straddles a pile-up can bind a neighbour’s number” [PAGE 6]. Veto predicate: if a crop intersects multiple players or the predicted box overlaps a contact cluster, do not trust the crop re-read’s binding; keep the first-pass read or abstain. Proof status: supported by the AER increase from 0.057 to 0.068 after re-query [PAGE 6, 9] and the contact-occlusion decomposition [PAGE 12]. This is a biomechanical veto because the failure is caused by bodies occluding bodies, not by text recognition alone.

**Weather layer: unadjudicated, therefore blocked.** The source lists “motion blur, heavy inter-player occlusion, extreme scale variation ... and small, rotated, warped typography” [PAGE 1], but it contains no rain, snow, glare, or weather labels and no weather-stratified experiment. There is no page in the provided source that reports a weather ablation. Therefore the weather veto cannot be proved or disproved from this paper. The correct adjudication is UNKNOWN, not PASS. Deployment in weather must be treated as distribution shift and blocked until a weather-stratified test set is run. This is not a claim that the model fails in weather; it is a claim that the source provides no evidence to clear the layer.

**Adjudication rule.** Deploy only if physical veto = clear or mitigated by re-query with confidence above the operating threshold; biomechanical veto = clear (no pile-up crop); weather veto = clear by external validation. If weather is UNKNOWN, the system may run in shadow mode with abstention, but not as an unqualified labeler.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
