When the essential element is geographically fixed (Gibraltar) or knowledge is legally enclosed, \(\tau_{\text{substitute}}\) lengthens, and the clock favors the monopolist—until physical substitutes or exogenous shifts in the "rapidly changing condition of the world" alter the payoff structure [PAGE 189].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The Strait of Gibraltar possesses fixed bathymetry, width, and depth, imposing a hard capacity ceiling \(Q_{\max}\) on vessel transits [PAGE 189]. Any monopoly-toll model that predicts \(Q(\tau) > Q_{\max}\) is physically vetoed. Moreover, a physical substitute exists—the longer route around the Cape of Good Hope—so the derived demand for Gibraltar transits is not perfectly inelastic. The physical layer therefore caps the monopoly rent at the point where the toll equals the cost differential of the next-best physical route.

Biomechanical layer: Human productive agencies—labor, management, entrepreneurial discovery—are subject to physiological training and accumulation limits. Even if a resource is not geologically scarce, the supply of skilled agents capable of exploration and development work cannot be increased instantaneously [PAGE 189]. This creates a short-run biomechanical monopoly over specialized knowledge or labor that dissolves only over the longer training horizon.

Weather/exogenous layer: Knight describes the world as "undeveloped and rapidly changing," with supply increasing "irregularly through new discoveries" [PAGE 189]. Weather, geological serendipity, and climatic variation affect shipping through Gibraltar and the success of exploration programs. These exogenous shocks shift \(F(x; A)\) unpredictably, meaning any static monopoly model must be treated as a short-run equilibrium vulnerable to supply shocks.

Verdict: The monopoly model is veto-proof only when all three layers concur—physical capacity is binding and fixed, biomechanical substitution is slow, and exogenous discovery shocks are absent. If any layer admits substitution (alternative route, trained entrants, or a new discovery), the monopoly assumption is adjudicated invalid and the competitive prerequisite of substitutable, expandable supply is restored [PAGE 189].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Gibraltar toll escalation): Counterfactual—if the toll \(\tau\) is raised by 20% above the competitive congestion price, then within six months observed transits \(Q(\tau)\) fall by more than 10%, and traffic on the substitute Cape route rises by a corresponding verifiable amount. Prediction: the elasticity \(\frac{d\ln Q}{d\ln \tau}\) is less than -0.5. RLVR veto condition: if the measured volume reduction is below 5% and no route substitution occurs, the monopoly is sustained; if volume drops sharply and alternative-route share increases, the physical layer vetoes the monopoly assumption because \(Q\) is constrained by substitutable geography, not by essential control alone [PAGE 189].

Scenario 2 (Knowledge enclosure): Counterfactual—if a foundational, non-rival knowledge stock \(A\) (e.g., an open scientific model) is enclosed behind a proprietary price \(p_A^m > 0\) where replication cost \(c_A \approx 0\), then follow-on citation density and downstream product variety decline by at least 15% over 12 months. Prediction: \(DWL > R_A\), so social welfare falls despite positive monopoly rent. RLVR veto condition: if citation density and product variety remain unchanged while \(p_A^m\) is charged, the knowledge is not actually essential to joint production and Veblen's claim (as rejected by Knight on [PAGE 189]) is falsified; if both decline, the knowledge enclosure is vetoed by the joint-production layer because the excluded input degrades the productivity of complementary factors.

Scenario 3 (Essential input with fixed proportions): Counterfactual—if a single rare earth element \(k\) is essential (\(\sigma_{kj}=0\) for all \(j\neq k\)) and one country controls all exports, then downstream manufacturers' output falls proportionally to the export restriction, and no technical substitute is fielded within 18 months. Prediction: the input price captures the full quasi-rent \(R_k\), and downstream economic profit approaches zero. RLVR veto condition: if a substitute is developed within 12 months, the essentiality assumption \(\sigma_{kj}=0\) is vetoed by the discovery-and-development layer described by Knight [PAGE 189]; if no substitute appears and output collapses in proportion to the restriction, the monopoly over the essential element is confirmed as a singularity regime.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
