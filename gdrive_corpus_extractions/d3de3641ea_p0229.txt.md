Level-1 reasoning invokes Theorem A and expects a unique extension to S; this is the normal, non-crisis mode. Level-2 reasoning notices the counterexample: μ(E_i)=1 for all i yet ∩_iE_i=∅, so the level-1 belief is false on non-interval spaces; the correct stance is to reject countable additivity unless tightness/continuity-from-above is independently verified.

Crisis habit regression: under deadline or data pressure, the analyst collapses from level-2 back to level-0, fitting finite-dimensional marginals and ignoring the cylinder algebra’s continuity requirement. The “clock” is the horizon n: as n grows, the gap between μ(E_i)=1 for every finite i and the empty infinite intersection becomes decision-relevant precisely at long horizons, so short-horizon validation cannot veto the long-horizon inconsistency. Counter-trap: impose the veto explicitly before simulation—check a decreasing sequence of cylinders with empty intersection and require their probabilities to tend to zero; if the empirical estimate stays near 1, regress to a finitely additive or truncated model rather than forcing a probability measure on S. Recursive depth matters because a level-1 model of another agent who is themselves regressing to level-0 will mispredict tail behavior: it will expect continuity from above where only finite additivity holds.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Treat the construction in three layers. Physical layer: the product probability space (X,S) and the requirement that μ be a countably additive probability measure on S. Biomechanical layer: the coordinate/cylinder maps S_n and the measurability of E↦μ(S_n^{-1}(E)); if the transformation notation on [PAGE 1] (“zi = xn”) is not a well-defined measurable map from X_n into X_1×…×X_n, then μ(E)=μ(S^{-1}(A)) is not even well-defined. Weather layer: external perturbations of the reference measure ν, e.g. ν_ε with density g_ε=dν_ε/dν, ‖g_ε−1‖_{L^1(ν)}<ε.

Veto 1 (countable additivity): if ∃A_k∈𝓕, A_k↓∅, with lim_kμ(A_k)≠0, veto any extension to S. The example E_i exercises this veto maximally: μ(E_i)=1 and ∩_iE_i=∅ on [PAGE 1], so the physical layer cannot accept μ as a probability measure on S.

Veto 2 (absolute continuity/Radon–Nikodym): if μ|F_n is not absolutely continuous wrt ⨂_{i=1}^nμ_i, the density f_n does not exist and p(E,T_n(x)) cannot be defined as an almost-everywhere probability kernel. Veto applies singularly whenever a finite cylinder has positive μ-measure but zero product reference measure.

Veto 3 (compactness/tightness): if each X_n is compact metric, tightness passes and the extension survives; if X_n=ℝ, one-point compactification rescues it; for arbitrary compact non-metrizable spaces, veto unless separability and regular Borel measure assumptions are added, because mass may escape to ∩_nX_n=∅ with no boundary point to absorb it.

Weather-perturbation proof: perturb ν to ν_ε≪ν. The definition μ_n(E)=ν(F) changes to μ_{n,ε}(E)=ν_ε(F)=∫_F g_ε dν. The obstruction is not numerical but set-theoretic: the sets E_i still have μ_{n,ε}(E_i)=1 for all i in the source construction while ∩_iE_i=∅, since the empty intersection comes from ∩_nX_n=∅, not from the size of ν. Hence small absolutely continuous weather perturbations cannot cure the continuity-from-above failure; the veto is structural and stable under ‖g_ε−1‖_1<ε. The only cure is to change the index-space layer (compact metric/tight) or to abandon countable additivity on S.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — finite-horizon diagonal concentration. Construct an estimator μ̂_N on cylinders up to coordinate N using the rule μ̂_N(E)=ν(F) for E=F∩X_N. Verifiable check: for increasing i≤N compute μ̂_N(E_i) on E_i={x_1=…=x_i} and test membership in ∩_{i=1}^M E_i for sample paths. Prediction: μ̂_N(E_i)=1 for every i≤N, yet no simulated path lies in ∩_{i=1}^M E_i once M exceeds the first index with x_M∉X_M (equivalently once M is large enough that ∩_{n=1}^M X_n=∅). Veto criterion: if lim_{M→∞} μ̂_N(E_M)=1 while μ̂_N(∩_{i=1}^M E_i)=0 for all sufficiently large M, reject countably additive extension; pass only finitely additive or truncated models.

Scenario 2 — Radon–Nikodym breakdown. Estimate the density f_n=d(μ|F_n)/d(⨂_{i=1}^n μ_i) by likelihood-ratio methods on finite cylinders. Verifiable check: find a cylinder C with (⨂_{i=1}^n μ_i)(C)=0 but μ(C)>0 (e.g. a diagonal-type cylinder concentrated on X_n traces). Prediction: the estimated likelihood ratio diverges (‖f_n‖_∞=∞) and conditional probabilities p(E,T_n(x)) are undefined on the null set supporting C. Veto criterion: any observed infinite or numerically unstable density ratio invalidates absolute continuity and the kernel-based extension proof on [PAGE 1].

Scenario 3 — compactification counterfactual. Replace each non-interval X_n by its closure in a compact metric space (or one-point compactification for ℝ) and run the extension algorithm. Verifiable check: compare total variation between the original finite-dimensional approximations μ^{(n)} and the compactified extension μ_comp on cylinders depending on coordinates ≤n. Prediction: μ_comp exists and is tight; mass that escaped to ∩_n X_n=∅ in the invalid model reappears at the compactification boundary, so ‖μ^{(n)}−μ_comp‖_{TV} on fixed cylinders does not converge to the invalid μ but to a different limit. Veto criterion: if the compactified model assigns positive boundary mass and the TV distance to the would-be μ does not vanish, the arbitrary-compact/non-interval case fails Theorem A, confirming the source’s open question in the negative direction without invoking any wagering quantities.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
