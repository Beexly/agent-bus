<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under deformation, occlusion, and out-of-plane rotation, the paper claims gains precisely because the branch switch "may give a better discriminativity" [PAGE 8]. But the mechanism holds a branch for T−1 = 6 frames after a crisis begins [PAGE 5]. A tracker's "belief" about the best branch is a bounded-rationality state that only updates on the clock tick; crisis onset is invisible between ticks.

**Counter-trap.** The naive counter-trap is to select every frame (T=1). The paper refutes it explicitly: "frequent execution of the selection mechanism increases the possibility of selecting an inappropriate branch" [PAGE 8]. So the tracker's self-model must accept that over-attending to appearance change is worse than under-attending.

**Clock.** T=7 is a designer-imposed metronome, not an event trigger. The correct recursive model is that the tracker should select on evidence (response-map entropy exceeding threshold), not on elapsed frames — the paper has no event-trigger path.

**Adjudication.** The recursive loop is therefore stable but not adaptive: it converges to persistence between ticks and to jitter under bad weights. Modern analog: gating networks solve exactly this by gradient descent over a continuous attention variable rather than a discrete argmax every T frames.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
