Extension to Borel sets uses regularity and [PAGE 263] reference “cf. 52.H”: two regular Borel measures agreeing on Baire sets agree on all Borel sets.

Exercise (1), [PAGE 263]: Multiplicative group \mathbb R^\times=\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}. Haar measure is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue measure \lambda, with Radon–Nikodym derivative
\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}, \qquad d\mu(x)=\frac{dx}{|x|}.
Check of left invariance: for a\in\mathbb R^\times,
\mu(aE)=\int_{aE}\frac{dx}{|x|}=\int_E\frac{|a|\,dy}{|a||y|}=\int_E\frac{dy}{|y|}=\mu(E).
Units: if x carries unit [X], then dx has unit [X] and |x| has unit [X], so d\mu=(1/|x|)dx is dimensionless, consistent with scale invariance. \mu is \sigma-finite on \mathbb R^\times since \mathbb R^\times=\bigcup_n\{1/n<|x|<n\} and each annulus has finite measure 4\log n. Singularity at x=0 is excluded because 0\notin\mathbb R^\times; the density 1/|x| is locally integrable on any compact K\Subset\mathbb R^\times where |x|\ge\delta>0.

Exercise (2), [PAGE 263]: For locally compact groups X,Y with Haar measures \mu,\nu and Haar measure \lambda on X\times Y, on Baire sets in X\times Y:
\lambda=c\,(\mu\otimes\nu)
for some c\in(0,\infty), because \mu\otimes\nu is itself a Haar measure on the product group and Theorem C applies to X\times Y.

Exercise (3), [PAGE 263]: metric transitivity from \S59.4 (not supplied) is invoked to prove uniqueness for measurable groups with finite measure; the ergodic/transitivity argument forces the ratio d\mu/d\nu to be constant \nu-a.e. when \nu(X)<\infty.

Breakdown / singularity regimes: \nu(E)=0 \Rightarrow division by zero; \nu(E)=\infty \Rightarrow c=0 or undefined; non-regular measures \Rightarrow Baire\Rightarrow Borel extension fails; X not locally compact \Rightarrow Haar measure may not exist; comparing left vs right Haar on non-unimodular groups introduces the modular function \Delta, so a single constant c relates only same-chirality Haar measures.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The result on [PAGE 263] is the Halmos (1950) textbook presentation of the Haar measure uniqueness theorem, itself descending from Alfréd Haar’s 1933 construction of invariant measure on compact groups and its extension to locally compact groups by Weil (L’intégration dans les groupes topologiques, 1940), Nachbin, and Kakutani. The phrase “measurable group” reflects an intermediate abstraction level between abstract measure spaces and topological groups: one assumes translation invariance of the \sigma-algebra and measure without necessarily assuming local compactness, which is exactly the hypothesis under which Theorem B operates. Theorem C then re-imposes local compactness and regularity to climb from Baire sets to Borel sets.

Exercise (3) reaches backward to \S59.4 “metric transitivity,” which in Halmos’s layout connects to the Birkhoff ergodic theorem and the von Neumann mean ergodic theorem: a finite invariant measure on a metrically transitive system is unique up to scalar because any two invariant measures induce the same conditional expectations on the invariant \sigma-algebra. This is a multi-week trap in the following sense: an analyst reasoning over a sequence of problems may (week 1) accept Theorem C as “constant c=1 by normalization,” (week 2) apply it to a non-unimodular group such as the ax+b group and forget that left and right Haar measures differ by \Delta(a,b)=1/|a|, and (week 3) use exercise (1)’s density 1/|x| on \mathbb R^\times while accidentally integrating against dx near x=0, producing a divergent integral because the singularity at the excluded origin was mishandled. Another trap is the Baire/Borel conflation: on a general locally compact space Baire sets need not equal Borel sets, so invoking “cf. 52.H” without verifying regularity silently invalidates the extension. The OCR corruption on [PAGE 263]—“non \mu(E) negative finite constant” and the garbled opening chain—is itself a historically recurring trap: readers of mid-century measure theory must constantly distinguish the author’s notation (\mu vs v vs \nu) from typesetting noise, or they will sign-error the constant c.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers of veto are adjudicated against the theorems on [PAGE 263].

Physical layer (underlying group/topology): Veto if X is not a locally compact topological group. Haar measure existence and uniqueness (Theorem C) presuppose local compactness; without it there is no guarantee of a non-trivial translation-invariant Radon measure, and Theorem B’s measurable-group structure may fail because translations need not preserve the \sigma-algebra \mathcal S. This veto is absolute: no constant c can rescue a non-group or non-locally-compact setting.

Biomechanical/structural layer (measure regularity and \sigma-algebra): Veto if \mu,\nu are not regular Borel measures. Theorem C’s proof rests on agreement on Baire sets lifting to Borel sets “cf. 52.H.” On spaces where Baire sets are strictly smaller than Borel sets (e.g., certain non-perfectly-normal spaces), two distinct Borel measures can vanish on the same Baire sets yet differ on a Borel set outside \mathcal S_0. Then \mu=c\nu on \mathcal S_0 does not imply \mu=c\nu on \mathcal B(X). The regularity hypothesis is therefore load-bearing; removing it vetoes the Borel conclusion while possibly retaining the Baire conclusion.

Weather/exogenous layer (source integrity and context): Veto if the OCR page is treated as self-contained. Missing Theorem A, missing \S52.H, missing \S59.4, and the garbled constant clause mean that any conclusion relying on those fragments is exogenous-noise dependent. The singularity conditions \nu(E)\in\{0,\infty\} also veto Theorem B’s normalization: c=\mu(E)/\nu(E) is undefined at \nu(E)=0 and collapses at \nu(E)=\infty. Non-\sigma-finite measures veto the Radon–Nikodym derivative in exercise (1); on \mathbb R^\times the derivative 1/|x| is valid precisely because both measures are \sigma-finite.

Adjudication order: first check the physical layer (local compact group?), then the structural layer (regularity, Baire vs Borel, \sigma-finiteness), then the singularity conditions on E, and finally the weather layer (are all cited sections available?). Only if all four pass does \mu=c\nu hold with c=\mu(E)/\nu(E)\in(0,\infty).
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Denominator collapse. Counterfactual: in Theorem B, the chosen normalization set satisfies \nu(E)=0 (e.g., X=\mathbb R with Lebesgue \nu and E a null Borel set). Prediction: the identity \mu(E)\nu(F)=\nu(E)\mu(F) reduces to 0=0 and yields no information about \mu; c=\mu(E)/\nu(E) is undefined. Verifiable veto: if \nu(E)=0 for every E with \mu(E)>0, then \mu is not absolutely continuous w.r.t. \nu and no finite constant c relates them. The theorem’s hypothesis 0<\nu(E)<\infty is therefore load-bearing; violation triggers veto of the scalar-uniqueness conclusion.

Scenario 2 — Regularity removal. Counterfactual: \mu,\nu are Borel measures on a locally compact space that agree on all Baire sets but one is not regular (or the space has Baire\ne Borel). Prediction: \exists B\in\mathcal B(X)\setminus\mathcal S_0 such that \mu(B)\neq c\nu(B) despite \mu=c\nu on \mathcal S_0. Verifiable veto: construct a measure concentrated on a Borel set that contains no non-empty Baire subset (possible on suitable non-perfect spaces); then the “cf. 52.H” lift fails and Theorem C’s Borel-level conclusion is vetoed even though its Baire-level premise holds.

Scenario 3 — Wrong reference measure on \mathbb R^\times. Counterfactual: one incorrectly takes Lebesgue measure dx itself as Haar measure on the multiplicative group \mathbb R^\times and predicts \mu(aE)=\mu(E). Prediction: for a=2 and E=(1,2), \mu(E)=1 but \mu(2E)=\mu((2,4))=2, so left invariance fails by factor |a|=2. The true Haar measure is d\mu=dx/|x| with Radon–Nikodym derivative d\mu/d\lambda=1/|x| (exercise (1), [PAGE 263]). Verifiable veto: using dx as the Haar measure fails the invariance test; the veto is removed only after inserting the density 1/|x|, restoring \int_{aE}dx/|x|=\int_E dx/|x|.
