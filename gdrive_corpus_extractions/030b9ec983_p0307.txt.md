**1. Kolmogorov’s Inequality (Submartingale Form)**
For a submartingale $(X_n)_{n\geq 0}$ with respect to a filtration $(\mathcal{F}_n)$, and $p \geq 1$:
\[
\lambda^p \mathbb{P}\left( \max_{0 \leq k \leq n} |X_k| \geq \lambda \right) \leq \mathbb{E}[|X_n|^p], \quad \lambda > 0.
\]
*Units*: Probability dimensionless ($[0,1]$); expectation in same units as $X^p$.  
*Assumptions*: $(X_n)$ is integrable; $L^p$ boundedness for $p>1$.  
*Singular Regime*: Breaks down if $\mathbb{E}[|X_n|^p] = \infty$ (e.g., Cauchy distribution). In information-theoretic terms, if Rényi entropy of order $p$ is undefined.

**2. Law of Large Numbers (Strong Form)**
Let $(X_i)_{i\geq 1}$ be i.i.d. with $\mathbb{E}[|X_1|] < \infty$ and mean $\mu$. Then:
\[
\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} \mu \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.
\]
*Units*: Convergence in probability/almost surely; $\mu$ has units of $X$.  
*Assumptions*: Identical distribution, finite first moment.  
*Breakdown*: For heavy-tailed variables with infinite mean (e.g., Pareto with $\alpha \leq 1$), the sample average does not converge to a constant but to a stable law.

**3. Normal Numbers (Borel’s Theorem)**
Define indicator function $d_k(x,b) = 1$ if the $k$-th digit of $x$ in base $b$ equals $a$, else $0$. Borel proved:
\[
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n d_k(x,b) = \frac{1}{b} \quad \text{for almost all } x \in [0,1] \text{ (Lebesgue measure)}.
\]
*Units*: Digit frequency dimensionless.  
*Assumption*: Uniform measure on Cantor-like construction.  
*Singularity*: Non-normal numbers form a set of measure zero but cardinality continuum; constructive examples (e.g., Champernowne constant) exist.

**4. Conditional Expectation (Radon-Nikodym Derivative)**
Given sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, for $X \in L^1(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$, $\mathbb{E}[X|\mathcal{G}]$ is the unique $\mathcal{G}$-measurable random variable satisfying:
\[
\int_G \mathbb{E}[X|\mathcal{G}] \, d\mathbb{P} = \int_G X \, d\mathbb{P}, \quad \forall G \in \mathcal{G}.
\]
Equivalently, if $\mathbb{Q} \ll \mathbb{P}$ on $\mathcal{G}$, then $\mathbb{E}[X|\mathcal{G}] = \frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}} \big|_{\mathcal{G}}$.  
*Units*: Same as $X$.  
*Assumption*: Absolute continuity $\mathbb{Q} \ll \mathbb{P}$.  
*Breakdown*: If $\mathbb{Q} \not\ll \mathbb{P}$ (mutually singular measures), conditional expectation undefined in classical sense; leads to robust statistics problems.

**5. Lusin’s Theorem**
Let $\mu$ be a finite Radon measure on a Hausdorff space $X$, $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ measurable. Then for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a closed set $F \subseteq X$ with $\mu(X \setminus F) < \epsilon$ such that $f|_F$ is continuous.  
*Units*: Measure $\mu$ in appropriate units (e.g., length, area).  
*Assumption*: $\mu$ is Radon (locally finite, inner regular on Borel sets, outer regular on open sets).  
*Singularity*: On non-locally compact spaces (e.g., infinite-dimensional Banach spaces), compact sets may have measure zero; approximation fails.

**6. Riesz Representation Theorem (for Linear Functionals)**
On a locally compact Hausdorff space $X$, every positive linear functional $\Lambda$ on $C_c(X)$ (continuous functions with compact support) corresponds uniquely to a regular Borel measure $\mu$ such that:
\[
\Lambda(f) = \int_X f \, d\mu, \quad \forall f \in C_c(X).
\]
*Units*: Functional output inherits units of measure times function.  
*Assumption*: Local compactness of $X$.  
*Breakdown*: In quantum mechanics (non-commutative geometry), states are positive functionals on $C^*$-algebras, not necessarily representable by classical measures.
