<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under infringement stress, both patentee and alleged infringer regress to familiar taxonomic habits. Galton notes that London carriage patterns resist change because of "the circumstances of London" [PAGE 26]; similarly, litigants regress to the three known types rather than entertain novel design spaces, since deviation within an accepted type is legally safer than claiming a fresh type and risking invalidation.

Counter-trap. The patentee anticipates that the infringer will recode a copy as a "fresh type" to escape the primary claim. The counter-trap is to file broad primary claims covering the subtype neighborhood and to oppose any subsidiary application as non-fundamental, forcing the infringer to bear the legal "cost" Galton mentions [PAGE 26] of proving fundamental modification.

Clock. The opposition process has a temporal deadline; improvements are "yearly seen" [PAGE 26], but legal decisions lag. The clock creates an asymmetry: the infringer can market a modification immediately, while the patentee's injunction arrives after weeks. Each party therefore simulates the other's classification strategy under time pressure, with the stable attractor—the old familiar pattern—serving as the Schelling point unless the deviation crosses θ_type before the clock expires.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any proposed deviation from the London carriage types must pass three veto layers before it can supersede the old patterns.

Physical layer. Horse traction and street geometry impose hard SI-bounded limits. An omnibus increasing passenger capacity beyond tractive force F_max [N] fails; a hansom whose turning radius exceeds London street width w [m] fails. These vetoes are independent of patent law.

Biomechanical layer. Driver and passenger ergonomics bound boarding/alighting frequency f_b [1/s], seat pitch s [m], and the whip/reach envelope. Galton's claim that patterns "cannot... be changed with advantage" [PAGE 26] is a biomechanical veto: deviations raising the driver's metabolic cost or dwell time at stops are selected against by the London circumstance.

Weather layer. London rain and fog impose visibility V [m] and wet-surface friction μ constraints. An open hansom performing poorly in precipitation relative to an enclosed omnibus is vetoed by the climate layer; an omnibus too heavy for muddy streets is vetoed by traction.

Adjudication rule. A design is admitted as a fresh type only if it passes V_physical ∩ V_biomechanical ∩ V_weather and d(i,i₀) > θ_type. Since Galton observes that yearly improvements fail to supersede the old patterns [PAGE 26], the empirical implication is that the joint veto set is currently empty for all proposed deviations; thus H(T) remains low and no bifurcation into a new type occurs.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
