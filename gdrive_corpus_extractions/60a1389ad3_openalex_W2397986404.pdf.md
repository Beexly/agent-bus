The thesis develops a graph-based abstraction for multi-sensor location tracking, called Ubiquitous Tracking (Ubitrack), and then maps it onto a distributed peer-to-peer middleware, a Dwarf-based implementation, and a mobile configuration architecture. The central derivation is: real spatial relationships form a complete graph \(G(\Omega)\), measurements form a sparse directed graph \(G(\Phi)\), and inferences form a graph \(G(\Psi)\) with attributed edges. The formalism defines measurement functions \(p:D_t\to S\times A\) and inference functions \(q:D_t\to S\times A\), where \(S\) is the spatial state space and \(A\) is the attribute space [PAGE 63][PAGE 67]. The 6-DOF state space is explicitly given as position plus unit quaternion: \(S_{6DOF}=\{(p_x,p_y,p_z)^T,(o_x,o_y,o_z,o_w)^T\}\) [PAGE 63]. Transitivity is formalized by position propagation \(p_{AC}(t)=p_{AB}(t)+o_{AB}(t)\cdot p_{BC}(t)\cdot o_{AB}^*(t)\) and orientation propagation \(o_{AC}(t)=o_{AB}(t)\cdot o_{BC}(t)\) [PAGE 69]. The evaluation function \(e:A^*\to\mathbb{R}\) selects among paths, with smaller values preferred, and the thesis states: “The general definition of the evaluation function provides a plug-in mechanism for designing arbitrary optimization criteria” [PAGE 69]. A concrete Ubitrack evaluation function is \(e_{Ubitrack}=\sqrt{\operatorname{trace}(ACA^T)}\), where \(C\) is a \(6\times6\) covariance matrix and \(A\) is a weighting matrix [PAGE 71].

The first gray zone is path explosion. The generic algorithm finds all paths from \(X\) to \(Y\), evaluates \(e\), and selects the minimum [PAGE 74]. But “the number of paths between two arbitrary nodes is exponential in the number of nodes of a graph” [PAGE 61]. The thesis therefore restricts practice to edgewise evaluation functions and shortest-path algorithms [PAGE 76]. The second gray zone is distributed search. The asynchronous Bellman-Ford variant has “both a worst case time and message complexity that is exponential in the number of nodes” [PAGE 83]. The lower bound is \(\Omega(c^n)\) messages and time \(\Omega(c^n d)\) for some \(c>1\), while the upper bound is \(O(n^n m)\) messages and \(O(n^{n+1}(l+d))\) time [PAGE 97][PAGE 98]. The third gray zone is the two-phase runtime assumption: “the two-phase implementation concept is based on some restricting assumptions” [PAGE 89]. The thesis assumes stable SR-graph topology and stable attributes, yet admits “a recomputation of the optimal path is required whenever a new measurement occurs, degrading the efficiency to an unacceptable level” [PAGE 101]. The fourth gray zone is service location: “it takes quite a long time (potentially up to several seconds) until a service location request can be answered” [PAGE 119], and “A reconfiguration on context change may take up to several seconds” [PAGE 161]. The fifth gray zone is numerical and graph-theoretic breakdown. Kalman gain inversion requires \(HP^-H^T+R\) to be invertible; if it is singular or ill-conditioned, the filter update is undefined or numerically unstable. Label-setting shortest-path algorithms “do not detect this problem and will not terminate then” when negative cycles exist [PAGE 77], and the thesis notes “The SR graph of the Ubitrack formalism is directed, but cycles might well occur and negative edge weights are allowed, too” [PAGE 77]. Thus the formal model is expressive, but its implementation is sound only under topology/attribute stability, nonnegative or cycle-detected weights, invertible covariance matrices, and service-location latency within the real-time budget. The audit threshold is when recomputation rate \(\lambda_{recompute}\) approaches measurement rate \(1/\Delta t_{meas}\), or when service-location delay \(\tau_{SLP}\) exceeds the application latency budget \(L_{max}\). At that point the two-phase optimization ceases to be an optimization and becomes a source of stale or late data.

Here \(C\) is the \(6\times6\) covariance matrix from the Gaussian error model \((e_{t,x},e_{t,y},e_{t,z},e_{r,x},e_{r,y},e_{r,z})^T\sim\mathcal{N}(0,C)\) [PAGE 51]; translational entries carry units of \(\mathrm{m}^2\), rotational entries carry units of \(\mathrm{rad}^2\) in the small-quaternion approximation, and \(e_{Ubitrack}\) is a real scalar in mixed units unless \(A\) normalizes them. Latency propagation is \(l(P)=\max_{i=1}^n l(e_i)\) [PAGE 71], in seconds or milliseconds. Update-rate propagation is \(u(P)=\max_{i=1}^n u(e_i)\), or alternatively \(u(P)=1/\operatorname{LCM}(u(e_1)^{-1},\ldots,u(e_n)^{-1})\), in hertz [PAGE 72]. Confidence propagation is \(c(P)=\prod_{i=1}^n c(e_i)\), a dimensionless probability in \([0,1]\) [PAGE 72]. Monetary cost propagation is \(mc(P)=\sum_{i=1}^n mc(e_i)\) [PAGE 73].

The Kalman filter operators are also explicit. The process model is \(x_k=Ax_{k-1}+Bu_k+w_{k-1}\) [PAGE 44]; the measurement model is \(z_k=Hx_k+v_k\) [PAGE 45]; noise assumptions are \(p(w)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q)\), \(p(v)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R)\) [PAGE 45]. The time update is \(\hat{x}^-_k=A\hat{x}_{k-1}+Bu_k\), \(P^-_k=AP_{k-1}A^T+Q\) [PAGE 45]. The measurement update is
\[
K_k=P^-_kH^T(HP^-_kH^T+R)^{-1},\quad
\hat{x}_k=\hat{x}^-_k+K_k(z_k-H\hat{x}^-_k),\quad
P_k=(I-K_kH)P^-_k
\]
[PAGE 46]. The exact singularity regime is \(\det(HP^-_kH^T+R)=0\), or more generally when \(HP^-_kH^T+R\) is not positive definite; then the Kalman gain is undefined or numerically unstable. The EKF is explicitly “just an ad-hoc approach to such problems, no optimality can be guaranteed” [PAGE 46]; its Jacobians are \(A_{[i,j]}=\partial f_{[i]}/\partial x_{[j]}\), \(W_{[i,j]}=\partial f_{[i]}/\partial w_{[j]}\), \(H_{[i,j]}=\partial h_{[i]}/\partial x_{[j]}\), \(V_{[i,j]}=\partial h_{[i]}/\partial v_{[j]}\) [PAGE 46][PAGE 47]. The distributed shortest-path operator updates \(d\leftarrow w+\operatorname{weight}(e_{j,i})\) when smaller [PAGE 96]. Breakdown regimes: label-setting algorithms fail on negative cycles [PAGE 77]; path enumeration is exponential [PAGE 61]; asynchronous Bellman-Ford is exponential in worst case [PAGE 83]; two-phase inference is invalid when topology or attributes change at measurement rate [PAGE 89][PAGE 101]; service-location latency can add seconds [PAGE 119][PAGE 161]. These are exact operational breakdowns, not toy ratios.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from Sutherland’s head-mounted display and Azuma’s AR definition [PAGE 16] through Weiser’s ubicomp vision [PAGE 21] to Kalman filtering [PAGE 44-47], Dijkstra and Bellman-Ford shortest paths [PAGE 76-78], the Dwarf AR framework [PAGE 114], and Ubitrack [PAGE 59]. The Ubitrack formalism retroactively reinterprets earlier tracker abstractions—OpenTracker, VRPN, Tinmith, the Location Stack, and EasyLiving—as restricted SR graphs: OpenTracker is a data-flow graph with static file-based configuration [PAGE 52]; VRPN handles static setups without runtime topology change [PAGE 53]; the Location Stack is layered but “currently does not address the real time and accuracy requirements of AR applications” [PAGE 54]. Ubitrack’s contribution is to make the graph directed, attributed, time-aware, and dynamically discoverable. The multi-week traps are: calibration drift, because “Static error can be eliminated by careful calibration” [PAGE 50] but only if calibration is repeated; service-location polling, because “constant requests have to be issued” [PAGE 119], so stale caches can persist for weeks; unbounded SR-graph growth, as in the hallway example where every new user adds nodes and edges [PAGE 100-101]; and attribute instability, because “Strictly speaking, a recomputation of the optimal path is required whenever a new measurement occurs” [PAGE 101]. A long deployment can therefore appear functional for days and then fail when churn, drift, or service-discovery latency crosses the two-phase assumption threshold.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In crisis, users regress to habitual manual coordination: they stop trusting the augmentation and fall back to speech, pointing, or known procedures. The emergency-building scenario explicitly notes that for mission-critical users “interacting with a device is often not their primary focus” [PAGE 11]. The counter-trap is over-automation: the system’s “best effort approach” does not have to provide the best possible solution [PAGE 29], so under churn it may silently select a stale path. The clock is brutal: “a latency of 1 ms will result in an error of 1 mm in the worst case” [PAGE 19], and the demo reports “overall additional latency of approximately 15 ms is acceptable” [PAGE 161]. But reconfiguration “may take up to several seconds” [PAGE 161]. A recursive theory of mind must therefore model the user’s trust state: if reconfiguration exceeds the user’s patience or the emergency clock, the user regresses to habit; if the system hides its uncertainty, the counter-trap is false confidence. The proof obligation is to measure time-to-reconfigure, time-to-first-correct-pose, and user recovery time under induced topology churn.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical vetoes are explicit in the thesis. Optical tracking requires “a line of sight between at least a single camera and a single marker has to be maintained all the time” [PAGE 28]. Magnetic tracking “suffers from relatively large error, especially if the tracking range is more than a meter” [PAGE 27]. Ultrasonic tracking suffers “distortions due to ambient noise, multipath reflections and variations in the speed of sound” [PAGE 25]. GPS requires “a direct line of sight to at least four satellites … making it impossible to work indoors” [PAGE 25]. Biomechanical veto: “The sensor sample rate needs to be twice as high as the expected frequency of motion, which is in case of head and arm motion ranging from 2 to 20 Hz” [PAGE 36]; if update rate falls below twice the motion frequency, dynamic error is unavoidable. Weather veto: passive markers “are subject to varying lighting conditions, e.g. a system with passive markers might work indoors, but has problems outdoors in full sunlight” [PAGE 29]. Adjudication rule: if any physical predicate \(V_{phys}\) is false—line-of-sight false, magnetic distortion above threshold, multipath above threshold, satellite visibility false, illumination outside range—the corresponding SR-graph edge must be vetoed or its covariance inflated before path evaluation. Otherwise the evaluation function can select a physically invalid edge because its stored attributes are stale.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Service-location latency veto. Counterfactual: if \( \tau_{SLP} > 2\,\mathrm{s} \) at the 95th percentile, the mobile setup cannot reconfigure within an interactive interval. Prediction: time-to-reconfigure exceeds \(2\,\mathrm{s}\), and the user observes a freeze or stale augmentation. Verification: instrument SLP calls and timestamp \(newSession/endSession\); veto the claim of seamless ad-hoc integration if p95 latency exceeds the threshold. This is supported by “potentially up to several seconds” [PAGE 119] and “may take up to several seconds” [PAGE 161].

Scenario 2: Topology-churn veto. Counterfactual: if graph churn rate \( \lambda_{churn} > 1/\Delta t_{meas} \), then path recomputation occurs at or above measurement rate. Prediction: CPU load saturates and path-search latency exceeds the 16.7 ms frame budget for 60 Hz tracking. Verification: synthetic \(G(n,p)\) graph with edges added/removed at 60 Hz; measure path-search time and CPU. Veto the two-phase real-time claim if median search time \(>16.7\,\mathrm{ms}\). This follows from the stable-topology assumption [PAGE 89][PAGE 100] and the admission that per-measurement recomputation is “unacceptable” [PAGE 101].

Scenario 3: EKF divergence veto. Counterfactual: if the true process is strongly nonlinear or non-Gaussian, the EKF covariance may diverge. Prediction: normalized estimation error squared (NEES) falls outside the chi-square confidence bounds for more than 5% of samples, or position error exceeds \(3\sigma\) from the filter covariance. Verification: motion-capture ground truth versus EKF estimate; compute NEES. Veto the Gaussian/EKF adequacy claim if the bound is violated. This is supported by “the EKF is just an ad-hoc approach … no optimality can be guaranteed” [PAGE 46] and the Gaussian error assumption [PAGE 51].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
