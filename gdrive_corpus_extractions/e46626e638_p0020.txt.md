The physiological layer yields \(q_p\) (pain quality) with \(P(\text{report } q_p \mid \text{nociception}) \approx 1\) and \(P(q_p \text{ is relational only}) \approx 0\), so \(V\) is satisfied. Because \(V \wedge \neg V\) is a contradiction, relationism is vetoed at Layer 1. Moreover, noetical clarity cannot be derived from sensation: \(I(\text{Sensation}; \text{Clarity}) \approx 0\) bits, so Malebranche’s injunction to silence the senses also fails as a complete account of knowledge, since feeling carries non-redundant information about the organism’s state.

Scenario 1 (sensory deprivation): Counterfactual — if all reality is relations among terms with no intrinsic relatum, then removing external relational input should nullify experience: \(E \mid \mathcal{R}_{\text{external}} = \varnothing \Rightarrow H(E) = 0\) bits. Verifiable prediction: relationism predicts subjects in flotation tanks report no intrinsic content. Actual psychology predicts vivid intrinsic imagery, affect, and thought with \(H(E \mid \varnothing_{\text{external}}) > \tau\) bits for threshold \(\tau > 0\). Veto: if measured intrinsic content exceeds \(\tau\), relationism is falsified.

Scenario 2 (congenital insensitivity to pain): Counterfactual — Malebranchean/Jame­sian relationist equivocation implies knowing and feeling pain are coextensive: \(K_p = 1 \Rightarrow F_p = 1\). Verifiable prediction: patients with intact injury knowledge but no pain report (\(K_p = 1, F_p = 0\)) should not exist. Veto: documented double dissociation \(P(K_p = 1 \wedge F_p = 0) > 0\) invalidates the identity of knowing and feeling.

Scenario 3 (blindsight): Counterfactual — if all knowledge is relational and relational knowledge entails phenomenal access, then above-chance discrimination \(D\) implies conscious visual awareness \(A\): \(I(D; A) = H(D)\). Verifiable prediction: blindsight patients should show no discrimination without awareness. Veto: measured \(I(D; A) \approx 0\) with \(P(D \text{ correct}) > 0.5\) triggers relationism’s collapse, because relational processing occurs without the “single feeling” James treats as real [PAGE 1].
