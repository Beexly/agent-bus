<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
James’s description can be formalized as a piecewise integration operator over time-delayed sensory streams. Let the visual field at time $t$ be $V_t$, represented as a function mapping retinotopic coordinates to intensity values. The observer’s gaze direction changes discretely or continuously.

Define a **Serial Field Transition** from state $i$ to $i+1$:
Given field $V_i$ with central focus at $c_i$ and peripheral extent $P_i$. A saccade re-centers attention to a new point $c_{i+1}$ within $P_i$ (or near it). The new field $V_{i+1}$ is acquired.

The **Concatenation Operator** $\mathcal{C}$ fuses sequential fields into a global map $M$. Following James’s logic:
1.  The overlap region between $V_i$ and $V_{i+1}$ (around the former periphery/new center) serves as a registration landmark.
2.  The non-overlapping part of $V_i$ (e.g., $A B$) is retained in a memory buffer $Mem(V_i \setminus Overlap)$.
3.  The new information $V_{i+1} \setminus Overlap$ (e.g., $D E$) is added.
4.  The operator performs a topological stitching: $M_{new} = Mem \cup V_{i+1}$ under the constraint that the overlap regions are identified as congruent.

Formally, let $\theta_i$ be the gaze angle at step $i$. The transformation from retinal coordinates $(u,v)$ in frame $i$ to a global spatial coordinate $x$ involves the rotation matrix $R(\theta_i)$. The integration is:
$$ M(x) = \bigcup_{i} \left\{ V_i(R^{-1}(\theta_i) \cdot x) \mid x \in \text{Domain}(V_i) \right\} $$
However, James emphasizes this is *not* a direct union but a constructed conception. Thus, we introduce a **Construction Confidence Weight** $w_i(t)$ that decays with time since acquisition and depends on salience:
$$ w_i(t) = \alpha \cdot e^{-\lambda (t - t_i)} \cdot S(V_i) $$
where $S$ is a saliency function. The final spatial perception at location $x$ is a weighted combination of all $V_i$ covering $x$, dominated by the most recent high-confidence observation.

**Units & Breakdown Regimes:**
*   **Spatial Unit:** Meters or degrees of visual angle.
*   **Temporal Unit:** Seconds (saccadic cycle ~200-400 ms).
*   **Information Unit:** Bits of resolved detail per field.
*   **Breakdown Regime 1 (High Speed):** If saccades are too fast ($dt <$ memory consolidation time), $V_{i+1}$ cannot be registered against $V_i$. Result: "streaming" or loss of spatial coherence.
*   **Breakdown Regime 2 (Low Redundancy):** If scenes lack distinct landmarks (e.g., uniform texture), the overlap registration fails. The concatenation becomes ambiguous, leading to misalignment or double-counting of segments.
*   **Singularity:** When attempting to perceive two widely separated points $A$ and $B$ without an intervening chain of overlapping fields, the construction process halts. The "space" between them is inferred from proprioceptive effort (eye movement amplitude) or prior knowledge, not direct construction.

**Citation:** [PAGE 185]: "Every single visual sensation or ‘field of view’is limited... A B and D E can never coalesce in one place... we must lose one to see the other."

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
James’s serial-field model sits at a critical junction in the genealogy of spatial cognition. Its intellectual ancestry includes:
*   **Kantian Synthesis:** Kant’s *Critique of Pure Reason* posited that space is an a priori intuition required to synthesize manifold sensations. James provides a mechanistic, empirical grounding for this synthesis: the "piecemeal" construction via eye movements is the empirical process fulfilling Kant’s transcendental requirement.
*   **Helmholtz’s Unconscious Inference:** Helmholtz argued that perception is hypothesis-testing based on past experience. James’s “memory image” that allows recovery of lost parts is a form of unconscious inference—the mind assumes continuity and projects the remembered $A B$ into the current spatial context of $C D E$.
*   **William James’s Own Radical Empiricism:** He rejects the idea of a pre-existing spatial container. Space is *made* of the relations between successive experiences. This directly influenced later behaviorists (who focused on observable scanning patterns) and Gestalt psychologists (who countered with holistic principles, e.g., Wertheimer’s phi phenomenon showing apparent motion can override serial displacement).

**Scientific Lineage & Modern Impact:**
This lineage flows into **David Marr’s computational theory of vision**, particularly the "2½-D sketch" (viewer-centered representation built from local patches) and the "3-D model" (object-centered). Marr explicitly modeled how saccadic eye movements stitch together local depth maps.
It also informs **predictive coding frameworks** (Friston, Clark), where the brain maintains a generative model of the environment. The "lost" field $A B$ is predicted from the current state and prior beliefs, minimizing prediction error when the gaze returns.
In **robotics and SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**, James’s principle is algorithmic law: a robot builds a map by integrating sensor readings over time, using odometry (analogous to eye position) to register new scans against the existing map. Failure to manage loop closure (when the robot revisits a location) leads to drift—exactly the kind of error James’s model implies if memory fidelity is imperfect.

**Multi-week Traps:**
A major trap in applying this model to modern AI is **over-indexing on the visual stream**. James noted the blind man uses tactile/auditory data. Current computer vision systems struggle with multi-modal spatial integration (e.g., aligning LiDAR point clouds with camera images under varying conditions). Another trap is assuming the "memory image" is static. Neurobiological evidence shows memory is reconstructive and malleable; thus, the spatial map is constantly being rewritten, not just appended to. Finally, there is a trap of **temporal myopia**: James describes a slow, deliberative construction. Yet, humans navigate complex spaces fluidly. The brain likely employs hierarchical chunking—integrating sub-segments locally while maintaining a coarse global schematic, a feature not explicit in the text but necessary for efficiency.

This leads to errors like believing two paths are contiguous when they are parallel, because the transition lacked sufficient landmark overlap. The agent's internal model becomes inconsistent with the environment's true geometry.

**Counter-Trap Strategy:**
To escape this, the mind must engage in **metacognitive monitoring**. It must detect inconsistencies in the constructed map (e.g., "if I go left then right, I should return to start, but I don't"). This triggers a shift from automatic concatenation to **active verification**—returning gaze to previously scanned areas to re-anchor memory, or switching modalities (reaching out to touch). This is a higher-order loop: the constructor evaluates its own product and initiates corrective actions.

**Clock Dynamics:**
Time pressure is a key variable. Under rapid navigation demands (e.g., driving), the luxury of careful overlap registration vanishes. The "clock" forces reliance on heuristics: treating the scene as globally stable, trusting peripheral vision more, and accepting lower-fidelity concatenations. Errors increase, but survival may depend on speed over precision. Conversely, in exploratory mode (slow walking), the clock allows for thorough scanning, robust memory encoding, and cross-modal checking, yielding a high-fidelity spatial model. The optimal strategy is thus dynamically regulated by task urgency and environmental familiarity.
