**2. Formalizing Risk vs. Uncertainty**
We distinguish two regimes based on the measurability of the probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$:
- **Risk Regime ($R$):** The outcome space $\Omega$ is known, and the probability measure $P$ is objectively quantifiable (e.g., actuarial tables). The expected return is $E[R] = \sum_{i} p_i x_i$. Here, deviations from $E[R]$ can be hedged or diversified away. Thus, any return attributed to risk is effectively a component of $I(K)$.
- **Uncertainty Regime ($U$):** The measure $P$ is undefined or subjective. There is no statistical aggregate to rely upon. This is the domain of Knightian uncertainty.

**3. The Information-Theoretic Limit of Management**
The "wages of management" $W$ can be approximated by the reduction in Shannon entropy $H$ achieved through coordination:
$$ W \propto \Delta H = H_{initial} - H_{post\_decision} $$
However, Knight argues that even when $H$ is minimized (perfect internal efficiency), $\Pi(U)$ persists. This is because external environmental shifts occur without a definable transition matrix.

**4. Breakdown Regimes and Singularity**
The classical model breaks down when attempting to assign a numerical value to $U$. If one assumes $P$ exists for all events (the "ergodic" assumption), then $\Pi(U) \to 0$, collapsing profit into interest. Knight’s singularity occurs when the number of unique decision contexts exceeds the historical data available to estimate $P$. In this regime, the standard deviation $\sigma$ becomes infinite or meaningless, and the only viable pricing mechanism is the entrepreneur's subjective judgment, which yields $\Pi(U)$.
