**Multi-hop derivation chain (source-anchored).** The thesis builds a single ladder from the scalar Bayes filter to a labeled multi-object consensus tracker, and every rung is a deliberate lossy projection. Hop 1 is the textbook recursion: predict with the Chapman–Kolmogorov integral and update by Bayes' rule, [PAGE 38] `p_{k|k-1}(x) = ∫ φ_{k|k-1}(x|ζ) p_{k-1}(ζ) dζ` and `p_k(x) = g_k(y_k|x) p_{k|k-1}(x) / ∫ g_k(y_k|ζ) p_{k|k-1}(ζ) dζ`. Hop 2 lifts this to a fusion centre that ingests every sensor's likelihood multiplicatively, [PAGE 39] eq. (2.10). Hop 2 is exactly what the network cannot afford, because the four design constraints are stated as "lack of a central fusion node", "scalable processing with respect to the network size", "each node operates without knowledge of the network topology", and "each node operates without knowledge of the dependence between its own information and the information received from other nodes" [PAGE 29]. Hop 3 therefore replaces the product-of-likelihoods with an information-theoretic surrogate, the weighted Kullback–Leibler Average, [PAGE 61] `p(·) = arg inf_{p(·)} Σ_{i∈N} ω_i D_KL(p‖p_i)`, which is shown to equal the normalized weighted geometric mean (2.106) and, for Gaussians, to collapse to Covariance Intersection on the information pair, [PAGE 62] `Ω = Σ_i ω_i Ω_i`, `q = Σ_i ω_i q_i`. Hop 4 replaces the global average with `L` regional averages via the consensus iteration (2.119), converging to the unweighted KLA because `lim_{l→∞} ω^{i,j}_l = 1/|N|` under primitivity and double stochasticity [PAGE 64]. Hop 5 raises the divergence from PDFs to random finite sets by defining the set-integral divergence (4.1) and proving the multi-object KLA (4.4) [PAGE 93]. Hop 6 restricts to i.i.d. cluster processes to obtain the CPHD fusion pair (4.15)–(4.16) [PAGE 95]. Hop 7 appends labels and re-proves the geometric-mean theorem, [PAGE 131] Theorem 3 eq. (6.5), then derives closed forms for Mδ-GLMB (6.6)–(6.8) and LMB (6.13)–(6.15). Hop 8 is the implementability wall: Gaussian-mixture exponentiation is not closed-form, so (4.27) is invoked with an explicit separation precondition [PAGE 97].

**Gray-zone #1 — the underweighting trap.** Consensus on posteriors is structurally lossy. Remark 11 states verbatim: "Whenever the number of consensus steps L tends to inﬁnity, consensus on posteriors is unable to recover the solution of the Bayesian ﬁltering problem. In fact, in the CP approach, the novel information undergoing consensus combined with the prior information, is unavoidably underweighted." [PAGE 64]. The CLCP fix splits the consensus into a prior channel and a likelihood channel and rescales the likelihood by `ρ^i_k ≥ 1` [PAGE 68]. But the rescaling is itself a two-sided trap: "it is important that, for any pair i, j, one has `ρ^i_k ω^{i,j}_L ≤ 1`" to preserve conservativeness, while "the product `ρ^i_k ω^{i,j}_L` should not be too small in order to avoid excessive underweighting" [PAGE 46]. The three admissible calibrations — `ρ = min_j 1/ω^{i,j}_L` (3.2), `ρ = |N|` (3.3), and `ρ = 1/b^i_{k,L}` (3.5) — are not interchangeable; (3.2) needs `Π^L`, which no node can compute for `L > 1`, and (3.3) needs `|N|`, which is unavailable in a time-varying network [PAGE 69].

**Gray-zone #2 — unit compatibility of set-density exponentiation.** Raising a FISST density to a fractional power is dimensionally incoherent unless the measure is fixed. The thesis concedes this explicitly at [PAGE 130]: "we adopt the measure theoretic notion of multi-object density given in [VSD05], which does not suffer of the unit compatibility problem in the integrals involving the product of powers of multi-object densities." This is the single most commonly elided step in downstream reimplementations.

**Gray-zone #3 — what marginalization actually preserves.** Proposition 2 guarantees the Mδ-GLMB matches "both (labeled) PHD and cardinality distribution of the original δ-GLMB density" [PAGE 114] — nothing more. The full labeled posterior is not preserved; the association history is aggregated by `w^{(I)} = Σ_ξ w^{(I,ξ)}` and `p^{(I)}(x,ℓ) = (1/w^{(I)}) Σ_ξ w^{(I,ξ)} p^{(ξ)}(x,ℓ)`. Any claim of "exactness" beyond first moment plus cardinality is a category error.

**Gray-zone #4 — the observability precondition.** Consensus is asserted to substitute for local observability only "provided that the network is strongly connected … and the jump Markovian system is collectively observable" [PAGE 77]. The CL-family additionally needs the *time-varying* effective sensor set `S^i_L = {j ∈ S : ω^{i,j}_L ≠ 0}` to be detectable [PAGE 79]. This is why CL diverges at small `L` while CP/CLCP remain bounded.

**Gray-zone #5 — metric saturation.** All headline comparisons use OSPA with `c = 600 [m]`, `p = 2` [PAGE 103], [PAGE 123], [PAGE 139]. OSPA is capped at `c`, so "comparable performance" statements are statements about the sub-600 m regime only; two trackers differing by kilometres can both read near `c` and appear equivalent.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is unusually explicit and can be reconstructed as a directed acyclic graph. Root: Gauss, *Theoria Motus* (1809), cited for least squares and the orbit-determination problem [PAGE 37] and [PAGE 157] entry [Gau04]. Second root: Kolmogorov (1950) and Stratonovich (1960) for the conditional Markov process [PAGE 160] entries [Kol50], [Str60]; Ho and Lee (1964) for the Bayesian stochastic estimation formulation [PAGE 160] entry [HL64]. Third root: Kalman (1960) and Kalman–Bucy (1961) [PAGE 160]. Fourth root: Mahalanobis (1936), which returns as the gating metric inside the GM fusion at [PAGE 97] — a 61-year retrospective arrow from classical statistics into mixture reduction. Fifth root: Mahler's FISST programme, 2000–2014, entries [Mah00] through [Mah14] on [PAGE 161], including the 2007 CPHD paper and the 2007 textbook. Sixth root: the Vo–Vo labeled RFS line — [VV13] (2013) conjugate priors, [VVP14] (2014) δ-GLMB, [RVVD14] (2014) LMB, [PVV+14] (2014) GLMB approximation, and the thesis's own [FVPV15] Mδ-GLMB and [FVV+15] consensus labeled RFS. Seventh root: the consensus literature, Olfati-Saber–Fax–Murray (2007) [PAGE 162], Xiao–Boyd–Lall (2005) [PAGE 163], Calafiore–Abrate (2009) [PAGE 158]; and the robust-fusion literature, Julier–Uhlmann (1997) CI [PAGE 160], Hurley (2002), Julier–Bailey–Uhlmann (2006) exponential mixture models, Mahler (2000) GCI, Clark–Julier–Mahler–Ristić (2010) [PAGE 158], Uney–Clark–Julier (2013) [PAGE 163]. Eighth root: the "spooky effect" critique, Franken–Schmidt–Ulmke (2009) [PAGE 159], which is the explicit motivation for moving from moment-based CPHD to labeled RFS [PAGE 9], [PAGE 130].

**Multi-week traps latent in the genealogy.** (T1) *Label-space blow-up.* Because labels are pairs `(k,i)` with `L_{0:k} = L_{0:k-1} ∪ L_k` disjoint [PAGE 59], the label space grows without bound; over a multi-week deployment `|F(L)|` grows exponentially and every Mδ-GLMB weight vector grows with it. The statement "the growth rate of the cardinality of `F(L_{0:k+1})` is exponential" [PAGE 115] is a warning, not a comfort. (T2) *Birth-model staleness.* The birth process is a fixed 10-component LMB with `r^{(ℓ)} = 0.09` and `P_B = diag(10^6, 10^4, 10^6, 10^4)` centred on the surveillance-border coordinates [PAGE 122, Table 5.1], [PAGE 139, Table 6.5]. Over weeks, any genuine change in entry geometry produces a persistent, invisible cardinality bias, and the source itself concedes "some of the LMB components cover a state space region where there is no birth. Clutter measurements are, therefore, more prone to generate false objects" [PAGE 122]. (T3) *Thesis-admitted gap.* The "future work" section names exactly the two multi-week failure modes: state-dependent `P_D` for heterogeneous fields of view, and "distributed measurement-driven object initialization" replacing a static birth process, [PAGE 148]. (T4) *Order-dependence via Salmond merging.* The thesis claims "by the associative and commutative properties of multiplication, the ordering of pairwise fusions is irrelevant" [PAGE 98] — true of multiplication, false of the subsequent merging step (Table 4.2, [PAGE 100]), whose `argmax_i α_i` seeding makes the reduction path-dependent. Over hundreds of consensus rounds this compounds into reproducible but arbitrary component sets.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**What each agent believes about its neighbours.** The fourth architectural constraint is that "each node operates without knowledge of the dependence between its own information and the information received from other nodes" [PAGE 29], [PAGE 36]. The agent therefore cannot maintain a model of *what its neighbours know*, only of *what they sent*. This is the theory-of-mind deficit that makes optimal Bayes fusion (which would need `f(X | I_i ∩ I_j)` per adjacent pair, [PAGE 92]) unaffordable, and it is precisely why the conservative KLA rule is adopted: the thesis invokes the Principle of Minimum Discrimination Information and quotes Jaynes verbatim — "the probability assignment which most honestly describes what we know should be the most conservative assignment in the sense that it does not permit one to draw any conclusions not warranted by the data" [PAGE 93]. The design intent is epistemic humility encoded as a fusion rule.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under link loss or low `P_D`, the baseline habit is to revert to the local posterior — the CP behaviour. This is the regression: a node that has lost its neighbours emits `g^i_k ⊕ p^i_{k|k-1}` with no consensus term, and because CP's fusion is a geometric mean, its confidence collapses toward the prior rather than toward the truth. The thesis quantifies the pathology directly: at `L = 1` CP yields PRMSE 1733/513 [PAGE 73, Table 3.3] while CLCP with `ρ = 1/b^i_{k,L}` yields 1645/332 — a 5 % steady-state improvement purchased solely by not letting the likelihood be crushed. The more dramatic regression is DIMM-CL, whose PRMSE entries are simply absent from Table 3.9 because "for all the considered values of L such an algorithm exhibited a divergent behaviour" [PAGE 82], and which the source states needs "a minimum number of `L = 10`" on circular networks with `N ≥ 4` nodes, and "`L = 80` consensus steps" on the communication-node-free network [PAGE 82].

**Counter-trap.** The counter-trap is the *over-correction*: assigning `ρ = |N|` restores asymptotic equivalence to the centralized filter as `L → ∞` (the source notes CLCP then becomes "mathematically equivalent … to the optimal distributed protocol of [OC10]" [PAGE 69]) but at moderate `L` it "leads to an overweighting of some likelihood components" [PAGE 69]. The evidence is unambiguous: at `L = 1`, `ρ = |N|` gives 1909/407 versus 1645/332 for (3.5); at `L = 2`, 1182/257 versus [PHONE]/491; the crossover only arrives near `L = 5`, where `ρ = |N|` gives 283/111 against 299/170 [PAGE 73]. So the trap has a *sign that flips in `L`*, and any fixed policy is wrong on one side of the crossover.

**Clock.** Three clocks are in play and they are not commensurate. (i) The physical sampling interval `T_s = 5 [s]` [PAGE 80], [PAGE 116], with a 129-sample navigation for the manoeuvring case study [PAGE 80] and a 200-sample horizon for the multi-object cases [PAGE 123], [PAGE 140]. (ii) The consensus clock `l = 1,…,L`, terminated at `L` steps with `L = 1` to `L = 5` in the manoeuvring study [PAGE 82] and a single step `L = 1` in all distributed labeled experiments [PAGE 140]. (iii) The communication clock — the thesis notes CLCP "requires transmitting, at each consensus step, approximately twice the number of ﬂoating-point data with respect to other algorithms like CP" [PAGE 71], and quantifies the CPHD case at "about `1.2 ÷ 1.8` kbytes" per consensus step at 20–30 components × 15 reals × 4 bytes [PAGE 106], with MATLAB timing "in the range of `45 ÷ 55` ms" per consensus step [PAGE 106]. The mismatch between a 5 s physical clock and a ~50 ms fusion clock means the binding constraint in practice is energy, not latency; and the mismatch between a 1-step fusion clock and a 10-to-80-step convergence requirement is exactly the gap that DIMM-CL falls into.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is any physical, geometric, or environmental condition that invalidates an algorithm's assumptions before a single line of code executes. Four are provable from the source parameters.

**Veto A — geometric observability (the strongest veto).** A single TOA sensor observes `h^i(x) = sqrt((p_x − x_i)² + (p_y − y_i)²)` [PAGE 81] eq. (3.38), which is invariant under rotation about the sensor: the pre-image of one range reading is a circle, not a point. Therefore a range-only node's local CPHD *cannot* be unimodal in position, and the thesis shows exactly this: "before consensus the CPHD reveals the presence of several false objects (see Fig. 4.9a); this is clearly due to the fact that the considered TOA sensor does not guarantee observability" [PAGE 104]. The veto is quantitative: with `σ_TOA = 100 [m]` the range annulus has width ~100 m but unbounded arc extent, so the local location PDF has effectively infinite positional entropy along the bearing direction. Any claim that a single TOA node "tracks" is vetoed. The mirror case is DOA with `σ_DOA = 1 [°]`, whose pre-image is a ray — unbounded along range. Only the *pairing* of range and bearing, or consensus across three or more TOA nodes [PAGE 121, Fig. 5.1], restores observability.

**Veto B — detection/clutter regime shift.** Three operating points are specified: high SNR `λ_c = 5, P_D = 0.99`; low SNR `λ_c = 15, P_D = 0.99`; low `P_D` `λ_c = 5, P_D = 0.7` [PAGE 139]. The veto is that GM-CLMB is *destroyed* by the first shift: "the GM-CLMB fails to track the objects. The problem is due to the approximation (5.65)-(5.66) made to convert a δ-GLMB to an LMB, becoming signiﬁcant with low SNR" [PAGE 118], with the causal chain spelled out as "(a) no local observability, (b) high clutter rate and (c) loss of the full posterior cardinality distribution after the probability density conversion" [PAGE 118–119]. Under `P_D = 0.7`, the source states flatly that "the only working distributed algorithm is, indeed, the GM-CMδGLMB" [PAGE 119–120]. Any deployment plan naming CLMB as the low-SNR fallback is physically vetoed.

**Veto C — kinematic envelope / manoeuvre saturation.** The tracked object reaches "speeds ranging in `[0, 300][m/s]` and acceleration magnitudes in `[0, 1.5] g`" [PAGE 80], with five coordinated-turn models at `ω ∈ {−1, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 1} [°/s]` and process-noise standard deviations `σ_w = 0.1 [m/s²]` for the `ω = 0` DWNA model and `σ_w = 0.5 [m/s²]` for the rest [PAGE 80]. The Markov transition matrix (3.37) has diagonal entries 0.90–0.95 and off-diagonal 0.05, i.e. a mean dwell time of 10–20 samples = 50–100 s per mode. This sets a hard veto on the fusion clock: with `T_s = 5 [s]` and a mode dwell of only 10–20 samples, a consensus chain needing `L = 80` steps per sample [PAGE 82] cannot resolve a turn before the mode has already switched. The empirical signature is in Table 3.9: at `L = 5`, DIMM achieves 648 m against centralized IMM's 446 m, and DGPB1 1052 m [PAGE 89]; the source attributes the gap to turns — "the main diﬃculty in tracking by communication nodes arises when a turn is approaching, especially, in this trajectory, during the last turn" [PAGE 83]. Communication nodes, which carry no measurement information, are the bottleneck: their presence "improves energy eﬃciency … but also hampers information diﬀusion across the network" [PAGE 81].

**Veto D — network topology / strong connectivity.** Consensus convergence demands primitivity, which demands strong connectivity [PAGE 63]. Metropolis weights (2.117)–(2.118) are only defined once each node knows `|N_i|` and `|N_j|` — i.e. two-hop degree knowledge — which the fourth architectural constraint does not grant. In a partition, the two components converge to *different* KLAs and there is no mechanism in the thesis that detects or repairs the split; the fusion rule is silent on partition detection.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each veto is stated as a falsifiable prediction with the metric, unit, and page anchor that would confirm or refute it.

**Veto 1 — "ρ^i_k = |N| is the correct CLCP calibration."** *Refutation prediction:* at `L = 1` on the 50-COM/10-SEN TOA network of Fig. 3.1, CLCP with `ρ = |N|` will produce a **higher** averaged PRMSE (in metres) than CLCP with `ρ = 1/b^i_{k,L}` from (3.5). *Ground truth already in source:* Table 3.3 reports, for `L = 1`, CP 1733 / CLCP `ρ=|N|` 1909 / CLCP (3.5) 1645 on the whole horizon, and 513 / 407 / 332 post-transient [PAGE 73]. The `|N|` policy is strictly worse at `L = 1, 2, 3, 4` and only overtakes at `L = 5` (283 vs 299). *Veto consequence:* any configuration asserting `ρ = |N|` as universally optimal must be rejected; the correct assertion is a crossover claim with the crossover located between `L = 4` and `L = 5` on this network, `N_mc = 1000` [PAGE 72].

**Veto 2 — "DIMM-CL is safe with a modest consensus budget."** *Refutation prediction:* on the 4-DOA / 4-TOA / 4-COM network of Fig. 3.3, DIMM-CL at `L ≤ 5` will exhibit **divergent** PRMSE for every `L` in `{1,2,3,4,5}`, while DIMM at `L = 5` will remain finite and below 700 m. *Ground truth already in source:* "the PRMSEs of DIMM-CL are not included in Table 3.9 since for all the considered values of `L` such an algorithm exhibited a divergent behaviour" [PAGE 82]; and DIMM at `L = 5` = 648 m [PAGE 89]. The source further quantifies the requirement as `L = 10` on circular networks and `L = 80` without communication nodes [PAGE 82]. *Veto consequence:* a proposed schedule with `L ≤ 5` on a COM-node-bearing network is physically inadmissible for the CL family; only the CP-derived DIMM/CLCP family survives that budget.

**Veto 3 — "Marginalizing association histories costs tracking accuracy in multi-sensor settings."** *Refutation prediction:* on the 3-TOA scenario with `λ_c = 15`, `P_D = 0.85`, `c = 600 [m]`, `p = 2`, over `N_mc = 100`, the Mδ-GLMB OSPA curve will remain within a small margin of the δ-GLMB curve, while the LMB curve will sit materially higher due to cardinality loss when 4–5 objects are co-present. *Ground truth already in source:* "the Mδ-GLMB tracker exhibits performance very close to that of δ-GLMB" and "the LMB exhibits poor performance and higher standard deviation due to losing some tracks when 4 or 5 objects are jointly present" [PAGE 123]; Fig. 5.10 plots all three at `c = 600 [m]` [PAGE 127]. *Mechanistic corroboration:* after each sensor update Mδ-GLMB retains exactly `|F(L)|` hypotheses [PAGE 116, Remark 17], so the per-sensor memory bound is time-invariant, whereas δ-GLMB grows as `|F(L) × Ξ| · Σ_I |Θ(I)|` [PAGE 116, Remark 16]. *Veto consequence:* the claim "marginalization degrades accuracy" is falsified for the labeled-PHD-plus-cardinality summary statistics; the honest claim is the narrower one — Proposition 2 preserves only labeled PHD and cardinality [PAGE 114], not the full posterior.

**Cross-cutting audit flag.** All three vetoes are evaluated under OSPA with a 600 m cutoff. Because the metric saturates at `c`, a fourth counterfactual test is required before any of the above is extended to wide-area operation: rerun with `c = 2000 [m]` and `p = 2`. If the Mδ-GLMB-vs-δ-GLMB gap remains small at the larger cutoff, the marginalization claim is robust; if it widens, the 600 m agreement was an artifact of saturation. The source does not report this sweep, so the claim is currently open rather than settled.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
