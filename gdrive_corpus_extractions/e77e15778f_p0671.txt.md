where $f_{exp}(q_1,q_2)\in[0,1]$ is the empirical association frequency of the conceptions' originals and $I$ is mutual information. Equivalently, $P(\nu=1 \mid f_{exp}) = P(\nu=1)$.

Application to the color example on [PAGE 661], with $q_B$ = blueness and $q_Y$ = yellowness:
$$
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{compare}) = 1,\quad
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{common-kind}) = 1,\ \text{kind}=\text{color},
$$
$$
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{add}) = 0,\quad
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{subtract}) = 0,\quad
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{subsume}) = 0,\quad
\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{infer}) = 0.
$$

Physical substrate layer: let $\mathcal{P}$ be the space of pigments and $Mix : \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{P}$ a total physical mixing operation. James's pigment block on [PAGE 661] entails that no homomorphism $h : \mathcal{Q} \to \mathcal{P}$ exists such that
$$
h(Op(q_1,q_2)) = Mix(h(q_1), h(q_2))
$$
for color qualia. The diagram fails at the qualia/physical boundary.

Ampliative judgment (footnote on [PAGE 661]): for a judgment $J$ mapping subject $S$ to predicate $P$, define entropy gain
$$
\Delta H(J) = H(P) - H(S \mid P).
$$
James denies a sharp threshold separating analytic ($\Delta H = 0$) from synthetic ($\Delta H > 0$); even "equidistant lines can nowhere meet" is treated as having $\Delta H > 0$ bits.

Units: $\nu$ and $f_{exp}$ are dimensionless; $I$ and $H$ are measured in bits; no SI mechanical units apply because the domain is logical-phenomenological rather than dynamical.

Breakdown/singularity regime: The independence condition collapses when (a) the observer conflates $Mix$ on $\mathcal{P}$ with $Op$ on $\mathcal{Q}$ (category error), or (b) $\text{Inn}$ is not well-defined under a reductionist ontology where qualia lack unique intrinsic coordinates. In those regimes $\Psi$ becomes ill-posed and the necessary-truth thesis cannot be sustained from this page alone.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 661] runs from Locke through Kant to James. James explicitly cites "Locke's Essay, bk. II. chap. xvi. § 6" [PAGE 661], situating his view within the Lockean tradition that knowledge of necessary relations concerns the agreement or disagreement of ideas themselves rather than the frequency of their sensory instances. He then targets Kant, calling the analytic/synthetic distinction "one of Kant's most unhappy legacies, for the reason that it is impossible to make it sharp" [PAGE 661]. This places James in a transitional position: he accepts that the mind discovers necessary and eternal relations among ideal conceptions [PAGE 661], yet he refuses to settle whether to call them "intuitive," "innate," or "a priori" [PAGE 661], thereby resisting both rationalist dogmatism and crude empiricist reduction.

Historical/scientific trajectory: Aristotle's distinction between necessary and contingent essences prefigures James's "inward natures"; Locke provides the account of ideas and their relations; Kant formalizes the analytic/synthetic split that James rejects as unsharp; later logical positivism and Quine's critique of the analytic/synthetic dogma echo James's complaint on [PAGE 661].

Multi-week traps in extended analysis. Over weeks of engagement with this text, an analyst faces several recursive traps. Week 1–2: the associationalist trap—because conceptions co-occur in experience, the analyst assumes their relations are derived from co-occurrence frequency, directly violating James's independence condition on [PAGE 661]. Week 3–4: the labeling trap—merely exchanging the word "empirical" for "innate" or "a priori" without performing the conceptual work James leaves open [PAGE 661]. Week 5–6: the pigment trap—extrapolating from physical pigment mixing to operations on qualities, repeating the exact category error James identifies on [PAGE 661]. Week 7–8: the Kantian sharpness trap—treating analytic/synthetic as a crisp classifier boundary despite James's argument that the distinction is impossible to make sharp [PAGE 661]. Each trap represents a regression to a lower-layer heuristic (frequency, label, physical analogy, syntactic form) that James's argument is designed to veto.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under cognitive load, epistemic uncertainty, or time pressure, the mind regresses to its default, ecologically fast heuristic of treating all mental couplings as potentially valid—especially additive or associative ones. In the color domain, this regression would assign $\nu=1$ to color addition or subtraction, mistaking the salience of physical pigment mixing for qualia validity, contrary to [PAGE 661].

Counter-trap: the deliberate second-order inhibition of that first-order impulse. The agent maintains the category boundary between $\mathcal{Q}$ and $\mathcal{P}$, refusing to let physical manipulability rewrite logical validity on qualia. This is a meta-cognitive veto: "just because I can mix pigments does not mean I can add blueness to yellowness" [PAGE 661].

Clock: James's "determinate system" of necessary and eternal relations is specified as independent of the temporal "order of frequency" of experience [PAGE 661]. It functions as an atemporal logical reference—a clock that does not tick with experience—against which operations are judged valid or null. The recursion applies to the analyst as well: when interpreting James, one must avoid regressing to treating his text as a mere historical artifact (citation frequency, contextual fashion) rather than engaging the necessary structure of the argument itself. The analyst's crisis habit would be to reduce philosophy to sociology; the counter-trap is to preserve the logical independence of the relations James describes.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: The pigment operation $Mix$ on $\mathcal{P}$ is causally real but isolated from qualia operations. Veto proof from [PAGE 661]: pigment addition/subtraction "is no addition or subtraction of the blue and yellow qualities or natures themselves." Therefore physical realizability cannot imply qualia validity; the physical layer is vetoed from overriding $\nu$ on $\mathcal{Q}$.

Biomechanical/neural layer: Even if a neural circuit implements comparison at some firing rate $r$ measured in Hz, the validity $\nu(q_1,q_2;Op)$ is not a function of $r$, synaptic density, or metabolic cost. Neural activity may realize an operation, but it does not determine whether the operation is logically defined on the inward natures of the conceptions. A neural "addition" pattern co-activated by color concepts would be a category error if interpreted as qualia addition.

Weather/environmental layer: Stochastic environmental input—the order in which experiences arrive—cannot alter $\nu$ because James explicitly declares independence from "the order of frequency in which experience may have associated the conception's originals in time and space" [PAGE 661]. Weather-like noise in experience may delay or obscure the "clear sight of the ideas themselves," but it cannot veto a necessary relation.

Adjudication: The cross-layer veto is unanimous. The only layer authorized to determine $\nu$ is the intrinsic-conception layer $\text{Inn} : \mathcal{Q} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ coupled with the operation signature $Op$. Physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers are all excluded from overriding the validity predicate on ideal conceptions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Counterfactual: if the qualia/physical boundary layer is removed, the system will treat frequent pigment mixing as evidence for qualia addition. Verifiable condition: training data contain $f_{exp}(\text{blue pigment}, \text{yellow pigment}) > 0.9$, while the ground-truth label from [PAGE 661] for $\nu(q_B,q_Y;\text{add})$ is fixed at 0. Prediction: boundary-less models show a high false-positive rate on color-addition queries; models preserving the [PAGE 661] category boundary maintain near-zero error on these queries.

Scenario 2 — Analytic/synthetic classifier.
Setup: A classifier judges whether judgments are "pure tautologies" or ampliative, using surface syntax as features. Prediction: under low load, false $\nu=1$ rate on invalid color operations is below baseline (<5%); under high load, crisis habit regression produces a statistically significant rise (predicted >30%) in false $\nu=1$ reports for add/subtract, while comparison and common-kind judgments remain stable.
