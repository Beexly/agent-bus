*   $W_{bot}$ is a weight matrix for bottom-up sensory pathways.

Modified Operator:
$$\frac{ds}{dt} = -k \nabla_s E(s, p; \theta) + W_{bot} \cdot u(t) + \eta(t) \quad \text{(1)}$$
When $k$ is small, the system becomes highly sensitive to the input $u(t)$ and noise $\eta(t)$, as the stabilizing force of the prior $p$ is weakened.

Specifically, it carves out a new, deep attractor basin centered around the representation of "General Grant's photograph", let's call this state $s_{GG}$.
Let $E_{new}(s) \approx V(s) + \lambda \cdot D(s, s_{GG})^2$, where $V(s)$ is some baseline potential and $D$ is a distance metric in representational space. The suggestion makes $s_{GG}$ a strong local minimum.

In equation (1), with $k \approx 0$, this small input is no longer suppressed by the top-down field. It acts as a persistent perturbation that pushes the state $s$ from its resting point towards the region of influence of the $s_{GG}$ attractor.

Once $s$ enters the basin of attraction of $s_{GG}$, the dynamics become dominated by the shape of $E_{new}$. Even if $u_{dot}$ is constant, the system evolves to $s_{GG}$, which is a rich, high-dimensional state representing a detailed face and uniform. When the experimenter magnifies the dot, $u_{dot}$ changes (its spatial spread increases). Because the system's state $s$ is now locked in the vicinity of $s_{GG}$ but still weakly influenced by the ongoing $u_{dot}$ (via the $W_{bot}u(t)$ term), a change in $u_{dot}$ causes a corresponding shift in the equilibrium position within the $s_{GG}$ basin. *   **Philosophy of Perception:** This analysis is a direct challenge to naive realism. This prefigures the Gestalt psychologists' insights about top-down organization.

A common trap is to read James anachronistically through the lens of later cognitive science. One might think he is describing a simple failure of "veridical perception." When the eye is nudged, there is a mismatch between the intended movement and the actual retinal slip. The brain, in its suggestible state, interprets this unexpected retinal motion as motion of the *object* (the photograph), not of the eye. This shows the hallucination resides in a cortical stage *after* initial sensory fusion, where visual signals are combined with motor and proprioceptive estimates. A severe vestibular disorder (e.g., vertigo) would likely veto the entire process, as the conflicting signals from the inner ear would overwhelm the fragile suggested reality.

**Physical Veto (Lighting and the Mirror):**
The example of reflecting the dot in a mirror is equally powerful. It introduces a new physical law (specular reflection) into the phenomenological space. For the hallucination to coherently incorporate this—"the photograph is now reflected"—the subject's brain must be running a simulated physics engine that includes optics. This engine is being driven by the real-time optical changes caused by the mirror. If the room lights were suddenly extinguished, removing the dot entirely, the hallucination would likely collapse (unless strongly maintained by suggestion alone, which is rarer). This demonstrates the continuous dependence on the physical substrate. The "objectivity" Binet speaks of is the hallucination's enslavement to the rules of the physical world as mediated by the senses.

Consider the subject's peripheral state. James mentions "any peripheral stimulation." More subtly, ambient auditory noise (traffic, conversation) could compete for attentional resources. While the hypnotic state narrows focus, extreme environmental stressors could act as a veto by pulling residual attention away from the delicate coupling between the dot and the suggestion. The stability of the hallucinatory state requires a controlled environment—not just to facilitate induction, but to maintain the precise conditions where a minimal visual anchor can dominate a major cognitive construct.

**Conclusion of Adjudication:**
These examples are not incidental; they are constitutive. James uses them to build a *multimodal* theory of hallucination. The hallucination is not a disembodied idea; it is a body-centered event, tethered to the exact position of the eyes, the lighting in the room, and the quietness of the space. Any significant deviation in these physical layers can destabilize the illusion, proving that even the most fantastical mental events are deeply embodied and embedded.
