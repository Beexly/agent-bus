Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression. When an experimentalist faces Hull’s unit question—“IN WHAT UNITS SHALL THE DIFFERENCE... BE MEASURED?” [PAGE 198]—and finds no clean answer, the habitual regression is to collapse the multidimensional stimulus space into a single scalar: physical energy difference \Delta S, or worse, to assume s \propto S. This is the crisis habit because it restores false tractability precisely where the text warns against linearity and monotonicity. The regressed model ignores the surround terms S', S'', ... and treats the retina as a collection of independent light meters.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to respond to non-monotonicity and interaction by claiming that no generalization law is possible, that every figure, rotation, and contrast patch requires its own idiosyncratic gradient. This overcorrects by making the parameter space unbounded: every retinal neighborhood and every spatial frequency becomes a separate dimension. The model becomes unidentifiable and loses predictive power, which is just as fatal as the oversimplified linear model.

Clock. The temporal dimension determines which layer of the system is being sampled. Immediately after conditioning, generalization may reflect feedforward afferent discharge \dot{s}_i = f(S_i). After delays of seconds to minutes, retinal lateral inhibition and adaptation recruit the S', S'' terms, reshaping the gradient. Across days to weeks, habit strength and central motivational states reweight the dimensions. Therefore the “clock” of the measurement—inter-trial interval, retention delay, circadian phase, and weeks of training—selects whether the observer sees a clean gradient, an interaction-dominated fold, or an adapted drift. A design that fixes only one time scale and claims to have measured “the” generalization gradient commits a temporal category error.

Physical / biomechanical / weather veto proof.

Three layers are adjudicated for veto validity.

Layer 1 — Physical optics and radiometry. Stimulus energy is characterized by spectral radiance L(\lambda) [W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹·nm⁻¹], spatial distribution across the retina, and visual angle [degrees]. If two stimuli differ only by rotation \phi but subtend the same retinal area and luminance, the physical energy at each receptor may be identical; the difference exists only in the population spatial code.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical / neural. The retinal transform is \dot{s}_i = f(S_i; \{S_j\}; t), units spikes·s⁻¹. Center-surround receptive fields implement lateral inhibition. Photoreceptor integration time constants (roughly tens of milliseconds for cones, longer for rods) and adaptation state set the effective gain. When ambient illumination shifts, the operating point of f moves along its non-linear, potentially non-monotonic curve [PAGE 198].

Layer 3 — Weather / environmental. Ambient skylight, cloud cover, time-of-day spectral shifts, and atmospheric scattering alter the incident spectrum and total irradiance before the stimulus even reaches the eye. These are not mere noise; they change the S', S'' surround field and the adaptation baseline, thereby redefining the units in which CS–ES difference is measured.

Veto proof. An experiment is vetoed if it claims to measure a primary generalization gradient while violating any of the following:
- Veto V1 (Isolation violation): Varying S_i while not holding or measuring the surround energies \{S_j\}. Because \partial \dot{s}_i / \partial S_j \neq 0 in general [PAGE 198], the measured effect of S_i is confounded by lateral interaction.
- Veto V2 (Linearity violation): Fitting s = aS + b across a luminance span where the text establishes s(S) is “by no means linear” [PAGE 198]. If residuals show systematic sign changes in ds/dS, the model is structurally invalid.
- Veto V3 (Emergence violation): Treating curvature, size, and rotation as independent scalar continua when they are functionals of a shared spatial energy map. If retinal sampling density cannot resolve the spatial frequency of the figure, the gradient collapses unpredictably (Lashley regime [PAGE 198]).
- Veto V4 (Environmental drift): Conducting multi-week sessions without controlling ambient illumination. A shift in weather or time-of-day changes the adaptation point, rendering pre- and post-shift gradients incommensurable.

Exact singularity: When surround inhibition equals center excitation, \dot{s}_i \to 0 for a range of S_i. The difference measure D(S_CS, S_ES) then has a zero denominator in any normalized contrast metric, and the generalization gradient is undefined (0/0 singularity).
