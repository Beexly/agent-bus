*Heat semigroup.* e^{εΔ}f := ∫_M K_ε(·,y)f(y)dy [PAGE 2]. On M = ℝⁿ, K_ε(x,y) = (4πε)^{−n/2} exp(−‖x−y‖²/(4ε)) [PAGE 2, eq. (2)]. Units: since ∫_{ℝⁿ}K_ε(x,y)dy = 1 over a measure of dimension Lⁿ, [K_ε] = L^{−n}, so e^{εΔ} is unit-preserving on functions. Parameter: [ε] = L²T^{−1}, matching the paper's statement that "the parameter ε has physical dimension as thermal diffusivity" [PAGE 6].

*Dimensional consistency certificate for the regularized Lagrangian.* With nats-normalized entropy E(ρ) = ∫_M ρ log ρ and Fisher information I(ρ) = ½∫_M |∇ρ|²/ρ [PAGE 6], in ℝⁿ: [ρ] = L^{−n}, [∇ρ] = L^{−n−1}, hence [|∇ρ|²/ρ] = L^{−n−2} and [I] = L^{−2}. Meanwhile the velocity potential S satisfies ρ̇ + div(ρ∇S) = 0 with [ρ̇] = L^{−n}T^{−1}, forcing [∇S] = LT^{−1} and [S] = L²T^{−1}; therefore [⟨S,S⟩*_ρ] = L²T^{−2}. Matching ε²I(ρ) to that kinetic term requires [ε²] = L⁴T^{−2}, i.e. [ε] = L²T^{−1}. The Lagrangian L = ½⟨ρ̇,ρ̇⟩_ρ + ε²I(ρ) [PAGE 6, eq. (10)] is therefore dimensionally closed **iff** ε is a diffusivity. This is the cross-layer veto: an ε carrying any other dimension breaks H(ρ,S) = ½⟨S,S⟩*_ρ − ε²I(ρ) [eq. (13)].

*Governing integral equation.*
∂_s f = −f − log(∫_M K_ε(·,y)e^{g(y)}dy) + log ρ₀, ∂_s g = −g − log(∫_M K_ε(·,y)e^{f(y)}dy) + log ρ₁ [PAGE 2, eq. (1)], f,g: M × [0,∞) → ℝ, with ρ₀, ρ₁ "strictly positive and smooth densities with the same total mass relative to the Riemannian volume form" [PAGE 2]. Mass equality is a hard admissibility constraint, not a normalization convenience.

*Almost-linear rewriting.* With T_ε(f) := log(e^{−f}e^{εΔ}e^f), the system becomes ∂_s f = −f − g + log ρ₀ − T_ε(g), ∂_s g = −g − f + log ρ₁ − T_ε(f); "(f,ε) ↦ T_ε(f) is a C^∞ mapping, for example in Sobolev topologies H^s" [PAGE 2, Remark 1].

*Exponential variables.* a = e^f, b = e^g gives ∂_s a = −a log(ae^{εΔ}b/ρ₀), ∂_s b = −b log(be^{εΔ}a/ρ₁) [PAGE 2, eq. (4)], requiring a ≪ ρ₀, b ≪ ρ₁.

*Atomic collocation.* ρ₀ = Σ_{i=1}^N p_iδ_{x_i}, ρ₁ = Σ_{i=1}^N q_iδ_{y_i} [PAGE 2, eq. (3)] reduces the integral operators to the matrix operators (K_εb)_i and (K_ε^⊤a)_i using K_ε(y_i,x_j) = K_ε(x_j,y_i).

*Finite-dimensional ODE and its loss of meaning.* ∂_s f = −f − log(K_ε exp g) + log q, ∂_s g = −g − log(K_ε^⊤ exp f) + log p [PAGE 3, eq. (5)]. Breakdown: "this ODE loses its meaning (or rather its connection to (4)) if ε = 0" [PAGE 2–3].

*Discretization operator.* Trotter splitting + forward Euler: f^{(k+1)} = (1−h)f^{(k)} − h log(K_ε exp g^{(k)}) + h log q; g^{(k+1)} = (1−h)g^{(k)} − h log(K_ε^⊤ exp f^{(k+1)}) + h log p [PAGE 3, eq. (6)]. **Definition 1**: "The discrete Sinkhorn algorithm on M is given by the time discretization (6) with h = 1." At h = 1, eq. (7) reproduces a^{(k+1)} = q/(K_εb^{(k)}), b^{(k+1)} = p/(K_ε^⊤a^{(k+1)}).

*Gradient-flow identity.* Fisher–Rao metric ⟨∂_sσ,∂_sσ⟩_σ = ∫_M (∂_s a)² e^{εΔ}b / a [PAGE 8, eq. (17)]; ∂_s a = −a log(ae^{εΔ}b/ρ₀) [eq. (16)] is the gradient flow of F₁(a) = H_{ρ₀}(ae^{εΔ}b); analogously for F₂.

*Linearization and spectral breakdown.* Test system ẋ = −x − (1−δ)y, ẏ = −(1−δ)x − y, δ = 10⁻², "roughly corresponding to the system (6) with ε ≃ δ" [PAGE 4]. Verbatim regimes: "The optimal step-size is where the two eigenvalues meet at about h ≈ 1.75. In the interval h ∈ [1.75, 2) we expect an almost linear decrease of the error, as the two eigenvalues are the same. For h ≥ 2 the method is not (linearly) stable." [PAGE 4]

*Measure-theoretic operator.* T_ε: ν ↦ ν₀ ∧ ν₁ with ∫_M h dν₀ = ∫_{M×M} K_ε(x,y)h(x)dν and ∫_M h dν₁ = ∫_{M×M} K_ε(x,y)h(y)dν [PAGE 8]; Schr ödinger's problem as T_ε(α∧β) = μ₀∧μ₁ [PAGE 9, eq. (18)]; T_ε: M₊ → P₊ [eq. (19)].

**Exact singularity/breakdown regimes.** (B1) ε → 0⁺: kernel degenerates to δ, log undefined, flow decouples from (4). (B2) h ≥ 2: loss of linear stability of the Trotter–Euler map [PAGE 4]. (B3) h ∈ (1,2): stable but error decay asymptotically linear, not exponential. (B4) a or b reaching zero: log-divergence in (4)/(16). (B5) M non-compact without |∫∫log K_ε dμ₀dμ₁| < ∞: Beurling Thm. II inapplicable [PAGE 9]. (B6) backward heat b̄ solved forward: exponentially amplified high modes; remedied only by the time reversal b(x,t) = b̄(x,1−t) [PAGE 7]. (B7) unequal total mass of ρ₀, ρ₁: (1) is ill-posed.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper is an explicit act of retroactive canon-formation, and it says so. Schr ödinger arrived at (15) in 1931 "by studying the most likely stochastic path for a system of particles from initial distribution ρ₀ to final distribution ρ₁," gave physical arguments, "but mathematically it was left open"; Bernstein addressed it at the 1932 ICM in Zürich; "A full resolution, however, did not come until 1960 through the work of Beurling" [PAGE 8]. Beurling's stated objective, quoted verbatim: "to derive general results concerning systems like (15) and, in particular, to disclose the inherent and rather simple nature of the problem." And then the retrocausal pivot: "But to his astonishment (and slight annoyance) no-one took notice. In fact, Schr ödinger's bridge problem was itself largely forgotten among physicists and mathematicians" [PAGE 8].

The causal arrow the paper draws runs *backwards* from Cuturi 2013 and Sinkhorn 1964 to Beurling 1960: "Beurling's results can be viewed as a generalization from matrices to measures of Sinkhorn's theorem [22] on doubly stochastic matrices, only it came four years before Sinkhorn's result" [PAGE 9]. The named algorithm is the younger object; the general theorem is the elder. Modin also notes the geographic-temporal dislocation: Beurling worked "independently of Kantorovich's formulation of optimal transport in terms of measures on a product space (which came to general knowledge in the West in the late 1960's)" [PAGE 9].

Re-entry path: Zambrini 1986 "rediscovered" and advocated both, while working "with an alternative version of Nelson's framework for stochastic mechanics" [PAGE 8]; then Léger 2019 (Wasserstein-geometric presentation, "close to mine and contains more details" [PAGE 1]); Léger–Li 2021; Berman 2020 on parabolic OT and geometric Monge–Ampère, whose dynamics "he proved, approximates the dynamics of f^{(k)} ↦ f^{(k+1)}" [PAGE 2]. The MSC codes on [PAGE 1] — 49Q22, 35Q49, 37K65, 65R20 — are the genealogy in compressed form: optimal transport, PDE, Euler–Arnold infinite-dimensional geometry, numerical integral equations.

**Multi-week traps in this lineage.** (T1) *The scaling-algorithm trap.* Treating Sinkhorn as matrix scaling only. The corpus's claim is stronger and narrower: it is a space-and-time discretization of (1) [PAGE 1]. Weeks are lost trying to derive stability from scaling theory alone, when the stability region comes from the integrator [PAGE 4]. (T2) *The single-gradient-structure trap.* "The question of assigning one Riemannian gradient structure to the entire flow is more intricate, since the functionals F₁ and F₂ depend on both a and b" [PAGE 8]. Attempting to close this in a week is a known dead end. (T3) *The gauge-fixing trap.* Believing a and b are individually determined. They are defined only up to (e^σa, e^{−σ}b̄) [PAGE 7], and the measure version makes non-uniqueness structural [PAGE 9]. Anything that fits a and b separately will fit infinitely many gauges. (T4) *The annealing trap.* Assuming ε is a schedule parameter with no physical meaning; it is a diffusivity with units [PAGE 6]. (T5) *The ε=0 shortcut.* "this ODE loses its meaning ... if ε = 0" [PAGE 2–3]. (T6) *The over-relaxation-as-free-lunch trap.* h ∈ (1,2) buys speed; h ≥ 2 buys divergence [PAGE 4].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis habit regression.** Under pressure, the practitioner reverts to h = 1 because Definition 1 declares it the Sinkhorn algorithm [PAGE 3]. This is correct but inert: h = 1 sits far from the spectral optimum near h ≈ 1.75 [PAGE 4]. The regression is not a mistake in identity — it is a refusal to use the integrator's free parameter. A second regression: reverting to "ε is just a smoothing knob" and shrinking it, when the source says the near-linear regime and the numerically stable regime are on opposite sides.

**Counter-trap.** The counter-trap is the over-correction: pushing h past 1.75 toward 2 in pursuit of the eigenvalue collision, or trying to assign a single gradient structure to the pair (F₁,F₂). Both are attractive because they are locally justified — larger steps do march faster "provided that the time step is small enough for the method to remain stable" [PAGE 3] — and both fail globally: "For h ≥ 2 the method is not (linearly) stable" [PAGE 4]. The way out is neither h = 1 nor h → 2, but reading the spectral radius off the test map and stopping where the eigenvalues meet.

**Clock.** Two clocks, and they must not be confused. The *inner* clock is k, the iteration index; the *outer* clock is s, the flow time of (1), with h the exchange rate between them. The third clock is ε, which is not a clock at all despite behaving like one in annealing schedules — it is a diffusivity [PAGE 6]. Confusing the ε-scale with the s-scale is the deepest recursion error in this literature, because the limit "s → ∞ (and possibly ε → 0 simultaneously)" is explicitly flagged as subtle [PAGE 2], meaning the two clocks do not commute.

**Recursive model of the reader.** The paper anticipates three readers. Reader A believes Sinkhorn is a matrix-scaling trick; the paper answers with [PAGE 1] and the Madelung linearization. Reader B believes entropic OT is a numerical hack; the paper answers with eq. (10)–(14) and the statement that "This viewpoint on entropic regularization is known to experts since long before the Sinkhorn algorithm became popular for OT" [PAGE 1]. Reader C believes the unregularized problem is the ε → 0 endpoint; the paper answers with (B1). A correct recursive model must hold all three simultaneously and must also model the paper's own epistemic hedges — "I expect this would yield a connection to the non-linear parabolic equation studied by Berman [3]" [PAGE 2], "we expect an almost linear decrease" [PAGE 4]. Hedges are load-bearing; promoting them to theorems is the habit regression of the secondary literature.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

This source contains **no biomechanical, meteorological, or physical-sensor layer**. Asserting one would violate the zero-fabrication invariant. The legitimate cross-layer vetoes available in this corpus are mathematical-physical, and they are adjudicated as follows.

**Veto 1 — Dimensional.** Passed only if [ε] = L²T^{−1}. Proof: the kinetic form ⟨S,S⟩*_ρ = ∫_M|∇S|²ρ requires [S] = L²T^{−1} from the continuity constraint ρ̇ + div(ρ∇S) = 0, hence [⟨S,S⟩*_ρ] = L²T^{−2}; dimensional closure of L = ½⟨ρ̇,ρ̇⟩_ρ + ε²I(ρ) then forces [ε²I] = L²T^{−2} with [I] = L^{−2}. Corroborated verbatim: "the parameter ε has physical dimension as thermal diffusivity" [PAGE 6]. *Any* parameterization in which ε is dimensionless or carries mass units is vetoed.

**Veto 2 — Positivity of the kernel.** Theorem 1 requires K_ε > 0 for the map T_ε: M₊ → P₊ [PAGE 8–9, eq. (19)]. For the Gaussian (2) this holds for every ε > 0 and every pair (x,y) ∈ ℝⁿ×ℝⁿ, so the operator is positivity-preserving and the logarithm in (4)/(5) is defined. At ε = 0 the kernel is a Dirac mass and positivity in the pointwise sense fails — an independent confirmation of breakdown B1.

**Veto 3 — Compactness / kernel bounds.** "The condition that M is compact is used to obtain a positive lower and upper bound on the kernel K_ε (these are, in fact, the only conditions that Beurling's proof imposes on K_ε)" [PAGE 9]. On a non-compact M the automorphism claim of Theorem 1 is not available; only Theorem 2 applies, requiring |∫_M∫_M log K_ε dμ₀dμ₁| < ∞. A claim resting on Theorem 1 while silently assuming non-compact M is vetoed.

**Veto 4 — Mass conservation.** (1) presumes ρ₀, ρ₁ have "the same total mass relative to the Riemannian volume form" [PAGE 2]. If not, no coupling exists and both the flow and the iteration are vacuous. This is the analogue of a conservation-law veto.

**Veto 5 — Well-posedness direction of the heat flow.** "It is, of course, not good to work with backward heat flows, but there is an easy fix" [PAGE 7]. Running b̄ forward in s is vetoed; the only admissible formulation is the time-reversed forward pair with mixed boundary conditions a₀b₁ = ρ₀, a₁b₀ = ρ₁.

**Veto 6 — Integrator stability.** h ≥ 2 is vetoed by the eigenvalue analysis [PAGE 4]; h ∈ (1,2) is admitted with the caveat that decay is asymptotically linear rather than exponential.
