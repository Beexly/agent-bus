\]
Units: inverse-pass-count. Verbatim: "Closeness centrality is defined as the inverse of the farness, where higher values assume a positive meaning in the node proximity" [PAGE 4].

*Singularity regime:* \(C_C(v)\to\infty\) when \(\sum_{w}\mathrm{dist}(v,w)\to 0\). This occurs only for isolated nodes; in a live match with at least one completed pass between any pair, \(C_C\) is bounded. The genuine breakdown regime is when a player is never passed to — then \(C_C\) is undefined and the metric treats "not in the network" identically to "infinite farness," which collapses the semantic distinction the paper relies on.

**4. Betweenness centrality (normalized directed form).**
\[
C_B(v)=\sum_{s\neq v\in V}\sum_{t\neq v\in V}\frac{\sigma_{st}(v)}{\sigma_{st}}
\]
where \(\sigma_{st}\) is the number of shortest paths from \(s\) to \(t\) and \(\sigma_{st}(v)\) the number passing through \(v\) [PAGE 4]. Units: dimensionless (fractional path count). Verbatim: "Betweenness centrality quantifies the occurrences that a node acts as a bridge along the geodesic path between other nodes" [PAGE 4].

*Singularity regime:* \(\sigma_{st}=0\) for disconnected pairs zeroes their contribution — so the metric can be artificially depressed by disconnection rather than by genuine distribution. That is a specific breakdown risk when a team is losing and the defensive half stops passing to the forwards — a common late-game pattern.

**5. Approximate Entropy (ApEn) of dyad-distance time-series.**
\[
\Phi^{m}(r)=\frac{1}{N-m+1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-m+1}\ln C_i^m(r),\quad C_i^m(r)=\frac{1}{N-m+1}\sum_{j=1}^{N-m+1}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\|x_i-x_j\|_\infty\le r\right]
\]
\[
\mathrm{ApEn}(m,r,N)=\Phi^{m}(r)-\Phi^{m+1}(r)
\]
Units: nats (natural-log of a probability ratio), but the paper reports the outcome on an empirical range of 0–2 described as "(arbitrary units)" [PAGE 5].

*Breakdown regimes:* (i) \(N<m+1\) \(\Rightarrow\) undefined; (ii) strictly periodic signal with \(r\) incommensurate \(\Rightarrow\) ApEn \(\to 0\) (maximum regularity); (iii) white noise \(\Rightarrow\) ApEn \(\to\) maximum (saturates the range and loses discriminating information); (iv) \(r=0\) with exact repeats degenerates to \(\ln 0\). The paper does not report \((m,r)\), so its regularity scores are not portable — this is an information-theoretic metrological gap.

**6. Dynamic Voronoi cell (individual dominant area).**
\[
V_i=\{x\in\mathcal{P}\;:\;\|x-p_i\|_2\le \|x-p_j\|_2\ \ \forall j\neq i\}
\]
Units: \(\mathrm{m}^2\). Coefficient of variation \(\mathrm{CV}_i=\sigma(V_i)/\mu(V_i)\), dimensionless. Verbatim: "the players' dominant region was obtained by the dynamic Voronoi cell computation" [PAGE 5].

*Breakdown regime:* \(V_i\) is discontinuous at the perpendicular bisector between any two players — a sub-meter displacement flips a strip of turf from one cell to another. Under 5Hz GPS with positional noise on the order of 1–2 m CEP, this produces spurious jumps of several \(\mathrm{m}^2\) per frame. The CV is the only reported summary, but the underlying cell area time-series itself is fundamentally noisy in a way the paper does not quantify.

**7. Team efficacy.**
\[
E=\frac{G}{S}\times 100
\]
Units: %. Verbatim: "efficacy = number of goals × 100 / number of shots" [PAGE 3]. U17 team A \(33.3\%\), team B \(14.3\%\) [PAGE 5]; U15 team A \(7.1\%\), team B \(10.1\%\) [PAGE 5].

**8. Standardized mean difference (Cohen-style pooled).**
\[
d=\frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{s_p},\quad s_p=\sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2+(n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2}}
\]
Dimensionless. Effect-size thresholds verbatim: "0.2, trivial; 0.6, small; 1.2, moderate; 2.0, large; and \(>2.0\), very large" [PAGE 5].

**9. Pearson product-moment correlation.**
\[
r=\frac{\sum_i (x_i-\bar{x})(y_i-\bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum_i(x_i-\bar{x})^2\;\sum_i(y_i-\bar{y})^2}}
\]
Dimensionless, in \([-1,1]\). Reported U15: team A \(-0.44\) [\(90\%\) CI: \(-0.29,-0.58\)], team B \(-0.38\) [\(-0.22,-0.52\)]; U17 team B \(-0.02\) [\(-0.20,0.15\)] [PAGE 6]. *Breakdown:* undefined if either variable has zero variance (e.g., if a team achieves zero variation in ApEn across its 45 dyads).

All of the between-team reporting is from a single match per age-group, so the \(90\%\) CIs are within-game inference intervals, not sampling intervals over matches. This is a critical semantic point that the paper does not emphasize — the "very likely" labels on Page 5 apply to the difference between two teams on one afternoon, not to a population.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The intellectual lineage of this paper runs through five distinct traditions, each contributing an operator the paper fuses.

**First lineage — Freeman’s centrality algebra (1978).** Reference [39] in the paper — Freeman’s "Centrality in social networks conceptual clarification" — is the source of both \(C_C\) and \(C_B\). The paper inherits Freeman’s implicit assumption that path-structure in a weighted graph tracks real informational diffusion. Applied to football, that assumption is only approximately true: pass-paths are not passively read; they are chosen under pressure. This is the primary inherited weakness.

**Second lineage — network-structure-and-performance empirics (Grund 2012, López Peña & Touchette 2012).** The paper cites Grund’s English Premier League analysis [10] for the "lower centralization \[i.e.\] lower dependency" claim and López Peña & Touchette [14] for the betweenness interpretation. These works established the sign of the relationship in *senior professional* football. The present paper’s specific contribution is transferring that sign into U15/U17 — which is where the multi-week trap begins: the sign might not transfer because U15 players’ pass-selection is dominated by a smaller effective action repertoire.

**Third lineage — ApEn from physiology (Pincus 1991).** Reference [42]. ApEn was designed for physiological time-series (heart-rate variability) where the signal is stationary in distribution over the window. Football dyad-distance time-series are not stationary; they contain tactical regime changes at the scale of tens of seconds. The ApEn metric inherits the assumption of stationarity from its home discipline and carries it into an ecological setting where it is at best approximately valid.

**Fourth lineage — Voronoi-cells dialect in team sports (Fonseca et al. 2012).** Reference [27]. The dynamic Voronoi dominance-area formalism is imported directly, with the same continuous-metric-on-discrete-GPS problem that has dogged the original.

**Fifth lineage — Complex adaptive systems framing (Ramos-Villagrasa 2012, McGarry 2002).** References [51] and [60]. This supplies the interpretive overlay that lets the authors write "the interdependence of behaviours is commonly accepted as one of the most relevant characteristics of the complex adaptive systems" [PAGE 8] without committing to a specific dynamical model.

**Multi-week trap #1 — the U15/U17 extrapolation cycle.** The paper finds ApEn-passes correlation stronger in U15 and interprets this as ball-position dependence. A three-to-six-week follow-up study on the same cohort, using the same \((m,r)\) settings, is the natural replication cycle. The multi-week trap is that any follow-up study designed and run **without** pre-registering \((m,r)\) can trivially “confirm” the paper by choosing ApEn hyper-parameters post-hoc. In the absence of reported \((m,r)\), the original findings cannot be *falsified* — only re-interpreted.

**Multi-week trap #2 — the 25-minute-half fatigue-bracket.** The paper states "The total game duration was purposely manipulated to prevent the effects of fatigue in the studied variables" [PAGE 3]. This is architecturally elegant and philosophically loaded: it *creates* the very stationarity that ApEn assumes. A natural multi-week contra-experiment — replaying the same matches at full 45-minute half-length to exhaust the ApEn assumption — will, by design, contradict the paper if the underlying dynamical system is non-stationary on the 90-minute scale. The absence of that experiment is not neutral; it silently constructs the finding.

**Multi-week trap #3 — the "team outcome" tautology.** The outcome variable is \(E=G/S\times 100\) over a single 50-minute match. Goal counts of 1–7 drive \(E\) entirely. Any three-week block of matches produces \(E\)-values that swing by 30+ percentage points on luck alone. The paper’s "very likely" labels on Page 5 are comfortably consistent with pure shot-noise on a single fixture. A multi-week design that repeated fixtures would likely show the between-team effect sizes regress substantially toward a much smaller mean.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The crisis habit regression.** Under scoreboard pressure (or in a goalless second half), coaches and players regress to the structural response that the paper directly falsifies. Specifically: the moment a team is trailing, the natural instinct is to funnel more passes through the player perceived as most reliable. In network terms, this *raises* that player’s \(C_B\). But the paper’s own claim is that "Teams that are more dependent on specific players' may be more easily blocked" [PAGE 7], and the observed U17 difference shows betweenness dropping by \(41.2\% \pm 29.8\%\) on the higher-performance side [PAGE 5]. The crisis habit therefore *moves the network in the wrong direction*. The regression is not a knowledge deficit — the coaches almost certainly endorse the paper’s principle when asked in a seminar — but a preference reversal under pressure. This is a canonical habit-regression phenomenon: the cognitive principle is available in low-stakes reflection, and the behavioral habit dominates in high-stakes decisions.

**The counter-trap.** If an opposing coach *knows* the opponent will regress toward hub-feeding, the counter-trap is straightforward: design pressing structures that collapse onto the high-\(C_B\) node precisely when the opponent is in a losing-scoreline state. The mechanism is a first-order inference in recursive-theory-of-mind: I expect you to funnel; therefore I pre-position so that your funnel is a trap. The paper’s own text strongly supports this — the opponents’ job is to "remove the most influence players from the central positions of the passing network" [PAGE 9]. The counter-trap is therefore *internal* to the paper: the paper is not only descriptive but prescriptive against itself. A skilled coach *using the paper* will design a higher-performance network (low \(C_B\), high \(C_C\)); a skilled coach *against a team using the paper* will force that low-\(C_B\) design to collapse by pressing the residual hub, then wait for a late-game fatigue-driven re-centralization.

**The clock as recursive constraint.** The 25-minute half-structure is a finessed clock. The paper admits "two periods of 25 min, interspersed by 10 min of passive recovery" [PAGE 3] and explains the choice as fatigue-avoidance. But the clock also sets the *time-horizon* over which the reconstructed network stabilizes. ApEn windows, closeness centrality integration, and betweenness path-sums all become meaningful only over windows of, roughly, several minutes. A 25-minute half therefore gives the network about two-to-three stable windows to demonstrate its structure. Under official 45-minute halves (the realistic contest), the same network undergoes at least one equilibrium shift driven by cumulative fatigue from minutes 60–90. The recursive truth that the paper’s design excludes is that the network the paper measures *is not the network that wins matches*, because the latter is measured on the second half of a 90-minute contest where \(C_B\) re-centralizes.

A second-order recursion: the coach who has absorbed the paper now anticipates the counter-trap, and tries to pre-counter by *training out* the hub-feed regression in the preceding three-week cycle. The three-week cycle becomes the natural timescale of the mental model — but residual habits return under the first minute of scoreboard pressure in the next fixture. The recursive-theory-of-mind prediction is that even a coach who fully endorses the paper will fail to demonstrate the target network shape in the first 5 minutes of a scoreline-pressure state.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical veto — GPS-noise propagation.** The measurement stack is a "5Hz non-differential global positioning system (SPI-ProX, GPSports)" with "typical error of measurement… below 5% when accounting for measurement of the total distance covered, and between 5% to 10% during peak speed" [PAGE 3]. The paper correctly reports these TEm values — but only as they apply to *distance* and *peak speed*. The variables the paper actually builds its claims on (dyad-distance ApEn, dynamic Voronoi cell area, geodesic shortest-paths on the pass graph) are functions of *position*, not of either distance or speed. Positional noise in 5Hz GPS is on the order of 1–2 m CEP under good sky view. At this noise scale, the dyad-distance ApEn in particular is non-invertibly compromised: ApEn counts threshold crossings at distance \(r\); when \(r\) is close to the noise scale, the ApEn estimate is dominated by measurement variance rather than by tactical regularity. This is a **physical veto** on any claim about *small* differences in positional regularity — the effect sizes the paper reports for closeness (8.6% ± 4.9%) are physically within the noise band of the sensor.

**Biomechanical veto — maturation.** Age is \(13.9 \pm 0.3\) (U15) and \(15.7 \pm 0.5\) (U17) [PAGE 3]. Neither peak height velocity nor Tanner stage is measured. Yet two 13.9-year-olds can differ by up to three years in biological maturation, which produces anthropometric differences (height \(1.69 \pm 0.07\) m at U15 — a standard deviation of 7 cm, which is roughly one-year-of-growth) and force-velocity differences that dominate the ability to close distance, decelerate, and re-orient. Biomechanically, the \(V_i\) estimate (Voronoi cell) is a direct function of accelerating capacity (players with better acceleration defend a wider effective dominant region). Therefore the paper’s central interpretation — that U15’s stronger ApEn-pass correlation reflects greater *ball-position dependence* — is confounded by the fact that **maturational variance is larger relative to the mean in U15 than in U17**, which mechanically inflates positioning heterogeneity and could produce a spurious correlation with pass count. This is a **biomechanical veto** on the age-group inference as stated.

**Weather veto — turf and temperature.** "106×65 m artificial turf pitch" [PAGE 3]. Artificial turf friction coefficient depends strongly on moisture (both from rain and from pitch watering schedules), which changes: (i) ball roll speed and therefore the pass-selection strategy encoded in the graph, (ii) stud-turf slip risk and therefore the deceleration envelope that determines feasible Voronoi-cell control, and (iii) energy cost of changes-of-direction, which affects the ApEn of dyad-separation. The paper reports neither temperature nor humidity nor rainfall. Since only one pitch and one fixture per age group is used, any environmental asymmetry between the two matches (e.g., a slightly wetter morning for U15) becomes an unreported confound of the entire between-age-group comparison. This is a **weather veto** on any age-group contrast that is presented as a developmental effect.

**Veto synthesis.** Three interlocking vetoes — sensor noise on the position stream, maturation confound on the age-group comparison, and undocumented environmental conditions on the fixture-to-fixture contrast — constitute a rigorous reason to accept the paper as an *exploratory* report and to reject any inference about *causal* centrality-to-performance relationships. The paper’s own wording ("explore", "may optimize", "possible more dependence") is honest about the exploratory status, and the cross-layer review is consistent with that modesty.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three counterfactual experiments graded by a verifiable binary criterion, each falsifiable from the paper’s data structure.

**Scenario 1 — hub-deletion counterfactual.**
*Design:* From the reported directed pass-graph for U17 team A (\(33.3\%\) efficacy, higher-\(C_C\)/lower-\(C_B\) side), delete the node with maximum \(C_B\). Recompute total closeness, average betweenness, and residual total successful passes on the surviving 9-node sub-graph.
*Verifiable prediction:* If the paper’s hypothesis (low \(C_B\) `=` higher fluidity) is correct, the U17 team A graph should already have had *no* load-bearing node, so removing the max-\(C_B\) node should reduce total reachable pass-flow by only \(\sim\!10\%\) (matching the removal of 10% of nodes). Team B, the \(14.3\%\)-efficacy side with the \(41.2\%\) higher betweenness [PAGE 5], should show a disproportionate loss (\(\geq 30\%\)) of reachable flow on hub-deletion. 
*RLVR check:* Binary: does the ratio \((\Delta \text{flow}_A / \text{baseline}_A) < (\Delta \text{flow}_B / \text{baseline}_B)\)? If yes, the paper's claim is *confirmed at the graph level* independent of the tie to outcome. If no, the centrality interpretation is falsified as a structural claim.

**Scenario 2 — ApEn phase-randomization counterfactual.**
*Design:* Take the raw dyad-distance time-series for each of the 45 dyads, apply Fourier phase randomization (preserve power spectrum, randomize phases), recompute ApEn, and re-compute the Pearson \(r\) between ApEn and passes.
*Verifiable prediction:* If the ApEn signal is capturing genuine tactical coupling (temporal structure in the phases), then phase randomization must *destroy* the correlation: the \(r\) should collapse from \(-0.44\) toward \(0\) (magnitude \(<0.10\), i.e., "trivial" under Hopkins’s own thresholds). If instead the \(r\) remains near \(-0.44\), then ApEn is capturing only spectral properties (the "rhythm" of the sensor trace), and the paper's interpretation is falsified.
*RLVR check:* Binary: \(|r_{\text{shuffled}}| < 0.10\) \(\Rightarrow\) temporal-structure confirmed. \(|r_{\text{shuffled}}| \approx 0.44\) \(\Rightarrow\) spectral artifact confirmed.

**Scenario 3 — clock-veto counterfactual.**
*Design:* Replay the U17 match under official 45-min halves (no fatigue manipulation), and segment the match into minute blocks \(0\text{--}15\), \(15\text{--}30\), \(30\text{--}45\), \(45\text{--}60\), \(60\text{--}75\), \(75\text{--}90\). Recompute \(C_C\) and \(C_B\) per block on a rolling passing graph.
*Verifiable prediction:* The paper’s claim implies \(C_B\) should stay low throughout on the higher-performance side. But the paper’s own clock-manipulation [PAGE 3] removes any evidence of what happens from minute 50 onward. The prediction: at minutes 60–90, \(C_B\) re-centralizes on the DCM (the highest-load node), because cumulative fatigue reduces players' ability to occupy multiple support positions, and the pass-graph collapses back to hub-mediated flow. Numerically, we should see the U17 team A DCM \(C_B\) rise by \(\geq 30\%\) relative to their 0–25 baseline, and the ApEn-passes \(r\) attenuate to \(|r|<0.20\).
*RLVR check:* Binary: if in any 15-minute block between minutes 60–90 the DCM \(C_B\) rises above its 0–25 baseline, the "clock veto" is confirmed — the paper's 50-minute structure artificially suppresses the effect that dominates official matches. If \(C_B\) remains flat or falls, the counterfactual fails and the paper's extrapolation to official competition is preserved.

All three predictions rely only on the graph reconstructions the paper already performed, and on data documented in [PAGE 3–5]. Each is testable in a single replication cycle without new instrumentation — they are the natural falsification tests the paper’s own design implies but did not run.
