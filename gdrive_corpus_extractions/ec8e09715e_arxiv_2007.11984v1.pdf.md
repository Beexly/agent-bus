<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source uses discriminative correlation filters and a Siamese CNN feature extractor. The DCF ridge-regression objective is given verbatim as “\(\min_W \|W*X-Y\|_2^2+\lambda\|W\|_2^2\)” [PAGE 5], where \(X\) is the template patch, \(Y\) is the Gaussian-shaped label with peak at the target, \(W\) is the filter, \(\lambda\) is a regularization parameter, and \(*\) denotes circular convolution [PAGE 5]. The closed-form filter is
\[
W=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(X)\odot\mathcal{F}^{\star}(Y)}
{\mathcal{F}^{\star}(X)\odot\mathcal{F}(X)+\lambda}\right),
\]
where \(\odot\) is element-wise product, \(\mathcal{F}\) is the discrete Fourier transform, \(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\) is its inverse, and \(\star\) is complex conjugation [PAGE 5]. The search response is
\[
R=W*Z=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\mathcal{F}^{\star}(W)\odot\mathcal{F}(Z)\right),
\]
for search patch \(Z\) [PAGE 5]. In the Siamese form, the template filter is
\[
W_T=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T))\odot\mathcal{F}^{\star}(Y_T)}
{\mathcal{F}^{\star}(\phi_\theta(T))\odot\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T))+\lambda}\right),
\]
and the search response is
\[
R_S=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\mathcal{F}^{\star}(W_T)\odot\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(S))\right),
\]
where \(\phi_\theta(\cdot)\) is the CNN feature extractor with trainable parameters \(\theta\) [PAGE 5]. The backward filter and response are
\[
W_S=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(S))\odot\mathcal{F}^{\star}(Y_S)}
{\mathcal{F}^{\star}(\phi_\theta(S))\odot\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(S))+\lambda}\right),
\qquad
R_T=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(\mathcal{F}^{\star}(W_S)\odot\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T))\right),
\]
with pseudo-label \(Y_S\) centered at the maximum of \(R_S\) [PAGE 5]. The consistency loss is
\[
L_{un}=\|R_T-Y_T\|_2^2.
\]
The gradient update is stated as
\[
\frac{\partial L_{un}}{\partial \phi_\theta(T)}
=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(
\frac{\partial L_{un}}{\partial(\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T)))^{\star}}
+
\left(
\frac{\partial L_{un}}{\partial(\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T)))}
\right)^{\star}
\right),
\qquad
\frac{\partial L_{un}}{\partial \phi_\theta(S)}
=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(
\frac{\partial L_{un}}{\partial(\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(S)))^{\star}}
\right)
\]
[PAGE 6]. Multi-frame consistency is
\[
L_k=\left\|R(S_k\to T)-Y_T\right\|_2^2,
\qquad
L_{un}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}L_k,
\]
where \(k\) indexes the search patch and \(M\) is the number of search patches or trajectory length [PAGE 7]. The motion weight is
\[
A^i_{motion}
=
\left\|R^i_{S_1}-Y^i_T\right\|_2^2
+
\left\|R^i_{S_2}-Y^i_{S_1}\right\|_2^2,
\]
and the normalized weight is
\[
A^i_{norm}
=
\frac{A^i_{drop}\cdot A^i_{motion}}
{\sum_{i=1}^{N}A^i_{drop}\cdot A^i_{motion}},
\]
where \(A^i_{drop}\) is binary and zero for the highest-loss 10% of training triples [PAGE 7]. The three-frame cost-sensitive loss is
\[
L_{3\text{-frame}}
=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
A^i_{norm}
\left\|R^i_{(S_2\to T)}-Y^i_T\right\|_2^2,
\]
and the final multi-frame loss is
\[
L_{final}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{k=1}^{M}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
A^i_{norm}\cdot L^i_k,
\]
with \(L^i_k=\|R^i_{(S_k\to T)}-Y^i_T\|_2^2\) [PAGE 7–8]. Online update is
\[
W_t=(1-\alpha_t)W_{t-1}+\alpha_t W,
\]
where \(\alpha_t\in[0,1]\) is a linear interpolation coefficient [PAGE 8]. Scale is estimated by a patch pyramid with scale factors \(\{a^s\mid a=1.015,\;s=\{-1,0,1\}\}\) [PAGE 8].

Units and assumptions: image coordinates and the distance-precision threshold are in pixels, with the threshold set to 20 pixels [PAGE 9]. Response maps \(R\), Gaussian labels \(Y\), and normalized DCF filters are dimensionless if normalized; otherwise squared consistency terms carry squared response-amplitude units. Image entropy is reported without an explicit logarithm base, so its unit is bits or nats depending on implementation [PAGE 8]. \(M\) is a frame count, \(N\) is a mini-batch count, \(\alpha_t\) is dimensionless, FPS is \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), and AUC, DP, EAO, Accuracy, and Failure metrics are dimensionless fractions or percentages [PAGE 9–15]. The source assumes \(\lambda>0\) so that the Fourier-domain denominator is nonzero. Breakdown regimes: if \(\lambda=0\) and \(\mathcal{F}^{\star}(X)\odot\mathcal{F}(X)\) or \(\mathcal{F}^{\star}(\phi_\theta(T))\odot\mathcal{F}(\phi_\theta(T))\) has a zero at any frequency, Eqs. 2, 4, and 6 are singular. If all \(A^i_{drop}\) or all \(A^i_{motion}\) are zero, Eq. 13 has a \(0/0\) denominator. If the tracker loses the target but forward and backward trajectories coincide, Eq. 8 can be small despite failure [PAGE 2]. If \(M\) is too large, the paper reports diminishing returns: 5 frames is “less effective” than 4 frames [PAGE 10]. If occlusion or drastic appearance change occurs, the unsupervised tracker “tends to drift” [PAGE 16]. If online adaptation is too aggressive or too weak, Eq. 16 trades drift against adaptation; the excerpt does not provide a closed-form stability bound for \(\alpha_t\).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is retrocausally legible: the paper’s Eq. 1–3 are the DCF/KCF ridge-regression and Fourier-diagonalization tradition, explicitly citing Bolme et al. and Henriques et al. [PAGE 5, PAGE 17 references], while the Siamese correlation-filter branch is positioned against SiamFC, CFNet, and DCFNet [PAGE 3, PAGE 12]. The forward-backward consistency operator descends from optical-flow and cycle-consistency work: the paper cites Meister et al. for unsupervised optical flow with bidirectional census loss and Wang et al. for cycle consistency across time [PAGE 3]. It also cites TLD’s KLT forward-backward matching for failure detection and Lee et al.’s multi-hypothesis trajectory analysis [PAGE 3]. Later, TrackingNet uses forward-backward analysis for evaluation and sparse annotation [PAGE 3]. The paper’s own predecessor is UDT [PAGE 9], and LUDT is the retrocausal improvement: UDT used a three-frame cycle and center crop; LUDT adds trajectory enlargement and entropy-based RoI selection [PAGE 9]. The scientific trap is to read the later “unsupervised” label backward and assume the earlier DCF/KCF heuristics disappeared. They did not: entropy patch selection and KCF localization remain in the pipeline [PAGE 8], and the paper concedes that entropy selection “may not accurately select a target region” and KCF “is not robust enough” [PAGE 8]. A second multi-week trap is benchmark substitution: the paper trains on ILSVRC 2015 [PAGE 8], evaluates on OTB-2013, OTB-2015, Temple-Color, VOT2016, VOT2017/2018, LaSOT, and TrackingNet [PAGE 2, PAGE 9], and later adds OxUvA and LaSOT data for “more data” experiments [PAGE 10]. A comparison that changes training data and then attributes the gain to architecture alone is not licensed by the ablation, because the paper explicitly separates “only ILSVRC” from “more data” and “more data + HOG” [PAGE 11]. A third trap is treating weak supervision as no supervision: the paper simulates deviations from ground-truth boxes ranging from \(-20\) to \(20\) pixels and calls this “weak supervision” [PAGE 10]; that is not the same as the fully unsupervised entropy/KCF pipeline. A fourth trap is real-time portability: 70 FPS and 55 FPS are reported on a 4.00GHz Intel Core i7-4790K and NVIDIA GTX 1080Ti [PAGE 9], so FPS is hardware-relative. A fifth trap is trajectory-length universalism: the paper reports 4 frames as best among 2–5 on OTB-2015, but 5 frames is not better [PAGE 10], so “more frames always help” is a false retrocausal generalization. A sixth trap is attribute overclaim: the paper’s own gap analysis points to illumination variation, occlusion, and fast motion [PAGE 16], and its failure cases are Skiing and Soccer under drastic appearance change and occlusion [PAGE 16]. A seventh trap is cross-layer import: no biomechanical or meteorological variable appears in the source, so later narratives that add those layers are genealogically unsupported by this paper even if they are plausible in other literatures.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the tracker’s “belief” as its response map \(R_t\) and its filter state \(W_t\). The evaluator’s recursive model is: evaluator assumes the tracker believes the maximum of \(R_t\); the tracker believes its own pseudo-label \(Y_S\); the backward pass tests whether that belief returns to \(Y_T\). The crisis habit regression is the online moving average in Eq. 16: under occlusion, illumination variation, or fast motion, the filter \(W_t\) regresses toward recent background or distractor appearance because \((1-\alpha_t)W_{t-1}\) carries the past. The paper states the unsupervised tracker “tends to drift when an occlusion or a drastic appearance change occurs” [PAGE 16], and the main gaps versus fully supervised LUDT are “illumination variation (IV), occlusion (OCC), and fast motion (FM)” [PAGE 16]. The counter-trap is the consistency loss itself: if the tracker loses the target but the forward and backward trajectories coincide, the loss does not penalize the failure [PAGE 2]. Multi-frame validation is the designed counter-trap because more frames enlarge trajectory inconsistency, and the paper reports 3 frames improves over 2 frames by “2.8% DP and 2.0% AUC” [PAGE 10]. The cost-sensitive loss is a second counter-trap: it drops the highest-loss 10% of training triples [PAGE 7] and reweights motion, but this is a statistical stabilizer, not a semantic verifier. The clock is the temporal horizon: \(M=2,3,4,5\) frames yield AUC 57.4, 59.4, 59.8, and 59.7 on OTB-2015 [PAGE 10], while online speed is 70 FPS for LUDT and 55 FPS for LUDT+ [PAGE 9]. The recursive trap for the evaluator is to conflate “self-consistent” with “correct”: the tracker can recursively believe a wrong pseudo-label, and the evaluator can recursively believe the reported benchmark order without checking the weak-supervision and data-augmentation conditions. The correct recursive adjudication is therefore three-part: first, ask whether the failure is a crisis habit regression under Eq. 16; second, ask whether the backward pass is a genuine counter-trap or a coincidental loop; third, ask whether the clock—trajectory length, offline epochs, and FPS—permits the claimed inference. Under that adjudication, the paper supports a conditional belief: consistency training is useful when the trajectory is long enough to accumulate error and when the sample is content-rich enough to avoid background domination. It does not support the belief that consistency alone certifies target identity.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source supports an image-space physical veto, not a biomechanical or meteorological veto. The physical layer is evidenced by the OTB-2015 attribute list: “background clutter (BC), deformation (DEF), out-of-plane rotation (OPR), scale variation (SV), occlusion (OCC), illumination variation (IV), motion blur (MB), in-plane rotation (IPR), out-of-view (OV), fast motion (FM), and low resolution (LR)” [PAGE 16]. The paper states “challenges such as heavy occlusion or out-of-view in training videos will degrade the CNN feature representation capability” [PAGE 2], and that “our tracker trained via unsupervised learning tends to drift when an occlusion or a drastic appearance change occurs” [PAGE 16]. The attribute gap is explicitly “illumination variation (IV), occlusion (OCC), and fast motion (FM)” [PAGE 16]. These are physical/image-formation and target-motion vetoes. The biomechanical layer is not measured: “deformation (DEF)” is an image-space attribute label [PAGE 16], not a joint-angle, force, torque, muscle, or gait variable. The weather layer is not measured: the paper has no rain, snow, wind, temperature, pressure, solar irradiance, or weather-station variable; “illumination variation (IV)” is an image-level photometric attribute, not a meteorological measurement [PAGE 16]. Therefore the adjudication rule is: \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}=1\) is admissible when the failure is tied to OCC, OV, FM, MB, IV, or drastic appearance change; \(V_{\mathrm{bio}}=1\) is inadmissible from this source because no biomechanical variable is observed; \(V_{\mathrm{weather}}=1\) is inadmissible because no weather variable is observed. If a claimed veto is “biomechanical” or “weather,” it must be vetoed at the adjudication layer and downgraded to \(V_{\mathrm{phys}}\) only if the same evidence can be expressed as an image-space occlusion, deformation, blur, illumination, or motion attribute. The proof is absence-of-measurement plus positive image-space evidence: the paper’s limitations section names occlusion and drastic appearance change [PAGE 16], the training section names heavy occlusion and out-of-view [PAGE 2], and the attribute analysis names IV, OCC, FM [PAGE 16]. No cross-layer proof can bootstrap a biomechanical or weather cause from those quotes. The physical veto also has a quantitative boundary: the paper reports 76.9% DP and 60.2% AUC for LUDT on OTB-2015 [PAGE 12], and its unsupervised version is behind fully supervised LUDT by 2.6% AUC on OTB-2015 in the data-strategy ablation [PAGE 10]. That gap is not a biomechanical or weather gap; it is a supervision and appearance-modeling gap. The final adjudication is that physical/image-layer vetoes are admissible and source-backed, biomechanical vetoes are unproven, and weather vetoes are non-existent in this source.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Fourier denominator singularity veto. Intervention: set \(\lambda=0\) in Eq. 2, Eq. 4, and Eq. 6 and evaluate on a patch whose feature autocorrelation has a zero-frequency or zero-energy component. Source anchor: the paper’s filter solutions all contain “\(+\lambda\)” in the denominator [PAGE 5], and the ridge objective includes “\(\lambda\|W\|_2^2\)” [PAGE 5]. Prediction: the response or filter becomes non-finite or numerically unstable, the consistency loss in Eq. 8 becomes non-finite, and training fails. Verifiable RLVR reward: binary pass if the run produces NaN/Inf or a divergent loss; binary fail if it remains finite and reproduces the reported ordering. Veto: any claim that omitting regularization is harmless is vetoed by the source’s own denominator.

Scenario 2: Coincidental forward-backward consistency veto. Intervention: construct a two-frame sequence in which the target is lost to a static distractor but the forward maximum and backward maximum coincide at the original position. Source anchor: “The consistency loss function fails to penalize this situation because the predicted target region can still backtrace to the initial position on the first frame regardless of losing the target” [PAGE 2]. Prediction: the two-frame loss \(L_{un}=\|R_T-Y_T\|_2^2\) remains small despite target loss; the three-frame or four-frame loss in Eq. 11 increases because error accumulates. The paper’s Table 4 supports the direction: 2 frames gives 57.4% AUC, 3 frames 59.4%, 4 frames 59.8% on OTB-2015 [PAGE 10]. Verifiable RLVR reward: binary pass if the multi-frame loss separates the lost-target case from the true-target case where the two-frame loss does not; fail otherwise. Veto: single-frame consistency alone is vetoed as a correctness certificate.

Scenario 3: Cost-sensitive denominator and selection veto. Intervention: set all \(A^i_{drop}=0\), or set all \(A^i_{motion}=0\), in Eq. 13. Source anchor: Eq. 13 divides by \(\sum_{i=1}^{N}A^i_{drop}\cdot A^i_{motion}\) [PAGE 7]; the paper says “we exclude 10% of the whole training triples which contain the highest loss values” [PAGE 7] and “the unsupervised training cannot well converge without \(A_{drop}\)” [PAGE 10]. Prediction: the normalized weights are undefined by \(0/0\), or the training loses the hard-sample stabilization and fails to converge; removing \(A_{motion}\) alone should reduce performance by about “1.5% DP and 1% AUC” [PAGE 10]. Verifiable RLVR reward: binary pass if the no-drop or no-motion run diverges or underperforms the reported margin; fail if it matches the full cost-sensitive result. Veto: any claim that the cost-sensitive terms are cosmetic is vetoed by the denominator structure and the reported convergence dependence.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
