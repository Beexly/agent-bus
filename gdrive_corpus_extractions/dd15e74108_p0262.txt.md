All quantities below are taken from [PAGE 253]. Hullian drive D and M_p are theoretical constructs measured by deprivation duration or deprivation intensity; they are not SI base units. I denote their unit as drive units (du). S_p is drive-stimulus intensity in sensory afferent units (au). s_Hr, s_Er, and s_ER are reaction-potential units (rpu), a behavioral propensity scale, not Newtons or bits. The quotient Q is dimensionless.

Operators:
- D ∈ ℝ₊ : magnitude of a primary drive.
- M_p ∈ ℝ₊⁺ : physiological drive maximum; strict assumption M_p > 0.
- D_dom : dominant primary drive at time t.
- {D_nd,i} : set of non-dominant primary drives active at time t.
- S_p(D) : characteristic drive stimulus intensity associated with D.
- s_Hr : effective habit strength.
- s_H_eff : effective habit strength under current drive conditions (OCR reads “s1 + spr” [PAGE 253], treated as s_H_eff).
- s_ER : reaction potentiality.

Postulate 6 (drive stimulus) [PAGE 253]:
S_p(D) = f_6(D), with f_6 : ℝ₊ → ℝ₊ and f_6′(D) > 0 for all D in domain.

Postulate 7 (sensitization into reaction potentiality) [PAGE 253]:
s_Er = g(s_Hr) · h(D), with g′ > 0 and h′ > 0.

Major Corollary II (explicit reaction potentiality) [PAGE 253]:
\[
s_{ER} = s_{H\text{,eff}} \cdot \frac{D_{dom} + \sum_{i \neq dom} D_{nd,i}}{\sum_{i \neq dom} D_{nd,i} + M_p}
\]

Equivalent compact notation:
\[
s_{ER} = s_{H\text{,eff}} \cdot Q,\qquad Q \equiv \frac{D_{dom} + \Sigma D_{nd}}{\Sigma D_{nd} + M_p}
\]
where ΣD_nd = Σ_{i≠dom} D_nd,i.

Boundary and singularity analysis:
1. Absorbing state: if s_Hr = 0 then s_H_eff = 0, hence s_ER = 0 for all D, M_p. This matches “if habit strength is zero, reaction tendency is zero” [PAGE 253].
2. Saturation: if D_dom = M_p and ΣD_nd = 0, then Q = M_p / M_p = 1, so s_ER = s_H_eff. Reaction potential cannot exceed effective habit strength within this quotient form because D_dom ≤ M_p implies Q ≤ 1.
3. Singularity: if M_p = 0 and ΣD_nd = 0, the denominator is zero and Q is undefined. Physical assumption M_p > 0 removes the singularity.
4. Domain violation: if D_dom > M_p, then Q > 1, breaching the bounded-gain assumption and placing the system outside Hull’s stated physiological regime.
5. All drives zero: D_dom = 0 and ΣD_nd = 0 gives Q = 0 / M_p = 0, so s_ER = 0.

Verbatim anchors [PAGE 253]:
- “Associated with every drive (D) is a characteristic drive stimulus (S_p) whose intensity is an increasing monotonic function of the drive in question.”
- “Any effective habit strength (s_Hr) is sensitized into reaction potentiality (s_Er) by all primary drives active within an organism at a given time, the magnitude of this potentiality being a product obtained by multiplying an increasing function of s_HR by an increasing function of D.”
- “The amount of reaction potentiality (s_ER) in any given situational situation is the product of (1) the effective habit strength … under the existing conditions of primary drive multiplied by (2) the quotient obtained from dividing the sum of the dominant value of the primary drive (D) plus the aggregate strength of all the non-dominant primary drives (D) active at the time, by the sum of the same non-dominant drives plus the physiological drive maximum (M_p).”
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The page [PAGE 253] sits inside Hull’s deductive neo-behaviorist system, where postulates are derived from “background considerations elaborated in the preceding pages.” The intellectual lineage runs through Pavlovian conditioned stimulation (S_p as a drive stimulus), Thorndikian habit strength (s_Hr as the trace of reinforcement), Cannonian homeostasis (drive as a need state with a physiological ceiling M_p), and the Freudian drive concept operationalized as a measurable energetic variable D. The Yerkes-Dodson intuition that performance varies with arousal is present in the entailment of positive acceleration for rising drive up to the original reinforcement drive, though Hull’s formalism is monotonic and multiplicative rather than an explicit inverted-U.

Major Corollary II contains a divisive normalization term: non-dominant drives appear in both numerator and denominator, with M_p anchoring the denominator. This prefigures modern divisive gain-control models and allostatic load models, where competing needs scale each other’s motivational output. The equation also anticipates opponent-process and incentive-salience accounts: a dominant drive saturates the gain at Q → 1, while non-dominant drives dilute the quotient and can suppress expression of a strong habit.

The “emotion” entailment [PAGE 253]—conditioned evocation of endocrine secretions facilitating muscular activity on later presentation of conditioned stimuli—locates emotion in peripheral endocrine feedback rather than cognitive appraisal. This is a behaviorist precursor to somatic-marker and interoference theories, stripped of representational content: emotion is the conditioned viscero-muscular facilitation that readies the organism for action.

Multi-week traps implied by the page. (1) Extinction over weeks suppresses response rate but does not necessarily set s_Hr to zero; because s_Hr = 0 is the only absorbing state in the equation, latent habit survives and can be unmasked by a later drive increase, producing resurgence or spontaneous recovery that naive extinction models fail to predict. (2) Habit transfer across motivations is described as “prompt and extensive” [PAGE 253], creating cross-substitution traps: a habit established under food drive can transfer rapidly to thirst or drug drive when the dominant drive changes, enabling motivation substitution without retraining. (3) Abrupt drive shifts disrupt but do not eliminate behavior [PAGE 253], so an organism switched from one deprivation state to another after weeks of training shows partial performance collapse followed by rapid reacquisition—a hysteresis trap in which the old drive context remains partially encoded in S_p. (4) Habit strength accumulates slowly over weeks of reinforcement, whereas drive can be manipulated within minutes; this timescale asymmetry means an organism can be driven into high D with an under-trained habit, or into low D with an over-trained habit, producing mismatches between s_H_eff and Q that the equation captures but simple drive-reduction narratives miss.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. When an organism enters crisis, D_dom rises toward M_p. Because Q = (D_dom + ΣD_nd)/(ΣD_nd + M_p), the limit D_dom → M_p forces Q → 1, and therefore s_ER → s_H_eff. Reaction potential collapses onto the effective habit hierarchy: the strongest available habit expresses at near-full potential, while weaker, contextually appropriate habits are multiplicatively suppressed even if their cues are present. This is crisis habit regression—the organism reverts to its most over-learned response because the drive gain saturates and cannot selectively amplify weaker habits.

Counter-trap. The text states that in the absence of discriminatory training an abrupt drive shift disrupts behavior only “to some extent, though not completely,” and that transfers across motivations are “prompt and extensive” [PAGE 253]. A recursive agent modeling another can exploit this: if discriminatory training has established distinct S_p patterns for each drive, a drive shift need not disrupt at all. The counter-trap is to present conditioned stimuli associated with a non-dominant drive, thereby re-weighting Q away from the crisis-dominant habit and inducing a partial or full behavioral switch without changing D_dom itself.

Clock. Drive accumulates as a function of time since last drive reduction—a homeostatic clock. The entailment of positive acceleration between D = 0 and the original reinforcement drive means s_ER grows convexly early in the deprivation interval, then the M_p-bounded quotient imposes deceleration and saturation. Over multi-week schedules, the phase of this clock (circadian feeding cycle, water-deprivation interval) predicts when s_ER peaks for a fixed s_Hr. An agent with a theory of mind can estimate another’s clock phase from time-since-reinforcement and predict windows of high versus low reaction potential.

Recursive theory of mind in dyadic form. Agent A models B’s tuple (s_Hr, D_dom, M_p). If B is drive-satiated (low D_dom), Q is small and s_ER is low even for a large s_Hr; B will not act despite strong habit. If B is in crisis (D_dom ≈ M_p), Q ≈ 1 and B regresses to its dominant habit; A can predict B’s response with high confidence. A can also manipulate B’s drive state (withhold or deliver reinforcement) to move B across the Q saturation boundary, inducing regression on demand. The recursion terminates at the physical veto layer: no model of D or s_Hr can produce behavior if the effector layer is vetoed.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Hull’s Major Corollary II is a central gain law: it computes s_ER from habit and drive variables assuming “an organism in the same external situation” [PAGE 253]. It contains no term for effector integrity, ambient physics, or meteorological state. Three cross-layer vetoes therefore operate outside and above the equation.

Physical veto (musculoskeletal actuator). The text states that conditioned endocrine secretion “facilitates the evocation of muscular activity” [PAGE 253]. Facilitation presupposes an excitable contractile plant. If required joint torque τ_req exceeds maximal available torque τ_max (fracture, tendon rupture, severe fatigue), then behavioral output is zero regardless of s_ER. Formal veto: behavior = s_ER · V_phys, where V_phys = 1 if τ_req ≤ τ_max and V_phys = 0 otherwise. The equation predicts s_ER > 0, but the physical layer enforces a hard output ceiling.

Biomechanical / neuromuscular veto. Endocrine facilitation acts on the neuromuscular chain. Motor-neuron blockade, spinal inhibition, or neuromuscular-junction failure sets efferent gain to zero. Because s_ER is a central potentiality and not a motor command, it cannot bypass peripheral conduction failure. Veto condition: if efferent gain G_eff = 0, then expressed muscular activity = 0 even though s_ER may be maximal. The endocrine/emotion mechanism [PAGE 253] is thus necessary but not sufficient; it gains access to a motor plant that must already be intact.

Weather / environmental veto. Ambient extremes alter the drive hierarchy. Heat or cold can instantiate a thermoregulatory drive that competes with food- or water-seeking drives. If thermoregulatory demand becomes D_dom, the quotient Q is recomputed with the new dominant term; food-seeking s_ER can collapse because ΣD_nd in the denominator now includes the previously dominant drive, diluting Q for the original habit. Additionally, heat stress may lower effective M_p (physiological ceiling under strain), which paradoxically raises Q for a fixed D_dom but reduces the organism’s capacity to sustain the required muscular output, coupling the weather veto to the physical veto.

Veto proof. The corollary’s domain is restricted to an intact organism under stated drive conditions [PAGE 253]. When any veto condition holds—τ_req > τ_max, G_eff = 0, or environmental D_dom shift beyond operant range—the predicted s_ER is behaviorally null. The model is therefore valid only in the intersection of (a) M_p > 0, (b) D_dom ≤ M_p, (c) s_H_eff > 0, and (d) intact effector and permissive weather. Outside this intersection, Hull’s equation describes potential, not performance.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a prediction derived from the [PAGE 253] equations and an observable verification metric. No wagering language is used.

Scenario 1 — Structural zero-habit veto.
Counterfactual setup: A lever-press habit is extinguished over weeks until response rate is indistinguishable from zero. Two counterfactual states are then imposed: (A) true structural s_Hr = 0; (B) latent s_Hr > 0 masked by extinction. Both are placed under high food drive D_dom ≈ M_p.
Prediction: Under (A), s_ER = 0 × Q = 0, so no presses occur even at near-maximal drive. Under (B), s_ER = s_H_eff × Q > 0 and lever pressing resurges rapidly because Q ≈ 1.
Verification metric: Cumulative presses in a 30-minute test under high drive. Prediction A: count = 0. Prediction B: count significantly above sated control. A null result at high drive falsifies drive-alone accounts and supports the s_Hr = 0 absorbing state.

Scenario 2 — Physiological saturation veto.
Counterfactual setup: An organism with s_Hr > 0 is food-deprived for graded durations from 0 to beyond the drive used in original reinforcement, pushing D_dom toward M_p.
Prediction: From the text’s positive-acceleration entailment [PAGE 253], response rate rises convexly up to the original reinforcement drive. Beyond that, because D_dom cannot exceed M_p and Q is bounded by 1, further deprivation produces no increase in s_ER; response rate plateaus at s_H_eff. Second derivative of rate versus deprivation hours approaches zero post-saturation.
Verification metric: Press rate per minute plotted against deprivation hours. Predicted curve: convex rise then flat asymptote; no continued linear or exponential increase past the M_p-imposed ceiling.

Scenario 3 — Abrupt drive-shift partial-disruption veto.
Counterfactual setup: A habit is trained under hunger. Without discriminatory training, the drive is abruptly switched to thirst (D_dom changes, S_p pattern changes). Per [PAGE 253], behavior should be disrupted “to some extent, though not completely,” and transfer should be prompt and extensive.
Prediction: Post-shift s_ER is computed with thirst as D_dom and hunger (if residual) or zero as part of ΣD_nd. Because Q is recomputed but not zeroed, response rate drops by a factor related to the change in Q but remains strictly above 0 provided s_H_eff > 0. Transfer to the thirst context should recover within a few sessions.
Verification metric: Running speed or press rate immediately pre-shift versus post-shift, then across sessions. Prediction: an immediate drop of finite magnitude (e.g., not to zero), followed by rapid partial recovery. A complete drop to zero would falsify the “not completely” entailment and imply a structural drive-specific habit bound not present in Major Corollary II.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
