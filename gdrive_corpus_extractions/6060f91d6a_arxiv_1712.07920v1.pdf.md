**Multi-hop derivation of the CAMOT claim chain, with gray-zone audit.**

The paper asserts a paradigm inversion: rather than *detect → track* (tracking-by-detection), it performs *propose → track → classify* — “we propose a model-free multi-object tracking approach that uses a category-agnostic image segmentation method to track objects” [PAGE 1]. To audit that claim I decompose it into eight inferential hops, each of which carries an independent failure probability that the paper does not quantify multiplicatively.

**Hop 1 — Proposal recall ceiling.** SharpMask supplies masks. The paper concedes “we used SharpMask (trained on COCO) as the object proposal component for tracking the generic objects (which limits the possibly achievable recall)” [PAGE 6]. This is a *hard upper bound*: no downstream stage can recover an object that was never proposed. Because KITTI annotations include only car, pedestrian, cyclist, van, truck, and misc, any tracked “animal, street furniture, bicycle, or street sign” is scored as a false positive [PAGE 6]. The recall ceiling and the false-positive definition are therefore **coupled**: increasing agnostic coverage mechanically depresses precision on the benchmark. The paper’s Fig. 6 sidesteps this by ranking agnostic tracks with the unary potential only, setting $w_{sem}=0$ — an evaluation protocol, not a tracking result.

**Hop 2 — Mask representation.** Masks are stored in RLE, “which typically reduces the storage size to $O(\sqrt{n})$, where $n$ corresponds to the number of pixels/points representing the mask” [PAGE 5]. The word *typically* is doing load-bearing work: the $\sqrt{n}$ bound presumes run-contiguity, i.e., spatially coherent blobs. Fragmented or checkerboard masks — exactly what under-segmentation produces — degrade toward $O(n)$. The efficiency argument for a “large hypothesis space” is thus conditional on the segmentation quality that the system is trying to establish.

**Hop 3 — 3D localization.** Median 3D position and velocity come from stereo plus scene flow, filtered by a RANSAC ground plane and height thresholds: “We filter out object proposals that are below and above certain height thresholds, keeping only the objects that ‘stick out’ of the ground (with some tolerance, as objects may be occluded)” [PAGE 3]. Stereo triangulation error grows as $z^2/(f\,b)$; this alone predicts monotone degradation with range, and the authors themselves restrict the competitive claim: “competitive with state-of-the-art tracking-by-detection methods for cars and pedestrians at a distance range of up to 30m” [PAGE 2].

**Hop 4 — Prediction.** The Kalman prior is constant-velocity, and mask prediction applies two rigid transforms, $m^t_{prio}=P\,T^t\,S^{t-1}(m^*)$ [PAGE 3], with the vertical velocity component forced to zero. This is a *rigidity assumption*: articulated objects (cyclist + bicycle) and deforming masks violate it, which is precisely the reported failure — “SharpMask tends to produce separate proposals for the bicycle and the rider and we usually track them separately” [PAGE 7].

**Hop 5 — Association.** The score is a product of a Gaussian motion likelihood and mask IoU [PAGE 4]. Multiplicative fusion is brittle: if either factor is near zero the product vanishes, so a single bad depth estimate can veto a correct mask association even when appearance is perfect. The paper reports no ablations isolating this product form; there is no table anywhere in the paper, only figures 4–7.

**Hop 6 — Hypothesis maintenance.** Temporal NMS over a window “(τ is in practice set to 10)” [PAGE 4] prunes duplicates but also prunes genuine co-located alternatives that have not yet diverged. The system’s tolerance for ambiguity is therefore bounded by a fixed clock, not by evidence.

**Hop 7 — Inference.** The CRF energy [PAGE 5] is admitted to be intractable: “The pairwise potentials in (5) are not submodular and hence the problem is NP-hard. However, an approximate solution can be computed efﬁciently using the multi-branch method by [33]” [PAGE 5]. Every reported number therefore rests on a relaxation with no stated optimality gap.

**Hop 8 — Evaluation.** MODA/Recall/Precision are plotted by range, and “we report the results as a function of the distance from the camera, which the KITTI benchmark does not support” [PAGE 6]. At long range the ground-truth count per bin collapses, making precision statistically unstable — a variance gray zone the figures do not shade.

**Gray-zone verdict.** The paper’s strongest honest sentence is comparative and hedged: “(CAMOT+RCNN) is on-par with (CIWT+SMRCNN) up to 20m range, after that, it lags behind” [PAGE 6]. The headline “competitive” result depends on a KITTI-fine-tuned Regionlets classifier bolted on after tracking — meaning the model-free tracker is validated *through* a model-based classifier. $$h^t_i=\big(p^t_i,\; v^t_i,\; m^t_i\big),\qquad p^t_i\in\mathbb{R}^3\ (\mathrm{m}),\;\; v^t_i\in\mathbb{R}^2\ (\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}),\;\; m^t_i\in\{0,1\}^{|\Omega|}.$$

**3. Mask prediction operator** [PAGE 3], Eq. (2):

with $P\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times4}$ the intrinsic matrix (dimensionless pixel units after projection), $T^t_{ego}\in SE(3)$ from visual odometry [11], $T^t_v\in SE(3)$ the velocity-induced rigid motion with vertical component set to zero, $S^{t-1}$ the lift of the mask into the stereo point cloud (units m), and $m^*\in\{m^{t-1},\,m^{t-1}_{prio}\}$.
**Singularity regime:** $T^t$ is undefined when visual odometry degenerates — pure rotation about the epipole, textureless surfaces, or dynamic-object-dominated fields where ego-motion estimation is contaminated by the very objects being tracked. The paper asserts the transforms are “computationally efﬁcient and provides us with precise appearance predictions” [PAGE 3] but offers no residual-error statistic on $T^t_{ego}$.

**4. Joint association score** [PAGE 4], Eq. (3):
$$o^t_i=\arg\max_{o^t\in O^t}\Big[p_{mask}(o^t,h^t_i)\cdot p_{motion}(o^t,h^t_i)\Big],$$
$$p_{motion}(o_i,h_i)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(p_{obs}\mid p_h,\Sigma_h\right)\quad[\mathrm{m^{-3}}],\qquad p_{mask}(o_i,h_i)=\mathrm{IoU}\!\left(m^t_{prio,i},\,o^t\right)\in[0,1].$$
**Breakdown (multiplicative veto):** as $\mathrm{IoU}\to 0$ or $\|p_{obs}-p_h\|^2_{\Sigma_h^{-1}}\to\infty$ the product $\to 0$ regardless of the other factor. There is no additive fallback, no gating on either term. Under heavy occlusion $\mathrm{IoU}$ collapses even for a correct identity, so occlusion is converted into an association failure rather than into a missing observation.

**5. Hypothesis-set similarity** [PAGE 4], Eq. (4):
$$\mathrm{sim}(h_i,h_j)=\frac{\sum_{t\in T(h_i,h_j)}\mathrm{IoU}\!\left(m^t_i,m^t_h\right)}{\big|T(h_i,h_j)\big|}\in[0,1].$$

with $\lambda$ a temporal decay constant measured in **frames** (dimensionless time index), $s^t_i\in[0,1]$ the mask score, and $\Phi_{sim}\in[0,1]$ a mask IoU. Note the semantic term enters with a **minus** sign inside an expression already subtracted — i.e., confident classification *increases* selection probability, while its absence contributes exactly zero, which is the mechanism preserving unknown objects.

**8. Truncated semantic term** [PAGE 5], Eq. (7):

**Lineage.** The paper sits at the confluence of two genealogies that had, until 2017, been kept apart. The *tracking-by-detection* line — Zhang, Yuan & Nevatia’s network-flow global association [46], Milan’s continuous energy minimization [22], Choi’s aggregated local flow [6], Yoon’s structural-constraint event aggregation [45], and the authors’ own CIWT [27] — all assume a category-specific detector exists and spend their effort on affinity design and camera-motion compensation. The *tracking-before-detection* line is robotics-native: Teichman & Thrun’s tracking-based semi-supervised learning [35] and group induction [36], Kaestner’s generative 3D detection [15], Dewan’s motion-based LiDAR detection [25], Moosmann & Stiller’s joint self-localization and tracking [25]. CAMOT’s explicit move is to import the second genealogy into the vision domain using stereo instead of LiDAR, motivated by the stated LiDAR defect: “current LiDAR sensors suffer from a very limited resolution, making recognition difﬁcult” [PAGE 1].

**Proposal genealogy.** The mask substrate descends from Alexe’s objectness [1], Uijlings’ selective search [37], Zitnick & Dollár’s Edge Boxes [48], and then the CNN turn: Pinheiro’s end-to-end proposal networks [29] and SharpMask [28], whose own generalization-to-unseen-categories experiment is the direct warrant for CAMOT’s category-agnostic premise. Horbert’s level-set spatio-temporal proposals [14] and Kang’s tubelet proposal networks [16] are the closest video-level relatives, and the paper is careful to distinguish itself: “their ultimate goal is to produce ‘smoother’ object proposals in video sequences. Multi-object tracking (MOT) is a higher-level task” [PAGE 3].

**Inference genealogy.** The hypothesize-and-select CRF is inherited from Leibe et al. [18] and reused in [27]; the non-submodular relaxation is Schindler’s multi-branch method [33]. The measurement back-end is a stack of classical primitives: RANSAC ground fitting [9], ICP [4], Quick-Shift [38], DBSCAN [8], piecewise-rigid scene flow [39], StereoScan visual odometry [11], and CLEAR MOT [3] for scoring.

**Retrocausal reading — how later work would rewrite this.** The 2017 formulation is retrocausally legible as a *pre-embedding* system: masks and 3D positions are tracked, and semantics are attached post hoc by a frozen COCO classifier. The acknowledged consequence is that the classifier’s training distribution silently governs the tracker’s operating envelope — “in COCO there is no speciﬁc pedestrian category, but only a general person category. In addition, in COCO cars and persons mainly appear close to the camera, therefore detecting these objects in far distances is challenging” [PAGE 6]. Any later system that swaps in an open-vocabulary or mask-specialized classifier inherits this architecture unchanged but changes its failure surface entirely.

**Multi-week traps (long-horizon, cumulative).**
1. *Velocity drift.* With a constant-velocity Kalman model and rigid mask warping, small per-frame velocity bias integrates over $\tau=10$ frame windows and beyond; the paper concedes that “solely using estimated velocities would result in a drift in the model estimate” [PAGE 3] but substitutes only a two-transform approximation, not a drift correction.
2. *Hypothesis-set inflation.* New hypotheses are seeded from every observation each frame [PAGE 3], with pruning deferred to NMS after $\tau$ frames. Over long sequences the hypothesis population grows superlinearly in clutter, and the $O(\sqrt{n})$ RLE bound is the only thing standing between the system and memory exhaustion.
3. *Class-prior hysteresis.* Once a track’s classifier output crosses $C_{min}$, the unary potential’s decay-weighted sum retains that support for roughly $\lambda$ frames. A single lucky classification can therefore sustain a background hypothesis for multiple seconds; conversely, a genuine object that is never confidently classified never receives the boost.
4. *Semantic-selection starvation.* Because $\Phi_{sem}$ enters with negative sign, unknown-category tracks compete against semantically boosted known-category tracks for the same physical space via $\psi$. Over many frames, unknown objects in overlap with recognized ones lose systematically — the exact opposite of the paper’s stated goal.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**The system’s model of the scene, and the analyst’s model of the authors.** CAMOT maintains an explicitly recursive belief: a hypothesis *about* a hypothesis set. The CRF does not ask “is this an object?”; it asks “given that other hypotheses also claim this space, which subset best explains the observations?” — a second-order selection over first-order tracks. The paper’s own framing is recursive: “The basic assumption behind our method is that correct object region hypotheses will lead to more consistent tracks and thus to a better explanation for the observed scene change. Thus, we use tracking consistency as a cue to identify ‘interesting’ objects” [PAGE 1]. Consistency is the system’s proxy for existence.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under stress — clutter, occlusion, far range — the architecture reverts to a prior it was designed to escape. Two regressions are visible. First, *detector regression*: the reported headline numbers depend on attaching a KITTI-fine-tuned Regionlets classifier (CAMOT+Regionlets), and the authors state plainly that with the non-fine-tuned RCNN classifier “(CAMOT+RCNN) is on-par with (CIWT+SMRCNN) up to 20m range, after that, it lags behind” [PAGE 6]. The model-free system’s best result is obtained by re-importing the model-based habit it was built to replace. Second, *appearance regression*: when the motion term degrades (poor depth), association falls back on mask IoU alone, which is appearance-only tracking — the very image-domain paradigm the paper criticizes CIWT-adjacent methods for.

**Counter-trap.** The designed counter-move is the truncated semantic term of Eq. (7): it lets the system *use* semantics without *requiring* them, and it is explicitly asymmetric — confidence is rewarded, absence is neutral, never penalized. This is a genuinely elegant anti-trap device, and it is the paper’s most transferable idea. But it is defeated by the pairwise term. Because $\psi$ penalizes overlap between a semantically-boosted hypothesis and an unboosted one on equal footing, the neutral treatment in the unary is undone in the pairwise. The trap is not removed; it is relocated from the score to the competition.

**The clock.** The real arbiter is temporal, not semantic. Three clocks govern the system: (i) the association clock — one frame, no gap-bridging beyond mask extrapolation; (ii) the pruning clock — $\tau=10$ frames of deferred NMS [PAGE 4]; (iii) the decay clock — $\lambda$ in the exponent $e^{-|t-t_e|/\lambda}$ of Eqs. (6) and (8), which sets the memory half-life of unary and pairwise evidence alike. The training clock is a fourth: temporal coverage is computed over “a small temporal window of 6 time frames” [PAGE 5], while inference prunes at 10. **Training and inference operate on different clocks.** A hypothesis optimized to look good over 6 frames is selected by inference weighting evidence over roughly $\lambda$ frames and pruned at 10 — the mismatch is a structural risk and is not discussed anywhere in the paper. Of the three, only the decay clock is smooth; the pruning clock is a hard threshold and therefore the dominant source of irreversibility. Once a hypothesis is pruned at frame $t$, no amount of later evidence can resurrect it — the recursion is memoryless in the strict sense.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer 1 — Geometric/physical veto (ground plane and height).** RANSAC ground fitting [9] plus height-threshold filtering is a *physical* prior: only things that stick out of the ground can be objects. This veto is sound on planar roads and fails on grades, ramps, speed bumps, or where the stereo point cloud is dominated by a large foreground surface (a nearby truck’s side plane can win the RANSAC consensus and rotate the ground normal). The paper hedges: “with some tolerance, as objects may be occluded” [PAGE 3] — but the tolerance band is a free parameter with no reported sensitivity study.

**Layer 2 — Sensor-physics veto (stereo depth).** Triangulation uncertainty scales as $\sigma_z \approx z^2\sigma_d/(f\,b)$ for disparity error $\sigma_d$, focal length $f$, baseline $b$. This is a *non-negotiable* physical wall, and it cleanly explains the paper’s own 30 m hedge [PAGE 2] and the range-dependent collapse in Figs. 4–5. The claim boundary is not a design choice; it is a consequence of the baseline. **Veto proof:** at fixed $\sigma_d$, doubling range quadruples depth variance, which inflates $\Sigma_h$ in the motion likelihood, which flattens $p_{motion}$ toward uniform, which reduces Eq. (3) to IoU-only association — the appearance fallback identified above. The chain is deterministic and requires no experiment to predict.

**Layer 3 — Representational veto (mask area).** Eq. (8)’s denominator $\min(|m_i|,|m_j|)$ is the physical extent of the smaller mask in pixels. At range, a car mask shrinks toward the segmentation resolution floor; the ratio becomes numerically unstable and, simultaneously, the *absolute* intersection is small. **Veto proof:** the penalty term loses discriminative power exactly where merges are reported to occur — “some distant cars are merged together” [PAGE 8]. The merge is not a tuning failure; it is the pairwise term becoming ill-conditioned.

**Layer 4 — Biomechanical/articulation veto.** The model-free premise implies no kinematic model, which is a strength for unknown objects and a weakness for articulated ones. Bicycle + rider is the canonical case: “SharpMask tends to produce separate proposals for the bicycle and the rider and we usually track them separately. We do not consider this to be incorrect. However, in the KITTI annotations the bicycle and the rider are annotated as single bounding box (cyclist)” [PAGE 7]. This is a **representation veto dressed as a metric veto** — the tracker is arguably right and the label is arguably wrong, and the benchmark cannot express the distinction. Any conclusion drawn from cyclist-category numbers in this paper is therefore unadjudicable.

**Layer 5 — Illumination/weather veto.** The paper reports no weather stratification whatsoever. Stereo disparity and SharpMask proposals both degrade under low sun, rain, and specular wet-road reflections; RANSAC ground fitting degrades on wet, low-texture asphalt. Since KITTI sequences span varied conditions and the paper pools them, the reported range curves implicitly average over an unmodeled environmental covariate. **Adjudication:** weather is a *latent confounder* here, not a controlled factor, and the paper’s per-range plots cannot separate sensor-physics decay from condition-specific failure. This is an honest gap, not misconduct — but it caps the generality of the 30 m claim.

**Layer 6 — Computational veto.** Equation (5) is NP-hard [PAGE 5]; the multi-branch approximation has no reported runtime, no memory footprint, and no hypothesis-count-vs-latency curve. For a mobile platform — the stated deployment context — this is the missing veto: a method that is accurate but not real-time cannot satisfy the stated application.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario below is a **verifiable** counterfactual: given fixed inputs, fixed seeds, and fixed hyperparameters, the outcome is deterministic and independently reproducible. Predictions are falsifiable against the paper’s stated design.

**Veto 1 — Ablate rigid mask warping (set $m^t_{prio}=m^{t-1}$).**
*Intervention:* replace Eq. (2) with identity propagation, keeping Eqs. (3), (6), (8) unchanged.
*Prediction:* association IoU $p_{mask}$ drops monotonically with per-frame ego-displacement and with object speed, and the drop is largest in frames where $T^t_{ego}$ has a large translational component. Because Eq. (3) is a product, the *identity-switch rate* rises superlinearly rather than linearly. **Verification:** log $\mathrm{IoU}(m^t_{prio}, o^t)$ per frame under both conditions and regress against $\|T^t_{ego}\|$; the paper’s own claim that the two-transform approach “provides us with precise appearance predictions in the image domain” [PAGE 3] predicts a strictly positive slope. A zero slope falsifies the necessity of Eq. (2).

**Veto 2 — Set $w_{sem}=0$ at inference (not just in Fig. 6 evaluation).**
*Intervention:* zero the semantic term in Eq. (6) during CRF inference, retaining the classifier only for reporting labels.
*Prediction:* raw agnostic mask recall is essentially unchanged (the proposal set and tracker are untouched), but **precision improves on the KITTI metric** because semantically boosted background hypotheses — signs, lights, building fragments — lose their competitive advantage in the pairwise overlap contest. Simultaneously, recall on *recognized* car/pedestrian tracks slightly *decreases* at close range, where $c_t>C_{min}$ reliably. **Verification:** the paper supplies the natural control in Fig. 6, which already ranks agnostic tracks with $w_{sem}=0$ [PAGE 7]. If the Fig. 6 agnostic curves show high recall on cars/pedestrians without semantics, then the semantic term is doing label assignment, not track selection — and claim (iii) is weaker than stated.

**Veto 3 — Replace intersection-over-min with standard IoU in Eq. (8).**
*Intervention:* denominator becomes $|m_i\cup m_j|$.
*Prediction:* suppression of over-segmentations collapses. The paper states the rationale explicitly — “We do not use IoU as an overlap criterion... Instead, we employ intersection over the size of the smaller mask, so that objects of different sizes can still suppress each other. This way, over-segmentations can be suppressed by the larger object” [PAGE 5]. Under standard IoU a small spurious fragment inside a large correct mask yields low IoU and survives; **the duplicate-track count rises**, and MOTA degrades predominantly at 5–20 m where masks are large enough for the ratio to matter. **Verification:** count surviving hypotheses whose mask is contained (intersection-over-min $>0.9$, IoU $<0.3$) in a larger selected hypothesis. A nonzero baseline under the paper’s formulation and a near-zero count under standard IoU would confirm the design choice is load-bearing — a claim the paper asserts but never ablates.

**Meta-veto.** All three counterfactuals share one unaddressed vulnerability: the paper contains no ablation table. Every component is argued for by construction rather than by controlled removal. The three scenarios above can be run with the described pipeline and would, taken together, convert the paper’s three stated contributions from assertions into measurements. Until then, the honest summary is the authors’ own: “we demonstrate that we are able to track arbitrary objects based on category-agnostic proposals” [PAGE 7] — a demonstration, at ≤30 m, with a frozen COCO proposal network and a benchmark that cannot score the objects the method was built to find.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
