<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage runs from Galton's 1889 biometric regression on this page to Pearson's correlation, to Fisher's 1918 infinitesimal model, and finally to modern quantitative genetics and GWAS. Eye colour, however, was resolved early in the 1900s as a Mendelian, major-gene trait (later mapped to OCA2/HERC2 and associated loci), not a continuously blending stature-like trait. Applying Galton's 19th-century blending framework to eye colour is therefore retrocausal: it projects a continuous-trait statistical architecture backward onto a phenotype later shown to be discrete and thresholded. Multi-week traps emerge at the measurement boundary. Iris melanin continues to deposit after birth, so eye colour is developmentally unstable over the first weeks to months of life; sampling a newborn's eye colour and comparing it to an adult parent creates a spurious decay that looks like regression to the mean but is actually a developmental clock mismatch. Seasonal recruitment across weeks of data collection can also introduce population-stratification confounds if ancestry is not randomized with respect to sampling date. Thus the "persistence" Galton expects from this page cannot be measured without first fixing the age of observation and the categorical coding of the trait.
