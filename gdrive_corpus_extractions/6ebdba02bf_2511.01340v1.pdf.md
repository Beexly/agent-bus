The "difficulty increase" from ControlNet is therefore confounded with a variance increase, and the paper reports no intervals at all.

C3 is where the audit turns adversarial. On [PAGE 5] the paper concedes that for Qwen2-VL-7B, "VisProg (0.214 substring acc, 0.248 F1) and only desc (0.111 substring acc, 0.250 F1) underperform compared to RebusDescProgICE (0.107 substring acc, 0.264 F1)." This sentence is an internal contradiction: 0.107 is lower than both 0.214 and 0.111, so on the metric named first, the proposed method does not underperform — it is outperform-ed. The method is dominant on F1 only (0.264 vs 0.248/0.250). More damaging: "GPT-4o reaches 0.536 (three-shot, only description)" [PAGE 5] versus the proposed method's 0.512 on the same row of Table 2. The paper's own headline system is beaten by a simpler ablation on the F1 metric. The word "competitive" on [PAGE 5] is doing load-bearing work it cannot support.

Now the abstract's arithmetic. It claims gains "by 2.1−4.1% and 20−30% using closed-source and open-source models respectively compared to Chain-of-Thought Reasoning" [PAGE 1]. Against CoT, this is false at the row level: GPT-4o three-shot RebusDescProgICE F1 0.512 versus three-shot CoT 0.517 is negative; GPT-4 Turbo 0.431 versus 0.451 is negative; GPT-4o-mini 0.352 versus 0.457 is strongly negative. The figure only reconstructs as *absolute* points against *zero-shot normal*: 0.512 − 0.489 = 0.023 (GPT-4o), 0.352 − 0.330 = 0.022 (4o-mini), 0.431 − 0.382 = 0.049 (Turbo). So the abstract mislabels both the comparator (normal, not CoT) and the unit (points, not percent). For the open-source 20–30%: 0.219 (one-shot VisProg) → 0.264 (three-shot RebusDescProgICE) is +20.5%, but it crosses both shot count and method, so no causal attribution is licensed.

Finally, statistical resolution. With n = 1,333, p̂ ≈ 0.5, the standard error of a difference in proportions is √(2 × 0.25 / 1333) ≈ 0.0194, giving a 95% detection floor of ≈ 0.038. The claimed closed-source effect of 0.023 sits *below* the detection floor. This is the gray-zone center of the paper: every closed-source claim is sub-threshold at the stated dataset size, and the true test-set size is smaller still because a holdout pool is carved out for in-context examples.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**1. Word-level F1 (the primary metric).** The paper defines it as "the harmonic mean of precision and recall over the tokenized prediction and reference answers" [PAGE 5] with SQuAD attribution [PAGE 7, ref. 28].

$$\mathrm{F1}(p,r)=\frac{2\,\lvert T(p)\cap T(r)\rvert}{\lvert T(p)\rvert+\lvert T(r)\rvert},\qquad T:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathcal{M}(\Sigma^{*})$$

Units: dimensionless, range $[0,1]$. Precondition: $\lvert T(p)\rvert+\lvert T(r)\rvert>0$. Singularity: the ratio is undefined ($0/0$) for an empty prediction; the paper does not state a guard, so an empty output's score depends on unstated implementation. Breakdown regime: F1 is a *lexical* operator with no synonymy — a semantically correct answer differing by one morpheme ("millimetres" vs "millimeters", or the paper's own "Mill"+"Lime"+"Ters" construction on [PAGE 1]) can score 0 while a partially overlapping wrong answer scores high.

**2. Substring accuracy.** "measures whether the predicted answer occurs as a contiguous substring within the reference" [PAGE 5].

$$S(p,r)=\mathbb{1}\Big[\exists\,i\in[0,\lvert r\rvert-\lvert p\rvert]\;:\;r_{i:i+\lvert p\rvert}=p\Big]$$

Units: dimensionless indicator. **Critical asymmetry:** $S(p,r)\neq S(r,p)$, and the direction is prediction-into-reference. This means $S$ is *not* monotone in answer quality and is maximized by short predictions. In the limit $\lvert p\rvert=1$, $S=1$ whenever $p$ is a character present in $r$ — a zero-information degenerate strategy. The paper does not report tokenization, case-folding, or minimum-length constraints, so this floor is unquantified. This is the sharpest formal breakdown in the document.

**3. Fano bound on achievable error (the missing ceiling).**

$$H(A\mid X)\;\le\;H_b(P_e)+P_e\log_2(\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert-1)$$

Units: bits (base-2). $P_e$ = Bayes error, $H_b$ = binary entropy. Precondition: $\mathcal{A}$ must be a finite answer vocabulary. **Breakdown:** the answer space here is open-vocabulary English phrases, so $\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert$ is effectively unbounded and the bound is vacuous — which is precisely why no calibrated ceiling exists for any number in Tables 1–4. The 18 ChatGPT-derived categories [PAGE 4] are the only finite partition, and they are semantic labels, not answer classes.

**4. ControlNet conditional denoising.** The paper states puzzles were treated "as Canny Edge Maps" [PAGE 3], with reference to Zhang et al. [PAGE 7, ref. 41].

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{z_0,\,c_E,\,\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}),\,t}\big[\lVert\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t,c_E)\rVert_2^2\big],\quad c_E=\mathrm{Canny}_\tau(I),\;\; z_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}z_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$$

Units: loss in squared latent-noise units; $\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}$ in noise-σ units. **Singularity:** as $\tau\to$ high, $c_E\to\varnothing$ and the conditional channel capacity collapses; the generator reverts to the unconditional prior and hallucinates content absent from the puzzle. The empirically observed retention, 611/722 = 0.846 [PAGE 3], is a direct estimator of this failure probability at the annotator's judgment threshold. The paper reports it as bookkeeping, not as a finding.

**5. In-context example-selection kernel.** "a novel technique (inspired by Poesia et al. [26]) to learn a unified embedding that effectively represents a Rebus Puzzle Image" [PAGE 4].

$$k(x_i,x_j)=\frac{\langle\phi(x_i),\phi(x_j)\rangle}{\lVert\phi(x_i)\rVert_2\,\lVert\phi(x_j)\rVert_2}$$

Units: dimensionless, $[-1,1]$. **Exact singularity:** if $\phi$ suffers representation collapse (kernel matrix $K$ rank $\to 1$), then $k\equiv 1$ for all pairs, the retrieval distribution becomes uniform, and RebusDescProgICE degenerates exactly to random-shot CoT. The rank of $K$ should be reported and is not.

**6. Statistical resolution of the headline claim.**

$$\mathrm{SE}(\hat p_1-\hat p_2)\approx\sqrt{\frac{\hat p_1(1-\hat p_1)}{n}+\frac{\hat p_2(1-\hat p_2)}{n}}$$

At $\hat p\approx0.5$, $n=1333$: $\mathrm{SE}\approx0.0194$, 95% floor $\approx0.038$. Claimed GPT-4o gain 0.489→0.512 is 0.023 $<0.038$. Assumption: independent items, test set $=1333$. Since the design carves a holdout pool for in-context sampling [PAGE 4], $n_{\text{test}}<1333$ and the floor is higher.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The rebus lineage is older than the benchmark: the Italian rebus paper cited as [32] is titled *"Non Verbis, Sed Rebus"* — "not by words, but by things" — the Latin formula that names the genre. The computational lineage bifurcates. One branch is constrained linguistic search: Littman, Keim and Shazeer's probabilistic crossword solver [16], WebCrow [3, 8], cryptic-crossword NLP [30, 31], Wordle [2], and NYT Connections [34], all of which assume a finite, verifiable answer lattice. The second branch is program-aided reasoning: PAL [11], PoT [7], Madaan et al.'s finding that "code LLMs do better when commonsense problems are framed as code generation" [PAGE 2], and VISPROG [14], which the paper inherits directly as its "VisProg" arm. RebusDescProgICE is the splice of branch two onto branch one's open-vocabulary target — and that splice is the structural weakness, because program-aided correctness is checkable while rebus correctness is not.

The **retrocausal inversion**: ControlNet [41] postdates every source puzzle. The three source sites — eslvault (black-and-white), NIEHS (text-based), flashbynight (colored), enumerated on [PAGE 2] — were authored under a pre-generative visual regime. The augmentation retroactively rewrites the difficulty distribution of that corpus, and the paper confirms the effect in embedding space: "despite sharing identical puzzle answers, the Rebus Puzzle images generated via ControlNet [41] are semantically far apart from their original counterparts" [PAGE 4]. So the 2025 benchmark cannot be compared to Gritsevskiy et al. [13] or Sarti et al. [32] as though the underlying difficulty construct were stable across time. It is not.

**Multi-week traps.** (i) A team that trains or prompts against the 722 originals and evaluates on the full 1,333 will see an apparent capability regression that is entirely an augmentation artifact; the Fix requires splitting by *provenance flag*, which the paper's metadata supports but its tables never stratify. (ii) The ChatGPT-derived 18-category taxonomy [PAGE 4] is a frozen 2024 model judgment baked into the benchmark; future model families may partition the answer space differently, making category-level comparisons non-portable. (iii) The four-annotator, second-year-undergraduate, English-medium pool [PAGE 2] anchors "Easy/Hard" to a specific population; a model that matches that population's difficulty curve may simply have absorbed that population's priors.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the solver as holding three competing policies under a fixed token budget: **H** (habit: describe-and-guess, the zero-shot normal path), **T** (counter-trap: emit a VisProg program that is syntactically valid but visually ungrounded), and **C** (clock: the in-context budget, i.e., how many exemplars to spend before answering). The paper's own data shows habit regression is real: "Increasing the number of in-context examples does not uniformly improve performance, confirming that puzzle-solving is not driven by pattern-matching" [PAGE 5]. Concretely, GPT-4o F1 goes 0.489 (zero, normal) → 0.503 (one, normal) → 0.513 (two, RebusDescProgICE) → 0.512 (three, RebusDescProgICE): the curve is non-monotone and plateaus, exactly the signature of H reasserting itself once the example window saturates.

The counter-trap is more insidious. The VisProg arm produces *plausible procedural text*, which the paper treats as reasoning; but Table 1 shows GPT-4o three-shot VisProg substring accuracy 0.383 versus three-shot only-desc 0.434 — the structured arm is *worse* at the metric that rewards exact retrieval. The model has learned to perform reasoning rather than to solve. For Qwen2-VL-7B, three-shot normal F1 is 0.264 and three-shot RebusDescProgICE F1 is also 0.264 [PAGE 6, Table 2] — identical to three decimals. That is the deepest single fact in the paper: for the flagship open-source model, the entire framework's contribution is indistinguishable from a plain prompt at equal shot count. The one-shot gap (0.176 normal → 0.241 RebusDescProgICE) is real but is a *shot-count* effect masquerading as a *framework* effect.

The clock is unreadable because the paper reports no seeds, no temperature, no decoding policy, and no variance. Under any reasonable model of stochastic decoding, the reported 0.264 ties and the 0.023 closed-source deltas are inside the noise band. A rational solver-policy under this evidence should allocate zero budget to RebusDescProgICE and all budget to shot-count and description quality, because "only desc" is the winning arm for the strongest model (0.536 F1, [PAGE 5]).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Physical layer (render substrate).** The Canny edge map $c_E$ is the physical carrier of puzzle identity. Veto proof: any operation that destroys thin strokes — the NIEHS text-heavy puzzles [PAGE 2], or low-resolution letter art — destroys the puzzle while preserving its "style." This is not hypothetical; it is measured. 111 of 722 generated images were rejected, so the physical layer vetoed 15.4% of augmentations *under the most permissive gate available* (a single qualified annotator, [PAGE 3]). The true veto rate is a lower bound only.

**Biomechanical layer (annotator cognition).** The metadata is produced by "4 annotators, all of whom were at least in their second year of undergraduate study and enrolled in institutions where English is the primary language of instruction" [PAGE 2]. Veto proof: an "Easy" label is a claim about human solve probability, and with four raters and no reported inter-annotator agreement statistic (no Cohen's κ, no Krippendorff's α), the label has unknown reliability. If κ < 0.4 — the conventional "fair" threshold — the Easy/Hard axis cannot veto any model claim, and the paper's difficulty stratification (Fig. 3's Venn decomposition, [PAGE 3]) is decorative. Additionally, second-year undergraduates in English-medium institutions are an unusually high-vocabulary population; their difficulty judgments are not transferable to the general solver population that a benchmark is meant to represent.

**Ambient/weather layer (injected background).** The paper explicitly frames augmentation as ambient: ControlNet "adds an ambient background as a backdrop while preserving the core content of the Rebus puzzle. This added background serves as a visual distraction" [PAGE 2]. Veto proof: the "preserving the core content" clause is asserted, not proven. The only evidence offered is UMAP separation [PAGE 4], which demonstrates *change* in CLIP embedding space, not *preservation* of solvability. Because style shift and distractor shift are applied as a single entangled intervention, the observed F1 collapse to ≤0.402 [PAGE 5] cannot be attributed to distraction rather than to semantic corruption of the puzzle itself. The ambient variable is unconfounded in the tables and confounded in reality — a veto on all causal claims derived from Tables 3 and 4.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
