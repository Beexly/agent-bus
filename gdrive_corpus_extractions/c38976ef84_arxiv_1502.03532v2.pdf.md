The recursive layer: whoever controls the equalization operator controls the equilibrium. By lowering $\mu_1$ toward $\mu_2$ (Eq. 21), the method deliberately weakens its own same-camera agent so the cross-camera agent can compete — a self-handicapping move that only pays off if the opponent (two-step) is brittle. The empirical proof of correct anticipation is Table V ([PAGE 11]): under detection-only inputs the method's average MCTA is $0.6099$ vs USC-Vision $0.6003$, whereas under perfect SCT (Table IV) it loses $0.6662$ vs $0.7625$. The theory-of-mind reading: the method correctly models that "the opponent's strength is conditional on clean inputs" and refuses to compete on that turf.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three physical/biomechanical layers can veto the model regardless of algebraic elegance.

Veto 1 — constant-velocity biomechanics. The periodicity segmentation rests on the assumption ([PAGE 6]): "We assume that pedestrians are always walking at a constant speed in scenes." Real pedestrians stop, turn, queue, and change cadence; when $\varpi_i<30$ frames the estimator $\tau_i$ over $[\gamma,\varpi_i/2)$ is empty and $d_i$, hence Eq. 12, is undefined. Verdict: the biomechanical layer vetoes short or aperiodic tracklets outright — no algebraic rescue exists because the arg-max domain is empty.

Veto 2 — appearance invariance under illumination/viewpoint. The equalization presumes two characterizable distributions $\mathcal{N}(\mu_1,\sigma_1^2)$ and $\mathcal{N}(\mu_2,\sigma_2^2)$. But appearance degradation from "camera settings, viewpoints and lighting conditions" ([PAGE 3]) is not a stationary affine shift; it is heavy-tailed and time-varying. A single $(\Delta\mu,\Delta\sigma)$ pair cannot track a distribution whose $\mu_2$ drifts as the sun moves. Verdict: the weather/photometric layer vetoes the stationarity premise of Eq. 22; the equalization is a first-moment correction to a non-stationary process.

Veto 3 — topology and the interval-time collapse. The motion model itself concedes its own breakdown ([PAGE 7]): "the pathes between cameras are hard to measure which renders the interval time useless for predicting positions. In this case, the relative distance mostly tends to be a huge wrong number." The min-relative-distance patch (Eq. 17-19) only functions if hand-placed disappearing points $x^{exit},x^{enter}$ are correct; a mislabeled enter/exit area injects a systematic bias into every cross-camera edge. Verdict: the geometric/topological layer holds veto authority over inter-camera motion; the model survives only by degrading gracefully to the appearance term.

Net adjudication: no single veto falsifies the whole system, but Veto 1 is a hard algebraic stop (empty domain), while Vetoes 2 and 3 are soft degradations the design partially absorbs. The honest reading is that the equalized graph is robust to SCT failure but fragile to gait irregularity, photometric non-stationarity, and topology mislabeling.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario A — perfect SCT is granted. Counterfactual: hold SCT fixed to ground truth (Table IV, [PAGE 11]). Verifiable prediction: the two-step USC-Vision method should win, because the K-partite assumption is restored and the joint method's equalization handicaps its own SCT. Confirmed by data: average MCTA Ours $0.6662$ < USC-Vision $0.7625$. RLVR label: the method's advantage is *rejected* in the clean-SCT regime. Prediction for a held-out clean-SCT benchmark: expect the two-step method to lead by roughly $0.08$–$0.10$ MCTA.

Scenario B — detection-only inputs (imperfect SCT). Counterfactual: replace SCT ground truth with raw detections (Table V, [PAGE 11]). Verifiable prediction: the joint equalized graph should overtake USC-Vision because "its powerful ICT method loses its advantage under the unperfect SCT results" ([PAGE 12]). Confirmed: Ours $0.6099$ > USC-Vision $0.6003$. RLVR label: *accepted*, but by a razor-thin $0.0096$ margin — statistically fragile. Prediction: on a harder fragment set (larger $\theta$), the margin should widen because USC-Vision's mismatch growth is steeper ([PAGE 12]: "the number of its ICT mismatches increases much more shapely than our method's").

Scenario C — real DPM detector, no ground truth. Counterfactual: run the actual DPM detector [59] (Table VI, [PAGE 12]). Verifiable prediction: the method should *lose* to USC-Vision because single-object-tracker drift corrupts whole tracklets ([PAGE 12]: "once the tracker drifts to a false detection, it would cause the whole tracklet unreliable"), whereas USC-Vision's hierarchical association compensates missing detections. Confirmed: average MCTA Ours $0.3405$ < USC-Vision $0.4052$. RLVR label: *rejected*. Prediction: swapping the AIF front-end for a drift-resistant tracker (e.g., TLD-style online learning [66]) would raise Ours above $0.40$, closing but likely not eliminating the gap, since the deficit is front-end SCT quality, not graph structure.

Cross-scenario synthesis: the RLVR verdicts flip sign three times (reject, accept-by-hair, reject) purely as a function of SCT input quality. The single verifiable invariant across all three is monotonic — the equalized global graph's relative advantage is a strictly decreasing function of SCT input fidelity, positive only in the mid-fidelity band. Any deployment claim must therefore be conditioned on measured SCT reliability; an unconditional "state-of-the-art" claim is falsified by Scenarios A and C.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Summary: the paper's real contribution is the equalized single MAP graph plus the affine equalizer (Eq. 21/26) and the MCTA metric (Eq. 27), and its advantage is provably conditional on imperfect SCT — strongest under detection-only inputs (Table V, $0.6099$ vs $0.6003$) and absent under perfect SCT (Table IV) or real detection (Table VI). The load-bearing risks are the unspecified negative-$P_a$ clamp, the empty-domain singularity for tracklets shorter than $2\gamma=30$ frames, the MCTA negativity edge case, and the non-stationarity of the appearance distributions that Eq. 22 assumes away.
