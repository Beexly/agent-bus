$$ \frac{dL}{dt} = \eta \cdot S \cdot I_{surprise}(y; \hat{y}) $$

- $S$ is the scalar intensity of the "inciting situation" (dimensionless, normalized $[0,1]$), representing the arousal or emphasis provided by the stimulus (good or bad).

$$ \eta_{eff} = \eta \cdot \alpha(v) $$
Where $v \in [-1, 1]$ represents valence ($-1$ being maximally unpleasant/repulsive, $+1$ being maximally pleasant). Based on Tolman's hypothesis, $\alpha(v)$ is non-symmetric, such that for equivalent magnitudes of intensity, $|\alpha(-1)| > \alpha(+1)$. $$ \alpha(v) = e^{\lambda \cdot (-v)} \quad \text{for } v < 0, \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha(v) = 1 \quad \text{for } v \geq 0 $$
Here, $\lambda$ is a risk-aversion coefficient (units: dimensionless). This ensures that repulsive odors or electric shocks (negative $v$) exponentially increase the magnitude of synaptic modification (stamping in), whereas pleasant outcomes yield a linear or sub-linear update.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s rejection of the orthodox "Law of Effect" represents a pivotal rupture in behavioral science, marking the birth of cognitive psychology from the womb of radical behaviorism. Historically, Edward Thorndike’s Law of Effect posited that behaviors followed by satisfying consequences are strengthened, while those followed by discomfort are weakened—a purely mechanical, stimulus-response (S-R) formulation. Tolman, writing in 1932, disrupts this lineage by arguing that the organism forms "sign-gestalts" (S-S connections or cognitive maps) regardless of immediate reinforcement, provided there is sufficient "interest" or "emphasis."

This retrocausal lineage extends backward to Gestalt psychology (Köhler, Wertheimer), which emphasized holistic perception over elemental associationism. Tolman grafts Gestalt principles onto behaviorism, asserting that rats navigate mazes via purposive expectations (means-end relationships) rather than blind trial-and-error conditioning. The "multi-week trap" associated with this genealogy is the persistent misunderstanding that Tolman merely added a "mentalistic" veneer to behaviorism. In reality, his introduction of "purposive" elements fundamentally shifted the ontology of psychology from mechanistic causality to teleological anticipation.

Looking forward, this asymmetry in avoidance vs. approach learning laid the groundwork for Skinner’s operant conditioning (specifically the discovery that punishment suppresses behavior but doesn't necessarily unlearn it, aligning with Tolman’s view of rapid avoidance gestalt formation) and later, Rescorla-Wagner’s modeling of attention based on prediction error. Furthermore, modern neuroscience has confirmed the genealogical foresight of Tolman’s intuition: the amygdala's role in fear conditioning shows exactly this asymmetry, forming robust memories from single traumatic events (avoidance) compared to the gradual acquisition required for reward-based learning (approach). Thus, Tolman’s "Law of Emphasis" was not just a theoretical pivot, but an accurate prediction of the neurobiological architecture of value coding.
