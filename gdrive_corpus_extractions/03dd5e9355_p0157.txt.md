<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The section machinery of Halmos §34 descends from Lebesgue’s 1902–1904 integration theory and Carathéodory’s 1918 extension method, and it is the set-theoretic substrate of Fubini (1907) and Tonelli (1909): before one can integrate f(x,y) over a product measure, one must know that sections f_x are measurable and that the section maps are measurable in the parameter. Halmos’s 1950 Measure Theory codifies this under σ-rings rather than only σ-algebras, reflecting the von Neumann–Stone school’s attention to non-locally-compact and non-σ-finite settings. The diagonal counterexample (item 2) anticipates the modern distinction between the product Borel σ-algebra on ℝ² and its Lebesgue completion: under the completion, D becomes measurable with measure zero even though D∉𝓑(ℝ)⊗𝓑(ℝ) in the uncountable/countable σ-ring presentation. Multi-week traps embedded here: (i) assuming sectionwise measurability implies product measurability—refuted by D; (ii) treating a σ-ring of countable sets as if closed under complements relative to X, thereby smuggling X∈S and collapsing the σ-ring/σ-algebra distinction; (iii) applying Fubini-style section arguments without σ-finiteness, so that iterated section integrals exist while the joint integral does not. Each trap can persist across multiple analytic steps because the failure is silent at the level of individual sections and only appears at the global product set.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer (cardinality obstruction): the diagonal D has uncountable cardinality while the product σ-ring generated by countable rectangles contains only countable subsets of X×Y. This is a “physical” set-theoretic veto: no sequence of allowed set operations (countable union, relative complement) can raise cardinality from countable to uncountable, so D is excluded regardless of how measurable its sections are. VETO-PHYSICAL: if X is uncountable and S,T consist only of countable sets, block any inference D∈S⊗T from {D_x},{D^y} measurable.

Biomechanical layer (closure kinematics): the σ-ring closure laws (F\E)_x = F_x\E_x and (∪E_n)_x = ∪(E_n)_x are the allowed motions. They transport measurability from product to sections, not the reverse. Attempting to “assemble” a product set from its sections requires an operation inverse to sectioning that the σ-ring does not provide unless X×B is available as a measurable rectangle for every B∈T, which demands X∈S. VETO-BIOMECHANICAL: if X∉S, block the step (E)_x∩B = (E∩(X×B))_x used to propagate ℙ(f) to sections and to global measurability.

Weather layer (external measure conditions): σ-finiteness and completeness are weather conditions that govern whether product measure exists and whether non-Borel sets such as the diagonal become measurable in the Lebesgue completion of ℝ². In raw §34 product σ-ring terms, these conditions are absent; in completed Lebesgue product measure, D is measurable with measure zero, so the “D nonmeasurable” verdict is vetoed by completion while remaining valid in the uncompleted σ-ring. VETO-WEATHER: before exchanging integrals or declaring a set nonmeasurable, check whether the ambient theory is uncompleted product σ-ring (§34) or completed Lebesgue product measure; the answer changes under completion.

Adjudication: all three vetoes are independent. VETO-PHYSICAL kills the converse of Theorem A; VETO-BIOMECHANICAL kills ℙ(f) ⇒ measurability in the σ-ring setting; VETO-WEATHER restricts the scope of any “nonmeasurable diagonal” claim to the uncompleted theory. A conclusion survives only if it passes all three.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1: A system asserts the converse of Theorem A: “If every section E_x and E^y is measurable, then E∈S⊗T.”
Veto condition: X=Y uncountable, S=T=countable subsets, E=D diagonal. Every section is a singleton (measurable/countable), yet D∉S⊗T because S⊗T contains only countable subsets of X×Y [PAGE 142].
Verifiable prediction: the system will fail to produce a proof and will instead, when checked, either (a) cite a false closure theorem, or (b) correctly identify D as a counterexample. Verification is exact: compute D_x={x}∈T, D^y={y}∈S, and prove D∉S⊗T since any countable union of countable rectangles is countable while D has cardinality |X|.

Scenario 2: A system asserts “Property ℙ(f) — f^{-1}(M) intersects every measurable set measurably for all Borel M — implies f is measurable,” even when measurable spaces are σ-rings with X∉S.
Veto condition: f = χ_D with D as in Scenario 1. For every measurable A∈S⊗T, A is countable, so D∩A is countable and hence measurable in the product σ-ring; thus ℙ(f) holds. But f^{-1}({1}) = D ∉ S⊗T, so f is not measurable [PAGE 142, item 2 and item 3].
Verifiable prediction: the system will incorrectly claim equivalence unless it explicitly requires X∈S (σ-algebra) and takes A=X×Y to recover measurability. Verification: test on f=χ_D; the reward is +1 only if the system outputs “ℙ(f) does not imply measurability without X∈S; counterexample χ_D.”

Scenario 3: A system drops the nonempty hypothesis and claims “A rectangle A×B is measurable iff it is a measurable rectangle,” allowing A=∅ with B∉T.
Veto condition: ∅×B = ∅ is measurable in any product σ-ring regardless of B, yet it is not a measurable rectangle unless B∈T [PAGE 142, item 4]. Nonemptiness is required to select x∈A and y∈B that force B=(A×B)_x and A=(A×B)^y measurable.
Verifiable prediction: the system will mark ∅×B (B∉T) as a measurable rectangle and fail. Verification: check whether the system’s predicate “measurable rectangle ⇒ both sides measurable” holds on the empty rectangle; it does not unless nonemptiness is assumed, so the correct output is “false without the non-empty hypothesis; counterexample ∅×B.”
