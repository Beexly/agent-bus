First-order theory of mind: S models L as a system that transforms sign objects into expectancies over E and then into action. S selects u to maximize the probability of the desired ΔE. Second-order: S anticipates that L knows S is trying to manipulate L through speech; S therefore chooses signs that survive L's suspicion. The counter-trap exploits the asymmetry Tolman identifies: the speaker manipulates one end of the tool, but the listener controls whether the other end "pushes" anything at all [PAGE 238].

Clock. The decisive variable is real-time latency relative to deadline. A proclamation allows slow ideational "running back and forth" [PAGE 238], but a command requires action before \(\Delta_{\text{deadline}}\). If \(\tau_{\text{total}} > \Delta_{\text{deadline}}\), the sign-gestalt cannot release in time; speech is vetoed and the clock forces direct bodily intervention. The counter-trap is most potent when the speaker is clock-bound: L can delay or distort sign processing precisely because S cannot afford the latency of clarification.

\]
If \(L_{p,\text{eff}}\) falls below audibility, the proclamation's sign object is never "immediately presented" [PAGE 238], and no sign-gestalt expectation can be released.

Combined veto: \(V = V_{\text{phys}} \lor V_{\text{bio}} \lor V_{\text{weather}}\). When \(V=1\), speech is inoperative; the system must switch to a nonverbal means-end tool. These are physical break-down regimes (audibility, channel capacity, shared discriminanda), not heuristic ratios.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Acoustic masking counterfactual. Same command and speaker-listener dyad; background broadband noise is raised so that \(L_p(\text{speech}) - L_p(\text{noise})\) falls below the masking margin. Verifiable with a sound-level meter and compliance-rate recording. Prediction: listener behavioral rearrangement drops to the baseline prior \(P(A)\); \(I(U;A\mid C) \approx 0\), so the speech tool is vetoed and no significant rearrangement beyond chance occurs.

Scenario 2 — Discriminanda/sign-lexicon mismatch counterfactual. A proclamation is delivered in a sign system (language or signal code) for which the listener has no trained discriminanda. Verifiable by pre-testing the listener's sign lexicon. Prediction: the likelihood \(P(s\mid E)\) is uninformative, posterior equals prior, \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P(E\mid s)\|P(E)) = 0\); no ideation or sign-gestalt release is observed; sounds are treated as non-signifying physical events.

Scenario 3 — Deadline-latency counterfactual. A practical behavior must occur within \(\Delta_{\text{deadline}} < \tau_{\text{total}}\) (e.g., under 300 ms), while the full speech-chain latency exceeds one second. Verifiable with motion-capture timing and a hard deadline. Prediction: the speaker abandons the command and reverts to direct somatic intervention (the scruff-of-the-neck push Tolman describes [PAGE 238]); a sharp modality switch from vocal to bodily contact occurs exactly at the latency boundary, confirming the clock veto.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
