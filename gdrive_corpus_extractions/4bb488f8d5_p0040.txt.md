**1. Formal Definitions of Set-Theoretic Structures:**
Let $X$ be a universal set. A class of sets $\mathcal{R}$ is defined as a **ring** if:
   a. $\emptyset \in \mathcal{R}$ (Implicitly required for difference closure to be well-defined without singularities).
   b. If $E, F \in \mathcal{R}$, then $E \cup F \in \mathcal{R}$ (Closure under finite union).
   c. If $E, F \in \mathcal{R}$, then $E - F \in \mathcal{R}$ (Closure under set difference).
   
A ring $\mathcal{R}$ is a **$\sigma$-ring** if it additionally satisfies:
   d. If $\{E_n\}_{n=1}^\infty \subseteq \mathcal{R}$, then $\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n \in \mathcal{R}$ (Closure under countable union).

**2. The Generated Ring Operator ($S(\cdot)$):**
For any arbitrary class of sets $\mathcal{E}$, $S(\mathcal{E})$ denotes the ring (or $\sigma$-ring) *generated* by $\mathcal{E}$. Formally, $S(\mathcal{E})$ is the intersection of all rings containing $\mathcal{E}$:
$$ S(\mathcal{E}) = \bigcap \{ \mathcal{R} : \mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{R}, \mathcal{R} \text{ is a ring} \} $$
*Assumption:* The intersection of any family of rings is itself a ring, guaranteeing existence and uniqueness of $S(\mathcal{E})$.

**3. Theorem E: Restriction Identity:**
Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a class of sets and $A \subseteq X$ be an arbitrary subset. Let $\mathcal{A}$ represent the collection of subsets restricted to $A$ (conceptually treating $A$ as a new universe). The theorem asserts:
$$ S(\mathcal{E} \cap \mathcal{A}) = S(\mathcal{E}) \cap \mathcal{A} $$
Here, $\mathcal{E} \cap \mathcal{A} = \{ E \cap A : E \in \mathcal{E} \}$ and $S(\mathcal{E}) \cap \mathcal{A} = \{ R \cap A : R \in S(\mathcal{E}) \}$.
