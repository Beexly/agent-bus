Verifiable prediction: under the paper's ELO-OLR architecture, optimal K remains constant and rating/forecast errors show no systematic trend with density; gains from transitivity are not captured unless the rating rule is changed to use indirect comparisons [PAGES 1, 12].

Scenario 2 — Degree-spread veto. Counterfactual: a tennis-style circuit introduces a large cohort of wildcard or new entrants, pushing degree-distribution spread $d_s$ from 0 to 1 while holding mean degree at 120 [PAGE 7]. Veto condition: if high-degree nodes' rating error rises with spread, or if low-degree nodes' error stays flat, the paper's degree-disparity mechanism is invalid [PAGE 9]. Verifiable prediction: low-degree decile nodes will show monotonically increasing rating and forecast error as $d_s$ grows, while high-degree decile nodes remain flat; optimal K for low-degree nodes will rise, and optimal K for high-degree nodes will fall or stay constant [PAGE 9].
