Unless the food’s caloric value \(V_{F}\) (joules) scaled by hunger discount \(\delta(H)\) exceeds \(L(X)\), this affordance is vetoed. The naive rat’s failure to discriminate alleys from wire cover [PAGE 33] reflects the absence of a learned veto, not an absence of biomechanical cost.

Weather/ambient layer: laboratory temperature modulates basal metabolic rate and thus the rate of gastric emptying and hunger recurrence. In cold ambient conditions, \(H(t)\) rises faster post-satiation, shortening quiescence and elevating \(E^{*}\) at a fixed post-feeding interval; in warm conditions, \(H(t)\) decays slower, extending quiescence. This environmental variable can shift the effective testing phase on the endogenous clock described above.

Veto proof: \(X\) is emitted only if (i) geometrically passable, (ii) \(L(X)\le\delta(H)V_{F}\), and (iii) for transferred habits, \(s(T_{\text{current}},T_{\text{trained}})>\theta\). Failure at any layer collapses the behavior to quiescence or low-cost exploration, regardless of how strongly hunger “dominates” the organism [PAGE 33].

Three verifiable RLVR (reward-verifiable counterfactual) veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable prediction grounded in [PAGE 33].

Scenario 1 — Satiety veto. Counterfactual: pre-feed the rat to satiety (\(H=0\)) before maze exposure, with food present in the box. Prediction: \(E^{*}\to0\), so the rat does not enter the food-box and remains quiescent until \(H\) rises again [PAGE 33]. Verification: continuous video shows zero maze entries for at least 30 minutes post-satiation despite visible food cues.

Scenario 2 — Context-similarity transfer veto. Counterfactual: train a right-turn preference at a T-junction in \(M_{1}\), then transfer to \(M_{2}\) where the T-junction is rotated 180 degrees and all distal visual cues are changed so that \(s(T_{1},T_{2})\le\theta\). Prediction: carryover of the right-turn abstraction collapses; \(P(\text{right-turn}\mid M_{2})\) reverts to the naïve baseline \(P_{0}\), approximately chance for a binary T-choice, and the rat resamples alleys without directional bias [PAGE 33]. Verification: right-turn frequency drops from the trained baseline to ~0.5 within the first five T-encounters in \(M_{2}\).

Scenario 3 — Biomechanical cost veto. Counterfactual: place identical food rewards behind an impossible crevice and atop an inverted wire cover requiring upside-down traversal, while the same reward is available in a standard alley, under high hunger (\(H\approx1\)). Prediction: despite strong means-end readiness for food generally, the rat vetoes crevice and wire-cover paths; \(P(X=\text{crevice})\to0\) and \(P(X=\text{wire-cover})\to0\), with alley selection dominating even though hunger is maximal [PAGE 33]. Verification: latency to first alley entry is at least tenfold shorter than latency to crevice approach, or zero crevice/wire entries occur across a 20-minute session.

Each scenario ties a [PAGE 33] behavioral claim to a specific, measurable parameter (\(H\), \(s\), or \(L\)) and yields a binary or continuous falsifiable outcome without reference to betting lines, spreads, or odds.
