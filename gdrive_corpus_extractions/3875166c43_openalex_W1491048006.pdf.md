A veto rule at this layer: if a candidate all-star's average defensive speed exceeds the non-all-star mean by more than one pooled standard deviation while simultaneously showing elite close/pull-up statistics, the discriminant function flags inconsistency, because lower defensive velocity is part of the all-star signature interpreted as efficient pathing rather than laziness [PAGE 10]. Distance-covered totals (season totals in miles, e.g., Cluster 2 offense 97.43±10.97 miles) are function-1 dominant (\(SC=0.83\) offense, \(SC=0.80\) defense) [PAGE 7, Table 2], but the paper stresses distance and velocity were generally not discriminant except defensive velocity [PAGE 9]. Therefore a model that ranks players by raw seasonal distance is biomechanically vetoed; distance is a function of minutes and role, not skill.

Sensor layer: The 25 Hz optical sampling ceiling [PAGE 3] and the absence of full-court video verification [PAGE 4] constitute a measurement veto. Any inference requiring sub-25-Hz temporal resolution (e.g., first-step acceleration, deceleration bursts) is outside the instrument's valid regime [PAGE 10, ref 16].

Decision rule: A player profile is admissible only if (a) it is derived from complete cases (no missing predictors; 339/548 available) [PAGE 5], (b) retained predictors satisfy \(|SC| > 0.30\) in the discriminant structure [PAGE 5], and (c) physical-contact variables (close touches, defensive rebounds) are weighted consistently with their reported structure coefficients [PAGE 6]. Profiles violating (a)-(c) are rejected.
