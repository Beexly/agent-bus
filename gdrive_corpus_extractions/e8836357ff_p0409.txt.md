Physical/substrate veto. Carpet is a high-friction, non-displaceable substrate. Scratching cannot displace it, so no burial is physically achieved; the object remains on the surface and the dog "went away and let it lie" [PAGE 409]. Because the consummatory end-state (object covered) is physically impossible, the act lacks the reinforcement contingency R = 1 required for habit fixation. This is a hard physical veto on the behavior–outcome loop.

Biomechanical veto. Neonatal sucking requires coordinated orofacial musculature and negative-pressure generation; early spoon-feeding installs a competing motor engram that interferes with later breast latch. The terrier, observed before four months, has immature fore- and hind-limb musculoskeletal systems; scratching carpet yields no substrate displacement, so the digging motor pattern receives no proprioceptive confirmation of burial. Repeated unreinforced scratching on a non-yielding surface produces biomechanical extinction rather than strengthening.

Weather/seasonal veto. The terrier was born in December on a stable floor—an outdoor, seasonally cued thermal and olfactory regime—and transferred six weeks later to a carpeted house with constant indoor conditions. Seasonal zeitgebers (temperature, light, substrate odor) that might gate food-caching motivation are removed by the indoor transfer. The text provides no meteorological data, but the change of housing is itself an environmental veto on ecological validity: the behavior is observed outside the thermal and substrate conditions in which it would be consummatory in the wild.

Adjudication. None of these vetoes are expressed as spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds. They are necessary-condition failures: if the substrate cannot be displaced, if the musculature cannot produce the consummatory act, or if the seasonal/gating context is absent, then R(s) = 0 and ℋ(t) cannot accumulate. The behavior is vetoed at the physical layer before any probabilistic consideration arises.

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning veto scenarios, each with a prediction and a falsifying veto condition.

Scenario 1 — Neonatal sucking critical window. Protocol: A cohort of human neonates is spoon-fed exclusively for the first 72 postnatal hours with zero breast exposure; at 96 hours the breast is offered. Model: R(s) = 0 on [0, 72h] ⇒ ℋ(t_c) < ℋ_crit ⇒ prolonged latch latency or refusal despite A(t) > 0. Prediction: mean time-to-effective-latch at 96 hours is significantly greater than in a continuously breast-exposed control cohort; some infants refuse entirely. Veto/falsification: if spoon-fed neonates latch within 2 minutes and sustain nutritive sucking at 96 hours indistinguishable from controls, then the claim that early non-breast feeding blocks later suckling is falsified for this parameter regime.

Scenario 2 — Canid food-burying contingency and clock. Protocol: Hand-reared puppies matched to James's terrier age (<4 months, post six-week indoor transfer) are assigned to three arms. (a) Glove on carpet only, ad libitum feeding (R = 0 for burial). (b) Meat buried in earth with mild food pre-deprivation, one reinforced trial daily for 14 days within the critical window (R = 1, n = 14). (c) Same as (b) but beginning at 8 months of age, after presumed t_c (R = 1 but t > t_c). Prediction: (a) extinction after 3–5 unreinforced trials with no later resurrection; (b) stable food-burying habit persisting for months; (c) no acquisition despite reinforcement because the clock has closed. Veto/falsification: if arm (c) acquires stable burying at the same rate as arm (b), the critical-window closure t_c is falsified; if arm (a) later resumes burying spontaneously after a month with no reinforcement, the claim that the act permanently extinguishes is falsified.

Scenario 3 — Calf foster-nurse after maternal refusal. Protocol: Calves are separated from the dam or the dam refuses suckling for 48 hours and calves are hand-fed; a foster dam is then introduced. Control: continuously dam-reared calves. Model: the earlier hand-feeding habit alters later instinctive responsiveness, so ℋ for the foster-nurse contingency is disrupted by the prior spoon/bottle habit trace. Prediction: hand-fed calves show increased latch latency and fewer suckling bouts with the foster dam compared with controls. Veto/falsification: if hand-fed calves latch with identical latency, duration, and pacing as continuously dam-reared controls, then the mechanism that "the effect of later instincts may be altered by the habits which earlier ones have left behind" [PAGE 409] is falsified for the foster-nurse transition.
