Counterfactual: If a diffuse arousal neuromodulator synchronizes the irritability fluctuations of fibers (Cov(δI_k, δI_l) = ρ σ_I² with ρ > 0), the central limit theorem condition fails. Verifiable prediction: trial-to-trial reaction strengths exhibit positive serial correlation (ρ > 0.1) and excess kurtosis κ > 3 + 0.5; the distribution is leptokurtic rather than Gaussian. VETO: reject Hull’s normal-law model for reaction magnitude [PAGE 310]; replace with a correlated latent-factor or copula model.

Scenario 2 — Small-factor / quantal recruitment. Counterfactual: If the act is mediated by few motor units (M ≈ 4–8) rather than a multitude of independent factors, the effective N is below the CLT reliable regime. Verifiable prediction: force histogram shows distinct modes separated by single-twitch quantum ΔF, with Berry–Esseen error bound ε_BE > 0.1; normal approximation fails a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test at p < 0.001. VETO: reject the Gaussian muscle-contraction claim [PAGE 310]; use a compound Poisson or Poisson-binomial force model.

Scenario 3 — Non-stationarity from fatigue/temperature drift. Counterfactual: If μ_F(t) or σ_F(t) drifts by more than 10% / 25% respectively across a session (due to fatigue or ΔT > 2 °C), pooled data are a mixture, not one normal law. Verifiable prediction: sliding-window estimates reject parameter constancy (p < 0.01), and the full-session distribution is rejected by normality tests (Shapiro–Wilk p < 0.001). VETO: reject stationarity and the single normal law [PAGE 310]; adopt a time-varying state-space model with μ(t), σ(t).
