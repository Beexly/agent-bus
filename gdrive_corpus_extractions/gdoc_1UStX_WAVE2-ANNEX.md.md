<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The technical lineage in the annex spans classical mechanics, operator learning, Bayesian computation, and conformal inference, with multi-week traps at each stratum. The ARGON design's classical core invokes the Runge-Kutta integrator (Kutta, 1901; Runge, 1895) with drag and Magnus-force closures. The Magnus effect dates to Heinrich Gustav Magnus (1852), and quadratic drag formulations are standard continuum-mechanics closures. These are energy- and momentum-consistent by construction when properly discretized, which is why they are retained as the forward core rather than replaced by learned operators.

The rejected neural operators belong to a specific genealogy: DeepONet (Lu et al., 2019), Fourier Neural Operator/FNO (Li et al., 2020), and Graph Network Simulators/GNS (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020). Their common failure mode here is not approximation theory but data availability: Register R2 identifies a missing field/trajectory corpus that GSE does not own [PAGE 1]. Operator learning requires paired input fields and observed trajectories for supervision; without that asset, the historical promise of these methods cannot be converted into a usable asset. This is a data-layer trap that predates any architectural judgment.

PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019) are narrowed to inversion only per Register R3 [PAGE 1]. Historically PINNs were proposed for forward simulation of PDEs via residual minimization; the annex's critic consensus reverses that historical use case for GSE's purposes, admitting only the calibration-harness/inversion pattern. SINDy (Brunton et al., 2016) enters in discrete form only, behind a planted-law gate [PAGE 1], which is a sparse-regression identifiability constraint rather than a free discovery tool.

On the inferential side, NUTS (Hoffman & Gelman, 2014) and Caron-Gibbs hierarchical Bradley-Terry modeling supply the posterior engine; Barber weighted conformal, Gelman/Betancourt/Vehtari process gates, and Romano CQR (Romano et al., 2019) form the interval stack. Blei et al.'s variational inference is adapted with a hard ban on raw VI intervals because of the documented 0.44× standard-deviation collapse at \rho=0.9 [PAGE 1].

The multi-week trap is that season-ordered data violate exchangeability, so a coverage guarantee valid at season scale (e.g., the ACI ±67pp bound [PAGE 1]) does not decompose into valid week-level statements. Autocorrelation across weeks, roster changes, and non-stationary team strength mean that stacking week-level inferences without explicit dependence modeling is a known failure mode. The annex mitigates this by restricting ACI to drift monitoring, requiring subgroup coverage checks, and banning joint conformal quotation [PAGE 1].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto adjudication proceeds across physical, biomechanical/data, weather/exogenous, and inferential layers. A design must pass all layers; failure at any layer is a hard veto.

Physical layer. The retained RK drag/Magnus core is a classical continuum-mechanics integrator. If formulated with consistent discretization, it respects mass, momentum, and energy balances. Any replacement with a neural forward operator (FNO, DeepONet, GNS) fails this layer because operator learning supplies no conservation-law guarantee: there is no enforced \nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0 or energy residual bound, and Register R2 removes the field/trajectory corpus needed to learn or validate such a closure empirically [PAGE 1]. Forward PINN simulation is likewise vetoed by Register R3 [PAGE 1].

Biomechanical / data layer. Inverse coefficient estimation requires observed trajectories to separate physical parameters from unobserved state. Because GSE does not own the field/trajectory corpus [PAGE 1], the mapping \theta\mapsto u(t) is unidentifiable without trajectory data: distinct (\theta, x_0) pairs can generate identical observations. This is an identifiability singularity, not a computational one. The PINN inverse arm is therefore blocked both by the missing corpus and by the Part II dossier gap [PAGE 1].

Weather / exogenous layer. Drag and Magnus coefficients depend on ambient air density \rho_{\rm air}, dynamic viscosity \mu, and temperature. If weather reanalysis or on-site meteorological data are absent, coefficient estimation suffers omitted-variable bias: changes in \theta may absorb weather-driven variation rather than true physical parameter shifts. This layer is not resolved in the annex; any design claiming coefficient stability across seasons without weather conditioning is vetoed at this layer unless a sensitivity analysis bounds the weather confounding.

Inferential layer. R1 prohibits joint quotation of the four conformal theorems on season-ordered data [PAGE 1]; doing so produces an invalid joint guarantee. Blei VI raw intervals are banned because of the 0.44× SD collapse at \rho=0.9 [PAGE 1]. Petersen & Zech numeric bounds (\approx33 at season scale) are vacuous and banned as gates [PAGE 1]. The SINDy planted-law gate (\ge80% true support, \le10% false [PAGE 1]) must pass before MOVE-37 proceeds. ACI is confined to drift monitoring because of its \pm67pp season-scale bound [PAGE 1].

Composite verdict. Design 1's RK core passes the physical layer; its PINN inverse arm is conditionally admissible but BLOCKED pending Part II [PAGE 1]. Design 2 passes only if the four conformal theorems are quoted separately, NUTS divergence/\hat R/ESS gates are enforced, and the subgroup kill thresholds are implemented as hard stops [PAGE 1]. Design 3 passes only if the planted-law gate is satisfied and the coverage arm beats ridge/persistence out-of-sample [PAGE 1]. Any layer failure halts the build.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
