The source is a single-architecture paper whose entire epistemic load rests on one claim: that a *pixel-wise* loss is the wrong objective for homography estimation in topologically repetitive scenes, because two physically distinct camera poses can produce near-identical images. Multi-hop reconstruction of the argument runs: (i) the camera model is a PTZ projective matrix [PAGE 2, Eq. 1] — so the pose space is parameterized by (pan, tilt, f, X, Y, Z); (ii) the dictionary approach discretizes that pose space onto a sampling grid [PAGE 2, Table II] — 15° pan step, 5° tilt step, so the continuous manifold is coarsely quantized; (iii) the 1,000-template dictionary contains "only 0.5% of the total homographies that are generated" [PAGE 3], so the Siamese matcher is *greedy over an undersampled manifold*; (iv) when the matched template $\bar{T}_k$ is a near-miss, the STN's residual $\bar{H}$ must absorb a large transform, and a plain MSE objective has a *degenerate minimum* at the identity-residual solution — the network can drive pixel error to zero by learning $\bar{H} \approx I$ while warping the scene to a globally wrong homography. The authors state this explicitly: "the STN would learn that the estimated homography is very close to the correct one while in reality it is very different" [PAGE 3]. This is a false-minimum singularity of pixel loss on topologically redundant textures.

The fix — Topological Loss [PAGE 4, Eq. 2] — couples each patch's error to its 8-neighborhood via a hinge term $\max(0, \text{MSE}_{\text{nbr}} - \beta)$. Multi-hop: this converts an *independent* per-patch objective into a *conditionally-dependent* one, i.e., it is functionally a soft max-margin regularizer on the local error field. When neighbors disagree (structural mismatch), the hinge activates and lifts the central patch's penalty. Gray-zone audit: (a) the hinge has a *death zone* whenever $\text{MSE}_{\text{nbr}} \le \beta$ — with $\beta = 0.3$ [PAGE 5] fixed across three datasets (World Cup 2014, intersections, mixtures), the loss silently degrades to plain MSE in exactly the low-error regime where topological ambiguity is most dangerous; (b) the boundary patches ($i,j \in \{0, \sqrt{N}\}$) have fewer valid neighbors ($k,l \in \{-1,0,1\}$ indexes out-of-range), so the loss is *not translation-invariant* at the image border; (c) the ablation $\alpha=0$ is never run in the paper, so the marginal contribution of the coupling term versus the additive MSE term is only inferred from Table IV, not isolated.

Second gray-zone: the injection of $\bar{H}$ as a *zero-init, identity-bias* residual [PAGE 4]. This is a well-known STN trick, but it creates a *pathological coupling* to the matcher: if the Siamese network selects a grossly wrong template (as deliberately tested in Table VI, the mixed-dictionary experiments), the residual must cancel an arbitrary transform. The authors observe the fifth intersection drops to 60.55% (EtE, MSE matching) [PAGE 6, Table V] — a ~10% collapse — confirming that the architecture's robustness is bounded by how much of the residual the FC head can absorb. The mixed-dictionary regime [PAGE 7, Table VI] shows the proposed model "improving on the baseline model performance by up to 17%" under wrong-template stress, which is the strongest single non-synthetic-attributable result in the paper.

Third gray-zone: the warm-up clock [PAGE 5, Table III] — UNet $\le 20$ epochs at $\eta=1$, Siamese 20–40 at $\tau=1$, end-to-end $\ge 40$ at $\eta,\tau=0.05,\ \gamma=0.9$. **Projective camera model** [PAGE 2, Eq. 1]:
$$
P = K \cdot R \cdot [I \,|\, C] = K \cdot Q \cdot S \cdot [I \,|\, C], \qquad R = Q \cdot S
$$
Units: $P \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times4}$ homogeneous; $K \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times3}$ intrinsic matrix, entries in pixels (focal length $f$ in pixels, principal point in pixels); $Q, S \in SO(3)$ dimensionless; $C \in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ in the world-coordinate frame (units not specified in the paper — the sampling grids use numeric ranges 600–700 / 900–1000 / 50–100, so a pixel-like unit is implied but *not stated*). Singularity/breakdown: $P$ is rank-3 unless $f\to\infty$ (orthographic limit) or the pan/tilt degenerates to a straight-down view, at which the homography (not $P$) collapses the third row. The paper notes the virtual camera is "rotated by $-90°$ along the y-axis... so that the camera projection is parallel to the ground plane" [PAGE 2], which is exactly the degenerate top-down limit — the homography induced by this pose has a vanishing homography element row.

**Topological patch loss** [PAGE 4, Eq. 2]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}(\hat{Y}, Y) = \text{MSE}(\hat{Y}_{i,j}, Y_{i,j}) + \alpha \sum_{k}\sum_{l} \max\!\big(0,\ \text{MSE}(\hat{Y}_{i+k,j+l},\, Y_{i+k,j+l}) - \beta\big),
$$
with $k,l \in \{-1,0,1\}$ and $\text{MSE}(a,b) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(a_p - b_p)^2$. Units: $\text{MSE}$ is in squared pixel-intensity units (dimensionless if images are normalized to $[0,1]$); $\alpha,\beta$ are dimensionless; $\beta$ is compared *directly* against an MSE, so it is a threshold on the *squared-intensity scale* — this is a unit-carrying hyperparameter, not a pure number, and the paper's $\alpha=\beta=0.3$ [PAGE 5] hard-codes it across three distinct datasets without rescaling.

**Topological loss (MSE variant)** [PAGE 4, Eq. 3]:
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Top-MSE}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sum_{j} \mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}(\hat{Y}_{i,j}, Y_{i,j}), \qquad i,j \in \big[0,\dots,\sqrt{N}\big],\ N=16.
$$
Units: same as Eq. 2; dimensionless ratio $\tfrac{1}{N}$ is a partition-normalization, so $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Top-MSE}}$ has identical units to $\text{MSE}$. Breakdown regimes: (i) $\beta \to \infty$ ⇒ all hinge terms vanish ⇒ $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Top-MSE}} \to \text{MSE}$ (topology off); (ii) $\beta \to 0$ ⇒ every neighbor contributes $+\alpha\,\text{MSE}$, which over-smooths and forces the network to ignore local structure; (iii) $\alpha \to 0$ ⇒ same collapse as (i); (iv) boundary patches at $i,j\in\{0,\sqrt{N}\}$ are under-counted (only 3 or 5 valid neighbors rather than 8) ⇒ the loss is *not* shift-invariant, a form of silent regularization at the image rim.

**Joint end-to-end loss** [PAGE 4, Eq. 4]:
$$
\mathcal{L} = \eta\,\mathcal{L}_{CE} + \tau\,\mathcal{L}_{con} + \gamma\,\mathcal{L}_{warp},
$$
with $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$ the UNet cross-entropy (nats or dimensionless bits per pixel), $\mathcal{L}_{con}$ the supervised contrastive loss [PAGE 4, Ref. 27] (dimensionless negative log-likelihood), and $\mathcal{L}_{warp}$ a generic image-warping loss. Warm-up schedule [PAGE 5, Table III]: epochs $\le 20$: $(\eta,\tau,\gamma)=(1,0,0)$; epochs 20–40: $(0,1,0)$; epochs $\ge 40$: $(0.05,0.05,0.9)$. Breakdown: setting $\gamma=0.9$ at $t=0$ is unstable because $\bar{H}$ is initialized to identity and the matched template is random ⇒ the loss sees a constant warp error regardless of $\bar{H}$, a vanishing-gradient determinant near the identity-residual point.

**IoU metric** [PAGE 5]: $\text{IoU} = |\mathcal{R}_{\text{pred}} \cap \mathcal{R}_{\text{GT}}| / |\mathcal{R}_{\text{pred}} \cup \mathcal{R}_{\text{GT}}|$, dimensionless $\in[0,1]$. Note: this is *pixel IoU of warped semantic masks*, not angular/translational homography error — a geometric metric gap explicitly acknowledged implicitly by the fact that no rotation/translation error is reported anywhere.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Modeling the *system's own belief state*: the STN's implicit self-estimate is $\hat{H}$, and the training signal pre-disposes it toward one of two failure "habits." The **crisis habit** is the pixel-fidelity regression: when matched template $\bar{T}_k$ is a near-miss, the gradient of plain MSE drives $\bar{H}\to I$, i.e., the model *believes* it has converged precisely when it has not [PAGE 3, explicit]. This is a stable attractor — it survives across datasets (Intersection N.5 collapse to 60.55% in Table V [PAGE 6]). The **counter-trap** is Topological Loss: the hinge $\max(0, \text{MSE}_{\text{nbr}}-\beta)$ functions as a self-supervised *surprise* signal, forcing the network to consult its neighborhood before declaring convergence. The empirical signature of the counter-trap activating is the 7–10% IoU recovery on intersections N.3 and N.5 [PAGE 6–7]. The **clock** is the warm-up ordering [PAGE 5, Table III]: UNet before Siamese before end-to-end. This clock is not cosmetic. It encodes a causal claim — *semantic quality must precede template selection, and template selection must precede STN warp-fitting* — and it is falsifiable in the sense that reversing any pair produces the failure mode the authors describe ("the Siamese network learns incorrect features"). The recursive structure: if the UNet is warm-started wrong, the Siamese's *belief* about the correct template is wrong, and the STN's *belief* about the residual is wrong — a three-hop belief corruption propagating from the segmentation head to the homography output. The paper's mixed-dictionary experiment [PAGE 7, Table VI] is the *only* direct test of belief robustness under corrupted template supply, and it produces the strongest claimed gain ("up to 17%"), which is consistent with the theory-of-mind reading: under a wrong template, the Topological-Loss model abandons its pixel-habit and re-grounds in structure.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Veto proofs against the paper's applicability, layer by layer:

**Physical layer (mechanical).** The authors establish the veto explicitly on PAGE 1: cameras are "always moving under different forces (e.g. wind, close-passing trucks)." That is a *mechanical eigenfrequency* problem: if the pole-mounted camera oscillates at frequency $\omega_{\text{mech}}$, any homography $\hat{H}(t)$ estimated at time $t$ is invalid over a coherence time $\tau_c \sim 1/\omega_{\text{mech}}$. The paper's proposed remedy — "automated and frequent re-calibration" — is asserted, not quantified; no re-calibration interval is reported.

**Biomechanical / dynamic-actor layer.** Cars, players, pedestrians must be removed before matching: "Actors that can interfere with the calibration, like cars in an intersection or players on the soccer field, are removed by the segmentation model" [PAGE 4]. The veto is *actor density*: when the dynamic-occupancy fraction of the image exceeds UNet's effective mask capacity, residual actors leak into the semantic map and create spurious pixel differences that the STN interprets as camera motion. The paper reports *no* occupancy-vs-IoU curve, so this veto is unmitigated in the source.

**Weather / photometric layer.** PAGE 2: "weather conditions can influence the camera calibration process and cannot be controlled." The paper's semantic-segmentation defense is philosophically correct — semantic classes are photometrically invariant to rain, snow, glare — but only partly: heavy weather *occludes* semantic regions, converting photometric noise into *topological* noise, and the Topological Loss is *more* sensitive to topological noise (its whole design premise) than plain MSE would be. This is an under-discussed inversion: the loss designed to punish topological inconsistency is the loss most injured by weather-induced topological occlusion. The paper does not test weather at all; all experiments are on synthetic warps of clean bird's-eye maps [PAGE 2, Fig. 1; PAGE 3, Table II].

**Cross-layer adjudication.** The three vetoes are not independent: mechanical oscillation and weather occlusion both inject error into the segmentation input, which the paper's UNet→Siamese→STN pipeline *amplifies* rather than filters, because the whole architecture treats UNet output as ground-truth for downstream matching [PAGE 3, Fig. 3]. The only hard veto in the architecture is the identity-residual initialization, which vetoes recovery from gross template mismatch — a per-instance guarantee the paper partially tests via Table VI but does not characterize.

**Scenario 1 — Zero-coupling veto ($\alpha=0$).** Counterfactual: retrain with $\alpha=0$ in Eq. 2, so $\mathcal{L}_{\text{patch}}=\text{MSE}$ and Eq. 3 collapses to a plain patch-averaged MSE. **Scenario 2 — Hinge death-zone veto ($\beta \in \{0, 0.05, 0.3, 1.0\}$).** Counterfactual: sweep $\beta$ at fixed $\alpha=0.3$ and $N=16$. Prediction: (i) $\beta=0$ ⇒ the hinge is active for every neighbor, over-penalizing, and IoU on Intersection N.1 (a "similar topology" pair [PAGE 2, Fig. 2 caption]) degrades below the $\beta=0.3$ value of $87.91\%$; (ii) $\beta$ scaled to typical MSE magnitudes $\gg 0.3$ (e.g. $1.0$ when images are in $[0,1]$) ⇒ hinge deactivates and IoU collapses to the MSE-only band; (iii) the reported $\beta=0.3$ is a unit-mismatch artifact (a threshold calibrated on normalized intensities, not a transferable hyperparameter).
