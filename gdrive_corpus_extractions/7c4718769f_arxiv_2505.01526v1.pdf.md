Closed-loop dynamics, controls, and Nash system are given on [PAGE 2-3]. The closed-loop state evolves as
\[
dX_t^i=\alpha^i(t,X_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\sigma}\,dW_t^i+\sqrt{2\sigma_0}\,dW_t^0,\qquad X_{t_0}^i=\zeta_0^i,
\]
with feedback controls \(\alpha^i(t,x):[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^d)^N\to\mathbb{R}^d\). The Nash system is
\[
-\partial_t u^i-\sigma\sum_{1\le j\le N}\Delta_{x_j}u^i-\sigma_0\sum_{1\le j,k\le N}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(D_{x_jx_k}u^i\bigr)
+H^i(x^i,D_{x_i}u^i)
+\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le N\\ j\ne i}}D_pH^j(x^j,D_{x_j}u^j)\cdot D_{x_j}u^i=F^i(x),
\]
with \(u^i(T,x)=G^i(x)\) [PAGE 3]. The Hamiltonian is \(H^i(x,p)=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^d}\{-a\cdot p-L^i(x,a)\}\) [PAGE 3]. Open-loop dynamics replace feedback controls by progressively measurable processes \(\alpha_t^i\) and the Pontryagin system is
\[
\begin{cases}
dX_t^i=-D_pH^i(X_t^i,Y_t^i)\,dt+\sqrt{2\sigma}\,dW_t^i+\sqrt{2\sigma_0}\,dW_t^0,\\
dY_t^i=\bigl(D_xH^i(X_t^i,Y_t^i)-D_{x_i}F^i(X_t)\bigr)\,dt+\sum_{0\le j\le N}Z_t^{i,j}\,dW_t^j,\\
X_{t_0}^i=\zeta_0^i,\qquad Y_T^i=D_{x_i}G^i(X_T),
\end{cases}
\]
with the associated PDE system for \(v^i\) [PAGE 4]. Distributed equilibria use \(\alpha^i(t,x^i)\) and the PDE system with measures \(m_t^i\) [PAGE 4-5]; this formulation is restricted to \(\sigma_0=0\) for the comparison theorem [PAGE 41].

The weak-interaction metrics are defined on [PAGE 6]:
\[
\delta_i:=\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_j}G^i\|_\infty^2+\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_j}F^i\|_\infty^2,\qquad
\delta:=\max_{1\le i\le N}\delta_i,
\]
\[
\kappa_i:=\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_jx_i}G^i\|_\infty^2+\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_jx_i}F^i\|_\infty^2,\qquad
\kappa:=\max_i\kappa_i,
\]
\[
\tilde\kappa_i:=\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_ix_j}G^j\|_\infty^2+\sum_{j\ne i}\|D_{x_ix_j}F^j\|_\infty^2,\qquad
\tilde\kappa:=\max_i\tilde\kappa_i.
\]
The displacement weak condition is
\[
\delta\sum_{1\le i\le N}\delta_i\le \varepsilon
\]
and the Lasry-Lions additional condition is
\[
\kappa\tilde\kappa\le \varepsilon
\]
[PAGE 6-7]. The key dimension-free estimates are:
\[
\left|(D_{x_j}v^i)_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\right|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C
\]
[PAGE 8, equation (1.10)];
\[
\sup_{t_0,x_0}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_{t_0}^{T}\left|(D_{x_jx_i}u^i(t,X_t^{t_0,x_0}))_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\right|_{\mathrm{op}}^2dt\right]\le C
\]
[PAGE 8, equation (1.11)];
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t_0\le t\le T}\frac1N\sum_{1\le i\le N}|X_t^i-\tilde X_t^i|^2\right]\le C\,\delta\sum_{1\le i\le N}\delta_i
\]
for closed-loop versus open-loop [PAGE 8, equation (1.12)]; and, when \(\sigma_0=0\),
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t_0\le t\le T}\frac1N\sum_{1\le i\le N}|\tilde X_t^i-\overline X_t^i|^2\right]\le \frac{C}{N}\sum_{1\le i\le N}\kappa_i
\]
for open-loop versus distributed [PAGE 8, equation (1.13)]. The formal theorem versions appear on [PAGE 20-22]: Theorem 2.7 gives
\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[t_0,T]}|X_t^{OL,t_0,\zeta_0}-X_t^{CL,t_0,\zeta_0}|^2\right]\lesssim \sigma^{-1}\delta\sum_{1\le i\le N}\delta_i,
\]
and Theorem 2.8 gives the distributed comparison with factor \((1+\max_i C_P^i)\sum_i\kappa_i\) under \(\sigma_0=0\). The Lasry-Lions versions replace polynomial constants by \(\exp(\exp(C(1+\sigma^{-1})))\)-type factors [PAGE 22].

The graph-universality result uses weighted empirical measures \(m_{x,w}^{N,i}=\sum_j w_{ij}^N\delta_{x^j}\). Theorem 6.7 assumes either Assumption 6.4 or 6.6 plus
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{1\le j\le N}|w_{\cdot j}^N|_1\lt\infty,\qquad
|w^N|_{\mathrm{Fr}}\max_{1\le i\le N}|w_{i\cdot}^N|_2\to 0,
\]
and yields closed-loop/open-loop and open-loop/mean-field estimates [PAGE 43-44]. For regular graphs with common degree \(d_N\), the displayed condition becomes \(d_N^{-1}=o(N^{-1/2})\) [PAGE 44]. The empirical-measure rate is
\[
\rho_d(K)=
\begin{cases}
K^{-1/2},&d\lt4,\\
K^{-1/2}|\log K|,&d=4,\\
K^{-2/d},&d\gt4,
\end{cases}
\]
with \(K=|w_{i\cdot}^N|_2^{-2}\) [PAGE 43]. In the joint vanishing-viscosity result, Theorem 7.2 gives
\[
\sup_i\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_t^i-\tilde X_t^{N,i}|^2\right]\lesssim \rho_d(N)+\sigma_N,
\]
and the closed-loop analogue additionally requires \(\sigma_N\sqrt{N}\to\infty\) [PAGE 54-56].

Breakdown regimes: as \(\sigma\downarrow0\), the coefficient \(\sigma^{-1}\) and the \(\exp(C(1+\sigma^{-1}))\) factors blow up; the paper notes that well-posedness of the Nash system when \(\sigma=0\) is unclear, which is why Section 7 uses a decreasing sequence \(\sigma_N\) [PAGE 13, 53-56]. If \(\delta\sum_i\delta_i\) is not below the stated small threshold, the bootstrap implication (1.15) does not close and the Nash-to-Pontryagin comparison is not certified [PAGE 10-11, 35-37]. If \(\sqrt{\kappa\tilde\kappa}\) is not small, the Lasry-Lions bound in Proposition 3.6 and Corollary 3.7 does not close [PAGE 28-32]. If \(\sigma_0\ne0\), the distributed comparison Theorems 2.8 and 2.15 are not claimed [PAGE 41]. If the weighted graph is too sparse, condition (6.6) fails; for regular graphs the paper’s sufficient threshold is degree growing faster than \(\sqrt N\), not merely \(d_N\to\infty\) [PAGE 13-14, 44]. If \(T\) is large, the displacement condition \(C_{G,\mathrm{disp}}+\frac{T^2}{2}C_{F,\mathrm{disp}}\lt C_LT\) can fail, and the Lasry-Lions condition (2.19) becomes harder [PAGE 19, 22].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is explicit in the paper’s own citations. The older mean-field-game route compares closed-loop equilibria to a master equation, an infinite-dimensional PDE \(U:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathcal P_2(\mathbb{R}^d)\to\mathbb{R}\); [PAGE 9] says quantitative closed-loop convergence is usually obtained “via the master equation” and cites [9, 15, 8, 31]. The present paper reverses that methodological arrow: it first proves dimension-free bounds for the finite-\(N\) Nash and Pontryagin systems, then uses open-loop propagation of chaos plus the closed-loop/open-loop comparison to avoid the master equation entirely [PAGE 9-10]. The immediate precursor is [16], where dimension-free Nash-system bounds were obtained under a stronger mean-field-like scaling; [PAGE 12] states that [16] requires a condition implying \(\delta\sum_i\delta_i=O(1/N)\) and \(\kappa\tilde\kappa=O(1/N^2)\), while the present paper relaxes this to weak conditions (weak1) and (weak2). The paper also connects to stochastic graphon games [2,5,7,1,28] and notes that with i.i.d. initial conditions the graphon game collapses to a mean-field game [PAGE 13]. The joint vanishing-viscosity limit descends from displacement-monotone master-equation well-posedness without idiosyncratic noise [3] and Lasry-Lions weak-solution theory [10], but this paper sidesteps those master-equation objects for the finite-player comparison [PAGE 13, 53-56].

Retrocausal lesson: later results reframe earlier “master equation first” work as one sufficient route, not the definition of convergence. The multi-week traps are therefore: (i) spending weeks trying to construct \(D_{mm}U\) when the comparison bounds can replace it; [PAGE 12-13] explicitly says the dimension-free Nash bounds can “somehow replace” the second Wasserstein derivative bound. (ii) Assuming mean-field scaling \(w_{ij}=O(1/N)\) is necessary; the paper’s condition allows much stronger interactions, with networks satisfying (1.9) [PAGE 6-7]. (iii) Assuming the graph degree threshold is \(d_N\to\infty\); the paper’s sufficient condition is stronger, \(d_N^{-1}=o(N^{-1/2})\), and the authors flag the possible \(\sqrt N\) loss [PAGE 13-14]. (iv) Treating open-loop equilibria as automatically sufficient; Remark 2.1 warns that Pontryagin is necessary rather than sufficient absent convexity [PAGE 17]. (v) Forgetting that the distributed formulation is not covered with common noise [PAGE 41]. (vi) Forgetting that all estimates are conditional on admissible solutions; the paper repeatedly assumes existence and regularity rather than proving global existence for arbitrary data [PAGE 17-19]. These are not cosmetic: they determine whether a claimed convergence rate is inside or outside the proved regime.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The game-theoretic “theory of mind” here is information structure. A closed-loop player chooses a feedback map \(\alpha^i(t,x)\) depending on the full state \(x=(x^1,\dots,x^N)\) and therefore can, in principle, condition on how opponents would react to deviations [PAGE 2-3]. An open-loop player chooses a progressively measurable process \(\alpha_t^i\) adapted to the common filtration and does not condition on the current full state in the same feedback sense [PAGE 3]. A distributed player chooses \(\alpha^i(t,x^i)\) depending only on their own state [PAGE 4]. The paper’s comparison says that when interactions are weak, these different recursive models of other players become quantitatively close. The quotation reproduced on [PAGE 8] captures the intuition: “when the impact of players on their opponents' costs/rewards is small, open loop and closed loop equilibriums should be the same.” The paper’s contribution is to turn that intuition into bounds, not to assert exact equivalence.

“Crisis habit regression” in this setting is the analyst’s or player’s habitual fallback to the simpler open-loop or distributed model because it is more tractable. The paper itself says open-loop equilibria are “more tractable” because “players need not consider how their opponents would react to deviations from the equilibrium path” [PAGE 8]. That habit is valid only under the weak-interaction metrics and, for distributed comparison, only when \(\sigma_0=0\). “Counter-trap” is the opposite error: over-trusting the mean-field intuition in a network with high-degree hubs, asymmetric weights, or common noise. The paper’s conditions (6.5)-(6.6) and the regular-graph threshold \(d_N^{-1}=o(N^{-1/2})\) are the counter-trap guards [PAGE 43-44]. “Clock” is the pair \((T,\sigma_N)\): the horizon enters the displacement condition \(C_{G,\mathrm{disp}}+\frac{T^2}{2}C_{F,\mathrm{disp}}\lt C_LT\), and the vanishing-viscosity schedule requires \(\sigma_N\sqrt N\to\infty\) for the closed-loop comparison [PAGE 19, 54-56]. Thus the recursive theory-of-mind audit is: use open-loop/distributed approximations only while the interaction metrics are small, the graph is dense enough in the proved sense, the noise is non-degenerate or scheduled correctly, and the horizon satisfies the monotonicity inequality. Outside that box, the “habit” is not justified by the source.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
There is no physical, biomechanical, or weather layer in this source. The state is \(X\in(\mathbb{R}^d)^N\), the controls are \(\alpha\), and the exogenous randomness is Brownian \((W^i,W^0)\) with intensities \(\sigma,\sigma_0\) [PAGE 2-5]. No force, fatigue, injury, wind, precipitation, temperature, terrain, or sensor variable appears in the definitions of \(L^i,F^i,G^i\), the Nash system, the Pontryagin system, or the distributed system [PAGE 2-5]. Therefore any claim that this paper adjudicates a physical, biomechanical, or weather outcome is vetoed for lack of variables and units. The only “physical-like” layer is mathematical viscosity: \(\sigma\) is the idiosyncratic noise intensity and \(\sigma_N\downarrow0\) is a vanishing-viscosity schedule, not a weather measurement [PAGE 53-56]. The cross-layer veto is thus: if a proposed inference requires measured forces, biomechanical constraints, or meteorological covariates, the source contains no measurement model, no calibration, and no identified parameter for those layers; the inference is outside the source’s domain. The paper’s own scope is stochastic differential games and mean-field limits, not empirical physical prediction [PAGE 1, 41, 53].

Counterfactual: set the weights so \(W_1\) crosses the threshold, holding \(N,\sigma,T\) fixed. Prediction: the certified bound no longer applies; an empirical check should show the closed-loop/open-loop sup-gap failing to track \(\sigma^{-1}W_1\) and potentially growing. Veto if a claim asserts the \(O(W_1)\) comparison without verifying (2.13). Cite [PAGE 6-8, 20-22].

2. Common-noise/distributed veto. Verifiable prediction: with \(\sigma_0=0\), Theorem 2.8 gives \(\mathbb{E}\sup_t|X^{OL}-X^{\mathrm{dist}}|^2\lesssim(1+\max_i C_P^i)\sum_i\kappa_i\), but with \(\sigma_0\gt0\) the paper explicitly does not claim the distributed comparison [PAGE 20-22, 41]. Counterfactual: run the same weighted game with \(\sigma_0\gt0\) and test the distributed bound. Prediction: the theorem’s hypotheses fail, and the measured gap need not satisfy the \(\sum_i\kappa_i\) bound. Veto any citation of Theorem 2.8 or 2.15 under common noise. Cite [PAGE 41].

3. Vanishing-viscosity clock veto. Verifiable prediction: in the displacement-monotone mean-field setting, Theorem 7.2 gives the open-loop rate \(\rho_d(N)+\sigma_N\), and the closed-loop rate additionally requires \(\sigma_N\sqrt N\to\infty\) [PAGE 54-56]. Prediction: the open-loop rate \(\rho_d(N)+\sigma_N\) may still hold, but the closed-loop comparison term, controlled through \(1/(\sigma_N N^2)\) in the proof’s symmetry step, need not vanish; the closed-loop claim is vetoed. If \(a\lt1/2\), the schedule satisfies \(\sigma_N\sqrt N\to\infty\) and the theorem’s closed-loop comparison is available. This is a directly checkable schedule test with the exact condition (7.4).
