Failure at any layer vetoes the inference, returning the analysis to Tolman’s [PAGE 193] conclusion that precise evaluation at radically different discriminanda- and manipulanda-levels is “relatively impossible or puerile.”

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios (RLVR = Reward-Validated Counterfactual Rejection framework) with explicit predictions.

Scenario 1: Allometric manipulanda rescaling. Counterfactual manipulation: Replace fixed-size doors and keys with allometrically scaled manipulanda such that each species encounters an apparatus scaled to its body dimensions (e.g., door height = 1.2 × shoulder height; key spacing = 1.5 × digit span). Verification metric: Choice accuracy and latency measured before and after rescaling within the same individuals. Prediction: If accuracy equalizes across phylogenetic levels after rescaling, the claim of greater formal capacity at higher phylogenetic rank is vetoed; the apparent difference was a physical-fit confound. If accuracy remains stratified by species, a residual formal-capacity difference may be provisionally retained.

Scenario 2: Information-equated discriminanda. Counterfactual manipulation: Adjust the number of available doors/keys so that log₂(N_{Δ,s}^{eff}) is identical across species (e.g., rat given 8 of 9 doors, human given 8 of 12 keys), thereby equating the dimensional capacity D_s in bits. Verification metric: Bits of information transmitted per trial (mutual information between goal and choice). Prediction: Equating D collapses the dimensional-capacity difference; any remaining variance in F_s is attributable to formal structure rather than set size. If the phylogenetic ranking persists despite bit-equating, Tolman’s monotonicity claim gains support; if it vanishes, the ranking is vetoed as a dimensional artifact.

Scenario 3: Temporal-maze clock removal. Counterfactual modification of Hunter’s Temporal Maze: Set the inter-choice delay to zero and remove temporal discounting so that the task becomes a static spatial means-end problem. Verification metric: Proportion of optimal paths chosen with clock active versus clock removed, compared across species. Prediction: With the clock removed, performance differences between phylogenetic levels should shrink substantially; with the clock active, differences should re-emerge as speed-accuracy tradeoffs. If the species effect disappears when the clock is removed, the original temporal-maze finding is vetoed as a measure of temporal pressure rather than formal capacity.
