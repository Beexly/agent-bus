**Units and assumptions.** Treat \(C\), \(f\), and \(g\) as having cost units \(U\); \(H(\pi\|\mu\otimes\nu)=\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij}\log(\pi_{ij}/(\mu_i\nu_j))\) is in nats; \(\tau\) has units \(U/\mathrm{nat}\), so \(\tau H\) has cost units. The logits \(U^k_{ij}=(f^k_i+g^k_j-C_{ij})/\tau\) and the rescaled increments \(v^k,w^k\) are dimensionless in natural-log units. The iteration index \(k\) is dimensionless; the paper’s scaled time is \(t=\tau k\) in the informal discussion and \(k\asymp2t/\tau\) in the theorems, so the factor \(2\) is only a scaling convention. Assumptions: \(\mu_{\min},\nu_{\min}>0\); the finite-cost bipartite graph \(E=\{(i,j):C_{ij}<\infty\}\) has no isolated vertex when infinite costs are used [PAGE 7].

**Dual OT and dual EOT.** The unregularized dual is
\[
\max_{f\in\mathbb R^m,g\in\mathbb R^n} f^\top\mu+g^\top\nu
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}\ \forall i,j,
\]
and the paper writes \(\Psi_0(f,g)=-f^\top\mu-g^\top\nu\), \(F=\{(f,g):f_i+g_j\le C_{ij}\ \forall i,j\}\) [PAGE 6]. The entropic dual objective is
\[
\Psi_\tau(f,g)
=
\tau\left(\sum_{ij}e^{(-C_{ij}+f_i+g_j)/\tau}\mu_i\nu_j-1\right)
-f^\top\mu-g^\top\nu.
\]
The corresponding relative entropy primal is
\[
\min_{\pi\in\Delta_{m\times n}}\sum_{ij}C_{ij}\pi_{ij}
+\tau H(\pi\|\mu\otimes\nu)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\sum_j\pi_{ij}=\mu_i,\quad
\sum_i\pi_{ij}=\nu_j.
\]
These are the paper’s equations (1.1), (1.2), and (2.2) [PAGE 1], [PAGE 6]. The source states at [PAGE 1] that “the evolution of the dual variables (the logarithm of the scaling factors) is approximately piecewise-linear, while the primal variables (the approximate transport plans) exhibit a saddle-to-saddle type behavior.”

**Hard and soft \(C\)-transforms and Sinkhorn.** The hard transforms are
\[
f_0[g]_i=\min_j(C_{ij}-g_j),\qquad
g_0[f]_j=\min_i(C_{ij}-f_i).
\]
The soft transforms are
\[
f_\tau[g]_i=-\tau\log\sum_j e^{(-C_{ij}+g_j)/\tau}\nu_j,\qquad
g_\tau[f]_j=-\tau\log\sum_i e^{(-C_{ij}+f_i)/\tau}\mu_i.
\]
The Sinkhorn update is, verbatim from [PAGE 6],
\[
\text{for }k\ge0\text{ even: } f^{k+1}=f_\tau[g^k],\quad g^{k+1}=g^k;
\]
\[
\text{for }k\text{ odd: } f^{k+1}=f^k,\quad g^{k+1}=g_\tau[f^k].
\]
The primal variable is \(\pi^k_{ij}=e^{(-C_{ij}+f^k_i+g^k_j)/\tau}\mu_i\nu_j\) for \(k\ge1\). The rescaled increments are
\[
v^k=\frac{f^k-f^{k-2}}{\tau},\qquad
w^k=\frac{g^k-g^{k-2}}{\tau},
\]
and the logits are \(U^k_{ij}=(f^k_i+g^k_j-C_{ij})/\tau\). The paper records the key regime at [PAGE 13]: “Since \(v^k,w^k=O(1)\), the matrix \(\tau U^k_{ij}=f^k_i+g^k_j-C_{ij}\) stays approximately constant for \(\Theta(1)\) iterations.”

\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\exists Q\in\Delta_E,\ X_\sharp Q=\mu',\ Y_\sharp Q=\nu,

\nu^*(E)=\arg\min_{\nu'}H(\nu'\|\nu)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\exists P\in\Delta_E,\ X_\sharp P=\mu,\ Y_\sharp P=\nu',
\]
and
\[
v^*(E)=\log\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^*(E)}\right),\qquad
w^*(E)=\log\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu^*(E)}\right).

and if finite,
\[

=
\left\{

\right\}.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The retrocausal lineage runs from the 1967 Sinkhorn–Knopp matrix-scaling procedure, through Cuturi’s 2013 reinvention of Sinkhorn as a fast entropic OT solver, to Altschuler–Niles-Weed–Rigollet’s rounding framework for unregularized OT, and then to the low-\(\tau\) asymptotics of Baradat–Ventre and the convergence refinements of Wang. In that older lineage, Sinkhorn is usually understood through contraction or polynomial suboptimality. The source reverses part of that expectation: the algorithm is not merely approximately solving a smooth entropic problem in the small-\(\tau\) regime; its dual iterates approximate a finite-phase, piecewise-linear, simplex-type path for the unregularized dual. This is why the paper can say at [PAGE 1] that the result implies a “novel guarantee” with \(O(1/\tau)\) iterations. The historical trap is the habit of extrapolating worst-case exponential contraction \(e^{-\Theta(1/\tau)}\) to explain all small-\(\tau\) behavior; the source shows that the relevant object for fixed finite marginals and generic costs is the cold dynamics, whose phase structure is hidden by global contraction estimates. Another trap is to assume that the Berman parabolic Monge–Ampère picture is necessary; the source states that its finite-domain result needs no geometric structure [PAGE 5]. A third multi-week trap is to read the primal’s long plateaus as convergence; in fact the off-support logits, not the visible primal masses, drive the next phase [PAGE 27]. A fourth trap is to treat the final phase as “no dynamics”; the cold dynamics is stationary there, but Sinkhorn still converges toward a distinguished entropic limit \(U^*_\tau\), and the two limits need not commute [PAGE 19]. The source also forces a retroactive reinterpretation of mirror-flow analogies: Léger’s mirror descent view of Sinkhorn and the Aubin-Frankowski–Korba–Léger measure-space view are genuine for entropic problems, but the paper’s \( \tau\to0 \) cold limit cannot be an autonomous mirror flow because \(\mu(t)\) is piecewise constant [PAGE 27].

The recursive trap is to mistake the observer’s clock for the algorithm’s clock: a run truncated before \(t_L/\tau\) looks like a stall, while a run after \(t_L/\tau\) looks like ordinary entropic convergence. The paper’s finite-phase theorem and finite-time \(t_L\le T\) are the adjudicating clock.
