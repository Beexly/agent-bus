<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
"Veto" here means a hard constraint that overrides the objective: no matter how much a trajectory reduces ℓ, a violated constraint rejects it. The dissertation's veto layers are physical and geometric; the "biomechanical/weather" layer is, importantly, largely absent — which is itself the key adjudication finding.

Layer 1 — Actuator/state admissibility (physical veto). u(t) ∈ U ⊆ ℝᵐ and x(t) ∈ X ⊆ ℝⁿ ([PAGE 32], 4.2), typically convex compact sets. Proof of veto force: in the SQP subproblem (2.14) and collocation NLP (3.10) these enter as c(x,u) ⪯ 0; the KKT multiplier λ_i⋆ > 0 (strongly active, [PAGE 33] Def 2.7) certifies the constraint is doing work. Layer 2 — Collision/freespace veto (geometric). x(t) ∈ X_free(t) = X \ X_obst(t) ([PAGE 50] 4.4). This is the nonconvex veto. Breakdown regime: with no feasible warm-start, the collision veto makes the NLP infeasible and the solver returns nothing ([PAGE 54] Fig 4.2 — the simplified-model initialization "from where it is impossible for an ocp solver to find a feasible solution").
