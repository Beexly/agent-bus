From [PAGE 386], the rodent's impulse is to "hurry to its home" when cheek-pouches are filled and to empty them at the "sight of the storehouse." If the storehouse is walled off, \(V_{\text{phys}}=0\) and no emptying occurs at the destination, regardless of the strength of the desire.

Biomechanical layer \(V_{\text{bio}}\): cheek-pouch capacity, brood patch, muscular ability to orient the body toward the middle of the room [PAGE 386]. A hen with a damaged brood patch cannot incubate effectively even if the nest and eggs are present; the impulse is there but the consummatory apparatus fails.

Weather layer \(V_{\text{weather}}\): ambient temperature \(T_{\text{amb}}\), precipitation, wind chill. On a cold day humans sit round the stove [PAGE 386], but if \(T_{\text{amb}}\) enters an extreme survival regime (blizzard, sub-zero exposure), stove-seeking may be vetoed by higher-priority thermoregulatory constraints. Similarly, a flooded or wind-swept nest vetoes incubation even if the hen is hormonally primed.

Adjudication rule:

\[
R = V_{\text{phys}}\cdot V_{\text{bio}}\cdot V_{\text{weather}}\cdot H(A-\theta).
\]

If any \(V_i=0\), then \(R=0\) irrespective of \(A\) or \(U\). This is veto-proof because it does not require estimating utility or reading the animal's mind; it only requires verifying whether the physical pathway from stimulus to act exists. Applied to [PAGE 386], the hen's incubation is observable only when nest, eggs, and brooding physiology are intact; remove the nest (physical veto) and the behavior ceases, no "prophetic inkling" required.
