- $\mu_c(t)$: Chemical gain factor, derived from plasma concentration $C(t)$ via a Hill equation: $\mu_c(t) = \frac{C(t)^n}{EC_{50}^n + C(t)^n}$, where $EC_{50}$ is the half-maximal effective concentration (mol/L) and $n$ is the Hill coefficient.

$$ sIr_{eff}(t) = sIr_{base} \cdot e^{-\lambda \cdot \mu_c(t)} $$
Where $\lambda$ is a species-specific sensitivity constant (L/mol). For caffeine, $\lambda$ is high, leading to rapid decay of inhibitory memory traces.

$$ P(R=1 | H_p) = \sigma(k \cdot (H_p - \theta)) $$

This creates a sharp discontinuity: increasing dose no longer increases $\mu_c(t)$ but triggers a systemic failure mode.

The "reaction potential" is vetoed by the physical impossibility of generating force.
3. **Weather/Environmental Veto:** Temperature affects thermoregulation. The model must include a veto function $V_{phys}$ such that if Physiological Stress > Threshold, $sEr \leftarrow 0$ regardless of $\mu_c(t)$.
4. **Hormonal Receptor Saturation:** In the sex hormone example, Beach notes that castrated animals respond promptly to injection. However, prolonged exposure leads to downregulation of androgen receptors. Once receptor density drops below a critical mass, even massive increases in serum testosterone yield diminishing returns, imposing a ceiling on reactivity that pure concentration models miss.
