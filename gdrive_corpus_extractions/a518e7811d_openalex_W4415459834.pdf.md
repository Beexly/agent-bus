**Operator 1 — 2D keypoint extraction (Stage 1).**

Let I_t ∈ ℝ^{H×W×3} be frame at time t ∈ [t_0, t_N] sampled at Δt = 1/f_s (f_s = frames·s⁻¹). A pose model f_θ produces

K_t = { (x_k, y_k, c_k) }_{k=1}^{K_p},    x_k, y_k ∈ [0, W] × [0, H] in pixels,   c_k ∈ [0,1]

with K_p = 17 (COCO topology) or 33 (YOLOv8-Pose), and c_k a pseudo-probability heatmap peak (dimensionless). [PAGE 2]

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* c_k → 0 under occlusion (limb out of frame during contact), motion blur at f_s ≤ 30 s⁻¹, low illumination, or truncation at image boundary. In that regime x_k, y_k are extrapolations, not measurements, and any downstream joint angle inherits an unbounded error.

**Operator 2 — Joint-angle conversion (Stage 2).**

For each anatomical triplet (parent, joint, child) with limb vectors u_j = p_{j} − p_{parent} and v_j = p_{child} − p_{j}:

θ_t^{(j)} = arccos( (u_j · v_j) / (‖u_j‖₂ ‖v_j‖₂) ) · (180/π)   [degrees]

[PAGE 3] "Pose sequences convert into joint-angle time series data, capturing intricate body movements and alignments over time."

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) ‖u_j‖₂ = 0 or ‖v_j‖₂ = 0 → arccos argument undefined (0/0). (ii) Because arccos is total only on ℝ→[0,π], sign of flexion/extension is unrecoverable — an elbow flexed at +60° and hyperextended at −60° map to the same θ if the reference frame is unanchored. (iii) Monocular 2D projection removes the depth component; for a swing plane tilted out of image plane by angle φ, the observed joint angle differs from true by Δ ≈ (1−cos φ)·θ_true, unbounded as φ → π/2.

**Operator 3 — Dynamic Time Warping (Stage 3a).**

Let q ∈ ℝ^{N×d} be query (student) and c ∈ ℝ^{M×d} be reference, both joint-angle series. DTW path π = (π_1, …, π_L) with π_l = (i_l, j_l) monotone (i_l, j_l non-decreasing, steps ∈ {(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)}):

D^{DTW}(q, c) = min_{π} Σ_{l=1}^{L} d(q_{i_l}, c_{j_l}),   d(x, y) = ‖x − y‖₂²   [deg²]

Recursive form: D(i,j) = d(q_i, c_j) + min{ D(i−1,j), D(i,j−1), D(i−1,j−1) }.

[PAGE 2] "Dynamic Time Warping aligns temporal sequences, enabling the evaluation of athlete movement variations even at different execution speeds."

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) When |N − M| exceeds the Sakoe-Chiba band radius r (typically 0.1·max(N,M)), the path must leave the band and warp degenerates — an athlete 2× slower than reference yields a meaningless alignment. (ii) Pathological warp against a constant reference produces L → N+M and a spuriously low cost. (iii) Cost is not invariant to frame rate f_s: a stroke recorded at 60 s⁻¹ versus 30 s⁻¹ halves N and changes D by an f_s-dependent factor unless it is normalized to arc length.

**Operator 4 — Cosine similarity (Stage 3b, alternative).**

For flattened joint-angle vectors a, b ∈ ℝ^d:

sim_cos(a, b) = (Σ_{k=1}^d a_k b_k) / √(Σ a_k² · Σ b_k²) ∈ [−1, 1],   dimensionless

[PAGE 3] "The system employs Dynamic Time Warping or cosine similarity to compare extracted sequences against reference templates."

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) ‖a‖₂ = 0 (all joint angles zero, e.g., a fully occluded pose) → undefined. (ii) sim_cos ignores magnitude: a timid 30° elbow bend and a maximal 150° bend score identically if their directional cosines agree — the metric is scale-blind in the exact quantity a coach cares about. (iii) High-d (concatenated time series) saturates because sim_cos → 1/√d asymptotically for random vectors; a "0.97 match" may be statistically equivalent to a "0.30 match" depending on baseline.

**Operator 5 — Match percentage (Stage 3c).**

Let s = D^{DTW} or 1 − (1 − sim_cos)/2. The paper [PAGE 3] reports "a match percentage indicating overall similarity" without specifying the map g. A defensible form with confidence weighting is

M = 100 · Σ_k c_k · g(s_k) / Σ_k c_k   [%]

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) Σ_k c_k → 0 (whole pose occluded) → M undefined; the paper gives no abstention rule. (ii) g unspecified → M is not comparable across template families. (iii) Anti-correlation is possible if g folds [−1,1] onto [0,100] without sign correction, so a perfectly anti-aligned stroke could score 50%.

**Operator 6 — Embedding clustering and style match (Stage 4).**

Let e = φ(K_{1:N}) ∈ ℝ^d_e. Assign closest centroid:

k* = argmin_{k ∈ {1,…,K_c}} ‖e − μ_k‖₂

[PAGE 3] "Advanced clustering algorithms map student motion embeddings to the nearest professional clusters, facilitating the identification of stylistic nuances."

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) silhouette coefficient → 0 (or Davies-Bouldin → ∞) means the "nearest cluster" is noise. (ii) If view/lighting variance exceeds style variance in the embedding (offline annotation), k* reflects camera not playstyle. (iii) In high d_e with small reference cohort (n_pro ≈ 200 ATP/WTA strokes), nearest-centroid is dominated by the manifold prior — most students will be assigned to the single largest cluster.

**Operator 7 — Federated averaging and privacy (Stage 5).**

θ^{(t+1)} = Σ_{i=1}^{N_u} (n_i / n) θ^{(t+1)}_i,   (ε, δ)-DP guarantee per [PAGE 4] "Federated learning implementations offer pathways for continuous model enhancement while maintaining strict privacy protections."

*Singularity/breakdown regimes:* (i) If Δ = n_max/n_min → ∞ (one academy with thousands of clips and one with ten), the aggregated model leaks the larger client's update — DP guarantee (ε, δ) is per-step, not end-to-end under non-IID video. (ii) Video tensors are high-dimensional; DP-SGD clipping norm C must scale with d · σ, often degrading utility below the accuracy needed for pose detection.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The platform's intellectual lineage is a fork-and-merge history, and each fork carries a prior assumption now under stress. Dynamic time warping descends from Sakoe & Chiba's 1978 speech-recognition DP algorithm [4] — an alignment method designed for quasi-periodic acoustic sequences where monotone warp is a natural assumption. Its transplantation into tennis biomechanics assumes strokes relax the same monotone alignment; that assumption fails for strokes with a distinct "pause-at-top" (delayed backswing), where DTW's monotone path must choose one phase to stretch and one to compress. The cosine-similarity branch descends from information-retrieval vector-space models (Salton, 1970s) — designed for sparse term-frequency vectors, not dense joint-angle time series; its scale-blindness noted above is a well-documented failure mode in that literature but is rarely mentioned in sports-analytics adaptations.

Pose estimation descends from OpenPose (Cao et al., IEEE TPAMI 2019, [2]) — Part Affinity Fields designed for multi-person social-scene parsing, not single-athlete dynamic sports; then MoveNet (Google, 2021) for mobile; then YOLOv8-Pose (Jocher, 2023, [3]) for deployment. Each generation reduced latency and mobile cost but formalized the same underlying projection geometry, so the depth ambiguity remains unchanged across all three: monocular 2D keypoints lose the out-of-plane coordinate.

The style-matching branch descends from clustering of motion-capture embeddings used in the 2018–2022 "nearest pro" retrieval era (marketing systems like HomeCourt, SwingVision for tennis/tracking); the paper's specific intervention — nearest-centroid over pro strokes — inherits the retrieval assumption that "style" is a low-dimensional latent feature. It is not; Rafa Nadal and Roger Federer forehands differ mostly in continuous parameters (spin, trajectory, racket-head speed), not in discrete cluster identity. The paper [PAGE 3] "actionable prompts like 'widen your stance'" implicitly assumes corrections are categorical, when the underlying biomechanics are parametric.

The LLM-coaching branch descends from Ma et al. 2025 [8] (table-tennis multimodal LLM), which was trained on expert-annotated table-tennis corpora. The transfer to tennis is not free: wrist action, footwork, and court spatial reasoning differ across sports. The paper [PAGE 4] claims transferability ("The core methodologies and training frameworks can be easily transferred to other sports, such as golf, baseball, and martial arts") — a strong cross-domain-generalization claim made without a single transfer experiment.

**Multi-week traps:** (i) *Retrofit-of-metrics trap* — once M is emitted weekly, coaches may anchor on M rather than on video evidence, a Goodhart race of two to three weeks. (ii) *Reference-template drift* — if the pro reference set is not periodically re-annotated (players' strokes evolve), M becomes increasingly non-comparable across seasons. (iii) *Federated-silent-drift* — a distributed learning loop can silently improve M while degrading the LLM feedback grounding, because the two heads are trained on different objectives; last-mile evaluation lags by quarters, not days.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three agents simulate each other in this design: (A) the platform's user model (K_t, θ_t, e, M), (B) the young player's model of the coach, and (C) the LLM's model of the player's intent.

*Belief-A about B (player modeling the platform).* Once a badge system and "match percentage" appear together, the player's fast-timescale model is "what pose maximizes M?" — not "what pose maximizes stroke quality?" This is a crisis-habit regression trap: the player may regress toward exaggerated stance-width because wider stance likely increases the knee-X keypoint distance and thus nudges M upward. The paper [PAGE 3] shows no anti-gaming constraint — no reward shaping that penalizes anatomically extreme poses.

*Belief-B about A (coach modeling the platform).* The paper [PAGE 4] says "Coaches can monitor multiple students simultaneously, using the platform's automated feedback to supplement rather than replace human instruction." This is the correct institutional guardrail, but it introduces a second-order risk: fatigue-inducing dashboards push coaches to trust M without checking keypoint confidence c_k. A coach who has seen M rise for six straight weeks is unlikely to distrust week seven — metacognitive anchoring.

*Belief-C (LLM hallucination).* An LLM fine-tuned on expert-commentary pairs will, under distributional shift, generate "widen your stance" from a context in which no keypoint delta justifies it — the same confabulation geometry as vision-language hallucination on out-of-distribution images. The paper [PAGE 3] gives no calibration check, no abstention fallback, no logit-based refusal.

*The counter-trap.* A properly-designed system would add a second head that scores "is M sensitive to actual biomechanical fidelity?" — a discriminator that catches reward hacking. The paper does not describe one.

*The clock.* Real-time feedback operates at ~2 Hz (per [PAGE 3] "low-latency feedback"). Motor-learning literature distinguishes immediate augmented feedback (which improves short-term performance but degrades retention) from delayed feedback at 2–5 s (which improves consolidation). The paper does not stagger its latency, so its very speed may be its retention weakness — a temporal novel in the design.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
*Physical veto.* Every pose correction prompt [PAGE 3] "widen your stance" or "increase your follow-through height" must respect goniometric bounds. The paper cites no anatomical range-of-motion constraint. A trivially valid veto: if the recommended stance width would drive hip abduction beyond the player's measured (or anthropometric-tabulated) limit, the prompt is physically inadmissible regardless of match score.

*Biomechanical veto.* The paper [PAGE 4] lists "wearable sensor technology for precise force measurement capabilities" as future work — meaning today's system has no force plate, no pressure insole, no inertial measurement. Any prompt whose justification requires knowledge of ground reaction force, joint torque, or center-of-pressure is therefore ungrounded by construction. "Widen your stance" affects base-of-support and lateral stability; without CoP data the strength of the recommendation cannot be verified.

*Weather/lighting veto.* Frame normalization [PAGE 2] "maintain[s] consistent lighting and color profiles," but outdoor tennis under 12:00 sun + shadow contrast routinely produces clipped specular highlights on skin and racket — the pose model's c_k collapses to near-zero on wrist and racket-head keypoints, which are exactly the keypoints driving "follow-through height." A relative humidity or dew glare event can silently floor the confidence. Outdoor play also introduces wind-induced camera shake on tripod-mounted phones.

*Camera-layer veto.* At f_s = 30 Hz and racket-head speed ≈ 40 m·s⁻¹ at contact, the racket moves ~1.33 m per frame — an aliasing regime in which racket keypoints cannot be reliably localized; joint-angle time series become temporally undersampled. The paper does not address anti-aliasing (high-speed capture, motion-compensated interpolation).

*Aggregate proof.* A recommendation is admissible only if all four layers pass simultaneously: goniometric bound OK, no force-dependent claim, c_k > c_min per keypoint, f_s·Δt < human response bandwidth. Any single failure invokes a veto over the full prompt — the LLM's fluency cannot override a layer-0 veto. The paper's architecture does not implement an explicit veto ladder; it is implicit in the (unspecified) fine-tuning data.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
