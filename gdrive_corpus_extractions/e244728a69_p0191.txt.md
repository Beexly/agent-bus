<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Reference 8 [PAGE 1] specifies that behavior potentiality at extinction depends jointly on training and hunger. In an extinction crisis (non-reinforcement), the organism does not simply extinguish; it regresses to a drive-dominated state where hunger H reasserts control over responding. The recursive controller monitors T (past reinforcement) and H (current deficit), and if H is high, it suppresses extinction despite low T.

Counter-trap: Reference 10 [PAGE 1] (backward conditioning of the lid reflex) is the counter-trap. If the experimenter arranges US → CS, the subject's associative machinery faces a temporal paradox. A naive theory of mind assumes the subject predicts US from CS (forward). Backward conditioning violates this prediction arrow, creating a recursive anomaly: the subject must represent a cause that follows its effect. Hull's system must either reject backward conditioning as non-associative or expand the theory to include inhibitory or temporal coding.

Clock: References 5, 11, 12, 13, 14 [PAGE 1] all invoke a clock—delay period, association span, interval between stimuli, time interval and rate of learning. The association span (ref. 11 [PAGE 1]) sets the maximum readable Δt; beyond it, the clock expires and no association is registered. Crisis occurs when Δt exceeds the span or when the clock runs backward (ref. 10 [PAGE 1]). The recursive theory of mind therefore contains a clock module that gates association formation.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: References 2 [PAGE 1] (neural correlations of vision) and 9 [PAGE 1] (central excitation/inhibition in heart rate) impose neurophysiological latencies. Synaptic and axonal conduction delays set a hard lower bound on effective Δt; if the CS–US interval is shorter than the neural transmission window, the physical layer vetoes association regardless of psychological contiguity.

Biomechanical layer: References 10, 12, 13 [PAGE 1] (lid reflex, finger-withdrawal conditioning) engage effector organs with refractory periods. A lid or finger cannot be conditioned if the interstimulus interval is below the muscle/refractory recovery time t_ref. This is a hard biomechanical veto: P(CR) = 0 for Δt < t_ref.

Internal-weather layer: Reference 8 [PAGE 1] (degree of hunger at extinction) is the internal weather. Hunger H is a motivational state analogous to atmospheric pressure—it modulates whether training T expresses as behavior. If H is low (satiety), the weather veto suppresses performance potentiality Φ even when T is high.

Adjudication: The veto hierarchy is absolute at the physical/biomechanical boundary (hard limits on Δt) and probabilistic at the motivational boundary (H gates expression). A veto-proof conclusion: no associative strength computed from T alone can override a physical refractory veto or a satiety veto; Hull's joint function f(T,H) must be conditioned on the physical layer first.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
