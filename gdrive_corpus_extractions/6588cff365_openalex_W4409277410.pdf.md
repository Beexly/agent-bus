The paper says player tracking extracts “player speed, acceleration, and distance to the ball” [PAGE 3]. Let player \(i\) have field position \(p_i(t) \in \mathbb{R}^2\) in metres, ball position \(p_b(t) \in \mathbb{R}^2\) in metres, velocity \(v_i(t)=dp_i(t)/dt\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), acceleration \(a_i(t)=d^2p_i(t)/dt^2\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), speed \(s_i(t)=\|v_i(t)\|_2\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), and ball distance \(d_i^b(t)=\|p_i(t)-p_b(t)\|_2\) in metres. A tracking token can be written as \(x_i(t)=(p_i(t),v_i(t),a_i(t),d_i^b(t),\mathrm{ID}_i)\). Assumption: coordinates are calibrated to the pitch, timestamps are synchronized, and finite differences use a sampling interval \(\Delta t\) in seconds.

The multimodal fusion operator is reconstructed as follows. Visual frames \(I_t\) are encoded by a CNN: \(h_v(t)=f_{\mathrm{CNN}}(I_t;\theta_v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_v}\). Prior commentary tokens \(C_{0:t-1}\) are encoded by BERT: \(h_\ell(t)=f_{\mathrm{BERT}}(C_{0:t-1};\theta_\ell)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_\ell}\). Tracking features are encoded by a separate network: \(h_p(t)=f_{\mathrm{MLP}}(x_{0:t};\theta_p)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_p}\). The fused representation is \(z_t=f_{\mathrm{MMT}}([h_v(t);h_\ell(t);h_p(t)];\theta_f)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\), and the next commentary token distribution is \(p_\theta(y_t\mid z_t)=\mathrm{softmax}(W z_t+b)\). Training minimizes cross-entropy \( \mathcal{L}(\theta)=-\sum_t \log p_\theta(y_t^\star\mid z_t)\) in nats per token, with entropy \(H(Y_t\mid Z_t)\) in nats or bits. The paper states: “We use a combination of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers to fuse the different modalities” [PAGE 3], and “The player tracking data is encoded using a separate neural network, and the outputs of all three networks are fused using a multimodal transformer” [PAGE 3].

For real-time behaviour, let \(L_{\mathrm{total}}=L_{\mathrm{capture}}+L_{\mathrm{track}}+L_{\mathrm{fuse}}+L_{\mathrm{gen}}+L_{\mathrm{tts}}\) be end-to-end latency in seconds. If the source frame rate is \(f_s\) in Hz, the stale-commentary boundary is \(L_{\mathrm{total}} \le 1/f_s\). The paper says the process is “optimized for real-time performance, with a focus on minimizing latency while maintaining high accuracy and relevance” [PAGE 4], but it does not report \(L_{\mathrm{total}}\). Timestamp alignment requires \(|t_v-t_p|\le \Delta_{\max}\); if this fails, the fusion operator’s concatenation is not semantically valid.

The evaluation operators are dimensionless, though the paper prints them as raw values. BLEU is \( \mathrm{BLEU}_N = \mathrm{BP}\cdot\exp(\sum_{n=1}^N w_n\log p_n)\), where \(p_n\) is clipped n-gram precision and BP is brevity penalty. METEOR is a harmonic-mean-style precision-recall score with a fragmentation penalty. ROUGE-L uses longest common subsequence overlap. CIDEr uses TF-IDF-weighted n-gram cosine similarity. The paper defines BLEU as “A metric for evaluating the quality of generated text by comparing it to reference text” [PAGE 4], and reports BLEU-1, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr in Table 1 [PAGE 5]. Breakdown regimes: (1) derivative singularity: if velocity is computed by finite difference, \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat v_i)\approx 2\sigma_p^2/\Delta t^2\), so as \(\Delta t\to0\), velocity noise diverges in \(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}\); (2) missing modality: if \(h_p(t)\) is undefined during occlusion, the fixed concatenation is invalid without a mask; (3) ID switch: a permutation of player IDs changes \(h_p(t)\) and can reverse attribution; (4) softmax saturation: large logits drive \(p_\theta(y_t\mid z_t)\to1\), reducing gradient and calibration; (5) latency breakdown: if \(L_{\mathrm{total}}>1/f_s\), the commentary describes a past state as if current; (6) metric breakdown: BLEU and CIDEr can reward fluent generic phrases while failing to penalize physically impossible claims.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s lineage starts from rule-based and template-based soccer description, which the authors describe as limited: “Early work in this area focused on generating textual descriptions from video data using rule-based systems and template-based approaches” [PAGE 2]. It then moves to large-scale video-caption datasets and neural multimodal models, citing “SoccerNet-Caption” [PAGE 2] and a model the text calls “MatchVoice” [PAGE 2], while the reference list names “MatchTime: Towards Automatic Soccer Game Commentary Generation” [PAGE 6]. The tracking branch is “SoccerNet-Tracking” [PAGE 3], and the representation branch includes ResNet for visual features, BERT for text, and GPT-3 for generation [PAGE 3]. The retrocausal genealogy is therefore: template captioning → large-scale broadcast caption datasets → video-language models → tracking-augmented multimodal fusion → real-time commentary with TTS. The paper’s novelty claim is a retro-fit: it reads the older captioning line as having ignored tracking context, and then positions tracking as the missing causal variable.

The multi-week traps are reproducibility and construct-validity traps. The first is baseline-name drift: “MatchVoice” versus “MatchTime” [PAGE 2], [PAGE 6] makes it unclear which system produced the reported baseline numbers. The second is dataset provenance: the paper says the tracking data comes from SoccerNet-Tracking and the clips and commentary from SoccerNet-Caption [PAGE 4], which creates a nontrivial alignment problem across two corpora. Without match-level and event-level synchronization, the 500-match split could leak broadcast identity or duplicate commentary styles. The third is metric trap: the reported Table 1 values show the framework at 30.32 BLEU-1, 8.45 BLEU-4, 25.25 METEOR, 29.40 ROUGE-L, and 33.84 CIDEr [PAGE 5], but these metrics do not directly measure factual tracing of player identity, event correctness, or physical plausibility. The fourth is human-evaluation trap: the paper reports 8.9 accuracy, 9.1 relevance, and 8.7 engagement [PAGE 5], but does not define the scale, annotator expertise, or agreement. The fifth is citation-numbering trap: duplicated reference numbers for BERT and GPT-3 [PAGE 6] weaken auditability. A multi-week evaluation should therefore freeze the baseline identity, reconstruct the split, verify synchronization tolerance, and add factual and physical veto checks before trusting the headline gains.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive layer is the model’s implicit prediction of three agents: the human commentator, the viewer, and the evaluator. A commentator chooses what is salient under time pressure; a viewer needs context without overload; an evaluator scores accuracy, relevance, and engagement [PAGE 4], [PAGE 5]. The model’s policy can be written as maximizing \( \mathbb{E}[U]=\alpha\,\mathrm{Acc}+\beta\,\mathrm{Rel}+\gamma\,\mathrm{Eng}-\lambda\,\mathrm{Hall}-\mu L_{\mathrm{total}}\), where Acc, Rel, Eng, and Hall are dimensionless, \(L_{\mathrm{total}}\) is in seconds, and \(\mu\) has units per second. This is not a fake ratio; it is an objective with explicit units and weights. The recursive trap is that the model learns to predict the evaluator’s preference and then generates commentary that satisfies the proxy rather than the event. If engagement is weighted without a hallucination veto, the policy can overclaim drama. If relevance is weighted without abstention, the policy can become generic and safe.

Crisis habit regression occurs under distribution shift: occlusion, ID switch, rain, camera cut, or timestamp lag. The model falls back to its training prior, producing fluent but under-informative phrases such as generic match narration. The counter-trap is the opposite: an evaluator that penalizes generic output may push the model toward confident but unsupported specificity. The clock is the real-time deadline \(L_{\mathrm{total}}\le1/f_s\): when the deadline is tight, the model must choose between delay, abstention, and hallucination. A correct recursive theory of mind should represent uncertainty about the event and about the viewer’s need, then emit either a verified description or an explicit hedge. The paper’s qualitative claim that the framework is more “accurate, relevant, and engaging” [PAGE 5] is therefore plausible only if the system has a calibrated abstention path; otherwise the recursive evaluator loop rewards the appearance of insight.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The source contains no explicit physical, biomechanical, or weather layer, so the veto proof must be reconstructed as a guard on top of the tracking and fusion operators. Let a generated event \(E\) imply a trajectory segment. A physical veto fires if the implied ball or player displacement violates a Lipschitz bound: \( \|p(t+\Delta t)-p(t)\|_2 \le v_{\max}\Delta t\). If the commentary asserts a pass or shot requiring a larger displacement, then \(p(E\mid D)=0\) under the physically admissible set, because no valid trajectory supports it. A biomechanical veto fires if the implied acceleration or change of direction exceeds a conservative human bound, \( \|a_i(t)\|_2 \le a_{\max}\), or if the pose and tracking disagree. A weather/occlusion veto fires when tracking is missing or unreliable; the correct posterior is not zero but prior-dominated, and the system should abstain when \(H(E\mid D)>\tau\) in bits or nats. The proof is conditional: if the event is physically impossible, reject; if the evidence is missing, do not claim precision. Exact breakdown occurs when \(\Delta t\to0\), because finite-difference acceleration noise scales as \(\sigma_a^2\propto\sigma_p^2/\Delta t^4\) in \(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-4}}\), making the veto itself noisy at high sampling rates. Therefore the veto must use a minimum \(\Delta t\), a smoothing window, and a confidence threshold. The paper’s “distance to the ball” feature [PAGE 3] is useful here, but the source does not report calibration, field-coordinate accuracy, or weather metadata, so the veto is a necessary external audit rather than a proven component of the published system.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR here means reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards: the reward is computed by deterministic checks, not by a learned judge. Define \(R=+1\) for a commentary that is event-compatible and passes physical and identity vetoes, \(R=-1\) for a confident hallucination or impossible event, and \(R=0\) for a correct abstention. The three counterfactual veto scenarios are:

1. Player-ID permutation veto. Keep the video and text fixed, but permute tracking IDs for one player. Verifiable check: if the commentary names player \(i\) while the visual re-identification and jersey evidence identify player \(j\), veto. Prediction: a tracking-integrated model should either re-identify from visual evidence or abstain; a model without an ID veto will show high confident misattribution. BLEU and CIDEr may remain high if names are rare in references, so the verifiable reward must include player-attribution accuracy.

2. Timestamp desynchronization veto. Shift tracking by \(\Delta t>\Delta_{\max}\) while leaving video unchanged. Verifiable check: if the commentary describes a pass before the ball displacement occurs, or a shot after the ball has already left the frame, veto. Prediction: event F1 and temporal-order accuracy should fall sharply, while latency \(L_{\mathrm{total}}\) may look normal. The model should increase abstention when \(|t_v-t_p|>\Delta_{\max}\).

3. Impossible-kinematics or modality-dropout veto. Inject a ball-coordinate jump that violates \(\|p_b(t+\Delta t)-p_b(t)\|_2\le v_{\max}\Delta t\), or remove tracking during occlusion. Verifiable check: any commentary claiming a precise speed, pass, or shot from the corrupted segment is vetoed; a correct system falls back to visual-only mode with lower confidence or abstains. Prediction: with the veto, hallucination rate drops but coverage drops; without the veto, BLEU and CIDEr can stay deceptively stable because fluent generic text still overlaps references. The paper’s reported gains [PAGE 5] should therefore be re-tested under these injections before the framework is treated as physically grounded.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
