Each of $K=4$ experts $f^{l,i}_{expert}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ is a "CONV-MLP-CONV structure but with different parameters" [PAGE 3]; a router $f^{l,t}_a:\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}\to\mathbb{R}^K$ emits attribute scores $W^{l,t}$ through a "CONV-BatchNorm-ReLU-CONV-Sigmoid" stack [PAGE 3], and the assembled feature is $F^l_{assemble}=\sum_{t=1}^{K} W^{l,t}H^l_t$. Hop 3 (interaction): the Contrastive Relation Modeling (CRM) module fuses modalities, then forms positive pairs (template ↔ target-content search tokens) and negative pairs (template ↔ background-content), optimized with a multi-positive InfoNCE (Eq. 4) [PAGE 4].

Hop 4 (gray-zone audit). (a) Attribution inconsistency: the backbone is variously described as coming from "[4]" [PAGE 2], "initialized by the transformer-based tracking backbone [22]" [PAGE 4], and "the same as the one in ViPT [10] ... pre-train the backbone ViT from scratch" [PAGE 9] — three inconsistent provenance statements plus an "initialized by [22]" vs "from scratch" contradiction. (b) Prose/table arithmetic mismatch: [PAGE 5] claims the model "surpasses the backbone network by 6.2% and 6.9% on SR and PR," but Table I gives 61.3−53.4 = 7.9 (SR), not 6.2; it claims it "exceeds ... ViPT by 0.4% and 0.6% ... on SR and PR," but 61.3−59.2 = 2.1 (SR), not 0.4. The PR figures (6.9, 0.6) do check out, so the SR figures appear mis-stated. (c) Causal under-identification: Table III shows the frozen-header model at 61.3/76.4 (VisEvent SR/PR) but unfreezing the header drops it to 59.0/74.2, i.e., the gain is contingent on prompt-tuning a frozen backbone — consistent with the gains being driven substantially by parameter-efficient adaptation rather than by "disentanglement" per se. (d) The experts' independence is never constrained (no orthogonality/statistics term), so "disentanglement" is nominal: it is supervised attribute prediction + soft gating, not ICA/VAE-style factorization. (e) $K=4$ is an annotation-budget ceiling, not a searched optimum: "the number of experts is unfeasible to extend over four due to the limitation of manual annotations" [PAGE 6].

Hop 5 (synthesis). The empirical gains are consistent and non-trivial across VisEvent, COESOT, and FELT-SOT (Table VII) and survive the expert-count and insertion-interval ablations. But the paper's causal story — that manually annotated video-level attribute labels are what drive robustness — is only partially identified: the same data are equally consistent with a multi-task-regularization account in which the attribute head merely shapes the router. The manual-annotation dependency is the load-bearing weak point, self-admitted as "highly dependent on the manual video-level attribute annotations ... thus putting restrictions on the generalization of the model" [PAGE 6].

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Backbone factorization [PAGE 2]: $F_{RGB-E}=F_E\circ F_D$, with $F_E:\{I_{RGB},I_E,B_0\}\to T_{RGB-E}$ and $F_D:T_{RGB-E}\to B$; 12 ViT encoder layers, each with MSA, LayerNorm, FFN, residuals. Input tokens $T^0_{RGB-E}=[T^z_{RGB},T^x_{RGB},T^z_E,T^x_E]$.

Layer injection (Eq. 1) [PAGE 3]:
$$T^l = T^l_{RGB-E} + P^{l+1},\quad l=1,\dots,L.$$
Units: $T^l\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ (dimensionless activation vectors; $N$ tokens, $D$ channels).

Assembled feature [PAGE 3]:
$$F^l_{assemble}=\sum_{t=1}^{K} W^{l,t} H^l_t,\quad W^{l,t}\in[0,1],\; H^l_t\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}.$$
Critical structural note: the router uses Sigmoid (not Softmax), so $\{W^{l,t}\}$ are $K$ independent gates in $[0,1]$ that do NOT sum to 1. Therefore $F^l_{assemble}$ is not a convex combination; its magnitude scales with the number of high-scoring experts. This contradicts the prose "The learnable score indicates the ratio of different challenging types in the corresponding scenario" [PAGE 3] — a ratio would require $\sum_t W^{l,t}=1$.

Optimization (Eq. 2) [PAGE 4]:
$$\theta=\arg\min\frac{1}{|D|}\sum \mathcal{L}\big(CRM(F_D(T^L_{RGB-E})),B_{gt}\big),$$
with $\theta$ "only existing in eMoE and CRM" [PAGE 4] (backbone frozen, "parameters are not updated" [PAGE 3]).

Tracking loss (Eq. 3) [PAGE 4]: $\mathcal{L}_{tracking}=\mathcal{L}_{cls}+\lambda_{iou}\mathcal{L}_{iou}+\lambda_{L1}\mathcal{L}_1$ (focal + GIoU + L1).

Contrastive loss (Eq. 4) [PAGE 4], with $s_i=\mathrm{sim}(T^z_{fused},t^{x,i}_{fused})/\tau$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{NCE}=-\log\!\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{pos}} e^{s_k^p}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{pos}} e^{s_k^p}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{neg}} e^{s_k^n}}\right).$$
Units: nats (natural-log cross-entropy over a multi-positive softmax). $\tau$ is a dimensionless temperature; $\mathrm{sim}(\cdot)$ is unspecified (cosine vs dot-product not stated) — a reproducibility gap.

Attribute loss (Eq. 5) [PAGE 4]: $\mathcal{L}_{attr}=\sum_{t,l}\lVert W^{l,t}-G_t\rVert_{l1}$, L1 distance (dimensionless) between per-layer scores and video-level binary labels $G=[G_1,\dots,G_K]$, e.g., "$[1,1,0,0]$" [PAGE 9].

Total (Eq. 6) [PAGE 4]: $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{tracking}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{NCE}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{attr}$; $\alpha,\beta$ are not reported.

Singularity/breakdown regimes. (i) Router saturation: if all $W^{l,t}\to1$, then $F^l_{assemble}\approx\sum_t H^l_t$ with magnitude $\sim K\times$ a single expert; injected additively (Eq. 1) this can inflate residual scale. If all $W^{l,t}\to0$, eMoE degenerates to the backbone (recovering the Table III "Backbone" row). (ii) NCE saturation: as $\tau\to0^+$ the softmax hardens to an argmax and gradients vanish except for the single largest logit; as $\tau\to\infty$ all logits equalize and $\mathcal{L}_{NCE}\to\log 2$ (for one dominant positive), erasing discrimination. (iii) Degenerate pairs: if positives/negatives are empty, the log argument $\to1$ and $\mathcal{L}_{NCE}\to0$, providing no signal.

Reported magnitudes (for grounding, not derivation): VisEvent SR/PR/NPR = 61.3/76.4/79.6 (Table I); COESOT = 67.1/79.9/82.3 (Table II); FELT-SOT = 65.1/72.9/73.6 (Table VII); 8.42 MB trainable (Table VI); 46 FPS (Table VIII); 1×A800, batch 64, 60 epochs, $6\times10^4$ pairs/epoch, AdamW, wd $10^{-4}$, lr $2\times10^{-4}$ halved once at epoch 32 [PAGE 5].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage of the operator stack. The backbone descends from ViT ("An image is worth 16x16 words" [PAGE 7, ref 11]) → one-stream trackers MixFormer [20] and OSTrack [22] (the "early elimination module in the ViT encoder" [PAGE 2] that eMoE builds on). The MoE idea traces to conditional computation / sparsely-gated MoE; here it is repurposed as a dense, prompt-tuning router over environmental axes rather than token routing. Contrastive relation modeling inherits from InfoNCE / contrastive self-supervised learning (ref [32], [PAGE 4]). The RGB-E track lineage runs from Gehrig et al.'s event+frame feature tracking (EKLT, refs [25],[26]) → DashNet [27] → ESVM [28] → FENet [6]/AFNet [7] → CEUTrack [4] → ViPT [10], the last being the closest prompt-based predecessor. eMoE's stated delta over ViPT is explicit: prior methods "fail to consider the impact of complex environmental attributes on the tracking performance while only improving the cross-modal fusion" [PAGE 2].

Multi-week traps (where the framing can seduce a reader over a long evaluation). (1) Provenance drift: because the backbone is attributed to [4], [22], and [10] on different pages, a careful re-implementer who "follows the paper" will build three different systems — a multi-week integration trap. (2) The "disentanglement" naming invites expecting factorial/independent representations; auditing for an independence constraint (there is none) costs days and yields null. (3) The manual 4-digit annotation is described as ground truth for both a router signal and an evaluation split; conflating training-time supervision with test-time inputs is a classic leakage trap — one must confirm the router scores are generated from tokens at inference, not from labels. (4) The "score = ratio" language [PAGE 3] misleads anyone assuming a simplex; verifying the Sigmoid (non-normalized) behavior is essential before re-deriving $F^l_{assemble}$.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Adversary model: the "opponent" is the challenge distribution (illumination, blur, scale, occlusion) plus distribution shift between train and test.

Habit regression (default failure). Absent the attribute head, the frozen-backbone+prompt regime tends to regress toward the backbone's dominant cue — appearance matching on the RGB stream — because RGB carries the strongest gradient signal at 25 FPS. Table III quantifies this baseline: Backbone = 53.4/69.5 (VisEvent SR/PR). Under a covariate shift (e.g., night scenes where events dominate), a purely appearance-tuned adapter reverts to backbone behavior; the paper's per-attribute PR (63.6% occlusion, 77.9% illumination, 73.5% blur, 80.7% scale on COESOT [PAGE 6]) is the evidence that the eMoE head resists that regression, most weakly under occlusion (63.6%).

Counter-trap (the architecture's move). The eMoE router + CRM is a counter to habit regression: CRM "pull[s] the target information in search tokens near template feature tokens while push[ing] background information away" [PAGE 4], which explicitly re-weights toward target-vs-background rather than raw appearance. The frozen-header result is the tell: unfreezing the header drops VisEvent to 59.0/74.2 (Table III) — i.e., giving the model more freedom to "do what it wants" makes it regress, so the frozen backbone + constrained prompt is itself the counter-trap that prevents catastrophic drift.

The clock (temporal/schedule dynamics). Two clocks matter. (i) Sensor clock: RGB at ~25 FPS with events accumulated into "stacked event frames ... not ... raw event streams" [PAGE 5] — this quantization is where sub-frame motion information is lost, bounding the achievable blur robustness. (ii) Optimization clock: lr $2\times10^{-4}$ decayed by 10× at epoch 32 of 60 [PAGE 5]. The theory-of-mind prediction: the attribute router's calibration is schedule-sensitive; a shorter schedule (no epoch-32 decay) would likely under-train the router and shrink the eMoE-vs-backbone margin. Recursive check: if a reviewer "improves" the model by unfreezing the header (natural instinct), performance regresses — the correct counter-move is to keep the backbone frozen and instead widen $K$ or insertion density (Table V shows interval = 1 is best), which the annotation budget forbids beyond $K=4$.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Cross-modal (RGB↔event) physical veto. The complementarity thesis is physically well-founded but bounded. Event cameras (DAVIS346 [PAGE 9]) respond to log-intensity contrast with microsecond latency and high dynamic range, so they physically veto the RGB failure modes under (a) motion blur — blur length $\approx$ (angular velocity)×(exposure time) is large for RGB but the event latency keeps edges sharp; and (b) extreme illumination — event contrast sensitivity persists where RGB saturates or crushes. This is why the "illumination variance" and "motion blur" experts plausibly help.

Veto limits (where physics overrides the model). (i) Photon starvation: in low illumination, event rate $\propto$ contrast flux drops; below the sensor's contrast threshold the event stream is near-empty and no expert can reconstruct target appearance — a hard physical veto the architecture cannot beat. (ii) Over-exposure saturation: bright scenes can flood the event pixels, again nulling the event channel. (iii) Spatial resolution: DAVIS346 is 346×260; stacked event frames cannot recover sub-pixel detail lost at this resolution, capping scale-variance precision. (iv) Stacking quantization: "stacked event frames ... not ... raw event streams" [PAGE 5] discards intra-frame microsecond timing, so the theoretical event advantage over blur is partially forfeited by the representation.

"Biomechanical" (target/camera kinematic) veto. Target motion (fast translation, aspect-ratio change) and ego-motion (camera motion) both appear in the 17-attribute set [PAGE 11–12]. A tracker without motion compensation will fail when ego-motion dominates; the paper reports aggregate attribute PR but does not isolate ego-motion vs object-motion, so a kinematic veto (fast + camera motion co-occurring) is plausible and untested — the reported per-attribute numbers cannot confirm robustness there.

Weather/environmental veto proof. Claim: the four chosen attributes are "sufficient to reflect environmental effects" [PAGE 2]. Proof status: partially supported. The COESOT attribute PR (63.6/77.9/73.5/80.7) shows monotonic degradation ordering (occlusion hardest), consistent with physical difficulty. But "sufficient" is not proven because $K=4$ is annotation-limited, not exhaustively validated against the full 17-attribute set at the mechanism level (Fig. 11–12 show curves, not a causal test). Adjudication: no physical law is violated by the method; the gains are physically admissible; but two regimes (photon-starved low light, saturation) constitute hard vetoes where the event modality collapses and the RGB-E premise fails regardless of eMoE.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactuals with concrete, falsifiable predictions (reward = SR/PR on VisEvent/COESOT). These test the paper's causal claim that manual attribute supervision drives robustness.

Veto 1 — Label randomization (tests causal role of attribute supervision). Intervention: keep architecture identical, replace $G_t$ in Eq. 5 with shuffled/random 4-digit labels. If "disentanglement" is causal, $\mathcal{L}_{attr}$ becomes noise and performance should fall toward the eMoE-only-without-CRM level. Prediction: VisEvent SR drops from 61.3 toward ~55–57 (i.e., losing roughly half the eMoE margin), PR toward ~71–73; if instead performance holds near 61.3/76.4, the attribute head is a mere regularizer and the paper's central claim is vetoed. Verifiable by re-running Table III's model ② with corrupted labels.

Veto 2 — Parameter-matched single expert (tests specialization vs capacity). Intervention: replace $K=4$ experts with $K=1$ expert of 4× width (equal FLOPs/params to the $K=4$ block). If specialization is real, $K=1$ underperforms the paper's $K=1$ narrow result (54.2/70.8 VisEvent, Table IV). Prediction: wide-$K{=}1$ lands ~56–58 SR — above narrow-$K{=}1$ but below $K{=}4$ (61.3). If wide-$K{=}1$ matches $K{=}4$, the gain is capacity not specialization, vetoing the disentanglement narrative. Verifiable by a width-controlled ablation.

Veto 3 — Attribute-free deployment / router collapse (tests generalization, the self-admitted weakness). Intervention: evaluate on sequences whose attribute distribution is disjoint from training (e.g., train on illumination+blur only, test on occlusion+scale), with no matching manual labels. Prediction: since scores are "manually annotate[d] ... at video level" [PAGE 2] and "unfeasible to extend over four" [PAGE 6], the Sigmoid router will be mis-calibrated for unseen axes; expect VisEvent-style SR to fall below the in-distribution 61.3, plausibly to ~56–59, and inference FPS (46, Table VIII) unchanged (compute is fixed) — isolating that the drop is calibration, not compute. If the model instead holds ≥61.3 out-of-distribution, the "restrictions on the generalization of the model" [PAGE 6] claim is itself vetoed.

All three are directly runnable against the released backbone (OSTrack/ViPT ViT), the frozen-backbone protocol, and the VisEvent/COESOT splits, making them verifiable RLVR-style checks whose reward (SR/PR deltas) cleanly confirms or vetoes the paper's causal attribution.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
