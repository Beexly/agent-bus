Multi-week trap: because this defense is formation-conditional, an offense that films it across weeks can diagnose the tell. Week 1, the defense plays base against a standard run formation. Week 3, the offense shows a kick or open formation early to force the defense to deepen per [PAGE 60], then executes a quick inside run against a now-thin line. Week 6, the offense uses play-action: the supporting halfback, habitually regressing toward the line to support the run, is sucked forward and vacates the intermediate zone. The retrocausal element is that later defensive schemes (1930s–1940s) solve the 1921 ambiguity by splitting the halfback role into distinct linebacker and defensive-back specialties; a defense that naively regresses to Daly's 1921 undifferentiated halfback structure re-opens the same multi-week trap—predictable depth rules that an offensive coordinator can script against. The lineage is therefore not merely historical but strategic: every later "hybrid" defender (nickelback, dollar back) is a technical patch on the exact underspecification visible in Daly's text. If the defense fails to deepen because of habit inertia, the offense attacks either deep over the withdrawn halfbacks or with a quick kick behind a line that has not adjusted. Clock interaction: the play clock imposes a hard deadline for formation diagnosis. If the offense hurries, the defensive halfbacks cannot resolve the competing demands of [PAGE 60]—"as close to the line ... as they can get" versus "still feel sure of being able to cover a forward-pass receiver"—before the snap. The halfback is then caught in a recursive belief state: "If I creep up, they pass; if I stay back, they run; if they think I think that, they run a draw." This is a level-k reasoning problem with a finite clock horizon. The eleventh man deep at kicker range adds another recursion: if the offense is out of field-goal range, the deep man is wasted depth; if the offense trails and must pass, the deep man must cover longer routes, but the text keys his depth to the kicker, not to the passing game. Under clock pressure, that mismatch becomes exploitable. [PAGE 60]
