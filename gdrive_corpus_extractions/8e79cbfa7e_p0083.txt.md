(3) The static-deficit trap. Over weeks of recovery from hemianopsia, patients often develop compensatory scanning strategies using intact brainstem reflex circuits. If a clinician accepts James’s framing that the “there” is permanently “cut out of his mind” [PAGE 73], they may underestimate plastic reweighting of subcortical orienting and fail to train scanning behavior that leverages preserved reflex arcs.

The retrocausal twist is that later discoveries reach backward to validate half of James’s ontology while falsifying the other half: the corpora quadrigemina are indeed organs for reflex movement of eye-muscles, but they are not categorically “not for vision”—they are not for conscious sight, yet they carry visual signals that guide action without the indexical “there” James describes as lost [PAGE 73]. The historical trap, therefore, is treating a 1890 phenomenological description as an ontological boundary condition rather than a clinical observation bounded by the measurement technology of its week.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates on both sides of the clinical encounter. The patient with acute occipital damage regresses to a pre-reflective bodily habit: “something wrong” with the eyes [PAGE 73], lacking the categorical resources to localize the complaint. The clinician regresses to a localizationist habit: occipital lesion equals cortical blindness, sensorial not ideational [PAGE 73]. Under crisis, both parties abandon the fine-grained distinction James himself draws between sensation, ideation, and conduction.

The counter-trap is recursive theory-of-mind failure. Let M1 be the patient’s first-order model of the visual world, containing representations S_t(r) for each location r at time t. Let M2 be the second-order model of M1, i.e., the patient’s theory of their own visual mind. In intact vision, the patient can compare M1_t(r) against M1_{t-1}(r) and form the metarepresentation “I see light at r” or “I no longer see at r.” After occipital destruction over H, M1_t(r) = ∅ for all t and all r ∈ H; the ideational trace I(r,t) is simultaneously destroyed by conduction failure [PAGE 73]. Consequently M2 cannot evaluate the proposition “I am blind in H,” because the indexical “there” has been deleted from the self-model [PAGE 73]. The mind cannot mind its own missing mind-content: the recursive loop M2 → M1(r) → “absence at r” terminates at null.

Clock structure: awareness of a continuous loss requires a temporal reference frame against which a previously present stream is compared. Formally, intact vision supports ΔM(r) = M_t(r) − M_{t-1}(r) ≠ 0 as a change-detection tick. In cortical hemianopsia, S_t(r) = ∅ and M_t(r) = ∅ for all t, so ΔM(r) is undefined, not negative. There is no clock tick marking the moment light vanished from H, because the coordinate H itself is no longer in the domain of the patient’s subjective present. The patient therefore reports a global unease rather than a dated, localized scotoma, which is exactly why “the defect ... has to be revealed to them by the doctor” [PAGE 73].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers must concur before James’s conclusion is accepted; any one layer can veto the strong claim.

Physical layer (ocular and retinal integrity): The veto test is whether the eyes themselves remain sensitive. James contrasts ocular blindness (“sees darkness”) with cortical blindness (“cannot … see darkness … than he can see it out of the skin of his back”) [PAGE 73]. If pupillary light reflex and retinal response are intact while conscious report is null in H, the deficit is not at the physical photoreceptor layer; it is centrally gated. This supports James’s “sensorial blindness” only if “sensorial” is defined as cortex-dependent conscious sensation, not retinal transduction.

Biomechanical layer (extraocular reflex): James assigns the corpora quadrigemina and lower optical ganglia to “reflex movement of eye-muscles and not for conscious sight” [PAGE 73]. The veto test is preservation of saccadic and pursuit reflexes despite cortical blindness. If eye movements toward stimuli in the blind field remain intact, the biomechanical layer is confirmed as visually responsive but nonconscious. This layer does not veto James’s reflex claim; however, if modern tracing shows the superior colliculus contributes spatial visual signals to orienting, James’s claim is incomplete rather than false: the ganglia move the eyes reflexively using visual input they do not consciously see.

Weather / environmental layer (luminance invariance): Vary illumination L over the scotoma H (fog, brightness, contrast). James’s phenomenology predicts invariance: the cortical blind cannot see darkness in H “out of the parts of his retina which are connected with the brainlesion” [PAGE 73], because the very notion of “there” is cut out [PAGE 73]. If varying environmental luminance changes detection in H, the deficit is sensorial/adaptation-like and James’s cortical exclusivity is vetoed. If report remains null across all L while the intact field reports normally, the environmental layer supports a central, ideational failure rather than a peripheral light-adaptation failure.

Adjudication rule: James’s diagnosis holds only if physical integrity coexists with biomechanical reflex preservation and environmental-report invariance. Any layer demonstrating residual visual behavior in H (e.g., luminance-dependent detection without conscious report) vetoes the strong claim “cannot see at all” [PAGE 73] while preserving the weaker phenomenological claim that the conscious “there” is lost.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios (RLVR = verifiable reward/condition structure), each with a concrete prediction.

Scenario 1 — Forced-choice blindsight under luminance variation. Patient with complete right occipital destruction and right hemianopsia [PAGE 73]. Present stimuli in the blind right hemifield across a randomized luminance series L ∈ {low, medium, high}. Require forced-choice detection (present/absent) plus confidence rating on a 0–1 scale. Veto condition: if detection accuracy exceeds chance (50% binomial, p < 0.01) while confidence remains at floor and the patient verbally denies any light or darkness in the field, James’s assertion that such patients “cannot see at all in that part of the field” [PAGE 73] is behaviorally falsified. Prediction: above-chance detection with zero confidence, preserving James’s phenomenological claim about the missing “there” [PAGE 73] but falsifying his strong behavioral claim.

Scenario 2 — Conduction lesion with spared cortex vs. cortical destruction. Group A: occipital cortical destruction as in James’s cases [PAGE 73]. Group B: optic-radiation/geniculostriate disconnection with V1 histologically spared. Both groups are tested for hemianopsic unawareness using the same report protocol. Veto condition: if Group B shows identical loss of visual awareness and inability to miss the hemifield (“what you have no idea of you cannot miss” [PAGE 73]), then James’s explanation via “disturbances of conduction between the optical and other centres” [PAGE 73, citing p. 50] is strengthened over a strict local occipital ideation-centre claim. Prediction: conduction lesions produce the same phenomenological null report as cortical lesions, indicating network disconnection as the operative singularity.

Scenario 3 — Focal intracortical microstimulation at the lesion edge. In a behaviorally hemianopic patient, deliver focal electrical microstimulation to preserved peri-lesional visual cortex corresponding to the scotoma H. Veto condition: if stimulation evokes localized phosphenes reported within H, then conscious visual content can be instantiated outside the destroyed sector, vetoing strict local necessity of the exact destroyed cortex while preserving network necessity. Prediction: phosphenes occur only when stimulation overlaps preserved retinotopic columns adjacent to H; stimulation of destroyed tissue yields no report, establishing a sharp singularity boundary at the lesion edge consistent with James’s rank-defective map but falsifying absolute exclusion of nearby intact cortex.
