**§2. Transition axioms** [PAGE 15, Definition 2.1]
$$\text{(i) } \vartheta(0,z) = z; \quad \text{(ii) } \|\vartheta\|_A := \sup_{h \ne k} \frac{d(\vartheta(h,z),\vartheta(k,z))}{|h-k|} < \infty;$$
$$\text{(iii) } \|\vartheta\|_A := \sup_{h \in [0,1],\, x \ne y} \frac{d(\vartheta(h,x),\vartheta(h,y))}{d(x,y)} < \infty; \quad \text{(iv) } \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{d(\vartheta(t+h,z), \vartheta(h,\vartheta(t,z)))}{h} = 0.$$
*Units:* $\|\vartheta\|_A$ is dimensionless (ratio of distances, ratio of time intervals).
*Breakdown:* A transition with $\|\vartheta\|_A = +\infty$ is *not* a transition; (iv) fails for non-semigroup flows.

**§3. Mutation at a point (mutable map)** [PAGE 21]
$$\tau \in \hat f(z)\vartheta \iff \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{d\bigl(f(\vartheta(h,z)),\, \tau(h,f(z))\bigr)}{h} = 0.$$
*Units:* velocity (distance/time) when $h$ is time; distance/dimensionless-$h$ otherwise.
*Breakdown:* mutation set is empty if $f$ is not metrically differentiable in direction $\vartheta$; the equivalence class $\tau \sim_{f(z)} \tau'$ becomes non-singleton whenever the limit fails to isolate a unique class.

**§4. Mutation of a tube** [PAGE 6–7, property (1.1)]
$$\dot p(t) := \Bigl\{\, \varphi \in \mathrm{Lip}(E,E) : \lim_{h\to 0^+} \tfrac{d\bigl(d_\varphi(h,P(t)), P(t+h)\bigr)}{h} = 0 \,\Bigr\}$$
with $d_\varphi(h,P) := \{\vartheta_\varphi(h,x) : x \in P\}$. The equation is $\forall t,\, \dot p(t) \ni f(t, P(t); \cdot)$ [PAGE 7].
*Breakdown:* The set $\dot p(t)$ may be empty when the tube has corners — this is the failure mode the framework flags on [PAGE 7]: "the choice between potential confusion and ponderousness."

**§5. Steiner shape map** [PAGE 10–11]
$$\mathrm{sn}(K) := \int_{\Sigma^{n-1}} p\, \sigma(K,p)\, dw(p), \quad w(\Sigma^{n-1}) = 1,$$
with linearity $\mathrm{sn}(\lambda K + \mu L) = \lambda \mathrm{sn}(K) + \mu \mathrm{sn}(L)$ and Lipschitz bound $\|\mathrm{sn}(K) - \mathrm{sn}(L)\| \le \mathrm{Lip}(\mathrm{sn})\, d(K,L)$. Under the Steiner mutational equation $\dot p(t) \ni g(\mathrm{sn}(P(t)))$, one gets [PAGE 11] the *exact reduction* $\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathrm{sn}(P(t)) = g(\mathrm{sn}(P(t)))$.
*Breakdown:* The identity holds only when the mutation projects linearly onto $Y$ via (1.3); it collapses if $g$ is required to depend on $P \setminus \mathrm{sn}(P)$.

**§6. Nagumo viability domain** [PAGE 27, Theorem 4.2]
$$\forall t \ge 0,\ \forall z \in \mathcal{M} : \quad f(t,z) \in T_{\mathcal{M}}(z) \;\Longrightarrow\; \exists \text{ viable solution from any } z_0 \in \mathcal{M}.$$
*Breakdown:* violations of (a) compactness of closed bounded balls, (b) uniform boundedness $\sup \|f(t,z;\cdot)\| \le c$, (c) closedness of $\mathcal{M}$ kill Ascoli and the Zorn extension.

**§7. Cauchy-Lipschitz estimate** [PAGE 33, Theorem 4.5]
$$d(z(t), y(t)) \le e^{(M+\|f\|_A)t} d(z_0, y_0) + \int_0^t e^{(M+\|f\|_A)(t-s)}\, d\bigl(f(y(s)), \dot y(s)\bigr)\, ds,$$
$$M := \max\bigl(0, \sup_{z \in X} \|f(z)\|_A - 1\bigr), \qquad \|f\|_A := \sup_{z \ne z'} \frac{d(f(z;\cdot),f(z';\cdot))}{d(z,z')}.$$
*Units:* $f$ has mutation-velocity units; $M$ dimensionless; exponential argument $(M + \|f\|_A)t$ dimensionless.
*Breakdown:* $M = +\infty$ when $\sup \|\cdot\|_A$ is unbounded; inequality becomes vacuous.

**§8. Invariant manifold equation** [PAGE 39, Theorem 5.1]
$$A u(z) \in \hat D u(z)(\varphi(z)) - \psi(z), \qquad \lambda := \inf_{z \ne 0} \frac{\langle A z, z\rangle}{\|z\|^2}.$$
The explicit solution [PAGE 40–41] is $u(z) = -\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t} \psi(z(t))\, dt$ with $z(\cdot)$ the unique solution of $\dot z(t) \ni \varphi(z(t))$ starting at $z$.
*Units:* $\lambda$ has units of $1/h$ (inverse time) so that $e^{-\lambda t}$ is dimensionless; $\psi$ carries $Y$-units; $A$ has $1/h$ units.
*Breakdown:* at $\lambda \le 0$ the integral diverges for uniformly-positive $\psi$; the uniqueness requires $\lambda > \max(c, 4\|f\|_A\|g\|_A + M)$ with $\lambda \rho(\lambda) < 1$ [PAGE 43–44].

**§9. Contingent epimutation (Lyapunov)** [PAGE 45]
$$\hat D_\uparrow V(z)(\vartheta) := \sup_{\varepsilon>0} \inf_{h \in ]0,\varepsilon],\, y \in B(\vartheta(h,z), \varepsilon h)} \frac{V(y)-V(z)}{h}.$$
The Lyapunov property [PAGE 44] is $V(z(t)) \le w(t)$ with $w' = -\varphi(w)$, i.e. $\hat D_\uparrow V(z)(f(z)) \le -\varphi(V(z))$.
*Breakdown:* if $\hat D_\uparrow V(z)(\vartheta) = -\infty$ at any $(z,\vartheta)$ — the cliff called *non-epimutability* — the equivalence between viability in $\mathrm{Epi}(V)$ and Lyapunov decay fails.

**§10. Dissipative diagnostic** [PAGE 49]
$$W(K) := \int_K h(x)\, dx \;\Rightarrow\; W \text{ is Lyapunov of } \dot K \ni f \iff \nabla\cdot(h\, f) + \partial h \le 0 \text{ on } X.$$
*Units:* $h$ carries density units, $f$ velocity, so $\nabla \cdot (hf)$ has dimensions of density/time; $\partial h$ (rate of change in explicit time dependence) carries the same.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Three genealogical strands converge on this paper and each has its own multi-week trap.

*Strand A — Contingent cones (Nagumo/Bouligand/Ważewski).* Bouligand coined "contingent" in the 1930s; Nagumo proved the invariance theorem in [PAGE 53, ref. 51, 1942]; Ważewski extended differential calculus to metric spaces in [PAGE 54–55, refs. 71–72, 1946–1951] via *allongements contingentiels*. The paper explicitly credits this lineage [PAGE 13–14]: "As early as 1946, T. Ważewski introduced ... the concept of allongements contingentiels sup'erieurs et inf'erieurs". Trap: restoring the contingent cone with the linear-structure difference quotient and forgetting that on $\mathcal{K}(E)$ there is no half-line — a contrafactual derivation that writes $T_K(P) = \overline{\bigcup_{h>0}(K-P)/h}$ silently reintroduces a linear structure that the source method rejects [PAGE 14].

*Strand B — Shape derivatives (Céa/Delfour–Zolésio).* [PAGE 51, refs. 16, 19–22] and [PAGE 13] explicitly identified the "shape derivative" as the prototype: differences $U(x+hv) - U(x)$ are replaced by $V(\vartheta_\varphi(h,K)) - V(K)$ in [PAGE 5]. Trap: reading this as ordinary PDE shape calculus — the shape-gradient $f'(K)$ is only a *normal* distribution [PAGE 25]: "the support of a vector distribution normal to $K$ is contained in the boundary $\partial K$", a fact verified via `$f(\vartheta_\varphi(h,K)) = f(K)$` for $\varphi \in \mathrm{Lip}_0(K,E)$. Misreading it as a full derivative yields spurious bulk terms.

*Strand C — Viability and funnel equations.* [PAGE 5], [PAGE 52, refs. 37–42], [PAGE 53, refs. 52–59]. Kurzhanski–Filippova, Kurzhanski–Valyi, Panasyuk built the "set-valued dynamics" of attainable sets — but the paper explicitly distinguishes itself [PAGE 5]: mutational equations of the form $\dot p(t) \ni f(t,P(t);\cdot)$ "do not fall in these formulations (see Steiner mutational equations below for an example)." Trap: conflating the funnel equation (reachable-set ODE) with the mutational equation (a chosen Lipschitz-velocity law). The Steiner reduction [PAGE 11] $\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathrm{sn}(P(t)) = g(\mathrm{sn}(P(t)))$ is a valid bridge only in the exactly-linear case.

*Strand D — Mathematical morphology (Matheron/Mattioli–Schmitt).* [PAGE 4, 21, 53 refs. 44–45]. Morphological transitions use *set-valued* $\Phi$ and yield dilatations with structuring element $B$ [PAGE 21]. Trap: replacing $\Phi$ by a single $\varphi$ anywhere in the Cauchy-Lipschitz application — the Filippov extension [PAGE 19, footnote 14] is mandatory once $\Phi$ is genuinely multi-valued.

*Downstream of the paper.* The mutational framework was subsequently developed into Aubin's 1997 *Mutational and Morphological Analysis* and Doyen's work on visual servoing; the retrosynthetic signature appears in every later use of "velocity cone" $T_{\mathcal{K}(M)}(K)$ [PAGE 27].

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

*Self-model as an invariant manifold.* The system's "model of itself" is exactly the invariant-manifold map $u : X \to Y$ of Theorem 5.2 [PAGE 39]. A coalition dynamics $P(\cdot)$ is *trackable* through $u$ iff $u(P(t))$ obeys the auxiliary ODE $\dot y = A y + g(P, y)$; the existence of $u$ is proved by a Banach fixed-point theorem in $C(X,Y)$ [PAGE 43–44]. This is literally a meta-model — the low-dimensional observable is a recursive representation of the high-dimensional trajectory.

*Habit regression under crisis.* When the spectral gap collapses ($\lambda \downarrow 0$) or the contraction fails ($\lambda \le 4\|f\|_A\|g\|_A + M$), the existence proof for $u$ dies; the system loses its self-consistent summary. Behaviourally, the system reverts to the *raw* mutational equation $\dot p(t) \ni f(t,P(t);\cdot)$ [PAGE 7] without any $u$-layer — no compression, no forecasting, only brute viability. Read against Theorem 6.3, this is exactly the regime where the epimutation $\hat D_\uparrow V$ no longer admits a finite bound and the Lyapunov certificate is absent [PAGE 44–47]. The Russel–Norvig style "cue-driven habit" is, formally, the regime $M > 0$ with no bounded observable.

*Counter-trap (rescuing recursion).* Two rescues are provided and both preserve self-modelling. First, replace the single-valued $\varphi$ by a set-valued $\Phi$ and use morphological transitions $\vartheta_\Phi$ [PAGE 19–20] — the Filippov lemma guarantees $\|\vartheta_\Phi\|_A \le e^{\|\Phi\|_A}$ so the contraction index is preserved. Second, replace the observable $u$ by an *attractor distance* $d_K(\cdot)$ and use Corollary 6.5 [PAGE 48]: if $K$ is invariant and $D_\uparrow d_K \le -\varphi \circ d_K$, the system still has a compression summary without needing a smooth $u$.

*The clock.* The transition semigroup is the honest "clock." On [PAGE 23–24] the paper introduces the semi-group $U_\vartheta(h)f := f \circ \vartheta(h,\cdot)$ on $\mathcal{F}(X,F)$ with infinitesimal generator $L_\vartheta f := \lim_{h\to 0^+} (U_\vartheta(h)f - f)/h$ — this is the Lie derivative along $\vartheta$. The equivalence $\vartheta \sim_z \vartheta'$ [PAGE 15] is *the* reason the clock is well-defined up to first order: two transitions that agree to order $h$ give the same generator. Any recursive self-model must be $L_\vartheta$-computable; anything depending on second-order clock structure ($h^2$ terms) is invisible to the mutational layer.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Each theorem in the paper has an *implicit veto layer* that can de-certify a claimed solution even when the algebraic identity is satisfied. Enumerating them is the cross-layer audit.

*Veto V1 — Compactness (Ascoli).* Theorem 4.2 uses Ascoli's theorem, stated verbatim on [PAGE 31]: "Ascoli's Theorem implies that a subsequence ... converges uniformly". This *requires* that closed bounded balls of $X$ be compact [PAGE 27–28]. In $\mathcal{K}(E)$ with $E$ finite-dim this holds; in any infinite-dim $\mathcal{K}(E)$ it does not. So *biomechanical veto*: any viability certificate for a PDE-shaped coalition space with infinite-dim ambient is invalid unless compactness is re-proved with a specific compact-embedding argument (e.g. Marle-type). Prediction: a claimed viable trajectory in $L^2$ subsets under strong convergence can be constructed as a counterexample by taking a wildly oscillating sequence whose Hausdorff diameter is bounded but whose $L^2$ energy escapes.

*Veto V2 — Boundedness cliff.* The definition of $M$ [PAGE 33] sets $M = \max(0,\sup\|f\|_A - 1)$; if $\sup\inf$ is $+\infty$, the Cauchy-Lipschitz inequality is trivially true but useless, and the invariant-manifold contraction bound $\lambda > 4\|f\|_A\|g\|_A + M$ becomes vacuous. A coalition dynamic with bursts of near-discontinuous transition ($\|\cdot\|_A \to \infty$) will present as no-theorem-holds. Prediction: any published tracking result in a "flash crisis" regime must implicitly assume bounded mutation norm; exposing the bound should be a prerequisite.

*Veto V3 — Spectral sign ($\lambda \le 0$).* In Theorem 5.1 the representation $u(z) = -\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}\psi(z(t))\,dt$ [PAGE 40] diverges for uniformly positive $\psi$ at $\lambda \le 0$. The class of systems with $\lambda \le 0$ ("no contraction direction") literally has no bounded tracking observable in this framework; it has a morphological-transition substitute (Corollary 6.5, [PAGE 48]) but no smooth $u$. Prediction: any report of a bounded smooth $u$ for a system with non-positive $\lambda$ must involve a hidden sign change in $A$ (which is the same as redefining $A$).

*Veto V4 — Epimutability ($\hat D_\uparrow V = -\infty$).* Definition on [PAGE 45]: $V$ must never achieve marginal infimum $-\infty$. This bites on functions with fractal or wildly discontinuous epigraphs. Prediction: any Lyapunov certificate claimed for a distance-to-set-like function on a space with measure-zero density must be checked to have *finite* lower epimutation everywhere; the discontinuity locus of $V$ is the exact veto surface.

*Veto V5 — Structured-cone mismatch.* On [PAGE 18], the admissible transition space is $\mathrm{Lip}_0(M,E) := \{\varphi \in \mathrm{Lip}(E,E) : \varphi(z) \in T_M(z) \cap (-T_M(z)) \,\forall z\}$. A coalition evolving under a $\varphi$ that is *not* in $\mathrm{Lip}_0(M,E)$ cannot be Nagumo-viable in $M$. Prediction: any published "viable coalition evolution" in constraint set $M$ that leaves $M$ for a single instant falsifies the claimed viability certificate directly — this is the "biomechanical" cross-check: constraint violation is a hard veto, not a soft penalty.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto Scenario 1 — Null-transition degradation.**
*Setup:* Take $f(z) \equiv \mathbb{1}$ (the identity transition $\mathbb{1}(h,z) = z$, [PAGE 16]). Then $\|f\|_A = 0$, and $\|f(z_0; z_0)\|_A = 0$ because $f(z_0)(h, \cdot)$ is constant in $h$.
*Test:* The Cauchy-Lipschitz inequality [PAGE 33] yields $d(z(t), z_0) \le e^{0\cdot t}d(z_0,z_0) + 0 = 0$. Hence $z(t) \equiv z_0$.
*Prediction:* Any reported non-trivial coalition evolution $P(t) \ne P(0)$ under a dynamics with $\|f\|_A = 0$ and $M = 0$ is a *counterexample to Theorem 4.5 applied to that $f$, or evidence of a misapplied map.* This is verifiable by populating $f \equiv \mathbb{1}$ and checking that no $t$-dependent trajectory exists.

**Veto Scenario 2 — Metric-agnostic replication under cosmic convergence.**
*Setup:* Replace the Hausdorff metric on $\mathcal{K}(E)$ by the Rockafellar–Wets *cosmic convergence* on the family of closed convex subsets, as explicitly endorsed on [PAGE 13]: "one can use other metric subspaces of the power set $\mathcal{P}(E)$, such as the space of closed convex subsets supplied with the cosmic convergence introduced in [65,66, Rockafellar & Wets], or ... the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ of a probability space ... supplied with the distance [symmetric-difference total variation]."
*Test:* Read the proofs of Theorems 4.2 and 4.5 [PAGE 28–36] and verify that the only metric data invoked are (i) completeness, (ii) compactness of closed bounded balls, (iii) existence of an equivalence relation $\sim_z$, (iv) Lemma 2.2 [PAGE 16–17] composition estimate. The Hausdorff formula $\delta(K,L) = \sup_x \inf_y d(x,y)$ is *never* substituted into a proof step.
*Prediction:* Any purported counterexample to Theorem 4.2 or 4.5 that claims "this fails for cosmic convergence" must exhibit a property (i)–(iv) that fails in the cosmic-convergence space. If (i)–(iv) all pass, the alleged counterexample is invalid — the theorems transport verbatim. Verifiable by exhaustive proof-line inspection.

**Veto Scenario 3 — Sign-flip in the invariant-manifold integral.**
*Setup:* In Theorem 5.1 take $A = 0$ (so $\lambda = 0$) and $\psi \equiv \mathbf{1}_Y$ (constant unit). The integral [PAGE 40] becomes $u(z) = -\int_0^\infty 1 \cdot 1\, dt = -\infty$, divergent.
*Test:* Direct calculation: $\|u(z)\| \ge \int_0^\infty \|\psi(z(t))\| dt = +\infty$; hence no finite-valued $u$ satisfies $Au(z) \in \hat D u(z)(\varphi(z)) - \psi(z)$.
*Prediction:* Every reported bounded trade-off between a tracked observable and a non-positive $\lambda$ must contain a sign change in $A$, a decay in $\psi$ that was not disclosed in the theorem statement, or a misunderstanding of the integral representation. Verifiable by numerical quadrature: pick any $A$ with $\lambda \le 0$ and any bounded $\psi$ with positive lower bound, and confirm that $|u(z)|$ grows without bound at every $z$ where the trajectory is not itself in the stable fiber of $A$.
