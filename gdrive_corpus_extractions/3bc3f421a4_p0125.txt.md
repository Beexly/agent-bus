<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The apparatus on [PAGE 125] sits inside Galton's Natural Inheritance (1889), the text that coined and mechanized "regression to the mean." Its intellectual lineage runs from Quetelet's "average man" and social physics through Galton's anthropometric labs, to Pearson's correlation, Fisher's quantitative genetics, and ultimately to modern SNP-based polygenic scoring and genomic prediction. The 2:3 diameter ratio is a material ancestor of the regression coefficient β: instead of estimating β from data, Galton gears it into wood and brass, converting statistical tendency into kinematic constraint. The female-to-male equivalent scale [PAGE 125] is an early normalization layer, anticipating feature scaling in statistical models but hiding its parameters in a physical ruler rather than an estimated intercept.

Multi-week traps: In a forecasting setting operationalized over weeks, this device creates four recursive traps. (1) Ancestral anchoring: because the output is mechanically bounded by parental inputs through a fixed 2:3 gain, the operator learns to expect reversion even when the environment shifts (e.g., secular increases in stature from nutrition), causing systematic under-prediction of trends. (2) Hidden affine bias: the unspecified female-to-male transformation [PAGE 125] means that over weeks of use, a small calibration offset in the "male equivalent" ruler compounds into a steadily drifting forecast error that is indistinguishable from real change. (3) Mechanical determinism fallacy: the operator treats the gear ratio as a law of nature rather than a statistical convenience, over-weighting parental stature and under-weighting environmental variance. (4) Calibration drift: hygroscopic thread swelling, wear on grooves, and thermal expansion alter μ and effective radii over a multi-week horizon, so the same parental inputs produce different outputs without any change in the underlying statistical law. Each trap is a failure to separate the invariant statistical estimand (the conditional mean of child stature) from the contingent physical implementation (the gear train and thread friction).

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under uncertainty or time pressure, human analysts regress to Galtonian habits—anchoring on parental or historical means, expecting extremes to normalize, and trusting mechanical-looking averages. In the context of this apparatus, the crisis habit is to set M and F from the most recent parental measures and accept the wheel's output as if the 2:3 ratio were a causal law rather than a chosen analog.

Counter-trap: sophisticated observers who know the machine implements β = 2/3 may anticipate that others will under-react to extreme parental stature. They therefore over-correct in the opposite direction, betting (figuratively, in the sense of epistemic commitment) on momentum rather than reversion. This second-order inversion is the counter-trap: if the true population process is stationary regression, the anti-regression trader loses; if the process is non-stationary (secular trend), the anti-regression stance temporarily wins, reinforcing the habit until the regime shifts again.

Clock: the mechanical apparatus has a physical response time. Thread take-up, pulley settling, and frictional stick-slip define τ_mech, while the human decision loop defines τ_dec. If τ_mech ≫ τ_dec (sticky threads, high inertia of the compound wheel [PAGE 125]), the operator reads a stale or oscillating output and over-adjusts the weights, producing hunting instability. If τ_dec ≪ τ_mech, the operator ignores the lag and treats a transient as a settled prediction. The recursive theory of mind therefore nests three layers: the physical clock of the machine, the statistical regression habit of the analyst, and the social expectation that others are themselves regressing—creating a meta-stable loop where the safest forecast is the one that explicitly models the observer's own regression-to-the-mean bias.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto proof. The output of the apparatus is valid only if all of the following hold simultaneously, with any violation producing a hard veto (null output, not interpolation).

Veto P1 — Capstan slip: exp(μ φ) must exceed the maximum tension ratio across grooves A and B. Because φ ∈ [4π, 6π] rad [PAGE 125] and μ is unstated, the operator cannot verify this a priori. If T_M/T_F or T_A/T_B exceeds the capstan limit, the thread slides, θ becomes multivalued, and the stature mapping is undefined.

Veto P2 — Torque saturation: the counterpoise must satisfy |m_cp g r_C| ≥ |T_B r_B − T_A r_A|. Since d_C is "any convenient diameter" [PAGE 125] and m_cp is unspecified, extreme parental inputs can require counterpoise torque beyond the physical range. The wheel then hits a mechanical end-stop and the output clips at the boundary; the effective regression gain drops to zero beyond saturation.

Veto P3 — Static indeterminacy at D: if m_M ≠ m_F and pulley D is frictionless, no static θ exists [PAGE 125]. The apparatus either accelerates indefinitely or requires operator-imposed friction not described in the text. This is a singular configuration: the prediction operator is not defined.

Biomechanical veto. The operator reads the wheel position by eye. Parallax and jitter introduce δx_error in meters; because the child output traverses groove A with radius r_A = (2/3) r_B, a hand-position error δx maps to a stature error δS = (r_A/r_B) δx = (2/3) δx. If the scale's least count exceeds the resolution needed for the claimed inheritance precision, the biomechanical layer vetoes the numerical output.

Weather veto. Thread is hygroscopic and the wheels are metallic. Humidity changes thread length by ΔL = κ ΔRH; temperature changes groove radii by Δr = α r ΔT. Over a multi-week campaign, seasonal ΔRH and ΔT can shift the zero point by amounts comparable to the intergenerational signal Galton sought to measure. If |ΔL| or |Δr| exceeds the thread take-up reserve on grooves A and B (wrapped only 2–3 times [PAGE 125]), the calibration is invalidated.

Adjudication order. Physical vetoes (P1–P3) are absolute and override biomechanical and weather corrections. If any physical veto fires, the correct output is "no valid prediction," not a best-guess interpolation. Biomechanical and weather vetoes degrade confidence intervals but do not, by themselves, nullify the point estimate unless the induced error exceeds the instrument's stated resolution.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
