The counter-trap operates at the next recursive level: the opponent anticipates this regression and positions off-side or over the line to draw the infraction, thereby converting a physical mistake into a structured penalty election under Rule G or an automatic yardage setback under Rules H and I [PAGE 1].

Recursive nesting: Level 0 is the foul itself. Level 1 is the opponent's trap-setting to provoke the foul. Level 2 is the offending team's awareness that the trap exists, leading it to play conservatively and suppress the habitual regression. Level 3 is the opponent's double-bluff, intentionally retreating to induce over-correction, then springing the trap on the next snap. Clock bounds the recursion depth: in 1905 football, free-kicks, scrimmages, and place-kicks consume game time; as the clock winds down, the feasible recursion depth collapses because there are insufficient plays remaining to complete a Level 2 or Level 3 counter-trap [PAGE 1]. The Rule G election (15 yards scrimmage vs 5 yards kick) is itself a clock-possession tradeoff: the scrimmage option preserves possession and running time, while the kick option surrenders possession but gains a fixed, possibly discounted, yardage [PAGE 1]. Thus crisis habit regression is not random noise but a depth-limited recursive game whose solution is constrained by the game clock and the goal-line singularity where the penalty metric becomes non-Euclidean.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: player mass, momentum, and impact velocity determine whether a fair-catch receiver is thrown to the ground or a kicker advances beyond his mark [PAGE 1]. Biomechanical layer: tripping, charging before the snap, and off-side obstruction are locomotor violations independent of the ball's flight [PAGE 1]. Weather layer: mud, precipitation, and ball wetness alter kick distance and grip but do not suspend penalty enforcement [PAGE 1].

Veto proof conditions. A physical outcome is nullified (vetoed) when an infraction is observed and enforced: (1) a fair catch physically completed but preceded by unlawful off-side obstruction is voided; the receiving side elects 15 yards scrimmage or 5 yards kick [PAGE 1]; (2) a free-kick physically struck but preceded by the kicker advancing beyond his mark is voided; opponents line up 5 yards nearer and the kick is remade from behind the mark [PAGE 1]; (3) a kick put in play but charged before it is live is voided; opponents are set back 5 yards per offense and the kick is repeated [PAGE 1]. The veto is robust to weather because the rules condition enforcement on the infraction, not on kicking success or ball trajectory [PAGE 1]. Breakdown occurs if the referee fails to observe the foul (no veto despite physical violation), if OCR corruption shifts the Rule J threshold from 10 to another value (wrong compression regime), or if the second-offense note is ignored, allowing repeat goal-line charges to escape exacted penalties behind the goal line [PAGE 1].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 (Rule H [PAGE 1]): Kicker advances beyond his mark before kicking on a free-kick. Veto: opponents line up 5 yards nearer; kick is remade from behind the first mark. Verifiable signal: positional data shows the kicker's plant foot beyond the mark at contact. Prediction: the remade kick from the rearward mark with the opponent line 5 yards nearer has a lower completion/field-position success rate than an uninterrupted kick; the veto reduces free-kick efficiency by an increment proportional to the 5-yard field-position disadvantage.

Scenario 2 (Rule G [PAGE 1] + Rule J [PAGE 1]): Fair-catch receiver is unlawfully obstructed by an off-side opponent inside the receiving team's own 10-yard line. Veto: receiving side elects 15 yards scrimmage or 5 yards kick; the 15-yard branch is compressed to half-distance (x/2 yards) rather than 15. Verifiable signal: off-side defender contacts the receiver before the catch is secured. Prediction: when x < 10, the half-distance contraction yields x/2 < 5, so the 5-yard kick option yields more raw yardage than the 15-yard scrimmage option; however, the kick surrenders possession. Rational election prediction: teams with a reliable kicker prefer the 5-yard kick inside the buffer; teams prioritizing possession prefer the compressed scrimmage despite fewer yards.

Scenario 3 (Rule I + Note [PAGE 1]): Opponents charge before a free-kick is put in play, committing a second offense inside their own goal line. Veto: each offense adds 5 yards; per the Note, the second offense inside the goal line is penalized exactly as if outside, permitting placement behind the goal line without the 5-yard floor. Verifiable signal: defensive players cross the restraining line before the kick on two consecutive plays inside the goal line. Prediction: the first offense places the ball at or behind the goal line; the second offense removes buffer protection and produces a discontinuous field-position loss greater than 5 yards, potentially resulting in the ball being downed behind the offending team's own goal line.
