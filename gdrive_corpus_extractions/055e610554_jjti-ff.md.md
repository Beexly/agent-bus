Because ρ_mn ≠ 0 (shared NFL feed), equal weighting is statistically inefficient; [PAGE 1] confirms "Averaging three sources is naive vs. proper weighting."

Aggregated projection. \bar p_i(t) = Σ_{m=1}^{3} w_m p_i^{(m)}(t), subject to w_m ≥ 0 and Σ w_m = 1. Under correlated errors the optimal weight vector is w* = Σ^{-1}1 / (1ᵀΣ^{-1}1), where Σ is the 3×3 error-covariance matrix. Output units: fantasy points; weights dimensionless.

Replacement baseline. For position q, let A_q(t) be the ordered available pool after simulating the draft to the current pick, given T, S, and live ADP [PAGE 1]. The baseline is r_q(L,t) = \bar p_{(k_q^*+1)}(t) if |A_q| > k_q^*, where k_q^* = T · s_q + b_q is the cumulative slot demand plus bench-depth buffer b_q ∈ ℕ. If A_q(t) = ∅, r_q is undefined. [PAGE 1] frames this as "rankings relative to what you'd get if you didn't draft the guy."

VOR operator. VOR_i(L,t) = \bar p_i(t) − r_{pos(i)}(L,t) [fantasy points]. This is cardinal, not ordinal. The draft ranking sorts by descending VOR_i, not descending \bar p_i. [PAGE 1] verdict: "dynamic VOR with league-parameterized replacement baselines."

Availability and information. Let P(i available at pick k) be derived from live ADP. The ADP entropy is H_i = −Σ_k P(k) log P(k) [bits]. High H_i signals volatile market consensus and low confidence in r_q.

Singularity and breakdown regimes.
1. Empty pool: A_{pos(i)}(t) = ∅ ⇒ r undefined ⇒ VOR_i → −∞ (NaN). Occurs in late draft during a position run.
2. Saturation: T small and s_q large such that k_q^* ≥ D_q (positional depth). Then r_q ≈ min_{j∈q} \bar p_j, compressing VOR_q → 0 for all q-players; the VOR differential loses discriminative power.
3. Source disagreement: If max_m |p_i^{(m)} − \bar p_i| > κ · σ_{\bar p} with κ = 3, flag p_i as unreliable; VOR vetoed.
4. Clock: If computation latency τ_comp > draft clock τ_clock, VOR cannot be computed in real time; fallback to static rank triggers cognitive regression (see Recursive Theory of Mind).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage plus multi-week traps.

Scientific lineage. Replacement-level thinking descends from marginalist economics (opportunity cost; Jevons, Menger, Walras): the value of an asset is its contribution over the next-best available alternative. In sports analytics this was formalized as Value Over Replacement Player (VORP) in baseball during the 1990s–2000s (Baseball Prospectus, Keith Woolner), then ported to fantasy football by analysts modeling positional scarcity. The ff repo operationalizes this as a live web service: [PAGE 1] pushed 2026-09-11 (alive), whereas GSE's rankings program began 2026-09-28 [PAGE 1], placing GSE roughly seventeen days behind a production reference implementation.

Multi-week traps.
Trap 1 — Week-1 projection staleness. Even with daily pulls [PAGE 1], Week 1 snap counts and injury statuses are unstable; a projection refreshed Tuesday may be stale by Sunday. VOR computed against Week-1 baselines misprices rookies and handcuffs.
Trap 2 — Bye-week clustering. The replacement baseline r_q shifts artificially in weeks where many starters at q are on bye. Naive VOR overvalues backups in bye weeks and undervalues starters because the baseline condition ignores schedule synchronization.
Trap 3 — ADP anchoring momentum. [PAGE 1] incorporates "live ADP." If managers anchor to early-season ADP, the replacement baseline lags true market value, creating a momentum trap: high-VOR players fall too far while low-VOR name players are overdrafted.
Trap 4 — Source-correlation overconfidence. Equal weighting of ESPN/CBS/NFL [PAGE 1] inherits shared-data correlations; errors do not average out, producing overconfident VOR in Weeks 1–4 before sample stabilization.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

Crisis habit regression. Under draft-clock pressure, managers regress from System 2 VOR computation to System 1 heuristics: raw projection rank, ADP, or name recognition. [PAGE 1] notes GSE publishes flat ordered lists, which is precisely the regression target. Hypothesis: draft stress increases P(select max \bar p_i) over P(select max VOR_i).

Counter-trap. Opponents model this regression. Level-1 opponents expect flat-rank drafters and reach for negative-VOR scarcity plays, forcing the regressor to overpay for replacement later. Level-2 opponents anticipate VOR drafters and intentionally draft bench depth at thin positions, raising r_q and depressing the VOR of q-players for the VOR user. This is a draft-game fixed point: each layer conditions its baseline on the expected layer of the other.

Clock. The physical constraint is τ_comp ≤ τ_clock (pick timer). If VOR recomputation latency exceeds the clock, the infrastructure forces fallback to a static list, triggering crisis habit regression independent of manager intent. The GSE/ff mechanic targets Level-1 cardinal reasoning; without sub-clock recomputation, the system collapses to Level-0 ordinal selection under time pressure.

Recursive structure. Level-0: pick best player by raw projection. Level-1: pick best VOR given replacement baseline. Level-2: anticipate opponent runs and adjust r_q dynamically. Level-3: anticipate Level-2 manipulation of live ADP. [PAGE 1] describes Level-1 with live-ADP input; the missing GSE component is real-time, clock-compliant recursion through Levels 2 and 3.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Although ffdraft.app is a web assistant, its projections p_i^{(m)} derive from physical NFL contests. A veto-proof ranking layer must reject any VOR whose physical input layer is corrupt.

Physical layer (game conditions). Weather variables: wind speed v_w [m/s], precipitation rate [mm/hr], temperature T_air [°C]. Passing efficiency is physically attenuated when v_w > v_max (e.g., 15 m/s) or precipitation exceeds threshold. If unadjusted p_i^{(m)} is used for QBs/WRs in such conditions, the projection violates physical consistency. Veto indicator W_i = 1{v_w > v_max ∨ precip > p_max}.

Biomechanical layer (health and snap count). Practice participation φ_i ∈ [0,1] (fraction of projected snaps) and injury designation δ_i ∈ {0,1}. If φ_i < φ_min (e.g., 0.50) or δ_i = 1, p_i is physically unrealizable. Veto indicator B_i = 1{φ_i < φ_min ∨ δ_i = 1}.

Data-staleness layer. [PAGE 1] requires daily projection pulls from ESPN/CBS/NFL via public endpoints (no key). If last update t_last precedes an injury or depth-chart report by Δt > τ_max, the source is stale. Veto indicator S_i = 1{t_report − t_last > τ_max}.

Adjudication rule. V_veto(i) = max(W_i, B_i, S_i). Adjusted value: VOR_i^adj = VOR_i · (1 − V_veto(i)). If V_veto(i) = 1, the player is removed from the draft board or assigned VOR = −∞ (do not draft). Fallback: use the last physically validated projection with an added entropy penalty σ_penalty [fantasy points] to reflect uncertainty.

Breakdown. If all sources for a position fail physical-layer validation simultaneously (e.g., league-wide severe weather), r_q cannot be computed; VOR collapses and the system reverts to flat rank with an explicit uncertainty flag rather than publishing a false precision.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) veto scenarios with predictions. All conditions are computable from [PAGE 1] data (ESPN/CBS/NFL pulls, league size, roster format, scoring rules, live ADP, system telemetry).

Scenario 1 — Source-entropy veto.
Condition: coefficient of variation across sources CV_i(t) = std_{m}(p_i^{(m)}(t)) / mean_{m}(p_i^{(m)}(t)) > θ_CV, θ_CV = 0.30. Verifiable per pull at time t. Veto: do not recommend player i; fallback to the highest-VOR available player with CV ≤ θ_CV. Prediction: Over four-week cross-validation, drafters who respect the veto outperform drafters who select vetoed high-CV players by ≥ 8–12 fantasy points, because projection noise, not true talent, drives the ranking.

Scenario 2 — Replacement-baseline singularity (small league).
Condition: league size T below T_min such that k_q^* = T · s_q + b_q ≥ D_q (available depth at position q). Verifiable by counting rostered versus available q-players. Veto: veto early selection of position-q players because r_q ≈ min_{j∈q} \bar p_j, compressing VOR_q ≈ 0; recommend the highest-VOR non-q player instead. Prediction: Flat-rank drafters who reach for RB1 in an 8-team league sacrifice approximately 15–25 VOR points relative to VOR-compliant drafters who take WR1; verified by draft-order simulation.

Scenario 3 — Clock-timeout veto.
Condition: dynamic VOR recomputation latency τ_comp > draft clock τ_clock (e.g., 120 seconds per pick). Verifiable via system telemetry logs per pick. Veto: if timeout occurs, the system vetoes the VOR-selected pick and outputs the precomputed static backup list with a timeout flag; the manager cannot panic-reach based on a live-ADP spike. Prediction: Clock-timeout fallback picks outperform panic reaches driven by live-ADP overreaction by ≥ 0.3 standard deviations of positional value over the season, because panic reaches correlate with ADP momentum rather than baseline-adjusted value.
