We formalize the operators and equations implicit in the source [PAGE 99]. Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space. Let $L^1(\mu)$ denote the space of integrable functions (real-valued, finite integral of absolute value).

**Definition (Mean Fundamental Sequence):**
A sequence $\{f_n\} \subset L^1(\mu)$ is mean fundamental if it satisfies the Cauchy criterion with respect to the $L^1$ norm:
$$ \lim_{n,m \to \infty} \|f_n - f_m\|_1 = 0, \quad \text{where } \|g\|_1 = \int_X |g| \, d\mu. $$
*Units:* If $\mu$ has units of mass $[M]$ and $f$ has units of density $[D]/[M]$, then $\int f d\mu$ has units $[D]$. The norm is dimensionally consistent with the integrated quantity.

**Theorem A Operator Chain:**
Define the deviation set for $\epsilon > 0$:
$$ E_{nm}(\epsilon) = \{ x \in X : |f_n(x) - f_m(x)| > \epsilon \}. $$
The proof utilizes the elementary inequality (Chebyshev/Markov):
$$ \epsilon \cdot \mu(E_{nm}(\epsilon)) \leq \int_{E_{nm}(\epsilon)} |f_n - f_m| \, d\mu \leq \int_X |f_n - f_m| \, d\mu. $$
Since $\{f_n\}$ is mean fundamental, the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $n,m \to \infty$. Thus,
$$ \lim_{n,m \to \infty} \mu(E_{nm}(\epsilon)) = 0. $$
This defines "fundamental in measure": for every $\epsilon > 0$, the measure of the set where the difference exceeds $\epsilon$ vanishes.

**Theorem B Set Function Limit:**
For each $n$, define the indefinite integral (a set function):
$$ \nu_n(E) = \int_E f_n \, d\mu, \quad E \in \mathcal{M}. $$
The proof establishes that for fixed $E$, the numerical sequence $\{\nu_n(E)\}$ is Cauchy in $\mathbb{R}$:
$$ |\nu_n(E) - \nu_m(E)| = \left| \int_E (f_n - f_m) \, d\mu \right| \leq \int_E |f_n - f_m| \, d\mu \leq \|f_n - f_m\|_1 \to 0. $$
Thus, the pointwise limit exists:
$$ \nu(E) := \lim_{n \to \infty} \nu_n(E). $$
*Properties:* $\nu$ is finite-valued (since limit of finite numbers). Finite additivity follows from the algebraic limit laws applied to finitely additive $\nu_n$.
*Countable Additivity Setup:* Let $\{E_k\}$ be disjoint with union $E$. We have $\nu_n(E) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \nu_n(E_k)$. To show $\nu(E) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \nu(E_k)$, one must justify interchanging limits:
$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^\infty \nu_n(E_k) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \lim_{n \to \infty} \nu_n(E_k). $$
The text begins this by considering partial sums involving "every pair of positive integers n and k", likely leading to an estimate of the tail $\sum_{k>K} |\nu_n(E_k)|$ uniform in $n$.
