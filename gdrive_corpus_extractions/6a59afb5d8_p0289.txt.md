<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: extinction is a crisis of non-reward. The rat’s overt behavior regresses from reinforced pressing toward suppression, but per the text this occurs “under constant conditions of habit strength and motivation” [PAGE 280]; therefore the regression is response inhibition (Ik accumulation), not habit unlearning.

Counter-trap: an outside theorist could infer that the heavier bar “weakened” the habit because N declines with W. The correct recursive read is that habit strength was experimentally equated by the equal preliminary reinforcement [PAGE 280], so the N(W) gradient indexes inhibitory cost, not s_{Er} decay. Attributing the effect to weakened motivation or weakened habit is the counter-trap; the page explicitly rules both out as controlled variables.

Clock: three extinction periods of 20 minutes each are separated by 24 hours [PAGE 280]. This inter-session clock permits inhibitory decay (spontaneous recovery) between periods. If Ik decays with time constant τ, then I_k(t) = I_{k,0} e^{−t/τ}; with 24 h ≫ τ, each session may begin near baseline, whereas with 24 h ≪ τ, inhibition accumulates across days. Because the page does not specify τ or report within-session trial-by-trial N, the clock parameter is a gray zone: failure to model the 24-hour recovery interval invalidates any cross-period comparison of mean unreinforced reactions.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: the independent variable is bar weight in grams [PAGE 280], but work is W = FΔx. Unless bar displacement Δx is held constant across all presses and groups, gram weight is not a work measure. Veto: if Δx varies by rat or group, the inverse law cannot be tested from mass alone.

Biomechanical layer: 80 gf imposes different force-to-body-mass ratios across rats of different size; muscle force-velocity and fatigue properties make cost per response non-linear at high loads. Veto: if no normalization by body mass or maximal voluntary press force is reported, the 80 gf condition may reflect physical inaccessibility rather than pav-scale inhibition.

Weather/environment layer: the text describes a controlled apparatus and does not mention ambient conditions [PAGE 280]. Assume constant temperature, humidity, and bar friction. Veto: if ambient variation alters bar friction or rat paw contact mechanics, effective W shifts independently of the nominal gram weight.

Adjudication: the inverse linear claim survives only if all three layers pass. If the physical layer fails (uncontrolled Δx), the biomechanical layer fails (unmatched strength), or the environmental layer fails (friction drift), observed N(W) differences are vetoed as artifacts rather than evidence of a 100-pav centigrade inhibitory scale.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Prediction: n*(W) shifts upward proportionally with increased s_{Er}, but the inverse slope across W remains invariant. Veto condition: if N becomes statistically independent of W after extra reinforcement, then response cost was not the operative constraint and the inverse law is vetoed.

Scenario 3 (compressed recovery clock): replace the 24-hour inter-session interval with 1 hour while holding W constant. Prediction: if τ is long, Ik accumulates across the three periods and N in session 3 falls below session 1; if τ is short, N recovers fully each period. Veto condition: if N remains perfectly stable across sessions despite 1-hour spacing, then either inhibitory decay is faster than assumed or pav accrual does not accumulate as modeled, vetoing the accrual assumption underlying n* ∝ 1/W.
