[PARTICLE_HYPOTHESIS]
Let there exist $L$ distinct hereditary locations (loci) within an organism. Each locus can be occupied by a particle derived from either the maternal or paternal genome. Let $X_i \in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether the $i$-th particle originates from the mother ($X_i=0$) or father ($X_i=1$). Assuming independent transmission at each locus,
\[
\Pr(X_i = x_i) = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \forall i \in \{1,2,\dots,L\}.
\]

[GAMETIC_PARTITION_COUNT]
The total number of possible gametic partitions is $2^L$. However, since the contributions from both parents are indistinguishable in terms of phenotypic outcome, the effective number of unique combinations is half this value:
\[
N_{\text{unique}} = \frac{2^L}{2} = 2^{L-1}.
\]

[EMBRYONIC_COMPETITION_PROCESS]
During embryogenesis, each particle competes stochastically for a specific developmental location based on intrinsic properties (e.g., binding affinity) and extrinsic factors (position, microenvironment). Let $p_i$ denote the probability that a given particle successfully occupies its designated slot. Under independent competition,
\[
\Pr(\text{All } L \text{ slots filled}) = \prod_{i=1}^{L} p_i.
\]
Typically, we assume $p_i = p$ for all loci, yielding $p^L$. Since complete fixation ($p=1$) is unrealistic due to genetic drift and mutation, $0<p<1$.

[FAMILY_RESEMBLANCE_DEFINITION]
Define the degree of familial resemblance $R$ as the proportion of shared alleles between two relatives. For full siblings sharing the same parents, the expectation is
\[
\mathbb{E}[R] = \frac{1}{2},
\]
since each of the $L$ loci is inherited independently with equal probability.

[VARIANCE_DERIVATION]
The variance in the number of shared alleles between full siblings follows from binomial sampling:
\[
\sigma_R^2 = L \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{L}{4}.
\]
As $L$ becomes large, the central limit theorem guarantees approximate normality of the distribution of $R$, implying that most siblings will cluster around the mean resemblance of 0.5, yet some will exhibit markedly higher or lower similarity. This quantifies the simultaneous presence of generically high resemblance and individual variation.

Galton describes these particles as "minute" and "aggregates" being derived from common ancestors. Our formulation mirrors his conceptual structure by treating each particle as an independent Bernoulli trial with equal bias.

BREAKDOWN: Loci may not be truly independent due to linkage, epistasis, or environmental interactions, leading to deviations from simple binomial behavior.
