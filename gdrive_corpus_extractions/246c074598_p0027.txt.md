Maximum isometric force F_0 (Newtons, N) and optimum fiber length l_0 (meters, m) define a hard capacity envelope. If the molar act "reach out toward" (from [PAGE 9]) requires joint torque \tau_{\text{req}} > \tau_{\max}(\theta, \dot{\theta}), the act is physically vetoed regardless of purposive intent or integrated reflex organization. No amount of systematic interdependence among reflex arcs can produce torque beyond the actuator's physical limit.

Biomechanical veto: The reach-grasp functional unit requires opposable digit coordination and wrist degrees of freedom. If object geometry violates the grasp aperture or the object's center of mass lies outside the stable base of support, the contact-stimulus leg of the functional unit cannot be consummated. The act is initiated (distance receptor engaged) but inhibited at the contact stage, violating the [PAGE 9] claim that the act is stimulated or inhibited as a whole, because the biomechanical layer fragments the whole.

Weather veto: Environmental conditions degrade the distance-receptor channel. Fog, precipitation, or wind-generated noise reduce the signal-to-noise ratio of vision or audition, preventing the "total response initiated by the distance receptor" described on [PAGE 9]. The contact stimulus may remain intact, but without the distance-receptor trigger the integrated functional unit never assembles. This is a veto of the molar act by sensory-channel physics, not by a failure of reflex integration.

These vetoes are decisive: they demonstrate that Tolman's molar behaviorism on [PAGE 9] must be embedded within a layered control architecture in which lower-layer constraints can override higher-level purposive organization. The breakdown regime is reached precisely when a veto constraint is active and no compensatory molar re-planning is available within the organism's behavioral repertoire.

Counterfactual: The distance-receptor channel (vision) is degraded by controlled fog such that contrast sensitivity falls below the threshold for target detection at 1 m, while the contact-stimulus channel (tactile/beak contact) remains fully functional. Verifiable prediction: Latency to initiate reach increases from a baseline of \approx 600 ms (clear conditions) to > 3500 ms, and the probability of completing the full integrated act (reach \to peck \to swallow) falls below p = 0.15 across 40 trials.
