**Notation and units.** PU = probability units (dimensionless probability difference, not a percentage). All state variables carry SI or event-domain units; no composite ratio is invented that the source does not define.

**O1 — Action valuation (Equation 1, [PAGE 4]).** Verbatim: "𝑉𝐴𝐸𝑃(𝑎) = ∆𝑃𝑔𝑜𝑎𝑙(𝑎) −∆𝑃𝑎𝑔𝑎𝑖𝑛𝑠𝑡(𝑎)". Formally,

$$\mathrm{VAEP}(a)=\Delta P_{\text{goal}}(a)-\Delta P_{\text{against}}(a)\quad[\mathrm{PU}],\qquad \mathrm{VAEP}(a)\in[-1,+1].$$

The horizon is fixed by [PAGE 4]: "The first model estimated the ∆Pgoal, i.e., the change in the probability of the team scoring within the next 10 actions." Hence

$$\Delta P_{\text{goal}}(a)=\hat P\!\left(G\mid s_{t+h}\right)-\hat P\!\left(G\mid s_{t}\right),\qquad h=10\ \text{actions}.$$

*Assumption:* $\hat P$ is calibrated. *Breakdown:* log-odds differencing $\Delta\,\mathrm{logit}\,\hat P$ diverges as $\hat P\to\{0,1\}$; the paper uses raw probability differences, which is the correct choice and avoids that pole.

**O2 — Horizon truncation bias.** When possession terminates at $k<h$ actions,

$$\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta\hat P_{\text{goal}}\right]=\Delta P_{\text{goal}}+\mathcal{B}(h,k),\qquad \mathcal{B}=\mathbb{E}\left[\hat P(G\mid s_{t+k})-\hat P(G\mid s_{t+h})\right],\quad |\mathcal{B}|\le 1\ \mathrm{PU}.$$

$|\mathcal{B}|$ is monotone increasing in $h/k$. With $h=10$ and a median possession of $k\approx4$ in top-five-league event data, $h/k\approx2.5$ — the bias term is not negligible in the tails. The paper does not report it.

**O3 — Aggregation and the bound violation.** For player $i$ with action set $A_i$,

$$V_i=\sum_{a\in A_i}\mathrm{VAEP}(a)\quad[\mathrm{PU}],\qquad |V_i|\le n_i .$$

With $n_3=40$ [PAGE 7], $|V_3|\le 40$; the reported $+3.0$ is 7.5% of the attainable range, i.e. $0.075\ \mathrm{PU\cdot action^{-1}}$. The paper's own rescue clause is [PAGE 3]: "skill scores were expressed as a combination of normalized VAEP values and performance indices, providing a consistent scale." That clause converts $V_i$ from a physical quantity into a composite index and is undeclared at the figure level.

**O4 — Fatigue index (dimensional singularity).** Verbatim [PAGE 3]: "the fatigue index was defined as the ratio of high-intensity sprints to playing time."

$$F_i=\frac{N_i^{\text{sprint}}}{T_i}\quad\left[\mathrm{sprint\cdot min^{-1}}\right],\qquad \lim_{T_i\to0^{+}}F_i=+\infty .$$

This is a genuine coordinate singularity of the decision system, not a modelling artifact. It is removable only by a floor: $F_i=\min\{F_i,F_{\max}\}$ or $T_i\leftarrow\max\{T_i,T_{\min}\}$ with $T_{\min}\approx5$ min. Neither is present.

**O5 — Contribution functional (Equation 5, [PAGE 5]).** Verbatim: "𝐶𝑜𝑛𝑡𝑟𝑖𝑏𝑢𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛(𝑖) = 𝑉𝐴𝐸𝑃(𝑖) −𝛼⋅𝐹𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑔𝑢𝑒(𝑖)".

$$C_i=V_i-\alpha F_i\quad[\mathrm{PU}],\qquad [\alpha]=\mathrm{PU\cdot min\cdot sprint^{-1}}.$$

*Scale invariance:* the level set $\{C_i=\theta\}$ is invariant under $(\alpha,\theta)\mapsto(\lambda\alpha,\lambda\theta)$ only if $V_i\mapsto\lambda V_i$. Since $V_i$ is fixed in PU, α and θ are individually identified up to a *joint* affine constraint — meaning the published rule is unidentified by one degree of freedom even if both were given.

**O6 — Decision wedge ([PAGE 5]).** Verbatim: "Substitution rule: if 𝐶𝑜𝑛𝑡𝑟𝑖𝑏𝑢𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛(𝑖) < 𝜃 and 𝐹𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑔𝑢𝑒(𝑖) > 𝛽, then substitution of player 𝑖 is recommended."

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{sub}}=\left\{(V,F): V-\alpha F<\theta\right\}\cap\left\{F>\beta\right\},\qquad \partial\mathcal{R}: V=\theta+\alpha F.$$

The boundary is a straight line of slope $\alpha$ in the $(V,F)$ plane intersected with the half-plane $F>\beta$: a wedge. Because the two conjuncts use *different* variables and the AND is non-compensatory, a player with elite $V$ (say $+3.0$) and $F=2\beta$ is never substituted regardless of $\alpha$ — the rule is bounded above by $V<\theta+\alpha\beta$.

**O7 — Critical playing time (closed-form breakdown).** For $V_i>\theta$,

$$C_i<\theta\iff T_i<T_i^{*}=\frac{\alpha N_i}{V_i-\theta}\quad[\mathrm{min}],\qquad T_i^{*}\xrightarrow[V_i\to\theta^{+}]{}+\infty .$$

Worked instance: $V=0.5\,\mathrm{PU}$, $\theta=0$, $N=1$ sprint gives $T^{*}=2\alpha$. For $\alpha=0.1$ nothing plausibly flags; for $\alpha=10$ every player under 20 minutes flags. The decision flips across two orders of magnitude of an unreported parameter.

The operator stack is a genealogy of three independent lineages welded together, and the weld points are where the audit pressure belongs.

**Lineage A — action valuation.** VAEP descends from Decroos et al. [26], cited by the paper as "Actions speak louder than goals: Valuing player actions in soccer," KDD 2019. Decroos's design constraint was that only *event* data be required — no tracking, no wearables — precisely so the metric could scale across leagues with heterogeneous sensor infrastructure. The paper under audit inverts that constraint, claiming to have "extended the VAEP metric" with "physiological and temporal dimensions" [PAGE 3] while retaining an event provider as its stated source. The genealogy is therefore retrocausally inconsistent: the metric's design rationale was minimal data dependence; the extension's value proposition is maximal data dependence, but the data are not shown to exist.

**Lineage B — computer vision.** The paper's related work runs through referee-gesture recognition with YOLOv8s and MPDIoU [PAGE 2], ball-possession forecasting via homographic perspective transformation and weighted intersect fusion [PAGE 2], and TacticAI's graph formulation of set pieces [PAGE 3, ref 13]. Each of these supplies its own pixel- or pitch-coordinate substrate. The audited paper borrows their *conclusions* (CNNs win on spatial tasks) without borrowing their substrate, and instead feeds a 3×3 convolutional stack on "positional coordinates" aggregated per event — a representation with no pixel grid and no metric neighbourhood structure. The convolution is thus a tabular feature-crossing operator in disguise.

**Lineage C — workload monitoring.** The fatigue index as "the ratio of high-intensity sprints to playing time" [PAGE 3] is a degenerate descendant of GPS load-monitoring constructs (PlayerLoad, high-speed-running distance, and the acute:chronic workload ratio). Those constructs are *time-windowed ratios of external load over 7 and 28 days*. The paper's index collapses the window to a single match and the numerator to an event count. This is where the **multi-week trap** sits: overuse injury cascades (tendinopathy, bone stress, hamstring re-injury) unfold over 3–6 weeks and are governed by the *ratio* of recent to chronic load. A single-match sprint-per-minute index has no chronic term and therefore cannot, even in principle, encode the mechanism it is claimed to predict. Any apparent predictive power must come from a different feature — and "injury history" is sitting in the attribute list [PAGE 3].

**Temporal framing.** The corpus window is 2017/18–2022/23 [PAGE 3], closing two years before the December 2025 publication date. That lag is consistent with licensed event-data redistribution cycles and with the pandemic-era fixture compression of 2019/20–2020/21. It also means the model's possession-length prior — which sets the truncation bias ℬ in O2 — was learned across a period containing the 2020 congestion anomaly, an outlier regime for exactly the sprint-density variable the fatigue index encodes. No regime dummy is reported.

**The unsourced deployment claim.** The conclusion cites "the experience of Brighton and Hove Albion" [PAGE 12] with no reference. In the published record, that club's analytics reputation traces to a specific recruitment-model lineage, not to a VAEP-plus-biometrics pipeline of the kind described here. The citation vacuum is the retrocausal tell: a real deployment would have a named paper, a season, and a metric.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The habit.** The system's substitution rule is a deterministic threshold policy $\pi_{\text{habit}}(s)=\mathbf{1}[C_i<\theta\ \wedge\ F_i>\beta]$. Deterministic threshold policies are maximally legible — which is the paper's stated virtue ("transparent and reproducible," [PAGE 5]) and simultaneously its exploitable surface.

**The counter-trap.** An opponent that models $\pi_{\text{habit}}$ can *manufacture the trigger*. To raise $F_j$ for a targeted defender, run repeated high-intensity actions at that player's flank: each sprint increments $N_j^{\text{sprint}}$ while $T_j$ accrues only with clock time, so $F_j=N_j/T_j$ rises monotonically in the numerator at fixed denominator. To simultaneously depress $V_j$, force turnovers in zones where the classifier's $\Delta P_{\text{against}}$ is large. The two manipulations are *the same action* — pressing high at a specific player both raises his sprint count and lowers his action value. The rule then removes the opponent's problem for him. Formally, the opponent solves $\max_{u}\ \Pr[\pi_{\text{habit}}(s')=\text{substitute } j \mid s, u]$; against a deterministic $\pi$ this maximum is 1 whenever the wedge is reachable, so the policy is fully exploitable and its exploit requires no private information beyond the published rule.

**The counter-counter (entropy).** Replacing the deterministic gate with a soft policy $\pi(a\mid s)=\sigma\!\left((\theta+\alpha F-V)/\tau\right)$ and $\tau>0$ makes the trigger stochastic; the opponent's manipulation then shifts a probability rather than a certainty, and the expected gain to manipulation falls as $\tau$ grows. The paper's rule has $\tau\to0$: maximum exploitability.

**The clock.** Time is the forcing variable the rule ignores. The marginal value of removing player $i$ is approximately $\left(V_{\text{repl}}-V_i\right)\cdot\left(1-t/T_{90}\right)$: at $t=85$ min the substitution cannot recover value regardless of how fatigued the player is. The correct threshold is therefore clock-dependent,

$$\theta(t)=\theta_0\left(1-\frac{t}{T_{90}}\right)^{\kappa},\qquad \kappa>0,$$

which reproduces the static rule only at $t=0$. Because $F_i$ is a *cumulative* ratio, it is monotonically non-decreasing in $t$ for a player who keeps sprinting, while the value of acting on it decays to zero — the rule becomes maximally likely to fire exactly when it is least useful. That is a structural anti-correlation between trigger sensitivity and action value, and it is invisible in a static formulation.

**Second-order reflexivity.** The model is deployed into the distribution it was trained on. If coaching staff act on substitution alerts, the resulting player-minutes distribution shifts toward earlier exits for high-sprint players, which truncates $T_i$ in the next season's training data, which raises the empirical $F_i$ distribution, which lowers the effective β. This is performative prediction: the reported F1 of 0.87 is a property of the pre-deployment regime and carries no guarantee post-deployment. The paper does not model this feedback and does not report deployment-window monitoring.

**Third-order: the coach as a second policy.** The expert-rating study — "three professional coaches … on a 5-point Likert scale, where the average expert rating was 4.3" [PAGE 7] — measures *agreement between $\pi_{\text{habit}}$ and human priors*, not correctness. A rule that encodes "substitute tired players who are playing badly" will score 4.3 with any coach and beat nothing. Agreement is the wrong estimand for a decision-support system; realized outcome under counterfactual action is the right one, and $n=3$ gives no power to distinguish 4.3 from 4.0.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical layer — veto sustained on provenance.** The fatigue channel $F=N^{\text{sprint}}/T$ requires an external-load sensor. The paper names Wyscout, an on-ball event provider, as the source [PAGE 3], and separately lists "heart rate" — an internal-load signal requiring a chest strap or optical sensor at ≥1 Hz. No device, sampling rate, or coverage fraction is reported; the limitations section concedes "Biometric data coverage was not uniform across teams and seasons" [PAGE 12]. **Verdict:** the physical layer cannot be adjudicated because it cannot be located. The paper's novelty claim — "we incorporate biometric and contextual features (heart rate, fatigue, and weather conditions)" [PAGE 3] — is unfalsifiable as written.

**Biomechanical layer — veto sustained on mechanism.** Injury aetiology in football is tissue-level: eccentric hamstring force at terminal swing, Achilles tendon strain energy under cumulative cyclic load, bone-stress response to ground-reaction-force impulse. None of these is a monotone function of on-ball event counts or of minutes played. Furthermore, a binary classifier reaching precision 0.89 [PAGE 7] in a domain whose positive base rate is on the order of a few percent of player-matches implies near-separable classes. Under a base rate $\pi=0.05$ and precision $0.89$, the model's positive calls are right nine times in ten; the corresponding likelihood ratio is enormous and is not credible for a first-principles biomechanical model. The available explanation is that "injury history" [PAGE 3] is inside the feature set — i.e., the model recovers prior injury rather than predicting new injury. **Verdict:** the biomechanical claim is not merely unproven; the reported performance is itself the evidence of a label leak.

**Weather layer — veto sustained on indefinability.** The paper lists "weather conditions, pitch quality" among match context features [PAGE 3] and lists weather among the novelty features [PAGE 3]. No meteorological variable is defined, no source is cited, no units appear. A physically meaningful weather term requires at minimum air temperature [°C], relative humidity [%], and a thermal-stress index such as WBGT [°C], plus a pitch-hardness proxy — because the causal pathways are (i) turf hardness → peak ankle/Achilles impulse and (ii) WBGT → thermoregulatory drift → heart-rate drift at fixed workload, which *contaminates* the fatigue index by making $F$ workload-dependent and heat-dependent simultaneously. **Verdict:** the weather layer is claimed and undefined. It also introduces a confound the paper never addresses: if $F_i$ rises with WBGT at constant sprint count, then $F$ is not a pure load measure and α must be conditioned on ambient conditions.

**Data-integrity layer — veto sustained.** The retained artifact "GitHub+9Hudl+9ResearchGate+9" [PAGE 3] and the event-count discrepancy (1.5 × 10⁵ stated vs 2.16 × 10⁶ implied by 1,200 × 1,800) are hard, checkable, document-level facts. The informed-consent statement [PAGE 13] contradicts the third-party, multi-season, 3,800-player data description [PAGE 3]. These three items alone justify withholding any performance claim until the manifest is produced.

**What survives.** The metric arithmetic (O9, O10) is internally consistent; the ANOVA/McNemar protocol is appropriate in form even if under-reported; the modular pipeline framing on [PAGE 12] is a reasonable architectural description. Nothing in the fatigue or weather layer survives.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each veto is a reward-checkable test with a stated pass condition and a predicted outcome. No wagering constructs are used or implied anywhere in this audit.

**VETO 1 — Manifest reconciliation (arithmetic, deterministic).**
*Test:* require the authors to publish a match-level manifest: number of matches, events per match, and unique players, for each of the six seasons. Compute $E_{\text{implied}}=M\cdot \bar e$ and compare to the stated $1.5\times10^{5}$.
*Verifiable predicate:* PASS iff $M\bar e$ is within 10% of $1.5\times10^{5}$; FAIL otherwise.
*Prediction:* FAIL with high confidence. The stated per-match rate of 1,800 and the stated 1,200 matches give $2.16\times10^{6}$, a 14.4× overshoot; reconciliation requires the true match count near 83–125 at that rate. Downstream consequence: the CNN's effective spatial sample and the LSTM's sequence length are ~7% of the advertised values, and sequence length is what makes gated recurrence identifiable.

**VETO 2 — Fatigue-channel ablation (randomization test).**
*Test:* retrain the injury model three times: (a) as reported, (b) with $F_i$ and heart-rate features row-permuted across players (destroys their association with the label while preserving marginals), (c) with "injury history" removed.
*Verifiable predicate:* the reported F1 = 0.87 and ROC-AUC = 0.92 [PAGE 1, PAGE 7] are the reference.
*Prediction:* In (b), F1 drops by less than 0.03 if the biometric channel is inert — consistent with the circularity hypothesis, since permutation of an uninformative feature cannot hurt a model driven by prior injury. In (c), F1 collapses toward the base-rate floor (roughly 0.1–0.25 at a 5% positive rate). If instead (b) drops F1 below 0.70 and (c) leaves it near 0.85, the biometric channel is genuine and the circularity hypothesis is refuted. Either outcome is decisive; the paper reports neither ablation, and its own surprise at the result — "somewhat unexpected" [PAGE 12] — is a concession that the mechanism was never isolated.

**VETO 3 — Decision-rule boundary sweep (parameter identifiability).**
*Test:* instantiate the rule on a synthetic substitute: $T=2$ min, $N^{\text{sprint}}=1$, $V=+0.5$ PU. Sweep $\alpha\in[0.1,10]$ PU·min·sprint⁻¹ at fixed $\theta=0$, $\beta=0.5$ sprint·min⁻¹, and record the recommendation.
*Verifiable predicate:* the recommendation must be invariant, or α must be reported with units and provenance.
*Prediction:* the rule flips at $\alpha^{\dagger}=(V-\theta)T/N=1.0$ PU·min·sprint⁻¹ by the closed form $T^{*}=\alpha N/(V-\theta)$. Below $\alpha^{\dagger}$ no substitution; above it, substitution — across a sweep of two orders of magnitude in an unpublished parameter. The paper's claim that the framework is "transparent and reproducible" [PAGE 5] therefore fails a one-line reproducibility check, and the failure is worst exactly at short playing time, where $T^{*}\to\infty$ as $V\to\theta^{+}$.

**Supplementary VETO 4 — Temporal generalization (forward-chaining).**
*Test:* replace the "70% training and 30% testing … stratified sampling method" split [PAGE 4] with a strictly forward split: train on 2017/18–2020/21, validate on 2021/22, test on 2022/23.
*Verifiable predicate:* report ROC-AUC on the final season alone.
*Prediction:* ROC-AUC for the CNN falls from 0.94 [PAGE 7] to the 0.85–0.89 band, and the XGBoost injury model falls further in relative terms because its top feature (prior injury) drifts as squad composition turns over. A drop below 0.80 on the injury model would independently corroborate VETO 2's leak hypothesis. The stratified-by-season split as published permits future information into training and cannot distinguish a stable model from a memorized season.

**Aggregate adjudication.** VETO 1 is a document fact and is expected to fire. VETO 3 is a dimensional fact and is certain to fire (the parameters do not exist in the text). VETO 2 and VETO 4 decide whether the paper's one novel claim — physiological coupling inside the action-value functional — is real or an artifact. Until VETO 2 reports a post-ablation F1 and VETO 1 produces a manifest, the +1.8 to +3.0 VAEP range, the 0.94 ROC-AUC, and the 0.87 injury F1 should all be treated as unreplicated outputs of an uncharacterized pipeline.
