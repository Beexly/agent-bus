2. **Non-injective relation (ambiguity singularity):** Two distinct gestalts \(G_a \neq G_b\) share the same immediate cue \(x_k\) but differ in remote significates or direction–distance relations, so \(\widehat{\Pr}(G \mid x_k)\) is multimodal and expectancy cannot resolve to a single means-end pathway.
3. **Missing re-presentation (domain error):** Condition (d) fails because the stimulus corresponding to the feature is not perceptually present; \(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_N\) is undefined on the empty input, and no expectation is evoked, regardless of prior training.
4. **Spatial relation violation (post-training shift):** The remote significate moves from \(y\) to \(y'\) while \(x_k\) remains unchanged; the stored \(\delta_{kj}\) no longer matches the physical maze, invalidating the sign–significate means-end relation.

All equations assume Euclidean maze geometry, stationary lighting and odor fields during a session, and that the animal’s sensory apparatus can resolve the relevant discriminanda. Citations to verbatim source language are marked [PAGE 153].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of the page’s argument runs from Tolman’s 1932 purposive behaviorism through the cognitive-map revolution and into modern spatial neuroscience. On [PAGE 153], Tolman explicitly cites “C. K. Ogden and I. A. Richards, *The Meaning of Meaning* … esp. p. 16,” importing a semiotic triangle in which the immediate stimulus is the sign, the remembered food or distant feature is the significate, and the evoked expectation is the interpretant. This anticipates the later sign–sign–interpretant triad of Peircean semiotics as applied to animal cognition.

Historically, the sign-gestalt of [PAGE 153] becomes Tolman’s “cognitive map” in his 1948 paper, where rats are shown to navigate to a goal even when the familiar route is blocked, implying a represented set of direction–distance relations among maze features—exactly the “total complex” described here. That representation is neurobiologically instantiated by place cells (O’Keefe & Dostrovsky, 1971), grid cells (Hafting et al., 2005), and boundary-vector cells, which encode the very metric relations Tolman treats as learned “enjoyments” of direction and distance.

Multi-week traps arise in chronic training. Over weeks, (a)–(c) are repeated across many occasions, but the “intimate conjunction” required for mnemonization can be degraded by proactive interference from earlier gestalts. If the food location is relocated after weeks of stable training, the sign still evokes an expectancy for the old significate—a retrospective revaluation trap in which the animal perseverates toward the former coordinates despite the new reward contingency. If the maze is rotated or distal cues are rotated relative to proximal cues, the direction–distance relations \(\delta_{ij}\) are violated, and the stored gestalt no longer matches the physical environment; the animal must either regress to habitual stimulus–response chains or re-mnemonize a new complex. These traps are longitudinal because they depend on the accumulation of trials and the stability of the spatial boundary conditions across sessions.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory of mind here concerns how an agent models its own expectancy formation under pressure. On [PAGE 153], conditions (a)–(c) must have occurred “in intimate conjunction” on preceding occasions, while condition (d) is “here and now.” This creates a temporal recursive structure: the present re-presentation queries a historically bound complex.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the immediate stimulus is present but the path to the remote significate is blocked or the animal is under stress. Because learning trials also cement simple S-R habits alongside the gestalt, a crisis biases the system toward the most recently reinforced or most frequently executed habit rather than the computed sign–gestalt expectancy. The agent regresses from “expectation of the whole” to a cached motor sequence.

The counter-trap is the experimenter’s manipulation of the remote significate or the spatial layout after the gestalt is consolidated. If the food moves or a wall is inserted, the sign-gestalt system points to a now-invalid location. The animal faces a recursive choice: trust the outdated expectancy (sign → old whole) or regress to habit. The counter-trap succeeds when the animal detects the mismatch between the re-presented cue and the expected spatial relation, inhibiting the habitual response and searching for a new means-end pathway.

The clock is the trial-timing variable. Because mnemonization depends on conditions (a)–(c) being bound across preceding occasions, the inter-trial interval and massed-versus-spaced schedule determine the strength of the sign–significate binding. Too short an interval produces proactive interference from the previous trial’s gestalt; too long an interval permits decay of the trace before the next re-presentation. The “here and now” of condition (d) must resonate with a sufficiently consolidated “there and then” of conditions (a)–(c). Thus the clock governs whether the recursive loop closes productively or collapses into habit regression.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The sign-gestalt expectancy is not autonomous; it is gated by physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers that can veto its expression.

Physical layer: Maze walls, barriers, and floor geometry define admissible manipulanda. If a physical barrier lies between the immediate cue and the remote significate, the direction–distance relation \(\delta_{ij}\) may still be represented in memory, but the motor execution of \(m_n\) is blocked. The veto is absolute at the behavioral level: no amount of expectancy can move the animal through a solid wall.

Biomechanical layer: The animal’s sensory and motor plant imposes limits on condition (d). If the immediate stimulus is outside the visual field, below olfactory threshold, or requires a body posture the animal cannot assume in the current location, the re-presentation fails. Because [PAGE 153] makes condition (d) the trigger for the entire mnemonization, a biomechanical veto at this layer prevents any expectancy from being aroused, regardless of prior learning.

Weather/environmental layer: Ambient temperature, humidity, airflow, and lighting alter the discriminability of maze features. A change in odor plume direction or light intensity can make the “now perceptually present” stimulus no longer match the trained feature vector \(x_k\). This is an input-layer veto: the cue is physically present but not perceptually identical to the element of the stored total complex, so \(\widehat{\Pr}(G \mid x_k)\) is not invoked.

Adjudication hierarchy: The sensory-perceptual gate (condition d) is upstream of the cognitive expectancy layer. If the gate vetoes, the sign-gestalt operator receives no input and remains dormant. If the gate passes but the physical layer blocks the manipulanda, the expectancy is formed but cannot be expressed behaviorally; the animal may exhibit vicarious trial and error or frustration behaviors without reaching the significate. The system therefore fails gracefully: either no expectation is generated (sensory/biomechanical veto), or an expectation is generated but its behavioral output is blocked (physical veto). No layer can override the absence of the re-presented stimulus because, on [PAGE 153], mnemonization is defined specifically by the action of re-presentation evoking the whole.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios for a reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) implementation of Tolman’s sign-gestalt system.

Scenario 1: Cue ablation. Train the agent on a maze where visual cue \(A\) at a choice point reliably precedes food at location \(F\), with direction–distance relation \(\delta_{AF}\) stored across trials. On a test trial, occlude \(A\) (e.g., extinguish the local light or place an opaque barrier) while leaving \(F\) accessible. Veto: condition (d) fails because the immediate stimulus is not perceptually present; \(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_N\) has no input. Prediction: the agent does not orient toward \(F\) before reaching the choice point; choice accuracy at the junction drops to chance; path entropy increases by more than 50 percent relative to cue-present trials; latency to \(F\) rises significantly. Verifiable via video tracking: no anticipatory head-fixation or trajectory bias toward the former food vector during the cue-absent trial.

Scenario 2: Spatial relation violation. After stable sign-gestalt learning, translocate the food from \(F\) to \(F' = F + \Delta \mathbf{r}\) while keeping cue \(A\) and all local maze geometry unchanged. Veto: condition (c) is violated because the stored direction–distance relation \(\delta_{AF}\) no longer matches the physical layout; the gestalt points to outdated coordinates. Prediction: the agent exhibits place perseveration, approaching the former location \(F\) and showing vicarious trial and error there despite the absence of food; the learned expectancy distribution \(\widehat{\Pr}(G \mid A)\) peaks at the old significate, not at \(F'\). Verifiable: endpoint distribution clustered within 5 cm of trained coordinates across 20 test trials, with less than 10 percent of trajectories terminating at \(F'\).

Scenario 3: Temporal contingency disruption. Randomize inter-trial intervals widely or insert distractor trials so that conditions (a)–(c) are not experienced “in intimate conjunction” on preceding occasions, as required on [PAGE 153]. Veto: the total complex is not bound as a single gestalt; mnemonization does not converge because the commerce-with complex is fragmented across time. Prediction: the learning curve remains flat; mutual information \(I(G; x_k)\) stays near zero bits across sessions; re-presentation of the immediate cue produces no statistically significant improvement in choice accuracy over baseline. Verifiable: after 10 sessions, no significant difference between cue-present and cue-absent conditions (\(p > 0.05\), two-tailed), confirming failure of sign–significate binding.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
