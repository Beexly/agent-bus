Without an explicit adversarial layer, the Dixon–Coles recursion is a univariate time-series filter applied to a multivariate game.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The Dixon–Coles model as described on [PAGE 1] is a purely statistical, score-history layer. It conditions on t_k, x_k, y_k, and φ(t − t_k), and nothing else. A cross-layer veto occurs when a physical, biomechanical, or weather event invalidates the assumption that λ_k and μ_k are exchangeable Poisson intensities generated by a stable attack–defense process.

Veto 1 — In-match structural break (red card or goalkeeper injury). If a team plays with ten men for the final 25 minutes, or loses its goalkeeper to injury, the post-event scoring intensity is not drawn from the same Poisson process as the pre-match intensity. The observation (x_k, y_k) is then a mixture of two regimes, yet equation (4.5) enters it with a single weight w_{k,t}. No choice of φ can veto this; φ only rescales the contribution, it does not change the generative model. The match must either be excluded from A_t or augmented with a match-specific covariate, neither of which is provided by equations (4.3)–(4.5).

Veto 2 — Weather and pitch condition. Heavy rain, snow, or a waterlogged pitch suppresses scoring and alters the dependence parameter ρ (fewer goals means the low-score adjustment matters less). Because φ is blind to weather, a match played in a storm receives the same temporal weight as one played in dry conditions. If weather systematically coincides with a period of the calendar (e.g., winter months), ξ estimation will confound seasonal weather effects with genuine team-performance drift.

Veto 3 — Fixture congestion and travel fatigue. A team playing its third match in seven days faces accumulated fatigue that depresses λ and μ relative to a rested opponent. Calendar time t_k − t_{k−1} is short, so φ assigns high weight to the congested match, but the physiological state is not part of the state vector θ_t. The model therefore treats a fatigue-depressed performance as if it were a true reduction in team quality, biasing α_t and β_t downward for the congested squad.

Adjudication rule: any veto at the physical/biomechanical/weather layer renders the Poisson likelihood kernel misspecified for that match. Because the pseudolikelihood is already not a true likelihood [PAGE 1], there is no robust sandwich variance that repairs this; the only valid response is exclusion from A_t, downweighting to zero via an exogenous mask, or adding a layer-specific covariate outside the Dixon–Coles specification.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios, each with a falsifiable prediction.

Scenario 1: Starting goalkeeper ruled out ≤60 minutes before kickoff. Verifiable condition: official lineup release timestamp precedes kickoff by less than one hour and the named goalkeeper is absent. Veto: exclude the match from A_t for both teams or set w_{k,t} = 0. Prediction: the unvetoed Dixon–Coles estimate of π_H will be miscalibrated relative to the vetoed estimate, because the Poisson intensity for the affected team is no longer invariant to the pre-match lineup assumption.

Scenario 2: Match abandoned after 70 minutes due to weather. Verifiable condition: referee match report confirms abandonment before full time and the score at abandonment is (x_k, y_k). Veto: do not enter the partial match into f_DC as if it were a completed 90-minute draw/win; exclude or impute under a separate censoring model. Prediction: including the abandoned match as a completed result biases λ_k and μ_k downward (fewer goals than a full match would produce) and corrupts φ weights for all subsequent dates t > t_k because the match remains in A_t with a decaying but non-zero weight.

Scenario 3: Effective sample size below critical threshold. Verifiable condition: compute N_eff(t; ξ) from the weight vector at decision time t; if N_eff(t; ξ) < 8 (twice the dimension of θ_t = 4), reject the exponential fit. Veto: fall back to a static prior or hierarchical shrinkage rather than the time-local MLE. Prediction: in the regime ξ → ∞ with N_eff → 1, the score equation ∇ℓ_t = 0 is underdetermined, parameter variance diverges, and out-of-sample predictive capability for match outcomes deteriorates relative to the vetoed static fallback. This is verifiable by monitoring N_eff across a rolling validation window.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
