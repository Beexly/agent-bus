If an affiliate drops a referral cookie via an invisible iframe in week 1, and the user organically returns in week 3 to purchase, the stale cookie attributes the sale retroactively to the stuffer rather than the organic discoverer; the longer \tau_{cookie}, the larger the misattribution surface. Trap 3: seasonal Q4 backlog. November–December click volume surges overwhelm report-generation (Section 6), delaying fraud detection until January payouts; associates who stuffed links in December can exploit the multi-week audit lag before bans are enforced. Trap 4: reputation arbitrage. An associate builds audience trust over weeks 1–8, then switches to low-quality or misleading link placement in week 9; conversion rates collapse retroactively, but previously earned commissions are not clawed absent explicit fraud findings. These traps are multi-week because the causal attribution window \tau_{attr} and the merchant’s audit window T_{audit} are not synchronized in the disclosure.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the associate and Amazon’s fraud detector as recursively reasoning agents. Level-0 associate: believes all clicks convert to commission; places links honestly. Level-1 associate: infers that only verified purchases pay (\chi=1 required) [PAGE 1], and fabricates secondary buyer accounts to self-refer. Level-0 Amazon: credits all \chi=1 events. Level-1 Amazon: observes that self-referrals cluster by IP, device fingerprint, or shared browser canvas; begins withholding commissions on linked accounts. Level-2 associate: anticipates IP/linkage detection and rotates residential proxies or clears cookies between self-referrals. Level-2 Amazon: upgrades to graph-anomaly detection over the referral tree, flagging non-tree structures (cycles, stars) generated by proxy farms.

Crisis habit regression occurs when commission withholding triggers a reversion to pre-detection behavior: associates increase link density and employ aggressive pop-under placements, a habit carried over from the unmonitored early web. The counter-trap is Amazon’s introduction of a quality-score throttle q_\alpha \in [0,1] that multiplies \mathbb{E}[C_\alpha] by site reputation; associates who regress to spam see q_\alpha \to 0 asymptotically. The clock is the payout cycle T_{pay} (e.g., net-30 days). An associate’s fraudulent conversion must survive from t_{click} to t_{pay}; if Amazon’s detection latency \tau_{det} > T_{pay}, the commission is paid before reversal. The counter-trap to the clock is payout deferral: Amazon extends T_{pay} by \Delta t > \tau_{det}, forcing the associate to sustain fraud cost longer than the expected payout horizon. Belief-state recursion follows Bayesian updating: P(\theta_{fraud} \mid h_t) \propto P(h_t \mid \theta_{fraud}) P(\theta_{fraud}), where h_t is the history of click-conversion pairs. The system’s stability requires that the associate’s discount factor \gamma < 1 makes long-horizon fraud less attractive than honest performance-based referrals [PAGE 1].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: the referral logger resides on Web-server infrastructure subject to uptime U \in [0,1]. If U = 0 (data-center outage, power failure, or network severance), the click-capture operator \mathcal{L} cannot record s(t_0); attribution is vetoed at the source regardless of link validity. Network layer: the patent operates over TCP/IP/HTTP [PAGE 2]; packet-loss rate p_{loss} > 0 can corrupt the referral token in the URL query string, turning a valid \alpha into a null or mismatched identifier. Biomechanical layer: human click latency \Delta t_{click} typically falls in 200–600 ms, whereas automated scripts click at \Delta t < 50 ms; timing analysis provides a biomechanical veto that flags non-human referral generation. Weather layer: meteorological events do not alter the protocol grammar, but regional storms raise P(stay\_home) and therefore p_{click}; conversely, a hurricane disabling a data-center region imposes a physical veto that cascades to zero referral volume for that geography.

Adjudication of the “single check-out” benefit [PAGE 1]: the patent assumes that once items are selected, a single check-out completes the purchase. This assumption is vetoed at the biomechanical layer by cart abandonment—if the user closes the browser before checkout, \chi = 0 and \Gamma = \varnothing, voiding commission despite a valid referral. It is further vetoed at the physical layer if the merchant’s order-processing subsystem (shipping, collections, customer service) is down [PAGE 1], because the merchant’s back-end must execute the crediting; the associate’s front-end link is necessary but not sufficient. No weather veto can overturn a valid \Gamma = \alpha attribution, but weather-induced infrastructure failure can prevent the crediting event from occurring at all.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Cookie-clearance counterfactual): A user clicks Associate \alpha’s link at t_0, clears all browser cookies at t_1, and completes purchase at t_2 > t_1. Veto rule: because s(t_2) = \varnothing, \Gamma(\varnothing, 1) = \varnothing; commission is zero USD. Verifiability: server logs must show absence of the referrer cookie at checkout while the access log records the t_0 click. Prediction: attribution failure occurs with probability \rho_{clear}, the measured cookie-clearance rate; the system under-rewards \alpha by \mathbb{E}[C_\alpha] \cdot \rho_{clear}.

Scenario 2 (Self-referral counterfactual): Associate \alpha registers buyer account \beta and purchases via \alpha’s own referral link from the same device fingerprint f. Veto rule: fraud linkage operator \Phi(f_\alpha, f_\beta) = 1 triggers account coalescence; \Gamma is suppressed and \mathbb{E}[C_\alpha] is reversed to 0 USD. Verifiability: audit banned associate accounts for shared fingerprints, IP blocks, or banking details. Prediction: honest associates are vetoed at the false-positive rate \epsilon_{FP}; if \epsilon_{FP} exceeds the appeal throughput, legitimate churn increases.

Scenario 3 (Multi-associate unified-cart counterfactual): User clicks Associate A’s link for item X, then Associate B’s link for item Y; the unified cart contains X + Y and checks out once. Veto rule: under last-touch attribution, \Gamma assigns the entire cart to B only; A receives 0 USD despite causing the discovery of X. Verifiability: run a randomized attribution experiment where cart credit is split proportionally versus last-touch, and measure A’s referral volume change. Prediction: A’s measured return-on-referral underestimates true causal contribution by the factor P(X \mid A) / [P(X \mid A) + P(Y \mid B)]; if true, A reduces link placement for discovery-oriented content, degrading the merchant’s top-of-funnel exposure.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
