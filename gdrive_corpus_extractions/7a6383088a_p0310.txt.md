If \(d_t\) is reduced faster than the fear decay time constant \(\tau_f\), the indicator term is violated by terror responses and sensitization replaces unconditioning.

6. Mechanical veto variable for the spider. Let \(M \in \{0,1\}\) denote the biomechanical capacity to drop off the web (dragline release, web tension, gravitational loading \(g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\)). Observed behavior:
\[
B_t = \mathcal{E}_t(\text{injury} \mid \text{stay}) \cdot M_t.
\]
If \(M_t = 0\) (e.g., injury prevents locomotion, or platform height exceeds safe dragline deployment), dropping is physically vetoed even if \(\mathcal{E}_t\) predicts it. [PAGE 1] does not specify platform height or web integrity, so this physical veto is an unexamined boundary condition.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage of the claims on [PAGE 1], with attention to multi-week traps.

The page sits at a convergence of three intellectual streams. Stream one: Watsonian behaviorism. Watson’s 1913 behaviorist manifesto and the Little Albert study (Watson & Rayner, 1920) established that specific fears could be acquired by pairing a neutral stimulus with a fear-evolving stimulus. Tolman cites this position on [PAGE 1] when he notes that, according to Watson, fears such as Peter’s rabbit fear are acquired through conditioning. Stream two: the Jones lineage. Mary Cover Jones’s 1924 Pedagogical Seminary study of Peter (cited in footnote 14 on [PAGE 1]) and her 1925 and 1928 papers demonstrate the gradual disappearance of fear through paired feeding and systematic approximation. This is the direct ancestor of Wolpe’s systematic desensitization (1950s) and modern exposure therapy, though Tolman in 1932 frames it narrowly as “docility of fright” within purposive behaviorism, not as a clinical technique. Stream three: Tolman’s own purposive behaviorism. The sign-gestalt-expectation is Tolman’s alternative to mechanistic S–R theory; it posits that the organism expects a sign to point to an outcome, a cognitive molar construct that anticipates Tolman’s later cognitive-map work (1948) and the Rescorla-Wagner expectancy model (1972).

Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy. Trap A: the spacing trap. Jones’s procedure on [PAGE 1] occurs over “a whole series of subsequent occasions” with no specified inter-trial interval. If sessions are massed (hours apart), observed fear reduction may reflect short-term habituation rather than durable unconditioning; if spaced over weeks, spontaneous recovery and context change can produce apparent relapse, falsely suggesting the procedure failed. Trap B: the innate/acquired trap. The footnote on [PAGE 1] dismisses the origin of Peter’s fear as irrelevant to the argument, but this is a multi-week trap because ethological preparedness (later formalized by Seligman, 1971) implies that fears of “strangeness and wigglingnesses” may re-emerge after apparent extinction if the conditioned stimulus matches an evolved fear template. A one-session or short-series success on [PAGE 1] cannot rule out delayed relapse. Trap C: the retrocausal clinical trap. Later histories read Jones’s 1924 study backward as the origin of systematic desensitization, imposing a teleology of “fear removal” that Tolman does not endorse on [PAGE 1]. Tolman uses Peter to illustrate the malleability of fright under purposive sign-gestalt control, not to establish a therapy protocol. Confusing these frameworks creates an anachronistic interpretation that the text itself resists by keeping the discussion at the level of experimental logic rather than clinical outcome.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock, applied to the spider and child cases on [PAGE 1].

Crisis habit regression. Under acute threat, an organism regresses to the dominant habit in its hierarchy. For Tolman’s spider, the sudden loud noise triggers a sign-gestalt-expectation of injury, and the spider regresses to dropping off the web as the default crisis habit. For Jones’s child, the rabbit elicits a regressive crisis habit of active terror (screaming, withdrawal) that competes with the feeding response. Recursive theory of mind enters because the experimenter must model the spider or child as modeling the world: the spider “expects” that noise → injury; the child learns that rabbit + food → safety. The experimenter’s theory of the subject’s mind is therefore second-order: the subject has an expectation, and the experimenter manipulates the contingency to test whether that expectation is genuinely representational or merely reflexive.

Counter-trap. Tolman’s reverse complementary experiment is a counter-trap set against his own initial interpretation. If the spider learns the reverse contingency (drop → freedom; stay → injury) and persists in dropping under the reverse regime, the original dropping is confirmed as expectancy-driven rather than motor-fixed. If, however, the spider persists in dropping regardless of contingency—dropping even when staying is now safe—the counter-trap springs shut on Tolman’s account: the behavior is not a sign-gestalt-expectation but a fixed reflex or habit insensitive to outcome reversal. The same counter-trap logic applies to Peter: if fear returns when food is withdrawn or when the rabbit is presented without warning, the “unconditioning” was suppression, not genuine expectation change.

Clock. The clock is the temporal parameter governing both cases. In the spider experiment, the clock is the inter-trial interval between noise exposures and injury deliveries; if too short, the spider cannot encode the contingency; if too long, the sign–outcome association decays. In Jones’s procedure, the clock has two hands: (i) the rate of spatial approximation (how quickly the rabbit cage is moved nearer) and (ii) the inter-session interval. The clock must be synchronized to the child’s fear-decay time constant \(\tau_f\) and satiety-recovery time constant \(\tau_s\). If the rabbit is moved closer before \(f_t\) has decayed below \(d_{\text{thresh}}\), the clock runs too fast and the child re-traumatizes, triggering crisis habit regression. If feeding occurs too frequently or to satiety, \(v(F)\) declines and the clock runs too slow, because the appetitive unconditioned stimulus loses incentive value. Tolman’s narrative on [PAGE 1] presents the procedure as a smooth monotonic sequence, but the clock parameters are unspecified, leaving open whether the observed fondling and eating reflect true expectancy change or merely a temporarily well-timed suppression of crisis habits.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proofs for the experiments described on [PAGE 1].

Physical/biomechanical layer (spider). Dropping off a web is not cost-free. The spider must overcome attachment forces and deploy dragline silk under gravitational acceleration \(g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2\). Let \(h\) be the platform height and \(h_{\text{max}}\) the maximum safe drop height determined by silk tensile strength and anchor geometry. If \(h > h_{\text{max}}\), or if the spider’s locomotor apparatus is injured such that it cannot release the dragline, the dropping response is mechanically vetoed: \(B_t = 0\) regardless of \(\mathcal{E}_t(\text{injury} \mid \text{stay})\). [PAGE 1] provides no platform height, silk properties, or injury severity, so this biomechanical veto is an unconstrained boundary condition. If the spider cannot drop, Tolman’s interpretation of dropping as an expectancy-driven choice cannot be tested.

Physiological layer (child). Food functions as the unconditioned stimulus in Jones’s procedure only if it carries incentive value. Let blood glucose \(G\) and insulin state determine \(v(F) \in [0,1]\). If the child is sated (\(G\) above threshold, \(v(F) \approx 0\)), pairing the rabbit with food produces no counter-conditioning because the US is devalued. The feeding component is vetoed by homeostatic state, and the gradual approximation described on [PAGE 1]—“the boy was actually fondling the animal with one hand, and eating with the other”—cannot proceed. Hunger state is not reported on [PAGE 1], making this a physiological blind spot.

Environmental/weather layer (auditory and visual signaling). The tuning fork’s loud noise must exceed the spider’s auditory detection threshold. Let \(L_{\text{signal}}\) be the sound pressure level in dB SPL and \(L_{\text{ambient}}\) the background laboratory or field noise. Detection requires \(L_{\text{signal}} - L_{\text{ambient}} > \Delta L_{\text{crit}}\). If traffic, HVAC, or weather (wind, rain) masks the tuning fork, the sign S is not detected and \(I(S; O) = 0\), vetoing the sign-gestalt-expectation of injury at the physical layer. Similarly, the rabbit’s “somewhat strange appearance and uncertain activities” and its “wigglingness” [PAGE 1] are visual CS components that depend on luminance, contrast, and motion cues; poor lighting or visual occlusion vetoes the CS and prevents the unconditioning contingency from being established.

Adjudication: No veto is triggered by the text itself, because [PAGE 1] omits all physical and physiological parameters. A rigorous replication must measure \(h\), \(M_t\), \(v(F)\), \(L_{\text{signal}} - L_{\text{ambient}}\), and \(d_t(t)\) before attributing behavior to sign-gestalt-expectation or unconditioning.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards / counterfactual veto) scenarios with explicit predictions. Each scenario is designed to veto Tolman’s sign-gestalt-expectation account or Jones’s unconditioning account if the predicted null result obtains.

Scenario 1: Non-contingent injury in the reverse spider design.
Setup: Run the reverse complementary experiment from [PAGE 1] but deliver injury on a random schedule independent of staying or dropping, so that \(p(\text{injury} \mid \text{stay}) = p(\text{injury} \mid \text{drop})\). This sets \(I(S; O) = 0\) by construction.
Veto condition: If the spider does not shift its staying/dropping distribution relative to a yoked control receiving identical random injury, the sign-gestalt-expectation account is vetoed because no contingency information exists to support an expectation of injury from staying.
Prediction: Behavior remains at baseline (no adaptive shift). Any observed shift under random injury would indicate superstitious adjunctive behavior rather than sign-gestalt-expectation, falsifying Tolman’s specific interpretation.

Scenario 2: Satiation veto in Peter’s unconditioning.
Setup: Replicate Jones’s gradual approximation but feed the child ad libitum immediately before each session so that \(v(F) \approx 0\) throughout. Keep the distance-approximation schedule identical to the original.
Veto condition: If the child fails to progress from terror to fondling and eating despite identical spatial steps, the unconditioning account is vetoed at the physiological layer because the appetitive US is devalued.
Prediction: Fear \(f_t\) does not decay; \(d_t\) cannot be reduced below \(d_{\text{thresh}}\) without eliciting active terror. Successful approach only after food deprivation confirms that the original result depended on \(v(F) > 0\), not on a pure sign-gestalt shift.

Scenario 3: Context renewal veto after delayed test.
Setup: Conduct unconditioning in Cage A (laboratory, white walls, experimenter present) until the child fondles the rabbit and eats. After a one-week delay, test in Cage B (different room, novel olfactory and visual context) at the original close distance.
Veto condition: If fear returns (active terror, withdrawal) in Cage B despite successful approach in Cage A, the claim that fear was “gradually overcome” is vetoed; the change is context-specific inhibition, not general unconditioning of the sign-gestalt-expectation.
Prediction: Renewal of fear in B. A null renewal result (no fear in B) would support genuine expectancy change; a positive renewal result supports the counter-trap that crisis habits are suppressed in the training context but recur under contextual shift. All three scenarios are verifiable: contingency can be randomized, satiety can be manipulated by pre-feeding, and context can be switched with delayed post-testing.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
