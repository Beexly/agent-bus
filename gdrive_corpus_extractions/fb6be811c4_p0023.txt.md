<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto-proof architecture must evaluate three independent layers before accepting a refined pose.

Physical layer (optics/sensor): shutter speed, sensor resolution, stadium illumination, precipitation, and lens glare set a lower bound on observable keypoint noise σ_sensor (px). If the estimated residual || n_i ||₂ falls below σ_sensor, the measurement is physically implausible and the frame is vetoed as sensor artifact; if motion blur during exposure exceeds one keypoint spacing, the pose estimate is biased and vetoed regardless of ML confidence.

Biomechanical layer (human constraints): limb-length ratios are bounded by population anthropometry, r ∈ [r_phys⁻, r_phys⁺], and joint angles are bounded by anatomical ranges of motion (radians). Any refined pose whose corrected ratio \hat r violates these bounds, or whose joint configuration implies impossible angular velocities given Δt, is vetoed. The “abnormal length ratio” correction on [PAGE 1] must therefore be a projection, not an extrapolation: \hat r = Π_[r_phys⁻, r_phys⁺](r_raw + Δr_θ).

Weather/environment layer: rain, fog, and stadium lighting changes modulate the effective point-spread function and thus Σ_k. If Σ_k grows beyond a pre-calibrated ceiling (px²), the Mahalanobis term in Eq. (2) dominates and the ML prior is down-weighted; beyond a second ceiling, the entire frame is vetoed and the previous temporally filtered pose is held out rather than refined.

Adjudication rule: define layer vetoes V_phys, V_bio, V_env ∈ {0,1}. The accepted pose \tilde P is emitted only if V_phys = V_bio = V_env = 0. If any layer vetoes, the output is flagged as low-confidence and the downstream broadcast graphic (speed, distance, or position overlay) is suppressed or reverted to the last valid tracklet state. No single layer’s confidence score can override another layer’s hard veto, which prevents the ML model on [PAGE 1] from talking its way out of a physical or biomechanical impossibility.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
