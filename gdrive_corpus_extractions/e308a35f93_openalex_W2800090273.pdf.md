**Mapping:** "retrocausal genealogy" is read strictly as technical lineage — which later results the paper's assumptions depend on, and which later work the paper pre-conditions.

*Backward lineage.* The distortion half descends from the overconstrained linear radial-distortion estimators of Steele & Jaynes [11, PAGE 6] and the multi-view improvements of Lakemond, Fookes & Sridharan [10, PAGE 6], with the non-square-pencil generalised-eigenvalue machinery from Boutry et al. [12, PAGE 6]. The feature half inherits SURF [7] and DAISY [8], both cited on PAGE 6; the panorama framing inherits Brown & Lowe [9] — which the paper deliberately *does not* adopt for correspondence pruning ("a probability model [9] is not employed here" [PAGE 3]), a conscious rollback of a 2007-state-of-the-art operator in favour of a temporal-linear K-D tree. The optimisation and multi-view formalism inherits Hartley & Zisserman [13]. The task lineage is Han et al. [1] for tennis, Hu et al. [2] for basketball, Lu et al. [3] for ICP-propagated broadcast tracking, Sha et al. [6] for manually registered swimming — i.e., a decade of key-frame-first architectures.

*Forward pre-conditioning.* The paper's own stated limitation seeds the next generation of methods: "Compared to a semi automatic analytical such as [3], the proposed approach is less accurate, however it is fully automatic and places no requirements on the coverage of individual frames" [PAGE 5]. That accuracy/automation trade is exactly the gap later data-driven pitch-keypoint regressors and learned line-segment detectors target; this paper is the explicit "fully automatic, lower accuracy" waypoint on that trajectory.

*Long-horizon traps that persist across study cycles.*
1. **The key-frame habit.** The field's default assumption — that one frame must see the full pitch — survives the paper only rhetorically; the evaluation still requires sequences that "sweep the whole playing area to ensure that the videos contain enough information for camera calibration" [PAGE 4]. The habit was relocated from the frame to the *clip*, not eliminated.
2. **The self-consistency metric trap.** Reporting RMS against one's own bundle-adjusted inliers (Table 1) remains the dominant evaluation idiom in this literature, and it is structurally incapable of detecting a systematically biased H.
3. **The shared-parameter trap.** Assuming λ is constant across a pair is defensible at 25 fps and indefensible at longer baselines; the paper flags it honestly ("this is not strictly correct" [PAGE 3]) but downstream reuse of the operator rarely carries the caveat.
4. **The anchor-as-tail trap.** Choosing "the last frame of a video clip" [PAGE 4] as anchor is a right-to-left causal convention with no invariance test. Every frame's reported coordinates inherit a directional bias that was never measured.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Mapping:** this section is read as a *self-model audit* — what the estimator implicitly assumes about its own reference frame, and where that self-model is circular.

*The circularity.* The panorama's coordinate system is defined by the anchor frame; the anchor frame is in turn defined as a member of the panorama. Frames are calibrated from the panorama, and the panorama is refined from the frames (Eq. 5). This is a fixed-point system, and its stability rests entirely on the LM refinement plus the gauge choice. The paper never states the fixed-point condition or reports the residual at convergence per sequence.

*Crisis habit regression.* The inherited crisis behaviour is key-frame search; when it fails the literature regresses to manual intervention — "Sha et.al [6] manually located reference points" [PAGE 2], and [3] "manually specifies the homogrpahy in the first frame of the video and derives other frames' location using ICP" [PAGE 5]. The paper's counter-move is a *change of the object of calibration*: "This system calibrates each individual frame through calibrating the panorama generated from these frames. It utilizes the holistic structure of the frames in a video rather than searching a key frame in isolation" [PAGE 5]. That is the counter-trap: refuse the search, redefine the reference.

*The clock.* The temporal ordering assumption is the clock that replaces probabilistic correspondence pruning: "consecutive frames have a clear temporal order" [PAGE 3]. A clock failure — dropped frames, variable frame rate, slow-motion inserts, or replay cuts spliced into the clip — silently mis-registers the K-D-tree chain with no error signal, because the linear registration has no global consistency check until Eq. 5, and Eq. 5 will happily fit a wrong chain to itself.

*Epistemic blindness.* The algorithm emits a point estimate and a scalar residual; it emits no covariance, no per-frame confidence, and no rejection flag. It therefore cannot represent "this frame's coordinate is unreliable." The counter-trap available to it is explicit: propagate \(\Sigma_j=\hat\sigma^2 (J_j^{\top}J_j)^{-1}\) through the chain composition and gate the downstream output on the trace of the world-space covariance. The paper's own admission that "the resultant panorama does contain some small visual artifacts that are caused by small misalignments in the panorama construction. The misalignments propagate to the final perspective mapping" [PAGE 5] is precisely a statement that this propagation is unmodelled.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Mapping:** a "veto" here is a physically grounded condition under which the pipeline's output must be rejected regardless of its residual.

*Optical veto (lens layer).* The division model has one free parameter and a hard-coded COD at the image centre: "we assume that the Center of Distortion (COD) is in the center of the frames" [PAGE 3]. Real wide-angle optics have decentering distortion, which the model cannot represent, so a mis-centred lens produces a systematic, radially asymmetric bias that the fit will absorb into H rather than into λ. **Veto proof:** if the recovered \(|\hat\lambda|\) is within a factor of two of \(8.24\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{px}^{-2}\), the singularity circle has entered the frame and the correction is non-physical; the output must be discarded.

*Sensor/motion veto.* On a 1920×1080 single camera panning across a pitch — "a moderate range of rotations and zooms" [PAGE 4] — rolling-shutter skew is a per-row homography, not a global one. The global-H model is misspecified during fast pans, and the residual will rise gently rather than catastrophically, hiding the violation.

*Biomechanical veto (players as structured outliers).* RANSAC assumes outliers are independent. Players are not: they cluster around the ball and along the pitch's long axis, and a cluster of players can supply a collinear set of "correspondences" that supports a degenerate H. Fig. 2's claim that "the outlier correspondences that usually appear on players have been filtered by RANSAC" [PAGE 3] is evidence of typical behaviour, not of a guarantee. **Veto proof:** for a minimal 4-point sample at inlier ratio w = 0.30, the probability of at least one clean sample in N iterations is \(1-(1-w^4)^N\); reaching 99% confidence requires N ≈ 567, and nothing in the paper indicates such a budget.

*Weather/environment veto (segmentation layer).* The playing-area extraction depends on green dominance: "the color of the playing area in pitch-like sports games is usually green and is typically much different to the surrounding area" [PAGE 4]. Low sun produces long shadows whose CIELAB a* coordinate drops toward neutral; wet or worn turf shifts toward brown; snow and standing water invert the contrast entirely. Floodlight flicker at 100/120 Hz imposes per-frame photometric change on a 25 fps sequence, perturbing DAISY descriptors between the very frames being matched. Each of these is a veto on the *boundary*, and a biased boundary propagates directly into the four-corner DLT.

*Geometric veto (the 180° arc).* Stated plainly: "As our technique is mapping images to a plane in front of the camera, we can only capture the portion of the field in a 180 degree arc in front of the camera" [PAGE 5], and "the near end of the field cannot be captured by our model" [PAGE 5]. Formally, the pitch plane's line at infinity maps to \(\mathbf{l}_\infty' = \mathbf{H}^{-\top}(0,0,1)^{\top}\); ground points at azimuth → ±π/2 map arbitrarily close to it. **Veto proof:** any emitted coordinate whose homogeneous scale factor has crossed zero, or which falls outside the 91.40 m × 55.00 m field-hockey rectangle, is a projective artefact and must be rejected rather than clipped.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Veto 3 — horizon/arc leakage (domain falsification).**
*Procedure:* construct or select frames in which a player occupies the near end of the pitch, beyond the "180 degree arc in front of the camera" [PAGE 5] — precisely the region the authors say "cannot be captured by our model" [PAGE 5]. *Prediction:* a correct implementation returns a coordinate with negative homogeneous scale or a value outside the 91.40 × 55.00 m rectangle, and thus a rejection; a leaking implementation returns a plausible-looking in-bounds coordinate generated by the projective fold.
*Veto condition:* reward = 0 if any point physically behind the camera horizon is emitted inside the pitch rectangle without a rejection flag.
