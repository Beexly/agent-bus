**Level assignment.** Level 0: the player's motor intent (which shot the athlete is attempting). Level 1: the coach's belief about that intent. Level 2: the system's estimate of the coach's belief — this is where the paper claims to operate: *"Provides objective and data-driven assessment, minimizing human bias and improving evaluation accuracy."* [PAGE 5]. **Crisis habit regression.** Under pressure, a player reverts to the dominant motor program; the classifier exhibits the exact analogue. A ResNet-50 fine-tuned on five classes of professional footage, presented with out-of-distribution input (amateur hall, different flooring, different camera height), maps its feature evidence toward the decision boundary and its softmax posterior collapses toward the class prior:

(2) **Annotator-recursion trap.** If the 92.5% figure rests on labels produced by coaches whose beliefs were themselves shaped by earlier versions of this tool, the ground truth is a fixed point of the model, not of the sport. (3) **Deployment-recursion trap.** Coaches act on tactical heatmaps [PAGE 5], players adapt, and next season's distribution differs from training. The system's own recommendations are a causal agent in its future test set — a closed loop with no correction term. The **adaptation clock** — how fast a player actually re-patterns a motor habit — is on the order of 3–6 weeks of deliberate practice. The system's output cadence (per-shot metrics) is 10⁴× faster than the adaptation clock it is supposed to serve, which means the binding constraint on coaching value is **not** pipeline latency but the causal distance between a heatmap and a motor change. **Veto A — Macro-F1 collapse under a non-orthogonal label space.**
