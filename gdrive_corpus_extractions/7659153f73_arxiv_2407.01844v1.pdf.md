<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Recursive theory of mind here is not psychological speculation; it is the nested optimization in the Bayes-Nash equilibrium. Player \(i\) chooses \(x_i\) by maximizing \(U_i(x_i,x^*_{-i})\), while conjecturing \(x^*_{-i}\) and updating beliefs \(P(A_j\mid x_i,x^*_{-i})\). The mechanism’s key recursive move is to make the optimal vote independent of the belief parameters that encode player \(i\)’s model of others. The paper states that the transfer terms “guarantee the independence of the optimum vote vector” from \(P^{(0)}_{ij}\) and \(p_i\) [PAGE 7]. Thus, at equilibrium, \(x^*_{ij}=\frac{1}{2a}(u_{ij}-\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{r\ne j}u_{ir})\) depends on the player’s own valuation and \(m\), not on her higher-order beliefs about others’ beliefs. This is a counter-trap against strategic belief manipulation: a player cannot gain by misreporting her model of other players because the equilibrium vote does not depend on that model.

Crisis habit regression: when a decentralized organization faces a Sybil crisis, the habitual responses are identity checks, proof-of-personhood, one-person-one-vote, or coin voting. The source directly warns that one-person-one-vote is “neither SA-proof nor efficient” [PAGE 1] and that coin voting is SA-proof but not efficient [PAGE 1, PAGE 3]. A crisis-driven regression to those habits therefore sacrifices the paper’s efficiency target even if it restores some Sybil resistance.

Counter-trap: the Sybil attacker reasons recursively about whether splitting into \(w\) envelopes increases total utility. The mechanism’s counter is the collective-harm term: “The collective harm is reflected in the second term of equation (9) since it depends on the number of participants \(h\) through the parameter \(a\). This non-negative term of the utility function decreases when \(h\) increases.” [PAGE 8] In other words, the attacker’s individual benefit is dominated by the reduction in the shared quadratic benefit term as \(h\) rises. The proof models the attacker as “being \(w\) different participants playing optimally with valuations that add up to the original valuation” [PAGE 11]. That is the recursive counter-trap: the attacker’s best response is bounded by the same utility function that rewards honest participation.
