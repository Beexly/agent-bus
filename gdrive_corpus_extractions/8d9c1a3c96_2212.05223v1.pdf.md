The method descends from SMPL (Loper et al. 2015, ACM TOG) [PAGE 3], which supplies the low-dimensional statistical human prior β ∈ R¹⁰, θ ∈ R⁷² and the differentiable vertex map v = S(θ, β). It inherits the iterative error feedback (IEF) regression scheme from Kanazawa et al. (2018, CVPR) [PAGE 5], extended here to three passes. Volumetric triangulation traces to Iskakov et al. (2019, ICCV) [PAGE 3-4], which made unprojection of 2D heatmaps into a shared 3D cube differentiable. Rotation continuity relies on Zhou et al. (2019b, CVPR) [PAGE 5], replacing Euler angles with a continuous 6D representation to avoid discontinuities during \hat{R}_{w→h} learning. Benchmark lineage includes Human3.6M (Ionescu et al. 2013, PAMI) [PAGE 4, 8], TotalCapture (Trumble et al. 2017, BMVC) [PAGE 4], and SkiPose (Spoerri 2016; Rhodin et al. 2018) [PAGE 4, 8], the latter involving eight moving PTZ cameras recording alpine slalom.

Multi-week deployment traps arise from temporal distribution shift. The synthetic priors are sampled from MoCap, UP-3D, 3DPW, and Human3.6M subjects S1, S5, S6, S7, S8 [PAGE 4]; these are fixed at training time and do not adapt to new clothing, lighting, or snow conditions over weeks. The test-time 2D detectors Keypoint-RCNN and DensePose-RCNN are frozen weights [PAGE 4]; if deployed across seasons, detector drift silently corrupts J_n and M_n before they ever reach the volumetric module. Because the model is a single static set of parameters tested on multiple datasets [PAGE 7], there is no online recalibration of \hat{R}_{h→cn} or \hat{T}_{h→cn} when physical cameras are bumped or zoom settings drift, as occurs with PTZ rigs in SkiPose. The consistency weighting in Eq. 11-13 can mask detector bias only if errors are view-independent; systematic detector failure under, for example, heavy snow occlusion produces correlated errors across views and is treated as consistent evidence, causing confident wrong meshes. No uncertainty head or out-of-distribution veto is specified beyond the consistency normalizer, which itself divides by Σ_n V(φ_n; ·) and therefore becomes unstable precisely when all views disagree.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under sensor degradation, occlusion, or domain gap, the regressor regresses to its prior habit, Θ0, the reposed pose and mean shape initialization [Eq. 10, PAGE 5]. If the intersection occupancy V_I^O is empty because no view sees the person consistently, V_I^F = 0 and Θ1 = Θ0; the model falls back to the statistical mean rather than hallucinating geometry. This is the crisis habit: under ambiguity, output the average human.

Counter-trap: the progressive consensus-diversity-consistency mechanism is designed to counter over-reliance on any single biased view. Consensus (intersection) restricts attention to mutually visible volume; diversity (union) tolerates single-view calibration bias [PAGE 5]; consistency weighting down-weights views whose rendered mask and joints disagree with input [Eq. 11-13, PAGE 5-6]. However, this counter-trap is recursive: it judges consistency using rendered outputs of the current mesh estimate against the same noisy 2D detections that fed the estimate. If detections carry a shared bias (e.g., Keypoint-RCNN systematically misplaces knees on snowy terrain), the consistency map may reinforce the error rather than reject it, because \hat{J}_n and J_n are drawn from the same corrupted distribution.

Clock: the system has two clocks. The inference clock is the three-iteration IEF regressor chain Θ0 → Θ1 → Θ2 → Θ3 [PAGE 5-6]; there is no further test-time refinement, so the latency budget is fixed by three forward passes. The training clock is 6 epochs at lr 1e−4, batch 16, approximately three days on one A100 GPU [PAGE 4]. This fixed training horizon means the synthetic domain prior is frozen after deployment; the model cannot learn from new in-the-wild camera geometries encountered in the field. The operational clock mismatch is acute for SkiPose PTZ, where cameras rotate and zoom at video rates [PAGE 4], yet the pipeline consumes static multi-view images with no temporal buffer, causing aliasing when skier pose changes between the rig’s capture and the model’s assumption of a consistent canonical human space.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: perspective projection Π is singular when a joint lies at the camera center or depth approaches zero; volumetric triangulation assumes the human fits inside the L×L×L cube discretized at G³ voxels. If the subject exits the cube, V_n^O = 0 for all views, intersection and union vanish, and the regressor receives zero signal. Grid resolution imposes a quantization floor: pelvis position error from argmax over voxels scales with L/G [Eq. 3, PAGE 4], so coarse G yields millimetre-scale bias inconsistent with the 53.8 mm MPJPE target on H3.6M. Memory cost is O(G³C), bounding feasible G on a single A100.

Biomechanical layer veto: SMPL pose uses axis-angle θ_j for 23 joints plus global θ_g [PAGE 3]. The learned \hat{R}_{w→h} is a continuous 6D vector converted to a rotation matrix, a mapping that is continuous in 6D space but discontinuous in Euler angle space [Zhou et al. 2019b via PAGE 5], creating gimbal-like singularities near specific orientations. SMPL enforces blend-skin kinematics but not hard joint-limit constraints; the regressor can emit anatomically impossible θ_j if volumetric features are ambiguous. Because global orientation is estimated per view in calibration [Eq. 4] while joint rotations are shared across views [PAGE 5], inconsistent per-view global rotations produce a non-rigid mesh unless the consistency weighting resolves them.

Weather/environmental layer veto: training is purely synthetic [PAGE 1-2] and lacks snow, motion blur, rain, or extreme cold. SkiPose PTZ captures alpine slalom with moving cameras [PAGE 4], so environmental appearance shift is borne entirely by the 2D detectors and the synthetic-to-real proxy gap. The paper claims intermediate representations reduce this gap [PAGE 1], but Keypoint-RCNN/DensePose-RCNN are not retrained for snow, so detector failure becomes a veto condition that the mesh regressor cannot override.

Veto proof conditions: (i) ε > 0 in Eq. 11 is mandatory; if ε = 0 and a view perfectly matches (\hat{M}_n = M_n and \hat{J}_n = J_n), the denominator is zero and \bar{V}_n^φ is undefined [PAGE 5]. (ii) Σ_n V(φ_n; ·) > 0 is required for normalization in Eq. 12; if all views are maximally inconsistent, view weights collapse and Eq. 13 has no defined distribution [PAGE 6]. (iii) V_I^O ≠ ∅ is required for non-trivial Θ1; otherwise Θ1 = Θ0 [PAGE 5]. (iv) N ≥ 2 for multi-view advantage; N = 1 makes intersection equal union and removes the consensus/diversity distinction [PAGE 5]. (v) Human must remain inside the volumetric cube; otherwise all V_n^O are empty.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 — total 2D detection failure. Set all input heatmaps J_n and masks M_n to zero for every view. Then O_n = 0, V_I^O = V_U^O = 0, V_I^F = V_U^F = 0, and by Eq. 10 the regressor output collapses to Θ0, the reposed mean-shape initialization [PAGE 5]. Verifiable prediction: on Human3.6M Protocol 1, MPJPE will equal the distance between mean pose and ground truth, substantially exceeding the reported 53.8 mm [PAGE 6], and PMPJPE will remain high because rigid alignment cannot fix pose-shape prior mismatch. A passing system must emit Θ0 or a detectable failure flag, not a plausible mesh.

Veto scenario 2 — adversarial pelvis translation offset. Perturb \hat{T}_{w→h,w} from Eq. 3 by a displacement δ larger than one voxel side length L/G before computing \hat{T}_{h→cn} in Eq. 4 [PAGE 4]. This misaligns the canonical human cube relative to the true body, so V_n^F samples empty or off-body voxels. Verifiable prediction: reprojection consistency φ_n will be low for every view because the rendered mesh \hat{v} will not project onto the input detections [Eq. 11, PAGE 5], causing \bar{V}_n^φ to flatten or become unstable under Eq. 12 normalization [PAGE 6]. MPJPE on TotalCapture unseen subjects S4,5 should degrade superlinearly beyond the reported mean 64.2 mm [PAGE 7], and on SkiPose PTZ PCK@150 mm should fall below 86.0 % [PAGE 7], while view weights cease to discriminate between cameras.

Veto scenario 3 — consistency normalizer singularity. Set ε = 0 and supply synthetically perfect inputs where \hat{M}_n = M_n and \hat{J}_n = J_n for all n. Then φ_n = 1 / 0 for every view and Σ_n V(φ_n; ·) = 0, making Eq. 12 undefined [PAGE 5-6]. Verifiable prediction: training on one A100 will produce NaN gradients in the consistency-weighted fusion layer within the first epoch and the 6-epoch schedule [PAGE 4] will fail to converge; loss will not reach the regime supporting Table 1 results (MPJPE 53.8 mm on H3.6M) [PAGE 6]. With any ε > 0, training remains finite; with ε = 0 but imperfect inputs, gradients explode as the denominator approaches zero, so RLVR must veto ε = 0 configurations and enforce a lower bound ε ≥ 1e−6.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
