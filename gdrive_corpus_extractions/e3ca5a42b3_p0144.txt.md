**1. The Separability Asymmetry Operator**

$$ \Sigma(a) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} P(\text{Transfer}(a) \mid S_i(t), S_i(t+\Delta t)) $$
For pure property $p$, Knight argues $\Sigma(p) \approx 1$. For labor $l$, $\Sigma(l) \ll 1$ because labor supply is contingent on survival and health ($S_i$). The "false separation" error occurs when policymakers assume $\Sigma(l) = \Sigma(p)$, leading to invalid redistributive models that ignore the mortality/health risk borne exclusively by laborers.

**2. Information-Theoretic Deservit Metric**

Earned Income Condition: $I(O; E \mid Z)$ is maximized.

Knight's theorem: In any joint production system with stochastic shocks, $I(O; Z) > 0$. Thus, Earned $\neq$ Deserved. The "evil result" is the systematic misalignment of social status with actual causal contribution due to unmeasured $Z$.
