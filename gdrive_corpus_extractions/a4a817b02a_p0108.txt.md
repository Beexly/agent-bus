Here \(\mu_{\text{req}} = |F_{\text{horizontal}}|/|N|\) is the required shoe-turf friction during the plant; if the plant foot slips, the approach kinetic energy cannot be fully transferred into \(J_{\text{foot}}\). If \(d_k\) deviates by more than 0.30 m from the Yostian four feet [PAGE 94], the kicker cannot generate the assumed approach momentum within the same stride count, and the launch-speed prediction from \(J_{\max}=F_{\max}\tau_c\) collapses toward the static-strength singularity (\(d_k\to0\)).

Weather veto:
\[
V_{\text{weather}} = 
\begin{cases}
1, & \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}}\|_{\perp} > 5\,\text{m/s} \;\lor\; p_{\text{precip}} > 0\,\text{mm/min} \;\lor\; T_{\text{air}} < 0^\circ\text{C},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Under crosswind or precipitation, the drag-free range \(R = v_0^2\sin(2\theta)/g\) and vacuum trajectory fail; a quadratic-drag/Magnus model becomes necessary. Sub-freezing temperatures alter leather compliance and effective \(m_b\) distribution, violating the rigid-spheroid assumption.

Adjudication order: physical veto is evaluated first because holder constraint failure occurs at or before contact and renders all downstream biomechanical and aerodynamic parameters unidentifiable. Biomechanical veto is second, because approach and plant errors occur during the contact buildup. Weather veto is last because atmospheric effects act only post-launch and cannot rescue a kick whose initial condition was already corrupted by holder or kicker layer failure. When \(\mathbf{V}\neq\mathbf{0}\), the only defensible output is “model undefined under [PAGE 94] assumptions,” not a corrected point prediction.
