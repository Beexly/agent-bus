James states the conditional: if all connections among ideas could be interpreted as combinations of sense-data wrought into fixity from without, then experience in the common legitimate sense would be the sole fashioner of the mind [PAGE 630]. This is a strong identity claim: Mind = f(Sense-data history). The empirical school, James notes, has mainly contended that this interpretation holds. Before James's generation, "experience" meant individual experience only; by his day, it includes the experience of ancestors as well [PAGE 630]. This shift is crucial because it moves the relevant time-scale from ontogenetic weeks or months to phylogenetic generations, introducing a retro-causal tension: present mental shape is said to be caused by experiences the individual never had.

The third hop introduces Spencer's formulation, which James quotes at length [PAGE 630]. Spencer argues that inner cohesions are adjusted to outer persistences by accumulated experience of those outer persistences. He concedes that for reflex actions and instincts the experience-hypothesis "seems insufficient," but locates the insufficiency where "the evidence is beyond our reach" [PAGE 630]. Even there, he maintains, available facts point to automatic psychical connections resulting from registration of experiences continued for numberless generations [PAGE 630]. The gray zone is now explicit: Spencer admits an explanatory gap for instincts while refusing to abandon the experience principle, substituting an unobservable, transgenerational evidence base.

The fourth hop is quantitative. Spencer's principle admits that strengths of psychical relations are, other things equal, proportionate to the multiplication of experiences [PAGE 630]. The footnote emphasizes that the essence of the explanation is to "demand numerically froquont [frequent] experiences" [PAGE 630]. This introduces a frequency threshold: association strength is not merely a function of presence or absence of experience but of its numerical multiplicity. Yet James leaves open the breaking point: what counts as "numerically frequent"? How many generations? What happens when h = 0 but the association is present (instincts)? And what are the "absolutely indissoluble" relations exempted from experiential determination [PAGE 630]?

Gray-zone audit: (1) equivocation on "experience" between individual and ancestral registers; (2) unfalsifiability of ancestral registration because evidence is "beyond our reach"; (3) potential circularity—associations must be registered before they can constitute experience, yet some associations are presupposed to register anything; (4) the exempted class of indissoluble relations threatens the universality of the empiricist operator. These are the breakdown regimes that any formal model must treat as singularities rather than smooth extensions. The deeper trap is treating Spencer's "numberless generations" as an empirical variable when James's own text concedes the evidence is inaccessible, which converts a psychological hypothesis into an article of faith unless later pages (§207 context, pp. 641–2) supply an operational criterion [PAGE 630].
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be the set of elementary sense-data or ideas. Let \(h_{ij}(t) \in \mathbb{N}_0\) be the count of experienced co-occurrences between elements \(x_i, x_j \in \mathcal{X}\) up to time \(t\). Let \(A_{ij}(t) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) denote psychical association strength; if interpreted informationally, \(A_{ij}\) is measured in bits as mutual information \(I(X_i;X_j)\), otherwise as a dimensionless normalized strength in \([0,1]\).

Empiricist association operator (from James's conditional, [PAGE 630]):
\[
A^{\text{emp}}_{ij}(t) = \Phi\!\left( \{ (x_i(\tau), x_j(\tau)) \}_{\tau \le t} \right),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is a functional mapping co-occurrence history to fixation strength. Under Spencer's quantitative clause ([PAGE 630]):
\[
\left. \frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial h_{ij}} \right|_{\text{ceteris paribus}} = \kappa > 0,
\]
with units of association-strength per experience count (\(\text{experience}^{-1}\)).

Ancestral extension ([PAGE 630], Spencer): total effective experience
\[
H_{ij} = h^{\text{ind}}_{ij} + h^{\text{anc}}_{ij},
\]
where \(h^{\text{anc}}_{ij}\) aggregates experiences across \(G\) generations. The model predicts
\[
A_{ij} = g(H_{ij}), \qquad g'(H) > 0.
\]

Breakdown / singularity regimes:
1. Reflex/instinct regime: \(h^{\text{ind}}_{ij} \approx 0\) yet \(A_{ij}\) is high. Spencer admits the experience-hypothesis "seems insufficient" here and places evidence "beyond our reach" [PAGE 630]. The empiricist operator is singular because \(g(0)\) should be minimal but observed \(A_{ij} \gg 0\). A nativist seed term is required: \(A_{ij} = g(H_{ij}) + A_0\), with \(A_0 > 0\).
2. Absolutely indissoluble relations: James notes all psychical relations "save the absolutely indissoluble" are determined by experiences [PAGE 630]. Formally, for these relations \(\partial A_{ij}/\partial H_{ij} = 0\) and \(A_{ij}\) is invariant under experience—a hard veto on empiricism.
3. Frequency threshold: the footnote demands "numerically froquont [frequent] experiences" [PAGE 630]. Below a critical count \(H_c\), \(g(H) \approx 0\); the model exhibits a phase transition at \(H_c\), not a smooth linear regime.

Verbatim citations [PAGE 630]: "The supposition that the inner cohesions are adjusted to the outer persistences by accumulated experience of those outer persistences is in harmony with all our actual knowledge of mental phenomena." "It is agreed that all psychical relations, save the absolutely indissoluble, are determined by experiences." Footnote: "the essence of this explanation is to demand numerically froquont experiences" [OCR: frequent]. Assumptions: co-occurrence counts are observable; association strength is a single scalar; ancestral experiences are commensurable with individual ones. These assumptions fail exactly in the singularity regimes above.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage. The page sits inside a tradition beginning with Locke's tabula rasa and Hartley's associationist vibrations, carried through James Mill and the British empirical school, and radically extended by Spencer's Lamarckian claim that ancestral experience becomes inscribed in "inner cohesions" [PAGE 630]. James's own Principles (1890) straddles this inheritance: he accepts experience as a vera causa of thought-forms while developing a psychology of habit that resists purely mechanistic associationism. The footnote's pointer to pp. 641–2 [PAGE 630] signals that James will later complicate the numerical-frequency thesis, likely in his discussion of reasoning and belief formation.

Multi-week traps. (1) Linear-accumulation trap: assuming association strength grows monotonically with weeks of repetition. James explicitly says experience "often undoes her own work" [PAGE 630], so ontogenetic learning is non-monotonic; a multi-week intervention can produce regression rather than consolidation. (2) Timescale trap: conflating Spencer's "numberless generations" of ancestral registration with the individual's weeks of practice. The former is phylogenetic, the latter ontogenetic; applying a generational clock to a week-long protocol is a category error. (3) Critical-period trap: assuming new experience can substitute a new order at any age. If indissoluble relations exist ([PAGE 630]), there are hard windows after which the empiricist operator loses efficacy. (4) Extinction-symmetry trap: assuming undoing experience mirrors forming it. James's wording—"undoes her own work... substitutes a new one"—implies replacement, not symmetric erasure; multi-week extinction may leave latent ancestral traces that recover under stress.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under cognitive or physiological crisis, the executive resources needed to maintain newly formed, experiential associations are depleted. Control then regresses to phylogenetically older, Spencerian "automatic psychical connections" registered over numberless generations [PAGE 630]. These ancestral associations require little online computation and dominate when \(h^{\text{ind}}\)-based pathways are too slow or metabolically costly to sustain.

Counter-trap. James's claim that experience "for an earlier order substitutes a new one" [PAGE 630] describes a deliberate counter-trap: effortful, repeated new experience can overwrite older orders. But this is not symmetric erasure; it is competitive substitution. The counter-trap succeeds only when the new order achieves sufficient numerical frequency to exceed the threshold demanded by Spencer's footnote [PAGE 630].

Clock. Introduce two clocks: \(t_{\text{onto}}\) (individual, days to weeks) and \(t_{\text{phylo}}\) (ancestral, generations). A learning rule:
\[
\dot{A}_{ij} = \alpha \big( A_{\text{target}}(t) - A_{ij} \big) - \beta A_{ij} + \gamma A_0,
\]
where \(\alpha\) is the experiential acquisition rate, \(\beta\) the decay/extinction rate, and \(\gamma A_0\) the ancestral leak. In crisis, \(\alpha \to 0\) for novel associations while \(\gamma A_0\) persists, producing regression. Recovery of a substituted order requires re-accumulating \(h^{\text{ind}}\) above \(H_c\) on the \(t_{\text{onto}}\) clock; it cannot be shortcut by appealing to the \(t_{\text{phylo}}\) clock because that evidence is "beyond our reach" [PAGE 630].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Layer 1 — Neural/physical veto. Reflex arcs and fixed action patterns are anatomically instantiated prior to individual experience. Spencer's concession that the experience-hypothesis is "insufficient" for reflex actions ([PAGE 630]) is a physical veto: no quantity of \(h^{\text{ind}}\) can abolish a hardwired reflex if the afferent–efferent pathway is intact. Formal veto condition: if \(A_{ij}\) corresponds to a reflex, then \(A_{ij} \ge A_{\text{reflex}} > 0\) even when \(h^{\text{ind}}_{ij} = h^{\text{anc}}_{ij} = 0\).

Layer 2 — Biomechanical/physiological veto. Association requires transduction of outer persistences into sense-data. If a sensory receptor, conduction pathway, or afferent apparatus is absent, the co-occurrence history \(\{ (x_i,x_j) \}\) is empty by physiological necessity, not by lack of opportunity. The empiricist operator \(\Phi\) receives a null input and cannot produce fixation; this is a hard input-layer veto independent of any learning rule.

Layer 3 — Environmental/weather-analog veto. James grounds thought-forms in "time- and space-conjunctions of things" [PAGE 630]. If the environment lacks stable outer persistences (chaotic, novel, or weather-like contingency structure), there is nothing for accumulated experience to register. The "order of experience" presupposes environmental order; without it, \(g(H)\) is undefined because \(H\) cannot be meaningfully incremented.

Adjudication proof. Suppose a pure empiricist model \(A = g(H)\) with \(g(0)=0\). Observe an instinctual association \(A^*\) with \(H=0\) (no individual or accessible ancestral record). Then \(g(0)=0 \neq A^*\), contradiction. Therefore the model must admit either an ancestral term \(h^{\text{anc}}\) extending beyond observable reach (unfalsifiable per Spencer, [PAGE 630]) or a non-experiential seed \(A_0\). Either way, pure individualist empiricism is vetoed at the neural/physical layer, and the "sole fashioner" thesis collapses.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Congenital sensory deprivation (verifiable). Raise a subject lacking modality \(M\) (e.g., congenital blindness). Verifiable veto: under pure empiricism, no associations involving \(M\)-sense-data can ever form because the co-occurrence history is null [PAGE 630]. Prediction: after sensory restoration beyond the critical period, the subject cannot form \(M\)-based psychical relations; any later acquisition implicates maturational constraints, vetoing the "sole fashioner" claim. RLVR prediction: a reward signal contingent on \(M\)-correct responses cannot shape behavior if the sensory transducer never emits \(x_i \in M\).

Scenario 2 — Cross-species fostering (verifiable). Foster a neonate of species \(S_1\) with caregivers of species \(S_2\). Verifiable veto: Spencer's ancestral-registration hypothesis predicts \(S_1\)-typical automatic psychical connections persist despite \(S_2\) rearing, because \(h^{\text{anc}}\) is carried phylogenetically [PAGE 630]. Prediction: if the subject exhibits \(S_2\)-typical associations exclusively, the ancestral term is vetoed and individual experience is the sole fashioner; if \(S_1\)-typical instincts appear under \(S_2\) rearing, pure individual empiricism is vetoed.

Scenario 3 — Stress-induced extinction asymmetry (verifiable). Train association \(A_{ij}\) for \(N\) weeks, then extinguish for \(N\) weeks, then test under acute crisis. Verifiable veto: James says experience substitutes a new order rather than symmetrically erasing the old [PAGE 630]. Prediction: under crisis, the original association recovers faster than the extinction association (spontaneous regression), demonstrating that the old order was not undone but displaced. RLVR prediction: counterfactual reward prediction errors during extinction fail to erase the ancestral/high-\(A_0\) path; the value function retains a latent baseline \(A_0\) that re-emerges when \(\alpha\) (online learning rate) collapses under stress.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
