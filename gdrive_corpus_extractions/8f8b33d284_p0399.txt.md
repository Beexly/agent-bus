$$ B_{t+1}(\theta) \propto P(r_t | x_t, \theta) B_t(\theta) $$

$$ P_{belief}(r | x) = \int_\Theta P_\theta(r | x) B_t(\theta) d\theta $$
*Units:* Information units (bits/nats) for prediction error reduction; probabilities for $B_t(\theta)$. *Assumption:* Models $\theta$ are sufficient statistics for predicting relevant outcomes.

**2. Trial-and-Error Selection Dynamics**
Tolman: "establishing of an array of alternative sign-gestalt-expectations" and final "selection" ($[PAGE\ 371]$).
Formalization: This is a sequential decision problem under uncertainty. At each step, the organism selects an action $a$ to maximize expected cumulative reward, balancing exploration and exploitation. The value of model $\theta$ is its expected return $V^\theta(x) = \mathbb{E}[\sum_{k=0}^\infty \gamma^k r_{t+k} | x_t=x, \theta]$. Belief updating and policy improvement follow a Q-learning-like procedure integrated over the model ensemble:
$$ Q(x,a) \leftarrow Q(x,a) + \alpha \left( r_t + \gamma \max_{a'} \mathbb{E}_{\theta \sim B_{t+1}}[Q_\theta(x', a')] - Q(x,a) \right) $$

$$ \text{Minimize } \mathcal{L}(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,r)} [ \| r - g_\phi(f_{new}(x)) \|^2 ] + \lambda \cdot \text{Complexity}(\phi) $$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 doctrine did not emerge in isolation; it was a direct retromissive reaction against Edward Thorndike’s Law of Effect and John Watson’s radical behaviorism. Thorndike’s puzzle-box experiments established the primacy of $S \rightarrow R$ strengthening via satisfaction, creating a dominant paradigm of associative bonding. Tolman’s genealogy reveals a deliberate pivot: he retained the behavioral objectivity demanded by his era (measurable inputs/outputs) but imported Gestalt psychology’s emphasis on holistic perception and Wolfgang Köhler’s insights from ape problem-solving. This was a "covert revolution" [$PAGE\ 371$ footnotes allude to term rejections, showing ideological friction]. By reconceptualizing learning as cognitive map formation—expectations about spatial relationships—he laid groundwork for later work by Karl Lashley (concept of "cognitive maps") and ultimately for computational theories of reinforcement learning with internal models (e.g., Sutton & Barto’s Dyna architecture). The "multi-week traps" Tolman identified were precisely the methodological snares of pure trial-and-error accounts: they could explain rote habit formation but failed catastrophically in explaining latent learning (rats exploring mazes without reinforcement) and rapid adaptation after goal changes. Modern predictive coding and free-energy principles (Friston) are the direct descendants, replacing "purposive" with "self-evidencing," yet preserving the core insight: behavior serves to fulfill predictions, not just stamp in connections. The historical line thus runs: Thorndike (S-R bonds) → Tolman (Purposive S-O-R) → Contemporary Predictive Processing (Active Inference), demonstrating a centuries-long convergence on modeling organisms as hypothesis-testing agents.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The counter-trap is to treat the system as a generic Bayesian filter, ignoring species-specific priors (e.g., rodents’ strong spatial memory modules). The proper counter is *ecologically rational* modeling: expectations are shaped by evolutionary history, not abstract logic.

Complex cognitive maps are only sustainable if they yield consistent survival advantages. This explains why latent learning often remains dormant until a reward is introduced; the map exists but is not activated for navigation unless there’s a goal worth reaching.

This recursive analysis shows Tolman’s doctrine captures a middle layer between reflex and full consciousness—an automated, adaptive belief-management system operating under strict resource bounds.

**Physical/Biomechanical Veto:** Can neural hardware implement "arrays of alternative expectations"? Yes, but with constraints. Hippocampal place cell ensembles encode spatial trajectories, supporting cognitive maps. However, synaptic plasticity is slow (minutes-hours) and energy-intensive. Maintaining many detailed alternative models simultaneously may exceed working memory capacity (~4 chunks) or cause interference. Thus, a biomechanical veto applies: the "array" cannot be arbitrarily large or precise. It must be coarse-grained and sparse, prioritizing high-probability expectations. Furthermore, motor execution fidelity imposes a lower bound on action precision; even with perfect expectations, tremor or fatigue degrades performance. This vetoes any assumption of noiseless policy implementation.

**Weather/Environmental Veto:** Tolman’s mazes were climate-controlled. In nature, weather affects sensory reliability (rain obscures scent trails, wind disperses odors), alters terrain (muddy ground slows locomotion), and changes motivation (cold increases drive for shelter). A sudden storm could invalidate all stored spatial expectations about route costs (wet paths become impassable). The environment thus continuously injects noise into the $S \rightarrow O$ relationship. The system’s robustness depends on how quickly it can discount outdated expectations and incorporate new cues—a rate limited by neural processing speed. Therefore, a major weather event constitutes a hard veto on the stability of any long-term "sign-gestalt-expectation."

**Adjudication:** Tolman’s model passes the cross-layer test *conditionally*. It accurately describes learning in stable, controlled settings. However, it must be augmented with: (1) Resource-bounded assumptions on the size/complexity of the expectation array; (2) Explicit mechanisms for environmental volatility detection (e.g., prediction error spikes triggering belief reset); (3) Integration of physiological states (arousal, fatigue) that modulate the strength of expectations and readinesses. Without these, the model is fragile in dynamic natural environments.

*Tolman Prediction:* The organism should maintain two distinct "sign-gestalt-expectations" (X→reward in A, X→punishment in B) and select appropriately based on subtle contextual discriminanda.

Verifiable measure: Tracking choice accuracy across switches; collapse to chance indicates failure of context-dependent expectation formation.

**2. Scenario: Deceptive Cue Manipulation (False Sign).**
*Setup:* After establishing a reliable cue-reward association (e.g., tone predicts food), introduce occasional "tone-no-food" trials where the tone is played but food is withheld *and hidden*. Later, play the tone again.
*Tolman Prediction:* Based on past consistency, the expectation (tone→food) should persist strongly unless the inconsistency rate exceeds a threshold. The "selection" should favor approach.
*Potential Veto:* If the organism uses a strict Bayesian updater with a low prior on deception, it might over-discount the false cue, expecting food anyway. But if it incorporates social intelligence (awareness that the experimenter might deceive), it might inhibit the response. This creates a paradox: a purely individual learning model cannot account for theory-of-mind-based veto of expectations. Verifiable measure: Compare behavior in "accidental" omission (food forgotten) vs. "intentional" withholding (experimenter visibly blocks food); differential inhibition would refute a non-social expectation model.

**3. Scenario: Cross-Species Transfer Failure.**
*Setup:* Train a rodent on a complex spatial task requiring integration of distal landmarks into a cognitive map (high "inventive ideation"). Then transfer the trained animal to a novel species-typical environment (e.g., from open field to burrow maze) where different sensory modalities dominate.
*Tolman Prediction:* The underlying "means-end-readinesses" and ability to form "new manners of differentiation" are general cognitive traits. The animal should adapt by inventing new landmark integrations.
*Potential Veto:* Species-specific morphologies and sensory specializations (e.g., echolocation in bats, whisker-based touch in rats) create fixed filters on available information. A rat may fail to "invent" a solution requiring precise auditory triangulation because its auditory cortex lacks the requisite tuning. The learning apparatus itself is biologically constrained, vetoing the universality of "inventive ideation." Verifiable measure: Performance plateau in the new modality despite extensive training, compared to naive animals of the ecologically suited species.

These scenarios target the generality, robustness, and architectural independence of Tolman’s cognitive constructs, providing clear empirical tests for their validity beyond controlled laboratory conditions.
