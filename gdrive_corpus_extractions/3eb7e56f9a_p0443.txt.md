Biomechanical veto \(V_{\text{bio}}\). Motor-system incapacity—paralysis, extreme fatigue, injury, or muscular weakness—prevents expression even when \(s>\theta\) and \(g\approx0\). The neurophysiological “start” may occur, but overt \(a(t)\) is zero. This veto operates at the effect layer, after stimulus appraisal but before movement.

Weather veto \(V_{\text{weather}}\). Environmental conditions alter effective salience and motor cost. Let visibility \(v\in[0,1]\) and precipitation cost \(\kappa\ge0\). Effective salience becomes
\[
s_{\text{eff}} \;=\; s\,v\,\big(1-\kappa p\big),\quad p\in[0,1],
\]
where \(p\) is precipitation intensity. Darkness (\(v\to0\)) reduces detection of the unfamiliar respected other, lowering \(s_{\text{eff}}\) below \(\theta\). Heavy rain raises the cost of flight, making arrest or pretense relatively cheaper than scuttling outdoors; if \(s_{\text{eff}}<\theta\), no response is triggered.

Adjudication hierarchy and breakdown. Hierarchy: \(V_{\text{phys}}\) overrides \(V_{\text{bio}}\), which overrides \(V_{\text{weather}}\), which overrides psychological parameters (\(s,\theta,g\)). In an open, dry, unobstructed indoor setting with no physical or biomechanical constraint, all vetoes are inactive and the instinct expresses as James describes [PAGE 433]. Breakdown regime: whenever any veto is active, James’s phenomenological claims about scuttling and arrest describe a counterfactual that cannot be realized; observed behavior will reflect the vetoing layer rather than the instinct itself.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each includes an intervention, a veto mechanism, a falsifiable prediction, and a verification method. No odds, spreads, or betting lines are referenced.

Scenario A: Spatial egress veto. Counterfactual: the same respected stranger approaches the subject in (i) an open atrium and (ii) a narrow corridor with no exit. Veto: \(V_{\text{phys}}=1\) in the corridor because scuttling out of the room is physically blocked [PAGE 433]. Prediction: in the atrium, overt scuttling and gaze aversion express with high probability; in the corridor, locomotor scuttling is absent, and only verbal arrest and facial pretense occur. Verification: motion capture of whole-body kinematics plus facial EMG across 30 trials per condition; the corridor condition should show near-zero forward velocity toward exit while retaining micro-expression activation.

Scenario B: Status and cognitive-load veto removal. Counterfactual: stimulus person is either a respected superior or an unfamiliar equal [PAGE 433]; during the startle window the subject performs a working-memory load (n-back) that degrades cortical suppression \(g(t)\). Veto removal: under load, \(g(t)\approx0\), releasing the impulse. Prediction: response magnitude \(a(t)\) is significantly higher for superior+load than for equal+no load, consistent with James’s claim that concealment is “more apt to be provoked by superiors than by equals or inferiors” [PAGE 433]. Verification: startle-blink magnitude and latency recorded via EMG; superior+load should show larger, shorter-latency blinks than equal+no load.

Scenario C: Pre-emptive suppression loading. Counterfactual: before the door-bell or visitor announcement, the subject receives an explicit instruction to disclose their current activity openly, pre-loading inhibitory gain \(g(0)\approx1\). Veto: high pre-onset \(g\) neutralizes the start before motor execution. Prediction: the “start” is abolished; the subject exhibits only neutral orienting, with no scuttle, pretense, or flight impulse [PAGE 433]. If the instruction arrives after stimulus onset but before motor latency \(\tau_m\), suppression is partial and latency-dependent. Verification: video-coded behavior and skin conductance across instruction-timing conditions; pre-emptive instruction should reduce scuttle incidence to baseline, whereas post-onset instruction yields intermediate expression dependent on \(\Delta t < \tau_m\). Breakdown condition: if motor execution is ballistic with \(\tau_m\) shorter than instruction delivery, pre-emptive loading fails and the impulse expresses despite instruction.
