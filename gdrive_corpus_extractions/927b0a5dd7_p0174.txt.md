The recursion operates at three levels. Level 0 is the animal's immediate stimulus–response loop: light on over Door L → approach Door L. Level 1 is the experimenter's inference that the animal, after cue removal during delay D, is acting on an internal representation of the absent cue; this is the "delayed reaction" as a signature of memory or representative cognition [PAGE 1]. Level 2 is the experimenter's model of the animal's model: Does the rat represent the experimenter's intention? In rats this is weak, but the logical recursion remains—the observer attributes a theory of the contingency to the subject.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the delay D is long, the cue is ambiguous, or the animal is stressed; the system regresses from Level 1 (representational, flexible sign gestalt-expectation) to Level 0 (rigid fixation on the previously reinforced alley) [PAGE 1]. The regression is recursive because the observer, facing a failure of the representational prediction, collapses their own theory of the animal's mind back into behaviorist habit strength. The counter-trap is the opposite error: under-mentalizing. If the observer assumes that all post-delay choice is mere motor perseveration, they may ignore genuine residual cue trace or incidental sensory cues (door mechanism sound, olfactory marker) that sustain performance without full representation. The clock is the physical parameter D itself; under scalar timing (σ_D = k·D), the animal's ability to discriminate delay conditions degrades linearly with D. If the clock resolution falls below the inter-delay difference, the delayed-reaction task ceases to measure memory and becomes a test of temporal discrimination, vetoing any claim about working memory per se.

The full recursive loop is: crisis → habit regression → observer infers no internal model → counter-trap of under-mentalizing → clock analysis reveals temporal precision → re-instatement of representational account → new crisis at longer D. The loop terminates only when cross-layer vetoes (physical cue extinction, biomechanical symmetry, environmental stability) isolate the variable D from the confound F_n. Without that termination, the observer's attribution of "delayed reaction" to memory is unfalsifiable: success is credited to representation, failure to habit, and the theory explains everything while predicting nothing.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The indirect method requires "removing this cue (light), at shorter or longer intervals, before the animal is released" [PAGE 1]. A physical veto is triggered if photometric measurement during the delay shows luminance L > L_c (e.g., L_c = 0.1 cd·m⁻²) or if light leaks through the maze doors. Doors must be identical in mass, visual contrast, and opening torque; a torque asymmetry ΔF/F > 0.05 between doors invalidates the choice as a measure of cue guidance rather than mechanical ease.

Biomechanical layer. Rats exhibit spontaneous turning biases and leg preferences. A baseline (no-cue, no-delay) side preference outside [0.45, 0.55] constitutes a biomechanical veto: the animal's "choice" is partially motor-determined, not cognitively mediated. Running speed and turning radius must be recorded; if velocity correlates with delay D (e.g., longer delays produce slower approaches due to fatigue), then the dependent measure is confounded by locomotor state.

Weather/environmental layer. Although the maze is indoor, the vivarium is not thermodynamically neutral by default. Ambient temperature outside the rat thermoneutral zone (~26–30 °C) or relative humidity outside 40–60 % RH elevates corticosterone and degrades prefrontal-dependent delayed response. A session block conducted outside 18–24 °C is vetoed unless climate covariates are modeled.

Adjudication rule. A delayed-reaction trial is admissible only if: (1) cue luminance during delay is below threshold, (2) baseline side bias is within [0.45, 0.55], and (3) ambient temperature and humidity are within range. From [PAGE 1], the indirect method's removal of the cue before release is the critical physical operation; failure to verify complete removal means the animal may be responding to the cue directly, not to a delayed internal trace, which collapses the entire distinction between direct and indirect methods.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Cue leakage counterfactual. Verifiable condition: place a calibrated photodiode in the maze during the delay interval and record luminance L(t). Veto trigger: any trial with mean L during D exceeding L_c = 0.1 cd·m⁻². Formal prediction: If cue leakage occurs, accuracy p(D) ≈ 1.0 for all D and does not follow p(D) = 0.5 + 0.5·exp(−D/τ). Observation of flat accuracy at high D under measured L > L_c falsifies the memory-decay interpretation and vetoes the session as a test of delayed reaction.

Scenario 2 — Fixation/habit counterfactual. Verifiable condition: log the correct side on every trial and compute the run length n of consecutive identical correct alleys; the source notes this exact condition—"the opposite alley to be the correct one consecutively for many trials" [PAGE 1]. Veto trigger: trials where n > n_crit (e.g., n_crit = 8). Formal prediction: Under strong fixation, P(correct | cue) becomes independent of C and converges to F_n; thus p_n(D) ≈ F_n rather than decaying with D. If a mixed-effects model shows n is a significant predictor of choice while D is not, the delayed-reaction effect is vetoed as a habit artifact.

Scenario 3 — Temporal/session drift counterfactual. Verifiable condition: timestamp every trial and record session clock time t_s; randomize D across the session block. Veto trigger: significant autocorrelation of trial-by-trial errors (lag-1 error correlation ρ_err > 0.2, p < 0.05) or a significant coefficient for t_s in a model p ~ D + t_s + (1|animal). Formal prediction: If performance tracks t_s (circadian/satiation drift) rather than D, then the apparent delay effect is spurious. The counterfactual prediction is that re-randomizing D across time blocks eliminates the D–performance correlation; persistence of the correlation after re-randomization supports a genuine delay effect, while its disappearance validates the veto.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
