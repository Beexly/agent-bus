Elo replaced the linear percentage-point rule with the logistic expected score and the K-factor self-correcting transfer, anchoring the scale so that an average player was 1500, a strong club player 2000, and a grandmaster 2500 [PAGE 2]. The system was designed for one-on-one, zero-sum chess and diffused into football, baseball, and basketball before reaching computer games [PAGE 2].

This observation echoes Reddit complaints about “ELO hell” and matchmaking perceived as gambling rather than skill [PAGE 3–5].

The physics analogy reaches back to Iida et al.’s Motion in Mind model and Game Refinement Theory [PAGE 6, References 5–6], where game attractiveness is mapped to velocity, mass, and potential energy via Table 2 [PAGE 6]. Equation 1 has no robustness term for network variance [PAGE 6]. Veto rule: matches with packet loss > p% or jitter > J_ms are flagged; the result is treated as a network exogenous event and excluded from zero-sum rating transfers to prevent corruption of the self-correcting fixed point.

Adjudication: Any match tripping a veto in any layer must be removed from the update pipeline before Equation 6, 7, or 8 is applied. Otherwise, exogenous variance is falsely attributed to player skill, biasing the convergence claims in Figures 5–8 [PAGE 7–10] and invalidating the experience metrics in Table 3 [PAGE 12].

Veto Scenario 1 — Division-by-zero in Dominance Coefficient (Equation 1 [PAGE 6]).
Veto condition: If S_oppo = 0 for any lane, PS = S²/0 is undefined. The match must be vetoed before Equation 7 or 8 is evaluated.
Counterfactual prediction: In a simulation run where zero-strength opponent lanes are permitted, the PS term diverges and ELO/ladder updates produce infinite or NaN values, preventing convergence in Figures 5–8 [PAGE 7–10]. With the veto enforced (defaulting DC = 1 or excluding the match), rating updates remain finite and the rating fixed point is preserved. Verifiable by scanning logs for S_oppo = 0 and comparing rating variance trajectories with and without the veto.

Veto Scenario 2 — K-factor non-positivity cliff (Equation 5 [PAGE 8]).
Veto condition: If ⌊ELO/400⌋ ≥ 7 (i.e., ELO ≥ 2800), then K ≤ 0. Matches involving such players must be vetoed or K must be floored at a positive minimum.
Counterfactual prediction: At ELO = 2800 exactly, K = 0 freezes ratings, creating an absorbing barrier. Above 2800, K < 0 inverts the Elo gradient: the higher-rated player gains points on a loss and loses points on a win. This generates a sign-flipped attractor or runaway divergence around the 2800 threshold. Verifiable by running the simulation with ELO uncapped and observing accumulation at ELO = 2800 or inverted transfer direction, then confirming elimination under the veto.

Veto Scenario 3 — Team performance degeneracy (Equations 7 and 8 [PAGE 8, 11]).
Veto condition: If PS_T = 0 (total team performance collapse) or PS = 0 on a loss, the ratios PS/PS_T and PS_T/PS are undefined or infinite.
Counterfactual prediction: A match in which every player on a team records PS = 0 makes PS_T = 0, causing division by zero in Equation 7’s win branch and Equation 8’s win branch. A loss with PS = 0 for a single player makes PS_T/PS → ∞ in Equation 7’s loss branch and Equation 8’s loss branch, producing an infinite point penalty that destroys the zero-sum balance. Verifiable by seeding a match with all PS = 0 and observing that the simulation fails to emit finite updates, contradicting the claimed convergence in Figures 5–8 [PAGE 7–10]. Enforcing the veto (floor PS and PS_T at a small positive ε) restores finite updates.
