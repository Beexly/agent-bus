The data show convergence from MD-5 onward [PAGE 1], meaning the squad re-synchronizes before the next match. The counter-trap fails, however, if MD+2 non-starter loading becomes a recurring panic response after every match, because then non-starters enter the MD-5…MD-1 taper already fatigued, degrading the quality of the mid-week acquisition days (MD-4 to MD-2) that are supposed to be the pyramid’s peak [PAGE 6, PAGE 7].

Clock: The hard constraint is the match-day clock. All periodization is phase-locked to MD; C is an exogenous integer imposed by the fixture list [PAGE 1, PAGE 2]. When C=3, the clock compresses recovery and acquisition so severely that MD-1 must carry more intensity than MD+2 [PAGE 6], and no pyramid can form. When C=5–7, the clock expands, permitting MD-3/MD-4 peaks [PAGE 6, PAGE 7]. The recursive error would be to treat the pyramid as a coaching choice rather than a clock artifact: in short cycles, trying to force a mid-week peak violates the clock and collapses into MD-1 loading. In long cycles, ignoring the clock and tapering too early (e.g., treating MD-2 as MD-1) sacrifices the acquisition window. The correct recursive model is: (a) respect C as given; (b) use MD+2 non-starter load as a bounded compensatory stimulus, not a default; (c) protect MD-1 as the taper; (d) converge starters and non-starters from MD-5 onward [PAGE 1, PAGE 7].

Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto adjudication.

Physical layer (measurement validity): STATSports APEX at 10 Hz has bias < 5% for distance and peak speed [PAGE 3, citing ref 20]; each player used the same GPS unit throughout to minimize inter-device error [PAGE 3]; units worn on the upper back per manufacturer instructions [PAGE 3]. Veto status: These controls are strong enough to accept TD, duration, and peak speed comparisons within this cohort, but they do not validate cross-study comparisons because threshold definitions (16/20 km·h⁻¹, 2.26 m·s⁻²) are cohort-specific [PAGE 3].

Biomechanical layer (surface and action structure): All home training and matches occurred on artificial grass, with only occasional away games on natural grass [PAGE 3]. Surface change is a veto candidate: acceleration and deceleration profiles differ between artificial and natural turf, and GPS/accelerometer error may vary. The paper notes AccDecdist is 59–79% of MD while SpD is only 22–51% in longer cycles, attributing the disparity partly to small-sided games in confined spaces [PAGE 7]. Veto: Any claim that “speed work is neglected” based on low SpD is vetoed unless surface and game-format confounds are controlled; small-sided games structurally limit maximum-speed expression regardless of coaching intent.

Physiological layer (speed and injury): Highest training peak speed was 24.2 km·h⁻¹ (~93% of MD starter peak speed of 26.0 km·h⁻¹), versus theoretical maximum 29.2 ± 1.4 km·h⁻¹ (~112% of MD), a ~19% capability gap [PAGE 7]. The paper links underdosed maximum-speed exposure to hamstring injury risk [PAGE 7, citing refs 36,37]. Veto: Injury-prevention conclusions are provisionally supported but vetoed for causal claims because the study is observational and did not measure injury outcomes or force-velocity profiles.

Weather/time-of-day layer: Training sessions typically 10 AM–4 PM; matches 1 PM–9 PM [PAGE 3]. Diurnal temperature variation in Tromsø (Arctic Norway) across two seasons could affect neuromuscular performance, but no weather or temperature covariates are modeled [PAGE 3]. Veto: Seasonal or diurnal effects on load cannot be ruled out; comparisons across cycles spanning different months are confounded by unmeasured weather.

Global veto synthesis: The strongest veto is on MD+1. Because 2208/2426 observations were missing and MD+1 was dropped [PAGE 3], the recovery-day layer is an imputation artifact rather than an observed layer. Any ranking that includes MD+1 as “lowest” is vetoed; the defensible claim is only that MD+2 and MD-1 are the lowest observed training days [PAGE 1]. Secondary veto: starter/non-starter categories are crude (early-sub starters pooled with full-game starters) [PAGE 8], so subgroup load differentiation claims beyond MD+2 are vetoed.

Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning verifiable reward) veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 — Phantom recovery day from imputation. The analysis removes MD+1 primarily because it is 91% missing [PAGE 3], then imputes remaining missingness across the dataset via PMM [PAGE 4]. Counterfactual: re-run the complete-case analysis without any imputation of MD+1 and test whether MD+1 load differs from zero. Prediction: MD+1 will not be statistically distinguishable from a true rest day; the imputed dataset may assign it non-zero marginal means, creating a phantom recovery signal. Verification: compare S1 Table (imputed between-day contrasts) against S4 Table (non-imputed contrasts) [PAGE 5, PAGE 9]. If MD+1 contrasts vanish in the non-imputed set, the reported “MD+2 and MD-1 lowest” conclusion holds only for observed days and MD+1 must be excluded from the pyramid narrative.

Veto scenario 2 — Starter/non-starter aggregation bias. Starters subbed early are pooled with full-game starters [PAGE 8]. Counterfactual: split squad status into three categories — (i) starter >60 min, (ii) starter subbed ≤60 min, (iii) non-starter — and re-estimate the MD+2 contrast. Prediction: the large non-starter advantage on MD+2 (TD +731 to +1197 m) [PAGE 6, PAGE 7] will attenuate or disappear for category (ii), because early-sub starters carry residual fatigue similar to full-game starters, while true non-starters retain the compensatory stimulus. Verification: refit the lmer/glmmTMB models with the three-level squad-status factor and inspect Tukey-adjusted pairwise contrasts on MD+2 for C=3–6. If category (ii) is statistically indistinguishable from category (i) on MD+2, the original binary contrast is confounded by playing-time heterogeneity.

Veto scenario 3 — Tweedie log-link zero inflation on recovery days. HSRD and SpD use a Tweedie GLMM with log link [PAGE 5]. On low-load days (MD+2, MD-1), many sessions may contain zero or near-zero high-speed/sprint distance. Counterfactual: replace the Tweedie GLMM with a zero-inflated or hurdle model (e.g., glmmTMB with ziformula ~ day). Prediction: the back-transformed marginal mean ordering between MD-1 and MD+2 for SpD in C=3 cycles will change; specifically, MD-1 will no longer be significantly higher than MD+2 for SpD once structural zeros are modeled separately from the positive continuous process. Verification: fit glmmTMB with family = tweedie and ziformula ~ MD|cycle, compare AIC to the non-zero-inflated model [PAGE 5], and re-run emmeans pairwise contrasts for MD-1 vs MD+2 on SpD. If the contrast flips to non-significant, the original finding is an artifact of the link function’s handling of zero mass.
