James enumerates five candidate constitutive cues and eliminates each by exhibiting a condition in which the cue varies without a corresponding change in perceived distance, or in which perceived distance obtains without the cue. First, binocular disparity: "Since one-eyed people have it, and are inferior to the two-eyed only in measuring its gradations, it can have no exclusive connection with the double and disparate images produced by binocular parallax." [PAGE 1] Monocular observers possess distance-feeling, so disparity cannot be the substrate of D itself; at most it refines the gradation of D within an already given scale.

Second, convergence: "Since people with closed eyes, looking at an after-image, do not usually see it draw near or recede with varying convergence, it cannot be simply constituted by the convergence-feeling." [PAGE 1] With the eyes closed, oculomotor convergence is clamped or dissociated from the visual object (the after-image), yet distance is still attributed to the image. Convergence is therefore not the feeling of distance.

Third, accommodation: "For the same reason it would appear non-identical with the feeling of accommodation." [PAGE 1] The same closed-eye after-image argument severs accommodation from D.

Fourth, motion parallax: "The differences of apparent parallactic movement between far and near objects as we move our head cannot constitute the distance-sensation, for such differences may be easily reproduced experimentally (in the movements of visible spots against a background) without engendering any illusion of perspective." [PAGE 1] A motion cue can be present while the perceptual depth interpretation is absent; sufficiency fails.

Fifth, pictorial variables: "Finally, it is obvious that visible faintness, dimness, and smallness are not per se the feeling of visible distance, however much in the case of well-known objects they may serve as signs to suggest it." [PAGE 1] These are signs, not the feeling itself; their function is associative and object-dependent.

After eliminating the cues, James leaves a residual: "A certain maximum distance-value, however, being given to the field of view of the moment, whatever it be, the feelings that accompany the processes just enumerated become so many local signs of the gradation of distances within this maximum depth." [PAGE 1] The local signs subdivide a pre-given total volume: "Itself, however, is felt as a unit, a total distance-value, determining the vastness of the whole field of view, which accordingly appears as an abyss of a certain volume." [PAGE 1] The question that persists is the neural origin of this absolute distance-value.

The gray zone is the mechanism by which the absolute scale is fixed. James invokes Hering: "experience in the widest sense of the word" (Beitrāge, p. 844) determines the absolute scale of the space-volume. [PAGE 1] This is a functional placeholder rather than a computational algorithm. The boundary between "native distance-values belonging to each point of the two retinæ" and learned association is not sharply drawn. Moreover, the opening of the page speaks of "association—the suggestion to the mind of optical objects not actually present... being imagined now with a quasi-hallucinatory strength." [PAGE 1] The precision or confidence attached to imagined, absent objects is left unquantified, creating an identifiability gap between the sensory cue vector and the perceptual scale parameter.

Multi-hop derivation: (1) Cue elimination → D is not any c_i. (2) Local-sign thesis → D = λ · d̃(x), where d̃ is dimensionless relative structure and λ is absolute scale. (3) Gauge fixing → λ is anchored by the judged distance of the Kernpunkt K, the momentarily fixated point. (4) Experience dependence → λ is set by "experience in the widest sense," not by the instantaneous cue array. Breakdown regimes: (a) λ → 0 collapses the perceived world onto the observer; (b) λ → ∞ destroys depth resolution; (c) if K localization fails, the entire volume loses its absolute origin while relative relations remain formally intact ("this space in its totality is as a fixed unit, so to speak, displaced"). [PAGE 1] The theory breaks down precisely when the scale anchor is removed or when cue conflict exceeds the binding capacity of local signs into a single unit volume.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let the visual field be a manifold M with points x ∈ M. Define perceived metric depth d(x) ∈ [0, D_max], unit: meter (m), where D_max is the "certain maximum distance-value" of the momentary field [PAGE 1]. Decompose depth into a dimensionless native structure d̃(x) ∈ ℝ⁺ and an absolute scale λ ∈ ℝ⁺, unit: m, such that

d(x) = λ · d̃(x).  (1)

The cue vector at (x,t) is c(x,t) = (δ, α, A, π, L, S), with SI/information units: binocular disparity δ (rad), convergence angle α (rad), accommodation A (diopter = m⁻¹), motion-parallax rate π (rad·s⁻¹), luminance L (cd·m⁻²), and angular size S (rad or sr). James's elimination argument states that for every cue component c_i there exists a stimulus transformation T_i such that either c_i(T_i x) ≠ c_i(x) while d(T_i x) = d(x), or c_i(T_i x) = c_i(x) while d(T_i x) ≠ d(x). This renders the likelihood scale-invariant: p(c | λ, E) = p(d̃ | E), where E denotes "experience in the widest sense of the word" [PAGE 1].

Gauge fixing is supplied by Hering's Kernpunkt K ∈ M, the point momentarily fixated [PAGE 1]. If the judged absolute distance of the Kernpunkt is d_K (m), then

d(K) = d_K  ⇒  d(x) = d_K · d̃(x) / d̃(K).  (2)

"By an alteration of the localization of the Kernpunkt, the inner relations of the seen space are nowise altered; this space in its totality is as a fixed unit, so to speak, displaced..." [PAGE 1] Thus relative depths ∇d̃ are cue-determined, while absolute scale λ is fixed by the posterior over experience:

p(λ | c, E) ∝ p(c | λ, E) · p(λ | E) = p(d̃ | E) · p(λ | E).  (3)

When the likelihood is scale-invariant, the posterior over λ collapses to the prior p(λ | E): experience sets the absolute scale, not the instantaneous retinal image. Information-theoretically, I(D; c) < H(D); the missing information about λ is supplied by E rather than by the cue array.

Breakdown and singularity regimes: (i) If Var(d_K) → ∞ because Kernpunkt localization becomes uncertain, then Var(d(x)) → ∞ for all x and absolute depth is unidentifiable. (ii) If λ → 0, the perceived volume collapses onto the observer; if λ → ∞, depth resolution is lost. (iii) When cue conflict exceeds the local-sign binding limit τ_bind, the "unit" volume V ceases to exist as a perceptual whole and relative structure fragments. These are the exact regimes in which James's residual "abyss of a certain volume" [PAGE 1] loses metric meaning.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from Berkeley's 1709 argument that distance is not directly seen but inferred, through Lotze's (1852) "local signs" doctrine, to Hering's Beiträge zur Physiologie des Sehens (cited p. 844 on [PAGE 1]), which posits native distance-values for each retinal point and a Kernpunkt that anchors the absolute scale of the perceived volume. Helmholtz's unconscious inference supplies the broader Helmholtzian framework in which perception is a probabilistic inference rather than a direct readout. James's 1890 synthesis accepts Hering's relative structure but insists that the absolute distance-value is felt as a unit and fixed by "experience in the widest sense" [PAGE 1].

The multi-week trap is temporal: modern psychophysics shows that absolute egocentric distance recalibration—after prism adaptation, monocular deprivation, or cataract removal—unfolds over days to weeks, not milliseconds. James's phrase "experience in the widest sense" names exactly this slow-timescale prior. A contemporary error is to model depth perception as purely instantaneous cue integration (ms-scale) and thereby miss that λ, the scale parameter in Eq. (1), is consolidated across sessions. Another trap is to treat Hering's native retinal distance-values as fixed; both Hering and James allow the entire spatial volume to be "displaced" with the Kernpunkt [PAGE 1], implying a recalibration dynamics whose clock is behavioral and experiential, not retinal.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the observer's belief state as a recursion over three operators: crisis habit regression H(t), counter-trap C(t), and clock τ(t). Let H(t) ∈ [0,1] denote the strength of the habitual cue policy (e.g., size-distance scaling for familiar objects, which James notes serve "as signs to suggest it" [PAGE 1]). Let C(t) ∈ [0,1] denote the activation of an explicit geometric counter-trap that overrides habit when cue conflict is detected. Let e(t) = |d_obs(t) − d_pred(t)| be the prediction error in meters. A crisis occurs when e(t) > e_crit; under crisis the observer regresses to H(t). The counter-trap engages only if C(t) > H(t), but it decays with time constant τ_C and is metabolically expensive.

The clock governing absolute scale is slow: dλ/dt = −γ(λ − λ*(E)), with γ⁻¹ = τ_λ ∼ days to weeks, where λ*(E) is the experience-set target from Eq. (3) [PAGE 1]. If the environment changes on a timescale faster than τ_λ, the observer is trapped in an outdated absolute scale; the counter-trap can momentarily override the habit but cannot re-anchor the Kernpunkt. This yields a nested failure mode: the fast loop (habit vs. counter-trap) cycles on a crisis clock of seconds, while the slow loop (λ recalibration) cycles on a multi-week clock. When the two clocks desynchronize, the observer reports a coherent "unit" distance-volume [PAGE 1] whose absolute metric is systematically wrong—a recursive theory-of-mind failure in which the mind models the world's scale but updates that model more slowly than the world changes.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: one-eyed observers retain distance-feeling, inferior only in measuring gradations [PAGE 1]; therefore binocular disparity is vetoed as a necessary condition for D. Biomechanical layer: closed-eye after-images show no near/far shift with varying convergence [PAGE 1]; convergence and accommodation are vetoed as sufficient conditions. Environmental/weather layer: visible faintness, dimness, and smallness are not per se distance [PAGE 1]; under uniform luminance, fog, or unfamiliar object size, atmospheric-perspective cues fail to specify d(x). Motion parallax against a featureless background is reproduced experimentally without perspective illusion [PAGE 1]; motion cue vetoed.

Formal veto logic: V_i = 1 iff ∃ stimulus pair (x, x′) such that c_i(x) ≠ c_i(x′) and d(x) = d(x′), or c_i(x) = c_i(x′) and d(x) ≠ d(x′). James establishes V_i = 1 for all five cues on [PAGE 1]. The only entity surviving the veto is the total distance-value V, felt as a unit and bounding the field with a maximum depth D_max [PAGE 1]. However, V is not a sensory cue; it is a pre-given scale field. Weather/atmospheric manipulations of L (luminance) that leave geometry invariant demonstrate that L cannot veto the depth structure itself, consistent with James's sign-versus-feeling distinction [PAGE 1].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Verifiable prediction: blind-walking judged distance scales as d̂_k(x) = k · d̂_1(x) within ±5% error, while relative depth ordering and local-sign gradients remain invariant. Veto condition: if relative depth ordering distorts under pure rescaling of λ, James's separability of local signs from absolute scale fails. Units: meters (m), percent error, radians for head position.

Scenario 2 (After-image convergence clamp): Subject forms an after-image with eyes closed, then views through base-in/base-out prisms imposing vergence change Δα ∈ {−10°, 0°, +10°} while retinal stimulation and luminance are held constant. Verifiable prediction: perceived distance of the after-image does not shift systematically (|Δd̂| < 0.1 m) across Δα. Veto: convergence-feeling cannot constitute distance [PAGE 1]. If Δd̂ correlates with Δα (Pearson r > 0.5), James's elimination claim is falsified.

Verifiable prediction: depth discrimination between spots is at chance (d′ ≈ 0, 95% CI including 0) even for π > 0.5 rad·s⁻¹. Veto: motion parallax alone cannot engender perspective illusion [PAGE 1]. If subjects report a robust depth interval (d′ > 1), James's experimental counterexample fails.
