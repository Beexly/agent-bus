A headwind and crosswind have different biomechanical effects on ball flight; pooling them into speed alone vetoes directional precision. Rain is modestly significant [PAGE 242-243] but the presence variable lumps rain, snow, and showers [PAGE 119-121]. Humidity is significant [PAGE 237-238] but confounded by ball wetness and hold quality [PAGE 239-242]. Veto condition: kickers in stadiums with prevailing crosswinds or frequent precipitation have expected probabilities that may be misestimated if direction and hold quality covary with stadium.

Biomechanical layer — hold quality and surface: Hold quality was not easily available and was not tested [PAGE 240-241]. Surface type was nonsignificant on univariate analysis and excluded [PAGE 132-133]. Veto condition: if hold quality varies by team (long snapper/holder consistency) and correlates with kicker assignment, EPAA attributes holder-driven variance to the kicker.

Indoor imputation layer: For indoor stadiums with missing weather, values are imputed at average humidity, 70°F, and no rain [PAGE 145-146]. Veto condition: this erases any true indoor climate deviation (e.g., cooler dome temperatures) and forces all indoor kickers onto a common expected-probability surface. Cross-comparison between an indoor kicker (imputed) and an outdoor kicker (actual weather) is not an apples-to-apples EPAA comparison.

Adjudication outcome: The EPAA metric survives as a screening tool only under the veto constraint that comparisons are restricted within similar stadium classes (outdoor vs. indoor), similar climates, and after adjusting for attempt-distance selection bias. Comparing a dome kicker's imputed EPAA to an outdoor cold-weather kicker's raw EPAA violates the physical layer and produces a vetoed inference.

Veto Scenario 1 — Indoor imputation veto. Construct two matched kickers with identical observed distance, actual outcome, and salary, where Kicker A plays all kicks in a dome (weather imputed: 70°F, average humidity, no rain [PAGE 145-146]) and Kicker B plays all kicks outdoors in a cold, windy climate (actual weather covariates). Veto rule: if EPAA_A > EPAA_B solely because Kicker B's expected probability is depressed by actual cold/wind while Kicker A's expected probability is inflated by imputed benign weather, the EPAA comparison is invalid. Prediction: In the 2023 dataset, indoor kickers will exhibit systematically higher mean EPAA than outdoor kickers in the lowest temperature quartile, conditional on distance distribution. This is verifiable by recomputing EPAA with stadium fixed effects or by excluding indoor kicks; if the indoor premium disappears, the imputation veto is confirmed.

Veto Scenario 2 — Distance-selection bias veto. Select kickers who attempted at least five field goals beyond 55 yards versus kickers whose longest attempt was under 40 yards, matched on total attempts. Veto rule: because only elite kickers receive long attempts [PAGE 284-289], the long-distance group faces a mechanically lower expected success rate p_i for those kicks. A make on a 58-yard attempt yields high EPAA; an average kicker never gets the opportunity to generate that EPAA. Prediction: Kickers with high deep-attempt volume will show lower season EPAA than kickers with identical true skill but only short-distance opportunities, because their expected-points baseline is inflated by the selection of low-probability kicks. Verifiable by stratifying EPAA by maximum attempt distance; if EPAA declines monotonically with max distance after controlling for accuracy, the selection-bias veto is confirmed.

Veto Scenario 3 — Cash-salary timing veto. Identify kickers on rookie contracts (low cash salary, e.g., < $1M [PAGE 44-45]) versus veterans on back-loaded multi-year deals (high cash salary in 2023 but low cap hit [PAGE 303-307]). Veto rule: comparing single-season 2023 EPAA to single-season cash salary misprices value because cash salary reflects contract accounting, not marginal 2023 cost. Prediction: Rookie-contract kickers will cluster in the high-value green box [PAGE 203-207] regardless of EPAA, while back-loaded veterans will cluster in low-value or intermediate cells even with positive EPAA. If the correlation between EPAA and salary remains null after replacing cash salary with cap hit or average annual value, the timing veto is confirmed and the null finding is structural rather than evidential of skill-salary independence.
