Sensory and motor centres are treated as solved; emotions are the remaining unsolved item, creating deadline pressure to either solve them now with existing centres or consign them indefinitely to the future. James’s recursive theory of mind models the theorist as a perceptual device that, under the clock of scientific progress, regresses to the habit of cortical projection but is counter-trapped into extending that projection to viscera, muscles, and joints [PAGE 473]. The recursion is epistemic: the theory of mind uses mind-like perceptual mechanisms to explain mind, while the clock limits how many exceptions can be granted before the system must close.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer (neural conduction and cortical topology): conduction velocity of unmyelinated visceral afferents is approximately 0.5–2 m/s. If emotional experience latency $T_E < 100$ ms and the viscus-cortex distance is $d > 0.1$ m, then $\tau_v = d/v > 50$ ms, leaving little or no margin for synaptic and cortical processing. Physical veto rule: if measured emotional onset precedes the earliest possible afferent arrival, cortical perception cannot be the cause. Prediction: rapid emotional responses (startle, threat detection) will be subcortical, vetoing James’s cortical seat for those responses.

Biomechanical layer (visceral and musculoskeletal transduction): smooth muscle and visceral walls lack discrete, high-fidelity transducers comparable to muscle spindles. Afference from viscera is diffuse and polysynaptic rather than topographically discrete. Biomechanical veto rule: if the periphery cannot provide a distinct channel for each viscus, then $\mathcal{C}$ cannot contain “parts” for each viscus [PAGE 473], breaking the projection postulate. Prediction: emotional granularity will be bounded by afferent channel capacity; fine-grained distinctions among similar emotions (e.g., fear vs. anxiety) will not map to distinct visceral afferent patterns.

Weather / environmental layer (ambient context): ambient temperature $T_{\text{amb}}$, humidity, and noise shift baseline afference, $\tilde a_i(t) = a_i(t) + w_i(t)$ where $w$ is context noise. Veto rule: if $I(E; \mathbf{A} \mid W) \ll I(E; \mathbf{A})$, then context, not bodily state, drives the emotional report. Prediction: identical internal affective states will be labeled differently depending on ambient temperature (misattribution of arousal), producing false positives for the Jamesian afference model unless weather variables are regressed out.

Adjudication: James’s model survives only if (1) afferent dimensionality satisfies $I(E;\mathbf{A})=H(E)$ [bits], (2) visceral topography provides distinct $c_v$ [PAGE 473], and (3) contextual noise is orthogonal to emotion. Failure at any layer issues a veto; the model is not veto-proof unless all three conditions hold simultaneously.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Selective cortical deafferentation. Condition: bilateral lesion to primary somatosensory cortex and insular cortex abolishing conscious afferent perception of visceral and muscular states. Veto rule: if emotional experience remains subjectively intact despite abolished afferent perception, James’s cortical perception model is vetoed. Prediction: patients will report diminished emotional intensity but preserved emotional behavior; if subjective emotion is preserved, veto confirmed. Verifiable via structural lesion mapping plus moment-to-moment self-report.

Scenario 2: Physiological non-specificity. Condition: pharmacologically induce identical heart rate and skin conductance (e.g., via isoproterenol) in two contexts, one fear-inducing and one neutral. Veto rule: if subjects label their state differently according to context alone, emotion is not determined by afferent pattern alone, vetoing pure afference sufficiency. Prediction: the Schachter-Singer misattribution effect—emotion label follows cognitive appraisal rather than afferent pattern. Verifiable via arousal manipulation and verbal report.

Scenario 3: Subcortical rapid response. Condition: present a threat stimulus with backward masking at under 30 ms so cortical perception is prevented. Veto rule: if amygdala activation and behavioral startle occur without reportable cortical perception, emotion does not require the cortical seat James assigns [PAGE 473]. Prediction: LeDoux low-road pattern—startle EMG present, subjective report absent—vetoing cortex-as-necessary for emotion. Verifiable via masked-affect paradigms and electromyography.
