</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes how each persona collapses toward its prior under informational stress. When pre-game information is thin or contradictory—late injury news, conflicting lineup rumors, or sparse social media—the Fan Predictor reverts to team-affirming extremes, the Home and Away Coach Predictors revert to loyalty-biased personnel assessments, while the Analyst and Sportsbook Trader Predictors revert to conservative, regression-to-the-mean base rates [PAGE 5, PAGE 11–12]. This regression is visible empirically: fans and coaches produce more extreme probabilities, analysts and traders more conservative ones [PAGE 11].

The counter-trap is market anticipation. Because Kalshi prices already aggregate a wide population of beliefs, the market-implied benchmark is “immensely consistent” and difficult to beat [PAGE 12]. The Kalshi Trader persona attempts to front-run public sentiment, but if market participants themselves anticipate LLM-driven aggregation, they adjust prices to erase the very edge the persona seeks. The result is an infinite regress of theory-of-mind: the LLM predicts the game, the market predicts the LLM’s prediction, and the LLM’s meta-predictor attempts to predict the market’s reaction to the LLM. The 24-hour Kalshi snapshot versus the LLM’s pre-tip close time creates a temporal asymmetry: the market observes information between those two timestamps that the LLM treats as outside \(I_j\) [PAGE 10]. In recursive equilibrium, the agent’s edge exists only in the gap between its faster textual synthesis and the market’s slower aggregation; once that gap closes, the EV threshold \(\tau\) admits no trades and the bankroll reverts to drift.

The clock is the hard constraint on all of this. Every forecast is timestamped; every search is bounded by a close time prior to tip-off; every trade is settled after realized outcome [PAGE 3, PAGE 10]. The clock converts probabilistic forecasting from a static classification problem into a dynamic adverse-selection problem: information arriving between LLM close and game start is unobservable to the agent but relevant to \(o_j\), producing systematic miscalibration precisely when the clock is shortest.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The NBA is an indoor league, so weather is a null veto except insofar as it disrupts travel to the arena; the Rest Searcher tracks travel distance but not weather delays [PAGE 4]. Biomechanical layer: the Extended Injury Searcher evaluates “minutes restrictions” and return timelines qualitatively [PAGE 4], but the model ingests no load-management telemetry, no player-tracking biomechanics, and no game-time scratch data beyond what is textually published before the close. If a star is declared inactive after the LLM close but before tip-off, the forecast \(p_j\) is conditioned on an incomplete \(I_j\) and the trade carries an unquantified physical veto.

Veto proof by exclusion: The system’s search dimensions are injury, star injury, extended injury, home advantage, rest, and team momentum [PAGE 4]. It omits referee assignments, back-to-back sequence position beyond rest days, altitude/travel-fatigue interactions, and real-time lineup confirmation. Therefore any game whose outcome is decisive factor lies outside these six dimensions fails the completeness assumption of \(I_j\). Since NBA games are decided by on-court physical execution, and the model has no physical-layer sensor or veto mechanism, every trade implicitly waives the right to condition on unobserved physical state. The only available veto is the EV threshold \(\tau\) and the MaxBet cap, which are statistical, not physical [PAGE 6].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-via-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios with counterfactual predictions.

Veto 1 — Brier-score singularity. If any predictor attains \(s_k = 0\) on a validation fold (perfect Brier Score), then \(w_k = \ln((1-0)/0) \to +\infty\), and the normalized weight \(\bar w_k\) becomes ill-defined or collapses the ensemble to that single predictor. Verifiable trigger: \(\min_k s_k = 0\) in a rolling 10-game window. Prediction: ensemble Brier Score ceases to improve and may equal the single best predictor’s score; the aggregation provides no diversification benefit and the system behaves as a non-ensemble.

Veto 2 — Zero-edge numerical instability. If \(p_{\text{adj}} = q_j\) exactly (or within floating-point tolerance), then \(EV_{\text{YES}} = EV_{\text{NO}} = 0\). With any \(\tau > 0\) the rule is HOLD, but with \(\tau = 0\) the algorithm is indifferent and implementation-defined tie-breaking can generate trades at zero expected edge. Verifiable trigger: \(|p_{\text{adj}} - q_j| < 10^{-6}\) on a non-trivial subset of games. Prediction: spurious trade frequency rises while total PnL trends negative due to implicit friction (slippage, settlement rounding) not modeled in the paper’s binary payoff.

Veto 3 — Late-scratch information violation. If an official injury report or lineup change is published after the LLM close time but before game start, the realized outcome \(o_j\) is conditioned on information outside \(I_j\). Verifiable trigger: official NBA injury status change between LLM close and tip-off for a traded game. Prediction: trades executed with positive ex-ante EV exhibit systematically negative realized PnL on exactly these games, because the market price \(q_j\) updates on the late information while the agent’s \(p_{\text{adj}}\) remains stale; the fractional Kelly stake amplifies losses rather than protecting capital.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
