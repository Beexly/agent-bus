Location-based routing descends from Xu, Heidemann, and Estrin's GAF (MobiCom 2001) [PAGE 6, ref 15], Yu, Govindan, and Estrin's GEAR (UCLA TR 2001) [PAGE 6, ref 20], and Chen et al.'s Span (MobiCom 2001) [PAGE 7, ref 21]. Data-centric inquiry reaches back to Heinzelman, Kulik, and Balakrishnan's SPIN (MobiCom 1999) [PAGE 9, ref 28], Intanagonwiwat, Govindan, and Estrin's Directed Diffusion (MobiCom 2000) [PAGE 10, ref 30], and Braginsky and Estrin's Rumor Routing (WSNA 2002) [PAGE 10, ref 32]. Mobility-aware routing inherits from Luo and Hubaux's joint mobility and routing for lifetime elongation (INFOCOM 2005) [PAGE 14, ref 45], Shah et al.'s Data MULEs (SNPA 2003) [PAGE 15, ref 46], and He et al.'s SPEED (ICDCS 2003) [PAGE 19, ref 54]. The minimum-power topology thread originates with Rodoplu and Meng's minimum energy mobile wireless networks (IEEE JSAC 1999) [PAGE 8, ref 26].

Multi-week traps are embedded in this lineage. Short simulation horizons typical of the cited era can mask week-scale cascading failures: in LEACH, randomly selected cluster heads may die faster than others, creating coverage holes only visible over extended rounds [PAGE 11]; in MECN, static minimum-power links guarantee the designated neighbor dies quickly, but the resulting network disconnection may not appear until the second or third week of operation [PAGE 8]; PEGASIS's greedy chain construction requires global knowledge and dynamic topology adjustment, introducing overhead that scales with utilization and can destabilize long-running networks [PAGE 12]. Diurnal duty cycles and environmental channel fading produce frequent topology changes [PAGE 3] that static 2D models fail to capture, motivating the survey's conclusion that 3D sensor fields are necessary for forests, multi-floor buildings, and underwater deployments [PAGE 21]. A multi-week deployment must therefore audit not just first-node death but the temporal correlation of cluster-head mortality, sink-hole migration, and duty-cycle misalignment between MAC sleep schedules and routing active states [PAGE 6].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model each sensor node as an agent maintaining a belief state about neighbor residual energy and link quality. Under nominal conditions, nodes execute location-based or hierarchical forwarding per their protocol. Under crisis—defined as E_i^{res}(t) approaching E_{thresh} or neighbor failure [PAGE 4]—nodes risk habit regression into blind behaviors inherited from pre-WSN ad hoc techniques: flooding, where every packet is rebroadcast indiscriminately, causing implosion and overlap [PAGE 2], or gossiping's random-walk delay that scales poorly with network size [PAGE 2]. This crisis habit regression is the primary failure recursion: energy-constrained nodes, lacking negotiated admission control, revert to brute-force replication, which further drains batteries and accelerates the sink-hole collapse near the sink [PAGE 14].

The counter-trap is explicit negotiation and stateful gating. SPIN requires nodes to exchange meta-data advertisements before data dissemination, ensuring only interested receivers request data and avoiding injection of redundant information [PAGE 9]. HEED counters static CH bias by probabilistically selecting cluster heads via residual energy and breaking ties with node degree/density, preventing any single node from becoming a permanent energy sink [PAGE 13]. PEGASIS bypasses dead sensors in its chain reconstruction, refusing to recurse through failed nodes [PAGE 12]. APTEEN extends TEEN by supporting historical, one-time, and persistent queries, allowing periodic baseline reporting alongside threshold-triggered alerts, thus preventing the silence trap where thresholds are never reached and no data arrives [PAGE 13].

The clock is the protocol's temporal arbiter. LEACH's setup-versus-steady-state phase transition [PAGE 11] determines whether a node invests energy in reorganization or exploits existing clusters; GeRaF's N_p RTS retry window [PAGE 7] bounds how long a sender recurses through priority regions before dropping the packet; SAR's base-station-triggered periodic path recomputation [PAGE 19] resets routing table consistency after local failures; and the round counter in hierarchical protocols governs CH rotation, preventing permanent battery concentration [PAGE 11]. Recursive theory of mind therefore predicts that nodes with accurate neighbor-energy models and short clock periods escape crisis regression, whereas nodes operating on stale topology or missing clock synchronization regress into flooding-like waste and precipitate network partition.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical-layer veto. The wireless network interface is often the single largest consumer of power [PAGE 7], motivating Span's radio-off-during-idle principle [PAGE 7]. Any routing protocol requiring continuous reception or idle listening is vetoed by the battery energy budget E_{bat} (J): E_{idle} integrated over duty cycle T_{cycle} must satisfy E_{idle} · T_{listen} < E_{bat} − E_{mission}. GAF's discovery/active/sleeping state machine [PAGE 6] operationalizes this veto by turning radios off in the sleeping state; protocols without sleep states fail the physical energy constraint.

Biomechanical/operational veto. Sensor nodes are battery-powered and deployed in harsh environments where changing or recharging batteries is very difficult or impossible [PAGE 1] [PAGE 2]; the authors' affiliation with Tata Steel Maintenance Engineering [PAGE 1] implies an industrial context with no manual node retrieval. Any protocol assuming battery replacement, manual reconfiguration, or physical access is vetoed. This eliminates static-optimal topologies like MECN's fixed minimum-power tree in static deployments [PAGE 8] and any scheme requiring node-level maintenance after deployment.

Weather/channel veto. The wireless medium is noisy, error-prone, and time-varying [PAGE 4]; channel fading causes frequent topology change [PAGE 3]; and communication links may be asymmetric [PAGE 5]. Protocols assuming symmetric reliable links or static topology are vetoed. SPEED's delay estimation relies on ACK elapsed time from neighbors [PAGE 19], which degrades under time-varying channel conditions; its lack of an explicit energy metric [PAGE 19] further fails under weather-induced transmission power fluctuations. GeRaF's best-effort model [PAGE 7] explicitly offers no delivery guarantee when channel conditions empty the highest-priority forwarding region [PAGE 7].

Cross-layer consistency veto. MAC duty cycles must align with routing state machines. If GAF's active state coincides with a MAC sleep interval, discovery messages are lost and the node may remain undiscovered [PAGE 6]. A veto is issued whenever routing-layer active periods are not Cartesian-product compatible with MAC wake-up schedules, because packet loss and control overhead then violate channel-utilization objectives [PAGE 4].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 — Static MECN deployment. Veto condition: MECN computes a static minimum-power topology rooted at the sink [PAGE 8]; in static networks this creates a fixed next-hop chain where one neighbor forwards all upstream data [PAGE 8]. Verifiable prediction: network lifetime T_net collapses to approximately E_{relay}^{res} / \dot{E}_{relay}, where the relay neighbor adjacent to the sink depletes first; upon its death, all upstream sensors lose connectivity to the sink and end-to-end delivery ratio → 0 for affected subtrees. Observable via timestamped node-death events and sink-received packet counts.

Scenario 2 — LEACH in large-region deployment. Veto condition: LEACH uses single-hop routing where each node transmits directly to its cluster head and the sink [PAGE 11]; the protocol is explicitly not applicable to networks deployed in large regions [PAGE 11]. Verifiable prediction: for any cluster whose CH is beyond radio range R_{radio} of the sink, d(CH,s) > R_{radio}, packets cannot reach the sink; delivery ratio for those clusters is exactly 0 regardless of residual energy, and network lifetime metrics based on node energy are meaningless because the sink is unreachable. Observable via field-radius sweep and per-cluster delivery ratio.

Scenario 3 — GeRaF below neighbor-activity threshold. Veto condition: GeRaF polls priority regions via RTS; if no CTS is received after N_p attempts, the sender backs off and eventually discards the data packet [PAGE 7]. Verifiable prediction: delivery probability is bounded by P_{deliv} ≤ 1 − (1 − p_{active})^{N_p}, where p_{active} is the probability a neighbor in a priority region is awake; as duty cycle → 0, p_{active} → 0, so P_{deliv} → 0 and latency → ∞. Observable by duty-cycle sweep measuring successful sink receptions versus transmitted packets.
