The paper argues that appearance cues are ambiguous in team sports: “where appearance cues are ambiguous and motion patterns do not necessarily follow a linear pattern” [PAGE 1]. <SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source’s first operator is the discretized state-space update. It states: “Mamba is a SSM technique which, upon discretization with Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) assumption over timestep \(\Delta\), can be formulated as:” [PAGE 3]. The formal equations are
\[
h'_k = A h_{k-1} + B x_k,\qquad y_k = C h_k,
\]
with \(A,B,C\) dynamically adjusted from input features \(x_k\) and hidden state \(h_k\) [PAGE 3]. Units: \(x_k\), \(h_k\), \(y_k\) are dimensionless embedding vectors of unspecified dimension; \(\Delta\) is a timestep in frames or seconds depending on the implementation; \(A,B,C\) are dimensionless matrices/parameters. Assumption: linear state recursion after ZOH discretization. Breakdown: the paper itself warns that “SSMs compress historical information, which will tend to be unreliable during prolonged occlusion or when nonlinear motion is observed [41]” [PAGE 2]. Thus the singularity regime is informational, not algebraic: when the hidden state has compressed away the identity-bearing history, the recursive update cannot reconstruct the missing player state.

The Mamba-attention block adds MHSA: “To model non-linear motion effectively by implicitly focusing on relevant dependencies, we add a Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) block to the mamba features \(y_k\)” [PAGE 4]. The source gives
\[
y_{ln}=\text{LayerNorm}(y_k),
\]
\[
x_{att}=\text{LayerNorm}(\text{MHSA}(y_{ln})+y_{ln}),
\]
and per head
\[
\text{head}_i=\text{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q^i(K^i)^T}{\sqrt d}\right)V^i,
\]
where \(Q,K,V\) are linear transforms of \(y_{ln}\) [PAGE 4]. Units: \(d\) is head width in dimensionless feature coordinates; softmax outputs probabilities in \([0,1]\). Assumption: attention logits are finite. Breakdown: saturated softmax when logits are extremely large or when all keys are uninformative; then attention becomes a near-one-hot shortcut and loses the multi-hypothesis behavior needed during occlusion. The feed-forward block is
\[
x_{ff}=\text{Linear}(\text{GeLU}(\text{Linear}(x_{att}))+x_{att}),
\]
and the prediction head is
\[
B_p=\sigma(\text{Linear}(x_{out}))
\]
[PAGE 4]. Units: \(B_p\) is a bounding-box vector. If \(B_p\) is in pixels, the sigmoid range \([0,1]\) is a representational bottleneck; if coordinates are normalized, the equation is consistent. Breakdown: sigmoid saturation at 0 or 1 gives vanishing gradients and clipped boxes.

The spatial association operator begins with Extended IoU:
\[
\text{EIoU}=\frac{|B'_p\cap B'_d|}{|B'_p\cup B'_d|},
\]
where
\[
B'_d=\{x_t-0.5bw_t,\; y_t-0.5bh_t,\; w_t+bw_t,\; h_t+bh_t\}
\]
and analogously for \(B'_p\) [PAGE 5]. Units: coordinates in pixels; EIou is dimensionless in \([0,1]\). Assumption: expanded boxes have positive area. Breakdown: denominator zero for degenerate boxes; excessive buffer \(b\) expands the matching region so far that neighboring players become interchangeable. The height-adaptive operator is
\[
\text{HA-EIoU}=\text{HIoU}\cdot\text{EIoU},
\]
with
\[
\text{HIoU}=\left|\frac{\min(y_t^2,\hat y_t^2)-\max(y_t^1,\hat y_t^1)}{\max(y_t^2,\hat y_t^2)-\min(y_t^1,\hat y_t^1)}\right|
\]
[PAGE 5]. Units: dimensionless height-overlap ratio. Assumption: \(y^2\) is the bottom coordinate and \(y^1\) is the top coordinate, with \(y^2>y^1\). Singularity: denominator zero when both boxes have zero total vertical span; invalid boxes can make the ratio non-interpretable. The paper justifies the operator by arguing that “relative height differences are often ignored in these matching metrics” and that “height-aware matching is necessary to tackle the issue with varying depths with the scale” [PAGE 5].

Appearance cost is cosine similarity:
\[
S_{reid}(i,j)=\frac{e_i^T\cdot e_j^D}{\|e_i^T\|\|e_j^D\|}.
\]
Units: dimensionless in \([-1,1]\) for real embeddings. Breakdown: zero-norm or near-zero-norm embeddings make the denominator singular or numerically unstable. The hybrid cost is
\[
\mathcal{J}_f=\lambda_{reid}(1-S_{reid})+\lambda_{ssim}(1-\text{HA-EIoU}),
\]
then a “linear assignment solver [26] is used to estimate the one-to-one matching” [PAGE 5]. Units: dimensionless cost. Assumption: \(\lambda_{reid}\) and \(\lambda_{ssim}\) are calibrated so that appearance and spatial costs are comparable. Breakdown: if same-jersey appearance is ambiguous, a large \(\lambda_{reid}\) overweights noise; if occlusion is severe, a large \(\lambda_{ssim}\) overweights geometry that may be wrong.

The memory operator is EMA:
\[
e_i^{t-1}=\alpha e_i^{t-2}+(1-\alpha)f_i^{t-1},
\]
where “\(\alpha\) is the smoothing factor that determines the contribution of the historical embedding … relative to the new detection embedding” [PAGE 6]. The dynamic version is
\[
\alpha_d=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\frac{1-(s_t-\sigma)}{1-\sigma},
\]
and
\[
e_i^{t-1}=\alpha e_i^{t-2}+(1-\alpha_d)f_i^{t-1}.
\]
Units: \(s_t,\sigma,\alpha,\alpha_d\) dimensionless; \(e,f\) embedding vectors. Singularity: \(\sigma=1\) makes \(\alpha_d\) undefined. Breakdown: if \(\sigma>0.5\) and \(\alpha\) is high, \(\alpha_d\) can exceed 1 at \(s_t=1\); for \(s_t<\sigma\), \(\alpha_d>1\) is even more likely. Then \(1-\alpha_d\) is negative, so the “update” is not a convex combination. The paper’s intent is clear, but the algebra needs clipping \(\alpha_d\in[0,1]\) or a corrected convex form such as \(e=\alpha_d e_{old}+(1-\alpha_d)f_{new}\).

The training objective is “a combination of a variant of L1 loss and IoU loss function” [PAGE 6]:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{L1}^s=
\begin{cases}
\frac12(P_t-G_t)^2, & |P_t-G_t|\leq 1,\\
|P_t-G_t|-\frac12, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{L1}^s\mathcal{L}_{L1}^s+\lambda_{ciou}\mathcal{L}_{ciou}.
\]
The paper sets \(\lambda_{L1}^s=50\) and \(\lambda_{ciou}=1\) [PAGE 6]. Units: if coordinates are pixels, the smooth-L1 threshold 1 is 1 pixel and the loss is pixel-squared; if coordinates are normalized, the threshold is dimensionless and the loss is dimensionless. Breakdown: the threshold choice changes the effective gradient scale, and the 50:1 weighting makes coordinate regression dominate CIoU unless the coordinates are normalized.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
In tracker terms, recursive theory of mind is the nested belief problem: the detector believes a box is a player, the motion predictor believes a future box belongs to the same player, the appearance model believes an embedding identifies that player, and the association solver must adjudicate those beliefs under partial information. The crisis habit regression is the fallback to linear extrapolation when occlusion or blur destroys evidence. The paper explicitly targets that habit by replacing KF with a Mamba-attention predictor, but the fallback can reappear inside the learned model if the hidden state is compressed or if the attention saturates. The counter-trap is over-trusting appearance because jerseys are similar. The paper warns that team sports have “appearance cues [that] are ambiguous” [PAGE 1], and its LCA stage deliberately avoids ReID for low-confidence detections because “detections with lower confidence have a higher tendency for noisy detections or artifacts present in it” [PAGE 5]. That is a counter-trap defense: when the detector’s belief is weak, do not let the appearance model dominate. The clock is tracklet lifetime. The source says lost tracklets are “temporarily stored in memory, allowing for re-identification if the player reappears,” but “if a tracklet remains unmatched within the timeframe, it is permanently deleted from the memory” [PAGE 5]. The clock is therefore a belief-decay parameter: too short a window causes identity fragmentation after occlusion; too long a window creates false re-identification when a different player re-enters. SportMamba’s dynamic EMA is another clock: it decides how much of the old appearance belief survives each detection. The gray-zone is that Equation (12) can make the new evidence weight negative, which is not a conservative belief update but a sign reversal. A robust implementation should clip \(\alpha_d\) to \([0,1]\) and verify that the update is a convex combination; otherwise the tracker’s “memory” can oscillate between old and new appearance rather than smoothly decaying.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer veto is straightforward. Let \(p_t=(x_t,y_t)\) be a player’s image-plane position and \(\Delta t\) the inter-frame time. A candidate association should be vetoed if
\[
v_t=\frac{\|p_t-p_{t-1}\|_2}{\Delta t}>v_{\max}(\text{sport})
\]
or if
\[
a_t=\frac{\|v_t-v_{t-1}\|}{\Delta t}>a_{\max}(\text{sport}),
\]
where \(v_{\max}\) and \(a_{\max}\) are domain-calibrated in pixels/s and pixels/s\(^2\), or in m/s and m/s\(^2\) after homography. This veto prevents a fast-motion buffer from matching a player to a physically impossible detection across the court or rink. The biomechanical layer uses scale-depth consistency. Under a pinhole model, a player of physical height \(Y\) at depth \(z_t\) has image height
\[
h_t=\frac{fY}{z_t},
\]
where \(f\) is focal length in pixels, \(Y\) in meters, and \(z_t\) in meters. For the same player, \(h_tz_t=fY\) is approximately constant. A candidate match can be vetoed if
\[
\left|\frac{h_t}{h_{t-1}}-1\right|>\rho_{\max}\Delta t
\]
or if the recovered depth jumps beyond a field-consistent bound. This is exactly the physical motivation for the paper’s height-adaptive metric: it aims “to reduce ID switches caused by partial occlusions by accounting for scale variations due to depth changes” [PAGE 1]. The weather and sensing layer veto is blur and illumination. The paper acknowledges that “severe motion blur can lead to missed detections and weakened appearance cues, resulting in broken tracklets” [PAGE 8]. A practical veto computes a blur score \(B=\operatorname{Var}(\nabla^2 I)\), where \(I\) is image intensity; if \(B<B_{\min}\), appearance cost should be downweighted or vetoed. VIP-HTD is especially relevant because the source says “due to the faster pace of the ice hockey game, it exhibits comparably more motion blur” [PAGE 6]. Thus the cross-layer adjudication is: if physical velocity, scale-depth, or blur vetoes fire, the association should fall back to spatial HA-EIoU or defer the identity decision, not force an appearance match. This is consistent with the paper’s HCA/LCA split, but the paper does not formalize the veto thresholds; that is an audit gap.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
