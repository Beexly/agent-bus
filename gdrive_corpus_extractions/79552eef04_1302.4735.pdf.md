Multi-week traps: (1) Temporal aggregation trap. The surrogate uses average away-game counts g(i,j) and correlates with multi-season actuals, but fatigue and injury risk accumulate over weeks in a non-linear fashion; a team with moderate total miles but clustered back-to-back road games may suffer more than a team with higher miles spread evenly. The model cannot detect this. (2) Endogeneity trap. Realignment changes future schedules, which changes future travel, yet the surrogate is calibrated on historical travel under the old structure; using past travel to choose future structure assumes the schedule-generation process is invariant to structure, which it is not. The NHLPA rejected the 4-conference proposal [PAGE 3], showing that “optimal” mileage is a necessary but insufficient condition; the model’s post-hoc constraint filters are ad hoc responses to veto players rather than endogenous game-theoretic equilibria. (4) Expansion trap.
