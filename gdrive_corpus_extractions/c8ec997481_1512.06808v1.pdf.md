Veto 1 — Ultimatum / Nash-bargaining non-credible threat.
Setup [PAGE 66, Ex 2.1]: assets \$100k; litigation gives $(40k,20k)$ after \$20k fees each. Candidate profile $(Offer\ 70\text{-}30,(Reject,Reject))$ is a Nash equilibrium [PAGE 96]. Veto: it is not subgame-perfect — at Player 2's off-path node, "Reject" is dominated by "Accept" in the subgame, so the threat is incredible (Thm 2.1 [PAGE 76]). Prediction (falsifiable): under common knowledge of these payoffs, any observed "reject-the-fair-offer" behavior cannot be an SPE outcome; the SPE play is the backward-induction profile $(Offer\ 50\text{-}50,(Accept,Reject))$ [PAGE 96]. If a subject with these exact payoffs commits to blanket rejection as an equilibrium path, the model is refuted.

Veto 2 — Chain-store reputation phase transition.
Setup [PAGE 514–518]: backward induction predicts entry + accommodation in both towns (reputation argument dies under certainty, [PAGE 78–80]). Introduce one-sided incomplete information: incumbent is "hotheaded" (fight-loving) with prior $p$. Candidate: rational incumbent *fights* PE-1's entry to deter PE-2. Veto/threshold: this deterrence profile is a sequential equilibrium iff $p\ge \tfrac13$ ([PAGE 536], Ex 14.2); the entry-accommodation profile is a (weak) sequential equilibrium iff $p\le\tfrac13$ ([PAGE 544], Ex 14.3 solution). Prediction (falsifiable): entry deterrence appears sharply at the phase boundary $p^\*=\tfrac13$; for $p<\tfrac13$ both entrants enter, for $p>\tfrac13$ both stay out. This is the "counter-trap vs clock": incomplete information revives the threat that pure backward induction vetoes, and the prior $p$ is the clock hand crossing $1/3$.

Veto 3 — Monty Hall (Bayesian veto of the "50–50" belief).
Setup [PAGE 328, Ex 8.12]: priors $P(D_1)=P(D_2)=P(D_3)=\tfrac13$; host opens door 3 (empty). Bayes (operator 6):
$$P(D_1\mid O_3)=\frac{P(O_3\mid D_1)P(D_1)}{P(O_3)}=\frac{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13}{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13+1\cdot\tfrac13+0\cdot\tfrac13}=\frac{1/6}{1/2}=\frac13,\quad P(D_2\mid O_3)=\frac{2}{3}.$$
Veto: the "conditional-on-opening it's 50–50" belief is Bayesian-inconsistent — it violates conditioning by ignoring the host's response asymmetry $P(O_3\mid D_2)=1$. Prediction (falsifiable): a Bayesian contestant switches (raising win probability $\tfrac13\to\tfrac23$) whenever the surplus value $\tfrac13 V>\$50$, i.e. $V>\$150$; a "50–50" contestant never pays to switch. The book's stated decision rule — "if $P(D_1\mid O_3)=\tfrac12$ then you should not switch" [PAGE 328] — is triggered *only* under the inconsistent belief; the correct value $\tfrac13$ vetoes inaction.

Cross-check of the three vetoes against the master theorem: each eliminates a candidate that survives a weaker layer — Veto 1 is killed by subgame perfection (Layer 1), Veto 3 by plain Bayesian conditioning (operator 6), and Veto 2 is *created*, not killed, by moving from certainty (backward induction) to incomplete-information sequential equilibrium — precisely the direction predicted by the refinement lattice of operator 13 (Thm 12.5 [PAGE 440]). All three predictions are decidable from the stated payoffs and priors, hence RLVR-verifiable.
