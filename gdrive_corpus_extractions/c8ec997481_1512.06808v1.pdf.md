The source (Bonanno, *Game Theory*, 585 pp.) builds a single ladder of solution concepts, and the deep derivation is a chain of refinements whose rungs are held together by one recurring singularity: conditioning on zero-probability events. I trace the ladder and audit where each rung leaks.

**Hop 1 — Dominance to equilibrium.** The base operator is Player $i$'s payoff $\pi_i : S \to \mathbb{R}$. Strict dominance ([PAGE 23], Def 1.4) is $\forall s_{-i}\in S_{-i},\ \pi_i(a,s_{-i})>\pi_i(b,s_{-i})$. Nash (Def 1.10, [PAGE 41]) weakens this to a fixed-point condition $\pi_i(s_i^\*,s_{-i}^\*)\ge \pi_i(s_i,s_{-i}^\*)$. The Vickrey auction ([PAGE 31]) and Clarke mechanism ([PAGE 35]) are the two constructive proofs that a *weakly* dominant truthful strategy exists — the mechanism-design anchors.

**Hop 2 — Ordinal to cardinal.** Everything above is ordinal. Expected-utility theory (Thm 4.1, [PAGE 169]) upgrades preferences over lotteries $\mathcal L(O)$ to $\mathbb E[U(L)]=\sum_i p_i U(o_i)$, unique only up to affine maps $V=aU+b,\ a>0$ (Thm 4.2, [PAGE 171]). This affine freedom is not cosmetic: it is exactly why mixed-strategy equilibrium (Ch. 5) is well-posed only with cardinal payoffs.

**Hop 3 — The gray zone the whole book leans on.** Remark 5.6 [PAGE 211] states the crack plainly: rationalizability/IDSDS deductions require that "the von Neumann-Morgenstern preferences of both players be common knowledge," and "Expecting a player to know the von Neumann-Morgenstern preferences of another player is often (almost always?) very unrealistic!" So the entire epistemic superstructure (Chs. 7–12) rests on *cardinal* common knowledge that the modeling story cannot deliver. That is the deepest gray zone in the text.

**Hop 4 — The zero-probability singularity.** In dynamic games an equilibrium strategy $\sigma$ can reach an information set with probability $0$. Bayes' rule (Def 8.5, [PAGE 301]) $P(E\mid F)=P(F\mid E)P(E)/P(F)$ is *undefined* at $P(F)=0$. This single singularity forces the entire refinement cascade: weak sequential equilibrium (Ch. 10) imposes Bayesian updating only at reached sets (Def 10.4, [PAGE 362]) and leaves off-path beliefs free; sequential equilibrium (Ch. 11) constrains off-path beliefs via Kreps–Wilson consistency, defined as a *limit of completely mixed strategies*.

**Hop 5 — KW consistency is a crutch; Bonanno replaces it.** The authors themselves distrust the limit device. Kreps–Wilson: "we do so with some doubts of our own concerning what 'ought' to be the definition of a consistent assessment" ([PAGE 405]). Osborne–Rubinstein: "it appears to be a rather opaque technical assumption" ([PAGE 405]). Bonanno's fix replaces limits with an AGM plausibility order $\preceq$ (Def 12.1, [PAGE 420]) plus a *distance-preservation* axiom, choice measurability (Def 12.7, [PAGE 435]): $F(h)-F(h')=F(ha)-F(h'a)$. Theorem 12.5 ([PAGE 441]) closes the ladder: $(\sigma,\mu)$ is a sequential equilibrium **iff** it is a PBE rationalized by a choice-measurable $\preceq$ that is uniformly Bayesian. **(O1) vNM expected utility** ([PAGE 169], Thm 4.1). Over $O=\{o_1,\dots,o_m\}$, $U:O\to\mathbb R$:
$$L\succ L' \iff \mathbb E[U(L)]=\sum_{i=1}^m p_iU(o_i) > \sum_{i=1}^m q_iU(o_i)=\mathbb E[U(L')].$$
Units: $U$ dimensionless; normalization $U(o_{\text{worst}})=0,\ U(o_{\text{best}})=1$ (Def 4.3, [PAGE 173]). **Affine uniqueness** (Thm 4.2, [PAGE 171]): $V(o)=aU(o)+b,\ a>0$. Breakdown: $a\le 0$ reverses/collapses the order ($a=0$ makes every lottery indifferent — a degenerate representation).

**(O2) Mixed extension & indifference** (Def 5.4, [PAGE 201]; Thm 5.2, [PAGE 203]). With $\sigma(s)=\prod_i\sigma_i(s_i)$:
$$\Pi_i(\sigma)=\sum_{s\in S}\sigma(s)\pi_i(s);\qquad \sigma_i^\*(s_{ij})>0,\ \sigma_i^\*(s_{ik})>0 \Rightarrow \Pi_i(s_{ij},\sigma_{-i}^\*)=\Pi_i(s_{ik},\sigma_{-i}^\*).$$
Breakdown: the product form assumes independent randomization; for $n\ge3$ it cannot represent correlated beliefs (Ex 5.14, [PAGE 217]).

**(O3) Cournot first-order condition** ([PAGE 45]): $\pi_1(q_1,q_2)=(a-c)q_1-bq_1^2-bq_1q_2$, so $\partial\pi_1/\partial q_1=a-c-2bq_1-bq_2=0$. Unique interior NE; breakdown at boundary $q_i=0$ or $a\le c$ (no production).

**(O4) Knowledge / common knowledge** (Def 7.2, [PAGE 261]; Thm 7.1, [PAGE 277]). $w\in KE \iff I(w)\subseteq E$. S5 axioms (Remark 7.1, [PAGE 267]): $KE\subseteq E$, $KKE=KE$, $K\neg KE=\neg KE$. Units: operators map the Boolean algebra $2^W\to 2^W$.

**(O5) Bayes & its singularity** (Def 8.5, [PAGE 301]): $P(E\mid F)=P(F\mid E)P(E)/P(F)$, defined only for $P(F)>0$. **Breakdown regime** $P(F)=0$: Bayes undefined $\Rightarrow$ must invoke AGM revision.

**(O6) AGM plausibility** (Thm 8.1, Grove 1988; Bonanno 2009, [PAGE 305]): $f(E)=\{w\in E: w\preceq w'\ \forall w'\in E\}$ = most-plausible states in $E$. This *is* the off-path belief-selection rule that repairs (O5)'s singularity.

**(O7) Choice measurability** (Def 12.7, [PAGE 435]): $\exists\,F:H\to\mathbb N$ with $F(h)-F(h')=F(ha)-F(h'a)$ for all $h'\in I(h),\ a\in A(h)$. **(O8) Harsanyi consistency / common prior** ([PAGE 560]): $\exists\,P:T\to[0,1]$ with $P_{i,t_i}(t_{-i})=P(t_{-i}\mid t_i)=\dfrac{P(t_i,t_{-i})}{\sum_{t_{-i}'}P(t_i,t_{-i}')}$. **Breakdown:** no solution $\Rightarrow$ beliefs not Harsanyi-consistent $\Rightarrow$ Aumann's "agreeing to disagree" collapse ([PAGE 313]) no longer holds.
