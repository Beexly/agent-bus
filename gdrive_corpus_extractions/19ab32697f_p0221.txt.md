Information-theoretic insufficiency: H(J | I = i) > 0 for ambiguous stimuli, so the mutual information I(J; C | I) = H(J | I) − H(J | I, C) must be strictly positive for perception to be determinate.

Singularity and breakdown regimes:
1. ∂j/∂i = 0 defines perceptual constancy: retinal change occurs but the percept is fixed.
2. ∂j/∂c → ∞ or ill-conditioned defines context-dominated breakdown, where optical modifications no longer determine judgments (e.g., recovered blindness, sensory deprivation).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of the argument on [PAGE 211] extends backward through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and forward into contemporary vision science, creating a retrocausal genealogy in which later frameworks are projected onto James's text.

Berkeley's New Theory of Vision (1709) argued that distance is not seen directly but inferred from the association of visible and tangible ideas—an early version of the context variable C. Descartes grappled with the inverted retinal image and the problem of upright perception, a physical-layer anomaly that James inherits. Helmholtz's doctrine of unconscious inference (unbewusste Schlussfolgerung) and Lotze's theory of local signs (Lokalsignen) supplied the mechanistic vocabulary that James adapts when he rejects "quasi-mythical mental powers" while still denying that perception is a direct optical readout.

The footnote on [PAGE 211] anchors the genealogy in specific nineteenth-century clinical literature: "Philosophical Transactions, 1841"; T. K. Abbot's Sight and Touch; Dr. Dufour of Lausanne's Guérison d'un Aveugle né; and the interpretations of MM. Naville and Dunan. The case of Noé M., an under-witted peasant blind from birth, is cited because after gaining sight he could not initially tell whether his first objects moved or stood still.

Multi-week traps emerge from this lineage. Stratton's reversing-lens experiments (1890s) required days to weeks before the perceptual world re-inverted. Cataract-recovery patients described by Sacks and Gregory required weeks to months before shape-from-shading, motion detection, and size constancy stabilized. Noé M.'s failure is the prototypical multi-week trap: without an extended sensorimotor history, the context variable C remains uncalibrated and the retinal image I cannot support a stable percept. The retrocausal trap is to assume that modern predictive-coding or efference-copy mechanisms can be cleanly mapped onto James's introspective categories without loss of historical specificity.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. When a common person is asked how he sees things as they are, James reports the innocent answer: "by opening his eyes and looking" [PAGE 211]. This is direct realism—the regressed habit under crisis or ambiguity. When optical conditions become paradoxical (identical images yielding different percepts), the naive observer abandons the constructive model and asserts that perception is immediate and unmediated.

Counter-trap. James's scientific counter-trap is to reject the innocent answer and insist that perception is inferential. But this installs a second-order, recursive theory of mind: the perceiver must model not only the distal scene but also their own modeling process. The observer becomes a second-order system that represents its own representational instability.

Clock. Temporal structure is the clock variable because "the same ocular movement varies in its perceptive import" [PAGE 211]. The phase of the ocular motor cycle—saccadic suppression, corollary-discharge timing, and the integration window of visual cortex—gates whether an eye movement produces perceived stability or perceived world motion. The same motor command has different perceptive import at different clock phases.

Recursive loop. Under crisis, the meta-model collapses back into naive realism (regression). The counter-trap requires maintaining the meta-model that perception is constructed even when phenomenology insists on direct access. The recursion closes only when the perceiver can simultaneously hold the scene model and the model of their own perceptual uncertainty.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A complete perceptual theory must survive vetoes from three independent layers.

1. Physical layer (optics). The retinal image is inverted and subjected to diffraction and aberration, yet we perceive upright, stable objects [PAGE 211]. Any theory claiming that the percept is a direct copy of the retinal image is vetoed by the inversion and constancy facts.

2. Biomechanical layer (oculomotor). Extraocular muscles generate vergence ν (degrees) and accommodation A (diopters), and efference copies accompany movement. Because "the same ocular movement varies in its perceptive import" [PAGE 211], a fixed mapping from motor command to percept is vetoed. The biomechanical signal must be gated by context C; without corollary discharge and attentional gain, the movement signal is perceptively ambiguous.

3. Weather/atmospheric layer. Under "seemingly identical optical conditions" [PAGE 211], atmospheric turbulence, haze, and mirages alter the proximal stimulus without changing the distal scene. A theory that ignores atmospheric modulation is vetoed when weather variation produces different retinal images I from a fixed scene S.

Veto proof. The theory survives only if it predicts (i) variable J under fixed I (context veto), (ii) constant J under varying I (constancy veto), and (iii) weather-dependent I under fixed S (atmospheric veto). James's framework satisfies (i) and (ii) but leaves (iii) implicit; a full modern account must integrate atmospheric radiative transfer with the retinal projection operator P_θ_opt.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with formal veto conditions and predictions.

Scenario A — Fixed retinal image, variable distance context.
Construction: Present two objects that cast identical retinal images I but are at different distances, manipulated by removing binocular depth cues (monocular viewing) versus providing stereopsis. Formal veto condition: If judged size satisfies ŝ(I, C_near) = ŝ(I, C_far) for identical I, then James's context-dependence claim is falsified for size judgments. Prediction: ŝ scales with perceived distance; context C shifts size constancy, so I(J; C | I) > 0 is verifiable.

Scenario B — Efference-copy disruption.
Construction: Match retinal image velocity between an active saccade (self-generated eye movement with efference copy) and a passive retinal slip produced by gentle pressure on the globe. Formal veto condition: If the perceptive import of identical ocular movement is invariant across active and passive conditions, the motor-context variable is unnecessary. Prediction: Active movement produces perceived world stability via saccadic suppression and corollary discharge; passive movement produces apparent world motion. The same motor signal has different import depending on efference-copy availability.

Scenario C — Sight recovery after congenital blindness.
Construction: Test a Noé M.-type observer within hours versus weeks after cataract removal. Formal veto condition: If immediate perfect size, shape, and position constancy is present at first sight, then perception is directly optical and C is unnecessary. Prediction: Initially the observer cannot tell whether an object moved or stood still [PAGE 211 footnote] and lacks stable size constancy; stable J(I, C) emerges only after weeks of sensorimotor contingency learning, confirming that C must be acquired.

RLVR verification: Each scenario yields a binary pass/fail on the veto condition and a continuous prediction on the magnitude of context-dependence, verifiable with standard psychophysical methods.
