<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 work stands in direct opposition to the dominant Pavlovian/Skinnerian paradigm, which viewed behavior as a chain of efficient causes (stimulus-response). Tolman introduced final causality—the idea that behavior is goal-directed and organized by *expectations* of outcomes. This is a proto-retrocausal stance: the future goal (food) structures the present perception (white side as sign).

The historical lineage flows through several key stages:
1.  **Husserlian Phenomenology & Gestalt Psychology:** Tolman borrowed the term "gestalt" to argue for holistic, meaningful perceptions rather than associative atomism. The "sign-gestalt" is a unified mental object containing the relationship.
2.  **Piagetian Genetic Epistemology:** The construction of schemas through assimilation and accommodation mirrors the building-up of sign-gestalts. Learning is the reorganization of cognitive structures to fit environmental regularities.
3.  **Cybernetics & Predictive Processing:** Modern theories frame perception as controlled hallucination, where the brain minimizes prediction error. The "belief" in the sign-relationship is literally a low-prediction-error state generated by internal models. Tolman anticipated this by emphasizing the organism's "commerce" with the world based on hypotheses.
4.  **Quantum Biology & Retrocausal Models:** Recent proposals suggest that certain biochemical processes (e.g., photosynthesis, enzyme catalysis) may exploit weak measurements or path integrals where future states influence present dynamics. While speculative, this provides a potential physical substrate for teleological pressure (\( \kappa_T \)). The "energy-cost of belief" model suggests that maintaining a predictive model is metabolically expensive, and thus such models are only sustained if they consistently yield high-value predictions—a natural selection pressure at the algorithmic level.

**Multi-Week Traps:** In experimental settings, researchers might assume that once a conditioned response is learned, it is fixed. However, the sign-gestalt remains fragile. If over several weeks the context around S2 changes subtly (different lighting, handler scent), the "immediate discriminanda" of O₂ change, potentially breaking the gestalt even if the S2-S1 contingency remains intact. The organism isn't failing to associate S2 and S1; it's correctly rejecting a corrupted sign because its total discriminand-manipulandum profile no longer matches the stored expectation. This is a common trap in animal cognition studies and RL training: confusing association strength with contextual robustness.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Tolman’s model inherently contains a first-order Theory of Mind (ToM): the rat models the environment’s structure ("White predicts Food"). For a second-order ToM, the system must ask: "What does another agent believe about the white side?" This becomes crucial in social or competitive contexts.

*   **Crisis Habit Regression:** Under stress or when rewards are withheld, systems often revert to simpler, more automatic habits (e.g., neophobia, stereotypic pacing). In ToM terms, this is a collapse from a rich, relational model ("I am navigating based on goals") to a flat, stimulus-bound model ("I see white, I freeze"). This regression is a defense mechanism against uncertainty.
*   **Counter-Trap Formation:** An agent that anticipates another's crisis regression can exploit it. If Agent A knows that Agent B will ignore the sign S2 under high ambiguity, A can create ambiguity (e.g., partial reinforcement schedules) to induce B's regression, thereby gaining an advantage. This requires modeling not just the world, but the fragility of another's model of the world.
*   **Clock & Timing:** The "clock" in this recursive game is the timescale of belief decay versus exploitation. A fast clock (rapid extinction) favors aggressive counter-traps, while a slow clock (stable signs) allows for deeper cooperative strategies built on shared expectations. Tolman’s observation that the response "goes off only so long as that sign-relationship is 'believed'" defines the fundamental tempo of this cognitive dance. If beliefs are costly (as our energy model suggests), then maintaining them in a noisy, deceptive environment is a constant race against entropy and exploitation.
