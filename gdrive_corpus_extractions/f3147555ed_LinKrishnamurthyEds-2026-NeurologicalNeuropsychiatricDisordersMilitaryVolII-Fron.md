The diagnostic tell is the sign reversal. On [PAGE 15, Table 4] the **unadjusted** hazard ratio for concussion → any dementia is 0.52 (0.46–0.57), and the **adjusted** AHR is 1.64 (1.47–1.83). A factor‑three reversal in direction under adjustment is not a nuisance; it is the dominant feature of the dataset. It is consistent with strong age/follow‑up confounding (concussed retirees are younger and accrue person‑time without reaching dementia incidence) plus surveillance bias (more contact → more codes). The authors do not decompose this. They do, on [PAGE 17], report the two‑diagnosis sensitivity analysis at AHR = 3.01 (2.83–3.20), a 35% attenuation from 4.61 — which is exactly the direction a misclassification‑inflated estimate should move. This is the first veto: the 4.61 figure is a **coded‑contact hazard ratio**, not a neuropathological hazard ratio, and no amount of adjustment recovers the latter from an administrative record.

Second gray zone: **N = 9**. EXERT‑Stroke randomized nine participants, five active and four sham, with 66.7% retention and a mean of 6.1 (SD 4.3) exercise sessions out of ten [PAGE 30]. The paper is honest — it pre‑declares no formal hypothesis tests — but the abstract's phrase "the intervention had few safety issues" is a statement about nine people over thirty days. A serious adverse event with true rate 1/50 has ~17% probability of appearing zero times in nine; the trial cannot exclude clinically meaningful harm. The 20%‑of‑participants stopping rule [PAGE 30] would require two events in nine to fire. The safety claim is therefore an absence‑of‑evidence claim, correctly scoped in the body and loosely scoped in the abstract.

Third: **n/p ratio in the C4 factor analysis**. Twenty‑two items were submitted to principal‑axis factoring in n = 53 [PAGE 51]. The cluster may partly encode documentation practice.

Fifth: **the network path lengths**. Hamama‑Raz and Hamama report mothers' average path length 9.44 versus fathers' 11.82 in a 13‑node network [PAGE 70]. On hop counts, a 13‑node graph with density 0.500 (39 of 78 edges) has expected shortest‑path length near 2–3, not 9–12; the only reconciliation is that these are **weight‑transformed** distances (typically inverse edge weights), which means the statistic is dominated by weak edges and is not a topological quantity at all. The interpretation — "distress signals travel more rapidly" — rides on a metric whose scale is arbitrary. The mean predictability of 0.081 versus 0.060 [PAGE 74] means the network explains 8.1% versus 6.0% of node variance; the reported Cohen's d = 1.04 is the standardized difference between two groups **on those predictability values**, not on health outcomes. It is a large effect on a small quantity. And of four nominally significant edge differences, "none survived FDR correction (all q > 0.10)" [PAGE 73]. The structural claims rest on centrality invariance tests at q < 0.10 with p = 0.058 and p = 0.065 [PAGE 70–71] — borderline by construction.

Sixth: **the exercise–cortisol direction**. Zhao et al. state exercise "stimulates cortisol production and helps regulate the dysfunctional HPA system" [PAGE 23]. Both halves cannot be simply true: PTSD is frequently associated with **hypo**cortisolemia and enhanced glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity, so an intervention that raises cortisol could plausibly worsen rather than normalize the axis. The review does not adjudicate. And it concedes its own evidentiary floor: "the specifics of the exercise program, such as the optimal type, dosage, and duration to alleviate PTSD symptoms, remain unclear" [PAGE 20], with "a lack of higher quality randomised controlled trials" [PAGE 24]. Fifty‑four articles from 193 screened, narratively synthesized, no risk‑of‑bias instrument, no funnel assessment.

Seventh: **CBS arithmetic**. On [PAGE 84], ~239 million with moderate‑to‑severe visual impairment globally, ~20% (~47.2 million) expected to experience CBS, ~11% of visually impaired UK veterans affected, UK prevalence ~1 million rising to ~4 million by 2050. The 20% × 239M multiplication is a prevalence‑of‑prevalence product: it assumes independence between visual‑impairment severity distribution and CBS risk, which the same page contradicts by noting age and female sex as independent risk factors. The veteran figure (11%) is a different estimator from a different sampling frame. These numbers should not be combined.

The volume's genuine achievement is architectural rather than numerical: it demonstrates, across nine papers, that the correct unit of analysis in military neurology is the **trajectory**, not the diagnosis — TBI→epilepsy versus epilepsy→TBI produce measurably different phenotype distributions [PAGE 61], and the polytrauma triad "is best understood not as three separate problems occurring side by side, but as an interacting, polysymptomatic condition" [PAGE 7]. That claim survives every audit above. The effect sizes do not.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**Operator 1 — Cox proportional hazards with coded‑outcome ascertainment.**
For subject \(i\), let \(T_i\) be years from service entry to first dementia code (unit: yr) and \(X_i\in\{0,1\}\) the concussion indicator. The model estimated is

$$
\lambda_i(t\mid X_i)=\lambda_0(t)\,\exp\!\big(\beta X_i + \gamma^\top Z_i\big),\qquad [\lambda]=\mathrm{yr}^{-1},\;[\beta]=[\gamma]=\text{dimensionless},
$$

with \(Z_i\) the covariate vector (branch, birth year, paygrade, race, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, hyperlipidemia, any mental health condition, obesity, sleep disturbance, substance abuse) enumerated on [PAGE 15]. The reported estimate is \(\widehat{\mathrm{AHR}}=e^{\hat\beta}=4.61\) (95% CI 4.46–4.77) for "any cognitive disorder" [PAGE 15, Table 4] and \(1.64\) (1.47–1.83) for "any dementia excluding memory loss and MCI." **Assumption:** proportionality, i.e. \(\beta(t)\equiv\beta\). **Breakdown regime:** if \(\beta(t)\) declines with time since injury — the expected shape for a neurodegenerative process with long latency — the Cox estimator returns a person‑time‑weighted average that is biased toward the early, high‑hazard period and is not interpretable as a constant multiplicative risk. The unadjusted/adjusted reversal (0.52 → 1.64) is prima facie evidence of either strong confounding or non‑proportionality; the paper reports no Schoenfeld residual test and no log‑minus‑log plot.

**Operator 2 — the ascertainment decomposition (derived here, not in the source).**
Let \(D\) be true neuropathology, \(C\) a recorded code, \(M\in\{0,1\}\) whether the retiree had a billable encounter in the window, and \(s=\Pr(C\mid D,M{=}1)\) the coding sensitivity. Under the simplifying assumptions (i) constant \(s\) across exposure groups, (ii) \(M\perp D\mid X\), and (iii) no false positives, the observed hazard ratio decomposes as

$$
\widehat{\mathrm{HR}}_{\text{obs}}
=\frac{\lambda_C^{(1)}}{\lambda_C^{(0)}}
=\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_D^{(1)}}{\lambda_D^{(0)}}}_{\text{true HR}}\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_0}}_{\text{contact‑rate ratio}}\cdot
\underbrace{\frac{s_1}{s_0}}_{\text{coding‑sensitivity ratio}},
$$

where \(\mu_x\) is the encounter rate (\(\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\)) in exposure stratum \(x\). **Units:** HRs and ratios dimensionless; \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\). **Breakdown regime:** assumption (ii) fails exactly in the case at hand — a documented concussion is itself a cause of subsequent neurological encounters — so \(\mu_1/\mu_0>1\) by design. Setting \(\mu_1/\mu_0=2\) and \(s_1/s_0=1\) maps a true HR of 2.3 onto an observed 4.6, which is the observed value. The paper partially acknowledges this with a single‑diagnosis sensitivity analysis reaching AHR = 3.01 (2.83–3.20) [PAGE 17], and separately concedes that codes "may result in misclassification bias" [PAGE 16]. The decomposition is not performed; the direction of residual bias is asserted rather than derived.

**Operator 3 — Karvonen reserve dosing (EXERT‑Stroke).**

$$
\mathrm{HRR}=\mathrm{HR}_{\max}-\mathrm{HR}_{\text{rest}},\qquad
\mathrm{HR}_{\text{target}}=f\,\mathrm{HRR}+\mathrm{HR}_{\text{rest}},\qquad
\mathrm{HR}_{\max}=220-\text{age},
$$

with \(\mathrm{HR}\) in \(\mathrm{beats\cdot min^{-1}}\) and \(f\in[0,1]\) dimensionless [PAGE 29]. The delivered protocol is 10 min warm‑up at 30% target, 25 min of 1‑min "on" at 60% of peak watts alternating with 1‑min "off" at 45%, cool‑down 10 min at 30%, with the additional constraint that mean "on" HR "should not exceed 60% HR reserve" [PAGE 29]. **Assumptions:** \(220-\text{age}\) is a population regression, not an individual maximum; the true \(\mathrm{HR}_{\max}\) is measured at the graded exercise test. **Singularity:** the formula is undefined for age ≥ 220 and has no physiological meaning above roughly age 90. **Breakdown regime:** beta‑adrenergic blockade flattens the HR–workload relation, so a subject at 60% HRR may be at a substantially higher fraction of true \(\dot V\mathrm{O}_{2\max}\); the dose actually delivered diverges from the dose prescribed, and the trial reports no adjustment for chronotropic competence.

**Operator 4 — quasi‑static TMS electric field.**

$$
\nabla\cdot\big(\sigma(\mathbf r)\,\nabla\phi\big)=0,\qquad
\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi,\qquad [\sigma]=\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}},\;[\phi]=\mathrm V,\;[\mathbf E]=\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}},
$$

solved by finite elements over a tetrahedral head mesh with tissue conductivities assigned from [PAGE 40]: skin \(0.465\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\), skull \(0.010\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\), CSF \(1.654\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\), gray matter \(0.265\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\), white matter \(0.126\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\). Optimization evaluates coil positions "on a fine 1‑mm grid and testing orientations in 4‑degree increments" to maximize field intensity "within a 20‑mm radius around the target brain coordinates" [PAGE 40]. **Assumption stated in the source:** "Isotropic conductivity values are assigned to the elements based on tissue type" [PAGE 40]. **Breakdown regime:** white matter is strongly anisotropic — longitudinal conductivity exceeds transverse by roughly an order of magnitude — so an isotropic model systematically misestimates the field along fiber tracts and can shift the optimum by several millimetres, precisely the quantity the trial is designed to test. **Singularity:** the conductivity jump at the skull/CSF interface produces a field discontinuity; FEM accuracy there is mesh‑dependent, and no mesh‑convergence study is reported.

**Operator 5 — Bayesian adaptive stopping boundaries.**

$$
\Pi_a=\Pr\big(\mu_a>\mu_s\mid \mathcal D_{n}\big),\qquad
a\in\{\text{rsfMRI},\text{Beam F3}\},
$$

with the decision rules: drop the inferior active arm if \(\max_a\Pi_a\ge 0.60\) relative to the *other active arm*; stop for futility if neither active arm exceeds 0.60 versus sham; stop for success if either reaches 0.9975; declare success at the maximum sample if either reaches 0.99 [PAGE 42]. Interim analyses at \(N=90\) and \(N=135\), maximum \(N=198\), initial allocation 1:1:1, post‑drop allocation 2:1 active:sham [PAGE 37]. **Units:** posterior probabilities dimensionless; the design controls "2.5% type 1 error accounting for both the multiplicity of 2 arms and the 2 interim analyses, based on 100,000 simulations" [PAGE 42]. **Assumption:** a non‑informative prior "that any arm is superior to any other arm" [PAGE 42] — which is not the same as a flat prior on effect size, and the two differ materially when the outcome is a bounded ordinal scale. **Breakdown regime:** if the MADRS change distribution is bimodal (responders/non‑responders rather than a shift), the posterior probability of superiority is unstable and the futility rule can fire on a genuinely effective arm during a run of non‑responders; the 60% futility threshold is generous enough that this is a low but non‑zero risk.

**Operator 6 — Gaussian graphical model and EBIC.**

$$
\mathbf\Theta=\mathbf\Sigma^{-1},\qquad
\rho_{ij\cdot V\setminus\{i,j\}}=-\frac{\Theta_{ij}}{\sqrt{\Theta_{ii}\Theta_{jj}}},\qquad
\mathrm{EBIC}=-2\ell(\hat{\mathbf\Theta})+|E|\log n+4|E|\gamma\log p,
$$

with \(\gamma=0.25\) (the EBICglasso hyperparameter reported on [PAGE 69]), \(p=13\) nodes, \(n=201\) per group. **Units:** \(\Theta\) has units of inverse variance; \(\rho\) and EBIC are dimensionless (EBIC in nats if \(\ell\) is a log‑likelihood in nats). **Assumption:** multivariate normality of the node vector; the paper explicitly notes that "all measures significantly deviated from normality (minimum A = 1.706, p = 0.0002)" and identifies 93 multivariate outliers [PAGE 69], then proceeds with Gaussian graphical estimation anyway. **Singularity:** \(\mathbf\Sigma\) is singular when \(p\ge n\) or under exact collinearity; here \(p=13\ll 201\), so regularization is a sparsity device rather than a numerical necessity, and the lasso shrinks true weak edges to zero — which is then read as "no connection."

**Operator 7 — small‑world coefficient and its scale dependence.**

$$
\sigma_{\text{SW}}=\frac{C/C_{\text{rand}}}{L/L_{\text{rand}}},\qquad
C=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \frac{2e_i}{k_i(k_i-1)},\qquad
L=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}d_{ij},
$$

with \(C\) dimensionless, \(L\) in hops (or in inverse‑weight units if \(d_{ij}=1/w_{ij}\)). Reported: clustering 0.611 (fathers) versus 0.559 (mothers); average path length 11.82 versus 9.44 in a 13‑node graph [PAGE 70]. **Breakdown regime:** a 13‑node graph at density 0.500 has mean degree 6 and hop‑count diameter 2–3; values of 9–12 are attainable only if \(d_{ij}\) is a weight‑transformed metric. The statistic is therefore not comparable across studies using hop counts, and the substantive claim ("distress signals travel more rapidly") inherits the arbitrariness of the weight transform.

**Operator 8 — the C4 bias check as an entropy probe.**
For an item endorsed by \(k\) of \(n=78\) respondents, the empirical endorsement probability is \(\hat p=k/n\) with Shannon entropy \(H(\hat p)=-\hat p\log_2\hat p-(1-\hat p)\log_2(1-\hat p)\) in bits. The bias‑check item 16 was endorsed "Often" or "All the time" by exactly one participant [PAGE 52], giving \(\hat p=0.0128\) and \(H\approx 0.084\ \mathrm{bit}\) — near‑zero entropy, i.e. near‑zero discriminative information. That is the design intent: a non‑declarative memory task should not vary with mTBI. **Breakdown regime:** low entropy also means low power; a single endorsement in 78 is compatible with a true base rate anywhere from ~0.03% to ~7% at 95% confidence, so the item cannot yet function as a validity screen without a much larger calibration sample.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The volume's citation graph reveals five lineages, each with a characteristic latency between the originating observation and the current claim.

**Lineage I — TBI to dementia.** Hoge et al. (2008, *NEJM*) established the 15% concussion prevalence among returning soldiers that Belding et al. use as their own mismatch benchmark [PAGE 10]. Plassman et al. (2000) supplied the WWII Navy/Marine cohort precedent. Barnes et al. (2014, *Neurology*) produced the 60% greater likelihood figure that the introduction cites. Graham and Sharp (2019) supplied the "50% greater risk … 5% of dementia cases" attribution. Belding et al. (2024) then land at AHR 4.61 for the composite. The trajectory is instructive: each generation uses a larger, more administrative dataset and reports a larger effect size, while the outcome definition drifts from clinically adjudicated dementia to a single ICD code. The 2026 volume inherits the largest number and the weakest outcome. **Multi‑week trap:** the latency structure. The mean retiree age at study close is 65.8 years [PAGE 12], and the authors concede this "is just barely over the range for late‑onset dementia" [PAGE 14]. Any follow‑up shorter than roughly a decade cannot distinguish a true hazard elevation from a surveillance artifact, and the field's publication cycle is ~18 months. The trap is that each new paper publishes before the previous cohort has aged into its own outcome window.

**Lineage II — the "second hit" and temporal sequence.** The epilepsy literature's move from lesion‑localization to network models is compressed into a sentence on [PAGE 59], citing Vlooswijk et al. (2011). Panahi et al. then import the RDoC framework (NIMH) as an organizing ontology and the PTE‑dementia link — Schneider et al. (2024), 4.5‑fold risk — as the mechanistic warrant [PAGE 63]. The retrocausal move is subtle: the 2024 dementia finding is used to explain a 2026 clustering result, but the clustering was computed on 39 NLP features that have no necessary relation to dementia pathology. The genealogy supplies plausibility, not identification.

**Lineage III — rTMS for depression.** O'Reardon et al. (2007) and Lisanby et al. (2009) established daily left DLPFC rTMS. Cole et al. (2022) produced the SNT result — 79% remission, Cohen's d = 1.4, trial stopped at 32 patients [PAGE 36] — which is the single most consequential number in the ADEPT protocol, because it sets the prior expectation for what an accelerated schedule can do. Oberman et al. then reduce from 10 sessions/day to 8 "because of logistical and staffing constraints at the research sites" [PAGE 40], a dose reduction that is not powered or justified against the SNT benchmark. **Multi‑week trap:** the 5‑day compressed schedule is a delivery innovation whose acceptability depends on command approval for alternate duty location [PAGE 43]; the efficacy question and the military‑HR question are entangled, and a negative result will be ambiguous between "aiTBS doesn't work here" and "the staffing‑limited dose was insufficient."

**Lineage IV — exercise for PTSD.** Caddick and Smith (2014) supply the SWB/PWB scaffolding; Hegberg et al. (2019) supply the mechanistic rationale; Zhao et al. (2025) supply the synthesis. The genealogy is almost entirely mechanistic‑plausibility citation rather than effect‑size accumulation, which is why the review can assert hippocampal volume gains of "approximately 2%" [PAGE 23] from a single Colcombe study without a pooled estimate. **Multi‑week trap:** the outdoor protocols in Table 1 run 3 days to 12 weeks, and the review warns that nature exposure may constitute "returning to the scene" of trauma [PAGE 25] — a iatrogenic risk that no included trial was designed to detect.

**Lineage V — Charles Bonnet Syndrome.** Menon et al. (2003) define; Ffytche (2005) supplies deafferentation; Manford and Andermann (1998) supply the phenomenological range; Jones et al. (2025a, 2025b) supply the veteran‑specific prevalence (11%) and the management audit (~50% receive anything) [PAGE 84–85]. The genealogy here is unusually clean because the mechanism has been stable for two decades. The gap is implementation, not science — Gordon and Felfeli's 499‑physician survey (55% unaware, 85% never discuss) is cited as the persisting failure [PAGE 84]. **Multi‑week trap:** the co‑production timetable. Phase 1 to Phase 2 required an inter‑workshop development period during which participant involvement was admittedly reduced [PAGE 86]; the toolkit's authority rests on a 7‑veteran/13‑clinician Phase 1 and a 4‑veteran/5‑clinician Phase 2, and the reduced Phase 2 veteran attendance is attributed to non‑attendance at the activity hub on the day.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model three agents: the patient \(P\), the clinician \(C\), and the system \(S\). Each holds a belief about the others' belief about the symptom. The volume's most interesting content is where those nested beliefs diverge from the measurement.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under acute threat, agents revert to the highest‑habit‑strength behaviour in their repertoire, not the highest‑expected‑value one. Three documented instances. (i) *Fathers and alcohol.* In the fathers' network, "alcohol use appeared isolated at the network's periphery" with "zero predictability" from other nodes, while in mothers it sat at 0.084 inside the symptom cluster [PAGE 72–74]. The correct reading is not that fathers drink less — the base rates are 4.5% versus 4.0%, statistically indistinguishable [PAGE 70] — but that paternal drinking, where it occurs, is *decoupled from the stress network*. Regression to a habitual behaviour that does not route through the symptom system is invisible to any symptom‑based screen. (ii) *Exercise as re‑exposure.* The Zhao review's own caution — avoid "returning to the scene" of an unfamiliar situation [PAGE 25] — identifies the failure mode of the intervention with the best acceptability profile, precisely because military personnel are "more familiar with exercise training" [PAGE 25]. Familiarity is the mechanism and the risk. (iii) *Disclosure avoidance in CBS.* Veterans conceal hallucinations "due to fears of being misdiagnosed with psychiatric conditions such as schizophrenia, psychosis, or dementia" [PAGE 84]. The habitual response to a stigmatized percept is silence, and silence is what the ~50%‑untreated figure measures.

**Counter‑trap.** The counter‑trap is the intervention that relieves the presenting node while loading the latent system. Four candidates. (i) *Opioid management of post‑stroke pain.* The EXERT‑Stroke introduction notes current guidelines "generally lead to pharmacologic treatments including some clinical management with opioids, which can lead to addiction issues" [PAGE 28]; the alternative under test is non‑pharmacological and the paper states flatly "There is currently no FDA approved treatment for post‑stroke headache" [PAGE 32]. (ii) *Treating the bridge in the mothers' network.* Healthcare visits carry bridge strength 1.302, "more than double any other variable" [PAGE 72]. A system that incentivizes reducing visits will not reduce distress; it will sever the only conduit by which distress reaches care, converting an integrated network into an invisible one — i.e. converting mothers into fathers. The paper's own framing supports this: the mothers' architecture is simultaneously higher‑risk and more *intervenable*. (iii) *Arm dropping in ADEPT.* The rule drops the inferior active arm at 60% posterior [PAGE 42]. Sixty percent is a low bar for a between‑arm comparison at N = 90 with an outcome that has a 10% dropout rate [PAGE 38]; a slow‑responder arm could be eliminated on noise. (iv) *Early dementia screening.* Belding et al. argue for "long‑term surveillance, early cognitive assessment" [PAGE 6], but their own estimate is a coded‑contact hazard; expanding screening expands contact, which raises the measured hazard without changing incidence. The counter‑trap is that the intervention validates the estimator that justified it.

**Clock.** The volume runs at least seven incommensurable clocks, and most of its inferential errors are clock mismatches. (1) ICHD‑3 persistent post‑stroke headache requires >3 months [PAGE 28]. (2) Post‑traumatic epilepsy is operationalized as epilepsy "within 6 months after a TBI diagnosis" [PAGE 59]. (3) Post‑concussive recovery is framed at 3 months, with ~30% failing to recover spontaneously [PAGE 48]. (4) The help‑seeking delay in PTSD is "up to 12 years of mental anguish before seeking help" [PAGE 20]. (5) The ADEPT intervention is 5 business days [PAGE 40], with follow‑up to 6 months [PAGE 41]. (6) The C4 test–retest interval is 4 weeks [PAGE 51]. (7) The Belding archive spans 40 years, 1980–2020, with inpatient codes from FY1980 and outpatient only from FY2001 [PAGE 11]. The seventh clock is the trap: **outpatient ascertainment changes mid‑cohort**, and the GWOT period is exactly when both TBI awareness and coding practice changed. Any hazard ratio spanning that discontinuity mixes epidemiological signal with administrative reform.

**Nested belief recursion.** A service member reasons: *the clinician will read my cognitive complaint as either injury or malingering; therefore I will report the somatic version.* This is exactly the C4's design problem — patients "often reported concerns with 'memory' and 'attention' but were unable to expand on those concerns" [PAGE 52] — and exactly the reason the instrument was built at the activity level. The clinician reasons: *the patient is minimizing; the spouse's report is more reliable.* The system reasons: *coded diagnoses are the billable truth.* Three levels, three different objects of belief (symptom, report, code), and the volume's strongest methodological contribution — the C4's "seamless transition" from assessment to treatment target [PAGE 52] — is precisely the device that collapses the recursion by making the patient's own prioritization the operative datum.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross‑layer veto is a constraint from a lower organizational layer that cannot be overridden by argument at a higher one. Four adjudications follow.

**Physical veto — blast overpressure.** The volume distinguishes high‑level blast (HLB, "blast exposure resulting from incoming munitions") from low‑level blast (LLB, "blast overpressure exposure that results from outgoing munitions, such as firing heavy caliber weapons") [PAGE 10]. The physics is an impulse problem: the relevant dose is \(\int p(t)\,\mathrm dt\) with \([p]=\mathrm{Pa}\) and impulse in \(\mathrm{Pa\cdot s}\), and injury thresholds scale with peak overpressure, positive‑phase duration, and the number of exposures within a recovery window. Belt‑fitting and occupational assignment (artillery, special forces versus "personnel and administration, food service" [PAGE 10]) determine exposure, not clinical intent. The **veto** is this: no pharmacological or neuromodulatory protocol can restore tissue that a repeated mechanical impulse has already disrupted, and no rehabilitation schedule can outrun an exposure schedule that continues. The paper's LLB finding — AHR 1.11 for any cognitive disorder, 1.16 for memory loss, but 1.01 and non‑significant for dementia excluding memory loss [PAGE 15] — is consistent with an acute/subacute mechanical effect that does not (yet) express as neurodegenerative code. The physical layer sets the ceiling on every downstream claim.

**Biomechanical veto — the E‑field shunt.** In TMS, the skull's conductivity of \(0.010\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\) against CSF at \(1.654\ \mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\) [PAGE 40] means most of the induced current is diverted at the scalp–skull interface; the fraction reaching cortex is governed by the layered geometry, not by coil power. The ADEPT protocol compensates by delivering 90% of resting motor threshold "corrected for scalp-to-target distance" [PAGE 40], but the correction is a scalar distance adjustment applied to an isotropic model. **Veto:** if the individualized DLPFC target lies over a frontal sinus or a thickened diploë, the delivered field can fall below the excitatory threshold regardless of the nominal percentage, and the trial will record a treatment‑refractory subject where the physics delivered an under‑dose. This is adjudicable only with per‑subject field dosimetry, which the protocol computes but does not report as a covariate.

**Physiological veto — the chronotropic ceiling.** In EXERT‑Stroke, the target is a percentage of heart rate reserve [PAGE 29]. In a post‑stroke veteran population with high rates of beta‑blockade, hypertension, and deconditioning, the HR–work relationship is flattened; the subject can be at 60% HRR and near their ventilatory threshold, or at 60% HRR and barely working. **Veto:** the exercise dose is physiologically undefined in a substantial fraction of the target population, and the protocol's only safety valve is workload reduction "e.g., 10% reduction" at the participant's discretion [PAGE 29] — which systematically under‑doses the tolerant and over‑doses the stoic.

**Magnetic and thermal veto — the MRI gate.** ADEPT requires three MRI scans per participant including susceptibility‑weighted imaging, and the protocol devotes a full subsection to metal screening: shrapnel, braces, piercings, implants, with referral to radiology and a "Positive Metal Screening Tracker" [PAGE 38]. **Veto:** the population with the highest concussion prevalence is the population with the highest retained‑fragment prevalence. Individualized rsfMRI targeting therefore has a *negative* selection gradient against the most severely exposed service members — the ones the intervention most needs to reach. This is the sharpest cross‑layer conflict in the volume: the physics of ferromagnetic resonance excludes the epidemiology of blast injury.

**Weather/environmental layer.** The volume contains no direct meteorological analysis. The nearest coupling is in the headache literature it cites: persistent post‑stroke headache is "more frequent and more severe than acute stroke‑related headaches" [PAGE 28], and the CBS paper documents hallucinations "occurring outside the home" in ~40% of cases, with associated "risks to mobility and social participation" [PAGE 84]. Environmental triggers (barometric flux, heat, glare) act on both conditions through the same afferent pathway — trigeminovascular activation and reduced visual input respectively — which means any trial conducted across seasons without a weather covariate is underpowered by construction. There is no barometric adjustment anywhere in the volume; this is an unadjudicated veto, not a refuted one.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is stated as a counterfactual with a prediction that is checkable against numbers printed in the drive source. A scenario fails the veto if the prediction is contradicted.

**Veto 1 — the misclassification counterfactual (Belding).**
*Intervention:* replace the single‑diagnosis case definition with a requirement of ≥2 dementia codes, holding the cohort and covariates fixed.
*Prediction:* the composite cognitive‑disorder AHR attenuates from 4.61 toward ~3.0, with the specific‑dementia estimates attenuating proportionally more (because specific dementias have smaller counts and thus higher per‑case misclassification).
*Verification anchor:* the source already reports this exact analysis — "the association between concussion and any cognitive disorder remained significant (AHR = 3.01, 95% CI: 2.83, 3.20)" [PAGE 17]. A 35% attenuation under a stricter case definition is the signature of coding‑noise inflation, not of a robust neurodegenerative hazard.
*Failure mode:* if the two‑diagnosis AHR had come back at ~4.5, the coding‑noise hypothesis would be refuted and the surveillance‑bias interpretation would lose its principal support. It did not.

**Veto 2 — the FDR counterfactual (Hamama‑Raz and Hamama).**
*Intervention:* apply Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10 across all 78 possible edges, as the authors state they did.
*Prediction:* zero edges survive. The four nominally significant edges (sleep–smoking p = 0.004; medication–pain severity p = 0.016; adverse events–sleep p = 0.040; intolerance of uncertainty–pain type p = 0.047) all have q > 0.10 given 78 tests.
*Verification anchor:* "Edge invariance testing revealed four edges that differed significantly between fathers' and mothers' networks at the nominal significance level (p < 0.05), though none survived FDR correction (all q > 0.10)" [PAGE 73]. Confirmed by inspection of Table 3 [PAGE 77].
*Downstream prediction:* the *structural* claims (density 0.551 vs 0.500; path length 11.82 vs 9.44; predictability 0.060 vs 0.081) must then carry the entire argument, and those are descriptive statistics of two separately estimated graphs with no between‑group test attached. If a formal invariance test were applied to the density difference, it would not reach significance at these edge counts. The clinical recommendation therefore rests on an untested contrast.

**Veto 3 — the Bayesian futility counterfactual (ADEPT).**
*Intervention:* run the trial to the first interim analysis at N = 90 with the stated priors and a true effect of exactly 4 MADRS points for one active arm over sham, the other arm null.
*Prediction:* (a) expected total sample ≈ 150, power 81–82%; (b) the arm‑drop rule fires only if the two active arms differ by enough to push the posterior past 0.60; (c) the futility rule does **not** fire, because one arm is at 0.60+ versus sham; (d) if instead the true effect is 5 points, power rises to ~95% at expected N = 137; (e) if both active arms are null, expected N ≈ 147 and the trial stops for futility at N = 90 or N = 135.
*Verification anchors:* "if one of the arms had a 4-point improvement over sham, the power was 81–82% with an expected sample size of 150 patients. Alternatively, 95% power and an expected sample size of 137 patients was obtained if one of the arms had a 5-point improvement over sham. If both arms were equal to sham … then the expected trial sample size was 147 patients" [PAGE 38]; adaptation thresholds on [PAGE 42]; the primary hypothesis is "a statistically significant and clinically meaningful ≥4 points depressive symptom reduction" [PAGE 37].
*Veto condition:* the design is falsified as *efficient* if the observed interim decision at N = 90 disagrees with the simulated distribution — specifically, if the trial stops for futility while an arm has a posterior between 0.40 and 0.60, which would indicate the simulation's assumed effect‑size grid missed the true operating region.

**Supplementary veto — the access counterfactual (EXERT‑Stroke).**
*Intervention:* deliver the identical rTMS + MIIT protocol with home‑based or telehealth components, holding eligibility fixed.
*Prediction:* consent rate rises materially above the observed 8.8%, because 53 of the 103 refusals (51.5%) cited distance.
*Verification anchor:* "Ten (8.8%) of the eligible patients consented to enrolling in the trial and 103 (91.1%) refused enrollment.
