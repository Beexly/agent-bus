\[
\mu(P) = \mathbb{E}_P[X] = \int_\Omega X(\omega)\, dP(\omega)
\]
and the variance is
\[
\sigma^2(P) = \mathbb{E}_P\left[(X - \mu(P))^2\right].
\]

\[
V(X) = \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[X],

\[
H(P) = -\int_\Omega p(\omega)\log p(\omega)\, d\omega

Veto trigger: at week \(k<T\), the true process shifts to \(P'\notin \mathcal{C}_{1-\alpha}(\hat{P})\), where \(\mathcal{C}_{1-\alpha}\) is the confidence set. Prediction: realized firm profit diverges from the theoretical competitive baseline; excess return variance exceeds \(\sigma^2(\hat{P})\) by a factor increasing in \(D_{\text{KL}}(P'\|\hat{P})\), and the divergence persists until \(\hat{P}\) is re-estimated under the new regime.

Scenario 2 — Uncompetable Knightian profit. A market assumes absence of true uncertainty and prices all claims under known \(P\). Veto trigger: an insider possesses non-quantifiable knowledge of an imminent structural break (true uncertainty) and acts before public information arrives. Prediction: the insider earns persistent profit that cannot be arbitraged away, because competitors cannot form a probability measure over the break and therefore cannot replicate the position; the profit margin remains above the zero-profit competitive baseline until the break becomes publicly measurable.

Scenario 3 — Stationary-learning collapse. A reinforcement-learning agent assumes stationarity of \(P\) over an \(H\)-week horizon and converges to policy \(\pi_\theta\). Veto trigger: a regime shift at week \(h<H\) invalidates the transition model underlying \(\pi_\theta\). Prediction: decision performance collapses at week \(h\); recovery time \(\tau_{\text{rec}} \approx |\mathcal{P}|/\eta\); during \(\tau_{\text{rec}}\), market outcomes deviate from perfect competition with temporary profit margins above baseline, consistent with Knight's claim that true uncertainty, not measurable risk, sustains divergence from theoretical competition.
