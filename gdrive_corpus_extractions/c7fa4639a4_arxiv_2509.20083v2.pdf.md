<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The statistical lineage traces from Pollard and Reep [1997], who proposed an early logistic expected-goals model using shot location, goalangle, and body part [PAGE 2], through modern flexible xG estimators based on boosted trees and neural networks [PAGE 2–4]. The paper’s core identifying device is Rao’s score test [Rao, 1948] [PAGE 5–6], which algebraically recovers GAX as a score statistic under a parametric logistic model [PAGE 6]. The semiparametric turn relies on Shah and Peters [2020] and their Generalised Covariance Measure [PAGE 6–7], embedded in the double/debiased machine-learning tradition of Chernozhukov et al. [2017] [PAGE 7] and assumption-lean inference of Vansteelandt and Dukes [2022] [PAGE 7], with Kennedy [2024] cited for semiparametric doubly robust targeted learning [PAGE 3]. Team-strength nuisance parameters are obtained from Karlis and Ntzoufras [2003] bivariate Poisson models on match outcomes [PAGE 14, Appendix D.2]. The injury extension uses martingale residuals from Therneau et al. [1990] [PAGE 22] and the TRAM-GCM test of Kook et al. [2025], generalized to censored responses via a partially linear Cox model [PAGE 22–23].

Multi-week traps are visible throughout. GAX is explicitly criticized as unstable across seasons—one season’s GAX poorly predicts the next [PAGE 2]—and the present analysis is confined to the single 2015/16 campaign [PAGE 4], so temporal generalization is untested. Selection bias compounds over a season: stronger players and teams take more shots, shifting the feature distribution Z and biasing any xG model trained on the observed sample [PAGE 11–12]. The paper acknowledges that multi-season stability of rGAX via p-values and confidence intervals is left for future work [PAGE 13–14]. Thus the historical arc moves from descriptive shot counting to model-adjusted above-expectation metrics, then to semiparametric residualization, yet remains trapped in short-panel, single-season evidence with unproven inter-season transportability.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under performance pressure—transfer windows, contract negotiations, relegation battles—analysts and sporting directors tend to regress to raw goal counts or unadjusted GAX because these are intuitively legible [PAGE 2]. That regression reintroduces selection bias, omits uncertainty quantification, and ignores the shot-circumstance model entirely [PAGE 2, 14].

Counter-trap: rGAX itself can be gamed by the residualization stage. If f̂_p(Z) is overfit—e.g., tuned xgboost on sparse player subsets—rGAX point estimates develop outliers (Figure 6C–D) even though formal GCM inference survives through double robustness provided ĥ converges quickly [PAGE 18]. Thus the “defense” against selection bias becomes a new attack surface when the propensity model is misspecified or over-tuned.

Clock: the analysis is anchored to one season (2015/16) [PAGE 4]. Elite players such as Messi and Ronaldo show high rGAX but wide confidence intervals and non-significance at conventional levels, partly because of a down season and limited shot volume [PAGE 10–11]. Without a multi-week or multi-season clock, the framework cannot yet distinguish a persistent shooting skill from a transient statistical fluctuation, and the authors explicitly decline to test cross-season stability [PAGE 13–14].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: the shot outcome depends on biomechanics (foot vs. head, strike technique), goalkeeper positioning, pitch condition, and weather. Z includes distance to goal, angles, distances to defenders and goalkeeper, shot type, body part, and technique [PAGE 4, Table 2], but not weather, pitch moisture, wind, or accumulated player fatigue. If any unmeasured factor affects both the shooter’s decision to shoot (X_p) and the goal probability (Y), conditional independence Y ⊥⊥ X_p | Z fails and rGAX is biased [PAGE 14 independence assumption].

Biomechanical layer: rebound shots depend causally on the preceding shot, creating sequential dependence within a match. The authors state that shots may be conditionally independent given Z only if Z is sufficiently rich [PAGE 14]; in practice, match state, minute, and fatigue are only partially captured, so within-match clustering remains a veto risk.

Goalkeeper layer: for GSAX/rGSAX, post-shot trajectory features enter psxG, and the goalkeeper’s shot-facing propensity is modeled via f_p(Z) to account for anticipation [PAGE 8–9]. Positive rGSAX indicates strong shot-stopping [PAGE 12].

Adjudication verdict: rGAX/rGSAX are valid only under the conditional-independence and overlap assumptions. Unmeasured game-state confounders (score differential, match fatigue) that correlate with shooter identity and scoring probability invalidate Proposition 1’s mapping between sign(GCM) and the causal β [PAGE 7]. In those regimes the statistic measures association under the observed design, not causally isolated shooting skill, and must be vetoed for high-stakes recruitment decisions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 — Overlap collapse: A dominant shooter (e.g., Messi) takes nearly all shots in a high-value region Z*, so f_p(Z*) ≈ 1 and (X_p − f̂_p(Z)) has near-zero variance there. Prediction: the GCM statistic’s empirical variance inflates, p-values become anti-conservative despite formal double robustness, and rGAX estimates for that player become unstable across resamples. Verifiable by stratifying the data by shot zone and checking whether standard errors diverge as f̂_p(Z) → 1.

Veto scenario 2 — Informative censoring in injury model: Players who suffer severe injuries drop out of the observation window, so loss to follow-up C depends on player identity and injury severity beyond Z. Prediction: rIAX sign and significance invert relative to IAX; the TRAM-GCM Type I error exceeds the nominal 5% level, and confidence intervals under-cover. Verifiable by comparing event vs. censoring rates across player subsets and testing whether censoring is independent of player indicator conditional on Z.

Veto scenario 3 — Unmeasured game-state confounding: Match score differential (omitted from Z) causes trailing teams’ stars to take more low-probability desperation shots. Prediction: rGAX overstates shooting skill for players on weaker teams and produces false-positive significance at above the nominal 5% rate even when the GCM test is asymptotically valid under the misspecified Z. Verifiable by adding match score and minute to Z and observing whether previously significant rGAX values fall below significance or change sign.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
