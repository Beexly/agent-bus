**1. Entropy and information gain (units: bits, because \(\lg\equiv\log_2\)).** For node \(v_i\) with class counts \(N_{ic}\) and total \(N_i\),
\[
\mathrm{ent}(v_i)=-\sum_{c\in L}\frac{N_{ic}}{N_i}\lg\frac{N_{ic}}{N_i}\quad[\text{bit}]
\]
[PAGE 38, Eq. 1.1]. The split gain is
\[
\mathrm{gain}(S)=N_p\,\mathrm{ent}(v_p)-\sum_{v_i\in V_S}N_i\,\mathrm{ent}(v_i)\quad[\text{bit}\cdot\text{sample}]
\]
[PAGE 38, Eq. 1.2]. *Assumption:* binary tree, \(K=2\) children. *Breakdown:* if \(N_i=0\) the term is undefined; the standard convention \(0\lg 0=0\) must be imposed, otherwise the reducer emits NaN and the total ordering over splits fails.

**2. Variance minimization (units: dimensionless, since labels are \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\)).**
\[
\mathrm{var}(v_i)=\frac{1}{N_i}\sum_{j=1}^{N_i}(c_{ij}-\bar c_i)^2,\qquad \bar c_i=\frac{1}{N_i}\sum_{j=1}^{N_i}c_{ij}
\]
[PAGE 38, Eqs. 1.3–1.4], with weighted split score
\[
\mathrm{weighted\_var}(S_i)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}N_k\,\mathrm{var}(v_k)
\]
[PAGE 39, Eq. 1.5]. *Breakdown:* variance is zero only for pure nodes; with \(K=2\) and a single-sample child, \(\mathrm{var}=0\) trivially, so the criterion over-favors degenerate splits unless a minimum-samples-per-leaf constraint is active.

**3. Minimum squared expected error (MSEE) for probability estimation trees (dimensionless).**
\[
P(x_i\text{ misclassified})=p_i(1-p_i),\qquad
\mathrm{Error}_i=p_i(1-p_i)\Big((1-p_i)^2\sum_{i\neq j}p_j^2\Big)
\]
[PAGE 39, Eqs. 1.6–1.7], and
\[
\mathrm{MSEE}(S_i)=\sum_{c=1}^{C}N_c\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathrm{Error}_i\Big)
\]
[PAGE 39, Eq. 1.8]. Weighted form with \(0\le w_i\le 1,\ \sum_i w_i=1\):
\[
\mathrm{Error}_i=w_i p_i(1-w_i p_i)\Big((1-w_i p_i)^2\sum_{i\neq j}w_i p_j^2\Big)
\]
[PAGE 40, Eq. 1.10]. *Breakdown:* if all leaf probabilities fall below 0.5, no sample is predicted positive, precision becomes \(0/0\), and the implementation reports the sentinel \(-1\) [PAGE 96].

**4. Boosting weight update and model weight (dimensionless).**
\[
w'_i=w_i\div\begin{cases}2\epsilon_j,& s_i\text{ misclassified by }m_j\\ 2(1-\epsilon_j),&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
\]
[PAGE 40, Eq. 1.11], and
\[
\alpha_j=\frac12\ln\frac{1-\epsilon_j}{\epsilon_j}
\]
[PAGE 41, Eq. 1.12]. *Singularity:* \(\lim_{\epsilon_j\to 0}\alpha_j=+\infty\). The dissertation smooths with \(\mu=10^{-5}\):
\[
\alpha_j=\frac12\ln\frac{1-\epsilon_j+\mu}{\epsilon_j+\mu}
\]
[PAGE 41, Eq. 1.13]. This is a genuine numerical-veto regime: without smoothing the model weight diverges and the weighted vote is dominated by one stump.

**5. Logistic regression (probability output in \([0,1]\), log-odds dimensionless).**
\[
p=P(y_i=c_k),\quad r=\frac{p}{1-p},\quad \ln r=\ln\frac{p}{1-p},\quad p=\frac{e^{r}}{1+e^{r}}
\]
[PAGE 41, Eqs. 1.14–1.17],
\[
r=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_j\beta_j=\vec x\cdot\vec\beta,\qquad
p=f(\vec\beta,\vec x)=\frac{e^{\vec x\cdot\vec\beta}}{1+e^{\vec x\cdot\vec\beta}}
\]
[PAGE 41–42, Eqs. 1.18–1.19]. Newton update:
\[
\beta'_d=\beta_d-\frac{\partial L/\partial\beta_d}{\partial^2 L/\partial\beta_d^2}
\]
[PAGE 43, Eq. 1.24] with first derivative
\[
\frac{\partial L}{\partial\beta_d}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i,d}\big(y_i-f_d(\vec x_i,\vec\beta,\beta_d)\big)
\]
[PAGE 43, Eq. 1.25] and Hessian
\[
\frac{\partial^2 L}{\partial\beta_d^2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i,d}^2 f_d(1-f_d)
\]
[PAGE 43, Eq. 1.26]. *Breakdown:* as \(|\vec x\cdot\vec\beta|\to\infty\), \(f(1-f)\to 0\), the Hessian vanishes, and Newton’s step is undefined; L2 regularization subtracts \(2\lambda\) from the Hessian diagonal [PAGE 50, Eq. 1.47], which is the mechanism that keeps the inversion non-singular.

**6. Histogram acceleration (complexity units: operations).** Binary-attribute histograms \(H,H^{+}\) with \(B\) bins give

\gamma=\text{recall}-(1-\text{specificity})
\]
[PAGE 47, Eq. 1.40];
\[
DP=\frac{\sqrt3}{\pi}(\log X+\log Y),\quad X=\frac{\text{recall}}{1-\text{recall}},\quad Y=\frac{\text{specificity}}{1-\text{specificity}}
\]
[PAGE 47, Eq. 1.41];
\[
\mathrm{AUC}=\int_0^1\mathrm{ROC}(X_{\text{test}})\,d(\text{specificity})
\]
[PAGE 47, Eq. 1.42];
\[
\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N(p_i-c_i)^2},\qquad
\mathrm{RMSA}=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\big(1-|p_i-c_i|\big)^2}
\]
[PAGE 49–50, Eqs. 1.43–1.44]. *Breakdown:* precision, recall, and \(F\)-score are undefined when the denominator is zero; the dissertation’s \(-1\) precision cells are exactly this singularity [PAGE 96].

\[
y_{ij}=q_0+q_1x_A+q_2x_B+q_3x_C+q_4x_Ax_B+q_5x_Ax_C+q_6x_Bx_C+q_7x_Ax_Bx_C+e_{ij}

[PAGE 74, Eq. 3.1],

q_j=\frac{1}{2^k}\sum_{i=1}^{2^k}X_{i,j}\hat y_i,\qquad SS_{q_j}=2^k r\,q_j^2,\qquad \%q_j=100\%\cdot\frac{SS_{q_j}}{SST}

[PAGE 75, Eqs. 3.2–3.7], with significance interval

**9. Speedup and model-difference operators.**
\[
\text{speedup}=\frac{t_{\text{serial}}}{t_{\text{parallel}}}
\]
[PAGE 119, Eq. 5.2], dimensionless. *Singularity:* \(t_{\text{parallel}}\to 0\) gives unbounded speedup; the physical ceiling on \(k\) cores is \(k\). Amdahl’s law \(S(k)=1/(s+(1-s)/k)\le 1/s\) means a reported \(S=17{,}000\) would require \(s<5.9\times10^{-5}\), which contradicts the measured 28-second MapReduce phase-start overhead [PAGE 52]. Model agreement is measured by
\[
\frac{1}{TN}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\hat p_{ti}-p_{ti}|
\]
[PAGE 115, Eq. 5.1], dimensionless.

**10. Meta-dataset sampling and MCC.**
\[
\text{interval}=10^{\lfloor\log \text{value}\rfloor-1}
\]
[PAGE 145, Eq. 6.20], in milliseconds; for \(\text{value}<1\) ms the exponent is negative and the grid becomes sub-millisecond. Matthews correlation:

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from Quinlan’s 1986 ID3 induction [PAGE 164, ref. 29] through Mingers’ 1989 paired studies on split selection [PAGE 164, ref. 31] and pruning [PAGE 164, ref. 42]. Mingers established the counter-intuitive result that “the different selection measures didn’t have any measurable effect on the accuracy of the trees, before or after pruning,” while selection measures did affect tree size [PAGE 56]. That result is the genealogical ancestor of the dissertation’s choice to treat information gain, variance minimization, and MSEE as interchangeable split criteria whose only difference is tree geometry, not accuracy. Breiman’s bagging [PAGE 164, ref. 32] and Quinlan’s boosting work [PAGE 164, ref. 33] supply the ensemble branch; Ferri et al.’s MSEE [PAGE 164, ref. 30] supplies the probability-estimation branch; Singh et al.’s SFO [PAGE 163, ref. 20] and Panda et al.’s PLANET [PAGE 163, ref. 21] supply the MapReduce branch. The empirical-comparison lineage is StatLog (King et al., 1995) [PAGE 163, ref. 10], Perlich, Provost, and Simonoff (2003) [PAGE 163, ref. 7], Caruana and Niculescu-Mizil (2006) [PAGE 163, ref. 13], and Ali and Smith (2006) [PAGE 164, ref. 47]. The meta-learning lineage is Kalousis (2002) [PAGE 165, ref. 65], Brazdil et al. [PAGE 164, ref. 48], and the recent hyper-parameter-optimization wave including Auto-WEKA [PAGE 166, ref. 69] and Nural et al. [PAGE 166, ref. 77]. The dissertation’s distinctive retrocausal move is to splice the Perlich learning-curve methodology onto the MapReduce runtime literature, producing the TLC. Multi-week traps are explicit in the record. First, the 92-node cluster used in Phase 2 “became unavailable,” forcing Phase 3 onto the 24-node cluster with 1 GB RAM per core [PAGE 116]. Second, three large datasets (CoverType, Intrusion, Thrombin) were allotted four weeks of search time each, and “any gap of more than twelve hours was not counted towards the overall time limit” [PAGE 94]—a rule that silently censors queueing delay and converts wall-clock budgets into effective-compute budgets. Third, the Adult and Example2 runs had to be rerun on a single core because “the target class had been specified incorrectly” and the original cluster was gone [PAGE 100]. Fourth, the Pima dataset was removed from UCI during the project’s lifetime, and the dissertation notes the removal notice without explanation [PAGE 42], which retroactively destabilizes reproducibility of the Pima conclusions. Fifth, the bagging implementation’s stump-count/sample-count interaction produced degenerate all-negative models that were not caught during the search because “only AUC, accuracy and the combination score were examined during spot checks” [PAGE 95]. Each of these traps is a case where a later phase inherits an unverified assumption from an earlier one, and the genealogy explains why: the field’s benchmark culture rewards final-score tables and under-rewards confusion-matrix auditing, so the silent failure survived until the final write-up.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive frame has three poles. **Crisis habit regression** is the model’s fallback to the majority class under distributional stress. The bagging results in Table 4.1 show \(TP=0\) for Ads (binorm), Adult (binorm), Credit, IntShopping, Pima, and Thrombin, with precision \(-1\) and recall \(0.000\) [PAGE 96]; the EM meta-model in Table 6.7 predicts “boosted” for every single training sample [PAGE 120, PAGE 148]. This is the algorithmic analogue of a habitual response under threat: when the margin is insufficient, collapse to the prior. **Counter-trap** is the meta-model’s own overconfidence. J48 achieves 0.936 accuracy and 0.906 MCC on the training meta-dataset [PAGE 148], yet on the eight held-out datasets the same learner achieves 0.331 accuracy and 0.065 MCC [PAGE 155]. The cross-validation later shows Random Forest AUC in \((0.758,0.801)\) [PAGE 176], so the counter-trap is not that the features are useless but that the original train/test partition was adversarial. The recursive lesson is that a meta-learner evaluating learners must itself be evaluated out-of-distribution, or it will mistake in-distribution separability for generalizable skill. **Clock** is the time-budget axis that makes the whole recursion non-stationary. The TLC for Mushrooms (binorm) [PAGE 146, Figure 6.1] shows tree induction leading until about 10 seconds, then overlapping intervals for sfo and boosting, then boosting pulling clear around 100–200 seconds. The identity of the “best” algorithm is therefore a function of \(\tau\), and any label assigned at a single \(\tau\) is a snapshot of a moving argmax. The recursive theory-of-mind implication for the practitioner is that the question “which algorithm should I use?” is ill-posed without a clock, a core count, and a metric; the dissertation’s own conclusion—“when training time is important, algorithm selection becomes an even more critical aspect” [PAGE 112]—is the explicit statement of this dependency. A further recursive layer is the researcher’s model of the algorithm’s model of the data: the hill climber expands the hyper-parameter “responsible for the most variation according to the \(2^k r\) analysis” first [PAGE 92], which means the search order encodes a belief about which knobs matter, and that belief was formed from only two small datasets (Pima and Mushrooms) [PAGE 74]. If that belief is wrong for a large dataset, the climber wastes its budget on the wrong axis. The observed wide dispersion of winning hyper-parameters across datasets in Tables 4.5–4.8 [PAGE 101–103] is the empirical signature of that mismatch.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The “cross-layer” vetoes here are physical and runtime-resource constraints that can invalidate an algorithmic claim before any statistical test is applied. **Memory layer.** Phase 3 ran on a 24-node cluster with “only has 1GB of RAM per core” [PAGE 121], whereas Phase 2’s lost cluster had 4GB per core. The JVM was a 32-bit build with a 3712 MB maximum heap, and nodes were configured with large swap [PAGE 120–121]. The author states: “for memory hungry learning tasks, most of the heap space would end up paged out” [PAGE 121]. This is a hard veto on speedup interpretation: single-core runs were penalized by paging, inflating \(t_{\text{serial}}\) and therefore inflating Eq. 5.2. **Network layer.** All MapReduce communication ran over 1000baseT because “IP over infiniband was not enabled on the cluster” [PAGE 84], while MPI calls used InfiniBand. The heterogeneous fabric means the communication term in the runtime model is not the one a well-provisioned cluster would exhibit. **Job-startup layer.** Hadoop required “at least 28 seconds to start each MapReduce phase” [PAGE 52], which is why Twister was selected; but the 28-second figure is the physical floor that any Amdahl-style serial-fraction argument must respect. **Algorithmic-mechanics layer.** The tree inducer’s MapReduce job choice—single-node splitting early, all-leaves splitting later [PAGE 52]—means available parallelism changes with tree depth, so a single speedup number is an average over regimes, not a constant. **Weather/volatility layer.** “Due to issues in the runtime environment, it was known that jobs would randomly terminate” [PAGE 94], and the SHIELD wrapper was built to requeue jobs [PAGE 63–64]. This is runtime weather: a stochastic kill process that adds variance unrelated to the algorithm. The veto proof is a reductio. The reported 95% confidence lower bound for Adult at 16 cores is “around 17,000 ... where the maximum possible speedup should be only 16” [PAGE 120]. By Eq. 5.2, either \(t_{\text{serial}}\) is inflated by a factor of roughly \(10^3\) or \(t_{\text{parallel}}\) is impossibly small. Since the physical ceiling \(S(k)\le k=16\) is a theorem for a fixed problem instance, and since Amdahl’s bound \(S\le 1/s\) would require a serial fraction below \(5.9\times10^{-5}\) to reach 17,000, the measurement cannot be an algorithmic property. The only consistent explanation is the memory-layer veto: paging on the single-core baseline. The author reaches the same verdict—“the results were a mix of mediocre results for smaller datasets and impossibly good results for many of the larger datasets” [PAGE 133]—and attributes them to “unfortunate interactions between the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and the host virtual memory system” [PAGE 133]. The cross-layer adjudication is therefore: physical resource constraints outrank statistical significance, and any time-limit label derived from these speedup curves inherits the veto.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
**Veto 1 — Metric-completeness veto.** *Counterfactual:* omit precision, recall, and the confusion matrix from the reporting, keeping only AUC, accuracy, and the composite score. *Source evidence:* the author states verbatim, “Had precision, recall and the confusion matrix values been omitted from these tables, it would be nearly impossible to detect that such a major error had occurred” [PAGE 95]. *Prediction:* re-scoring the stored bagging model files with only the three retained metrics will accept the degenerate all-negative models for Ads (binorm), Adult (binorm), Credit, and Thrombin, because their composite scores are 0.415, 0.380, 0.347, and 0.510 respectively [PAGE 96, Table 4.1], all above a plausible 0.30 acceptance threshold. Restoring precision/recall restores the veto because precision is the sentinel \(-1\). *Falsification test:* if the composite score alone already falls below threshold for any of these rows, the veto is unnecessary for that row; the prediction is that at least three of the four survive.

**Veto 2 — Split-resolution veto.** *Counterfactual:* treat the number of candidate split points for real-valued attributes as a searched hyper-parameter rather than fixing it at twenty 5-percentile points. *Source evidence:* “this is assumed to be impractical, so instead twenty split points at 5-percentile intervals are considered. This alternate strategy will skip potentially useful split points. In hindsight, the number of split points to consider for a real attribute should have been a hyper-parameter to be optimized” [PAGE 96]. *Prediction:* the binorm penalty will shrink. Concretely, Ads bagging composite rises from 0.415 toward the 0.770 non-binorm value; Adult (binorm) bagging rises from 0.380 toward 0.749; Example2 (binorm) rises from 0.146 toward 0.705 [PAGE 96, Table 4.1]. *Falsification test:* rerun the same five splits with 50 and 100 candidate split points; if the binorm composite does not move by at least 0.10 on at least two of these three datasets, the veto is rejected.

**Veto 3 — Partition-validity veto.** *Counterfactual:* use 5×5 cross-validation as the primary protocol and treat the single holdout split as a diagnostic rather than a verdict. *Source evidence:* the holdout results are “abysmal” and “even the best models only performed slightly better than random” [PAGE 159], whereas cross-validation gives Random Forest AUC in \((0.758,0.801)\) and REPTree in \((0.640,0.719)\) [PAGE 176, Table 6.32]. The author concedes “the original splitting between training and testing was problematic” [PAGE 159]. *Prediction:* a domain-matched holdout—e.g. leave-one-hyperspectral-scene-out among Botswana, Indian Pines, KSC, PaviaU, and SalinasA—will recover AUC \(\ge 0.65\) for Random Forest, because the PCA plots show the training and test points collapsing into “one big cluster” [PAGE 128, Figures 6.2–6.3], meaning the failure is domain shift, not feature insufficiency. *Falsification test:* if the leave-one-scene-out AUC remains below 0.55, the partition-validity veto is rejected and the feature set is genuinely insufficient, consistent with the author’s own PCA reading that “the dataset characteristics are insufficient for the learning task” [PAGE 128].
