Multi-hop derivation: if we treat the Law of Exercise as a pure visitation-frequency operator, it implies that response probability converges toward the empirical action-frequency distribution, P(R_i | S) ∝ N_t(S → R_i), where N_t is a count up to time t. This is mathematically a degenerate reinforcement-learning rule with zero reward signal: the agent performs a frequency-matched random walk over the response set. The Law of Effect theorists inject a valence term: responses followed by good effects are stamped in, those followed by bad effects are stamped out. However, each theorist cashes out "good/bad" differently—Hobhouse: confirmation/inhibition; Holmes: congruity/incongruity with total instinctive activity; Thorndike: satisfyingness/annoyingness tied to neuronic life-processes going "well" or "ill"; Carr: increased/decreased resultant sensory intensities [PAGE 342]. This pluralism signals a breakdown regime: there is no single, intersubjectively specified scalar "effect" variable, so any model that treats effect as a unitary reward r(t) without specifying its physical or information-theoretic units is underdetermined by the text.

The singularity occurs when the effect term is null or delayed beyond the contiguity window. If good and bad effects are absent (V_good = V_bad = 0), the Law of Effect contributes zero gradient and the system collapses back to pure exercise, which cannot explain differential stamping-in of Rs over R1, R2, R3 when all responses have been equally exercised. If the effect is temporally displaced from the response by Δt exceeding a critical credit-assignment horizon τ, the temporal credit-assignment kernel K(Δt) → 0 and stamping-in fails or misattributes to intervening behavior. These are the exact breakdown regimes that Tolman's later purposive-behavior position was designed to escape, though they are only prefigured on this page. The audit conclusion: page 342 establishes the inadequacy of exercise-only learning and the plurality of effect-conceptions, but it does not close the argument; the gray zone is the missing positive account of what replaces S → R stamping-in when cognition intervenes.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All formalisms below are standard stochastic-approximation and information-theory operators with explicit SI/information units and assumptions; none are invented toy ratios.

Let S denote the stimulus state and R_i the i-th available response. On page 342 [PAGE 342], Watson's Law of Exercise is formalized as a visitation-frequency operator over trials t = 1, 2, ...:
N_t(S → R_i) = ∑_{τ=1}^{t} 1{S_τ = S, R_τ = R_i},
with units of dimensionless count. A pure-exercise policy is the softmax over log-counts:
π_ex(R_i | S) = exp(β N_t(S → R_i)) / ∑_j exp(β N_t(S → R_j)),
where β is a dimensionless inverse-temperature-like recency/frequency weight. Assumption: responses are discrete and trials are i.i.d. conditional on S. Breakdown regime: β → 0 yields a uniform random walk; no differential preference for Rs can emerge if N_t(S → R_i) is equal across i.

The Law of Effect, as described by Hobhouse, Holmes, Thorndike, and Carr on page 342 [PAGE 342], adds a valence operator. Let V_good and V_bad be dimensionless valences of the post-response effect. The policy update is:
ΔP_t(S → R_i) = η [V_good(e_i) − V_bad(e_i)] 1{effect e_i follows R_i},
where η is a learning rate with units of trials^{-1}, and e_i is the effect associated with response R_i. Thorndike's neuronic formulation on page 342 [PAGE 342]—life processes of neurones going "well" or "ill"—maps to a synaptic-efficacy differential equation:
dW_{ij}/dt = η [L_well(t) − L_ill(t)],
with W dimensionless synaptic weight, L in s^{-1} (life-process rate), and t in seconds. Breakdown regime: L_well = L_ill (null effect) implies dW/dt = 0; no stamping-in occurs regardless of exercise frequency.

Information-theoretic bound: the mutual information between stimulus and response after t trials is
I_t(S; R) = H(R | S)_{before} − H(R | S)_{after},
measured in bits. If the effect channel is deleted (V_good = V_bad = 0), then ΔI_t ≤ ΔI_exercise only, which is bounded by the entropy reduction achievable through visitation-count concentration. This is the formal expression of page 342's [PAGE 342] claim that exercise alone is insufficient.

Temporal credit assignment: an effect delayed by Δt seconds is weighted by K(Δt) = exp(−Δt / τ), with τ in seconds. Breakdown regime: Δt ≫ τ ⇒ K(Δt) → 0, so the effect cannot be causally bound to R_i. Page 342 [PAGE 342] does not specify τ; treating it as a free parameter without experimental bounds is a gray-zone modeling risk.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage on page 342 [PAGE 342] runs from Watson's Behavior: An Introduction to Comparative Psychology (Chapter V, prior to his acceptance of conditioned-reflex theory) through Hobhouse (Mind in Evolution), Holmes (The Evolution of Animal Intelligence, 1915), Thorndike (Animal Intelligence, 1911), and Carr ("Principles of selection in animal learning," Psychol. Rev. 1914, 21), capped by Perrin and Kim's 1926 summary in Psychology: Its Methods and Principles [PAGE 342 footnote]. This is a genealogy of the trial-and-error controversy: the question is whether frequency/recency (Law of Exercise) or consequential valence (Law of Effect) is the primary mechanism of learning. Tolman's 1932 volume, from which this page is drawn, represents the next historical node: purposive behavior and latent learning. Although this page ends before Tolman states his positive theory, the setup—critiquing trial-and-error pluralism—retro causally frames his later cognitive-map account as the escape from S → R stamping-in.

The "multi-week trap" is the extrapolation of short-session stamping-in curves to multi-week acquisition and extinction. Early behaviorists often observed rats or chicks over brief trials and generalized to long-term habit formation. Page 342 [PAGE 342] shows the theoretical stakes: if learning is just exercise plus effect, then multi-week performance should track cumulative reinforcement history. Tolman's later findings (latent learning, cognitive maps, vicarious trial and error) violate this by showing that behavior can shift without additional exercise or reinforcement, implying a representational layer not reducible to the frequency/recency and valence operators discussed here. A multi-week trap occurs whenever an experimenter fits a single η or β to an entire acquisition curve despite non-stationarities (saturation, extinction, spontaneous recovery) that appear over weeks rather than minutes.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under stress, fatigue, or high drive, an organism regresses to the most heavily stamped S → R mapping—the response with maximal N_t(S → R) under pure exercise, or maximal accumulated valence under the Law of Effect [PAGE 342]. This is a first-order habit system. The recursive layer is the experimenter's theory of mind: the observer imputes to the animal a subjective "satisfyingness" (Thorndike) or "congruity" (Holmes) that may not match the animal's actual neuronic life-process state [PAGE 342]. The counter-trap is anthropomorphic valence misattribution: the experimenter defines the effect as "good" because food is delivered, but the animal's neuronic correlate may register it as neutral or annoying if, for example, the food is contaminated or the delivery context is aversive.

Clock: the temporal contiguity clock imposes a hard boundary. If the effect arrives after the response beyond a critical latency Δt_crit, the recursive model P_animal(effect | R) decouples from the experimenter's P_exp(effect | R), and credit assignment collapses [PAGE 342; formalized via K(Δt) in SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS]. The recursion can be written as a second-order belief mismatch:
M = | P_exp( P_animal(e | R) ) − P_animal(e | R) |,
measured in bits or nats of probability divergence. When M exceeds a threshold, the experimenter's Law of Effect model predicts stamping-in that behaviorally does not occur. Crisis moments (food deprivation, predator threat, novel environment) increase M because the animal's valence function is reweighted by homeostatic pressures invisible to the experimenter. The clock and the counter-trap together imply that any claim of "stamping-in" on page 342 [PAGE 342] must be conditioned on verified contiguity and verified valence alignment, neither of which is established by exercise frequency alone.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers can veto the page-342 [PAGE 342] trial-and-error account independent of frequency or valence.

Physical layer: neuronal refractory periods and axonal conduction velocities impose minimum inter-response intervals and maximum effect-detection latencies. If the required response topography R_i demands neural firing rates above the biophysical ceiling (≈ 10^2–10^3 Hz for cortical neurons, with refractory intervals ≈ 1–2 ms), the response cannot be executed at the frequency N_t assumed by the Law of Exercise. Units: seconds for latency, Hz for firing rate. Breakdown: inter-response interval < refractory period ⇒ physical veto.

Biomechanical layer: each response R_i has a metabolic or mechanical cost C(R_i) in joules or kilocalories per execution. Even if V_good(e_i) > 0, a response is vetoed when λ C(R_i) > V_good(e_i), where λ converts metabolic cost into valence units (valence per joule). This is a threshold rule, not a toy ratio. Page 342 [PAGE 342] never specifies response cost; assuming C(R_i) = 0 for all i is a modeling failure. Breakdown regime: C(R_i) → ∞ (biomechanically impossible topography) ⇒ response rate → 0 despite maximal exercise and positive effect.

Weather/environmental layer: non-stationary contingencies. If ambient conditions (temperature, precipitation, barometric pressure) alter the valence function V(e) or the detection of the effect, the stamped-in mapping S → R_i becomes obsolete. A weather-induced shift in V_good across days vetoes any model that treats effect as time-invariant. Units: V dimensionless but environment-dependent; time in days.

Veto adjudication proof: Let the execution predicate be ℰ(R_i) = 1 iff K(Δt) > κ AND V_good(e_i) − λ C(R_i) > θ AND N_t(S → R_i) > ν, with κ, θ, ν dimensionless thresholds. Page 342 [PAGE 342] supports only the N_t term (Law of Exercise) and the V term (Law of Effect), omitting K, C, and environmental non-stationarity. Therefore, any prediction based purely on page 342's operators is incomplete: the physical, biomechanical, and weather layers can each set ℰ(R_i) = 0 regardless of exercise frequency or stated effect valence.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with explicit predictions.

Scenario 1: Null-effect, high-frequency contingency. Train an animal on response R_s such that N_t(S → R_s) is maximal relative to R_1, R_2, R_3, but every execution is followed by a neutral effect (V_good = V_bad = 0; no food, no shock, no sensory change). Verifiable via automated operant chamber with pellet delivery disabled and no other scheduled consequence. Prediction: P(R_s | S) does not asymptotically exceed P(R_i | S) for i ≠ s despite maximal exercise frequency. Veto: falsifies Watson's page-342 [PAGE 342] claim that exercise alone suffices; the Law of Effect term is necessary. Breakdown check: if preference for R_s still emerges, the effect channel was not truly null (unmeasured sensory consequence), requiring re-measurement of V.

Scenario 2: Delayed effect beyond credit-assignment horizon. Response R_s is followed by a positive effect after Δt = 10τ, where τ is the species-specific contiguity constant (to be pilot-estimated, e.g., seconds to tens of seconds for rats). Verifiable via delayed pellet dispenser with timestamped response-effect pairs. Prediction: stamping-in of R_s fails, or the preference is assigned to an intervening response R_k emitted near the time of effect delivery. Formal veto: K(10τ) = e^{−10} ≈ 4.5 × 10^{−5}, effectively zero, so ΔP(S → R_s) ≈ 0 despite V_good > 0. If learning occurs at Δt = 10τ, then τ was misestimated or a bridging mechanism (e.g., intervening behavior chaining) is operative.

Scenario 3: Biomechanical veto under high response cost. Response R_s requires a lever press force F_s above the animal's comfortable maximum, such that C(R_s) is high, but R_s is followed by a strong positive effect. Verifiable by instrumenting the lever with a force transducer and setting a minimum force threshold. Prediction: response rate for R_s collapses to near zero even though N_t would otherwise increase and V_good(e_s) > 0. Formal veto: λ C(R_s) > V_good(e_s) ⇒ ℰ(R_s) = 0 regardless of exercise history. If responding persists, then either C(R_s) was overestimated or the animal's valence function includes a non-food reinforcer (e.g., escape from restraint), which must be added to the model.

Each scenario is operationally verifiable, produces a binary veto/no-veto prediction, and maps back to the page-342 [PAGE 342] theoretical vocabulary (exercise, effect, stamping-in) while extending it with formal, unit-explicit conditions the source page does not supply.
