- $Q(x,\omega)=+\infty$ (second-stage infeasible) iff $h(\omega)-T(\omega)x\notin \operatorname{pos}W$; the induced feasible set is $K_2=\{x:Q(x,\omega)<\infty\ \text{w.p. }1\}$ [PAGE 57], (2.15).
- $Q(x,\omega)=-\infty$ (dual unbounded) is excluded by assuming $W^\top u\le q$ solvable for every realization [PAGE 57].

**Certainty-equivalence operator and its failure.** The mean-substitution map $x\mapsto l_i(x,\bar w)$, $\bar w=\mathbb{E}\{w\}$, is exact iff $l_i$ is affine in $w$. The text's counterexample, [PAGE 25]:
$$
f_0(x,\omega)=(\omega x)^2,\quad P[\omega=+1]=P[\omega=-1]=\tfrac12
\;\Rightarrow\; f_0(x,\bar w)\equiv 0,\ \text{yet}\ \mathbb{E}\{f_0(x,\omega)\}=x^2 .
$$
For convex $l_i$, Jensen gives the one-sided bound $E\{l_i(z,w)\}\ge l_i(z,\bar w)$ [PAGE 37], so the certainty feasible set strictly contains the true one — a *relaxation*, hence a lower bound, not a solution.

**Probabilistic (chance) constraint operator.** [PAGE 141], (5.2)–(5.3): $P\{Tx\le e\}\ge p \iff F(Tx)\ge p$, with $F$ the joint CDF. Its gradient (5.27) is
$$
\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_i}=\int_{-\infty}^{z_1}\!\!\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{z_{i-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{z_{i+1}}\!\!\cdots f(z_1,\dots,z_r)\,dz_{-i},
$$
units of $f\times[\text{length}]^{r-1}$.

**Stochastic quasigradient (SQG) recursion.** [PAGE 161], (6.11): $x^{\nu+1}=\pi_X\!\big(x^\nu-\rho_\nu\xi^\nu\big)$, with $\mathbb{E}\{\xi^\nu\mid x^0,\dots,x^\nu\}\in\partial F_0(x^\nu)+\psi^\nu$. Dimensional audit: $[\rho_\nu]=[x]/[\xi]=[x]^2/[F]$ (decision-squared per unit cost). Convergence rests on the stochastic Fejér inequality, [PAGE 161] (6.14):
$$
\mathbb{E}\{\|z-z^{\nu+1}\|^2\mid z^0,\dots,z^\nu\}\le \|z-z^\nu\|^2+\tau_\nu,\quad \tau_\nu\ge0,\ \textstyle\sum\tau_\nu<\infty,
$$
under the Robbins–Monro step schedule, [PAGE 163] (6.15): $\rho_\nu\ge0,\ \sum\rho_\nu=\infty,\ \sum\mathbb{E}(\rho_\nu\tau_\nu+\rho_\nu^2)<\infty$.

**Curse-of-dimensionality / information operator.** The discrete measure $P_\nu=\sum_{e=1}^L p_e\delta_{w_e}$ gives $F_{0\nu}(x)=\sum_e p_e l_0(x,w_e)$ [PAGE 41], (1.46). For $w\in\mathbb{R}^{10}$ continuous, the guaranteed accuracy demands $10^{10}\lesssim L\lesssim 10^{11}$ scenarios [PAGE 41] — "jumping from the fire into the frying pan."

**Bellman (multistage) operator.** [PAGE 35], (1.36): $V_t(z_t)=\min_{x\in X(t)}\mathbb{E}\{g_{0t}(h(t,z_t,x,w(t)),x,w(t))+V_{t+1}(z_t+h(\cdot))\}$; terminal $V_T(z_T)=\mathbb{E}\{g_{0T}(z_T,w(T))\}$.

</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<THINK_TRACE>

The multi-hop derivation begins from a single structural fact: every operator above evaluates $F(x)$ *through* an integral against $P$, but the algorithmic primitives only ever deliver random observations $l_i(x,\omega)$. This is the pivot stated at [PAGE 43]: "it is only possible to calculate random observations $l_i(x,w)$ of $F_i(x)$." Everything downstream is a consequence.

Hop 1 — Why scenario/mean substitution is illegitimate. At [PAGE 19] the production model (1.3) fixes $a$ = unit surplus cost, $\beta$ = unit shortage cost. Its expectation (1.13) is $F_0(x)=\mathbb{E}\max[a(x-w),\beta(w-x)]$, a convex, generically *nonsmooth* function. ThePreface names scenario analysis "seriously flawed" precisely because per-scenario optima $x^\*(\omega_1),x^\*(\omega_2)$ do not combine: a convex mix "may lead to an infeasible decision for both possibilities" [PAGE 19]. The $(\omega x)^2$ example [PAGE 25] is the sharpest gray-zone: mean substitution sends the objective *identically to zero*, erasing all curvature, so the surrogate certifies every $x$ as costless while the true expected cost is $x^2$. The lesson — the flaw is not bias but *information destruction* — is why the volume pivots to expectation-preserving discretization and to SQG.

Hop 2 — Why discretization alone fails. Replacing $P$ by $P_\nu$ with conditional means $e_\epsilon=\mathbb{E}\{e\mid e\in E_\epsilon\}$, $p_\epsilon=P\{e\in E_\epsilon\}$ [PAGE 55], (2.8)–(2.9), is exact when $f$ is linear in $e$ on $E_\epsilon$ (2.10) and yields the Jensen lower bound $\Phi(x)\le\tilde\Phi(x)$ that is *monotone under refinement* [PAGE 61]. But to make a naive grid sharp in $\mathbb{R}^{10}$ you need $L\sim10^{10}$–$10^{11}$ atoms [PAGE 41]. Hence the resolution is *sequential*: solve on a coarse partition, use LP multipliers $\pi^\nu_{\epsilon}$ at the vertices as a nonlinearity detector (multipliers constant on $E_\epsilon$ ⟺ $Q$ linear there [PAGE 69]), and refine only where $Q(x^\nu,\cdot)$ bends.

Hop 3 — Why SQG sidesteps integration entirely. The recursion (6.11) never forms $F$; it consumes $\xi^\nu$ with $\mathbb{E}\{\xi^\nu\mid\mathcal{B}_\nu\}\in\partial F_0(x^\nu)+\psi^\nu$ and $\psi^\nu\to0$. The Fejér contraction (6.14) plus $\sum\rho_\nu=\infty,\ \sum\rho_\nu^2<\infty$ forces $\|x^\nu-x^\*\|$ to converge a.s. For the newsvendor this reduces to the critical-fractile condition $H(x^\*)=\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$ [PAGE 185], yet SQG needs no density $H$, only simulable draws — its decisive robustness property.

Hop 4 — Cross-checking the large-scale LP path. The L-shaped method [PAGE 95] is the *same* object as SQG-in-the-limit but solved exactly per iteration: feasibility cuts $D_{\nu+1}x\ge d_{\nu+1}$ from $\pi^\nu$ with $v^\nu>0$, optimality cuts $E_\nu x+\theta\ge e_\nu$ from $E_\nu=\sum_l p_l\pi_l^{\nu\top}T_l$, $e_\nu=\sum_l p_l\pi_l^\nu h_l$, stopping when $\theta^\nu\le w^\nu=e_\nu-E_\nu x^\nu$. Birge proves basis-factorization, L-shaped, and dual Dantzig–Wolfe decomposition perform identical per-iteration work [PAGE 289, 291] — a triangulation that raises confidence the derived quantities are correct.

Gray-zone audit: (i) the discontinuity example $F_0(x)=P\{wx\ge1\}$ [PAGE 25] is $0$ on $(-1,1)$, $\tfrac12$ for $|x|\ge1$ — a chance-constraint feasible set that is *closed but the objective is a step*, so subgradient descent needs the $\psi^\nu$ slack. (ii) Integer second stage destroys convexity: the parametric value function of [PAGE 221] shows "peculiar discontinuities and nonconvexities," so the Jensen machinery of Hop 2 is void there. (iii) The a.s. convergence theorems are asymptotic; the volume repeatedly stresses (Beer's ≤3% early-error [PAGE 109]) that practice only needs a neighborhood, not the limit.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The genuine intellectual lineage, anchored in the volume's own references:

1. **Dantzig–Beale (1955–63)** origin of two-stage linear recourse with fixed $W$; Beale appears via the multi-time-period extension [PAGE 285] and Dantzig's aircraft-allocation test [PAGE 309].
2. **Charnes–Cooper chance constraints** — the $P\{Tx\le e\}\ge p$ form (5.2) [PAGE 141] — and **Prékopa's log-concave measure theory** (1971) [PAGE 249, ref [1]–[2]], which supplies convexity of $F(Tx)\ge p$ (Theorems 5.1.1–5.1.2, 5.2.1–5.2.2) and hence makes the SUMT penalty $T(z,\delta)=h(z)-\delta[\ln h_0(z)-p_0+\sum M_i^{-1}\ln(h_i(z)-p_i)]$ [PAGE 143], (5.5) tractable.
3. **Van Slyke–Wets L-shaped method** (1969) [PAGE 93, ref [20]] as outer linearization, unified by **Birge** with **Strazicky basis factorization** [PAGE 287–291].
4. **Robbins–Monro (1951) / Kiefer–Wolfowitz** → **Ermoliev's stochastic quasigradient** generalization to constrained nondifferentiable problems [PAGE 159, refs], later made *adaptive* by Pflug, Uryasev (Urasiev), Gaivoronski, Marti (Chapters 16–20).
5. **Kall–Stoyan / Birge–Wets bounding theory**: Jensen lower, Edmundson–Madansky upper (2.33) [PAGE 63].

Multi-week *methodological* traps the lineage exposes (the "traps" worth flagging, not a retro-causal device): (a) the certainty-equivalence fallacy [PAGE 25]; (b) the discretization curse $L\sim10^{10\text{–}11}$ [PAGE 41]; (c) the illusion that scenario analysis yields combinable decisions [PAGE 19]; (d) the $\rho_\nu=1/\nu$ "asymptotic optimality" trap — Ermoliev explicitly warns it "mislead[s] in the use of stochastic approximation" and is "unsatisfactory for practical application" [PAGE 159], motivating adaptive step-size rules.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Map the "adversary" honestly: in this text the second player is *nature* (the measure $P$) or, under incomplete information, a *worst-case measure* $P\in\mathcal{P}$ [PAGE 79], (2.76): $F(x)=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\int f(x,e)P(de)$, attained at vertex-concentrated $\rho_v$ (2.77)–(2.81). Three behavioral regimes:

- **Habit regression (naïve).** Choose $x$ by plugging in $\bar w$ or by per-scenario optimization. The $(\omega x)^2$ trap and the minimax over-hedge $x^\*=\bar w$ (which the text calls "a quite expensive solution in the long run," [PAGE 21]) are the regression modes.
- **Counter-trap (correct anticipative posture).** Two-stage recourse: commit $x$ now, correct with $y(x,\omega)$ after observing $\omega$ — "a trade-off between long-term anticipatory strategies and the associated short-term adaptive adjustments" [PAGE 29]. For incomplete information, adopt the least-favorable measure; the sup is an *implementable LP* over vertex probabilities (2.81) [PAGE 79].
- **Clock (the step-size schedule).** The recursion carries an explicit temporal controller: $\rho_\nu=1/\nu$ is the theoretically canonical clock but practically "misleading" [PAGE 159]; the adaptive rules of Pflug (oscillation test, OTSR [PAGE 363]) and Uryasev ($p_{\nu+1}=p_\nu a_\nu\langle e^{\nu+1},e^\nu\rangle$) replace the fixed clock with a *feedback* clock that decreases $\rho$ only once stationarity is statistically detected. This is the counter-trap against premature step decay (slow convergence) versus late decay (oscillation).

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Honest adjudication: **the requested "physical / biomechanical / weather veto" categories do not apply to this corpus.** This is a pure operations-research text on numerical stochastic optimization; it contains no biomechanical, kinematic, or meteorological state variables. Inventing such a veto would be exactly the "toy equation" fabrication the invariants forbid. The "weather" content that *does* appear — Lake Balaton meteorological drivers (temperature, radiation, wind, precipitation) [PAGE 457] and electricity load curves [PAGE 467] — enters only as exogenous random inputs $\omega$ to LEM/load models, never as a physical feasibility veto on an algorithm. The genuine, provable *ill-posedness/breakdown* vetoes are:

1. **Recourse infeasibility veto:** $Q(x,\omega)=+\infty \iff h(\omega)-T(\omega)x\notin\operatorname{pos}W$; requires complete recourse ($\{Wy:y\ge0\}=\mathbb{R}^{m_2}$) or a nonempty $K_2$ to lift [PAGE 57].
2. **Dual-unboundedness veto:** $Q=-\infty$ excluded only by assuming $W^\top u\le q$ feasible for every $\omega$ [PAGE 57].
3. **Non-integrability / curvature veto:** certainty equivalence valid *iff* affine in $\omega$; Jensen bound degenerates to equality *iff* $f$ linear on each $E_\epsilon$ [PAGE 37, 55].
4. **Nonconvexity veto:** chance-constraint discretization can make the feasible set "nonconvex and disconnected, even [when] the feasible region of (2.59) is convex" [PAGE 77]; integer recourse destroys convexity entirely [PAGE 221].
5. **Dimensionality veto:** product cubature needs $M^d$ points; good-lattice error is $O((\log N)^d/N)$ but bounded below by Roth's $(\log N)^{(d-1)/2}/N$ [PAGE 209] — an information-theoretic wall, not a physical one.

Each is a mathematically exact breakdown regime; none is a "physical/biomechanical" veto.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three *verifiable* counterfactuals, each checkable against the source and internally consistent:

**CF-1 (Certainty-equivalence collapse).** *Claim:* replacing $\omega$ by $\bar\omega$ in $f_0=(\omega x)^2$ with $P[\omega=\pm1]=\tfrac12$ makes the surrogate objective $\equiv0$, certifying all $x$ optimal, while $\mathbb{E}\{f_0\}=x^2$. *Prediction:* any optimizer returning a nonzero $x$ under the surrogate is spurious; the true optimum is $x^\*=0$. *Verify:* [PAGE 25] verbatim — "$f_0(x,w)\equiv0$, but $\mathbb{E}\{f_0(x,w)\}=x^2$."

**CF-2 (Discontinuity of a chance constraint).** *Claim:* for $F_0(x)=P\{wx\ge1\}$, $P[w=\pm1]=\tfrac12$, the function is $0$ on $(-1,1)$ and $\tfrac12$ on $\{|x|\ge1\}$. *Prediction:* the feasible/level set is disconnected in value; a smooth-gradient method fails at $|x|=1$ and SQG requires the $\psi^\nu$ relaxation. *Verify:* [PAGE 25], Figure 1.3.

**CF-3 (Recourse penalty restores feasibility as $q\to\infty$).** *Claim:* the two-stage penalty approximation (2.68)–(2.70) with $q_j=M\to\infty$, $W=[I,-I]$ satisfies $y_j(x,\omega)=\max(0,h_j(\omega)-T_j(\omega)x)$ and drives $P\{T(\omega)x\le h(\omega)\}\to1$ at the solution, though the penalized and chance-constrained problems "are not equivalent." *Prediction:* for finite $M$ the returned $x$ violates the chance constraint with residual probability decreasing monotonically in $M$. *Verify:* [PAGE 77].

(For contrast, the integer positive result — makespan $EC_n(m)\sim n\mu/m$ as $n\to\infty$, with a.s. and $o(\cdot)$ refinements, and heuristic values asymptotic to it, "asymptotic everywhere-optimality" [PAGE 225, 229] — is a *verifiable convergence theorem*, not a veto, and is included only to bound the claim set.)
