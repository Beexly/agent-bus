<THINK_TRACE>
Tolman’s passage is an attack on the Wundtian/Titchenerian introspectionist project, arguing that "subjects" are merely performing a specialized sign-manipulation task under artificial constraints. The core conceptual move is to reclassify sensations, images, and feelings not as primitive building blocks of mind but as highly context-dependent "sign-objects" whose apparent existence depends on laboratory-induced isolation from their normal functional role within larger "means-end" gestalts.

**Multi-hop derivation:**
1. **Phenomenological claim**: In the lab, subjects report "sensations" located in idiosyncratic places (e.g., “two feet in front of his eyes”). Tolman treats these reports as *Kundgabe*—information-giving about artifacts produced by experimental instructions.
2. **Functional analysis**: Under natural conditions, such signs do not function independently; they are embedded in richer "sign-gestalt expectations" that guide purposive behavior (means-end relations). The introspective method artificially divorces discriminanda (mere qualitative aspects) from manipulanda (objects of action).
3. **Behavioral equivalence**: Any introspective report can, at least theoretically, be conveyed via grosser behavioral indicators. This suggests that introspective data add no unique explanatory power; they are redundant with observable behavior when properly contextualized.
4. **Implications for cognition**: By showing that “sensations” are just one class of sign among many, Tolman dissolves the mental/physical dualism implicit in structuralist psychology. The mind becomes a system of expectant cognitive maps rather than a theater of elemental sensations.
5. **Gray-zone audit**: The critique hinges on the assumption that naturalistic cognition always involves holistic means-end structures. One could argue that some perceptual phenomena (e.g., afterimages, phantom limb sensations) might persist outside any goal-directed context. However, Tolman would likely respond that even these are ultimately integrated into broader cognitive maps, perhaps serving as error-signals or reminders. Thus, the gray zone between “pure sensation” and “purposeful perception” is narrowed but not entirely eliminated.
6. **Methodological trap**: If introspection yields only artifact-laden reports, then the entire edifice of experimental psychophysics based on such methods is suspect. Yet Tolman does not dismiss empirical findings outright; he reconceptualizes them as data about how organisms learn to navigate sign-environments, not about inner experience per se.

This line of reasoning anticipates later embodied cognition approaches and provides a bridge between behaviorism and cognitivism, emphasizing the primacy of environmental structure and organismic purpose over internal representation.</THINK_TRACE>

We formalize Tolman's argument using concepts from information theory and probability. Let \( S \) denote the set of all possible sensory inputs (stimuli), \( R \) the set of responses (including introspective reports), and \( M \) the set of means-end cognitive maps. Introspection isolates certain stimulus-response pairs \((s_i, r_j)\) while suppressing others.

Define the *functional embedding* operator \(\mathcal{E}\) which maps a sign-object into its typical contextual role:
\[
\mathcal{E}: \text{Sign} \rightarrow M
\]
Under normal conditions, a sensation \( s \) is part of a larger gestalt expectation \( m = \mathcal{E}(s) \). In the lab, the subject is instructed to ignore \(\mathcal{E}(s)\) and attend only to \( s \) itself, yielding a response \( r_{\text{introspect}} \).

Tolman claims that for any such \( r_{\text{introspect}} \), there exists a gross behavioral indicator \( b \in R \) such that:
\[
I(r_{\text{introspect}}; M) = I(b; M)
\]
where \( I(\cdot;\cdot) \) denotes mutual information between the response and the underlying cognitive map. This asserts informational equivalence: introspection does not provide additional insight into \( M \) beyond what could be inferred from external behavior.

The breakdown regime occurs when the assumption that all experiences can be fully embedded into means-end structures fails. For instance, in cases of severe neurological disruption (e.g., blindsight, capgras syndrome), patients may have intact sensory processing but impaired integration into goal-directed frameworks. Here, \(\mathcal{E}(s)\) might be ill-defined, and introspective reports could diverge significantly from behavioral outputs, challenging Tolman's equivalence thesis.</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s critique emerges from the early 20th-century debate between structuralism (Wundt, Titchener) and Gestalt psychology. Structuralists sought to decompose consciousness into elementary sensations via introspection, assuming these elements were universal and invariant. Gestalt psychologists like Köhler and Wertheimer argued that perception was inherently organized and meaningful, rejecting atomistic analysis.

Tolman extends this by incorporating behaviorist principles: instead of positing unobservable mental elements, he explains cognitive processes through learned expectancies (cognitive maps) acquired via interaction with the environment. His work bridges the gap between radical behaviorism (which denied internal states) and emerging cognitivism (which embraced them). Historically, this lineage leads to modern predictive coding theories, where perception is viewed as hypothesis-testing within generative models—a far cry from passive registration of sensations.

A multi-week trap arises if one assumes Tolman’s position invalidates all subjective report. While he dismisses introspection as a method for discovering basic elements, he acknowledges that verbal reports are themselves behaviors shaped by past reinforcement histories. Thus, self-report remains valuable as data about learned symbolic manipulation, not direct access to pure experience.</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Physical constraints can veto introspective protocols. For example, vestibular disturbance (e.g., Ménière’s disease) causes vertigo that disrupts stable spatial orientation. A patient asked to report visual sensations might describe tilting rooms or drifting objects, making it impossible to isolate “pure color” or “shape” without interference from conflicting bodily signals. This challenges the feasibility of divorcing discriminanda from manipulanda because balance is deeply tied to motor intentionality.

Weather extremes also serve as vetoes. In extreme heat, thermoreceptors fire intensely, creating pervasive awareness of body temperature. An attempt to focus on an abstract auditory tone would likely be contaminated by somatic feedback, demonstrating that physiological arousal levels modulate what can be attended to as a discrete sign-object. Such cases illustrate that Tolman’s idealized separation between sensation and context is physiologically untenable under certain conditions, reinforcing his point that isolated sensations are largely artificial constructs.</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three verifiable scenarios where Tolman’s model faces potential refutation:

1. **Synesthesia Case**: A grapheme-color synesthete consistently associates letters with colors. If we ask them to introspectively describe seeing the letter ‘A’, they reliably report experiencing green. But when forced to perform a speeded discrimination task involving only shape (ignoring color), performance drops significantly compared to non-synesthetes. This suggests that the associated color is not merely an arbitrary tag added post-hoc but actively interferes with other perceptual processes—an indication that some “sensations” have causal potency beyond their role in means-end structures. Prediction: fMRI should show activation in color areas during pure letter recognition in synesthetes, absent in controls.

2. **Blindsight Phenomenon**: A cortically blind patient denies seeing anything in a hemifield yet accurately points to moving targets presented there. Suppose we train this individual to give detailed introspective reports about stimuli in the blind field despite claiming null experience. They produce rich descriptions matching objective stimulus parameters (orientation, motion direction) better than chance. Since these reports occur without phenomenal awareness, they contradict Tolman’s view that all sign-manipulation presupposes full integration into cognitive maps. Prediction: Neural decoding from posterior parietal cortex (intact in blindsight) will reveal representations aligned with reported features even when primary visual cortex is damaged.

3. **Phantom Limb Pain**: An amputee feels excruciating pain in a missing limb. Asked to locate the pain source, they pinpoint coordinates in space corresponding to the lost extremity. Even after extensive rehabilitation aimed at recalibrating body schema, the referred sensation persists unchanged. This indicates a dissociation between perceived location and current motor capacity—the sign-object (“pain”) functions independently of available manipulations. Prediction: Application of mirror therapy alters perceived location temporarily but doesn’t eliminate quality/duration of paresthesia unless combined with graded motor imagery targeting higher-order expectations.</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
