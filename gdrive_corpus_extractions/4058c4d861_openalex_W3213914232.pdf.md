The central count operator is the univariate PoARX recursion from [PAGE 59]:
\[
Y_t\mid \mathcal F_{t-1}\sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_t),\qquad
\lambda_t=\omega+\sum_{l=1}^{p}\alpha_lY_{t-l}+\sum_{l=1}^{q}\beta_l\lambda_{t-l}+\eta\cdot x_{t-1}.
\]
Here \(Y_t\) is a dimensionless event count per match interval; \(\lambda_t\) has units of expected events per match interval; \(\omega\), \(\alpha_l\), \(\beta_l\), and \(\eta\) are dimensionless coefficients under the linear intensity specification. The stationarity condition is stated on [PAGE 59] as
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{\max(p,q)}(\alpha_i+\beta_i)\lt 1.
\]
Breakdown regime: if the sum approaches or exceeds one, the intensity recursion loses the stability used for the ergodic theorem on [PAGE 66]. The multivariate extension on [PAGE 64] uses Frank’s copula:
\[
C_\rho(u_1,\ldots,u_K)=\phi_\rho^{-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\phi_\rho(u_k)\right),
\]
with generator and inverse from [PAGE 61]:
\[
\phi_\rho(t)=-\log\left(\frac{\exp(-\rho t)-1}{\exp(-\rho)-1}\right),\qquad
\phi_\rho^{-1}(t)=-\frac{1}{\rho}\log\left(1+\exp(-t)(\exp(-\rho)-1)\right).
\]
The copula arguments \(u_k\) are probabilities in \([0,1]\); \(\rho\) is dimensionless. The joint pmf is obtained by rectangle probabilities on [PAGE 62], but [PAGE 62] warns: “Due to the stepwise cumulative density functions, the copula is no longer unique (Joe, 2014).” Breakdown regime: discrete margins make the copula non-unique; identification is empirical, not automatic.

Renewal counts are defined on [PAGE 105] by \(N(t)=\sup\{m\ge0:a_m\le t\}\), and the convolution on [PAGE 106] is
\[
P_{m+1}(t)=\int_0^t P_m(t-u)f(u)\,du,\qquad P_0(t)=S(t).
\]
Here \(t\) is match time, \(f(u)\) has units of inverse time, \(S(t)\) is dimensionless survival, and \(P_m(t)\) is a probability. Numerical evaluation on [PAGE 106] uses the composite midpoint rule:
\[
\int_0^{Nh}f(x)\,dx=h\sum_{j=1}^{N}f\left((j-1/2)h\right)+O(h^2).
\]
Breakdown regime: small \(h\) increases cost; large counts and long horizons stress convolution accuracy; gamma shape derivatives require numerical integration on [PAGE 110]–[PAGE 111].

Scoring operators are explicit. The log score on [PAGE 77] is
\[
L(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(p_i),
\]
in nats if natural logarithms are used. The Brier score on [PAGE 104] is
\[
\mathrm{BS}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i-y_i)^2,
\]
with units of probability squared. The ranked probability score on [PAGE 81]–[PAGE 82] is
\[
\mathrm{RPS}=\frac{1}{r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_j-\sum_{j=1}^{i}e_j\right)^2,
\]
dimensionless for ordered categories. MSPE on [PAGE 113] is
\[
\mathrm{MSPE}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2,
\]
with units of the response squared; for goals, goals squared. The renewal boosting results on [PAGE 114] report gamma CV MSPE \(0.80207\) and test MSPE \(0.80915\), versus exponential CV \(0.95730\) and test \(0.94691\), and Weibull CV \(0.91240\) and test \(0.93728\). The feature state-space operator on [PAGE 126] is
\[
y_t\sim P(y_t|\theta_t,\xi_t),\qquad \theta_t=Z_t\alpha_t,\qquad \alpha_{t+1}=T_t\alpha_t+R_t\eta_t,\qquad \eta_t\sim N(0,Q_t).
\]
The state vector on [PAGE 127] includes team strengths, weaknesses, and home advantage; [PAGE 128] imposes \(\sum_j A_t^j=1\) for identifiability and adds a seasonal shock. Breakdown regime: without the sum constraint, the strength parameters are not identified; without stable AR transformations, the AR(p) states can leave the stationary region. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive loop has three agents: the team, the opponent, and the modeler. The team’s latent strength is modeled as AR(p) on [PAGE 127]; the opponent’s projection depends on that latent state; the modeler observes only counts and covariates. This creates a theory-of-mind problem: a team may change tactics because it anticipates the opponent’s projection, and the opponent may counter-adapt. The model’s AR state is a statistical summary of form, not a strategic equilibrium. Crisis habit regression appears when the modeler, under uncertainty, reverts to simple rolling averages. The thesis explicitly contrasts that habit with state-space projection on [PAGE 123]–[PAGE 125]: averages ignore opponent identity, while the state-space feature model attempts to account for strength and weakness. The counter-trap is to avoid overcorrecting in the opposite direction: treating every recent shot or possession spike as a permanent strength change. The AR(p) and seasonal-shock structure on [PAGE 127]–[PAGE 128] is a disciplined way to allow change without letting one match dominate. The clock is the chronological validation order. [PAGE 77] and [PAGE 101] use time-ordered cross-validation; [PAGE 129] fixes an initial window and refits on a 9.5-game-week clock. A recursive model that ignores the clock will leak future information into past projections. The correct stance is: use latent state for slow adaptation, use the shock term for known breaks, and use chronological holdouts to test whether the projected features actually improve classification. The evidence on [PAGE 132]–[PAGE 135] supports the state-space family over simple, linear, and exponential averages, but that is a predictive result, not proof that teams reason in the same state-space language.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather claims are vetoed unless they enter the likelihood. The thesis likelihood is built from count, intensity, copula, renewal, and state-space operators on [PAGE 59], [PAGE 64], [PAGE 106], and [PAGE 126]. The covariate set includes match statistics, FIFA and PPI ratings, xG, and home advantage, as described on [PAGE 18] and [PAGE 81]. Weather is mentioned only as pre-match information on [PAGE 17]; it is not an argument of \(\lambda_t\), not a state variable in \(\alpha_t\), and not a covariate in the reported classifiers. Therefore, for any candidate weather variable \(W_t\), the fitted model satisfies \(p(Y_t\mid \mathcal F_{t-1},\theta,W_t)=p(Y_t\mid \mathcal F_{t-1},\theta)\) unless \(W_t\) is added and identified. A claim that weather changes scoring through this model is non-identifiable. The same veto applies to biomechanical force, acceleration, or fatigue variables: the thesis reports normalized pitch coordinates on [PAGE 103], not SI force or acceleration; no force, torque, or metabolic variable appears in the equations. If an omitted physical variable \(W_t\) truly affects \(\lambda_t\) and is correlated with included covariates, the fitted coefficients absorb omitted-variable bias and the residual hazard becomes nonstationary. Breakdown regime: the veto is absolute for causal claims from the published model; it can be lifted only by adding \(W_t\) with units, estimating its coefficient, and showing stability under chronological validation. The physical-layer proof is therefore a support argument: the model’s support does not contain the cross-layer variable, so the posterior or likelihood is invariant to that variable, and no cross-layer effect can be adjudicated from the source alone.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto 1: temporal leakage. If a verifier detects that same-match statistics are used to predict a pre-match result, the run is rejected. The thesis itself supplies the firewall on [PAGE 118]: “The predictions rely on the collection of match statistics and therefore the match must already be complete.” Prediction: a leakage model will show inflated in-sample classification but fail a chronological holdout; the valid alternative is the projected-feature pipeline on [PAGE 123]–[PAGE 135], where state-space features are generated before the match.

Veto 2: distributional mis-specification. If a claim asserts that the exponential/Poisson renewal model is optimal for goals, the verifier checks out-of-sample MSPE. [PAGE 114] gives gamma CV \(0.80207\) and test \(0.80915\), Weibull CV \(0.91240\) and test \(0.93728\), exponential CV \(0.95730\) and test \(0.94691\). Prediction: the Poisson claim is vetoed on both CV and test MSPE; the gamma renewal model is the better supported count model in this source.

Veto 3: cross-layer overclaim. If a claim asserts a weather or biomechanical effect without the variable entering the model, the verifier checks the covariate list and units. Weather is only listed as pre-match information on [PAGE 17], and the equations on [PAGE 59], [PAGE 64], [PAGE 106], and [PAGE 126] contain no weather or force term. Prediction: the claim is vetoed as non-identifiable; a valid retest must add the variable, estimate its coefficient, and report chronological holdout performance. A secondary verifiable prediction is that state-space features should outperform simple averages: [PAGE 134] reports glmnet-SS RPS \(0.1971\) versus glmnet-EMA \(0.1986\), and XGBoost-SS RPS \(0.1975\) versus XGBoost-EMA \(0.1998\). That prediction is testable on the same splits and is independent of any cross-layer causal claim.
