Prediction: teams that cram generalship into the final week before a championship game exhibit elevated decision latency and unforced errors in that game, even if their mechanical drill metrics improve in practice. The trap is retrospectively causal because the failure appears in Week N+1, but its origin is the compressed Week-N training schedule.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Layer 0 — Own crisis habit regression. Under acute stress, motor control regresses to the earliest-consolidated habits. If mechanics were trained last but not overlearned, pressure triggers a mechanical default rather than generalship. Layer 1 — Opponent modeling of that regression. A defensive coordinator observes the quarterback reverting to predictable shotgun reads under blitz and disguises coverage to exploit the regressed habit. Layer 2 — Second-order modeling. The quarterback models the opponent's model of his regression and varies tempo, audibles, and snap timing to break the recursion.

The counter-trap is to train generalship first so the default under stress is the deliberative frame, not the mechanical reflex. However, deliberation consumes τ_cog [s], creating a clock vulnerability: if τ_cog + τ_mech > τ_playclock [s], the quarterback is forced into premature action or a delay penalty. Roper's target state—"thinks first and without effort because he has practised it" [PAGE 75]—means deliberation must be compiled into automaticity so recursion depth does not exceed the play-clock budget. Thus the counter-trap is bounded by the clock: over-reliance on slow deliberation under a compressed play clock becomes a new failure mode, converting a cognitive advantage into a temporal liability.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof with adjudication hierarchy.

Define an effective generalship output as a Boolean veto composition:
\[
G_{\text{eff}} = G \cdot \mathbb{I}(V_{\text{phys}} = 1) \cdot \mathbb{I}(V_{\text{bio}} = 1) \cdot \mathbb{I}(V_{\text{env}} = 1),
\]
where G ∈ [0,1] is dimensionless generalship quality, each veto indicator V ∈ {0,1}, and ℐ is the indicator function. If any V = 0, then G_eff = 0 regardless of training dose. This is a veto operator, not a ratio; no force-over-fatigue or similar invented quotient is used.

Layer 1 — Physical veto. Injury, illness, or concussion sets V_phys = 0. Cognitive primacy [PAGE 75] cannot override tissue failure; a physically compromised quarterback cannot execute the selected action. Layer 2 — Biomechanical veto. Throwing kinematics, grip integrity, and footwork stability determine τ_mech and execution reliability. Pain or instability increases τ_mech and failure probability; if the kinetic chain is broken, the correct target selection from π_G still yields a failed pass. Layer 3 — Environmental veto. Wind, precipitation, and temperature act upstream of decision quality. Crosswinds alter ball trajectory; precipitation lowers ball-surface friction; cold reduces manual dexterity. These factors are external to Roper's text but constitute real vetoes that nullify cognitive advantage.

Adjudication hierarchy: Physical > Biomechanical > Environmental > Cognitive. A cognitive "win" cannot veto a physical loss. The product-form operator assumes statistical independence among veto layers. Breakdown regime: correlated failures—e.g., freezing temperature simultaneously stiffening the throwing shoulder and slicking the ball—violate independence and require a joint veto measure rather than independent multiplication.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
