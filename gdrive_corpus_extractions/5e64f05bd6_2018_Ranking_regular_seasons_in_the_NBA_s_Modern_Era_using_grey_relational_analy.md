\dfrac{x_i(k)-\min_{j}x_j(k)}{\max_{j}x_j(k)-\min_{j}x_j(k)}, & k\in\mathcal B,\\[6pt]

\[

\]

\Delta_{\min}=\min_{i,k}\Delta_i(k).
\]

\[
\xi_i(k)=\frac{\Delta_{\min}+p\,\Delta_{\max}}{\Delta_i(k)+p\,\Delta_{\max}},
\qquad p\in(0,1],
\]
where \(p\) is the distinguishing coefficient; the paper notes it is “most commonly set to 0.5” [PAGE 33]. Then \(\xi_i(k)\in(0,1]\) is dimensionless. The group grades are
\[
r_i^{T}=\frac{1}{n_T}\sum_{k\in T}\xi_i(k),\qquad
r_i^{A}=\frac{1}{n_A}\sum_{k\in A}\xi_i(k),
\]
and the overall grade with unequal group sizes is
\[
r_i=\frac{n_T r_i^{T}+n_A r_i^{A}}{n_T+n_A}

\]
assuming equal weight within the full 45-criterion set. The paper reports grades as percentages, e.g. Curry’s traditional \(62.19\%\), advanced \(63.37\%\), overall \(62.72\%\) [PAGE 62]. \]
with the two-sided \(p\)-value in \([0,1]\). The paper reports \((z=-0.31,p=0.76)\) for traditional versus advanced, \((z=-0.22,p=0.83)\) versus PER, and \((z=-0.38,p=0.70)\) versus VORP [PAGE 35]. Spearman’s rank correlation is

dimensionless on \([-1,1]\). The paper reports \(r_s(100)=0.59\), \(r_s=0.45\) with PER, and \(r_s=0.67\) with VORP [PAGE 35]. These are measures of monotone rank association, not causal effects.
