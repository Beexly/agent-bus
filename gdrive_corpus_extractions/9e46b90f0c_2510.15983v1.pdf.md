<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The MO|RE ontology's scientific lineage traces through nested formal-ontology projects. BFO (Arp et al., 2015; Smith & BFO Developers, 2020) supplies the continuant/occurrent split [PAGE 4, 9]. IAO (Smith & Ceusters, 2015) contributes plan specifications and information content entities [PAGE 3, 9]. OBI contributes assay processes, evaluant roles, specified outputs, and value specifications [PAGE 3, 6, 9]. PACO contributes physical-activity concepts (frequency, regularity, intensity, location) but is criticized for lacking upper-level BFO grounding [PAGE 2, 3]. NFDIcore (Bruns et al., 2024) and PMDco (Bayerlein et al., 2024) demonstrate BFO 2020 mid-level patterning for research workflows and materials processing, respectively [PAGE 3]. The Leibniz Science Campus "Digital Transformation of Research" (DiTraRe) funds the broader exploration-and-knowledge-organization dimension in which MO|RE is a sensitive-data sports-science use case [PAGE 1, 2, 8].

Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy: (1) The longitudinal application scenario spans 2015–2020 [PAGE 6]. Any ontology version drift or IRI instability between ingestion years breaks retroactive SPARQL queries unless the MO|RE ontology implements explicit versioning and immutable class IRIs, which the "vision"-status paper does not yet specify [PAGE 1, 5]. (2) Protocol drift across years—e.g., shuttle-run distance or handgrip-dynamometer protocol changes—is concealed by the more:measures disposition shortcut that links test item directly to disposition [PAGE 4, 6, Appendix A]. Retroactive equivalence claims across 2015 and 2020 are therefore ontologically fragile unless process context (date, protocol parameters, test-process instance) is reified in the query graph. (3) Anonymization decay: linkage to KonsortSWD socio-economic data requires identifiable or pseudonymous keys [PAGE 2]; GDPR-driven suppression of age, BMI, or postal codes creates an irreversible singularity for retroactive interdisciplinary joins [PAGE 2, 5]. (4) The publication clock (5th International Workshop on Scientific Knowledge, Nov 2024 [PAGE 1]) forces the paper to present an aspirational ontology rather than a deployed, queryable knowledge graph; access control and full realization are explicitly future work [PAGE 4, 5]. Hence current citations describe a prospective graph, not an operational endpoint.

The clock—the Nov 2024 workshop deadline and the explicitly deferred ODRL role-based access-control layer [PAGE 5]—compresses all three into a publishable "vision" rather than a deployed system. Under crisis, the most likely outcome is a shallow ontology that wears BFO vocabulary but is queried like SQL, producing silent mismatches between plan specifications and realized measurements, especially in the Handgrip use case where process output is the only link to disposition [PAGE 6].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Four layers are adjudicated for veto validity.

Physical/measurement layer: A hand dynamometer records maximum force; the paper reports the value as "a decimal value in kilogram" [PAGE 6]. Force is dimensionally \(M L T^{-2}\), with SI unit newton (\(\text{N} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}\)); kilogram is mass, not force, unless kilogram-force (\(\text{kgf}\)) is explicitly intended. Veto: any cross-study force comparison using raw kilogram values without unit normalization to newtons is dimensionally invalid and must be vetoed.

Biomechanical layer: The Handgrip test is a maximal voluntary contraction performed one hand at a time [PAGE 4]. The ontology models no hand dominance, fatigue accumulation, learning effect, or repeated-trial structure, although the paper acknowledges "contextual factors" broadly [PAGE 2]. Veto: comparing raw handgrip values across participants without dominance or fatigue covariates introduces biomechanical confound; the KG must veto unadjusted comparisons unless these factors are attached to the test process.

Environmental/weather layer: Field test items such as shuttle run may occur outdoors [PAGE 4]. The source defines no weather schema (temperature, humidity, surface, wind), only the generic mention of contextual factors [PAGE 2]. Veto: longitudinal comparison of outdoor motor tests across seasons or years is vetoed unless environmental covariates are represented as qualities inhering in the test process context.

Policy/privacy layer: GDPR-compliant annotation of sensitive entities (age, BMI, postal codes) and ODRL-based role-based access control are explicitly future work [PAGE 5]. Veto: any SPARQL query returning raw age, BMI, or postal code for potentially identifiable participants is unlawful until the access-control layer is implemented; the KG must return \(\emptyset\) or anonymized age bins instead.

Proof of veto activation: Because role-based access control is absent [PAGE 5], the policy-layer veto is currently active. Therefore CQ1 may legally execute only over anonymized age groupings; raw-age bindings are vetoed by the governance layer independent of the ontology's logical consistency.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
