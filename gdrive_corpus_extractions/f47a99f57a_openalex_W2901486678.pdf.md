<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage is a three-generation cascade. Generation 0: Anderson & Bushman's General Aggression Model (GAM) ([PAGE 8–9]; [PAGE 19]) posits that repeated VVG exposure "priming aggressive cognitions, increasing arousal and creating aggressive states," feeding a personality-level accumulation loop (Figure 2). Generation 1: the meta-analytic turn — Anderson & Bushman (2001) and Anderson et al. (2010) supply the r≈.15–.19 priors; Ferguson & Kilburn (2009) and Ferguson (2007a,b) counter that "studies which introduce a third variable... had lower effect sizes" and that unstandardised measures inflate effects ([PAGE 25], [PAGE 109]). Generation 2: the risk-and-resilience reframing (Gentile & Bushman, 2012) plus the Catalyst Model (Ferguson et al., 2008; Figure 4) recast media violence as a "stylistic catalyst" acting on pre-existing aggressive personality rather than a direct cause — the exact reading the thesis ultimately endorses ([PAGE 182]).

The multi-week (here, multi-year) traps are twofold. First, the author's own developmental arc — [PAGE 182] "exploring the relationship between violent media and aggression since A-Level psychology," through undergraduate dissertation on MW2's release, to this doctorate — is a longitudinal commitment trap: the "No Russian" choice was pre-registered in imagination as a moral-avoidance signal, and that framing survived years before the data contradicted it. Second, the "moral panic" cycle Ferguson describes ([PAGE 7]) is a societal commitment trap: controversial game → non-scientist condemnation (Keith Vaz; the Domodedovo-airport copycat claim) → "speculative 'evidence.'" The thesis explicitly positions itself against this cycle. The "file drawer effect" named in the expert-solicitation email ([PAGE 211]: "prevent the 'file draw effect'") is the publication-bias ancestor that motivates the whole systematic-review enterprise and the conservative normative anchoring.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the participant as a strategic agent inside a consequence-free choice architecture. The game withholds feedback: [PAGE 125] "there is no record of the players' actions," and [PAGE 294] shooting the perpetrators merely forces a replay. Stripped of incentive, the observed choice (Skip/Watch/Shoot ≈ 53/141/712) becomes a low-cost signal of disposition — but only if the response categories are unconfounded.

Crisis-habit regression (the expected prior): theory predicts avoiders of on-screen atrocity score low on trait aggression and high on empathy, while active participants score high/low respectively. The data run the other way: [PAGE 145] "higher levels of anger and hostility were observed in participants who chose to skip," and skippers were also highest on Competition motivation.

Counter-trap (the revealed structure): "skip" is a polymorphic cell. It contains both genuine content-avoiders and multiplayer-only players who never intended to run the single-player campaign. Because MW2's competitive mode is the higher-arousal, higher-competition context, the multiplayer subset loads the skip cell with exactly the anger/hostility/competition profile the GAM attributes to competitive arousal, not to violent content. The author concedes this ([PAGE 156]). The correct counter-move is to re-partition skippers by "owns/plays multiplayer," which the instrument never captured — a specification gap, not a data failure.

Clock (temporal validity decay): MW2 shipped Nov 2009 ([PAGE 209]); ethics approval and testing ran 2014 ([PAGE 141]). The choice being modeled is a ~5-year-retrospective binary self-report, and [PAGE 309] flags that "the game is five years old and may not be remembered in detail." Recall decay biases the categorical assignment non-randomly (memory-availability heuristic: vivid "shoot" episodes are recalled more reliably than a passive "watch"), inflating the shoot cell and shrinking watch/skip — consistent with the 712/141/53 imbalance. Under this clock, the model estimates a mixture of true disposition and differential recall, and the regression's small R² (.03–.06) is what one expects when a heavily decayed categorical proxy is regressed on stable traits.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Here the "physical/biomechanical/weather" veto layer is instantiated as the measurement-and-physiology layer that can override a surface behavioral signal. Three vetoes fire:

Veto 1 (Normative-physical veto — decisive). A causal "VVG→pathological aggression" claim requires the trait scores to be elevated. They are not: standardized contrasts against Buss & Perry norms give max |d|≈0.24 (Hostility-skip), and Physical/Verbal aggression for the active shooters sit slightly *below* norm (d≈−0.19, −0.13). The author states the veto explicitly ([PAGE 139]): "the levels of trait aggression in VG players was within the norm group average." Any downstream narrative of clinical elevation is overruled at this layer.

Veto 2 (Arousal-source veto). The physiological literature cited shows competition, not violent content, drives arousal: [PAGE 88] "differences in the level of competition were responsible for changes in heart rate rather than violent content" (Adachi & Willoughby, 2011). This is a biomechanical/physiological override that collapses the "violent content causes arousal→aggression" pathway into a competition pathway — precisely the mechanism that explains why the multiplayer-loaded "skip" cell carries the anger signal.

Veto 3 (Measurement-instrument veto). Empathy effects invert by method: pupil-dilation studies disagree (Bartholow et al. lower PDR vs Arriaga et al. higher, [PAGE 85]), and only Perspective Taking (not Empathic Concern/Fantasy/Personal Distress) reached significance in the primary study (F=10.78). With one IRI subscale carrying the effect and two items pro-rated after the Q19/Q20 merge error ([PAGE 137–138]), the empathy layer cannot support a stand-alone causal verdict.

Adjudication: Vetoes 1 and 2 jointly force the conclusion toward the Catalyst Model reading ([PAGE 182]) — media as stylistic catalyst on pre-existing disposition — and away from the GAM's direct-effect reading. The thesis's own model comparison (GAM vs Browne-Pennell vs Catalyst) resolves in favor of the catalyst interpretation, consistent with these physical-layer vetoes.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactuals, each with a pre-registered quantitative prediction that would veto (or confirm) the thesis's soft conclusion. All are testable against the same cohort or a matched replication.

Veto A — Exposure specification. The study measured only a single-game yes/no plus years/weekly hours ([PAGE 148]), not cumulative violent-content exposure. Counterfactual: re-run with a validated exposure index (e.g., Anderson-Dill weighted-title × hours). Prediction: the choice↔aggression association attenuates, |r| falling below 0.10, because the current signal is carried by the competition confound, not content. Falsifier: |r| stays ≥0.15 with content covaried.

Veto B — Competition deconfounding. Add multiplayer-hours and Competition-motivation as covariates to the skip/play model. Prediction: β_Anger loses significance at the Bonferroni threshold (α'≈.007) once competition is partialled out, and model R² does not increase beyond .05. Rationale: [PAGE 156] shows skippers are competition-motivated multiplayer players. Falsifier: β_Anger remains significant with |β|≥.15 after adjustment.

Veto C — Population transport (forensic reversal). The normative anchoring holds in a general-adult sample (max |d|≈0.24). In a high-secure forensic cohort (the thesis's own Ch. 5 setting), baseline aggression is elevated. Prediction: the same trait battery yields within-group |d|≥0.5 against college norms, and the skip/shoot direction may re-invert because content-avoidance in a forensic sample would correlate with *lower* trait aggression — reproducing the intuitive prior that failed in the general sample. Falsifier: forensic cohort also sits within norm (max |d|<0.30), implying the trait profile is population-invariant.

Cross-veto prediction: if A and B both hold (attenuation and deconfounding succeed) while C also holds (forensic elevation), the coherent synthesis is that trait aggression predicts *self-selection into competitive play*, not a content-driven effect — the selection-effect interpretation independently reached by Breuer, Vogelgesang, Quandt & Festl (2015) ("Evidence for a selection effect among adolescents," [PAGE 197]). That triangulation would upgrade the thesis's correlational caution ([PAGE 2]: "a simple cause and effect relationship is not present") into a testable selection model.
