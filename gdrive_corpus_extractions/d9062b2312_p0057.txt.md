2. Misregistration: if ||R(θ_i) − I||_F > η for any i, structured aliasing enters G_N and h(G_N) may increase rather than decrease. Regime: unaligned sensory inputs or temporal jitter exceeding the integration window.
3. Correlated idiosyncrasy: if Cov(ε_i, ε_j) = Σ_c ≠ 0, Cov(G_N) does not vanish as 1/N; a systematic bias persists. Regime: all impressions share the same optical or attentional distortion.
4. Sensory blockade (dreaming): external afferent gain g_ext → 0, so the cross-layer veto is offline and G_N can be experienced as a percept without external correction [PAGE 1].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical lineage runs from Aristotle’s induction to universals, through Locke’s abstract general ideas as particular ideas “made” general, to Berkeley’s denial of abstract ideas in favor of particular ideas annexed to signs, and Hume’s endorsement of that move as “one of the greatest and the most valuable discoveries” in the republic of letters [PAGE 1]. James, writing in 1890, occupies a revisionary node: he accepts the associationist and averaging mechanics but resists linguistic reductionism for ideas of sensible objects, allowing pre-linguistic generics evidenced by dream imagery [PAGE 1]. Later scientific descendants include Wittgenstein’s family-resemblance concept, Rosch and Mervis’s prototype theory and typicality gradients, Hinton et al.’s distributed connectionist representations in which similarity emerges from co-activation weights, and Friston’s predictive-coding hierarchy in which prediction error is suppressed across levels—structurally analogous to James’s neutralization of differing impressions.

Multi-week traps embedded in this genealogy: (1) reifying the compound photograph as a literal neural photograph, ignoring that optical superposition is instantaneous and passive whereas neural averaging requires recurrent loops, synaptic plasticity, and membrane time constants (τ_m ≈ 10–100 ms) that James does not specify; (2) assuming “generic” invariably means “fuzzy and weak,” when for an anatomist examining several specimens of a new animal the generic type becomes the diagnostic specific against which variants are judged; (3) conflating dream vagueness with abstraction, when oneiric ill-focus may instead reflect cholinergic–aminergic neuromodulation and reduced afferent gating rather than abstraction per se; and (4) extrapolating Berkeley’s linguistic annexation to pre-verbal or non-human cognition despite James’s explicit reservation that general ideas of sensible objects may exist independently of language [PAGE 1].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under uncertainty, time pressure, or sensory degradation, the perceiver regresses from exemplar-specific retrieval to prototype-based categorization, forming G_N rather than storing x_i. This is fast but low-precision: a generic “man” glimpsed in twilight may be misidentified, and a generic “hill” with ill-marked outlines may conceal a precipice [PAGE 1]. The recursion operates at nested orders: order 0 is the subject forming G_N from impressions; order 1 is the annexation of G_N to a term t; order 2 is James’s theorizing that t may be unnecessary for sensible-object generics; order 3 is the present foundry modeling James’s theory while auditing its breakdown regimes and counterfactual vetoes.

Counter-trap: the assumption that generic = vague = less adaptive. The counter-argument is that prototypes support high-speed categorization under noise (high recall, lower precision), which is adaptive when the cost of delay exceeds the cost of occasional false positives. James’s “badly-focussed magic lantern” [PAGE 1] describes low spatial precision, not necessarily incorrect category membership. The trap is to judge generality by vividness rather than by decision utility.

Clock: introduce an integration window T_int and inter-stimulus interval Δt. If T_int < N·Δt, the impressions do not co-occupy the same “plate” and averaging fails; specific impressions dominate. If T_int ≫ N·Δt with attention drifting, the composite may include non-target impressions, contaminating μ. During dreaming, the effective T_int is extended while external input g_ext ≈ 0, so generic imagery persists unmoored from present sensing and is experienced as if it were a specific percept [PAGE 1]. Upon waking, the biomechanical and exteroceptive veto layers reengage and the generic dream geography collapses.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Formal veto operator: let E_ext be exteroceptive evidence (retinal, vestibular, proprioceptive) and Π(G_N) the sensory consequences predicted by the generic idea. Define a cross-layer veto
ℒ(E_ext, G_N) = 1 if ||E_ext − Π(G_N)|| > κ, else 0,
where κ is a tolerance threshold measured in just-noticeable differences (dimensionless JNDs) or radians for proprioceptive angle. If ℒ = 1, the generic idea is suppressed or tagged as imaginary.

Physical layer: photoreceptor quantum noise and lens aberration impose an optical modulation-transfer cutoff (cycles·degree⁻¹); no neural averaging can recover spatial information below this floor, vetoing any assumption that arbitrary N impressions can be perfectly registered into a sharp generic.

Biomechanical layer: proprioceptive and vestibular afferents constrain motor and spatial imagery. Dream reports of traveling through vague countries where “every feature of the scenery is vague” [PAGE 1] are possible because REM-related afferent blockade suppresses the biomechanical veto; on waking, ℒ reengages and the dream body cannot sustain the impossible geography.

Weather / environmental layer: ambient structure supplies the idiosyncratic ε_i that prevent over-averaging into monotonic blur. In fog or featureless terrain, ε_i become correlated across views, so Cov(G_N) does not vanish as 1/N; the result is a generic with “rivers [having] no defined banks” [PAGE 1], useless for navigation. This is the over-averaging breakdown regime.

Adjudication: James’s dream examples show the veto layers are offline (g_ext ≈ 0), permitting generic ideas to be experienced as percepts [PAGE 1]. In waking vision, ℒ = 1 terminates a generic unless E_ext is absent or ambiguous. Failure mode: sensory deprivation or release hallucinations, where ℒ fails despite E_ext being present, so a generic idea is mistaken for veridical perception.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each names a counterfactual model prediction, a veto ground truth from the source, a verifiable prediction, and a rejection criterion.

Scenario 1 — Language-dependence veto.
Counterfactual model M₁ predicts: all abstract/general ideas require a linguistic term; without language, no generic idea of sensible objects can be formed or maintained.
Veto ground truth: James [PAGE 1] states that general ideas of sensible objects “may nevertheless be produced in the way indicated, and may exist independently of language,” with dream houses and trees as the illustration.
Verifiable prediction: pre-linguistic infants (3–6 months) or adults with profound aphasia will still exhibit category-selective habituation or fMRI adaptation to novel members of a previously familiar object class (e.g., generalizing from exemplar dogs to a novel dog).
Rejection criterion: if category generalization is absent before productive vocabulary and absent in aphasia, M₁ is supported; if generalization appears before word use or persists without semantic language, M₁ is vetoed and James’s account is corroborated.

Scenario 2 — Monotonic averaging veto.
Counterfactual model M₂ predicts: increasing the number N of similar impressions always increases generic clarity; entropy h(G_N) strictly decreases with N with no performance decline.
Veto ground truth: the compound-photograph analogy requires points of agreement to be “brought out strongly” and differences “left vague,” implying registration is a precondition for the effect [PAGE 1].
Verifiable prediction: observers view N noisy images of the same object class with random spatial jitter exceeding the receptive-field integration scale. Categorization accuracy and reported clarity will follow an inverted-U function of N, peaking at N* and declining as misregistration aliasing accumulates.
Rejection criterion: if accuracy asymptotes monotonically with N and never declines, M₂ is supported; if a post-N* decline due to blur/aliasing is observed, M₂ is vetoed. Breakdown regime: registration error ||R(θ_i) − I||_F > η.

Scenario 3 — Dream-specificity veto.
Counterfactual model M₃ predicts: dream objects are degraded copies of specific episodic impressions, retaining high feature detail traceable to a single prior experience.
Veto ground truth: James [PAGE 1] describes dream houses and trees as “generic ideas of many past impressions,” recognizable as classes but with “ill-marked” outlines and rivers with “no defined banks,” like a badly-focussed magic lantern.
Verifiable prediction: dream journals coded for categorical identification accuracy (house vs. tree vs. man) versus episodic-detail specificity (door color, tree species, individual identity) will show high category accuracy but near-chance detail specificity, and composite dream persons will blend features from multiple distinct real individuals.
Rejection criterion: if dream reports show high episodic-detail specificity matching single prior impressions, M₃ is supported; if they show category-level recognition with low detail and feature-compositing, M₃ is vetoed and the generic-idea model is corroborated.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
