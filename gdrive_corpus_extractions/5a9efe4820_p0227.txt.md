Counter-trap. The deliberate counter-trap is to compute \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X) explicitly, estimate H(Y ∣ X) in bits, and run a pre-mortem on the ¬Y branch before acting. The counter-trap succeeds only if the decision clock τ permits enough samples N_X for \widehat{P}_N to stabilize; if τ is shorter than the sampling horizon, the agent must choose under Knightian uncertainty rather than measurable risk.

Clock vs. recursion. The recursive theory of mind nests these layers: Agent A reasons that Agent B treats the 90% association as universal [PAGE 212]; A therefore pre-positions for B's ¬Y failure. B, reasoning recursively, suspects A will do the same and over-invests in contingency, altering the empirical base rates that generated the 0.90 proportion in the first place. If both regress to habit, the joint failure probability P(¬Y_A ∧ ¬Y_B) is not recoverable from marginal proportions alone; the footnote's correlation machinery [PAGE 212] is required, but correlation presupposes stable joint moments that crisis conditions tend to destroy. Thus recursive modeling under a finite clock converts a manageable proportion into a second-order epistemic externality: the very act of treating "some X is Y" as near-universal changes the frequency with which ¬Y is observed, invalidating the original proportion for future rounds.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto layers are constraints that override Knightian proportion reasoning regardless of the magnitude of \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X).

Physical layer (hard invariants). Let V_phys(ω) ∈ {0,1} encode a conservation law or boundary condition (energy, mass, momentum). The admissible sample space is Ω′ = {ω ∈ Ω : V_phys(ω) = 1}. Any proportion computed over the full Ω that includes physically impossible states is invalid. Formally, if a state ω ∈ ¬Y violates V_phys, then P(¬Y ∣ X, V_phys = 1) = 0 even if the raw historical proportion \widehat{P}_N(¬Y ∣ X) > 0 [PACE 212 analogy]. Proof: conditionalization on Ω′ re-normalizes frequencies; impossible states carry zero measure under the physical model. Breakdown: if the physical boundary condition itself is time-varying and unobserved, the proportion estimated under one regime is vetoed when applied to another.

Biomechanical layer (information rate). Human probability estimation under stress is bounded by an effective channel capacity C_eff [bits/sec], below the Shannon capacity because panic, fatigue, and cognitive load reduce usable bandwidth. If a crisis requires estimating \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X) to precision δ bits within decision time t, and δ > C_eff · t, the biomechanical layer vetoes numerical estimation and forces regression to the heuristic "some X is Y" [PAGE 212]. Assumption: C_eff is finite and state-dependent. Breakdown: in extreme stress, C_eff → 0, making even ordinal ranking of X vs. ¬Y unreliable.

Weather/environmental layer (non-stationarity). Open environmental systems exhibit sensitivity to initial conditions; the conditional law P_t(Y ∣ X) drifts over the decision-relevant horizon. If the environmental Lyapunov time T_L is shorter than the exposure horizon W, then the historical proportion \widehat{P}_{historical}(Y ∣ X) [PAGE 212] is vetoed because P_t ≠ P_historical for t within W. This directly invalidates Knight's claim that a high proportion has "much the same significance for conduct as if the association were universal" [PAGE 212] when applied to weather-exposed systems, because the 10% exception may be systematically clustered in the very conditions under which the decision is made.

Adjudication order. A statistical proportion is admissible only if it passes, in sequence: physical admissibility (Ω′), biomechanical estimability (δ ≤ C_eff · t), and environmental stationarity (T_L ≥ W). Failure at any layer voids the proportion and forces a non-proportional contingency protocol.

Scenario 1 — Clinical triage under allergy constraint.
Setup: X = patient presents with syndrome S; Y = survives standard protocol. Historical \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X) = 0.95, supporting a heuristic "treat all X as if Y were assured" [PAGE 212 analogy]. Heuristic: administer treatment T to all X without screening. Veto: allergy marker A ∈ {0,1}; physical layer imposes P(death ∣ X, A = 1, T) = 1, a hard invariant. Verifiable condition: observe the subset {X = 1, A = 1, T administered}. Prediction: fatality rate in this subgroup is 100%, contradicting the 95% survival heuristic; the veto triggers on A = 1 regardless of the population proportion.

Scenario 2 — Autonomous vehicle in precipitation.
Setup: X = pedestrian detected in crosswalk; Y = pedestrian remains stationary until vehicle clears. Clear-weather \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X) = 0.99. Heuristic: maintain nominal speed upon X. Veto: weather layer breaks stationarity; heavy rain reduces visibility and tire-road friction, so P_rain(Y ∣ X) < P_clear(Y ∣ X). Verifiable condition: compare near-miss or contact rates per 1,000 X-events in clear vs. heavy-rain conditions. Prediction: the rainy-day ¬Y rate exceeds the clear-day rate by a factor greater than 5; nominal-speed policy is vetoed when rainfall intensity passes the visibility/friction threshold, because the historical proportion no longer governs conduct [PAGE 212].

Scenario 3 — Power transformer under correlated heat stress.
Setup: X = nominal top-oil temperature; Y = no failure in the next operating hour. Historical \widehat{P}_N(Y ∣ X) = 0.999. Heuristic: no load derating when X is nominal. Veto: graded-attribute correlation from footnote 1 [PAGE 212]; during a regional heat wave, ambient temperature and system load L become strongly positively correlated (ρ_{X,L} → 1), creating a common-cause shock that violates the independence implicit in a simple proportion. Verifiable condition: monitor transformer failure events on days with maximum ambient temperature > 40 °C. Prediction: conditional on X nominal, the failure rate during correlated heat events rises above 1 − 0.999 = 0.001, and system-wide outage correlation exceeds the i.i.d. estimate; the no-derating rule is vetoed when ρ_{X,L} and regional load correlation are observed above critical levels, collapsing Knight's conduct-equivalence assumption [PAGE 212].
