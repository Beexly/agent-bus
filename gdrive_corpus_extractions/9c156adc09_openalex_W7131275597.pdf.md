### 1. System tuple and feasibility predicate

Verbatim: "Physical systems P(K°A0S°E0U) consist of kinematics K, actuators and transducers A, sensors S, electronics E, and processing units U." [PAGE 20]

Reconstruction:

$$
P \;=\; \bigl(K,\,A,\,S,\,E,\,U\bigr)\;\in\;\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{U}
$$

**Units:** each factor is a *technology class*, not a numeric quantity; no SI dimension attaches to the tuple itself. The dimensioned quantities enter only through the evaluation maps below.

Feasibility (Eq. 1.2, [PAGE 20], OCR‑reconstructed):

$$
Q\!\left(\Phi_p,\Psi_c\right)\;\subset\;S\times R,\qquad \forall\,(\varphi,p,c)\in\Phi\times P\times C,\;\;\forall\,\mathcal{D}_{\text{data}}
$$

where $\Phi_p$ = performance measures, $\Psi_c$ = capability measures, $S\times R$ = specification‑requirement product set. **This is a set‑containment predicate**, i.e. a constraint, not an objective. *Breakdown regime:* the predicate is infeasible (empty solution set) whenever the specification box $S\times R$ does not intersect the achievable image $Q(\Phi\times P\times C)$ — the author's "problem incompleteness" [PAGE 22].

### 2. Evaluation functional (Eq. 1.1, [PAGE 20])

$$
Q:\;T\times\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{U}\;\to\;\mathbb{R}^{k},\qquad
(t,q,x,y,u)\;\mapsto\;Q(t,q,x,y,u)
$$

**Units:** $t$ in s; $q$ in application units (V, A, N·m, N, m·s⁻¹, rad, Pa, K); $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$; $y\in\mathbb{R}^{b}$; $u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ in V (PWM duty → amplifier) or N·m (servo torque) or N (thrust). $Q$ returns $k$ scalar metrics, each with its own unit (bandwidth in rad·s⁻¹, efficiency dimensionless, entropy in bit, data rate in bit·s⁻¹).

### 3. Minimax design problem (Eqs. 1.3–1.4, [PAGE 21–22])

$$
J \;=\; \min_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\ \max_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\ W(p,c),
\qquad
W(p,c)=W\!\left(q,x,y,u\right),\quad
p=f_p(q,x,y,u),\;\; c=f_c(q,x,y,u)
$$

$$
\text{s.t.}\qquad L_{\min}\;\le\;L(\varphi,p,c)\;\le\;L_{\max},\qquad (\varphi,p,c)\in\Phi\times P\times C
$$

with, verbatim, "$\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{P}$ and $f_c(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{C}$ are the nonlinear maps" [PAGE 21].

**Units:** $W$ is a scalar cost with composite units (typically weighted sum of s, rad, J, bit⁻¹); $L$ is a constraint margin with the units of its parent quantity (dimensionless for load factors, m for clearance, V for headroom).

**Exact singularity / breakdown regimes:**
- *Non‑existence of saddle point:* $W$ not quasi‑convex in $p$ or $\mathcal{P}$ non‑convex (combinatorial component selection) ⇒ Sion's conditions fail ⇒ $\min\max \neq \max\min$ and no saddle point exists. This is the precise mathematical content of the author's "cannot be solved" [PAGE 22].
- *Constraint‑set collapse:* $L_{\max}<L_{\min}$ under any admissible $(\varphi,p,c)$ ⇒ infeasible.
- *Dimensional inconsistency:* $W$ summing quantities of different units without explicit weights ⇒ the minimax is not well‑posed (a real risk given the prose list of "minimal‑time, minimal tracking error, minimal energy, maximum endurance, range and efficiency" [PAGE 22] — these have incompatible dimensions and **require** normalization).

### 4. Governing and constitutive equations (Eq. 1.5, [PAGE 22])

y = H(x),\qquad
\varphi = \psi(x,y,u),\qquad
u_{\min} \le u \le u_{\max}
$$

**Units:** $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ (mixed SI), $t$ in s, $u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$ (V / N·m / N), $B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$ with units of [state·s⁻¹·u⁻¹], $F:\mathbb{R}^{\ge0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{b}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}$.

**Breakdown regimes:**
- $\operatorname{rank}B(t,x) < n$ ⇒ uncontrollable subspace; no control law can drive $x$ in that subspace.

### 5. Implemented PID — continuous and velocity‑form discrete [PAGE 25]

$$

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Formal recursion.** Treat each agent $i$ (pilot, autopilot, adversary, maintainer) as carrying a level‑$k$ belief over the joint state:

$$
b_i^{(k+1)} \;=\; \tau_i\!\left(b_i^{(k)},\; o_i(t),\; \hat{u}_{-i}^{(k)}\right),
\qquad
\hat{u}_{-i}^{(k)} = \arg\max_{u_{-i}} \; \mathbb{E}_{b_i^{(k)}}\!\left[W_i\right]
$$

where $o_i$ is agent $i$'s observation and $\hat{u}_{-i}^{(k)}$ is its level‑$k$ model of the other agents' policy. Level 0 = "the plant is as modeled"; level 1 = "the sensor may be lying"; level 2 = "the adversary knows my level‑1 test and is shaping the lie to pass it." The source's flight‑management architecture at [PAGE 23] is a physical instantiation: the FMS issues references $r^{*}(t)$ to servo loops, and the servo loops issue $u(t)$ to aerodynamic surfaces — two nested levels of intent, with the inner loop's model of the outer loop being nothing more than the reference buffer.

**Crisis habit regression.** The source states the operational fact and then endorses it. Verbatim: "Model‑free, model‑following, learning‑enabled and other design premises have being investigated. These concepts are restricted to open‑loop stable systems, assume homogeneous measurements, exceptional superior processing capabilities, etc." [PAGE 25]. And: "These minimal complexity robust, low‑latency controllers are implemented by low‑power analog electronics and MPUs, ensuring near‑optimal performance of commercialized systems. Designers minimize the use of mathematic‑centric optimization and design findings, and, focus on adjusting preconfigured reconfigurable controllers on operational simulators, hardware experimental platforms or functioning systems" [PAGE 25].

That *is* habit regression, described approvingly: under real conditions, the deployed policy collapses to the lowest‑complexity controller in the repertoire — a fixed‑structure PID with tuned gains. It is a **feature** under bounded rationality (it is auditable, it has a closed‑form stability certificate, it runs on a 216 MHz Cortex‑M7 [PAGE 17]) and a **bug** when the plant regime has silently changed (trap 2 above). The regression is only safe if a monitor exists whose detection window is shorter than the regime change's time constant.

**Counter‑trap.** The counter‑trap is the adversary *inducing* the regression. Sequence: (i) present the aggressive model‑based controller with measurements that make its innovations look anomalous; (ii) the supervisor, following the author's own doctrine, "reconfigures" down to the conservative fixed‑gain PID; (iii) the adversary now knows the exact closed‑loop bandwidth, because it is a published, fixed structure; (iv) the attack is delivered inside that bandwidth. The defense is to make the reconfiguration itself unobservable in timing — i.e., to randomize $T_s$ within the stability‑feasible interval.

**Clock.** The recursion is only coherent if all agents share a timebase, and the source says so: "Coding and decoding are synchronized by clock" [PAGE 24]. Without a common $\{t_k\}$, level‑$k$ and level‑$(k+1)$ beliefs are computed over *different* sample sets, and the mutual model degrades into stale replay. This is precisely the failure the reference set flags at [PAGE 30] with replay/GCM authentication literature (McGrew, "Efficient authentication of large, dynamic data sets using Galois/counter mode (GCM)," 2005) — the cryptographic countermeasure to clock desynchronization.

**Three‑way adjudication summary:** *habit regression* is the correct response to genuine model uncertainty and the wrong response to adversarial perturbation; *counter‑trap* is the adversary exploiting the regression; *clock* is the shared resource whose integrity determines whether either agent can even tell which scenario it is in.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Veto algebra.** Define a seven‑component veto vector

$$
V(x,u,t)=\bigl(V_{\mathrm{EM}},\,V_{\mathrm{aero}},\,V_{\mathrm{thermo}},\,V_{\mathrm{struct}},\,V_{\mathrm{comp}},\,V_{\mathrm{human}},\,V_{\mathrm{cyber}}\bigr)\in\{0,1\}^{7}
$$

and the admissible set

$$
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{adm}}(x,t)=\Bigl\{u\in[u_{\min},u_{\max}]\;:\;V(x,u,t)=\mathbf{0}\Bigr\}
$$

*Lexicographic precedence:* safety ($V_{\mathrm{struct}}, V_{\mathrm{human}}$) ≻ mission ($V_{\mathrm{aero}}, V_{\mathrm{thermo}}$) ≻ security ($V_{\mathrm{cyber}}, V_{\mathrm{EM}}$) ≻ performance ≻ optimality. **Proof obligation:** if $u^{*}$ from Eq. (1.4) satisfies $u^{*}\notin\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{adm}}$, the deployed control is the projection $u=\Pi_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{adm}}}(u^{*})$. Stability under projection holds iff the projected vector field remains in the contingent cone of $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ at every $x$ on the boundary — the standard viability condition — which for the box constraint $[u_{\min},u_{\max}]$ is automatically satisfied, but for the *union* of multiple vetoes it is **not** automatic and can fail at corners where two veto surfaces intersect.

**Layer 1 — Electromagnetic.** Source: "resiliency to electromagnetic spectrum, data quality, information security and security services are essential" [PAGE 17]. Or: "The intrinsic multi‑physics complexity pose design and deployment challenges due to … electromagnetic interference, electromagnetic spectrum, high‑throughput resilient communication needs, etc." [PAGE 19]. *Veto predicate:* commanded control authority exceeds the EMI‑derated actuator authority ⇒ degrade to the last certified command. *Level‑0 proof:* the derating factor must be measured on the installed platform, never assumed — the source gives no numeric derating, which is itself the honest position.

**Layer 2 — Aerodynamic / wake.** Source: "Satellite navigation, wind‑and‑wake sensing and trajectory tracking for extended close‑proximity formation flight are implemented" [PAGE 18]; "fifth‑generation fighter aircraft and quadrotor helicopters are flying in close‑proximity formation" [PAGE 17]. *Veto:* predicted wake‑induced Δα exceeds the certified envelope ⇒ abort formation geometry. *Layer‑0 proof:* requires the wind‑and‑wake sensor to have bandwidth ≥ formation control bandwidth; if not, the veto fires too late and the formation is already in the upset.

**Layer 3 — Thermodynamic / environmental.** Source anchors: the barometric sensor in the Pixhawk stack (TE MS5611) [PAGE 17]; §4.8 "Identification of Dynamic Environmental System" [PAGE 8]; climate‑modeling references (Trenberth, *Climate System Modeling*; Washington & Parkinson; Wells, *The Atmosphere and Ocean*) and Milanković [PAGE 28]. *Veto:* measured density/altitude/temperature outside the identification envelope of the control model ⇒ the gains are out of distribution ⇒ revert to scheduled gains or conservative fixed structure. *Explicit flag:* icing, precipitation, and turbulence‑severity thresholds are **not** stated anywhere in these 31 pages; any numeric weather veto threshold used in practice is imported from airworthiness standards external to this source, and I label it as such rather than attribute it here.

**Layer 4 — Structural / actuation.** Source: Eq. (1.5) "u_min < u < u_max" [PAGE 22]; "control surfaces are displaced using hydraulic and ele[ctric] servos" [PAGE 24]. *Veto:* servo rate limit hit ⇒ effective delay grows ⇒ $\Delta\mathrm{PM}$ grows (see RLVR §A). *Layer‑0 proof:* rate saturation is a *delay*, not a gain reduction, and it erodes phase margin faster than amplitude saturation.

**Layer 5 — Computational.** Source: "the Pixhawk 4 has a main flight management unit processor STM32F765 (32‑bit Arm Cortex‑M7, 216 MHz), IO processor STM32F100 (32‑bit Arm Cortex‑M3, 24 MHz)" [PAGE 17]; "TMS320F280 digital signal processors features 16 PWM outputs, 6 high resolution PWM outputs, 16 channels 12‑bit ADC" [PAGE 24]. *Veto predicate:* required loop rate exceeds the achievable rate given the ADC channel count and PWM resolution. Worked bound: 16 channels at 12 bits ⇒ per‑channel LSB = FS/4096 ≈ 2.44×10⁻⁴ FS; if aggregate throughput is shared across 16 channels, the per‑channel rate falls by 16×, and the sampling bound $f_s \gtrsim 10\omega_c/\pi$ converts directly into a maximum admissible $\omega_c$.

**Layer 6 — Human / biomechanical.** Source: the book speaks of "human interactions, adaptiveness, data quality and seamless secure communication" [PAGE 15] and of supervisory control by a "core flight management system processor" [PAGE 23], but **gives no human physiological limits**. *Honest adjudication:* the biomechanical veto layer (G‑tolerance, control‑input bandwidth of the human neuromuscular loop, fatigue over a multi‑hour sortie) exists and is decisive for manned platforms, but **its numeric thresholds are not in this source**. Any figure quoted here would be fabrication. ### Scenario A — Clock / sampling‑rate veto

**Counterfactual:** the 100 Hz control loop (the class of loop implemented on the Cortex‑M7 class processor at [PAGE 17]) is halved to 50 Hz without retuning.

**Verifiable reward:** measured phase margin from a closed‑loop frequency sweep, plus the presence/absence of a sustained limit cycle in the actuator command.

**Veto:** fire if $\Delta\mathrm{PM} > 30°$, i.e. if $\omega_c(T_s/2 + T_d) > \pi/6$.

**Prediction:** with $\omega_c = 50\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $T_s = 10\ \mathrm{ms}$, $T_d = 5\ \mathrm{ms}$: $\Delta\mathrm{PM} \approx 50(0.005+0.005) = 0.50\ \mathrm{rad} \approx 28.6°$. If the plant has any additional unmodeled lag, the loop goes unstable rather than degrading — a sharp, easily falsified bimodal signature.

### Scenario B — Saturation / anti‑windup veto

**Counterfactual:** a step reference large enough to drive the servo into $u_{\max}$ for ≈0.5 s, run with and without integrator clamping, on the law $u_n = u_{n-1}+k_{e0}e_n+k_{e1}e_{n-1}+k_{e2}e_{n-2}$ [PAGE 25].

**Verifiable reward:** overshoot percentage and recovery time after leaving saturation.

**Veto:** fire if the integrator state exceeds $u_{\max}$ while $u$ is saturated — a directly instrumentable condition, requiring no model.

**Prediction:** with integral time $T_i \approx 1\ \mathrm{s}$ and a 0.5 s saturation dwell, the unclamped loop accumulates roughly half a unit of $T_i$ worth of integral, producing overshoot in the **30–50%** band versus **<10%** clamped; recovery time inflates by roughly $T_i\ln(1+\text{windup fraction})$. The falsifiable content is the *sign of the difference and its rough magnitude*, not the exact percentage. This is the single highest‑yield RLVR check in the entire architecture, because it requires no plant model at all — only the actuator command and the saturation flag.

### Scenario C — Sensor‑integrity / replay veto

**Counterfactual:** replay a recorded IMU stream (the ICM‑20689 / BMI055 / IST8310 / MS5611 ensemble at [PAGE 17]) into a live estimator while the vehicle is stationary, and separately while it is manoeuvring.

**Verifiable reward:** normalized innovation squared (NIS) statistic from the filter — a scalar computable online, with a known $\chi^2_m$ null distribution under the assumed noise model (Kalman–Bucy lineage [PAGE 29]).

**Veto:** fire if NIS exceeds the $\chi^2_m$ threshold for $k$ consecutive samples.

**Prediction:** (i) **Stationary case:** the replay is nearly invisible — the true and replayed states coincide, innovations stay inside the null band, and the veto **silently fails**. This is the important negative result and the one most likely to be missed. (ii) **Manoeuvring case:** innovations leave the null band within a small number of samples once the commanded specific force diverges from the replayed specific force, and the detection latency scales inversely with manoeuvre aggressiveness. The falsifiable prediction is the *asymmetry*: detection probability is a strong increasing function of manoeuvre amplitude and a near‑zero function at rest, so a stationary replay test proves nothing.
