<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical, biomechanical, and weather/schedule vetoes, adjudicated as a layered proof. A 1915 projection passes only if no layer vetoes it.

Physical layer (mass and momentum): Tennessee’s back-field averaged 147 lb (66.68 kg) and Lindsay was 155 lb (70.31 kg) [PAGE 241]. Against a “very heavy line-plunger” such as VMI’s Oakes [PAGE 241], the momentum mismatch is \(\Delta p = (m_{plugger}-m_{back})v\). If opposing line mass exceeds back-field mass by more than ~15 kg, the light back-field enters a high-impulse collision regime where \(K=\tfrac12 mv^2\) scales with opponent mass and square of relative speed. Veto: any projection of Tennessee as a 1915 “topnotcher” [PAGE 241] is vetoed if the returning nucleus cannot replace retiring mass at the line of scrimmage; light backs facing heavy plungers suffer disproportionate injury and stoppage risk.

Biomechanical layer (tackle impulse and availability): Lindsay “never had to be taken from a game” [PAGE 241] is right-censored availability, not proof of low tackle impulse. Impulse \(\mathbf J=\int\mathbf Fdt=\Delta\mathbf p\) implies that for a given change in momentum, a lighter player receiving a tackle from a heavier opponent experiences larger acceleration (\(a=F/m\)). Veto: a durability claim extrapolated from Lindsay’s 1914 season to a 1915 back-field with lower mean mass and higher opponent mass is biomechanically unsound unless tackle-force distributions are supplied. They are not on page 241, so the durability projection fails by missing-data veto.

Weather/environmental layer (fields and travel): Vermont played “most of the games… on the opponents’ grounds, which necessitated long and tiresome trips” [PAGE 241], and Tennessee’s noteworthy games were “on foreign flelds and bitterly contested” [PAGE 241]. Foreign fields and long trips introduce covariates (field hardness, travel fatigue, climate) that are unmodeled in the narrative scores. Veto: any 1915 road-game projection for Vermont or Tennessee that does not adjust for travel load and field conditions is vetoed; observed home/road score differentials are confounded by environment.

Adjudication: The physical layer (mass mismatch) and biomechanical layer (impulse under mass asymmetry) jointly veto any 1915 “topnotcher” claim for Tennessee unless returning mass is demonstrated to exceed 1914 levels. The weather/travel layer independently vetoes unadjusted road projections for Vermont and Tennessee. Because page 241 supplies no variance, no injury exposure, no field-condition metrics, and no opponent mass data, the evidentiary bar for projecting 1915 success is not met; the only non-vetoed statement is the retrospective description of 1914 events exactly as printed.
