If the user’s crisis behavior involves unusual movement at presumed-rest times, the low-power mode may undersample the PPG and miss the beat entirely, confirming a false “rest” belief—a self-fulfilling observability trap.

The clock layer is the diurnal constraint: HRV is captured “right before a user awakes” [PAGE 19]. This anchors recovery assessment to a narrow circadian gate. Recursive theory of mind therefore predicts failure modes at the intersection of (a) the algorithm’s predicted wake time, (b) the user’s actual sleep inertia, and (c) the device’s duty-cycle state at that instant. If the algorithm mispredicts wake time by even one sleep cycle, the pre-awake HRV sample is either missed or taken during sleep-stage transition, where PPG-ECG equivalence is least assured. The system thus optimizes for power efficiency at the exact temporal resolution required by its own recovery metric, creating a recursive tension between energy conservation and measurement validity.

Because the source ties LED current to ~10 mA per emitter at ~80% efficiency and total system draw to ~40 mW [PAGE 20], there is no dynamic headroom described for compensating low perfusion by increasing optical power beyond the nominal envelope. Veto rule: SNR_peak < SNR_threshold ⇒ suppress HRV output and flag “insufficient optical signal.” No numeric SNR_threshold is provided in the text, which itself is a governance gap.

Biomechanical layer veto: Motion status is derived from an accelerometer and partitioned into exercise, light motion (walking), no motion/rest, and sleep [PAGE 20]. If motion status = exercise but duty cycle is scheduled at the low end (1%–20% [PAGE 20]), effective sampling may violate the Nyquist requirement for the pulse-wave harmonic content, causing aliasing. Veto rule: (motion_status = exercise) ∧ (f_s · D < 2 f_max) ⇒ veto HR measurement and force transition to continuous sampling, consistent with the source’s statement that detectors “may sample continuously when the user is performing an exercise routine so that the error standard deviation is kept within 5 beats per minute (BPM)” [PAGE 20]. Treadmill running introduces repetitive impact artifacts that can spectrally overlap cardiac harmonics; absent motion-artifact cancellation details in the excerpt, the conservative veto is to treat steady-state running as a low-confidence HRV epoch regardless of spectral algorithm output.

Environmental/weather layer veto: Ambient light is an explicit contextual input [PAGE 20]. High ambient irradiance (direct sunlight, bright indoor lighting) injects shot noise and possible detector saturation into the reflected-light measurement. Because PPG relies on detecting small pulsatile variations superimposed on a large DC optical background, strong ambient light reduces contrast. Veto rule: ambient_irradiance > detector_saturation_level ⇒ veto PPG-derived HR/HRV until the processing module adjusts LED brightness, duty cycle, or sampling rate per its power-management authority [PAGE 20].

Cross-layer adjudication order: physical veto (no usable signal) takes precedence over biomechanical veto (signal present but motion-corrupted), which takes precedence over environmental veto (signal corrupted by ambient light). Only when all three layers pass does the system emit an HRV recovery value, and only under the rest/sleep precondition established at rest [PAGE 19].
