**1. Drive-State Dynamics & Effector Recruitment**

$$\tau_D \frac{dD}{dt} = -D + k_E \cdot f(S^E)$$

The activation probability $p_i(t)$ for each effector unit $i$ (e.g., a motor neuron pool) is modeled as a logistic function of the drive relative to its threshold $\theta_i$:
$$p_i(t) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-\gamma (D(t) - \theta_i)\right)}$$
where $\gamma$ is the steepness parameter (units: 1/dimensionless). The "range of effectors" activated is the set $\mathcal{A} = \{ i \mid p_i > p_{thresh} \}$ for some operational threshold $p_{thresh} \approx 0.5$. As $D$ increases, $|\mathcal{A}|$ increases monotonically.

**2. Variance Decomposition in Motor Output**
The actual motor command vector $\mathbf{u}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^M$ is a stochastic function of the active set $\mathcal{A}$ and internal noise. The total variance covariance matrix $\Sigma_u$ can be decomposed into two components consistent with Hull's "neural interactions":
$$\Sigma_u = \underbrace{\eta_E(D) \cdot \mathbf{I}}_{\text{Efferent Noise}} + \underbrace{\eta_A(D) \cdot \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x})}_{\text{Afferent/Proprioceptive Noise}}$$
*   $\eta_E(D)$: Efferent noise intensity, assumed proportional to drive (higher firing rates increase Poissonian variance). Units: (motor units)$^2$/s.
*   $\eta_A(D)$: Afferent noise scaling factor. It depends on the sensitivity of proprioceptive receptors to the ongoing movement.
*   $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x})$: The Jacobian of the forward kinematics/sensory map, mapping joint-space movements $\mathbf{x}$ to sensory feedback space. This term makes the noise state-dependent and potentially non-diagonal.

**3. Behavioral Search & Functional Probability**
Define the "behavioral space" $\mathcal{B}$ as the space of all possible gross motor patterns. A specific stimulus condition (object in eye) defines a small subset $\mathcal{B}_{sol} \subset \mathcal{B}$ of solutions (e.g., precise wiping motions). The high-drive state generates a probability density $q_D(\mathbf{b})$ over $\mathcal{B}$.
*   Low $D$: $q_D(\mathbf{b})$ is a narrow, possibly learned, distribution centered on efficient solutions.
*   High $D$: $q_D(\mathbf{b})$ becomes broad and diffuse, approximating a uniform or prior distribution $q_0(\mathbf{b})$ shaped by phylogenetic constraints (e.g., bias toward bringing hands near face).

The "functional success" is the probability $P_{success} = \int_{\mathcal{B}_{sol}} q_D(\mathbf{b}) d\mathbf{b}$. Hull's observation is that even for very broad $q_D$, $P_{success}$ remains significant because $\mathcal{B}_{sol}$ has substantial measure under the evolutionary prior $q_0$. Precision (high $P_{success}$ for a specific $\mathbf{b}$) requires $D$ to be low enough to sharpen $q_D$ around $\mathcal{B}_{sol}$.
