**1. The Mid-Parent Projection Operator**
Let the statures of the two parents be represented as vectors in a feature space $\mathcal{H} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, denoted by $\mathbf{p} = (h_{father}, h_{mother})^\top$, where height is measured in meters [m]. Due to sexual dimorphism, Galton explicitly mentions transmuting the mother's stature. We define a scaling factor $\kappa \approx 1.08$ (historical approximation) to align female metrics to the male reference frame.
The projection onto the Mid-Parent scalar $\mu$ is defined by the linear operator $\hat{M}$:
$$ \mu = \hat{M}[\mathbf{p}] = \frac{1}{2} \left( h_{father} + \kappa \cdot h_{mother} \right) $$
Units: $[\mu] = \text{m}$.
Assumption: The projection assumes linearity of genetic contribution, ignoring epistasis.

**2. The Regression Operator and Variance Dissipation**
Galton discusses how children do not perfectly match their Mid-Parent. Let $Y$ be the random variable representing the offspring's stature, and $X$ be the Mid-Parent stature. The conditional expectation $E[Y|X]$ follows the regression equation:
$$ E[Y|X=x] = \bar{y} + r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} (x - \bar{x}) $$
Where:
- $\bar{y}, \bar{x}$ are population means [m].
- $\sigma_y, \sigma_x$ are standard deviations [m].
- $r$ is the correlation coefficient (dimensionless, $r \in [-1, 1]$).
For blending inheritance, Galton empirically found $r < 1$ (specifically around 0.66). 
This introduces a contraction mapping on the variance. If we iterate this process over generations, the variance of the distribution $\sigma^2_n$ at generation $n$ evolves according to:
$$ \sigma^2_{n+1} = r^2 \sigma^2_n + \sigma^2_\epsilon $$
where $\sigma^2_\epsilon$ represents the environmental/noise variance added each generation (measured in $\text{m}^2$).
Singularity/Breakdown Regime: If $\sigma^2_\epsilon = 0$ (perfect determinism), then as $n \to \infty$, $\sigma^2_n \to 0$ because $r^2 < 1$. All populations would converge to the exact mean height, leading to a singularity of zero diversity. Biological reality avoids this breakdown via constant injection of genetic entropy (mutation/recombination).

**3. Information-Theoretic Cost of Blending**
We quantify the loss of distinct parental information when mapping two parents to one Mid-Parent using Mutual Information $I(X; Y)$.
$$ I(\mathbf{p}; Y) = H(Y) - H(Y|\mathbf{p}) $$
If blending is perfect averaging, $Y = \hat{M}[\mathbf{p}]$, then $H(Y|\mathbf{p}) = 0$, and all information about the individual variances of $h_{father}$ and $h_{mother}$ (the difference term $\Delta h = |h_f - \kappa h_m|$) is discarded. The operator $\hat{M}$ is many-to-one, creating a massive kernel of lost information.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s 1889 work sits at a precise intersection of historical scientific lineage and impending paradigm shifts. To understand the "Mid-Parent" concept, we must trace its retrocausal roots.
**Scientific Lineage:**
The idea of blending directly inherits from Darwin’s *Variation of Animals and Plants*, where Darwin postulated pangenosis—a hypothetical mechanism where gemmules from all parts of the body blend in the gonads. Galton was testing this continuum hypothesis. Furthermore, the methodology owes a debt to Quetelet’s "l'homme moyen" (the average man), applying Laplace’s error theory to biology. Galton transformed biology from a qualitative descriptive science into a quantitative statistical discipline.
**The Multi-Week Trap (Historical Blind Spot):**
Galton fell into a profound trap that lasted decades: the assumption that variation was inherently continuous and fluid. By focusing entirely on the Mid-Parent (the mean), he created a blind spot for discrete particulate inheritance (Mendelism), which had been rediscovered just a few years prior in 1865 but remained obscure until 1900. 
His trap was methodological: by using averages to wash away the "noise" of individual differences, he inadvertently washed away the very mechanism of segregation. If you average a tall dominant allele and a short recessive allele across millions of families, you see regression to the mean. But by doing so, you miss the underlying binary switching that actually drives evolution. His statistical brilliance blinded him to the microscopic algebra of genetics. It took Fisher, Yates, and Haldane in the 1910s-1930s to reconcile Galton’s regression with Mendel’s particles, realizing that the "blending" Galton saw was merely the phenotypic shadow of genotypic assortment.

He traps his critics by proposing a falsifiable test: separate fraternities into those with unlike parents and those with like parents. By predicting that the variance between these groups should be statistically identical *if* blending is true, he forces the empirical data to either vindicate his Mid-Parent construct or destroy it.

The urgency (the clock) driving Galton was his eugenic ambitions. To engineer human improvement, he needed predictable, mathematically tractable laws of heredity.
