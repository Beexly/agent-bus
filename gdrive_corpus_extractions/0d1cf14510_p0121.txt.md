Let R_{\max}(v, f) be the maximum response amplitude at contraction velocity v and fatigue state f. Habit strength s_{HR} cannot drive R beyond R_{\max}. Veto rule V_bio: if the predicted Φ(N; θ) implies an R exceeding R_{\max}(v, f) for the observed effector state, the equation is vetoed; the observed response is capped by physiology, not learning.

Weather layer (environmental stimulus): The interchangeability S ≅ s [PAGE 112] assumes stable stimulus representation. Ambient temperature T, humidity H, and lighting L alter receptor thresholds and neural conduction, shifting the mapping from physical stimulus S to neural representation \dot{S} during conditioning [PAGE 112]. Veto rule V_weather: if |ΔT| > δ_T or |ΔL| > δ_L between conditioning and evocation, the dots-and-context rule is breached and the one-to-one correspondence collapses; s_{HR}(N) estimated under one weather regime does not transfer to another.

Adjudication: A finding from [PAGE 112] is accepted only if V_phys, V_bio, and V_weather all pass. Failure of any one veto voids the inference that changes in N caused changes in s_{HR}. The hierarchy is physical veto first (data invalid), then biomechanical (model output impossible), then weather (context mismatch). This is a veto proof because no amount of curve-fitting to Figure 21 [PAGE 112] can override a saturated transducer, a fatigued muscle, or a changed stimulus context.

Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Transducer-ceiling counterfactual.
Protocol: Train subjects to N = 100 reinforcements, measuring reaction amplitude y on a transducer with known saturation A_{\max} = 50 mm. Then independently raise expected habit strength (via increased reinforcement magnitude) while holding N fixed.
Veto condition: If y plateaus at 48–50 mm despite the magnitude increase, V_phys fires.
Prediction: The observed asymptote is a measurement ceiling, not a true s_{HR} asymptote; a higher-resolution transducer (δ_y smaller, A_{\max} larger) will reveal continued growth in y with N, falsifying the simple Φ(N) plateau read from Figure 21 [PAGE 112].

Scenario 2: Stimulus-modality interchangeability counterfactual.
Protocol: Condition with visual stimulus S_v (dots applied during conditioning per [PAGE 112]); test evocation with auditory stimulus S_a while erroneously using undotted S as if S ≅ s across modalities.
Veto condition: V_weather fires if |Δ modality| crosses the generalization gradient threshold and performance drops below 20% of the visual-evocation baseline despite N = 50.
Prediction: s_{HR}(N) estimated under visual conditioning will not predict auditory responding; the curve fitted to visual data [PAGE 112] is vetoed for cross-modal transfer, demonstrating that the dots/context rule and interchangeability assumption are boundary conditions, not universal laws.

Scenario 3: Crisis habit-regression counterfactual.
Protocol: Establish Habit A with N_A = 20 reinforcements, then Habit B with N_B = 5 reinforcements. Induce acute crisis (cold pressor stressor) immediately before test, and measure which habit is expressed.
Veto condition: If performance expresses Habit A (N_A = 20) rather than Habit B (N_B = 5, the most recent), the simple recursive model s_{HR} = Φ(N_current) is vetoed in favor of a dominant-history model s_{HR} = max_i Φ(N_i) weighted by stress sensitivity.
Prediction: Crisis regression magnitude is inversely proportional to |N_A − N_B| and directly proportional to stressor intensity; the clock term (time since last reinforcement of A) moderates the regression, so s_{HR} is a function of the full reinforcement vector \mathbf{N} = (N_A, N_B) and inter-reinforcement timing, not the scalar N alone [PAGE 112].
