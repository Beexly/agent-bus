$$R_i^{Coach}=R_i^{Player}\cdot\Delta_t,\qquad \nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m R_i^{Coach}\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi^C_\theta(x_i).$$

Singularities / breakdown regimes:
1. GRPO $\sigma_i\to0$: undefined advantage; regularized to a zero gradient at [PAGE 12]. Filtered batch keeps $\sigma_i>0$ on filter rollouts, but training-rollout re-sampling ([PAGE 12] lines 6 vs 14) can re-introduce $\sigma_i=0$.
2. Coach REINFORCE has no baseline; variance $\mathrm{Var}[R_i^{Coach}\nabla\log\pi]$ is unmitigated; $|\Delta_t|\sim10^{-3}$ ([PAGE 7], Fig. 4) makes $R_i^{Coach}$ tiny.
3. Sign inversion: $\Delta_t<0 \Rightarrow R_i^{Coach}\le0 \Rightarrow$ suppression of accepted tasks, weighted toward high $R_i^{Player}$.
4. Majority-vote truth gap: $r_{i,j}$ measures agreement with $y_i^\star$, not correctness; a confidently-wrong plurality ($acc_i\to1$) is filtered out, but a *split* wrong plurality can pass.

Hyperparameters ([PAGE 15–16]): Coach lr $1\!\times\!10^{-6}$, temp $0.7$, entropy coeff $+1\!\times\!10^{-2}$, KL $1\!\times\!10^{-3}$, $1000$ steps. Player lr $1\!\times\!10^{-6}$, KL $1\!\times\!10^{-3}$, entropy coeff $-1\!\times\!10^{-2}$; response lengths (tokens): 2048 (1.5B), 3300 (7B), 18000 (Nemotron), 8192 (OctoThinker).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is a clean descent through policy-gradient and self-play families. The optimizer core is REINFORCE (Williams) generalized through PPO's clipped surrogate (Schulman et al., 2017, [PAGE 1]) into GRPO (Shao et al., 2024, [PAGE 3]), whose defining move is dropping the critic and replacing it with a group-relative z-score (Eq. 1). The verifiable-reward layer descends from RLVR (Lambert et al., 2024, [PAGE 2–3]) and its rule-based binary form (Eq. 3). The pseudo-labeling descends from self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023, [PAGE 3]). The Coach warm-up uses PRIME/Eurus-2 implicit process rewards (Cui et al., 2025, [PAGE 5]).

The adversarial branch it deliberately departs from is R-Zero (Huang et al., 2025, [PAGE 6–7]) and Absolute-Zero (Zhao et al., 2025, [PAGE 3]), both cited. The paper's stated differentiation is cooperative vs adversarial: "Instead of casting the Player model as competitors, the Coach is responsible for adapting the task difficulty" ([PAGE 2]), and it argues adversarial setups are "prone to instability, collapsing into nonsensical or unlearnable proposed tasks" ([PAGE 2]). The self-play seed traces to AlphaZero (Silver et al., 2017, [PAGE 1]).

Multi-week reproducibility traps embedded in this lineage:

Trap 1 — compute matching. Table 1 ([PAGE 6]) runs CPMöbius up to $1000$ steps but compares against "R-Zero (Iter 3)," which converges in three iterations ([PAGE 13]). When forced to matched budget (60th-step checkpoint, Table 3, [PAGE 13]), CPMöbius *underperforms* R-Zero on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: overall $26.9$ vs $27.1$. The headline win on that model (28.8 vs 27.1) is a longer-run artifact, not a same-compute result. The paper's defense — "the improvement in model capability stems from our framework raising the performance upper bound, rather than simply leveraging additional compute" ([PAGE 13]) — is an assertion, not a controlled ablation.

Trap 2 — the "data-free" scope. It is explicitly narrowed: "'data-free' refers only to the co-evolution stage after Coach–Player collaboration begins" ([PAGE 2]), and the Coach is "warmed up with 4K PRIME Eurus-2-RL-Data" ([PAGE 5]). The environment signal $\Delta_t$ is computed on AMC ([PAGE 5–6]). So three external data touchpoints exist (warm-up corpus, AMC labels for $Acc_{val}$, base-model priors). The A.4 (20% AMC) and A.5 (Minerva/OlympiadBench as $D_{val}$) experiments ([PAGE 13–14]) are the leakage rebuttals.

Trap 3 — EMA smoothing. Figures 3–5 ([PAGE 7–8]) are "smoothed with Time Weighted EMA," which can mask high-variance or unstable single-seed trajectories — precisely the regime where an un-baselined REINFORCE Coach (Eq. 7) is most fragile.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The system is a fixed-depth theory-of-mind machine. The Coach maintains a first-order model of the Player (via $acc_i$ and $\Delta_t$), and the Player implicitly models the Coach's task distribution through the curriculum it is trained on. Neither has an explicit nested belief representation; the "mind-reading" is entirely mediated by two scalars — $acc_i$ (per task) and $\Delta_t$ (per round).

Crisis-habit regression: the dominant crisis mode is $\Delta_t<0$ persistence. Mechanically, a regression makes every $R_i^{Coach}\le0$; REINFORCE then reduces the likelihood of the *entire* accepted batch, with the steepest suppression on easy (high-$R^{Player}$) tasks. If $\Delta_t$ stays negative for many rounds (e.g., because $D_{val}$=AMC is small and noisy, inflating $\mathrm{Var}[\Delta_t]$), the Coach's policy drifts away from the current capability frontier — the opposite of the intended zone. The design counter-trap is the difficulty filter $0.2\le acc_i\le0.8$, which re-anchors the batch each round regardless of Coach drift, and the positive Coach entropy coefficient ($+10^{-2}$, [PAGE 15]) which resists premature collapse to a narrow task mode. The Player's *negative* entropy coefficient ($-10^{-2}$, [PAGE 15–16]) is the countervailing force: it drives the Player toward confident, low-entropy solutions, which is what makes majority-vote pseudo-labels sharp in the first place.

The "clock" here is the two-timescale structure: the Player updates $G$ inner GRPO groups per round on fresh rollouts, then the Coach takes one REINFORCE step on the batch summary. Because $\Delta_t$ reflects the Player's *aggregate* post-update accuracy change, the Coach is always one round behind the Player's true capability — a lag that, combined with $|R^{Coach}|\sim10^{-3}$, means the Coach reacts slowly. Observed training dynamics are consistent with a stable-but-slow clock: answer consistency declines for non-RL models (1.5B, Nemotron) as difficulty ramps, but "maintains a reasonably range" for already-RL-tuned models (7B, OctoThinker) ([PAGE 8]) — the latter have less headroom for the Coach to exploit, so $\Delta_t$ saturates and the curriculum self-limits.

The adversarial counter-move (R-Zero's Challenger) is what CPMöbius deliberately removes to avoid the "stump the other" collapse ([PAGE 2]); the recursive cost is that a purely cooperative Coach has no incentive to *stress-test* beyond the $[0.2,0.8]$ band, so systematic Player weaknesses that produce confidently-consistent-but-wrong answers near the band edges can be reinforced rather than exposed.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
I adjudicate three physical/information-theoretic vetoes on the reward pipeline.

Veto A — truth vs agreement (majority-vote ceiling). Formally, the reward is $r_{i,j}=\mathbb{I}[y_{i,j}=y_i^\star]$ where $y_i^\star=\arg\max_a \#\{j:y_{i,j}=a\}$. Let $p$ be the Player's true per-task correctness probability. The pseudo-label equals truth only when the plurality is correct; by a Condorcet-type bound, $P(y_i^\star=\text{truth})$ grows with $p$ and $n$ but is *not* guaranteed for adversarial or systematically-biased tasks. Veto proof: if the Player has a shared bias so that $p<\tfrac12$ but a wrong answer $a'$ commands a plurality, then $y_i^\star=a'$ and the update *reinforces* $a'$. Survival clause: the filter $acc_i\le0.8$ discards near-unanimous (likely biased-confident) tasks, so the surviving risk is the *split* regime $\tfrac{0.2\le acc_i\le0.8}$ with wrong plurality. This veto is not eliminated, only bounded. Information units: mutual information $I(y_{i,j};\text{truth})$ upper-bounds learning progress; when biases align, $I\to0$ despite high reward.

Veto B — GRPO zero-variance (thermodynamic-style degeneracy). When $\sigma_i=0$ (all rollouts agree), $A_{i,j}=0/0$; regularized to $0$ ([PAGE 12]). Such groups carry exactly zero policy gradient — a "dead" state. The filter lowers incidence on filter-rollouts, but re-sampling for training ([PAGE 12] lines 6 vs 14) reintroduces it. Veto bound: the fraction of accepted tasks with degenerate training rollouts is nonzero whenever empirical training accuracy hits $\{0,1\}$, most likely near the $0.8$ acceptance edge.

Veto C — credit-assignment collapse. $R_i^{Coach}=R_i^{Player}\cdot\Delta_t$ assigns one scalar to $m=16$ heterogeneous tasks. Formal proof of coarseness: $\partial R_i^{Coach}/\partial(\text{task }i\text{ quality})$ is identical across all $i$ except through $R_i^{Player}$; the true per-task causal effect on $\Delta_t$ is unidentifiable from a single scalar. With $|\Delta_t|\sim10^{-3}$ and no REINFORCE baseline (Eq. 7), the Coach's per-task signal-to-noise ratio is $O(10^{-3})/\mathrm{std}$, forcing reliance on the difficulty filter and entropy regularization for stability rather than on the reward itself.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Confident-wrong plurality reinforcement. Setup: hold the Coach fixed, feed a task family where the Player has a systematic shared error producing a split-but-wrong plurality landing in $[0.2,0.8]$. Prediction (falsifiable): after training, Player accuracy on a *ground-truth-labeled* version of that family drops or stalls while self-consistency (agreement) rises. Verifiable via the gap between $acc_i$ (agreement) and true accuracy on a labeled probe. The paper never reports agreement-vs-truth calibration, so this is an open, testable veto.

Scenario 2 — $\Delta_t$ sign-inversion drift under noisy $D_{val}$. Setup: replace $D_{val}$ with a smaller/harder set so $\mathrm{Var}[\Delta_t]$ rises and negative excursions persist. Prediction: Coach output length (Fig. 6, [PAGE 17]) escalates faster and answer consistency (Fig. 3, [PAGE 7]) over-shoots downward, eventually pushing $acc_i$ below $0.2$ for most candidates (over-hard curriculum), collapsing the acceptance rate and stalling $\Delta_t\to0$. Falsifiable: measure acceptance rate vs training step; the veto predicts a U-shaped collapse in accepted-batch yield under high $\mathrm{Var}[\Delta_t]$.

Scenario 3 — Coach-capability ceiling. Setup: use a Player strictly stronger than the Coach (Coach is fixed Qwen2.5-Math-7B, [PAGE 5]; take a $>$7B RL-tuned Player). Prediction: the Coach cannot generate tasks above its own competence, so $acc_i$ pins near the top of $[0.2,0.8]$ or candidates fail the upper bound, $\Delta_t\to0^+$, and gains vanish. Consistent evidence already in-hand: OpenMath-Nemotron (a strong SFT model) shows the smallest gain, $+2.6$ overall ([PAGE 7]), and the paper itself notes "a weak or unskilled Coach tends to generate ambiguous or unhelpful tasks" ([PAGE 2]). Falsifiable prediction: gain magnitude is monotonically decreasing in (Player strength − Coach strength).

Cross-check against reported numbers: the "highest OOD across all four base models" claim ([PAGE 7]) is contradicted by Table 1 ([PAGE 6]) on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B ($26.9$ vs RENT $27.1$) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B ($38.3$ vs RENT $38.8$); and the compute-matched result (Table 3, [PAGE 13]) shows CPMöbius below R-Zero on the 1.5B. Both are concrete, verifiable vetoes on the paper's headline framing, distinct from the method's internal mechanics.
