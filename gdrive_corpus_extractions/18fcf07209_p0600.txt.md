Environmental/weather layer: The continuous discharge chain James describes can be interrupted by stochastic external input—sensory noise, distraction, or changes in the stimulus stream—that acts analogously to weather in a dynamical system. If a loud noise occurs between the presentation of letter C and letter B, the association path may fail to propagate because the "later" cell is not in the appropriate discharge state when the earlier cell's signal arrives. The veto condition is that inter-trial or inter-item environmental variance exceeds the temporal integration window of the cortical cell assembly, breaking the chain \(S_t \to S_{t+1}\).

Cross-layer adjudication rule: James's hypothesis on [PAGE 590] is upheld only if all three layers permit transmission. Münsterberg's intervention [PAGE 590]—removing intervening movements—is specifically a biomechanical veto. If serial association persists without movement, the biomechanical layer is not necessary and James's "drainage" hypothesis is weakened. If association collapses under movement suppression, the biomechanical layer is confirmed as a gate, and the purely cortical transaction must be reformulated as a sensori-motor transaction rather than an ideational one. Physical layer veto is absolute (no synaptic asymmetry = no directionality); environmental veto is probabilistic (noise degrades but need not eliminate association).
