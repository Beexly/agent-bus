Thus the recursion has a built-in pause: the model outputs a candidate list, but the executive’s second-order belief about trend and fit can veto the model’s first-order output.

The clock is bounded by the data window and the CBA timeline. The analysis covers only 2013–2014 and 2014–2015 [PAGE 7], and only post-2011 contracts [PAGE 11], so the clock cannot be extended backward without re-estimating the labor-market pricing regression under a different collective-bargaining regime. Prospective validation on a held-out season is absent [PAGE 26–27], meaning the recursive loop is open: the model generates salary recommendations for the next offseason, but no counterfactual confirmation exists within the paper. The termination condition is situational, not universal: there is no equilibrium salary because team cap space and utility from winning vary [PAGE 24], so the recursion ends at the executive’s idiosyncratic optimization rather than at a market-clearing price.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three layers can veto the model’s conclusions: physical, biomechanical, and environmental/strategic.

Physical layer: The data require at least one snap for inclusion [PAGE 7], so garbage-time snaps, limited substitution packages, and partial-season appearances are weighted equally with full-season starter snaps in the game aggregates [PAGE 8]. No injury or missed-time variable exists in Table 1 [PAGE 7], so a lineman who plays eight games at high quality and eight at low quality after injury is collapsed into one season average, which Section 6.1 explicitly flags as potentially inaccurate [PAGE 24]. If injury-driven heterogeneity is systematic across clusters, the t-tests at α = 0.01 [PAGE 15] may reject cluster-versus-sample equality for the wrong reason.

Biomechanical layer: The observable statistics are play outcomes—sacks, pressures, stuffs, rushing yards, knockdowns, quarterback release time—plus holding penalties [PAGE 7]. Hand placement, footwork, anchor strength, and technique are not recorded. PFF grades attempt to capture technique subjectively [PAGE 6], but the objective model has no biomechanical control. Therefore two linemen with identical differential statistics can differ materially in true blocking quality, and the regression coefficients in Table 11 [PAGE 33] are reduced-form reduced-form associations, not structural biomechanical effects.

Environmental / weather layer: Weather variables (wind, temperature, precipitation) are absent from the dataset [PAGE 7 Table 1]. Wind can asymmetrically affect passing and rushing by side of the field, and temperature can alter snap counts and substitution patterns. If weather covaries with the “to side” / “not to side” split, the differential statistic Δg = g_to − g_not loses exogeneity: part of the differential reflects weather rather than lineman quality [PAGE 8–9]. The authors acknowledge strategic confounding: a defensive coordinator may align the best defensive lineman against the worst offensive lineman, exaggerating the latter’s poor statistics, while a strong teammate on the same side can inflate them [PAGE 26–27, Section 6.3]. Since defensive alignment and opponent quality are not in the regression [PAGE 33], omitted-variable bias is possible.

Veto proof conditions: The conclusions hold only if (i) snap counts are representative of true playing opportunity, (ii) biomechanical quality is orthogonal to the observed outcome differentials conditional on the controls, and (iii) weather and defensive alignment are uncorrelated with side-of-line assignment within the sample. Violations of (iii) directly threaten the exogeneity of Yards per Attempt Differential (β = 382,197, p = 0.044516 [PAGE 33]) and Stuff % Differential (β = −82,247, p = 0.012284 [PAGE 33]), which are central to cluster separation and therefore to tail-probability classification.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
