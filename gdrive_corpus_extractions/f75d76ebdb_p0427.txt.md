This creates a multi-week trap for researchers: if one only measures immediate $S$ and $R$, the intervening cognitive architecture ($M_c$) appears statistically insignificant or random noise. It takes longitudinal observation (weeks of latent learning experiments, as Tolman later demonstrated with rats in mazes) to reveal that the "silent" phase of $M_c$ development is actually constructing a cognitive map. The historical trap was assuming that behavior without immediate reinforcement was meaningless, whereas Tolman showed it was highly structured and predictive of future efficiency.
