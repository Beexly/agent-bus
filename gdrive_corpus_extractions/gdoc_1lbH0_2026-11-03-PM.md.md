**1. Completion Percentage Over Expected (CPOE).** For a set of pass attempts $i = 1,\dots,n$ with completion indicator $I_i \in \{0,1\}$ and model-expected completion probability $\hat p_i \in (0,1)$ conditioned on throw features (air yards, separation, pressure, etc.),

$$\mathrm{CPOE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(I_i - \hat p_i\bigr).$$

Units: probability, dimensionless, bounded in $[-1,1]$; reported here as percentage points. Source use: W2 cites Geno Smith at "4th CPOE" among 30 qualified QBs [PAGE 5], and Caleb Williams at "30th of 32 qualified passers in 2025, 2nd in 2026" [PAGE 5]. **Assumptions:** $\hat p_i$ is a fixed, well-calibrated model; the qualification floor is stated; the same model version is used across both seasons being compared. **Singularity/breakdown regimes:** (a) *Calibration collapse* — if $\hat p_i \to 0$ or $1$ for a subset of attempts (e.g. wide-open screens), the log-loss calibration target degenerates and CPOE becomes a near-deterministic indicator; (b) *rank instability at the floor* — the sampling variance of a rank statistic scales like $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ in the number of qualified attempts, so a 30→2 swing of 28 places is only credible if $n$ is large enough that $\mathrm{SE}(\text{rank}) \ll 28$, which the sweep does not state; (c) *model-version drift* — comparing a 2025 CPOE to a 2026 CPOE requires the same expected-completion model, and the sweep flags exactly this: "CPOE is model-dependent (expected-completion model varies by source)" [PAGE 6].

**2. Expected Points Added (EPA).**

$$\mathrm{EPA}_{\text{play}} = \mathbb{E}\bigl[\Delta \mathrm{EP} \mid \text{pre-snap state}\bigr], \qquad \mathrm{EPA}_{\text{agg}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{EPA}_{\text{play},i}.$$

Units: points per play (aggregate: points per play, not points per game). Source use: W5's "EPA/dropback ... against the blitz" [PAGE 10]; W1's "QB pressure rate faced vs QB EPA when pressured" [PAGE 3]. **Assumptions:** the EP surface is estimated on a representative play population; garbage-time and end-of-half boundary conditions are handled; the blitz/pressure flag is defined (5+ rushers vs simulated pressure is the standard ambiguity the sweep names [PAGE 10]). **Breakdown regimes:** (a) *boundary singularity* at $\Pr(\text{score next}) \to$ deterministic (end of half, kneel-downs) where $\Delta\mathrm{EP}$ is dominated by clock, not skill; (b) *definition sensitivity* — blitz-tagged EPA is not comparable across vendors; (c) *selection on the pressure flag* — EPA-when-pressured is a conditional mean on a post-treatment state, so it is not a causal estimate of "pressure cost" without the unconfoundedness assumption, which the sweep implicitly assumes when it calls the scatter a "pressure-cost" instrument [PAGE 3].

**3. Success rate and explosive rate (the W4 conjunction).** With $\mathrm{SR} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{EPA}_{\text{play},i} > 0]$ and $\mathrm{XR} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[\text{play}_i \in \mathcal{X}]$ for an explosive-play set $\mathcal{X}$ (typically a yardage threshold by down),

$$\text{Conjunction claim: } \mathrm{rank}_{\text{SR}}(\text{def}) \le 5 \;\wedge\; \mathrm{rank}_{\text{XR}}(\text{def}) \le 5.$$

Source: "The only top 5 run defenses in terms of both success rate and explosives allowed: Jacksonville and Seattle" [PAGE 9]. **Null model.** If the two rankings were independent across $N=32$ teams, $\Pr(\text{both top-5}) = (5/32)^2 = 0.0244$ per team, so the expected number of teams in the intersection is $32 \times 0.0244 = 0.781$, and under a Poisson approximation $\Pr(\ge 2 \text{ teams}) \approx 1 - e^{-0.781}(1 + 0.781) \approx 0.19$. **Gray-zone finding:** observing exactly 2 teams is *not* strong evidence against independence at $N=32$; the claim is plausible and checkable but the intersection count is underpowered. The conjunction format is methodologically stronger than a single ranking because it rules out two failure modes at once, but the sweep's own "harder to fluke than a single ranking" language [PAGE 9] overstates the statistical power at this $N$. **Breakdown regime:** the test degenerates when SR and XR are strongly positively correlated in the population (which they are — both are functions of defensive quality), because then the independence null is wrong in the *conservative* direction (expected intersection $> 0.781$), and the observed 2 is even less surprising.

**4. Expected fantasy points (xFP).**

$$\mathrm{xFP} = \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} n_t \, v_t,$$

where $\mathcal{T}$ = {carry, target, goal-line carry, red-zone target, …}, $n_t$ is the realized count of touch type $t$, and $v_t$ is the author's value table (points per touch type). Units: points per game. Source: W9's "2026 Expected Fantasy Points Per Game — Running Backs" [PAGE 18]. **Assumptions:** the value table $v_t$ is fixed and published; byes are handled consistently; the "expected" is over average outcomes, not over the player's own distribution. **Breakdown regimes:** (a) *value-table singularity at the goal line* — $v_t$ for a goal-line carry is the most sensitive choice in the whole table, and the sweep names it: "goal-line and reception values are the sensitive choices" [PAGE 18]; (b) *per-game slicing across byes* distorts ranks [PAGE 18]; (c) the gap $\mathrm{actual} - \mathrm{xFP}$ is *not* a pure efficiency residual, because it also contains TD-location variance and opponent quality.

**5. TRACER survival formalism.** Let $T$ be the time-to-expiry of an empirical claim, with survival $S(t) = \Pr(T > t)$ and hazard $h(t) = f(t)/S(t)$. The reported empirical distribution is $\Pr(\text{expired}) = 0.44$, $\Pr(\text{weakened}) = 0.34$, $\Pr(\text{held}) = 0.16$, $\Pr(\text{strengthened}) = 0.06$, with median lifespan $\hat t_{50} \approx 15.7$ months [PAGE 23]. **Assumptions:** claim population is representative; right-censoring at study end is non-informative; "expired/weakened/held" are reliably coded across claims. **Breakdown regimes:** (a) *hazard non-constancy* — if the hazard is front-loaded (claims die young), $S(t)$ is not exponential and the 15.7-month median overstates the tail; (b) *selection* — the 50-claim headline subset vs the 215-claim corpus are different populations, and the sweep flags the reconciliation as "inference, not fact" [PAGE 23]; (c) *coding drift* — "weakened" vs "held" is a boundary that different coders will draw differently.

**6. Passer rating (bounded composite).** The NFL formula is a sum of four bounded components $a,b,c,d \in [0, 2.375]$ scaled as $\mathrm{PR} = 100 \cdot \frac{a+b+c+d}{6}$, hence $\mathrm{PR} \in [0, 158.3]$. Source: "Drake Maye led the week at 141.5" [PAGE 17]. **Breakdown regime:** the composite is a known-flawed ordinal — it saturates at 158.3 and is insensitive to sack/EPA context — so it is cited "as the post's claim, not endorsed" [PAGE 17].

**7. Harness² self-modification loop (agents).** With frozen model weights $M$ and harness state $\theta_h$ (system prompt, guardrails, memory, tools, subagents, skills), a propose/probe/compose operator $\mathcal{A}$ updates $\theta_{h+1} = \mathcal{A}(\theta_h, \mathcal{D}_h)$ using observed task outcomes $\mathcal{D}_h$. Source: "an agent [that can] rewrite its own harness ... around frozen model weights" with "one-step gains up to ~11 points across three benchmarks" [PAGES 19–20]. **Assumptions:** no ground-truth answer is required; human-diff review is in the loop; sandboxing is enforced. **Singularity/breakdown regime:** the feedback loop is the attack surface — if $\mathcal{D}_h$ is manipulable, the operator $\mathcal{A}$ can drift to reward-hacking or RCE, which the sweep explicitly flags: "self-modifying harnesses carry obvious runaway/RCE risk if the feedback loop is manipulated" [PAGE 20].

**8. No-regret / Stackelberg reference (F8).** For a repeated game with utility $u_t$ and comparator $a^\ast$, $\mathrm{Regret}_T = \max_{a^\ast}\sum_{t=1}^{T} u_t(a^\ast) - \sum_{t=1}^{T} u_t(a_t) = O(\sqrt{T})$ for standard no-regret algorithms; the Stackelberg variant fixes a leader mixed strategy and best-responds in the follower. Source: Kroer's *Games, Markets, and Online Learning* [PAGE 27]. This is the theory under the corpus's game-theory file and the AM W2 correction the sweep references.

**9. Rotation representations (F5).** Unit quaternions $q = w + xi + yj + zk$ with $q\bar q = w^2+x^2+y^2+z^2 = 1$ form a double cover of $SO(3)$; slerp interpolates on the great circle. **Singularity:** Euler-angle gimbal lock is the coordinate singularity that quaternions remove; the residual quaternion singularity is the antipodal identification $q \equiv -q$, which forces a sign convention and can flip an interpolation near $q\cdot q' \to -1$. Source: Van Verth GDC 2012 deck as posted [PAGE 24].

**10. UHP conformance grading (F1).** Task-centric API with sessions, streaming, files, cancellation and a closed error set, graded by a 64-check suite in Core/Extended/Full classes [PAGE 20]. Formal reading: a conformance predicate $C(\text{impl}) \in \{\text{Core}, \text{Extended}, \text{Full}, \bot\}$; **breakdown regime:** the spec is "explicitly still a draft, maintainer-led" with "one independent client so far" [PAGE 20], so $C$ is not yet a stable contract.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage in this sweep is denser than the football framing suggests, and the multi-week traps are mostly *inherited* traps from that lineage.

*CPOE and EPA.* The expected-completion model under CPOE descends from the rbsdm model family and the nflfastR/nflverse stack the sweep names [PAGE 5], which in turn sits on the logistic-regression / binary-classification and calibration literature (Platt scaling, isotonic regression) and on the expected-points tradition initiated by Romer's 2006 fourth-down work and extended by the win-probability literature. The trap that lineage imports is *model-version non-comparability*: a CPOE computed with one expected-completion model is not the same statistic as a CPOE computed with another, which is exactly the sweep's caveat at [PAGE 6]. The Williams "30th of 32 in 2025, 2nd in 2026" [PAGE 5] is therefore a claim about a rank *under a stated model and floor*, not an absolute statement about accuracy.

*Pressure charting.* The pressure family — PFF charting → NGS tracking → vendor-specific "pressure" flags — has a definitional lineage that explains the volatility the sweep observes. When the crown moves from Penix (Week 6) to Lawrence (Week 8) [PAGE 9], the most parsimonious explanation is not that two QBs changed trait in two weeks but that the underlying pressure population and the definition boundary shifted. This is a classic measurement-invariance trap: a metric whose rank ordering is unstable across short windows is a metric whose construct validity is not yet established.

*TRACER.* The 44/34/16/6 split and the 15.7-month median [PAGE 23] are the ML-research analogue of the reproducibility crisis (Open Science Collaboration 2015; Ioannidis 2005) and of the older "half-life of facts" literature. The recursive move — apply TRACER to the sweep's own claims — is the correct one, and it is the same move the corpus already applies with its "UNVERIFIED-until-reproduced" rule [PAGE 28]. The trap it prevents is *citation inertia*: a chart quoted in run $k$ gets re-quoted in run $k+1$ without a retest, and after ~16 months the quote is more likely wrong than right.

*Harness² and UHP.* Self-modifying agents descend from the Gödel-machine / self-referential-optimizer lineage (Schmidhuber) and the broader meta-learning tradition. The historical trap is the one the sweep names: a self-modifying loop without a sandbox and human-diff review is the classic paperclip/RCE failure mode [PAGE 20]. UHP's lineage is the OpenAPI/JSON-Schema standardization tradition — a draft contract that becomes real only when a second independent implementation appears, which the sweep says has not yet happened.

*Library of Layers.* The "router selects the next transformer block from a global library" architecture [PAGE 22] is a direct descendant of mixture-of-depths (Raposo et al.) and mixture-of-experts (Shazeer et al.), with a depth-recurrent special case; the "one hyperparameter slides between dense, depth-recurrent and MoE" framing is the clean statement of that family's unification. The trap is small-scale extrapolation: 360M-parameter results on synthetic tasks are evidence about *mechanism*, not about frontier scaling, and the sweep correctly rates it LEARN-ONLY.

*Rotations.* Shoemake's 1985 quaternion tutorial → Van Verth's GDC 2012 deck → geometric-algebra rotors (Hestenes) is a 40-year lineage that is *mathematically stable* even though the tooling references in the deck are stale [PAGE 24]. That is the rare case in this sweep where the primary source's age is a feature, not a bug.

*No-regret.* Blackwell approachability → Hart–Mas–Colell → CFR for poker (Bowling et al.) → Kroer's textbook [PAGE 27] is the applied-game-theory spine under the corpus's equilibrium work. The trap is assuming no-regret convergence rates transfer from full-information to bandit feedback without adjustment; the textbook's security-games chapters are the correct place to check that.

*Multi-week traps visible in this run.* (a) The *repost double-count* trap: @GridironInfo_ re-issues Week 8 charts as "repost editions" with new status IDs but the same values [PAGE 17]; any downstream scraper that counts IDs rather than values will inflate the week. (b) The *stale-batch* trap: @KoalatyStats returned seven already-seen posts and one new one, and @StatsHubCom returned zero usable NFL posts [PAGE 18]; a naive "watchlist freshness" assumption fails here. (c) The *convergence-illusion* trap: JSN's four instruments share charting and nflverse ancestry, so the "four-way stack" is less independent than it reads. (d) The *correlated-conjunction* trap: W4's JAX/SEA claim is underpowered at $N=32$ unless the SR–XR correlation is modeled [PAGE 9].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under posting-time pressure, each account reverts to its oldest reliable format. @sfdata9ers reverts to the DPI leaderboard and the single-week fault-split [PAGE 3]; @boxscorescout reverts to the ADP-value top-10 list and the year-over-year rank card [PAGE 5]; @GridironInfo_ reverts to the repost edition of the week's charts [PAGE 17]; @KoalatyStats reverts to the stale batch [PAGE 18]. The regression is *informative*: an account that posts a repost edition is telling you it has no new measurement tonight, which is a different signal than an account that posts a fresh instrument. The Foundry should read the format, not just the content — the format is the tell. The one account that did *not* regress is @HaydenWinks, whose Week 8 Film Room article was read end-to-end and pairs every film claim with a checkable number [PAGES 14–16]; that is the counter-example the others are measured against.

**Counter-trap.** The sweep pre-registers its own verification queue in the Synthesis: "(1) W4 post 3 conjunction check ... (2) W2 Williams CPOE flip + Smith ranks ... (3) W7 Odunze explosive-TD drought screen ... (4) W8 Maye 141.5 box-score check ... (5) W1 DPI team totals ... (6) F1 Harness² demo ... (7) F6/F7 one-fetch verifications" [PAGE 28]. That list is a counter-trap against the sweep's own confirmation bias: it converts "CITED, UNVERIFIED" from a disclaimer into a to-do. The recursive move is to apply the same discipline to the *counter-trap itself* — the test order is a hypothesis about which claims are cheapest to falsify, and it could be wrong (the JAX/SEA conjunction may be underpowered even if it reproduces, per the Poisson null above). A stronger counter-trap states, for each test, what result would *veto* the claim, not just what result would confirm it.

**Clock.** The slot is 20:08 CST, the browser ran 20:09–20:37, the article read 20:41–20:43 [PAGE 1]. The internal clocks are the post timestamps (Nov 3 22:18–Wed Nov 4 00:09 GMT [PAGE 3]) and the external deadline (Thursday night Jacksonville at Baltimore, the Fagnano start [PAGE 2]). The clock creates three asymmetries: (i) *freshness asymmetry* — a post published at 00:09 GMT is fresher than the sweep that reads it at 20:37 CST, so the sweep is always slightly behind; (ii) *deadline asymmetry* — the Baltimore health news is decision-relevant for a game ~72 hours out, which is why it is the only health item with an explicit countdown; (iii) *repost-cycle asymmetry* — the evening repost batch [PAGE 17] is a deliberate second engagement cycle on identical assets, which means "new status ID" ≠ "new information" and the sweep must dedupe on values, not IDs.

**Recursive layer.** The sweep models the posters; the Foundry models the sweep. The recursion is not decorative — it is the reason the "CITED, UNVERIFIED" tag must survive into the published reasoning. If the published output quotes a chart value as fact, the recursion collapses and the Foundry inherits the posters' errors. The correct posture is: model each poster's incentive (engagement cycle, stale batch, headline grab), model the sweep's sensor gap (image-locked values), and model the Foundry's own temptation (to write a confident sentence from an unread chart). The three-model stack is the only way the trace stays honest.
