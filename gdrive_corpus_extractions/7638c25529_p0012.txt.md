(Trap 3) Assuming that because messages between man and machine, machine and man, and machine and machine are "destined to play an ever-increasing part" [PAGE 9], they are therefore stabilizing; feedback loops with delay and high gain can oscillate, so proliferation of messages does not imply improved control.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under pressure, human agents tend to regress from kitten-like adaptive feedback to music-box open-loop scripts: fixed action patterns selected in advance and executed without re-sampling the environment. Wiener's music-box figures "dance in accordance with a pattern... set in advance" and cannot vary from it [PAGE 9]; a crisis habit is the behavioral equivalent—pre-loaded, insensitive to new messages, and blind to whether the recipient's state has changed. Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to assume that every emitted signal is a message that changes behavior. Wiener explicitly denies this for conventional messages: their meaning is "disproportionately small compared with the length of the message" [PAGE 9], and originality voids the cheap-rate protocol [PAGE 9]. Thus a high-volume signal stream may carry near-zero Wiener-meaning if p(m)\approx1 for all transmitted symbols. Clock. The thermostat is a homeostatic clock: it samples room temperature and issues a corrective message to the oil burner [PAGE 9]. If the loop contains a time delay \tau and gain K such that K\cdot\text{plant}(j\omega) exceeds unity at phase -180^\circ, the closed loop hunts; Wiener's qualitative definition supplies no stability margin, so the clock can become an oscillator rather than a regulator.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto. A button press transmits a message only if the circuit conducts; broken wiring, relay failure, or absent power vetoes the message before it reaches the apparatus. Biomechanical veto. The human pressing the button is bounded by motor latency, force, and range of motion; if the user is physically unable to actuate the switch, no message is generated. Weather/environmental veto. Wiener's thermostat controls the oil burner based on room temperature [PAGE 9]; solar loading, drafts, or sensor drift bias the measurement, causing the thermostat to issue an "off" message while the room is actually cold. Veto proof. By Wiener's own definition, control requires that messages "effectively change the behavior of the recipient" [PAGE 9]. If the actuator lacks authority, the sensor chain is vetoed by environmental noise, or the recipient state is invariant to the input, the transmission is noise, not control, regardless of intent.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
