<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
To formally analyze the Sports Intelligence OS, we define two primary operators governing the system's compliance and epistemic integrity.

**Operator 1: The Compliance Readiness Function ($\mathcal{R}$)**
For any content artifact $A$ (where $A \in \{\text{Promotion, Brief, Pick}\}$), let $\phi_i(A)$ be a binary predicate representing the presence of a mandatory compliance attribute (e.g., Responsible Gaming text, Affiliate Disclosure, Terms URL, Performance Gate Open).
Let $\Omega$ be the set of banned phrases defined in `trust-claims.ts`.
The readiness score $R(A) \in [0,1]$ is defined as:
$$ R(A) = \left( \prod_{i=1}^{n} \phi_i(A) \right) \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{scan}(A) \cap \Omega = \emptyset) $$
where $\mathbb{I}$ is the indicator function. 
*Assumptions:* All predicates $\phi_i$ are independently verifiable via the `guards.ts` and `compliance.ts` services. The scan function operates over word boundaries (e.g., distinguishing "lock" from "unlock").
*Breakdown Regime:* If any $\phi_i(A) = 0$ or a banned phrase is detected, $R(A) = 0$, triggering a `BLOCKED` or `NEEDS_REVIEW` state in the Prisma schema. This enforces the "zero-trust" policy observed in Phase 2 and Phase 8.

**Operator 2: Epistemic Information Gain ($\Delta I_E$)**
During the Frontier recovery, the architecture shifted to `IntelligenceEvent` -> `PickEvidenceEnvelope`. We quantify the value of an incoming event $E_t$ at time $t$ relative to the betting market's prior distribution $P_{prior}$ (opening line) and the system's current belief $P_{curr}$.
Using Kullback-Leibler divergence as the metric for informational surprise:
$$ \Delta I_E(E_t) = D_{KL}(P_{curr} \| P_{prior}) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P_{curr}(x) \ln \left( \frac{P_{curr}(x)}{P_{prior}(x)} \right) $$
*Units:* Nats (or bits if log base 2 is used).
*Assumptions:* $P_{prior}$ and $P_{curr}$ are normalized probability distributions over game outcomes derived from the odds ingestion pipeline (`packages/data-ingestion`).
*Singularity/Breakdown Regime:* If $P_{prior}(x) = 0$ for any $x$ where $P_{curr}(x) > 0$ (i.e., the market suddenly prices in an outcome it previously deemed impossible, such as a sudden cancellation or massive lineup change), the divergence approaches infinity. In the system, this manifests as a `contradictory_source` flag in `source-intelligence/index.ts`, forcing a `REVIEW` verdict to prevent the model from hallucinating validity on highly volatile signals.

**2. Weather Veto (The Delay Nullification):**
*Scenario:* A baseball game scheduled for 1 PM is delayed indefinitely due to rain.

If `gameStatus === DELAYED`, any associated `Pick` or `BriefItem` is marked as `VOID` or `STALE`. The `compose.ts` logic for the Daily Brief will detect this and render a "Data Quality Summary" warning instead of listing the matchup as playable. This prevents the "illusion of opportunity" where a user thinks they can bet on a game that isn't happening.

**3. Regulatory Veto (The State-Eligibility Block):**

It compares the user's inferred jurisdiction (via IP or account settings) against the `eligibleStates` JSON array in the `Promotion` model. If there is no intersection, the promotion is stripped from the payload entirely. This is a hard veto: the frontend simply never receives the HTML for that offer, making accidental compliance breaches impossible at the UI layer.

**Proof of Integrity:** The `route-smoke.test.ts` and `content-engine.test.ts` suites `[PAGE 23]` explicitly test these veto paths.
