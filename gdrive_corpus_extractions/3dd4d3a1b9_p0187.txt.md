To formalize Tolman’s distinction and the conditions required for the emergence of a means-end-field, we employ concepts from Information Theory and Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).

**1. The Mnemonization Operator (Stimulus-Response Mapping)**
Mnemonization can be modeled as a direct mapping from specific perceptual states to action probabilities, devoid of underlying causal structure. Let $S$ be the state space and $A$ be the action space. A mnemonized policy $\pi_{mnemo}$ minimizes the expected cumulative discounted reward:
$$ J(\pi_{mnemo}) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_{mnemo}} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t R(s_t, a_t) \right] $$
where $\tau$ represents a trajectory generated by following the policy. Crucially, in pure mnemonization, there is no explicit estimate of the transition function $P(s'|s,a)$; learning relies entirely on the co-occurrence statistics of $(s,a)$ pairs leading to rewards.

**2. The Means-End-Field Operator (Cognitive Mapping)**
Tolman’s means-end field implies an internal representation of the environment's connectivity. We define this as a learned transition model $\hat{P}(s'|s,a)$ and a value function derived from planning over this model. The agent constructs a graph $G=(V,E)$ where nodes represent cognitive states and edges represent valid transitions weighted by costs and goal proximity.
The capacity to form a means-end field requires minimizing the uncertainty of the global state space. We invoke the **Mutual Information** between the current state $s$ and the distal goal $g$, conditioned on the entire explored history $H$:
$$ I(s; g | H) = \int p(s, g | H) \log \frac{p(s | g, H)}{p(s | H)} ds dg $$
High mutual information indicates that the current state provides strong predictive power regarding the goal, mediated by a richly explored history.

**3. The Distribution Training Threshold (Hsiao's Condition)**
The text posits that "widely distributed preliminary training" is necessary to verify the grasping of commonality. We formalize this using **Coverage Entropy**. Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the dataset of visited states. The coverage entropy $H_C$ measures the uniformity of exploration across the reachable state space $S_R$:
$$ H_C = - \sum_{s \in S_R} P_{\mathcal{D}}(s) \log P_{\mathcal{D}}(s) $$
For a means-end field to emerge, the training regime must ensure $H_C$ exceeds a critical threshold $H_{crit}$. If exploration is sparse (low $H_C$), the agent remains trapped in the mnemonization basin of attraction. Thus, the condition for cognitive mapping is:
$$ \text{If } H_C < H_{crit} \implies \text{Behavior} \approx \pi_{mnemo} $$
$$ \text{If } H_C \geq H_{crit} \implies \text{Behavior} \approx \pi_{map} $$
