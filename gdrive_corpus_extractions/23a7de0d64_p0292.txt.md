<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s formulation emerges directly from the historical battle against reductionist physiology. William James and Carl Lange had posited that emotion *is* the perception of bodily changes (visceral feedback). Cannon famously refuted this, showing via denervation experiments that decapitated frogs still displayed rage postures, and that adrenaline injections alone didn't produce distinct emotions. Cannon argued for a central thalamic trigger.

Tolman takes the next logical step: if neither peripheral feedback nor thalamic firing uniquely identifies an emotion, then the identity of the emotion must reside in what it *does*—its teleological role. This is the birth of *purposeful behaviorism*.

**Historical Lineage to Frontier Reasoning:**
1.  **Teleology to Information:** Tolman's "expectation" regarding the "lie" of quiescences anticipates modern predictive coding and information geometry. The agent maintains a generative model of where satisfactions are located.
2.  **Retrocausal Trap Mechanism:** The multi-week trap arises from *stale expectations*. If an agent successfully satisfies a demand using a specific directional vector in Environment A, and then moves to Environment B where the topology of "quiescences" has shifted, the agent will persist in applying $\vec{v}_{E}^{(A)}$. Because the demand is "immanent," the agent feels the compulsion strongly, but the direction is now geometrically wrong. The trap is sustained by the very strength of the original successful learning.
3.  **Genealogical Insight:** The error of defining emotion by "sensations" (James/Lange) leads to systems vulnerable to physiological spoofing (e.g., using caffeine to fake alertness). Defining it by "direction" (Tolman) makes the system robust to physiological noise but highly sensitive to topological errors in the cognitive map. The "retrocausal" aspect is that the future goal (quiescence) determines the validity of the present sensorimotor filter.

In a reasoning context, this explains why agents (human or AI) often get stuck in local optima: they are faithfully executing an emotional-directional program derived from past success, failing to recognize that the "lie" of the current problem landscape requires a fundamentally different vector orientation.
