Layer 2 (biomechanical): if the commanded stroke rate violates \(T_{\text{stroke}} \ge \tau_{\text{catch}}+\tau_{\text{recover}}+\tau_{\text{safety}}\), blades clash and the shell loses way; at \(r=35\) min\(^{-1}\), \(T_{\text{stroke}}=1.71\) s, leaving a narrow recovery budget. Layer 3 (structural): cranky shells have small metacentric height \(GM\); if roll heel \(\theta\) exceeds the angle of vanishing stability, the shell capsizes or ships water irrespective of crew skill. Layer 4 (procedural): \(R\)’s “Are you ready!” gate [PAGE 77] can delay any crew not settled at the line, and “exposed points” on the course [PAGE 77] act as spatial vetoes where local wave refraction may exceed freeboard even if the harbor is calm.

Adjudication: the weather veto is absolute and temporal; if it had not lifted at noon, no race occurs. Once lifted, the biomechanical veto binds during the race, capping sustainable rate. The structural veto is state-dependent and catastrophic. The procedural veto is minor but can shift start time by seconds, which matters because the calm window is finite. No lower-layer veto can override an upper-layer safety veto; hence the referee’s decision to wait for drooping flags [PAGE 77] is the controlling constraint.
