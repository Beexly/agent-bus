$$ P_{ij} = \frac{\exp\left( \beta \cdot (\vec{u}_{ij} \cdot \vec{d}_{goal}) \right)}{\sum_{k} \exp\left( \beta \cdot (\vec{u}_{ik} \cdot \vec{d}_{goal}) \right)} $$

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 framework represents a critical juncture in the genealogy of cognitive science, breaking away from the strict behaviorist dogma of Watson and early Hullian algebraic systems. The lineage traces back to Köhler’s insights on chimpanzee insight learning (1917) and Wertheimer’s Gestalt principles, which posited that perception and action are organized wholes rather than aggregates of elements. Tolman operationalized these abstract concepts through rigorous rodent maze studies. Historically, this created a "multi-week trap" for subsequent researchers: the pendulum swung so far toward mentalism and "cognitive maps" (Tolman & Honzik, 1930) that it nearly completely ignored the biomechanical and physiological constraints of the animal—a trap later corrected by the rise of neurophysiology and ethology (Lorenz, Tinbergen). Furthermore, the retrocausal influence is evident today in Reinforcement Learning (RL); modern RL agents utilizing value functions ($Q$-learning) essentially compute the very "mnemonic expectations" Tolman described phenomenologically, proving his forward-looking hypothesis about purposive systems decades before computational implementation.
