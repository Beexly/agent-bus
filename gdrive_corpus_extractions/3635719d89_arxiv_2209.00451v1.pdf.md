Biomechanical veto: ball possession is not inferred from a single coordinate but from a conjunctive biomechanical rule: the player must be closest to the ball for at least 5 consecutive frames, the ball within 5 ft, below 10 ft height, and ball speed under 25 ft/s [PAGE 11]. Verifiable condition: Table 6 shows H = 40 yields weak-label-test F1 p&r 0.831 / handoff 0.719 versus H = 20 p&r 0.856 / handoff 0.768 [PAGE 11]. Prediction: on the manual-test set, H = 40 will reduce pick-and-roll F1 by at least 0.025 and handoff F1 by at least 0.040 relative to the H = 20 baseline, because the longer horizon injects post-label kinematics that blur the tactical decision boundary.

Veto Scenario 2 — Pooling removal under input permutation. Counterfactual: deploy the no-team-wise-pooling ablation (Figure 7) and evaluate on play sequences where attacker input indices are permuted (e.g., swap A2 and A3 roles). Verifiable condition: without pooling, the model forms C(5,2) = 10 clusters keyed to offensive-player input positions rather than relational structure [PAGE 8–9]. Prediction: permuting player order will cause the no-pooling model’s pick-and-roll F1 to drop by more than 15 percentage points, whereas the pooled NETS remains invariant (F1 change < 2 points), because pooling is the only operator enforcing permutation symmetry within each team [PAGE 4 Eq. (4)].

Veto Scenario 3 — Manual-label-only training without weak-label pretraining. Counterfactual: train NETS from scratch (random initialization, no trajectory pretraining, no weak-label fine-tuning) on only the 900 manual labels (450 per class train). Verifiable condition: Table 5 row 3 shows manual-only fine-tuning after pretraining still underperforms weak-label rules (p&r 0.784 vs 0.869) [PAGE 7]; Table 5 row 1 shows weak-label specificity ceiling is ~0.87–0.89 [PAGE 7]. Prediction: scratch training on 900 manual labels will yield pick-and-roll F1 below 0.80 and handoff F1 below 0.75, failing to beat the rule-based weak-label baseline and confirming that 900 manual labels lie below the sample complexity of the Transformer without the weak-label pretraining scaffold.
