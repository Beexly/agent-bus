$$\frac{dH_{oral}}{dt} = -\lambda H_{oral} + \eta \, \mathbb{I}[S_{oral} \text{ paired with } S_R], \quad \lambda \ll \lambda_{naive}$$
because of lifelong pairing [PAGE 108].

Breakdown / singularity regimes:
1. Surgical confound singularity: if fistula leakage or infection raises baseline drive $\Delta S_D^{base} > \theta_{stress}$, the observed preference shift is vetoed.
2. Preference non-exchangeability: if $P(\text{food}_A) - P(\text{food}_B) \neq 0$ pre-experiment [PAGE 108], the choice statistic is confounded.
3. Extinction threshold singularity: if trials to extinction $N_{ext} < N_{crit}$, the claim that secondary power “ought not to be lost” [PAGE 108] is falsified.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage. Hull (1943) situates secondary reinforcement inside a drive-reduction system descending from Thorndike’s law of effect and Watsonian behaviorism, while importing Pavlov’s contention that reinforcement is “primarily a phenomenon of stimulation” [PAGE 108]. The Pavlovian link is causally retroactive: Hull reads Pavlov backwards through his own $S_D \rightarrow S_p$ formalism, treating stimulation onset as the common currency of primary and secondary cases. This genealogy continues forward into Rescorla-Wagner (1972), where the secondary stimulus acquires a learned expectation $V$ via a delta rule $\delta = r - V$, and into dopaminergic reward-prediction-error models (Schultz et al., 1997), where phasic dopamine signals the discrepancy between predicted and actual nutritional outcomes.

Multi-week traps. In longitudinal applications, secondary reinforcers (token economies, points, conditioned cues) face three traps. First, backup-devaluation: if the primary reinforcer backing the secondary cue is degraded, the secondary cue loses control after a lag of weeks. Second, extinction burst followed by spontaneous recovery: responding to the sham food may initially surge before collapsing, then reappear after weeks in the original context (renewal). Third, context-dependency: a preference for nutritive food established in the laboratory may not transfer to a novel environment, violating Hull’s assumption of a “uniform and practically invariable sequence” [PAGE 108] across contexts.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the dog as an agent maintaining a belief over contingencies, and the experimenter as a second-order agent modeling the dog.

Crisis habit regression. Under elevated drive (food restriction), the animal regresses from secondary to primary reinforcement control. When $S_D$ crosses a crisis threshold $S_D^{crisis}$, the secondary value $V(S_{oral})$ is downweighted and the animal prioritizes immediate oral stimulation or escape from the fistula apparatus. Hull’s prediction that the dog “might even refuse to eat the sham food” [PAGE 108] assumes the animal has already attributed primary reinforcement to the nutritive food; under crisis, regression to sham-food consumption could occur if the nutritive food is delayed or effortful.

Counter-trap. The experimenter is trapped by the hypothesis: if the dog prefers the sham food, the experimenter may attribute this to “experimental extinction” not having proceeded far enough, or to residual post-ingestive leakage through the fistula, rather than rejecting secondary reinforcement. The counter-trap is that the surgery itself changes the animal’s model of the experimenter: the dog learns that food sometimes disappears into a receptacle, violating the lifelong contiguity assumption and causing a contingency-reasoning collapse.

Clock. The temporal dynamics are governed by competing clocks. Acquisition of secondary reinforcement operates on a lifespan timescale ($\tau_{life} \sim$ years), whereas extinction under sham feeding operates on an experimental timescale ($\tau_{exp} \sim$ sessions) [PAGE 108]. The differential equation
$$\frac{dH}{dt} = \alpha \, \mathbb{I}[contiguity] - \beta \, \mathbb{I}[extinction]$$
has different time constants; Hull’s claim that secondary power “ought not to be lost” [PAGE 108] assumes $\beta \tau_{exp} \ll 1$, an assumption unverified without a specified clock.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three veto layers must be simultaneously satisfied; failure in any layer invalidates the preference inference.

Physical layer. The esophageal severance and cervical fistulas [PAGE 108] impose a physical state constraint. If the lower fistula permits aspiration, peritonitis, or nutritional leakage, the dog’s homeostatic state $S_D$ is perturbed independently of oral stimulation. Veto condition: core temperature $T_{core} < 37.5^\circ\text{C}$ or leukocyte count elevated for $>48$ h post-surgery. Under this veto, any observed food preference reflects illness-induced anhedonia, not reinforcement structure.

Biomechanical layer. Mastication requires intact dentition, salivary flow, and temporomandibular coordination. Hull assumes receptor discharges are in a “uniform and practically invariable sequence” [PAGE 108] across foods, but kibble texture, moisture content, and particle size alter masticatory burst patterns. Veto condition: if electromyographic masticatory burst duration differs by $>20\%$ between the sham and nutritive foods, the orosensory stimulus $S_{oral}$ is not equated, and the secondary reinforcement comparison is confounded.

Weather/environmental layer. Canine metabolic drive is temperature-dependent. Ambient temperature $T_{amb}$ shifts basal metabolic rate and thus the salience of nutritional need reduction. Veto condition: if $\Delta T_{amb} > 5^\circ\text{C}$ between acquisition and test sessions, the drive signal $S_D$ drifts, violating the stationarity assumption required to attribute choice to reinforcement type rather than thermoregulatory motivation.

Adjudication rule. The hypothesis “eating of food as such brings about learning through secondary reinforcement rather than through primary reinforcement” [PAGE 108] is provisionally upheld only if the dog prefers nutritive food while all three layers remain within veto bounds. If any layer breaches, the inference is vetoed and the data are assigned to the confound layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios for a reinforcement-learning model of the dog’s choice, with predictions.

Scenario 1 (Zero-nutrient counterfactual). Intervention: the dog receives full orosensory stimulation from Food A but zero post-ingestive nutrients (complete sham), while Food B delivers full nutrition. Counterfactual: if after $N=60$ paired trials the dog does not show a statistically reliable preference for Food B ($p > 0.05$, two-tailed), the secondary-reinforcement-only account is vetoed. Prediction: under the Hullian account, choice proportion for Food B should approach $P(B) \ge 0.80$ by session 60; under the veto, $P(B) \approx 0.50 \pm 0.05$, indicating that oral stimulation alone is insufficient and primary reinforcement is necessary.

Scenario 2 (Pre-existing preference confound). Intervention: pre-surgery baseline shows the dog prefers Food A at $P(A)=0.75$. Counterfactual: if, after sham feeding A and real feeding B, the dog continues to prefer A at $P(A) \approx 0.75 \pm 0.05$, the nutritive-preference prediction is vetoed. Prediction: Hull’s hypothesis requires a reversal toward B; the veto predicts persistence of baseline preference, confirming Hull’s own caveat that controls for pre-existing preferences are essential [PAGE 108] and that without them the effect is not identifiable.

Scenario 3 (Incomplete-sham leakage). Intervention: the esophageal fistula allows slow, unintended nutrient trickle into the stomach during “sham” feeding. Counterfactual: if the dog shows no extinction of responding to Food A and maintains $P(A) \approx 0.50$, the claim that sham food should suffer experimental extinction [PAGE 108] is vetoed. Prediction: under the intact-sham model, $H_A(t) = H_0 e^{-t/\tau}$ with $\tau \approx 8$ sessions; under leakage, $I(S_{oral}; S_R)$ remains high and $H_A(t)$ remains near asymptote, so no extinction occurs and the secondary reinforcement confound cannot be isolated.

All three scenarios are verifiable via session-by-session choice logs, video-scored masticatory bursts, and post-mortem fistula patency checks.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
