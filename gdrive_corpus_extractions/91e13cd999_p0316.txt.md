Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions:

Scenario A — Forced synchrony.
Intervention: Impose a common oscillatory drive on both competing tendencies (e.g., rhythmic sensory entrainment or common transcranial stimulation) such that ξ₁(t) = ξ₂(t) for all t, enforcing perfect correlation ρ = 1.
Prediction: Under [PAGE 309] synchrony logic, the tendency with higher baseline H wins 100% of trials; the weaker tendency's occurrence rate is exactly zero.
Verifiable veto: If the weaker tendency is observed on any nonzero fraction of trials under verified common drive, the synchrony assumption is false or unmeasured noise sources break the correlation; the molar dominance model is vetoed.

Scenario B — Variance suppression.
Intervention: Reduce submolar fluctuation amplitude Γ_eff (e.g., pharmacological suppression of stochastic ion-channel noise or genetic stabilization of axonal thresholds) while holding H₁ − H₂ constant and small.
Prediction: The gradual S-shaped increase in relative frequency described on [PAGE 309] steepens into an abrupt step function; the system transitions from irregular alternation to winner-take-all at a critical ΔH.
Verifiable veto: If a smooth gradual transition persists after Γ_eff is independently verified to have shrunk, then neural threshold variance is not the dominant submolar cause, and Hull's physiological reduction is incomplete.

Scenario C — Null habit difference with asynchrony.
Intervention: Equate baseline habit strengths H₁ = H₂ (matched training) and confirm asynchronous fluctuations ρ < 1.
Prediction: Over a large number of trials, the relative frequencies of the two reactions converge to 0.5 each, with irregular run lengths rather than strict alternation, consistent with [PAGE 309].
Verifiable veto: If one reaction exceeds 55% of occurrences over, say, 200+ trials under verified H equality and asynchrony, then either a hidden ΔH ≠ 0 exists or a synchronous artifact is present; the null-habit assumption is falsified.
