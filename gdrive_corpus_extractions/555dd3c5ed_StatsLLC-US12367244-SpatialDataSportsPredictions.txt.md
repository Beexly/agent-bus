<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(r_P\in\mathbb{R}^2\) or \(\mathbb{R}^3\) be the first player’s position in meters, \(r_D\) the second or defending player’s position in meters, and \(r_T\) the target-area position in meters. The virtual line is
\[
L(t)=(1-t)r_P+t r_T,\qquad t\in[0,1],
\]
with length \(\|r_T-r_P\|\) in meters. A useful defender-to-line distance is
\[
d_{D L}=\min_{t\in[0,1]}\|r_D-L(t)\|\quad\text{(m)}.
\]
The angle between the shooter-to-target vector and the shooter-to-defender vector is
\[
\theta_D=\arccos\!\left(
\frac{(r_T-r_P)\cdot(r_D-r_P)}
{\|r_T-r_P\|\,\|r_D-r_P\|}
\right)\quad\text{(rad)}.
\]
The patent describes this geometry and says the virtual line “may be utilized to calculate angles of shots on goal relative to a goalkeeper's position” [PAGE 28]. The clarity feature is a count:
\[
\kappa=\#\{j:\operatorname{dist}(r_j,L)\le\delta\},
\]
where the specification gives \(\delta\in\{1,3,5,10\}\) feet [PAGE 28], i.e. approximately \(0.3048,0.9144,1.524,3.048\) m. The initial and updated probabilities are
\[
p_0=f_\theta(x_0),\qquad p_1=f_\theta(x_0,F),
\]
with \(p_0,p_1\in[0,1]\) dimensionless. The specification states that the output “can include a continuous value between 0-1 ×G for all open-play and direct free kick shots” [PAGE 27]. For a target-plane shot location \(s_k\), the goalkeeper save permutation can be written as
\[
p_{\mathrm{save}}(s_k\mid r_G,L)=h_\psi(s_k,r_G,d_{G L},\theta_{G L},\kappa),
\]
which matches the patent’s language of a “goalkeeper save probability permutation modifying a goalkeeper save probability for each of a set of shot locations at different angles from the virtual line” [PAGE 38]. The expected-goals and expected-goals-on-target quantities are distinct probability functionals:
\[
xG=P(\text{goal}\mid x_0,F),\qquad xGoT=P(\text{goal}\mid \text{shot on target},\text{placement},\text{trajectory}).
\]
The patent says xGoT “can model the likelihood of a shot on target being a goal (or being saved) by including information about shot placement and trajectory” [PAGE 27].

For a monotonic constraint on feature \(j\), let \(\sigma_j\in\{+1,-1\}\) encode the permitted direction. A formal almost-everywhere constraint is
\[
\sigma_j\,\frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial x_j}\ge 0
\quad\text{or, equivalently,}\quad
\frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial x_j}\ge 0 \text{ when } \sigma_j=+1.
\]
The patent does not enumerate the signs, so this is an assumption to be verified, not a disclosed fact. Calibration bias over \(N\) shots is
\[
B_{1000}=\frac{1000}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(y_i-\hat p_i\right)
\quad\text{(goals per 1000 shots)},
\]
and log loss is
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\log}
=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left[
y_i\ln \hat p_i+(1-y_i)\ln(1-\hat p_i)
\right]
\quad\text{(nats per shot)}.
\]
AUC-ROC is dimensionless. The specification reports an example where “log loss=0.267 and a bias=4.9 goals above expected” [PAGE 31]. SHAP explanations can be written additively in a link space:
\[
f(x)=\phi_0+\sum_j \phi_j,
\]
where the \(\phi_j\) are feature attributions; the patent references a “Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) plot” for individual shot explanation [PAGE 31].

Breakdown regimes are exact and must be declared. First, if \(r_P\to r_T\), then \(\|r_T-r_P\|\to 0\), so \(\theta_D\) is undefined and \(L(t)\) has zero length; the virtual-line geometry is singular. Second, if \(\hat p_i=0\) or \(\hat p_i=1\), log loss diverges; practical implementations must clip to \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\). Third, if \(\delta\) is too small, \(\kappa=0\) almost always; if \(\delta\) is too large, \(\kappa\) saturates and loses discriminative power. Fourth, GAN training can suffer mode collapse or non-identifiability, and monotonic constraints can conflict with calibration; the patent’s high-dimensional warning about “leakage or overfitting” [PAGE 26] is a direct breakdown condition. Fifth, goalkeeper-out-of-goal states are low-support edge cases; the model may extrapolate a save probability rather than interpolate it. Sixth, if weather, surface, and 3D pose are omitted, the probability model is not physically closed and can violate kinematic reachability even when aggregate calibration is acceptable. The patent lists “temperature and wind information; time of day” and “three dimensional body-pose information of players” as possible features [PAGE 27], but it does not give a full weather-constrained trajectory model.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive model is a best-response loop. The shooter chooses placement and shot type while modeling the goalkeeper’s likely position; the goalkeeper chooses position while modeling the shooter’s likely placement; the analytics model estimates both counterfactuals. The patent’s save permutation is precisely a counterfactual over shot locations: for each candidate location \(s_k\), the model asks what save probability would obtain given the goalkeeper’s position relative to the virtual line [PAGE 29]. This is recursive because the shooter’s action depends on anticipated goalkeeper behavior, and the goalkeeper’s behavior depends on anticipated shooter behavior.

There are three failure modes. First, crisis habit regression: under a low-support outlier, the model reverts to its simple habitual features, such as shooter position and target position, and neglects the robust feature set. The specification names this exact weakness: traditional systems may generate “a noisy output as the scenario may occur infrequently” [PAGE 26]. In recursive terms, the model stops modeling the defender’s counterfactual and collapses to a base xG. Second, counter-trap: the permutation feature can generate a plausible but non-physical counterfactual. A goalkeeper may be modeled as saving a shot that no human could reach given ball speed, reaction time, and body pose. The counter-trap is that the model appears more sophisticated because it uses a high-dimensional feature, yet it violates the physical layer. Third, clock: the model must update on an in-game clock and a retraining clock. The patent says a tennis prediction “may update with each hit in a rally” [PAGE 34], and it describes retraining with additional edge-case data [PAGE 27]. A model that is recursively accurate but temporally stale is not recursively trustworthy.

The adjudication rule is: recursive probability is admissible only when it is consistent with the physical veto layer and with the model’s own calibration history. If the shooter’s anticipated placement and the goalkeeper’s anticipated position produce a probability that violates reachability, the counterfactual is rejected. If the model’s edge-case output is unstable across retraining windows, the clock veto applies. If the model ignores the defender’s counterfactual and reduces to a base rate, the crisis-habit veto applies.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer has priority over the statistical layer. Let \(r_B(t)=r_B(0)+v_B t+\frac12 a_B t^2\) be the ball trajectory in meters, with \(v_B\) in m/s and \(a_B\) in m/s\(^2\). A goal is physically possible only if there exists \(t>0\) such that \(r_B(t)\) lies inside the goal frame \(G\). If no such \(t\) exists, then
\[
P(\text{goal}\mid x_0,F)=0.
\]
A save is physically possible only if the goalkeeper can move from \(r_G(0)\) to the required intercept in time. Let
\[
t_{\mathrm{avail}}=\min\{t>0:r_B(t)\in G\},
\qquad
\Delta r_G=\|r_G(t_{\mathrm{avail}})-r_G(0)\|.
\]
With maximum goalkeeper speed \(v_{G,\max}\) and acceleration \(a_{G,\max}\), a conservative reachable displacement is
\[
R_{\max}=v_{G,\max}t_{\mathrm{avail}}+\frac12 a_{G,\max}t_{\mathrm{avail}}^2.
\]
If \(\Delta r_G>R_{\max}\), then
\[
P(\text{save}\mid x_0,F)\le \epsilon.
\]
This is a kinematic veto, not a style preference. The patent’s feature list includes “three dimensional body-pose information of players” and “velocity and acceleration of players” [PAGE 27], which are exactly the variables needed to enforce this layer. The biomechanical veto is stronger still: if the 3D pose and limb-length constraints make the intercept impossible, the probability must be zero regardless of the learned model. The weather veto applies when wind vector \(w\) or surface wetness changes the trajectory enough that the model’s residual is outside its calibrated interval; the patent lists “temperature and wind information” and “court surfaces” as possible inputs [PAGE 27], but if the deployed model omits them, the statistical output cannot be adjudicated as physically valid. The adjudication order is therefore: trajectory intersection first, kinematic reach second, biomechanical pose third, weather and surface residual fourth, and only then the learned probability. If any physical predicate fails, the learned probability is vetoed or clipped; if all pass, the learned probability is admissible subject to calibration.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
