The etymological root of "brand" traces to Old English "brand/brond," meaning fire, flame, destruction by fire, burning wood, or torch (Harper, 2022) [PAGE 2]. Historically, livestock stamps distinguished one herd from another, evolving into modern product and service differentiation [PAGE 2]. With industrialization and packaged goods, manufacturers adopted symbols to express identity and distinguish outputs [PAGE 2]. Roffer (2000/2002) emphasized personal brand strategy as a combination of talents, career goals, and image [PAGE 3, 12]. Purkiss and Royston-Lee (2014) defined personal brand as the thoughts and comments others hold about a person in that person's absence, encompassing value, consistency, trust, emotion, sincerity, and expectations [PAGE 3].

The athletic lineage begins with Mark McCormack, who founded the International Management Group in 1960 and recognized the commercial potential of golf players, thereby placing the personal athlete brand on the industry agenda (cited via Carter, 2010) [PAGE 4]. The counter-trap is therefore an agency inversion: the paper treats a Google-curated memorial as if it were an athlete-controlled branding channel. If a living athlete attempted to replicate the sample's pattern—securing a Doodle on their birthday—they would discover that Doodle initiation rests with Google's editorial and brand-awareness objectives, not with athlete self-positioning [PAGE 9–10].

The clock variable is the calendrical trigger: publication dates align with birth or death anniversaries (e.g., Sarah Durack on 12/07/2017, born 1889; Stephen Keshi on 23/01/2018, born 1962) [PAGE 6–8]. When the clock runs out—when no further milestone anniversary remains—the Doodle-based visibility event does not recur unless Google elects a new commemorative cycle. This makes GDoodle visibility episodic and calendrically gated rather than continuous, which limits its utility as an ongoing personal-branding instrument for living athletes who require sustained audience engagement.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Five veto layers gate entry into the athlete GDoodle sample \(\mathcal{S}\). Each layer can exclude a candidate athlete Doodle \(d\); admission requires passage through all layers.

Layer 1 — Archival/access veto: \(d\) must exist in the official Google Doodles Archive and fall within 01/01/2017–31/12/2021 [PAGE 5–6]. Any Doodle outside this window, or not archived, is vetoed. The "saturation" rationale for the window is unverified [PAGE 6], so this layer's boundary condition is an untested assumption.

Layer 2 — Vital-status veto: \(d\) must center a deceased athlete [PAGE 8]. All 33 observed subjects satisfy \(V(d)=1\); living athletes are categorically excluded from the sample, whether by Google's editorial policy, rights clearance, or commemorative norm. This veto fundamentally determines the sample's commemorative character.

Layer 3 — Institutional/biomechanical-vocabulary veto: the sport branch must generally be recognized by the International Olympic Committee; cricket is the sole exception, having appeared only at the 1900 Summer Olympics [PAGE 8]. Non-Olympic or niche physical disciplines (e.g., emerging esports, niche strength sports) are de facto vetoed unless they carry historical Olympic legacy. The paper records no biomechanical performance metrics, VO2 max, reaction time, or load profiles; the physical layer enters only as categorical branch membership, not as measured athlete physiology.

Layer 4 — Geographic/publication-region veto: \(d\) must appear in at least one country/region [PAGE 6, 8–9]. Local athletes are overrepresented relative to globally famous athletes [PAGE 9], and Türkiye/Turkish athletes are absent entirely [PAGE 10], indicating a geographic veto that privileges countries with existing Doodle publication histories. Meteorological and weather variables constitute the null set \(\emptyset\) for this digital content analysis; no weather, temperature, or atmospheric condition appears in the coding categories or Table 1 [PAGE 6–8]. Therefore weather exerts zero veto power in the observed design, though geographic climate zones may correlate indirectly with sport popularity (not tested).

Layer 5 — Gender-representation veto: the observed empirical filter admits males at frequency \(24/33\) and females at \(9/33\) [PAGE 8]. Whether this reflects an active editorial veto by Google, a supply-side veto from the pool of historically notable athletes, or a coding artifact is not isolated in the source [PAGE 8–9].

Formal veto predicate:
\[
\operatorname{Admit}(d) \iff \bigwedge_{\ell \in \{1,2,3,4,5\}} \neg \operatorname{Veto}_{\ell}(d).
\]
Breakdown occurs if any layer's criterion changes (e.g., Google begins featuring living athletes, or the IOC recognizes new branches), because the sample composition and all downstream proportions are conditional on the current layer configuration.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
