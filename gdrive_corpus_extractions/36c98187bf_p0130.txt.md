**Breakdown / singularity regimes:**  
(S1) Joint production or fixed coefficients: \(MP_{ij}\) is not independently defined; the law of variation of proportions is inapplicable.  
(S2) Price endogeneity singularity: \(\frac{d VP_{ij}}{d x_{ij}} = 0\) when the price-reaction term exactly cancels the diminishing-returns term, yielding neutral equilibrium or multiple rest points.  
(S3) Normative intrusion: an exogenous \(D_i\)-based constraint on \(w_i\) breaks \(p_j = c_j\), so the cost-based equilibrium fails to clear.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The page sits at a crossroads of classical and neoclassical distribution theory. Ricardo treated distribution (rent, profit, wages) as determined by scarcity and margin of cultivation, not by desert; Marshall refined imputation through supply and demand for factors; Wicksteed formalized the "coordinate doctrine" that payment should correspond to marginal productivity; Clark most explicitly tied marginal product to ethical entitlement, arguing that in a static natural order each factor gets what it produces. Knight accepts the analytic machinery of marginal productivity on [PAGE 115] but performs a crucial amputation: he denies that causal contribution has any necessary relation to moral desert. The footnote is a direct polemic against the popular dogma that "productivity remuneration" is synonymous with ideal justice [PAGE 115]. The genealogy therefore runs from Ricardo's class-based distribution, through Marshallian imputation and Clarkian natural rights, to Knight's skeptical severance.

Two multi-week traps follow from this lineage. First, the retroactive justification trap: in week 1 an economy settles into a static distribution of shares determined by initial endowments, power, and technical conditions; by week 4 observers look at the equilibrium and narrate each share as if it were caused by that factor's marginal productivity, thereby converting a descriptive rest point into a normative desert story. Knight explicitly warns that this is muddled reasoning designed to avoid a repugnant conclusion about the justice of things as they are [PAGE 115]. Second, the inherited-endowment trap: because inequalities in inherited property and opportunity are obvious [PAGE 115], any week-over-week comparison that treats the current distribution as a meritocracy ignores that the starting vector of factor ownership was itself historically given and morally arbitrary. The trap is temporal: the static equilibrium of page 115 is a snapshot, but agents recursively treat it as a trans-historical moral ledger, allowing past accidents of birth to be re-read as present productive desert.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates as follows. Under stress—war, depression, scarcity, or rapid reallocation—agents and theorists experience cognitive pressure to naturalize outcomes. The popular dogma Knight identifies on [PAGE 115] ("causal contribution = moral desert") functions as a regressive heuristic: it resolves the moral discomfort of inequality by asserting that market shares are earned. In a crisis, the regression intensifies because the cost of acknowledging arbitrariness (inherited property, natural capacity, luck) is politically high; the mind reverts to the just-world heuristic.

The counter-trap is more subtle and more dangerous. A sophisticated analyst who has read Knight's footnote may verbally reject the identification of productivity with desert, yet when pressed to justify a specific wage, rent, or profit share, smuggles moral desert back in through the equilibrium condition. The analyst says, "The share is determined by value productivity," and then treats that value-productivity figure as if it were a desert claim \(D_i\). This is a counter-trap because it uses the very formalism Knight deployed to sever the link, then quietly restores the link in the interpretation layer.

The clock is the sequence of analytic stages Knight prescribes. Stage \(t_1\): study equilibrium shares as remunerations to agencies. Stage \(t_2\): shift viewpoint and regard the same shares as costs of goods. Stage \(t_3\): let those costs feed back into prices, which then generate new remunerations. If the clock is not made explicit, the analyst collapses \(t_1\), \(t_2\), and \(t_3\) into a single timeless relation and treats cost-embedding as a physical fact rather than a chosen analytic stance. The recursive theory of mind problem is infinite: Agent A models whether Agent B believes that marginal productivity justifies pay; Agent A knows that B may be deploying the popular dogma strategically; Agent A must decide whether to deploy the same dogma in response, knowing B is modeling A's moral stance. Knight's page breaks this regress only by refusing to close the loop—he identifies the gap but states the ethical question "cannot be gone into here" [PAGE 115], leaving the recursive modeling unresolved.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. Productive services include human labor, and labor is bounded by biomechanical and physiological ceilings. Let \(\bar{x}_i\) denote the maximum sustainable flow of service \(i\) (work hours, cognitive load, physical exertion). The marginal productivity schedule \(MP_i(x_i)\) is defined only on \(0 \le x_i \le \bar{x}_i\); at \(x_i = \bar{x}_i\) the derivative fails to exist (kink) or the service cannot be increased at any price. Knight's transfer mechanism presumes that resources can be diverted from one organization to another in response to price signals [PAGE 115]. If the source organization is already at or near \(\bar{x}_i\), no price increase can induce further transfer; the physical layer issues a hard veto. Singularity: as \(x_i \to \bar{x}_i\), the supply of factor service becomes perfectly inelastic, and the price-reaction term \(\partial p_j/\partial x_{ij}\) cannot generate the quantity response Knight's mechanism requires.

Biomechanical layer. Knight's abstraction treats productive services as homogeneous fluid quantities that can be varied in proportions [PAGE 115]. Human labor is not homogeneous: skills, training, organization-specific knowledge, and fatigue create mismatch costs. A transfer of "labor" from Organization A to B is not frictionless; the receiving organization may not be able to use the service effectively, so the assumed price change and output adjustment do not map one-to-one onto physical reality. Veto: heterogeneous factor specificity invalidates the law of variation of proportions as a universal organizing principle.

Weather / environmental layer. The production function \(f_j\) implicitly holds the environmental state \(\omega\) (weather, temperature, soil moisture, energy availability) fixed. Under a weather shock, \(f_j(\cdot;\omega)\) shifts, technical coefficients \(a_{ij}\) change (e.g., agricultural yield per labor-hour falls), and the cost condition \(p_j = \sum_i w_i a_{ij}\) computed for \(\omega_0\) no longer holds [PAGE 115]. Because Knight's analysis is static, it has no mechanism to re-solve the cost structure when \(\omega\) changes mid-equilibrium. Veto proof: if \(\Delta \omega\) alters \(a_{ij}\) such that \(\sum_i w_i a_{ij}(\omega_1) > p_j\) for all feasible \(w_i\), production of good \(j\) becomes unprofitable at the inherited prices; the system cannot rest in the posited equilibrium without a complete re-computation of distributive shares and prices.

Adjudication rule. Physical, biomechanical, and environmental vetoes are lexically prior to price-theoretic equilibration. When a veto binds—\(\Delta x_i\) exceeds \(\bar{x}_i\), factor specificity blocks transfer, or \(\omega\) shifts the production set—the price-reaction mechanism on [PAGE 115] cannot operate as stated. Knight's "superficial view" of price reactions collapses because the underlying physical transfer is infeasible or the cost coefficients are no longer invariant. Any equilibrium prediction that ignores these layers is not a breakdown of the mathematics alone but a category error: it treats an abstract price relation as if it could override conservation, physiology, and thermodynamics.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
**Scenario 1: Fixed world price (perfectly elastic output market).**  
Counterfactual: A small economy faces an exogenously fixed price \(\bar p_j\) for good \(j\), so \(\partial p_j / \partial x_i = 0\) regardless of resource transfers. Knight's price-reaction term vanishes.  
Veto: The reinforcing coincidence between price change and diminishing physical returns fails; value productivity moves only through \(p_j (\partial MP_{ij}/\partial x_{ij})\).  
Prediction: After a transfer of resources into production of \(j\), no domestic price change is observed; the movement in value productivity matches marginal physical product alone and may be too weak to generate the equalizing force Knight describes. Observable in trade data: resource shifts do not alter the good's price, and factor returns do not equalize across uses via the price channel.

**Scenario 2: Fixed-proportions (Leontief) technology.**  
Counterfactual: Factors must be used in fixed technical ratios; there is no smooth law of variation of proportions. \(MP_{ij}\) is zero or undefined except at the exact kink.  
Veto: The law of diminishing value productivity is inapplicable because marginal products are not independently variable.  
Prediction: Distributive shares are determined by technical coefficients and zero-profit conditions, not by marginal-value-productivity equalization. A transfer of one factor without its complementary fixed factor yields no output change and no price reaction; idle capacity or bottlenecks appear instead of smooth price adjustment. Observable in assembly-line data: adding workers without machines raises neither output nor price, and the worker's marginal value productivity is zero.

**Scenario 3: Exogenous moral-desert constraint on factor prices.**  
Counterfactual: A regulator imposes a wage floor or income floor based on need/desert, setting \(w_i\) above (or below) the competitive value productivity \(VP_i\).  
Veto: The share \(s_i = w_i x_i\) is no longer a cost derived from equilibrium but a normative transfer, breaking the identity between remuneration and embedded cost.  
Prediction: Unit cost \(c_j = \sum_i w_i a_{ij}\) diverges from market price \(p_j\); firms exit (if \(c_j > p_j\)) or enter (if \(c_j < p_j\)) until quantity adjusts, and the static clearing condition \(p_j = c_j\) fails. Persistent surplus of the factor (unemployment) or surplus of the good is the observable signature. Observable in labor-market data: sectors with binding wage floors show factor prices decoupled from marginal revenue product and quantity rationing consistent with non-clearing, violating Knight's equilibrium price conditions [PAGE 115].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
