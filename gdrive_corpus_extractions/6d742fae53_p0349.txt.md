3. Observability layer. If the clock case back is occluded, the pendulum-bob contact is unobservable; the mutual information \(I(C;\text{visual input})\) drops to zero, and no amount of reasoning can localize \(\tau_f\) without disassembly.

Composite veto function:
\[
V(\boldsymbol\omega,\boldsymbol\theta)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \exists\,\ell\in\{\mathrm{phys},\mathrm{bio},\mathrm{obs}\}: C_\ell(\boldsymbol\omega,\boldsymbol\theta)\ \text{violated},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
James’s reasoning output is admissible only when \(V=0\). Breakdown regime: when \(C_\ell\) depends on exogenous variables outside the sensorium (e.g., interior case humidity, hidden bob position), the agent cannot compute \(V\) and may execute an invalid action with high confidence.

Scenario A — Biomechanical veto on the door. Setup: door latch requires a 2 cm bodily lift; door mass \(m=40\;\mathrm{kg}\); subject maximum lift \(F_{\max}=300\;\mathrm{N}\), while \(mg\approx392\;\mathrm{N}\). Jamesian prediction: a reasoner identifies “insufficient elevation” and raises the door [PAGE 349]. Counterfactual veto: the biomechanical constraint makes the action impossible. Verifiable prediction: without an external tool, success rate for reasoners on the heavy door is approximately \(0\%\), even among subjects who verbally state the correct feature; control door (\(m=5\;\mathrm{kg}\)) yields high success. Falsifiable by measuring lift attempts and verbal reports.

Scenario B — Observability veto on the clock. Setup: identical pendulum-bob friction fault [PAGE 349]; one condition has a transparent case back, another an opaque back. Prediction from James: educated subject diagnoses in roughly \(300\;\mathrm{s}\) by analyzing friction. Counterfactual veto: physical occlusion reduces \(I(C;\text{sensory input})\approx0\). Verifiable prediction: diagnosis time \(T_{\mathrm{diag}}\) is significantly longer in the opaque condition for all education levels; education level does not mediate \(T_{\mathrm{diag}}\) when occlusion is present. Null hypothesis (education effect persists) is rejected if \(T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{opaque}}\gg T_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{transparent}}\) across cohorts.

Scenario C — Naming/pragmatic veto. Setup: subjects shown a Shakespeare portrait labeled “Hawthorne” [cf. Black’s example, PAGE 349]. Two downstream tasks: (i) factual author identification, (ii) social valuation (“sell as Hawthorne”). Footnote prediction: naming satisfies unity and may be accepted if no other end is served. Counterfactual veto: the factual task vetoes the label because the referent mismatch causes failure. Verifiable prediction: subjects who accept the mislabel perform at chance on task (i) but above chance on task (ii). The veto is confirmed by a significant divergence between tasks; absence of divergence falsifies the “mere naming suffices” account for that population.
