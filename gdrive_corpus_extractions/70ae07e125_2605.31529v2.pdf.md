<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All quantities are the metrics the source names; equations are standard definitions, not invented ratios. Units stated explicitly.

1. Compositional retrieval, T3 [PAGE 8].
$$R@K=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}\!\left[\operatorname{rank}_i(q_i,v_i^{+})\le K\right],\quad R@K\in[0,1]\ \text{(dimensionless)}.$$
Chance with one positive among 5,000 negatives: $c=1/5001\approx2.0\times10^{-4}$. Reported $R@1=0.030$ is $\approx150\times$ chance; $R@10=0.133$. Source: "aggregate R@1 of 3.0% and R@10 of 13.3%" [PAGE 8]. Breakdown regime: with near-duplicate fraction $\rho\to1$, the positive–negative margin $\delta=\lVert f(q)-f(v^{+})\rVert-\max_{j}\lVert f(q)-f(v_j^{-})\rVert$ falls below embedding noise $\sigma$; when $\delta\lesssim\sigma$, $R@K\to c$. Consistent with "R@100 drops by nearly half" [PAGE 8].

2. Calibration, T5 [PAGE 10]. Expected Calibration Error over $M$ confidence bins $B_m$ of size $n$:
$$\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{|B_m|}{n}\,\bigl|\operatorname{acc}(B_m)-\operatorname{conf}(B_m)\bigr|\in[0,1].$$
Reported as an aggregate "confidence exceeding accuracy by 28 points" (i.e., $\overline{\operatorname{conf}}-\operatorname{acc}\approx0.28$). Caveat: a mean gap is not ECE; the compact paper omits the binned ECE value.

3. Atomic-fact factual precision, T6 [PAGE 10], FActScore-style (ref 48):
$$\mathrm{Prec}_{\text{fact}}=\frac{\#\{\text{atomic claims supported by source}\}}{\#\{\text{atomic claims emitted}\}}\in[0,1].$$
Reported 0.7301 (video) → 0.8719 (oracle text) [PAGE 14].

4. Simulation geometry, T7/T8 [PAGE 11–12].
$$\mathrm{mIoU}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{|b_t^{gen}\cap b_t^{ref}|}{|b_t^{gen}\cup b_t^{ref}|}\in[0,1],\qquad \mathrm{Sim}_{\cos}=\frac{\langle\phi(x),\phi(y)\rangle}{\lVert\phi(x)\rVert\,\lVert\phi(y)\rVert},$$
with $\phi$ = SigLIP features. T7 basketball: 0.513 vs 0.466 (MagicMotion) vs 0.397 (ATI); soccer 0.611 vs 0.544 vs 0.402 [PAGE 12]. T8 final-frame mIoU 0.344 vs 0.129 vs 0.047 [PAGE 12]. Physical-plausibility predicate is absent: define $\Phi(\text{vid})\in\{0,1\}$ enforcing $v\le v_{\max}\approx12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, bounded joint angles, no interpenetration, foot–ground contact. The source optimizes $P(\text{mIoU high})$ but never reports $P(\Phi{=}1\mid \text{mIoU high})$ — an unmeasured distribution.

5. Agentic search, T9 [PAGE 6, 13] — information units (bits, $\log_2$). Identifying one event over 7,430 games / $1.8\times10^{6}$ clips requires resolving
$$H_{\text{game}}=\log_2 7430\approx12.86\ \text{bits},\qquad H_{\text{clip}}=\log_2(1.8\times10^{6})\approx20.78\ \text{bits}.$$
With $T$ tool calls of mean gain $\bar{\Delta H}$ bits/call, success needs $T\,\bar{\Delta H}\gtrsim H_{\text{res}}$ (residual entropy after seed facts + constraints). GPT-5.2 uses $T\approx21$; Qwen uses $T\in[3,8]$ and "often terminate early with insufficient evidence" [PAGE 13]. If $\bar{\Delta H}\approx1$–$2$ bits/call, Qwen's budget $\le\sim16$ bits can under-resolve a $\sim20$-bit clip space → collapse toward 0, matching 4.6% vs 2.1%.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The four-pillar stack is a modern re-labeling of a 40-year-old control hierarchy: sense → model → plan → act (classical robotics) and Marr's computational/algorithmic/implementational levels. Epistemically it tracks Bloom's taxonomy (remember/understand → analyze → create), which is why "perception easy, synthesis hard" is almost preordained rather than discovered. The video-benchmark lineage runs Kinetics-700 → ActivityNet → EgoSchema/Video-MME/MLVU/LongVideoBench [PAGE 3–4]; each added temporal scope but, as the authors note, "lack verifiable causal ground truth" [PAGE 4]. The causal branch descends from synthetic physics microworlds — CLEVRER, PHYRE, COPHY, Physion (refs 3–5, 88) — which have "verifiable ground truth ... but in-volve single objects in simplified worlds" [PAGE 4]. SVI-Bench's genuine contribution is the substitution: real multi-agent sport as a *naturally verifiable* microworld, borrowing the verifiability of CLEVRER without its toy physics. The agentic pillar is a direct graft of BrowseComp's "hard-to-find but easy-to-verify principle from prior agentic search work [77]" [PAGE 6] onto multimodal corpora, itself descended from ReAct/Toolformer/Voyager/WebArena (refs 62, 72, 87, 96). Simulation descends from trajectory-conditioned diffusion (TrailBlazer, Boximator, ATI, MagicMotion; refs 43, 71, 73, 89) and the Wan/Dreamer/Genie/Cosmos world-model tree (refs 8, 25–27, 50, 70).

Multi-week trap for downstream authors: the genealogy tempts a "cliff is fundamental" reading. It is partly structural. Because the pillars are stacked on *different* metrics (MCQ accuracy, 0–5 Likert, mIoU, exact-match), any team reproducing the figure can move a pillar's height by re-choosing its representative task (e.g., T6 factual 73% vs saliency 7.3%). A researcher who fine-tunes perception and reports "no transfer to reasoning" [PAGE 14] must first rule out that the reasoning metric's ceiling — not the model — is binding.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Two nested mind-modeling problems operate here. Object level: T4/T5 require the model to represent adversaries' intentions — "an aggressive first step will force his defender to overextend" [PAGE 2] is second-order theory-of-mind (model of a model). The oracle result localizes the deficit: T4 barely moves with perfect text (2.06→2.46) [PAGE 14], so the missing faculty is not perceiving the defense but *reasoning about* it. Meta level: the LLM judge is itself a theory-of-mind engine scoring "reasoning coherence" [PAGE 9] against a reference — an unfalsifiable target when reference reports are themselves LLM-generated [PAGE 10].

Crisis-habit regression: under long-horizon pressure, models default to base-rate/salience heuristics — describe the most visually salient action, predict the most frequent event. This is exactly the observed split: "describing what happened while failing to cover salient events" (T6 factual 73% vs saliency 7.33%) [PAGE 10], and perception ≫ reasoning. Counter-trap (the model that resists regression): the human experts, who "know when they are uncertain," with "accuracy ... from 30% to 90% across confidence levels on T2" [PAGE 15] — calibrated abstention is the anti-regression signature; GPT-5.2's flat confidence (28-point miscalibration) is the regression signature. The clock: the benchmark's temporal windows are the pressure variable — 10 s clips (T1–T3) reward fast perception, whereas 55–150 min games (T4/T6) and 3–15 min forecasts (T5) punish greedy early commitment. The strategic lesson is that time-horizon, not raw capability, gates the transition from perception to planning.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto is an independent constraint that overrides a surface-good result. I adjudicate three layers.

Physical/biomechanical (T7/T8): mIoU and SigLIP similarity can be high while the video is physically impossible — a player can sit inside the correct bounding box while limbs interpenetrate or velocity exceeds $v_{\max}$. Because $P(\Phi{=}1\mid \text{mIoU high})\ne P(\text{mIoU high})$, the reported "0.513 mIoU means roughly half of generated player positions deviate significantly" [PAGE 12] *understates* the failure: positional correctness does not certify dynamic plausibility. Verdict: simulation claims are not physical-plausibility-vetted; a hard veto layer is missing.

Weather/exogenous (soccer): outdoor ball aerodynamics (Magnus force $\propto \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{v}$), wet-pitch traction, and wind are unmodeled; the microworld implicitly conditions on canonical physics. Verdict: "simulation of alternative futures" is scoped to a fixed environment — a valid but stated-narrowly limitation.

Perception-vs-reasoning (the decisive veto): the oracle experiment is a clean cross-layer refutation. If low reasoning scores were caused by perceptual error, then perfect text perception (oracle) should raise T4/T5 substantially. It does not (T4 2.06→2.46; T5 38.2→41.9) [PAGE 14]. Therefore the perception layer is *vetoed* as the primary bottleneck for reasoning/forecasting. Conversely, for T9 the oracle jumps 4.6→54.0%, so retrieval/search is vetoed as the primary T9 bottleneck, leaving reasoning+planning as the residual cap (~54%). These two vetoes jointly prove the authors' "no single capability" thesis and are the strongest evidence in the paper.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR = reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards; the source already leans on verifiable short answers (T9) and RL-trained video agents (refs 52, 74, 92). Three falsifiable scenarios:

1. RLVR on T9 retrieval planning. Train an agent with reward = exact-match on the short factual answer. Prediction (verifiable): $T_{\text{calls}}$ and answer accuracy rise for small models as the policy learns to persist past the ~8-call termination point; but accuracy saturates below the oracle ceiling, i.e., $\text{acc}_{\text{RL}}<0.54$, because residual bottleneck is reasoning not search. Falsifier: if RL training drives $\text{acc}\to0.54$ without new perception/reasoning capacity, the "reasoning cap" claim is wrong.

2. RLVR on T2 perception. Since fine-tuning already yields 73.91% vs human 75.78% [PAGE 8, 15], verifiable-reward RL should add little. Prediction: $\Delta\text{acc}\lesssim2$–$3$ points; ceiling-bound. Falsifier: a double-digit jump would indicate the fine-tuned baseline was under-trained, not saturated.

3. Counterfactual veto against judge-gaming. An RL policy rewarded by the *LLM judge* on T4/T6 can inflate Likert/factual scores via fluent-but-vacuous prose while failing verifiable T9. Prediction: $\text{corr}(\text{T6 saliency},\ \text{T9 acc})\approx0$ and judge-rewarded models show high T4/T6 with persistently low T9. This is a structural veto: T9's exact-match reward is the only pillar immune to stylistic reward hacking, which is why the 4.6%→5% headline is more trustworthy than the 73% T6 factual figure. Recommendation for the benchmark: report binned ECE for T5, CIs on T4/T9, judge–judge cross-agreement (not just human–judge), and an explicit $\Phi$ physical-plausibility metric for T7/T8.
