Canonical Elo underlying it ([PAGE 5] cites "Elo rankings originally used in chess [3]"; formula reconstructed, not in source):
$$E_A = \frac{1}{1+10^{-(R_A-R_B)/400}},\qquad R_A' = R_A + K\,(S_A - E_A),\quad S_A\in\{0,0.5,1\}.$$

2. Daily min–max scaling to Power Ranking ([PAGE 6], "best team … 100, … worst … 0"):
$$P_t = 100\cdot\frac{E_t - \min_s E_{s,t}}{\max_s E_{s,t} - \min_s E_{s,t}}\in[0,100].$$

3. Per-90 target/feature construction ([PAGE 4]): $R_{i,j} = 90\cdot \dfrac{\text{count}_{i,j}}{M_{i,j}}$ [events·min⁻¹·90].

4. Prior–rolling convex blend ([PAGE 8], verbatim structure), c=1000 min for players ([PAGE 9]):
$$X_{i,j,g} = (1-w_{j,g})\,P_{i,j} + w_{j,g}\,R_{i,j,g},\qquad w_{j,g}=\min\!\Big(1,\ \tfrac{\sum m_{j,g}}{c}\Big),\ w\in[0,1].$$

5. Team adjustment model ([PAGE 23], verbatim $y_{i,j}=x_{i,j}+\alpha_j+\beta_j z_{i,j}+\epsilon_{i,j}$), $\epsilon_{i,j}\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_j^2)$; the naive-league-expectation offset x enters with unit coefficient.

6. Player adjustment model ([PAGE 24]; OCR-degraded, best-defensible reading with polynomial expansion in the relative-ability change $x_4$ = team Power Ranking − league mean, [PAGE 24]):
$$y_{i,j,k}=\alpha_j+\beta_{1,j}x_{1,i,j,k}+\beta_{2,j}x_{2,i,j,k}+\beta_{3,j}x_{3,i,j,k}+\beta_{4,j}x_{4,i,j}+\beta_{5,j}x_{4,i,j}^{2}+\beta_{6,j}x_{4,i,j}^{3}+\epsilon_{i,j,k}.$$

7. Shortlist score ([PAGE 12], verbal): $\text{Score}_p=\dfrac{\sum_j w_j\,\tilde y_{p,j}}{\sum_j w_j}$, $\tilde y$ mean-normalized, $w_j\in[0,1]$.

Singular / breakdown regimes (exact):
- $P_t$: undefined when $\max_s E = \min_s E$; ill-conditioned and outlier-compressed otherwise.
- Per-90 rate: singular as $M_{i,j}\to 0^+$; the blend (operator 4) regularizes it because $w\to 0$ forces $X\to P$.
- Team regression (5): $\beta_j$ non-identified when $x$ (offset) and $z$ (relative prev-league value) are collinear; promotion transitions are rare over 32 leagues → high-variance $\hat\beta$.
- Player regression (6): the $x_4^3$ term explodes under extrapolation — for extreme ability gaps (e.g., Independiente→Man City, [PAGE 6]) $x_4$ sits in the tail and the cubic dominates → Runge-type instability. This is the sharpest breakdown surface.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage chain: Elo (Arpad Elo, USCF, 1961, [PAGE 21 ref 3]) → soccer World-Football-Elo-style ratings → the paper's four-level hierarchical variant ([PAGE 5-6]). The paper's genuine novelty over prior sports projection is stated on [PAGE 19]: it differs from PECOTA (baseball, [7]), Imburgio & Goldberg's team-coarsened soccer value [8], WARP (basketball, [10]), and FiveThirtyEight's roster machine [9] by staying at within-team player interaction granularity across 13 KPIs. Style lineage: SciSports role labels [12] and Torvaney's player2vec [13]; ability lineage: smarterscout league-adjusted single-value ratings [15].

Multi-week traps embedded in the design: (1) Elo inflation — explicitly countered by daily re-scaling ([PAGE 6]), but daily re-scaling breaks longitudinal metric comparability, a slow-acting trap for any model retrained across seasons. (2) The 1000-minute rolling window with old-data discard ([PAGE 7], N=1000; team-position M=3000) creates concept-drift lag: a role change faster than window turnover is invisible. (3) The "only adjust highest level" rule ([PAGE 6]) means continental ratings for rarely-traveling confederations stay stale for months — a temporal trap for cross-confederation transfers.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The "clock" is explicit: deadline day, "over $200 million … on this day alone," [PAGE 1], with decisions needed faster than manual scouting allows. Model the actors recursively. Level-0: a committee wants a per-90 forecast. Level-1: the selling club knows buyers are forecasting and holds out for a higher fee. Level-2: the buyer knows the seller is holding out and discounts its own willingness-to-pay. Crisis-habit regression: under deadline compression, human committees revert to salient, name-recognition priors (the "big money signing" bias, [PAGE 1]), which is precisely the persistence baseline the model beats by 49% ([PAGE 10]) — the model's edge is largest exactly where human time-pressure habit is worst.

Counter-trap: the paper's edge is decodable. If rival clubs infer the feature set (Elo hierarchy + per-90 style blocks), the predicted player's price rises toward the model-implied value and the informational alpha decays (reflexivity). The RAG system ([PAGE 9]) is the built-in counter-trap for adverse selection on the seller's side — a red-rated low-minute player (e.g., Demir, "less than 500 minutes," [PAGE 14]) is flagged as high-variance, deterring overpayment on noise. A rational counter-trap for the buyer: never act on a point estimate for a red/RAG-flagged or high-|x₄| transfer; widen the effective interval and overlay an out-of-model veto (next section).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The decisive structural fact is [PAGE 19]: "we currently restrict the input data to event level." Therefore any determinant of per-90 output that is not encoded in Opta spatial-event counts is an out-of-model veto layer capable of nullifying a prediction.

Physical/biomechanical veto: the target is the first 1,000 minutes ([PAGE 2]). Reaching that target requires durability. A move from a lower-tempo league (Ligue 1, Eredivisie) into a higher-physical-load league raises duel volume, sprint count, and recovery demand — none of which are event features. The model even concedes take-ons are "somewhat irreducible element of player style" ([PAGE 16]), but irreducible-in-style is not irreducible-in-durability: an elite take-on volume maintained against more physical opposition raises soft-tissue injury hazard, which censors the 1,000-minute target itself (selection/censoring bias, not captured). Proof of veto: if minutes-to-1000 is not reached, the target is undefined and the per-90 prediction is untestable — a genuine breakdown the feature space cannot represent.

Weather veto: latitude/climate shifts (e.g., Netherlands→England winter slate) affect technical wingers' execution in cold/wet conditions; there is no weather covariate. Any prediction implicitly conditions on climate-invariance.

Adjudication: because these layers act on both the numerator (event realization) and the denominator (minutes available), and neither is in the input vector, every Transfer Portal forecast carries an untestable embedded assumption — "the player remains healthy, adapts physically, and the climate is neutral." That assumption, not the network, is the true weakest link. The rational adjudication is to treat a high point prediction as valid only conditional on passing an independent medical/biomechanical and environmental screen.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactuals, each with a concrete falsification test.

1. Mbappé→Real Madrid ("Hot," [PAGE 18]). Verifiable claim: an "8-15% decrease in predicted outputs," yet "94th percentile … for xG and xA." Prediction: post-transfer per-90 xG/xA lands within ~8–15% below the Ligue 1 baseline and ≥90th percentile in La Liga. Veto condition: realized drop exceeds 15%, or percentile falls below 90th — the league-difficulty coefficient is understated.

2. Frenkie de Jong→Man United ("Not," [PAGE 18]). Verifiable causal claim: outputs "slash … by up to 50% due to large stylistic differences … more than differences in league difficulty." This decomposes two causes. Test: regress realized per-90 change on (style-mismatch feature) vs (league-ability delta). Veto condition: the drop is statistically attributable to league difficulty rather than style — the model's causal attribution (its core novelty, [PAGE 19]) is falsified even if the point magnitude is right.

3. Elite-tail attenuation ([PAGE 11]). Verifiable systematic bias: "slight over prediction for the lowest xG players and a slight under prediction for the highest." Test on the 2,659-transfer set: stratify residuals by baseline xG decile; the top decile should show significant negative mean residual (under-prediction). Veto condition: top-decile mean residual is not significantly negative — the shrinkage claim fails, and point estimates for elite targets would be trustworthy (contradicting the calibration figure).

Each counterfactual is falsifiable against the paper's own reported statistics or a defined future sample, satisfying RLVR's verifiable-reward requirement, and each yields an actionable veto for a decision-maker relying on the model's point output.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
