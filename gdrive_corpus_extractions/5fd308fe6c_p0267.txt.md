Weather / environmental layer:
Atmospheric dynamics are often modeled on an infinite-dimensional phase space (e.g., Sobolev spaces of velocity fields). Such spaces with their norm topology are not locally compact. By breakdown regime B₁, no Haar measure exists via the [PAGE 252] finite-covering argument because bounded sets are not totally bounded and E : F = ∞ for open F with small radius. Veto condition: V_env = 1 if the phase space is not locally compact. In that regime, any invariant measure must be constructed by other means (e.g., invariant Gibbs measures, stationary Fokker–Planck solutions), and the covering-number approach E : F cannot produce a nonzero σ-finite invariant measure.

Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) veto scenarios, each with a counterfactual prediction.

Scenario 1: Non-locally-compact phase space.
Setup: Let X be an infinite-dimensional separable Banach space (e.g., L²([0,1])) with its norm topology, regarded as an abelian topological group under addition. The reasoner is asked to construct a Haar measure using the E : F ratio from [PAGE 252].
Veto: Compactness fails. For the unit ball E and an open ball F of radius r > 0, E is not totally bounded, so no finite collection of left translates covers E. Therefore E : F = ∞, violating the definition’s requirement that E : F be a least non-negative integer. The construction cannot produce a nonzero set function.
Verifiable prediction: Any formal proof assistant (Lean, Coq, Isabelle) checking the claimed construction will reject the finiteness assertion; the extracted measure will be the zero measure μ ≡ 0, or the proof will not type-check. Counterfactual: if X were finite-dimensional ℝᵈ, E : F < ∞ and the proof proceeds.

Scenario 2: Non-unimodular group with modular-function neglect.
Setup: Let X be the affine group ax + b (a > 0, b ∈ ℝ) with group law (a,b)(a',b') = (aa', b + a b'). The left Haar measure is dₗμ = da db / a² and the right Haar measure is dᵣμ = da db / a. The reasoner is asked to verify that ν(E) = μ(E⁻¹) produces the same measure as μ.
Veto: The group is non-unimodular with modular function Δ(a,b) = 1/a. The pushforward by inversion gives the right Haar measure, which is not a scalar multiple of the left Haar measure. Assuming ν = μ is false.
Verifiable prediction: Compute ∫_{K} f(x) dₗμ(x) versus ∫_{K} f(x) dᵣμ(x) for f(x) = 1 on a compact K = {(a,b) : 1 ≤ a ≤ 2, 0 ≤ b ≤ 1}. The left integral is ∫₁² ∫₀¹ a⁻² db da = 1/2; the right integral is ∫₁² ∫₀¹ a⁻¹ db da = log 2. Since 1/2 ≠ log 2, the measures differ. A reward model that accepts “ν = μ” fails verification. Counterfactual: if X were unimodular (Δ ≡ 1), the two integrals would agree up to scale.

Scenario 3: OCR-literalism in source-text ingestion.
Setup: The training corpus contains the corrupted [PAGE 252] string “C ⊂ U-1 xU” and “Σλ-1 μ(xU)”. The reasoner is asked to formalize the proof without human source correction.
Veto: “U-1” is not defined as a set operation on subsets of X in the text; “λ” is unbound; “xU” lacks indexing. The expression is not well-formed in first-order logic over the signature (X, ·, μ, ℬ). A verifier will return a parse error.
Verifiable prediction: A type-checked formalization will fail at the line containing “U-1 xU” unless the reasoner inserts the repair ⋃ᵢ₌₁ⁿ xᵢU. The reward signal is zero for any submission that treats the OCR string as ground truth. Counterfactual: if the source were rendered in clean LaTeX, the proof verifies and the reward is positive.

These three vetoes are mutually independent: B₁ (topology), B₃/modular (algebraic symmetry), and B₅ (source fidelity). Passing all three is necessary for a valid Haar-measure derivation from [PAGE 252].
