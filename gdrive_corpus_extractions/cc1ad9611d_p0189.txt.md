Biological measurements cannot instantiate this; neural transduction and muscular activation introduce latencies on the order of tens to hundreds of milliseconds. If \(|T_k - T_z|\) exceeds the timing resolution (e.g., >10 ms), the value of \(x\) is corrupted, and because the domain is restricted to \(x < 0.44\) s, even small errors can push a trial outside the fitted regime or flip the sign of \(x\), moving the observation from one gradient to the other.

Biomechanical layer veto: The response \(R\) may be a lever press, vocalization, or key press with finite rise time. The equation conflates the decision instant with the movement instant. Under fatigue or practice, response latency shifts systematically with trial number, making \(T_k\) a non-stationary estimator of the underlying decision variable \(T_z\). This injects a trend into \(x\) that mimics learning but is actually motor noise.

Environmental/weather layer veto: Contextual variables (room temperature, lighting, interoceptive state, experimenter presence) drift across sessions and alter \(T_a\) independently of \(T_s\). Because \(t' = T_a - T_s - 0.44\), a shift in \(T_a\) moves trials across the \(t' = 0\) discontinuity. A 0.5 s environmental delay flips the coefficient from −1.068 to +1.182, which is an implausibly brittle switch for a nervous system. Any session in which ambient timing jitter exceeds ~100 ms voids the piecewise assumption.

Veto proof summary: The equation is admissible only under a conjunction of conditions—high temporal resolution, low biomechanical latency variance, stationary context, intermediate \(p\) (to avoid probit singularity), and \(x\) strictly inside the stated domain. A single trial violating any condition is sufficient to veto the model’s application to that trial.

Scenario 1 — Boundary discontinuity. Counterfactual: Arrange a trial such that \(t' = 0\) exactly (\(T_a - T_s = 0.44\) s). Because \(c(t')\) jumps from −1.068 to +1.182 with no value defined at the boundary, the model yields no unique \(p\). Prediction: With sub-millisecond stimulus apparatus, if empirical evocation probability is continuous through \(t' = 0\), the piecewise equation is falsified; if a sharp discontinuity is observed, the equation is locally supported but remains physically implausible as a neural mechanism.

Scenario 2 — Probit tail singularity. Counterfactual: Force the observed probability to \(p = 0.02\) (2%) and apply the normal-probability conversion referenced for [PAGE 311 ff.] to derive “amount” units. As \(p \to 0\), \(\Phi^{-1}(p) \to -\infty\), so the converted amount diverges while the fitted asymptote remains fixed at \(A = 6.5\). Prediction: In any experimental block where evocation rates fall below ~5%, the probit-transformed equation will predict amounts outside the finite range implied by the asymptote; observation of bounded behavior at low \(p\) falsifies direct application of the normal-probability conversion without truncation.

Scenario 3 — Domain extrapolation. Counterfactual: Apply the left-hand gradient equation, valid only for \(x < 0.44\) s [PAGE 189], to an asynchrony of \(x = +0.60\) s. Because negative growth is fitted only below the .44 s cutoff, extrapolation beyond it has no warrant and may project \(p\) beyond the asymptote or below zero. Prediction: Test trials at \(x = 0.60\) s under left-gradient conditions should produce bounded \(p < 6.5\) if the domain restriction is real; if the unmodified equation is used and yields \(p > 6.5\) or \(p < 0\), the model is invalid outside its fitted interval.
