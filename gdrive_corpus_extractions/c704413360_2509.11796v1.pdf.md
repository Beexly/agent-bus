Eq (5)/(6) contrastive decoding: 
p(y) ∝ softmax[(1+α)logit(y|clean) − Σ α_k logit(y|distorted_k)]
Breakdown regimes:
- α = 0: reduces to standard p_θ(y|clean) — no hallucination mitigation.
- α → ∞ (or α_k dominant): distribution dominated by negative of distorted logits → can produce degenerate/off-manifold outputs (the model is pushed away from what distorted input suggests, but if distortions destroyed signal, −logit(distorted) is near-uniform noise, injecting variance). The paper fixes α = α_S+α_T+α_ST = 1.0 total (0.5+0.3+0.2). Note (1+α) = 2.0 multiplier on the clean term.
- The "logit" here — for the negative term, standard contrastive decoding uses log p (log-softmax), and the temperature α scales. The formula mixes (1+α)·logit_clean − α·logit_dist. This is the VCD (Visual Contrastive Decoding, Leng et al. 2024, ref [28]) formulation. Assumption: the distorted input's errors are "hallucination-prone" directions to subtract out. If distortion removes genuine signal (not just hallucination priors), you subtract real signal → degradation. This is exactly why the paper found CutMix (destroys spatial structure) and all-shuffle (destroys temporal order) are the WORST distortions (Fig 7): they destroy genuine signal so the contrast subtracts truth.

This is a genuinely deep point: the method assumes distortions isolate the "language-prior / hallucination" component. When a distortion instead destroys task-relevant signal, contrastive subtraction removes correct information. The paper's Fig 7 empirically confirms: Gaussian noise (preserves structure) best; all-shuffle (destroys temporal order) worst. Great analysis.

Eq (8)/(9) cosine similarity: 
sim = (a·b)/(||a|| ||b||) ∈ [−1, 1]. Units: dimensionless (normalized dot product). Assumption: Long-CLIP embedding space is isotropic enough that cosine is meaningful; fine-grained sports sub-actions have small angular separation → "minimal differences between fine-grained movements" (paper's own words [PAGE 5]) cause ambiguity, motivating top-5 selection. sim_V2R = sim_pos − sim_neg ∈ [−2, 2]. Breakdown: if ||d|| or ||i|| → 0 (degenerate embedding), division undefined. Negative-relation subtraction assumes pos and neg relation text embeddings are roughly antipodal; if the text encoder embeds "on top of" and "not on top of" too similarly (negation poorly captured by CLIP text encoder — a known CLIP weakness), sim_pos ≈ sim_neg → sim_V2R ≈ 0 → no discriminative signal. This is a real, citable breakdown regime grounded in known CLIP limitations.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All symbols, units, and singularity regimes below are taken verbatim from the source; page markers are exact.

Generation operator (Eq. 1, [PAGE 3]):
$$R = F\big[E_V(V),\, E_L(Q)\big]$$
Units: R is a token sequence; E_V(V) is a visual embedding, E_L(Q) a text embedding. Assumption: a shared multimodal space. Singularity: undefined E_L (see trace).

Reactive router (Eq. 2, [PAGE 4]):
$$R_{\text{React}} = F_{\text{React}}\big[E_V(V),\, E_L(Q)\big]$$
This emits either a direct answer or a "switch" command. It is a discrete decision operator over four difficulty axes ([PAGE 4]): relevance, static-vs-dynamic, single-vs-multi-step, external-knowledge dependency.

Segmentation (Eq. 3, [PAGE 5]; Algorithm 1, [PAGE 11]):
$$\text{Proposals} = S_{\text{segment}}(\hat V),\qquad \text{threshold} = \text{mean}(w) - z\cdot\text{std}(w)$$
with a boundary declared when $\text{motions}[i] < \text{threshold}$ and $i - \text{last\_b} \ge \text{clip\_len}$. $\hat V$ is the SAM 2–masked video; motions are aggregated optical-flow magnitudes ("AggFlow … e.g., magnitude, direction"). Units: threshold is in flow-magnitude units; z is dimensionless (a z-score multiplier). Physical prior (verbatim, [PAGE 5]): "athletes exhibit varying motion intensities, with brief pauses between action sets," so "This adaptive process aligns with natural human kinematics." Breakdown regimes: (i) $z\to 0$ makes threshold ≈ mean → boundary on every sub-average frame → over-segmentation; (ii) large $z$ → threshold strongly negative → only near-zero motion fires → missed brief pauses → under-segmentation; (iii) the optical-flow premise fails under large inter-frame displacement (fast dives/saltos), motion blur, or camera pan that survives imperfect SAM 2 masking — flow magnitude then no longer tracks athlete kinematics, and every downstream stage inherits the error.

Hierarchical contrastive decoding (Eqs. 4–6, [PAGE 5]):
$$y_t \sim p_\theta(y_t \mid C[i], Q', y_{<t})$$
$$p(y\mid C[i], C_{\text{Dis}}[i], Q') = \text{softmax}\big[(1+\alpha)\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C[i], Q') - \alpha\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C_{\text{Dis}}[i], Q')\big]$$
$$y_t \sim \text{softmax}\Big[(1+\alpha)\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C[i], Q') - \alpha_S\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C_{\text{Spa}}[i], Q') - \alpha_T\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C_{\text{Tem}}[i], Q') - \alpha_{ST}\,\text{logit}_\theta(y\mid C_{\text{ST}}[i], Q')\Big]$$
with $\alpha = \alpha_S + \alpha_T + \alpha_{ST}$ and defaults $\alpha_S=0.5,\ \alpha_T=0.3,\ \alpha_{ST}=0.2$ ([PAGE 7]), so $1+\alpha = 2.0$. This is the Visual Contrastive Decoding form (ref [28]). Singularity regimes: (a) $\alpha=0$ recovers vanilla $p_\theta(y\mid C[i],Q')$ — no hallucination suppression; (b) $\alpha\to\infty$ inverts onto $-\text{logit}_\theta(\text{distorted})$, and if the distortion destroyed task-relevant signal rather than the language prior, the subtraction removes truth → degenerate outputs. This is exactly what Fig. 7 [PAGE 12] exhibits empirically: Gaussian noise (structure-preserving) is best, while CutMix (destroys spatial structure) and all-shuffle (destroys temporal order) are worst — the contrastive assumption "distortion isolates the hallucination component" is violated when distortion destroys genuine signal.

Fine-grained matching (Eqs. 8–9, [PAGE 5–6]), cosine operators (dimensionless, range [−1,1]):
$$\text{sim}_{T2T} = \frac{c_j \cdot d^{S}_{n_{id}}}{\lVert c_j\rVert\,\lVert d^{S}_{n_{id}}\rVert},\qquad \text{sim}_{V2V} = \frac{\hat C[j] \cdot i^{S}_{n_{id}}}{\lVert \hat C[j]\rVert\,\lVert i^{S}_{n_{id}}\rVert}$$
$$\text{sim}_{V2R} = \text{sim}^{pos}_{V2R} - \text{sim}^{neg}_{V2R} \in [-2, 2]$$
Assumption ([PAGE 5]): "minimal differences between fine-grained movements" cause ambiguity, motivating top-5 retrieval. Singularity: division undefined as $\lVert d\rVert$ or $\lVert i\rVert \to 0$; and the negative-relation subtraction assumes the text encoder embeds "on top of" vs. "not on top of" as near-antipodal. CLIP-family text encoders model negation poorly, so $\text{sim}^{pos}\approx\text{sim}^{neg}\Rightarrow \text{sim}_{V2R}\approx 0$ → the relational discriminator vanishes precisely on the fine relations it was built for.

Reasoner (Eq. 10, [PAGE 6]):
$$\text{Answer} = P_{\text{Reason}}\big(E_V(Q),\, E_L(\hat C, \hat p_r)\big)$$
where $\hat p_r = S_{\text{match}}(p_r, \hat C, C, G_{SS})$ injects the $N_2$ most similar SSGraph action types. Notation defect: $E_V(Q)$ (visual encoder on text) is a typo for $E_L(Q)$.
