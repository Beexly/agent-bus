Adjudication rule: the physical and biomechanical vetoes are jointly sufficient to reject the claim that \(\mathcal{S}\) and \(\mathcal{M}\) alone produce spatial form on [PAGE 276]; the environmental veto is conditional on task demands for visual spatial precision. Breakdown regime: when \(\mathcal{S}\) is non-spatial and \(\mathcal{M}\) is non-metric, no composition operator defined in the text can satisfy the metric axioms.

Scenario 1: Pure ordinal subject. A subject is trained exclusively on tone-pitch sequences (a serially ordered series with no spatial correlate) for several weeks, then asked to estimate spatial distances between imagined tones placed on a line. Veto prediction: ordinal adjacency is reproduced with high accuracy, but distance estimates are at chance or follow a logarithmic compression (Weber-Fechner law) rather than a linear SI meter scale. This falsifies the [PAGE 276] claim that serial order suffices for space-order; the musical scale objection is empirically upheld.

Scenario 2: Forward-backward symmetry test. A subject learns a spatial path \(A \to B \to C\), then is tested on backward recall \(C \to B \to A\). If reversed-order fusion generated spatial form, backward recall should generate an identical spatial representation. Veto prediction: serial-position effects produce asymmetric reaction times and error patterns (primacy and recency asymmetry), so forward and backward recall are not spatially identical. This vetoes James's fusion-of-contrasted-reproductions mechanism as sufficient for spatial form on [PAGE 276].

Scenario 3: Local-sign invariance test. Two points are presented at identical physical separation but at different retinal eccentricities (fovea versus periphery). Lotze's local-sign doctrine, if taken as sensation-intrinsic spatial reconstruction, predicts equivalent local signs. Veto prediction: two-point discrimination threshold increases with eccentricity, following a cortical magnification factor approximately \(M(\epsilon) \approx k/(a + \epsilon)\), so local signs differ by retinal location. This vetoes a pure local-sign reconstruction of space without motor or retinotopic metric coupling, consistent with Wundt's heterogeneity condition quoted on [PAGE 276].
