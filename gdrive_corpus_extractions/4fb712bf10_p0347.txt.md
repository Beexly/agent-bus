The lineage of the constructs on [PAGE 340] traces through several distinct scientific traditions that Hull attempted to unify in *Principles of Behavior* (1943). The reaction-threshold formalism descends from psychophysics (Weber–Fechner) and from Pavlovian reflex physiology, where a stimulus must exceed a threshold to elicit a response. Hull’s sER (excitatory/evoked reaction potential) operationalizes the excitatory gradient derived from Edward Thorndike’s law of effect but expresses it in a deductive, equation-driven form intended to rival Newtonian mechanics in rigor. The galvanic skin reaction itself has a separate genealogy: Tarchanoff (1890) demonstrated that skin electrical activity varies with psychological states, and Jung’s word-association laboratory used GSR as an index of affective arousal. Hull imports this autonomic measure into a S–R framework that was originally designed for skeletal, overt responses.

The sB construct [PAGE 340] is historically significant because it anticipates modern latent-variable state-space models: an unobserved state (behavioral tendency) linking two observable components of a learning curve. However, Hull’s sB lacks the recursive Bayesian updating of a Kalman filter or POMDP; it is a static theoretical bridge rather than a dynamic filter with prediction-error correction.
