**Operator 1 — Domain coverage proportion.** For a reporting domain \(d\) and study set \(S_d\),

\[
R_d=\frac{1}{|S_d|}\sum_{i\in S_d}\mathbf{1}\!\left[r_{i,d}=1\right],\qquad R_d\in[0,1]\ \text{(dimensionless)}
\]

where \(r_{i,d}\in\{0,1\}\) is the binary extraction code ('+' = 1, 'NR' = 0, '?' excluded or set to 0). Units: dimensionless proportion of studies. **Quantisation breakdown:** with \(|S_d|=12\) the attainable values are \(\{0, 1/12, \dots, 1\}\), so one study equals 8.33 percentage points; any between-study difference smaller than \(1/12\) is not identifiable at this sample size. **Singularity:** undefined if \(|S_d|=0\).

Applied values from Table 2 [PAGE 8]: \(R_{\text{spec}}=10/12=0.833\); \(R_{\text{prog}}=4/12=0.333\); \(R_{\text{over}}=2/12=0.167\); \(R_{\text{init}}=4/12=0.333\); \(R_{\text{rev}}=1/12=0.083\); \(R_{\text{dim}}=1/12=0.083\). The review's own summary matches: "specificity was the most reported (83.3%), and diminished returns and reversibility were least reported (8.3%)" [PAGE 1].

**Operator 2 — Unweighted composite coverage.**

\[
C_{\text{core}}=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^{6}R_{d_k}=0.3056,\qquad
C_{\text{FITT}}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{4}R_{d_k}
\]

\(C_{\text{core}}=0.3056\) (dimensionless). For FITT, using the text's own figures [PAGE 7]: \(C_{\text{FITT}}=(0.667+0.500+0.500+0.500)/4=0.542\); using the Table 3 cell counts (9/12, 7/12, 6/12, 6/12) it is \(0.583\). **Assumption:** equal domain weights, chosen because the source assigns none; any non-uniform weighting would require an external elicitation and is not derivable from the text. The review's null claim is stronger than the composite: "No individual study reported all six core exercise principles" [PAGE 4], and "no study reported all four FITT principles" [PAGE 1] with Hirko the sole near-exception ("Only one study (Hirko et al., 2021) reported on all four FITT principles") [PAGE 4].

**Operator 3 — The missing dose operator.** The review defines dose as "completion of exercise program as prescribed" [PAGE 3]. Operationally, for study \(j\):

\[
D_j=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n_j}s^{c}_{jk}\,r^{c}_{jk}\,w^{c}_{jk}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n_j}s^{p}_{jk}\,r^{p}_{jk}\,w^{p}_{jk}}
\]

with sets \(s\) (count), repetitions \(r\) (count), external load \(w\) (kg); superscripts \(c\) = completed, \(p\) = prescribed. Units: kg·repetitions divided by kg·repetitions, hence dimensionless. **Breakdown regimes:** (i) \(D_j\) is undefined when the denominator is zero — precisely the case for bodyweight-only prescriptions where \(w\) is not codable (Wagoner 2023/2024 in Table 3, [PAGE 8]); (ii) \(D_j\) is unidentifiable when \(s^c\) or \(r^c\) is unmeasured, which is the reported state for the whole corpus, hence \(D_j\equiv\text{missing}\ \forall j\). The review confirms this is total, not partial: "dose (i.e., adherence to prescribed plan) not reported in any study" [PAGE 1]; "No included study provided information on the prescribed dose of exercise, or on any deviations from the intended prescription" [PAGE 9].

**Operator 4 — Intensity anchoring.** Intensity is a function of a reference frame:

\[
\text{Intensity}=g\!\left(\%1\text{RM},\ \text{RPE}_{\text{CR10}},\ \text{RIR}\right),\qquad
\%1\text{RM}=\frac{w}{1\text{RM}}\times 100
\]

with \(w\) in kg, \(\%1\text{RM}\) dimensionless, \(\text{RPE}_{\text{CR10}}\in[0,10]\) dimensionless, RIR in repetitions-in-reserve. When only a 1RM and a repetition count are available, the Epley estimator gives \(1\text{RM}\approx w\left(1+r/30\right)\) (kg). **Breakdown:** \(g\) is not identified when no anchor is reported; the review states the anchor is absent in every case — "none of these six studies explained how this intensity was determined (i.e., as a percentage of one repetition maximum (1RM), or ratings of perceived exertion (RPE))" [PAGE 7]; "without clarification of how intensity is specifically determined (i.e., %1RM, RPE) – which was omitted from all included studies – effectiveness of an intervention cannot be fully assessed" [PAGE 11]. An ordinal label ("light", "moderate", "moderate to vigorous") is a rank, not a quantity; it cannot be mapped to \(g\) without an assumption that is not stated anywhere in the corpus.

**Operator 5 — Diminishing returns as a saturating response.** Following the review's definition — "each individual has an upper limit of possible adaptation to an exercise stimulus" [PAGE 3] —

\[
A(t)=A_{\infty}\left(1-e^{-kt}\right)
\]

\(A\) in outcome units (kg lifted, seconds on 30STS, FACT-G points), \(t\) in weeks, \(k\) in \(\text{week}^{-1}\). **Singularity/breakdown:** \(k\to 0\) is the non-responder or under-loaded case; \(A_\infty\to 0\) is the ceiling-at-baseline case; and \(k\) is unidentifiable if the observation window satisfies \(t\ll 1/k\). Only one study addressed this at all — "diminished returns similarly reported in only one study (Marker et al., 2025)" [PAGE 4].

**Operator 6 — Reversibility as exponential detraining.**

\[
A(t)=A(t_0)\,e^{-\lambda(t-t_0)},\qquad t\ge t_0,\ \lambda\ \text{in week}^{-1}
\]

Reported in 1/12 studies (Marker), via "performing follow-up objective measures related to primary outcomes at completion of the intervention" [PAGE 4]. **Breakdown:** \(\lambda\) is unidentifiable if no post-cessation measurement exists — the state of 11/12 studies.

**Operator 7 — Attenuation of the estimable treatment effect.**

\[
\Delta Y_{\text{obs}}=Q\cdot D\cdot \Delta Y_{\text{true}}+\varepsilon
\]

\(Q\in[0,1]\) quality-of-delivery index, \(D\in[0,1]\) dose fidelity, \(\Delta Y\) in outcome units. **Breakdown:** as \(D\to 0\) or \(Q\) is unmeasured, \(\Delta Y_{\text{obs}}\) carries no lower bound on \(\Delta Y_{\text{true}}\); only an upper bound is recoverable. Since \(Q\) is reported in 11/12 ("Quality of delivery was reported in 11/12 studies (91.7%)") [PAGE 10] but \(D\) in 0/12, the product is unidentified for every study in the corpus.

**Operator 8 — Load ceiling under bodyweight-only prescription.** For a bodyweight movement, external load \(L_{\text{ext}}=0\), so

\[
\text{Overload}\in\{\Delta s,\ \Delta r,\ \Delta T_{\text{TUT}},\ \Delta \text{ROM},\ \Delta\text{unilateral}\}
\]

and joint torque is \(\tau=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}\), \(|\tau|=Fd\sin\theta\) in N·m. **Breakdown:** if the only overloadable variables listed are unreported, "progression" collapses to an assertion. Table 2 [PAGE 8] marks progression in four studies "\(+ (*)\)" — "Reported but not rationalised" — which is exactly this collapse.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The measurement apparatus used here was not built for rural oncology; it was built elsewhere and imported. Hoffman (2002) supplies the six-principle skeleton, Carroll et al. (2007) supply the five-element fidelity frame — the review is explicit that "all aspects of fidelity were predefined according to work by Carroll et al. (2007)" [PAGE 3] — and the exercise-oncology auditing lineage runs through Campbell et al. (2012), Winters-Stone et al. (2014), and Neil-Sztramko et al. (2019a; 2019b), each of which asked the same question of urban trial corpora and each of which found the same answer: principles are under-reported. The genealogy is therefore iterative and self-confirming: the instrument was calibrated on metropolitan RCTs, found them wanting, and is now pointed at a rural corpus that is smaller, more heterogeneous, and structurally less able to satisfy the instrument.

The retrocausal inversion is this: the rural setting does not merely *receive* a lower-fidelity literature; it *produces* the reporting deficit that the instrument then measures. As [PAGE 10] concedes, "given the reduced access to adequate facilities, equipment, and oncology-trained exercise professionals in comparison to metropolitan settings, accurate delivery and monitoring of exercise interventions is challenging, and is often reliant on self-reported measures – likely impacting the quality of reporting." The deficit is upstream of the manuscript. Acknowledging this does not excuse it — the same page argues "this does not warrant its exclusion from exercise interventions" — but it reframes the target of remediation from authorial diligence to infrastructural capacity.

**Multi-week traps.** Four are visible in the corpus and each is a different failure mode. (1) *The 12-week horizon trap.* Durations run 10–12 weeks (Wagoner 2024, Wagoner 2023, Marker, Gallant, Gell) up to 52 weeks (Gray), with one study "Not Fixed" (Hirko) and others at 24–32 weeks. Against Operator 5, a 12-week window with a plausible \(k\sim0.1\text{–}0.2\ \text{week}^{-1}\) yields \(kt\approx1.2\text{–}2.4\), which is only marginally sufficient to resolve a plateau; the minimum-effective-dose claim the review wants cannot be extracted from horizons shorter than the response time constant. (2) *The no-washout trap.* Reversibility requires a post-cessation window; only Marker measured after completion, so \(\lambda\) is unidentified in eleven studies. (3) *The optional-arm trap.* "Two of the 12 studies (16.7%) prescribed optional resistance training ... with no information provided on the adherence to the 'optional' FITT principles recommended" [PAGE 7] — an optional component makes the delivered dose a self-selected subsample, and no multi-week window recovers it. (4) *The completion-clock trap.* Wagoner et al. (2023) reports "Ret 81.4%; Adh 78.2%" and Wagoner et al. (2024) 80.1%/86.9% [PAGE 9], but these are whole-programme figures across a multimodal curriculum; the resistance-specific clock is never isolated, so a high adherence figure can coexist with near-zero resistance dose.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under resource constraint, the prescriber regresses to the habitual, lowest-equipment, lowest-cognitive-load option: bands, bodyweight circuits, mailed workbooks. The corpus documents this regression rather than merely implying it — Modesitt et al. (2021) delivered "Aerobic, RT with band on every other day with six exercises (squat, forward leg raises, side leg raises, backward leg raises, hip flexion, and knee flexion)" [PAGE 8], and two studies demoted resistance entirely to "Optional Light RT based on Strength and courage DVD" [PAGE 8]. The regression is rational at the level of the individual prescriber — it maximises the probability that *something* is delivered — and it is exactly what makes the dose unrecordable. The recursive loop is: constraint → simplification → loss of quantifiable load → loss of dose → inability to demonstrate effect → reduced institutional support → deeper constraint.

**Counter-trap.** The apparent escape is tele-supervision by qualified professionals, and the corpus contains its best instance: Wagoner et al. (2023) with "Qualified Exercise Professionals" achieving reach of "84.3% rural/remote" and adherence "78.2%" [PAGE 9]. But the counter-trap is reach-versus-completion substitution: Hirko et al. (2021) reports uptake of "0.59%" alongside adherence of 72.7%, and the review names the mechanism — "low health literacy, logistical barriers (e.g. transport), lack of referral or conflicting information provided by medical professionals, and fears and uncertainty can also contribute to reduced uptake" [PAGE 12]. A programme can post excellent completion among the tiny fraction who enter; the denominator, not the numerator, is where rural failure lives. Optimising adherence while reach collapses is the trap in its purest form.

**The clock.** Three clocks run simultaneously and the paper only instruments one. The *protocol clock* (10–52 weeks) is measured. The *participant clock* — fear-avoidance, exercise identity, the "fears and uncertainty" named above — is measured only as a static covariate, never as a trajectory. The *institutional clock* — workforce churn among "Qualified Exercise Professionals" and "Certified Cancer Exercise Trainers" — is not measured at all, though it is the binding constraint on any multi-year translation. A theory-of-mind read of the prescriber is that they anticipate the institutional clock (a trainer who will leave) and therefore choose prescriptions that a successor can plausibly continue; that anticipation is a hidden prior on every FITT decision and it systematically biases toward low-specificity, low-load, high-portability programmes. The review's recommendation set — TIDieR, CONSORT 2025, CERT [PAGE 12] — addresses the protocol clock only.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer 1 — Physical (neuromuscular).** A hypertrophy or strength claim is vetoed unless the prescribed external load exceeds the recruitment threshold sufficient to drive high-threshold motor units. Formally, with torque \(|\tau|=Fd\sin\theta\) (N·m), the claim \(\Delta\text{strength}>0\) requires a reported \(F\) or a reported proxy for it. The corpus reports neither: intensity is available only as an ordinal label in at most seven of twelve studies, and "none of these six studies explained how this intensity was determined (i.e., as a percentage of one repetition maximum (1RM), or ratings of perceived exertion (RPE))" [PAGE 7]. **Verdict: veto sustained** for any study prescribing bodyweight-only or band-only resistance where load, band tension, or tempo is unreported — the physical stimulus is not reconstructible.

**Layer 2 — Biomechanical (skeletal and tissue).** Progressive overload is a load-increasing operation; in a population with stage I–IV disease ("participants ranging from stage I-IV cancer, and from pre-treatment to survivorship" [PAGE 3]), the safe envelope depends on skeletal-metastasis status, bone density, arthralgia, neuropathy, and lymphedema risk. The extraction frame on [PAGE 3] contains no field for any of these, and none appears in Tables 1–4. Adverse events are reported in only two of the seven rows legible across [PAGE 5]–[PAGE 7] — Marker et al. (2025) "7 in 6 participants" and Wagoner et al. (2023) "1 mild." — with the remaining five printing "NR". The review itself notes "it is not clear if reporting quality impacted the outcomes and effectiveness of the interventions prescribed" [PAGE 13]. **Verdict: veto sustained** on any safety inference. A progression that is not rationalised — Table 2's "\(+ (*)\) – Reported but not rationalised" [PAGE 8] — cannot be adjudicated against a contraindication screen that was never reported.

**Layer 3 — Weather/geography.** Rurality enters as a transport and environment term, not a co-morbidity. The review names "larger distances to treatment" [PAGE 1] and "logistical barriers (e.g. transport)" [PAGE 12], and the delivery modalities that survive are the ones that remove the journey: telehealth (Gell, Marker), mailed workbooks and bands (Modesitt), online group sessions (Wagoner). But removal of the journey also removes the observation: a home-based session generates no load record, which is why the dose column is empty. Weather adds a further veto on RPE anchoring, since thermal and humidity load shift perceived exertion independently of external load — an RPE-anchored prescription would need environmental covariate control that no study reports. **Verdict: veto sustained** on any claim that home/remote delivery preserves prescription fidelity; it preserves *participation* while structurally degrading *measurability*.

**Composite rule.** A claim of the form "this intervention delivered a safe, effective, progressive resistance stimulus" requires simultaneously: (i) a numeric load or tension, (ii) a numeric sets × reps or time-under-tension trajectory, (iii) a contraindication screen, and (iv) an adverse-event count. No study in the corpus satisfies all four; the review's own summary — "No individual study included in this review reported on all core exercise prescription principles, FITT principles, or measures of fidelity" [PAGE 15] — is the reporting-level shadow of this four-part failure.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
