The deck sits in a lineage that runs through three distinct scientific traditions, and it borrows operators from two of them without reconciling them.

**Lineage 1: Bill James's Pythagorean expectation (Baseball Abstract, 1979–1983).** Originally exponent 2 on run differential; later work (Miller, 2007, using a Weibull-derived continuous model) showed the exponent is not a constant but a function of the league's mean scoring environment, and that 1.83 is the empirical baseball value. **Lineage 2: Wage-and-table econometrics.** The expense-performance relationship entered football analytics via Szymanski (2003) on promotion/relegation and wage structures, and was extended by Kuper & Szymanski's *Soccernomics* (2009), which established that wage bills explain the majority of season-to-season point variance in the Premier League. The deck's r = 0.734335 (R² = 0.539) is *below* the canonical 0.75–0.80 range for wage-based models, which is the deck's real finding and one it never names: its expense construction (weighted payroll + transfer spend) is a weaker predictor of points than a wage bill alone would be.

**Lineage 3: Luck-and-skill decomposition.** Mauboussin's *The Success Equation* (2012) formalized the observed SD, true-skill SD, and luck SD identity: σ²_obs = σ²_skill + σ²_luck.
