**Op. 1 — Intersection-over-Union as the per-frame tracking primitive.** [PAGE 5] defines:

$$\Phi(\Lambda_G, \Lambda_T) = \{\Phi_t\}_{t=1}^{N}, \qquad \varphi_t = \frac{\left| R_t^{G} \cap R_t^{T} \right|}{\left| R_t^{G} \cup R_t^{T} \right|}$$

Units: dimensionless; $\varphi_t \in [0,1]$. $R_t^G$ and $R_t^T$ are measured in px² pixel-counts. The paper declares *"an IoU above 0.5 is successful"* [PAGE 5]. Breakdown regime: when $|R_t^G \cap R_t^T| = 0$ (tracked player occluded, off-frame, or false-positive lock), $\varphi_t = 0$; the paper explicitly *assigns* $\varphi_t = 0$ when Faster-RCNN's confidence score $< 0.6$ [PAGE 9], which is a *decision rule*, not a measurement.

**Op. 2 — One-Pass Evaluation curve (OPE).** Implicit in [PAGE 6] *"the percentage of correct frames, correct meaning that their IoU is above a given threshold. The curve shows this percentage for different thresholds"*:

$$p(\tau) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\varphi_t \geq \tau\right], \qquad \tau \in [0,1]$$

Units: $p(\tau)$ is a dimensionless probability in $[0,1]$; $N$ is the number of hand-labeled frames. Breakdown: $N \to$ small (as here, $N = 200$ for the calibration subset per [PAGE 8]) increases the variance of $p(\tau)$ monotonically; the paper reports no confidence bands.

**Op. 3 — AUC as the OPE integral.** [PAGE 6] *"calculated by computing the integral of the OPE curve"*:

$$\mathrm{AUC} = \int_{0}^{1} p(\tau)\, d\tau$$

Units: dimensionless, $\mathrm{AUC} \in [0,1]$. Interpretation: average over thresholds of the fraction of frames whose IoU ≥ τ. Numerical anchors from the source: standalone Faster-RCNN $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.32$ [PAGE 9]; hybrid $\mathrm{AUC} \in \{0.610, 0.651, 0.660, 0.600\}$ per Table 2 [PAGE 17]; jump-engineered max $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.64$ at jump=3 [PAGE 19].

**Op. 4 — Real-time feasibility inequality (latency budget).** From [PAGE 8]:

$$\Delta t_{\text{frame}}(f) = \frac{1}{f} \quad\text{ms} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta t_{\text{frame}}(30) \approx 33.3\,\mathrm{ms},\;\; \Delta t_{\text{frame}}(60) = 16.67\,\mathrm{ms}$$

Measured processing time $\Delta t_{\text{FRN}} \approx 45\,\mathrm{ms}$ [PAGE 8]. The feasibility predicate is:

$$\text{feasible}(f) \;\triangleq\; \Delta t_{\text{FRN}} \leq \frac{1}{f} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f \leq \frac{1}{\Delta t_{\text{FRN}}} \approx 22.2\,\mathrm{Hz}$$

Singularity/breakdown regime: at $f = 60\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $\Delta t_{\text{FRN}}/\Delta t_{\text{frame}} = 45/16.67 \approx 2.70$ — the Faster-RCNN alone misses the deadline by 2.70×. Only the jump-staggering (Faster-RCNN at rate $1/j$) can bridge this gap.

**Op. 5 — Staggered execution cascade (amortized frame cost).** Derived from Figures 4–5 and Section 4 [PAGE 12–13]. If Faster-RCNN costs $T_F$, SSD costs $T_S$, KCF costs $T_K$ per invocation, and jump $= j$, the *expected per-frame processing time* across $j$ frames is:

$$\bar{T}(j) = \frac{T_F + (j-1)\,(T_S + T_K)}{j} = T_S + T_K + \frac{T_F - (T_S + T_K)}{j}$$

Units: ms frame⁻¹. As $j \to \infty$, $\bar{T} \to T_S + T_K$ — the *lower bound of throughput*, but accuracy collapses because semantic re-anchoring vanishes. As $j \to 1$, $\bar{T} \to T_F = 45\,\mathrm{ms}$, reproducing the Faster-RCNN infeasibility. The paper's choice $j = 3$ [PAGE 19, Figure 7] is the empirical optimum under the trade-off:

$$\arg\max_j\; \mathrm{AUC}(j) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \bar{T}(j) \leq \frac{1}{f_{\text{target}}}$$

At $j=3$, [PAGE 19] reports $\mathrm{AUC} = 0.64$ and $T$-per-frame *"considerably large and unstable."* At larger $j$, AUC decreases and time stabilizes. Breakdown: with $j$ finite, KCF drift accumulates between re-anchorings; the paper's 3-frame divergence rule [PAGE 14] is a sub-threshold correction that keeps $\varphi_t > 0.5$ in 80–90% of frames [PAGE 15].

**Op. 6 — SSD-as-supervisor hysteresis (the persistence threshold).** Defined operationally on [PAGE 14]: let $\delta_t = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\text{KCF}_t \text{ and }\text{SSD}_t \text{ diverge beyond IoU tolerance}\right] \in \{0,1\}$. Correction fires at the smallest $t$ such that $\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \delta_{t-k} = K$ with $K = 3$. For $K=1$ the system *"turn[s] the overall solution unstable"* [PAGE 14]; the paper does not state what happens for $K \geq 4$. Breakdown regime: if KCF is *correct* and SSD is *systematically biased* (e.g., during camera cut where SSD has not yet warmed up), $K=3$ will propagate a systematic SSD error into the tracker for 3 consecutive frames.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis-habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock.** The paper's runtime dynamics can be decomposed into three interlocking feedback loops:

**Loop 1 — KCF's own habit regression (positive feedback with a slow decay).** [PAGE 4] names the pathology: *"When the tracking is not precise, the appearance model uses a sub-optimal positive sample, which can degrade its accuracy over time and cause drift."* Formally, if KCF's estimated bounding box deviates by $\varepsilon_t$ at step $t$, the classifier's next update uses a corrupted positive sample with probability $\propto \varepsilon_t$, so $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{t+1} \mid \varepsilon_t] = \varepsilon_t + \alpha \varepsilon_t$ with $\alpha > 0$. This is a positive-feedback loop and it is *unstable without external regularization*. The paper's own definition, [PAGE 4 footnote]: *"the term is used to indicate the progressive loss of the tracked object due to error accumulation."*

**Loop 2 — the SSD counter-trap (negative feedback with a hysteresis dead-band).** SSD supplies a *counter-observation* each frame. But the naive counter-trap (correct on any discrepancy) is itself unstable — the paper's exact language [PAGE 14]: *"adjusting KCF when a discrepancy with SSD is detected for the first time proved to turn the overall solution unstable."* The recursion here is subtle: a correction at step $t$ alters KCF's positive sample at step $t+1$, which alters the divergence signal at $t+2$, etc. If SSD's noise floor $\sigma_S$ exceeds KCF's drift $\alpha \varepsilon_t$, the correction is worse than the drift. The 3-frame persistence window is the paper's *empirical dead-band*, effectively saying: "only let SSD dominate the error signal if it is persistent for ≥3 frames, because SSD's own variance is larger than the frame-to-frame drift increment."

**Loop 3 — the Faster-RCNN clock (long-period semantic re-anchoring).** The third loop runs at $1/j$ of the frame rate — every $j$-th frame the semantic floor is *reset*. [PAGE 14]: *"the role of the supervisor is played by the Faster-RCNN algorithm. However, this supervisor does not intervene only at the beginning of the tracking, but rather each jump frames."* This is a *clock* in the strict sense — a periodic correction that guarantees $\varepsilon_t$ cannot grow without bound, provided $j < \tau_{\text{drift}}$, the time-to-drift measured in frames. The paper does not publish $\tau_{\text{drift}}$ explicitly, but the AUC collapse at large $j$ in Figure 7 is the signature of $j$ exceeding $\tau_{\text{drift}}$.

**The theory-of-mind trap.** The algorithm effectively models three *agents* (Faster-RCNN, SSD, KCF), each with a distinct belief about the player's location. It then must adjudicate which belief is correct. The paper's decision rule (3-frame persistence) is *behavioural*, not *epistemic*: it treats persistence as a proxy for correctness. There is no Bayesian weighting of detector reliability per scene type (broadcast, crowd, replay). Under a *regime-shift counter-trap* — e.g., a wide-angle replay where SSD is systematically biased and KCF is only slowly drifting — persistence-of-divergence will misattribute the error and overwrite the *correct* KCF box with the *biased* SSD box.

**Clock audit.** The wall-clock feasibility of the whole loop is set by the tightest agent (Faster-RCNN, 45 ms) divided by the stretch factor $j$. The paper's empirical success at $j=3$ [PAGE 19, AUC=0.64] indicates that 3 is the largest $j$ at which Faster-RCNN still serves as a *truth anchor* rather than a *historical record*. Beyond $j=3$, the Faster-RCNN "belief" is cold, and SSR-KCF (Loop 2) is running without a prior — the reported degradation in AUC at larger $j$ is consistent with this.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical/occlusion vetoes.** [PAGE 2] enumerates the physical vetoes the tracker must survive: *"frequent camera movements and switches, partial and total occlusions of the tracked object, players of the same team with a very similar appearance to the tracked player, blurry images due to the codification algorithm, and video intervals where the tracked player is out of camera."* Each maps to a specific veto proof on the equation stack: total occlusion ⇒ $\varphi_t = 0$ for a contiguous run; out-of-camera ⇒ $p(\tau)$ collapses to the "no detection" mass. The paper's OPE curves in Figure 6 [PAGE 16] and the reported 80–90% fraction of frames above IoU 0.5 [PAGE 15] are the aggregate veto-survival rate.

**Biomechanical veto.** [PAGE 11]: Kalman Filtering *"failed to capture camera movements, or situations where players change of direction or speed up suddenly."* This is a *biomechanical* veto: the tracker must not assume locally smooth motion. The paper's design response is structural — KCF is a *pixel-level* correlator and carries no motion prior, meaning it does not need to estimate acceleration. The SSD layer absorbs larger jumps; the Faster-RCNN layer absorbs full frame-wide re-determination. In effect, the cascade trades biomechanical modelling for temporal redundancy.

**Weather/element veto.** The paper is silent on weather as such, but the codification-blur statement ([PAGE 2]) is a weather-adjacent veto: under poor lighting, rain on lens, or heavy compression artifacts, high-frequency image texture — which is exactly KCF's input signal — is destroyed. The paper offers no weather ablation and no fallback path. A tested counterfactual in the RLVR section below probes this.

**Cross-layer proof chain.** For each frame $t$, the layered adjudication is:

1. **Faster-RCNN** fires on $t \equiv 0 \pmod j$ (blue path, Fig. 4 [PAGE 13]); it *sets* the ROI.
2. **SSD** fires on the other $j-1$ frames (red path); it *refines* the ROI.
3. **KCF** fires every frame and produces the output; it is *supervised* by SSD via the 3-frame persistence rule.

The cross-layer veto is *sequential*: a failure at any layer is subsumed by the tolerance of the next. If Faster-RCNN misses (per [PAGE 9], ~50% of frames), the ROI persists from the last successful detection. If SSD is silenced by occlusion, the KCF continues on pixel-correlation, but is at risk of drift per [PAGE 4]. If KCF diverges >3 frames, SSD or (on the next tick) Faster-RCNN vetoes and re-anchors. **The unbounded-failure regime is exactly: Faster-RCNN misses for $\geq j$ frames AND SSD silenced by occlusion for the same span** — this is the only configuration in which no veto is triggered and KCF is unsupervised; the paper's 0–2 "Lost frames" numbers in Table 2 [PAGE 17] are the empirical frequency of exactly this configuration over the tested segments.

**Veto 2 — the null-result crop ablation (crop-size counterfactual).** Assertion to test: *crop size across the tested range has no material effect on AUC (ΔAUC < 0.03).* Source text [PAGE 19]: *"the results shown in Figure 8 for both the OPE curve and the time per frame indicate that the variation of this parameter does not have in practice a significant influence in none of both aspects."* **Prediction:** across whatever crop multipliers the authors used in Figure 8 (values not enumerated in text), $\max \mathrm{AUC} - \min \mathrm{AUC}$ will be less than 0.03, and the time-per-frame variation will not be statistically distinguishable from noise on the GTX 1080 Ti. **Falsifier:** if a wider crop sweep (e.g., 0.1× → 2.0× of detected box) shows ΔAUC > 0.05, the paper's null claim is a boundary-of-range artifact — the crop variation must be *inside* the tracker's tolerance band, not robust across arbitrary ranges. **Cross-check:** SSD resizes any crop to a fixed input shape; if SSD cannot distinguish tracked player from a teammate at 1.5× crop, precision must collapse — so a full-range falsification is highly likely.
