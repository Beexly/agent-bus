(2) Regression-under-crisis loop: when field markings are sparse or shadowed, the retrieval stage returns a neighbor outside LK's convergence basin; the method "regresses" to the prior mean rather than the true pose, which is why the fifth-column qualitative case degrades [PAGE 5]. (3) Clock loop: the retrieval-first design exists to satisfy a real-time constraint — "the running time is about 0.5 seconds/frame" [PAGE 6] — so accuracy is deliberately traded against a per-frame latency budget. The "counter-trap vs clock" tension is concrete: a denser database improves retrieval but raises query cost; the 100k-pose / 16-dim choice is the negotiated equilibrium. No literal opponent-modeling applies here, and I won't fabricate one.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/veto conditions that legitimately break the method, with proof sketches.

- Base-rigidity veto. The reduced model presupposes `φ' = −90°` and `|ρ| ≤ 0.1°`. If a camera base is not level, the "cancellation" of `S_{φ'}` by `Q_φ` [PAGE 3] is only first-order; the residual rotation `\Delta R` propagates into `H` and biases IoU. Proof: exact cancellation requires `Q_φ S_{φ'} = I`, which holds only on the measure-zero set `φ' = -90°`.
- Location-prior veto. `C ∼ N(μ,Σ)` with `μ` near the midline. Empirically demonstrated: beyond ≈5 m displacement IoU_part falls from ≥92% toward ≈70% at ≈10 m [PAGE 5]. The retrieval database has finite spatial support; outside it, nearest-neighbor returns an out-of-basin seed and LK cannot recover.
- Weather/visibility veto. The authors state plainly: "It fails when there is heavy rain/snow in the playing ground" [PAGE 6]. Proof by dependency: the entire pose estimate is conditioned on detected edge image `E`; if `E = ∅` (no reliable markings), `DT_T` is constant, LK has zero gradient, and the estimate collapses to the retrieved prior. Heavy shadow is named the dominant real-image failure [PAGE 5].
- Occlusion/coverage veto. When "the bottom touch lines are not visible" [PAGE 5], geometric constraint on the far field weakens and accuracy drops — a rank-deficiency of the correspondence set, not a tuning issue.

These are physical and informational, not beatable by hyperparameter search.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactuals, each tied to the paper's own ablations or stated future work, each with a falsifiable prediction.

1. Remove the refinement (LK + distance image). Ablation says IoU_part goes 94.5 → 91.5 (Δ 3.0) [PAGE 5, Table II]. Prediction: on *sparser-marking* data (volleyball is noted as harder in marking count [PAGE 5]), removing LK should cost *more* than 3.0 points because retrieval quantization error is uncorrected. Verifiable by re-running the volleyball set w/o LK warp.

2. Remove the segmentation GAN. Ablation: 94.5 → 90.2 (Δ 4.3) [PAGE 5]. Counterfactual veto scenario: apply the model to a non-grass foreground (ice hockey rink, indoor court). Prediction: the segmentation GAN's grassland prior fails, background lines leak in, and the IoU drop exceeds the 4.3 seen on soccer — because the "negative influence of background objects (e.g. white lines on commercial boards)" [PAGE 4] is exactly what segmentation was added to suppress.

3. Cross-sport transfer without re-estimating the prior. The three-parameter reduction is *only valid* under soccer-specific `μ, Σ` and pan/tilt/focal ranges. Prediction: keep `N = 100{,}000` but plug in basketball/ice-hockey priors (they name these as future work [PAGE 6]); if `φ'` is not −90° or `C` is off-midline, mean IoU_part falls below the 89.4 IoU_whole reported for soccer, and possibly below the DSM baseline of 83 [PAGE 5]. Verifiable by re-sampling the engine with new priors and re-running; it directly tests whether the reparameterization or merely the tuned prior carried the accuracy.

Common thread: all three are cheap to falsify on existing data or one new dataset, and all three probe whether the paper's gains are architectural or prior-tuning artifacts.
