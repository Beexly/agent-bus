<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Citation anchors are exact page markers from the supplied source; verbatim quotes in quotation marks.

**1. The process and its index (definitional).** From [PAGE 42]: "A time series is deﬁned as a sequence of data reading in successive order and can be taken on any variable that changes over time." Formally a real-valued stochastic process $\{X_t\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}$ on $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$, with the ordering of $t$ carrying structural meaning per [PAGE 43]: "the ordering of the data points in time matters." Units of $X_t$ are whatever the measured variable carries; for the Apple example the source chooses log-returns ([PAGE 14] Fig 1.1, "log returns for Apple Inc."), which are dimensionless:
$$r_t = \ln\!\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}, \qquad P_t>0.$$
Breakdown/singularity: $r_t$ is undefined at $P_{t-1}=0$; the log transform assumes strictly positive prices.

**2. White-noise innovation (second-moment structure).** [PAGE 14] Fig 1.6: "White noise with zero mean, constant variance, and zero correlation." Formally $\{\varepsilon_t\}\sim\mathrm{WN}(0,\sigma^2)$:
$$\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t]=0,\quad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t)=\sigma^2\ (\text{constant, units }[X]^2),\quad \gamma(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-h})=0\ \ \forall h\neq 0.$$
Information content: for a *Gaussian* white-noise process this is the maximum-entropy distribution at fixed variance, differential entropy $h(\varepsilon)=\tfrac12\ln(2\pi e\,\sigma^2)$ nats. Assumption to flag: "zero correlation" is weaker than independence; only the Gaussian case upgrades WN to i.i.d.

**3. Simple moving average.** Implied by [PAGE 30] "moving averages and exponential smoothing let us get a ﬁrst approach at forecasting." Trailing window $m$:
$$\hat{X}_t=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}X_{t-k}.$$
For WN input this reduces variance to $\sigma^2/m$ and introduces a phase lag of $(m-1)/2$ periods — the classic smoothing-vs-lag trade. Singular regime: $m=1$ gives no smoothing ($\hat X_t=X_t$); $m\to\infty$ diverges to the process mean under stationarity.

**4. Exponential smoothing (`ewm`).** [PAGE 24] Table 1.4: "Parameters specifying the decay applied to an exponential smoothing calculation with ewm." With smoothing factor $\alpha\in(0,1]$:
$$S_t=\alpha X_t+(1-\alpha)S_{t-1},\qquad S_0=X_0.$$
Pandas 0.25 ([PAGE 28], "Pandas - 0.25") fixes the decay parametrizations (weights dimensionless, $\alpha$ dimensionless):
$$\alpha=\frac{1}{1+c}=\frac{2}{s+1},\qquad \alpha = 1-e^{-\ln 2/h_{\text{HL}}}=1-2^{-1/h_{\text{HL}}},$$
where $c$ = center-of-mass, $s$ = span, $h_{\text{HL}}$ = half-life (units: periods). Breakdown: $\alpha\to 1 \Rightarrow S_t\to X_t$ (no smoothing, forecast variance $\to\sigma^2$); $\alpha\to 0^+ \Rightarrow$ effective memory $c=(1-\alpha)/\alpha\to\infty$ (sluggish, initialization-dominated). With `adjust=False` the geometric weights $\alpha(1-\alpha)^k$ sum to 1 only as $k\to\infty$, so early-sample forecasts are biased by $S_0$ — a genuine singularity at the series start.

**5. Stationarity (weak/covariance).** Named in [PAGE 8] §1.4.3 (p.39) and §1.4.4 "Determining Stationarity" (p.42).
$$\mathbb{E}[X_t]=\mu\ (\text{const}),\quad \operatorname{Var}(X_t)=\gamma(0)<\infty,\quad \gamma(h)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t-h})\ \text{independent of }t.$$
Units: $\mu$ in $[X]$, $\gamma(h)$ in $[X]^2$.

**6. Autoregression / ARMA.** [PAGE 8] §1.5 "Autoregressive Models" (p.51); [PAGE 15] Fig 1.15 "Prediction for the sunspot activity using an ARMA(9, 0) model." General ARMA$(p,q)$:
$$X_t=c+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\phi_i X_{t-i}+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.$$
The book's ARMA(9,0) is a pure AR(9): $q=0$. AR(1) special case $X_t=c+\phi X_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$ has $\mu=c/(1-\phi)$, $\gamma(0)=\sigma^2/(1-\phi^2)$, ACF $\rho(h)=\phi^{|h|}$. Stationarity condition: all roots of $\phi(z)=1-\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_i z^i$ satisfy $|z|>1$; invertibility: all roots of $\theta(z)=1+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j z^j$ satisfy $|z|>1$.

**7. Spectral claim.** [PAGE 14] Fig 1.9: "a maximum in activity occurs approximately every 11 years." Periodogram
$$I(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi n}\Big|\sum_{t=1}^{n}X_t e^{-i\omega t}\Big|^2,$$
peak frequency $f\approx 1/11\ \text{yr}^{-1}=0.0909\ \text{yr}^{-1}$, angular $\omega=2\pi f\approx 0.571\ \text{rad yr}^{-1}$. Breakdown: $I(\omega)$ is an *inconsistent* estimator ($\operatorname{Var}[I(\omega)]\not\to 0$), so an apparent peak can be spurious without Blackman–Tukey smoothing — see the RLVR section.

**8. Spurious-regression regime.** [PAGE 8] §1.4.1 "Regression. . . (Not) a Good Idea?" (p.34). Regressing one random walk on another inflates $R^2$ and $t$-statistics while residuals stay non-stationary (Durbin–Watson $\to 0$). This is the formal reason the chapter title question is answered "not a good idea": OLS i.i.d. assumptions are violated by autocorrelated, unit-root residuals.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Honest reframing: the foundry's "retro-causal genealogy" is a betting-match artifact with no referent here. The genuine scientific lineage of the *exact* operators this preview names is, however, unusually clean and worth stating — and it is not metaphorical.

The book's headline worked example is sunspots ([PAGE 14] Fig 1.9, [PAGE 15] Figs 1.10/1.13/1.15). That is a direct descendant of Udny Yule's 1927 paper "On a Method of Investigating Periodicities in Disturbed Series, with special reference to Wolfer's sunspot numbers," which introduced autoregressive modelling precisely to explain a quasi-periodic ~11-year sunspot signal as the output of a *stochastic difference equation* rather than a deterministic cycle. Yule's AR(2) with complex-conjugate roots produces pseudo-periodic oscillation; the preview's ARMA(9,0) on the same data ([PAGE 15] Fig 1.15) is the modern, higher-order descendant of that idea.

Second lineage node: Eugen Slutzky (1937) proved that summing/smoothing an i.i.d. series with a moving average can *manufacture* apparent periodicity out of pure randomness. This is the multi-week trap encoded in the book's own pairing of moving averages ([PAGE 8] §1.4.2) with a spectral peak: a smoothed series can display a "cycle" that is an artifact of the filter, not the phenomenon. Any analysis that reports the "11-year cycle" from smoothed data without testing against Slutzky's null is repeating a 90-year-old error.

Third node: Box & Jenkins (1970) systematized AR/ARMA/ARIMA identification via the ACF/PACF — exactly the diagnostic pair the book plots ([PAGE 15] Figs 1.13–1.14, "Autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation for the sunspot dataset"). Granger & Newbold (1974) then supplied the spurious-regression warning that the chapter flags on [PAGE 8] §1.4.1. The intellectual arc Yule→Slutzky→Box–Jenkins→Granger–Newbold is the actual ancestry of every operator this preview commits to; it is a chain of *identifiability and breakdown* results, not fate or prediction.

"Physical/biomechanical/weather veto" has no referent. Reinterpreted honestly as *mathematical veto conditions* — the provable regimes in which the preview's own methods are invalid and a forecast must be discarded. Each is a proof, not an opinion.

Veto 1 — Unit root. If the ADF/KPSS check ([PAGE 8] §1.4.4 "Determining Stationarity") fails to reject a unit root, ARMA forecasting on levels is invalid. Proof: for two independent random walks the OLS $t$-statistic diverges like $\sqrt{n}$ while the true coefficient is 0; Durbin–Watson $\to 0$. Vetoed; model differences, not levels.

Proof: $\operatorname{Var}[I(\omega)]\to \sigma^4$ (does not vanish) as $n\to\infty$, so a single-estimate peak is not a consistent estimator of spectral density; also, moving-average smoothing injects spectral weight (Slutzky). Vetoed unless a smoothed/consistent estimator and a WN-null test agree.
