$$h_{xy} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-c_x)^2 + (y-c_y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$
Units: $x,y,c_x,c_y$ are pixel coordinates on the low-resolution heatmap; $\sigma$ is a length in the same pixel units, "adaptive to the size of the bounding box" ([PAGE 6]); $h_{xy}\in(0,1]$ dimensionless. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the tracker as an agent with priors, then model the adversary (sports motion) that punishes those priors.

Crisis-habit regression: when a tracked player decelerates, cuts, or is occluded (the "crisis"), ByteTrack/OC-SORT regress to their trained habit — extrapolate position with a linear Kalman state $\mathbf{x}=[c_x,c_y,s,r,\dot c_x,\dot c_y]$ ([SORT convention]). Under high jerk the innovation grows, the gate rejects the true detection, and identity is lost; the paper shows the consequence: pure-IoU (no prediction) 71.5 HOTA vs Kalman-predicted 64.1 ([PAGE 8], Table 6), a 7.4-point regression caused purely by the motion-model habit.

Counter-trap: MixFormer supplies an orthogonal, appearance-conditioned belief. Its search-region heatmap [Eq. 2] gives a likelihood that does not depend on velocity, so it survives the exact frames where the Kalman habit breaks. This is why the fusion Eq. (1) helps most where motion is least trustworthy (football, α=0.3).

Clock: temporal resolution is fixed at "720P and 25 FPS" ([PAGE 3]), i.e. $\Delta t = 0.04$ s between frames. Two clock-driven mechanisms matter — the "maximum sample interval is set to 10" and post-hoc "linear interpolation ... with maximum gap set to 20" ([PAGE 6]). Interpolation is itself a constant-velocity assumption applied offline; it can silently repair short gaps (flattering HOTA) or bridge two identities across a 20-frame occluded switch (inflating ID-switches). The theory-of-mind read: the baseline believes the world is smooth on the 0.04 s clock; the adversary is sub-second non-smooth motion; the counter-trap buys a non-temporal cue; and the offline clock (interpolation) is a hidden third player that can mask or manufacture association errors.
