This regression violates the passage’s assumption that equivalence is automatic once superposition is available; instead, the equivalence judgment collapses into a univariate form-length comparison under load.

The counter-trap is that the experimenter may interpret regression as a perceptual deficit in the “retinal compass” when it is actually a strategic withdrawal: the subject no longer trusts the equivalence instruction and defaults to a safer, monocular, within-fixation judgment. Distinguishing these requires process data, not just binary equivalence responses.

The clock variable is the post-saccadic decision window. Trans-saccadic memory and receptive-field remapping operate on a scale of roughly 100–300 ms after saccade termination. If the equivalence judgment latency exceeds this window, the superposition operator \(\Sigma_E\) cannot bind \(\gamma_1\) and \(\gamma_2\) before the peripheral trace decays. In that regime the judgment reverts to a sequential, memory-based comparison, which is precisely the regime where James’s claim that movements “compare” retinal streaks becomes temporally implausible without an explicit memory buffer.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The retina is not a rigid Euclidean plane. Photoreceptor density and receptive-field size vary with eccentricity, so a peripheral trace \(AB\) and a central trace \(ab\) are sampled by different neural populations. The compass metaphor assumes \(d(p_i,p_j)=C\) is translation-invariant, but cortical magnification means equal retinal distances do not correspond to equal neural distances. Veto condition: if eccentricity exceeds the range over which sampling is approximately uniform (roughly beyond \(10^\circ\)–\(15^\circ\) for fine form), the equivalence judgment \(AB\sim ab\) cannot rest on a single invariant metric.

Biomechanical layer: Extraocular muscles are constrained by Listing’s law and by pulley connective tissues that limit torsion during saccades. If a subject has a paresis or mechanical restriction, the eye cannot apply “different parts of the retinal surface to the same objective thing” in the clean superposition James describes. Veto condition: any oculomotor plant pathology that prevents accurate foveation of the same object after a saccade breaks \(\exists E\in\mathcal{E}\), invalidating the equivalence operator.

Weather/environmental layer: Luminance and atmospheric scattering alter the effective point-spread function on the retina. Under scotopic or low-contrast conditions, the peripheral trace \(AB\) may be blurred relative to the central trace \(ab\) even when both fall on intact retina. Veto condition: when luminance falls below photopic thresholds or when veiling glare differentially affects peripheral versus central loci, the visual input no longer satisfies the premise that the same object produces comparable traces; equivalence judgments will then reflect optical degradation, not retinal geometry.

Adjudication: The James–Helmholtz equivalence claim holds only within the intersection of (i) intact oculomotor plant, (ii) corresponding retinal loci with matched acuity, (iii) photopic illumination equalizing trace quality, and (iv) directed attention to the trans-saccadic comparison. Outside this conjunctive regime, the cross-layer veto applies and the passage’s universal-sounding claim about spatial determination is falsified for that condition.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Oculomotor blockade. Independent variable: temporary chemical denervation of extraocular muscles (e.g., botulinum toxin) reducing saccadic amplitude to near zero. Dependent variable: proportion of correct equivalence judgments \(AB\sim ab\) across peripheral-central pairs. Verifiable criterion: if accuracy falls to chance (\(p=0.5\)) or requires latencies \(>2\) s compared to baseline \(<1\) s, the claim that eye movements merely “educate” but do not constitute cross-locus equivalence is vetoed. Prediction: equivalence judgments collapse because \(\mathcal{E}\) no longer contains a nontrivial \(E\) that maps \(\gamma_1\) to \(\gamma_2\); superposition cannot occur.

Scenario 2: Central scotoma induction. Independent variable: laser photocoagulation creating a dense scotoma on the central retinal tract in one eye. Dependent variable: binary equivalence response when the object trace \(ab\) is placed on the scotoma versus matched intact retina. Verifiable criterion: if judgment accuracy on scotoma trials drops below \(0.6\) while intact-retina trials remain above \(0.9\), the claim that equivalence is independent of the “feel” of individual retinal pictures is vetoed. Prediction: the subject cannot equate \(AB\) and \(ab\) when \(ab\) falls on the scotoma, demonstrating that local retinal quality is constitutive, not merely educative.

Scenario 3: Reversing-prism adaptation. Independent variable: two weeks of wear of reversing prisms that invert the retinal image. Dependent variable: the sign and magnitude of the measured equivalence constant \(C\) before, during, and after adaptation. Verifiable criterion: if the point of subjective equivalence shifts systematically (e.g., peripheral lines are now judged equal to central lines of opposite orientation) and does not return to baseline within one week of prism removal, the inference from Le Conte’s inter-individual divergence to fixed “organic identity” is vetoed. Prediction: the compass constant \(C\) is plastic and recalibrates over weeks, showing that what James treats as an organic given is actually a long-term sensorimotor set-point subject to counterfactual intervention.
