Veto Scenario 2: Introspective report–behavior decoupling in humans.
Design: Human subjects perform a two-alternative forced-choice discrimination while providing concurrent introspective Protokolls (confidence ratings, sensation reports) on each trial, mirroring the Wundtian method criticized on [PAGE 1].  
Behaviorist assumption: Introspective reports are “dumb” epiphenomena carrying no information about trial-by-trial accuracy.  
Cognitive alternative: Reports covary with accuracy because subjects have access to decision-related evidence.  
Veto statistic: Mutual information \(I[\text{Report}_t; \text{Accuracy}_t]\) [bits], computed trial-by-trial and compared against a trial-shuffled null distribution.  
Verification: Shuffle report labels across trials 10,000 times to generate the null; reject behaviorist epiphenomenalism if observed \(I\) exceeds the 95th percentile of the null.  
Prediction: Observed \(I \geq 0.5\) bits, whereas the shuffled null centers near 0 bits. A significant positive \(I\) vetoes the behaviorist assumption that conscious reports are behaviorally inert [PAGE 1].

Veto Scenario 3: Expectancy violation during extinction.
Design: Rats are trained to run a rewarded path to a goal box. On extinction day, reward is removed and a previously blocked shortcut is opened.  
Behaviorist null: Responding extinguishes monotonically as reinforcement is withheld; no goal-directed rerouting should occur because no new reinforcement is delivered.  
Cognitive alternative: Rats use their cognitive map and expectancy of the goal to switch to the shortcut despite non-reward on the original path.  
Veto statistic: Path-switch probability \(P_{\text{switch}}(t)\) as a function of extinction trial \(t\), measured from video tracking.  
Verification: Fit a hazard model to \(P_{\text{switch}}(t)\); test for an extinction-onset spike using a change-point analysis at \(t = t_{\text{ext}}\).  
Prediction: \(P_{\text{switch}}(t)\) spikes immediately after extinction onset and decays with a time constant of roughly 5–10 trials. A spike in goal-directed switching during non-reward vetoes the behaviorist law of effect as a complete account of behavior.
