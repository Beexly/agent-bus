**Triplet loss (sample), eq. 1 [PAGE 4]:**
$$L_T(A)=\big[m + D_{A,P} - D_{A,N}\big]_+,\quad [W]_+=\max(0,W)$$
Units: $m, D \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ (distance units in $\mathbb{R}^{512}$). Assumption: a valid hardest positive $P$ (same id) and hardest negative $N$ (different id) exist within the batch. **Breakdown regime:** if a batch contains $<2$ ids or a single sample per id, no valid $(P,N)$ pair exists and $L_T$ is undefined for that anchor.

**Batch triplet loss, eq. 2 [PAGE 4]:** $L_T=\sum_{A\in B}[m+D_{A,P}-D_{A,N}]_+$. Gradient saturates to $0$ once the margin is satisfied ($D_{A,N}-D_{A,P}\ge m$) — the standard triplet "dead zone."

**Triplet-centroid loss, eq. 3 [PAGE 5]:**
$$L_{TC}(A)=\big[m + D_{A,\text{CentroidP}} - D_{A,\text{CentroidN}}\big]_+$$
Assumption: centroids estimated from finite samples. Under scarce data, $\mathrm{Var}(\text{Centroid})\sim \sigma^2/K$ is large, injecting noise into $D_{A,\text{CentroidP}}$; per-sample hinge then amplifies that noise.

**Cluster centroids, eq. 4–5 [PAGE 6]:**
$$C_{\text{cluster-I}}(M_i)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{J,\,y(J)=M_i}F_J,\qquad C_{\text{cluster-II}}(M_i)=\frac{1}{K\!\cdot\!M-K}\sum_{J,\,y(J)\ne M_i}F_J$$
**Singularity:** cluster-II has $K\!\cdot\!M-K=K(M-1)$ members. At $M=1$ the denominator is $0$ → division-by-zero; the loss is only defined for $M\ge 2$. Cluster-I is well-posed for any $K\ge 1$.

**Centroid loss (per id and per batch), eq. 6–7 [PAGE 6]:**
$$L_{\text{Centroid}}(M_i)=\big\|C_{\text{cluster-I}}(M_i)-C_{\text{cluster-II}}(M_i)\big\|_2,\qquad L_{\text{Centroid}}=\sum_{M_i}L_{\text{Centroid}}(M_i)$$
Units: $L_2$ norm in $\mathbb{R}^{512}$. **Lower breakdown:** $L_{\text{Centroid}}\to 0$ only when the intra-id and extra-id centroids coincide — i.e., degenerate/collapsed embeddings, which is the failure the loss is designed to prevent. **Non-identifiability caveat:** because cluster-II pools *all* other ids, a dominant majority team shifts $C_{\text{cluster-II}}$ toward that team, so the effective repulsion is team-weighted, not uniform per rival id.

**Composite objective, eq. 8 [PAGE 7]:**
$$L=\alpha L_T+\beta L_C+\gamma L_{\text{Centroid}},\quad \alpha=0.9,\ \beta=0.5,\ \gamma=0.5$$
$L_C$ is the classification (cross-entropy) term. Weights "set empirically" ([PAGE 7]); no sensitivity curve is reported — a documented gray zone. Training: 40 epochs, best-checkpoint reporting ([PAGE 7]); optimizers ADAM (CNN) and ADAMW with 3000-iteration warmup + cosine annealing (transformers).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is a three-parent cross. Parent 1 is Hermans et al. 2017, "In defense of the triplet loss," [PAGE 10 ref 10], which introduced BATCH HARD — the very $K\!\times\!M$ sampling the paper modifies. Parent 2 is the centroid-retrieval school: Wieczorek et al. 2021 "On the unreasonable effectiveness of centroids" ([PAGE 11 ref 24], which supplies eq. 3's triplet-centroid form) and Wang et al. 2019's centroid metric learning for speaker recognition ([PAGE 11 ref 20]). Parent 3 is the sports-re-id vacuum itself: the author states "Research on re-id for sports is very limited" and that the only near-neighbor is TGC20ReID ([PAGE 11 ref 17], long-distance running) ([PAGE 3]). The backbones trace separately — ResNet ([PAGE 10 ref 8]), OSNet/omni-scale (Zhou et al., refs 33/34), and the transformer triad ViT ([PAGE 10 ref 6]), DeiT ([PAGE 11 ref 19]), TransReID ([PAGE 10 ref 9]).

The multi-week trap is structural, not incidental. Because "player identity labels ... do not hold across actions" ([PAGE 6]), any engineer who spends weeks tuning cross-action retrieval is optimizing a target the benchmark scores as zero — the metric silently redefines the problem to within-action matching. The second trap is the leaderboard delta trap: the SoccerNet baseline ResNet50 already jumps from "an mAP of 57.40 and a R1 of 45.89" to "70.3 / 61.2" via "just adjusting the batchsize and a few hyperparameters" ([PAGE 7]). That +12.9 mAP from tuning alone means raw leaderboard movement conflates sampling/loss innovation with hyperparameter luck unless the ablation ladder (Table 2) is held fixed — which this paper commendably does.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model three agents. **Crisis-regression agent** (the incumbent field): under deadline pressure it defaults to the SOTA recipe — triplet or triplet-centroid loss with random sampling — because that is the low-variance, literature-sanctioned move. Prediction of its behavior: it reports marginal gains and attributes them to backbone swaps. **Counter-trap agent** (this paper): it reads the "few samples per id" ([PAGE 5]) constraint as the binding one and swaps the per-sample hinge for a cluster-mean objective, exactly inverting the crisis-regression default. **Clock agent:** the challenge imposes two clocks — the 40-epoch training budget and the submission cap ("a hard limit on the number of submissions," [PAGE 7]) — which is why only the single best ViT-B/16 config is evaluated on the sequestered split.

The recursive subtlety: the counter-trap agent's win is partly *forced by* the clock, not purely by insight. With few samples per id and a fixed epoch budget, the triplet dead-zone (eq. 2 saturating at margin) wastes gradient, whereas the centroid loss (eq. 6) produces nonzero, low-variance signal on nearly every batch. So the correct theory-of-mind read is that limited-data + submission-clock jointly make the centroid loss the dominant strategy; a data-rich agent under no clock would rationally prefer the triplet-centroid loss the paper rejects. Anyone copying this paper into a surveillance-scale regime is mis-modeling the opponent — they would import a solution optimized for scarcity into an abundance regime where its advantage inverts.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather veto, adjudicated against the benchmark's actual scope:

1. **Weather/lighting veto — NOT EXERCISABLE.** Because "only samples within the same action are matched against each other" ([PAGE 6]) and action/replay frames "represent roughly the same timestamp" ([PAGE 4]), every query–gallery pair shares the same weather, floodlight state, and pitch. The benchmark therefore *cannot* produce a cross-weather negative; a rain-vs-clear generalization claim is unfalsifiable on this data. Verdict: no weather veto can be proven, and none can be disproven — the layer is simply absent.

2. **Occlusion/biomechanical veto — PARTIALLY SURVIVED.** The method is explicitly tested on occlusion: "our approach correctly identifies a player from different viewpoints even in the presence of occlusions" (Fig. 1c/1d, [PAGE 2]), on partial bodies ("only a small part of the player," Fig. 1a/1b), on kit-number location shifts ("the same number on the jersey ... and in the shorts," Fig. 2a, [PAGE 9]), and on pose differences ("subtle differences in the poses of the legs, despite all the players wearing the same white jersey," Fig. 2b, [PAGE 9]). These are genuine cross-viewpoint biomechanical variations and the method survives them. Verdict: pose/occlusion veto is *not* sustained by the evidence.

3. **Intra-team kit-similarity veto — SURVIVED BY CONSTRUCTION.** The central physical confound (same jersey) is precisely what hierarchical sampling is designed to defeat: aligning the training competitor set to the inference competitor set ([PAGE 4]). Table 2 shows the largest gains exactly here (ResNet +11.5 mAP, [PAGE 8]).

Residual physical veto that *does* bite: the assumed-solved detector. "A detection can contain multiple players with occlusions" ([PAGE 4]) and "person detection is beyond the scope of this paper" ([PAGE 4]). Any real deployment inherits upstream detector error the paper never prices in. This is the one physical-layer veto that survives adjudication.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable, falsifiable veto scenarios with concrete predictions (all numbers from Tables 2–3):

1. **Centroid-value veto (Table 3, [PAGE 9]).** Counterfactual: does triplet-centroid ($L_{TC}$) contribute meaningfully once triplet loss is present? Verified data — ViT-B/16: triplet-only 81.4 mAP → triplet+$L_{TC}$ 81.7 (**+0.3**), whereas triplet-only → triplet+centroid 86.0 (**+4.6**). *Prediction:* on any SoccerNet-scale set (≤ a few hundred samples/id), $L_{TC}$ marginal value stays under +0.5 mAP; adding $L_{TC}$ on top of centroid should be neutral-to-negative (confirmed: 86.0 → 85.8, [PAGE 9]). Veto holds: $L_{TC}$ is a near-null operator in the scarce-data regime.

2. **CNN/transformer split veto (Table 2, [PAGE 8]).** Counterfactual: does hierarchical sampling help without centroid loss uniformly? Data refutes uniformity — transformers gain (DeiT-Tiny +7.3 mAP: 73.2→80.5) but CNNs lose (ResNet −3.6: 70.3→66.7; OSNet −0.6: 76.4→75.8). *Prediction:* the split correlates with backbone inductive bias — CNNs already encode locality/appearance priors that random sampling's cross-team variety was reinforcing, so removing that variety hurts them; transformers, lacking those priors, benefit from the aligned distribution. Falsifiable prediction: a stronger CNN (e.g., a higher-accuracy ResNet variant) should show a *smaller* negative swing than ResNet50, since the effect tracks "weakest of the five" ([PAGE 8]).

3. **Batch-composition singularity veto (eq. 4–5, [PAGE 6]).** Counterfactual: can the loss be driven to $M=1$? *Prediction:* any hierarchical configuration that collapses to a single id per batch (e.g., a tiny action with one identity, Level O/I on sparse actions, [PAGE 5]) makes cluster-II's denominator $K(M-1)=0$ → the loss is undefined and must be guarded/skipped. Verifiable failure mode: on the smallest actions in the 340,993-thumbnail corpus ([PAGE 6]), expect dropped/degenerate batches unless $M\ge 2$ is enforced. This is a structural, testable breakdown, not a tuning artifact.

Meta-prediction across all three: the reported headline ("mAP by 7 - 11.5 and ... R1 by 8.8 - 14.9," [PAGE 1]) is internally consistent — recomputing from Table 2 gives per-network deltas of exactly mAP $\in[7.0,11.5]$, R1 $\in[8.8,14.9]$ (OSNet 7.0/8.8 floor, ResNet 11.5/14.9 ceiling). The claim survives arithmetic audit; the vulnerability is scope (within-action, detection-assumed), not the numbers.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
