<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
**1. Possession-normalized scoring (points-per-possession, PPP).** Football scoring is a renewal process on possessions, not a homogeneous Poisson in points. Let $N$ = possessions per team per game, derived from time-of-possession (TOP). With Baylor TOP 30:35 and TCU 31:24 [PAGE 7], total game clock 60:00 implies a possession split near 12 vs 11 at an assumed mean drive length $\bar\tau \approx 2.6\ \text{min}$.
$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{PTS}_i] = N_i \cdot \text{PPP}_i, \qquad \text{PPP}_i \in [0,\,7]\ \text{[points}\cdot\text{possession}^{-1}\text{]}.
$$
Raw: $\text{PPP}_{\text{BU}} \approx 34.8/12 \approx 2.9$; $\text{PPP}_{\text{TCU}} \approx 25.4/11 \approx 2.3$. These are consistent with the yards-per-play gap: $2307/404 = 5.71\ \text{yd}\cdot\text{play}^{-1}$ (Baylor) versus $1633/361 = 4.52$ allowed [PAGE 34].

**2. Opponent-adjustment (log-linear) and its singularity.** The standard multiplicative form
$$
\widehat{\text{PTS}}_{\text{BU}} = \text{PPP}_{\text{BU}}\cdot N \cdot \frac{\text{PPP}^{\text{allow}}_{\text{TCU}}}{\text{PPP}_{\text{lg}}}
$$
is scale-invariant only if $\text{PPP}_{\text{lg}}$ (league baseline, **not** in the corpus) is correctly specified. **Breakdown regime:** as $\text{PPP}_{\text{lg}}\to 0$ the estimator diverges; more practically, when SOS is mis-specified (Baylor's FCS/SBC padding), this over-corrects and can push $\widehat{\text{PTS}}_{\text{BU}}$ below 17, which is implausible given the Auburn/P4 sample. I therefore bound the projection between the raw and the over-corrected value.

**3. Margin as a Gaussian and its tails.** Let $M = \text{PTS}_{\text{BU}} - \text{PTS}_{\text{TCU}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_M,\sigma_M^2)$. For CFB I adopt $\sigma_M \approx 16$ points (wide, given $n=5$). Decompose
$$
\mu_M = \underbrace{\beta_0}_{\text{HFA (Waco, }\approx +3.5)} + \beta_1\Delta\text{PPP} + \beta_2\Delta\text{TOV} + \beta_3\Delta\text{explosive}.
$$
Plugging a modest home-adjusted efficiency edge, a $+3$ turnover edge ([PAGE 5]), and Baylor's explosive-play rate (75- and 63-yard TD runs, [PAGE 42]) against TCU's negative recent turnover/error signal, yields $\mu_M \approx +6$ to $+9$. **Singularity:** the Gaussian is invalid in the garbage-time regime (a 4-TD lead inflates both teams' counting stats, biasing $\mu_M$); I therefore cap the projection and rely on the *pre-garbage* first-three-quarters data.

**4. Win probability (logistic) and its boundary degeneracy.**
$$
\Pr(\text{BU win}) = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\mu_M}{\sigma_M}\right)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\mu_M/\sigma_M}}.
$$
With $\mu_M=+7,\ \sigma_M=16$: $\Pr \approx \sigma(0.44) \approx 0.61$. With $\mu_M=+6$: $\approx 0.59$. **Breakdown:** as $|\mu_M/\sigma_M|\to\infty$, $\Pr\to\{0,1\}$ and the logit is singular; small-$\mu_M$ games are decision-dominated by turnovers, which this linear-in-$\mu$ form understates.

**5. Turnover count (Poisson).** Takeaways $\sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_{TO})$. Baylor $\lambda_{TO}\approx 2.0\,\text{game}^{-1}$ (10 in 5, [PAGE 5]); giveaways $\approx 1.4\,\text{game}^{-1}$. Net-margin swing value $\approx 4$ points per +1 turnover (corpus-consistent with [PAGE 3] "Baylor fell behind 42-21 … a trio of interceptions clinched a 42-36 win" and [PAGE 20] "119 takeaways … and 99 giveaways … in 78 games").

**6. Bayesian shrinkage.** With $n=5$, posterior $\theta_{\text{post}} = w\,\hat\theta_{\text{ML}} + (1-w)\,\theta_{\text{prior}}$; the weight $w=\frac{n}{n+k}$ is small, so the estimate is prior-dominated. This is the *honest* regime: I refuse to treat 5 games as a settled mean.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
