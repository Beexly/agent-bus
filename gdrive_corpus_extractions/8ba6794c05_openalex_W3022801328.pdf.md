- The headline "~71%" detection rate ([PAGE 11]) is not the arithmetic mean of the per-camera true rates (mean = 75.9%); the aggregation rule is unstated.
- Equation (6) adds a pixel²-scaled residual (normalized by $(\sum_p A_{pl})^2$) to a pure-count term — dimensionally heterogeneous unless the residual is nondimensionalized by the pixel tolerance of constraint (10); the paper never shows that normalization.
- Singularity: Eq (6) diverges when $\sum_p A_{pl}=0$; it is implicitly avoided by constraint (9) ($\geq 2$ points) and the greedy stopping rule.
- Resolution "$1{,}038 \times 7{,}765$ Hz" ([PAGE 2]) is an OCR error; [PAGE 11] confirms $1{,}038 \times 776$ pixels.

These findings matter: the addition-vs-product fix changes the objective's minimizer; the ~71% vs 75.9% gap changes any cross-study comparison in Table 3 ([PAGE 9]).

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All units SI (meters, pixels) unless noted. $X,Y,Z$ = court coordinates [m]; $x_{pk},y_{pk}$ = image coordinate of point $p$ in camera $k$ [px]; $\lambda_{1k}\dots\lambda_{11k}$ = DLT parameters of camera $k$ (mixed-unit coefficients absorbing the meters→pixels map); $w$ = number of cameras (here 3); $A_{pl}\in\{0,1\}$.

DLT forward model (implied by Eqs 1–2, [PAGE 5]):
$$x = \frac{\lambda_1 X + \lambda_4 Y + \lambda_7 Z + \lambda_{10}}{\lambda_3 X + \lambda_6 Y + \lambda_9 Z + 1}, \quad y = \frac{\lambda_2 X + \lambda_5 Y + \lambda_8 Z + \lambda_{11}}{\lambda_3 X + \lambda_6 Y + \lambda_9 Z + 1}$$

Linearized (as printed, correcting the Eq 2 typo):
$$(\lambda_{1k}-\lambda_{3k}x_{pk})X + (\lambda_{4k}-\lambda_{6k}x_{pk})Y + (\lambda_{7k}-\lambda_{9k}x_{pk})Z + \lambda_{10k}-x_{pk}=0 \tag{1}$$
$$(\lambda_{2k}-\lambda_{3k}y_{pk})X + (\lambda_{5k}-\lambda_{6k}y_{pk})Y + (\lambda_{8k}-\lambda_{9k}y_{pk})Z + \lambda_{11k}-y_{pk}=0 \tag{2}$$

Assumption & breakdown: 11 unknowns need $\geq 11$ equations, i.e. "$\geq 6$ pairs of points" since "a point pair provides two equations" ([PAGE 5], Wood & Marshall 1986; Abdel-Aziz et al. 2015). Breakdown regime: rank deficiency when control points are collinear or coplanar through the camera center; and extrapolation blow-up outside the calibrated convex hull ([PAGE 5] cites Wood & Marshall on "DLT extrapolation"). Reported calibration residuals: "0.026 m (X), 0.031 m (Y), 0.043 m (Z)" over 36 doubly-visible points ([PAGE 5]) — note the ~1.65× growth from $x$/$y$ to $Z$, the classic depth-uncertainty amplification.

Assignment matrix (Eq 3): $A_{m,n}=[a_{p,l}]$, $a_{p,l}=1$ iff detected point $p$ is attributed to player $l$.

Conflicting costs (Eqs 4–5), residual operator corrected to $+$:
$$\min \sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{m}\big[(x_{pk}-x_{lk})^2+(y_{pk}-y_{lk})^2\big]A_{pl} \tag{4}$$
$$\max \sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{m} A_{pl} \tag{5}$$

Merged scalarized objective (Eq 6, [PAGE 6]):
$$\min \sum_{l=1}^{n}\left[\frac{(x_{pk}-x_{lk})^2+(y_{pk}-y_{lk})^2}{\left(\sum_{p=1}^{m}A_{pl}\right)^{2}} \;-\; \sum_{p=1}^{m}A_{pl}\right]A_{pl} \tag{6}$$
Justification quote: "cost functions ... are in conflict because a larger number of designated players increases the re-projection error" ([PAGE 6]). Singularity: undefined at $\sum_p A_{pl}=0$ (0/0); dimensional note: the two terms are heterogeneous (px² vs count) absent tolerance-normalization.

Constraints ([PAGE 6]):
$$\sum_{l=1}^{n} A_{pl}\le 1,\ \forall p \quad\text{(one label per point)} \tag{7}$$
$$\sum_{p} A_{pl}\le w,\ \forall l \quad\text{(}\le\text{ one point per camera)} \tag{8}$$
$$\sum_{p} A_{pl}\ge 2,\ \forall l \quad\text{(3D needs }\ge 2\text{ views)} \tag{9}$$
$$A_{pl}\big[(x_{pk}-x_{lk})^2+(y_{pk}-y_{lk})^2\big]\le \varepsilon,\ \forall p \quad\text{(pixel tolerance, Eq 10 as intended)} \tag{10}$$
$$h_{\min}\le Z_l \le h_{\max}\quad\text{(head-height bounds)} \tag{11}$$

Estimation results with units ([PAGE 8–9]): detection true-rates cameras 1/2/3 = 78.9/68.9/79.8%; FP = 2/1.2/5%; misdetection = 19.1/29.9/19.7%; detection RMSE = 6.59 px; true if pixel distance < 25 px. Localization: 2,941 samples in $\ge 2$ views → RMSE 0.16 m (X,Y), 0.18 m (X,Y,Z); 917 fallback samples (mean height) → 0.30 m and 0.33 m. Median 3D error 0.25 m on a 14×15 m half-court ([PAGE 11]).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
"Retro-causal genealogy" = tracing each operator backward to its scientific parent and the traps that recur across reimplementation attempts (the "multi-week traps").

- DLT: Abdel-Aziz & Karara's comparator-to-object-space transform (reprinted 2015, [PAGE 12]); the 11-parameter close-range form is refined by Qingchao et al. (1996, CCD calibration) and Rossi et al. (2015, "improved accuracy ... after lens distortion correction"). Distortion itself is removed first via Zhang (2000) planar-pattern calibration ([PAGE 2]). Trap: applying DLT before undistortion, or calibrating with a near-planar, low-baseline point set — precisely the ill-conditioning Wood & Marshall (1986) warn about.
- Head reference: lineage from Iwase & Saito (2004) and Figueroa et al. (2006b) who used feet/bounding-box-bottom; the divergence here is the deliberate move to $Z\neq 0$ ([PAGE 10]). Trap: reusing feet-based intuition (color-matching failures) on a head detector.
- Segmentation: Zivkovic (2004) adaptive GMM + Prati et al. (2003) shadow detection + Figueroa et al. (2006a) morphology. Trap (multi-week): GMM learning-rate and shadow-threshold sensitivity under arena lighting changes between games — the paper's own Game 1→Game 2 transfer (2,027 samples, [PAGE 4]) is the stress test.
- Contour/Hough/HOG/MLP: Suzuki & Be (1985), Yuen et al. (1990), Dalal & Triggs (2005), Rumelhart-Hinton-Williams (1986). The 1,764-neuron input equals $42^2$, implying a flattened HOG descriptor over a $42\times42$ region ([PAGE 3–4]) — a reimplementation must recover cell/orientation bin counts to reproduce it.
- Institutional lineage: the DVideo/UNICAMP biomechanics group (Barros, Misuta, Figueroa) supplies the ground-truth pipeline used to grade this very method ([PAGE 8]).
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reinterpreted for a non-agentic estimator: how the algorithm implicitly "models" the latent identity it cannot observe, and its three control loops — habit-regression (fallback), counter-trap (feasibility guards), clock (temporal/compute budget).

- Implicit identity inference: the estimator has no per-player state; identity is inferred purely from geometric consistency (low reprojection error, Eq 4/6) plus the mutual-exclusion prior (Eq 7). Its "belief" about which points belong together is entirely re-derived each frame — there is no memory.
- Crisis/habit regression: when optimization cannot place a point (e.g., single-view), it regresses to a fixed prior — "$Z$ equal to the mean player height" ([PAGE 6]). This is a Bayesian MAP fallback under a degenerate likelihood. Cost is measured: 917 such samples degrade RMSE from 0.16→0.30 m and 0.18→0.33 m ([PAGE 9]).
- Counter-trap: constraints (7)–(11) are the anti-degeneracy guards — they forbid assigning one point to two players, exceeding camera count, dropping below the 2-view reconstruction floor, exceeding pixel tolerance, or violating anthropometric head-height bounds. Without (9)+(10), the greedy would happily "explain" a phantom player with one noisy point.
- Clock: two clocks run. (i) Compute clock: "Processing time ... grows exponentially according to the number of player heads detected" ([PAGE 10]), each round removing $\ge 2$ points ([PAGE 10]) — so per-frame cost $\sim \sum_r \binom{m-2r}{\ge 2}$. (ii) Temporal clock: the method is memoryless — "no temporal information was used ... results could be improved by using the players' trajectories to help predict their current positions, filtering ... outlier positions" ([PAGE 11]). Adding a Kalman/particle temporal prior is the natural upgrade the authors defer.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/biomechanical/weather layers act as *hard vetoes* that can invalidate an otherwise low-error solution. Proof-style adjudication:

1. View-count veto (kinematic-geometric): constraint (9) $\sum_p A_{pl}\ge 2$ is necessary and sufficient for a determined $(X,Y,Z)$ from two DLT correspondences. Proposition: any head detected in a single camera *cannot* be reconstructed by optimization (rank of the stacked Eq 1–2 system $<3$). Corollary confirmed by data: the 917 single-view points are exactly those routed to the mean-height fallback ([PAGE 6–9]). Veto upheld.
2. Occlusion/contour-merge veto: when two heads merge into one contour ([PAGE 3], "the highest point can identify only one player's head"), the Hough stage can seed only one candidate, so a second player has no point to assign — Eq (9) cannot be met → that player is vetoed from the frame. Predicted mechanism for camera-2's 29.9% misdetection ([PAGE 8]).
3. Illumination/shadow veto (weather-of-the-arena): polished-floor reflections and cast shadows "significantly affect the segmentation process" ([PAGE 2], Alahi et al. 2009). The GMM+Prati relabel mitigates but cannot fully veto-proof the foreground mask; residual shadow blobs generate false candidates that must be killed by the MLP or by constraint (10).
4. Height-bound veto (biomechanical): Eq (11) $h_{\min}\le Z_l\le h_{\max}$ vetoes reconstructions placing a head outside plausible standing/jumping range — the guard that turns the a-priori fallback from arbitrary to bounded.
5. Extrapolation veto (camera geometry): cameras at 12 m ([PAGE 2]) give steep views; points near court edges push DLT toward extrapolation, where Wood & Marshall (1986) show error growth — consistent with the larger $Z$ residual (0.043 m) vs $X$/$Y$.
Adjudication result: the constraints are not cosmetic regularizers; each encodes a physical impossibility, and the reported error stratification (0.16 m multi-view vs 0.30 m fallback) is the empirical signature of these vetoes firing.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable veto scenarios, each falsifiable against the paper's own reported numbers.

1. Single-view head. Scenario: a head visible only in camera 1 (top-left, per Figure 9). Prediction: constraint (9) veto → not localized by optimization → mean-height fallback → error rises to $\approx$0.30 m (2D) / 0.33 m (3D). Verifiable via the 917-sample bucket ([PAGE 9]).
2. Adjacent-head occlusion. Scenario: two players' heads merge in one contour during a screen/pick. Prediction: only one candidate seeded → second player starves of points → misdetection, concentrated in the more occluded camera. Verifiable signature: camera 2's elevated 29.9% misdetection vs ~19% for cameras 1/3 ([PAGE 8]).
3. Background-matched appearance + tolerance exceedance. Scenario: a candidate whose best multi-camera assignment yields reprojection error above the Eq (10) pixel tolerance (or a raised-arm/ball false candidate, Figure 5 N1/N4). Prediction: the combination is "infeasible" (Figure 8: "provide an infeasible solution due to the one constraint") → player dropped this round rather than mis-placed. Verifiable via the MLP false-positive rates (2/1.2/5%) and the greedy round videos S2–S4.

Each prediction is a direct consequence of Eqs (6)–(11) plus the cited rates; none requires data beyond the PDF.
