<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual trajectory leading to Tolman's 1932 critique represents a fundamental paradigm shift from mechanism to meaning. The "pure conditioned reflex doctrine," as championed by Pavlov and early behaviorists, viewed the organism as a passive conduit for stimulus-response (S-R) connections, driven by external frequencies. Tolman [PAGE 1] does not merely correct this; he initiates a retrocausal loop where the *purpose* of the action determines its history. By identifying Guthrie, Hull, and Winsor as internal critics, Tolman establishes a genealogy of thought that moves beyond mere associationism toward an understanding of "knowledge and purpose as habit mechanism" [PAGE 1]. This lineage suggests that what we perceive as learning is often the result of latent cognitive maps reorganizing in response to environmental changes—a process that defies the linear S-R model. This perspective warns against the trap of interpreting immediate behavioral shifts as evidence of simple reinforcement; instead, one must look for the underlying gestalts that are revived or extinguished based on their predictive utility over time.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Tolman introduces a recursive layer to behavioral analysis by framing the conditioned reflex not as a mechanical output, but as a projection of future states. When an animal anticipates O1 following O2, it is engaging in a form of primitive theory-of-mind regarding its own environment—it holds an internal "sign-gestalt-expectation" [PAGE 1]. This expectation operates recursively: the strength of the expectation influences the behavior, which in turn generates feedback that either confirms or refutes the initial prediction. In crisis situations, such as the abrupt removal of a reward, this recursive loop can break down into a "habit regression." Instead of adapting fluidly to the new reality, the organism reverts to the most deeply entrenched gestalts. This regression serves as a counter-trap to the unpredictability of the environment; by falling back on the 'primacy' of earlier learnings, the organism buys time to reconstruct its cognitive map without succumbing to total behavioral chaos.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Tolman’s assertion that "frequency, recency, and something like primacy" [PAGE 1] govern learning faces a severe cross-layer veto when examined through modern biomechanical and neurobiological lenses. While psychological laws describe the phenomenology of behavior, they do not account for the physical veto of synaptic saturation or myelination limits. For instance, a "sign-gestalt-expectation" requires the coordinated firing of distributed neural networks. If an organism is subjected to extreme metabolic fatigue or peripheral nerve damage, the physical capacity to execute the conditioned reflex is completely overridden, regardless of the psychological strength of the expectation. Weather and environmental variables further complicate this; extreme temperatures can induce a physiological freeze response that acts as a hard veto on any purposive behavior, rendering the frequency or recency of past reinforcements irrelevant. Thus, while Tolman correctly identifies the inadequacies of the pure S-R doctrine, his replacement model remains vulnerable to absolute negation by the body's biomechanical constraints.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let $E(S \rightarrow O_1 | O_2)$ denote the **Sign-Gestalt-Expectation Strength**, representing the subjective probability assigned by the cognitive map that the occurrence of stimulus $O_2$ will culminate in outcome $O_1$. The formal operator for the evolution of this expectation across discrete trials $n$ is postulated as:

$$ E_n = f(\Phi_n, \Psi_{t}, \Omega_{0}) $$

Where the constituent components are defined and bounded as follows:

**1. Frequency Operator ($\Phi_n$):**
Represents the cumulative reinforcement history.
$$ \Phi_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot \mathbb{I}(O_2 \rightarrow O_1)_i $$
*   **Units:** Dimensionless count (cumulative successes), weighted by decay factors $w_i$ to account for varying salience.
*   **Assumptions:** Assumes additive integration of historical pairings as posited by Tolman's correlation statement [PAGE 1].

**2. Recency Operator ($\Psi_{t}$):**
Captures the influence of the most recent experiences, introducing time-dependency.
$$ \Psi_{t} = e^{-\lambda (t_{now} - t_{last\_success})} $$
*   **Units:** Probability density / normalized weighting factor ($0 < \Psi \leq 1$).
*   **Assumptions:** $\lambda$ is the temporal decay constant of short-term plasticity. High values indicate rapid forgetting of the sequence if not recently experienced.

**3. Primacy / Extinction Revival Operator ($\Omega_{0}$):**
Formalizes the "law of primacy, or revival after extinction" [PAGE 1]. This operator accounts for the re-emergence of the foundational expectation $E_0$ after an interruption in confirmation.
$$ \Omega_{0} = \alpha \cdot E_0 \cdot \delta_{session\_start} \cdot (1 - H_{inhib})^k $$
*   **Units:** Restored expectation potential.
*   **Assumptions:** $E_0$ is the primordial expectation established during initial acquisition. $\delta_{session\_start}$ is a Dirac-like trigger at the beginning of a new session. $H_{inhib}$ represents the habituation/inhibition accrued during extinction phases, which decays exponentially ($k$) during rest periods.

**Singularity and Breakdown Regimes:**
*   **The Associative Collapse Singularity:** Occurs when $E_n \rightarrow 0$ despite infinite frequency ($\Phi_n \rightarrow \infty$) due to extreme negative punishment or neurological trauma, violating the core assumption that correlation dictates strength.
*   **The Overlearning Plateau:** A breakdown of the recency operator where $\Psi_t \approx 1$ permanently, making the system impervious to new contradictory data (a rigid gestalt).
*   **Informational Saturation:** If the number of unique $O_2 \rightarrow O_1$ mappings exceeds the structural capacity of the cognitive map, the differentiation of expectations fails, leading to generalized stimulus confusion (Pavlovian pseudconditioning).
