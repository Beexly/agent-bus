Physical-layer veto: if bio-banded and comparator matches differ in load or peak strain, then technical/psychological differences cannot be cleanly attributed to maturity matching. The source gives direct evidence of such differences: Arede et al. report “↓ Distance covered”, “↓ Decelerations”, “↓ Average speed”, “↓ Body impacts”, “↓ Peak heart rate” [PAGE 8]; Salter et al. report “↓ Absolute stiffness”, “↓ Stride length, ↓ Cadence” [PAGE 8]; de Macedo et al. report external-load redistribution [PAGE 8]. These are not nuisance details; they are cross-layer confounds. Biomechanical-layer veto: foot-mounted IMUs and wearable sensors are used to assess technical and locomotor characteristics [PAGE 8], so changes in stride mechanics, stiffness, cadence, or fatigue can alter touch counts, possession time, and passing metrics. If biomechanical state differs, the technical outcome is not a pure skill signal. Weather/environment-layer veto: the source does not report WBGT, wind, rain, altitude, or surface, and therefore weather cannot be adjudicated from this paper. Pitch size is reported as a constraint—“smaller pitch areas tended to elicit more tactical behaviors” [PAGE 9]—so at minimum pitch geometry must be balanced. Formal veto proof: if \(\Delta L\neq0\) and \(\gamma_L\neq0\), or \(\Delta B\neq0\) and \(\gamma_B\neq0\), or \(\Delta W\neq0\) and \(\gamma_W\neq0\), then \(\widehat{\tau}_{BB-CA/MM}\) is not identified as a bio-banding effect. The source supplies evidence that \(\Delta L\) and \(\Delta B\) can be non-zero; it supplies no evidence that \(\Delta W=0\). Therefore the weather veto remains open, and the physical/biomechanical veto requires covariance adjustment, matched conditions, or randomized counterbalancing before causal claims are accepted.

Verifiable reward: \(\Delta\mathrm{AUC}=\mathrm{AUC}_{BB+CA}-\mathrm{AUC}_{CA}\) for predicting 24-month academy selection/progression, with 95% CI. Prediction: current short-term proxy evidence will fail to show incremental selection accuracy, because the source says direct evidence is lacking [PAGE 1] and long-term prediction “remains unproven” [PAGE 10].

Scenario 2 — blinded randomized crossover with standardized maturity thresholds and environmental controls. Verifiable reward: maturity-by-condition interaction \(\hat\delta\) in the latent-talent model, with blinded assessors, balanced pitch size, and recorded prior load. Veto if \(|\hat\delta|\) is below the smallest effect of interest and the 95% CI excludes a meaningful interaction, or if the effect disappears after adjustment for load and biomechanical state. Prediction: many technical/psycho-social “improvements” will attenuate toward null once assessor blinding and physical-load balance are enforced, because the source already reports inconsistent perceptual responses [PAGE 7] and context-dependent physiological effects [PAGE 6].

Scenario 3 — weather/biomechanical confounding adjudication. Verifiable reward: proportion of the BB effect attenuated after adding WBGT, surface, wind/rain, pitch dimensions, prior neuromuscular fatigue, and stride/stiffness covariates. Veto if attenuation is \(\ge80\%\) or if the adjusted 95% CI includes null. Prediction: at least some reported effects will lose significance after adjustment, because the source reports load and biomechanical differences [PAGE 8] but no weather control, and because “Large variation in maturation assessment, banding thresholds, game rules, and outcome reporting limits comparability” [PAGE 12]. The verifiable veto rule is simple: if the effect does not survive the pre-specified confound-adjustment reward, the causal claim is rejected even if the original unadjusted comparison was statistically significant.
