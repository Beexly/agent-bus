Hop 1: Sensory input is initially bound to an egocentric frame; an infant grasps at the moon because the visual sensation has not yet been projected beyond reach [PAGE 46]. Hop 2: Self-motion provides an efference copy that updates the egocentric frame, but the old location is no longer directly stimulated; it becomes a memory trace whose status shifts from actual to possible. Hop 3: With sufficient repetition, the allocentric frame dominates because it is invariant across self-motion, whereas egocentric coordinates are body-relative and therefore unstable. Hop 4: The dominance of the ideal coordinate system produces systematic translocation errors when the observer misjudges the transformation between frames, as when a fly's humming is mistaken for a distant steam whistle or an out-of-focus fly is taken for a distant bird [PAGE 46].

Gray-zone audit: James never specifies the metric by which a sensation is "shoved farther off," nor does he provide a psychophysical function relating perceived distance to physical distance. He conflates source-identity errors (hum vs. whistle) with distance errors, though the two are dissociable: one can correctly judge a sound as distant yet misidentify its source. The Cheselden case is anecdotal and the sentence trails off mid-argument ("other patients born blind, but relieved by surgical op-" [PAGE 46]), so the claimed generalization to all congenitally blind surgical patients lacks evidential closure. The infant/moon example confuses motor intention (grasping) with perceptual distance; reaching for the moon may reflect a failure of motor planning rather than a failure of spatial perception per se. Finally, James treats the transition from subjective to objective coordinates as inevitable and smooth, but provides no breakdown condition: there is no stated regime in which the observer fails to adopt ideal coordinates, nor any measure of the uncertainty (in bits) associated with the remembered "where." These gaps are not flaws in the phenomenology but absences of formal operators, which the next section must supply without inventing toy ratios.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Formal reconstruction (not James's original notation). Let world frame be W ∈ ℝ³ with SI units meters. Observer pose at time t is X_t = (p_t, R_t) ∈ SE(3), p_t ∈ ℝ³ position, R_t ∈ SO(3) orientation. Object location l ∈ ℝ³. Sensory measurement z_t = h(l, X_t) + ε_t, ε_t ~ N(0, Σ_t), where spatial components of Σ_t have units m² and angular components rad².

James's split of sensation into whereabouts and quality is formalized as a posterior over location given sensation: p(l | z_t, X_t). The sensation "where they were" at t₀, after observer motion to X_t, is no longer an actual sensation if it is not re-afferented; it becomes a counterfactual/conceived possible location \tilde{l}_{t₀|t}. Its egocentric coordinates relative to the new pose are l_e,t = R_tᵀ(l - p_t). If self-motion is not tracked (efference copy null), the transformation cannot be computed and the remembered location drifts relative to the body.

The replacement of actual sensations by possible sensations is modeled as an increasing convex weight on an ideal fixed allocentric frame A: \hat{l}_n = (1 - β_n) l_actual,n + β_n l_ideal, β_n ∈ [0,1], with β_n monotonically increasing in the number of independent movements n. Units of \hat{l}, l_actual, l_ideal are meters. Uncertainty is quantified by Shannon entropy H[L] = -∫_ℝ³ p(l) log₂ p(l) dl in bits. Breakdown/singularity regimes: (1) β_n → 1 without at least three non-collinear fixed landmarks leaves the allocentric frame unanchored (gauge freedom in origin and orientation); localization becomes unobservable. (2) If X_t is unknown because movement is passive or efference copy is absent, the map from remembered world location to current egocentric coordinates is singular; egocentric and allocentric frames decouple. (3) If sensory evidence never crosses absolute threshold (photon flux Φ < Φ_min or sound pressure level SPL < SPL_min), p(l | z) remains the prior and H[L] is maximal; no translocation or localization can be inferred [PAGE 46].

Verbatim anchors from [PAGE 46]: "when he first saw, he was so far from making any judgment about distances, that he thought all objects whatever touched his eyes (as he expressed it) as what he felt did his skin"; "Up' and'down'become'subjective’notions; east and west grow more'correct'than'right'and'left'etc."; "things get at last more'truly'located by their relation to certain ideal fixed co-ordinates than by their relation either to our bodies or to those objects by which their place was originally defined"; "translocations whereby sensations get shoved farther off than they originally appeared"; "A fly's humming may be taken for a distant steam-whistle"; "Our whole optical education indeed is largely taken up with assigning their proper distances to the objects of our retinal sensations"; "An infant will grasp at the moon."

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage: The passage sits at the intersection of Berkeley's New Theory of Vision (1709), which argued that distance is not immediately seen but inferred from tactile-motor experience, and Kant's transcendental doctrine that space is an a priori form of intuition. James explicitly invokes the Cheselden case—the 1728 cataract couching of a congenitally blind young man, cited as "Mr. Chesselden" [PAGE 46]—which is the historical antecedent of Molyneux's question about whether shape knowledge transfers across modalities upon sight restoration. The text also anticipates Helmholtz's "unconscious inference" (Handbuch der physiologischen Optik, 1866), Mach's analysis of spatial sensation, and Gibson's later ecological optics, in which the optic array and self-motion specify affordances rather than requiring a reconstruction from ideal coordinates.

Multi-week traps: In longitudinal sensorimotor adaptation (prism goggles, virtual reality), the egocentric-to-allocentric recalibration James describes does not stabilize immediately. Aftereffects persist for days to weeks, and the "after-effect" of translocation can regress when the observer returns to natural vision. A trap is to treat the post-adaptation state as permanent; another is to assume cross-modal distance constancy (audio-visual) when it is state-dependent on recent efference history. Studies of astronauts and deep-sea divers show that the allocentric frame requires weeks of stable gravitational and visual reference to consolidate; removal of that reference produces a delayed regression to body-centered heuristics, often misdiagnosed as a new deficit rather than a recrudescence of the pre-adaptation habit.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under perceptual uncertainty, cognitive load, or threat, the observer regresses from James's ideal fixed coordinates to body-centered heuristics (right/left, near/far relative to reach). This regression is recursive because the allocentric system, once dominant, must be actively suppressed under stress; the prefrontal-hippocampal map network is metabolically costly and is the first to go offline, leaving the parietal egocentric network in control.

Counter-trap: The opposite failure is over-reliance on abstract coordinates (map, GPS, instrument flight rules) while ignoring local exafferent cues (optic flow, vestibular gravito-inertial force, proprioceptive limb position). This produces "map-terrain dissociation": the observer acts as if the ideal coordinate frame is veridical even when the body-centered frame signals imminent collision or mislocated target.

Clock: The two processes operate on different timescales. Egocentric updating via efference copy occurs on a sub-second to seconds scale (τ_ego ~ O(1–10 s) per movement sequence), whereas allocentric consolidation requires minutes to hours of stable landmark exposure (τ_alloc ~ O(minutes–hours)). Under sensory deprivation (darkness, silence), the vestibular-proprioceptive estimate of self-motion decays on the order of tens of seconds, imposing a soft deadline after which the observer cannot reliably transform remembered locations into the current body frame. These are empirical ranges under assumptions (healthy adult, no pharmacological modulation, normal gravity), not invariant laws.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Localization requires exafferent energy. If photon flux at the cornea Φ < Φ_threshold (approximately absolute visual threshold) or sound pressure level at the cochlea SPL < SPL_threshold (approximately absolute auditory threshold), the likelihood p(z | l) is flat and H[L] remains maximal; no distance estimate can be formed. Veto condition: min(Φ, SPL) below absolute threshold => veto localization output.

Biomechanical layer veto: James's mechanism depends on self-motion to convert actual into possible sensations. If the observer is immobilized (rank of motor Jacobian < 6 DOF, or efference copy absent due to paralysis or passive displacement), the transformation l_e,t = R_tᵀ(l - p_t) cannot be updated. Veto condition: rank(J_motor) < required DOF or efference_copy = null => veto translocation computation; the system collapses to a static egocentric snapshot.

Weather layer veto: Atmospheric conditions corrupt the forward model h(l, X_t). Refractive index gradient ∇n(h), turbulence, fog, and precipitation introduce bias δL in meters between apparent and true location. For optical cues, visibility < 100 m collapses stereopsis and texture-gradient depth. For auditory cues, wind speed > 5 m/s advects sound and creates spurious distance cues (consistent with the fly/whistle confusion [PAGE 46]). Veto condition: |δL| > safety_margin for the intended action (e.g., reaching, navigation) => veto action based on the translocated estimate.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Post-surgical sight restoration): Counterfactual to the Cheselden case [PAGE 46]. If a congenitally blind patient gains sight but receives no correlated visuo-haptic training, the prediction is immediate failure to allocate distance: the patient will report objects "touch his eyes" and exhibit reaching endpoint error near 100% of arm's length for targets beyond near space. Verifiable measure: mean absolute reaching error in meters over the first 72 hours post-op; prediction: error remains high (arm's length ± σ) until visuo-haptic contingency is learned, after which error decays. Veto: if no haptic-visual contingency is provided, the allocentric frame never anchors (β_n → 1 but ungrounded).

Scenario 2 (Virtual reality optic-flow gain mismatch): Counterfactual to the opera-glass distance distortion [PAGE 46]. If HMD gain g ≠ 1 (displayed flow faster/slower than head movement), the observer cannot reconcile X_t with retinal flow. Prediction: egocentric distance estimates compress or translocate outward systematically with |g - 1|; after HMD removal, real-world pointing shows a signed after-effect in meters persisting > 5 min. Verifiable: pointing error (m) before/during/after; prediction: bias sign correlated with g - 1, variance σ²_L increases during mismatch.

Scenario 3 (Fog/heavy precipitation): Counterfactual to the infant/moon and fly/whistle cases [PAGE 46]. In dense fog, optical depth cues are attenuated; the observer defaults to audition or body-based distance. Prediction: perceived distance to a known 10 m target is overestimated by several meters with increased variance σ_L (m); without an auditory landmark, localization fails entirely. Verifiable: method of adjustment in fog vs clear; prediction: mean shift outward > 0 and σ_L, fog > σ_L, clear. Veto: if visibility drops below the minimum for disparity/stereopsis, the system must veto depth-based action and switch to proactive avoidance.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
