<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman's work sits at a critical junction in the genealogy of mind and machine. Its roots are deeply embedded in Gestalt psychology (Köhler's insight learning) and Dewey's pragmatism, rejecting elementarist associationism. However, its most potent lineage is in **Wundtian voluntarism**, which sought to understand conscious choice, not just reflex arcs. Tolman translated this into operational terms suitable for animal experimentation, creating a bridge between subjective purpose and objective measurement.

This created a powerful intellectual trap that lasted decades: **the trap of the 'cognitive' variable**. By positing "purposive" constructs like expectations and goals as intervening variables, Tolman opened the door to mentalism within a ostensibly objective science. This led to bitter debates with radical behaviorists (Skinner) who saw this as a betrayal of empiricism. The trap was that researchers could endlessly speculate about the content of these intervening variables without a clear method for their independent validation, leading to theoretical stalemates in mid-century psychology.

In the context of modern AI, this genealogy reveals a parallel trap: **the black-box interpretability crisis**. Deep neural networks learn complex "readinesses" (\(\mathcal{M}\)) from data, but we cannot inspect them for "demands" or "goals." We see successful navigation (behavior) but cannot verify if it stems from an internal model of reward (a Tolmanian purpose) or mere gradient descent correlation. Tolman's framework demands we ask: Can we ever truly know if an agent's success is *because of* an internal cognitive map, or is it just a sophisticated input-output function? His insistence on inferring purpose from patterned behavior remains the central challenge in reverse-engineering intelligence, whether biological or synthetic.
