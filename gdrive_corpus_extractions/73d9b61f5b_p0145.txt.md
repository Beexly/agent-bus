The crisis, counter-trap, and clock layers together delineate where the recursion ends and where simple biomechanics begins.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

Physical layer. The maze is an indoor apparatus on a fixed east-west line [PAGE 127]. A physical veto is triggered if any north-south asymmetrical physical covariate exists. Candidate vectors: irradiance (a window or lamp to the north), thermal gradient (a radiator or cold wall), and surface texture (different wall material on north vs south). If the north wall presents a temperature differential ΔT detectable by rat thermoreception (threshold on the order of 0.3–0.5 °C for rodent skin), or a luminance differential above the rat's contrast threshold (~0.1–0.2 log units), then wall preference can be explained by physical comfort or vision rather than spatial memory. Veto condition: preference persists under double-blind conditions with wall temperature equalized to within ±0.1 °C and illumination uniform to within ±5 lux across the outer square.

Biomechanical layer. Rats possess a strong default thigmotactic response mediated by whisker (vibrissae) contact and somatosensory wall-following circuits. Removing the inner square eliminates an internal haptic boundary, leaving the outer square as the dominant somatosensory corridor. A biomechanical veto is triggered if wall-following occurs in an identical empty maze with no food box and no goal direction. Prediction: naive rats placed in the open outer square will hug walls at rates above chance regardless of cardinal orientation. If the north-wall preference disappears under no-food controls, the behavior is a motor default, not a mnemonic expectation. Veto condition: north-wall contact frequency in an empty-maze control matches the north-wall contact frequency in the goal-present condition.

Weather/environmental layer. Though indoor, the room is subject to HVAC air currents, humidity gradients, and barometric fluctuations that can transport odor from the food box. If air moves from the food-box location toward the north wall at velocities above the rodent olfactory detection threshold for food odor (roughly > 0.02–0.05 m/s plume velocity depending on odorant), an olfactory gradient could guide the rat to the north wall independent of directional cognition. Veto condition: airflow is homogenized (laminar, < 0.01 m/s) or the food-box odor is masked by a uniform background odor; preference for the north wall must persist under these conditions to survive the veto.

Adjudication matrix. The "mnemonic expectation" conclusion [PAGE 127] is provisionally upheld only if: (a) north-wall preference survives physical equalization (light and temperature); (b) it exceeds the empty-maze biomechanical baseline; and (c) it survives odor-plasma homogenization. Failure of any one test triggers a veto and collapses the inference to a lower-level physical or biomechanical explanation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Allocentric rotation veto. Between trials, rotate the entire maze apparatus by 180° in the room while keeping the food box fixed at geographic 90° north relative to the room walls (not maze-relative). After the inner square is removed, measure wall choice relative to the room's north wall versus the maze's north wall. Prediction under mnemonic expectation (allocentric room code): rats hug the room's geographic north wall even when it becomes the maze's south wall after rotation. Prediction under maze-relative or biomechanical wall-following: rats hug whichever physical wall is on the north side of the rotated maze frame. Verifiable outcome: a significant interaction between rotation state and wall choice (chi-square, α = 0.05). Failure to shift with the room constitutes a veto of pure allocentric spatial memory.

Scenario 2: Extinction time-constant veto. After establishing the north-wall preference with the inner square removed and food at 90° north, remove the food reward entirely (extinction) while keeping the apparatus unchanged. Record north-wall choice probability P(N) across daily sessions. Prediction under mnemonic expectation: P(N) decays slowly because the directional expectation is cognitively represented and resistant to immediate reward removal (multi-session decay with time constant τ > 3 sessions). Prediction under pure reward-driven habit or thigmotaxis: P(N) collapses rapidly to chance within 1–2 sessions (τ < 1 session). Verifiable outcome: fit an exponential decay P(N)_t = 0.5 + (P₀ − 0.5) exp(−t/τ); if the fitted τ is not significantly different from the extinction rate of a non-spatial wall-following control, the mnemonic interpretation is vetoed.

Scenario 3: Competing landmark cue veto. Introduce a salient, stable visual landmark (e.g., a black-and-white striped panel) on the south wall of the outer square while maintaining the food box at 90° north and the inner square removed. Measure whether the south-wall choice rate rises above the 0.5 chance baseline. Prediction under mnemonic expectation of goal direction: the directional memory for "north" dominates; south-wall choice remains at or below chance despite the landmark. Prediction under cue-competition or associative overshadowing: the conspicuous south landmark captures orienting behavior, driving south-wall choice significantly above chance. Verifiable outcome: a binomial test comparing south-wall choices with landmark present versus absent; a significant increase constitutes a veto of goal-direction primacy and supports a stimulus-competition account instead.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
