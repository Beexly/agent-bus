<THINK_TRACE>
The unifying derivation threads one idea through the whole excerpt: mutual information and divergence *contract* under a channel, and every "frontier" theorem is a quantitative accounting of how much survives.

Hop 1 — DPI. For any kernel $P_{Y|X}$, $D_f(P_Y\|Q_Y)\le D_f(P_X\|Q_X)$ (Theorem 7.4, [PAGE 145]). This is qualitative: information cannot increase.

Hop 2 — SDPI sharpens DPI into a *multiplicative* contraction $\eta_f$: $D_f(P_Y\|Q_Y)\le \eta_f\,D_f(P_X\|Q_X)$ with $\eta_f=\sup_{P_X\ne Q_X} D_f(P_Y\|Q_Y)/D_f(P_X\|Q_X)\in[0,1]$ (Ch. 33, [PAGE 655]). For KL on the BSC, $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}=(1-2\delta)^2$ (Example 33.2, [PAGE 658]). The scalar $\eta$ is the "surviving fraction" of information per hop.

Hop 3 — Tensorize over a graph. On a DAG whose per-edge contraction is $\le\eta$, information percolation gives $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{Y|X_i})\le \mathrm{perc}(X_i\!\to\!Y)$, and for a $K$-ary circuit of depth $d_i$, $I(X_i;Y)\le K^{d_i}(1-2\delta)^{2d_i}\log 2$ (Evans–Schulman proof, [PAGE 661]). **Breakdown regime:** the geometric series converges (information dies) iff $K(1-2\delta)^2\le 1$; it diverges (information survives) otherwise. This is the same $K\eta\lessgtr 1$ transition as von Neumann's noisy-gate threshold $\delta^\*$ (Theorem 33.1, [PAGE 652]) and as the Kesten–Stigum reconstruction threshold on trees, $b\eta^2=1$ ([PAGE 667]).

Hop 4 — Channel coding converse. Feeding the meta-converse $\tfrac1M\ge \beta_{1-\varepsilon}(P_{X,Y},P_XQ_Y)$ ([PAGE 454]) with $Q_Y=P_Y$ and applying Chebyshev to the information density $i(X;Y)=\log\frac{dP_{X,Y}}{dP_XdP_Y}$ yields the strong converse: at any $R>C$, $\varepsilon_n\to 1$ (Theorem 22.1, [PAGE 448]). The weak converse $\log M\le \frac{\sup I(X;Y)+h(\varepsilon)}{1-\varepsilon}$ (Theorem 17.3, [PAGE 373]) is only the $\varepsilon\to 0$ shadow.

Hop 5 — Second order. Refining Chebyshev to Berry–Esseen/CLT converts the crude $o(n)$ into the dispersion expansion $\log M^\*(n,\varepsilon)=nC-\sqrt{nV}\,Q^{-1}(\varepsilon)+O(\log n)$, $V=\mathrm{Var}[\,i(X^\*;Y^\*)\mid X^\*]$ (Theorem 22.2, [PAGE 460]). Here $C$ is first order (nats/use), $\sqrt{nV}$ is the *stochastic* penalty for finite $n$, and $\varepsilon$ enters only through $Q^{-1}$.

Gray-zone audit — where each step breaks: (a) $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}<1$ strictly fails when the channel is noiseless ($\eta\to1$) or has a zero-cost noiseless input, whereupon $C_{\mathrm{puc}}=\infty$ ([PAGE 439]). (b) The dispersion Gaussian picture fails if $V=0$ (penalty vanishes, faster approach) or $V\to\infty$; the $O(\log n)$ remainder is only controlled under finite $V$ ([PAGE 435] footnote on non-unique capacity-achieving inputs). (c) The Fisher-quadratic law $D(P_{\theta_0}\|P_{\theta_0+\xi})=\tfrac{\log e}{2}\xi^\top J_F\xi+o(\|\xi\|^2)$ (2.34, [PAGE 64]) silently assumes smoothness *in the Hellinger/density parametrization*; the counterexample $P_t=\mathrm{Ber}(t^2)$ has $J_F(0)=4$ but $J^\#_F(0)=1$, so the naive $\mathbb E[(\dot p/p)^2]$ gives the *wrong* $\chi^2$ coefficient ([PAGE 169]). (d) Absolute continuity is a hard boundary: $D(P\|Q)=\infty$ iff $P\not\ll Q$, yet the mixture derivative $\frac{d}{d\lambda}D(\lambda P+\bar\lambda Q\|Q)\big|_{0}=0$ *always*, while $\frac{d}{d\lambda}D(Q\|\lambda P+\bar\lambda Q)\big|_0=0\iff P\ll Q$ (Prop 2.20, (2.29), [PAGE 61]). The contraction story is exact in the interior and singular on $\partial\{P\ll Q\}$.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

All logs base 2 ⇒ information in **bits**; base $e$ ⇒ **nats**. I mark units explicitly.

1. **Information density** (Definition 18.1, [PAGE 376]):
$$ i_{P_{X,Y}}(x;y)=\mathrm{Log}\,\frac{dP_{X,Y}}{dP_X\,dP_Y}(x,y),\qquad \mathbb E[i(X;Y)]=I(X;Y). $$
Units: bits/use. Breakdown: $i=+\infty$ on $\{dP_{X,Y}>0, dP_XdP_Y=0\}$ (e.g. $X=Y$ continuous ⇒ $I=\infty$, Example 3.1 [PAGE 67]).

2. **SDPI contraction coefficient** ([PAGE 655], [PAGE 658]):
$$ \eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{Y|X})=\sup_{U\to X\to Y}\frac{I(U;Y)}{I(U;X)},\qquad \eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{BSC}_\delta)=(1-2\delta)^2,\ \ \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_{x\ne x'}\mathrm{TV}(P_{Y|X=x},P_{Y|X=x'}). $$
Units: dimensionless, $\eta\in[0,1]$. Breakdown: $\eta=1$ iff the channel is sufficient/noiseless (no contraction); $\eta=0$ iff $Y\perp X$.

3. **Percolation/von Neumann bound** ([PAGE 652], [PAGE 661]): $K$-ary, depth $d_i$, gate noise $\delta$:
$$ I(X_i;Y)\le K^{d_i}(1-2\delta)^{2d_i}\log 2\quad\text{(bits)},\qquad \text{convergent}\iff K(1-2\delta)^2\le 1. $$
Breakdown at the critical line $K(1-2\delta)^2=1$; $\delta^\*$ (best correctable gate noise) is still **open** ([PAGE 652]).

4. **Meta-converse** ([PAGE 454]): $\ \dfrac1M\ge \beta_{1-\varepsilon}(P_{X,Y},P_XQ_Y)$. Units: dimensionless bound on rate $\tfrac1n\log M$. Breakdown: requires only $P_{X,Y}\ll$ test algebra; holds for arbitrary (non-memoryless, continuous) channels.

5. **Strong converse** (Theorem 22.1, (22.1) [PAGE 448]): $\ \epsilon^\*(n,e^{nR})\to \mathbf 1\{R>C\}$. Sharp threshold at $R=C$.

6. **Dispersion / normal approximation** (Theorem 22.2, (22.15) [PAGE 460]):
$$ \log M^\*(n,\varepsilon)=nC-\sqrt{nV}\,Q^{-1}(\varepsilon)+O(\log n),\quad C=I(X^\*;Y^\*),\ V=\mathrm{Var}[\,i(X^\*;Y^\*)\mid X^\*]. $$
Units: $C$ bits/use, $V$ bits²/use, $\varepsilon$ dimensionless. Breakdown: $V=\infty$ invalidates the $O(\log n)$ control; multiple capacity-achieving inputs require minimizing $V$ over them ([PAGE 435]).

7. **Feedback: zero-dispersion VLF** (Theorem 23.9, (23.20) [PAGE 481]) and **Schalkwijk–Kailath** ([PAGE 484]):
$$ \log M^\*_{\mathrm{VLF}}(\ell,\varepsilon)=\frac{\ell C}{1-\varepsilon}+O(\log\ell),\qquad \log M\ge (n-1)C+\log\tfrac{\sqrt P}{2}-\log Q^{-1}(\tfrac\varepsilon2)=nC+O(1). $$
Feedback deletes the $\sqrt{nV}$ term. Breakdown: capacity itself is *not* increased for stationary memoryless channels (Theorem 23.3).

8. **Fisher-quadratic law and its breakdown** ((2.34) [PAGE 64]; Theorem 7.22 & [PAGE 169]):
$$ \chi^2(P_t\|P_0)=J_F(0)t^2+o(t^2),\quad D(P_t\|P_0)=\tfrac{\log e}{2}J_F(0)t^2+o(t^2),\quad J_F(0)=\int \frac{\dot p_0^2}{p_0}\,d\mu. $$
Units: $J_F$ nats/param². Breakdown: $P_t=\mathrm{Ber}(t^2)$ gives naive $\mathbb E[(\dot p/p)^2]$ **discontinuous** at $0$ and unequal to $J_F(0)$; correct constants are $J_F(0)=4$, $J^\#_F(0)=1$.

9. **Chernoff information** ((16.2) [PAGE 340]) and **I-projection Pythagoras** (Theorem 15.10, [PAGE 328]):
$$ C(P,Q)=-\inf_{\lambda\in[0,1]}\log\!\int (dP)^{1-\lambda}(dQ)^{\lambda},\qquad D(Q\|P)\ge D(Q\|Q^\*)+D(Q^\*\|P). $$
Units: nats/observation. Breakdown: $C(P,Q)>0$ iff $P\ne Q$; the Pythagorean identity requires $Q^\*$ to be the $I$-projection onto a *convex* set.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY> (read as: forward scientific lineage & multi-decade open-problem "traps")

The lineage is strictly forward-causal. Shannon's noisy-channel theorem (`log M^\*(ϵ)=nC+o(n)`, [PAGE 402]) is the $n\to\infty$ skeleton. **Fano** supplied the first converse via $H(X|Y)\le F_M(1-P_e)$ (Theorem 3.12, [PAGE 82]); **Feinstein** gave the maximal-coding greedy bound $M\ge \gamma(\epsilon-P[i<\log\gamma])$ ([PAGE 382]); **Gallager** the error exponent $E_r(R)=\sup_\rho[E_0(\rho)-\rho R]$ ([PAGE 457]). The $\sqrt n$ correction traces to **Strassen** and was revived as "channel dispersion" by **Polyanskiy–Poor–Verdú**, appearing here as Theorem 22.2 ([PAGE 435] cites [130,402,334,333]). The SDPI branch runs **Ahlswede–Gács → Evans–Schulman** (noisy circuits, Theorem 33.2, [PAGE 652]) → **Mossel–Peres / broadcasting-on-trees** ([PAGE 667]).

The genuine "traps" are the *long-open problems the book itself flags*: (i) the optimal von Neumann threshold $\delta^\*$ — "the main (still open) question" ([PAGE 652]); (ii) the reliability function $E(R)$ for $R<R_{\mathrm{cr}}$ — "E(R) remains unknown," with the MRRW minimum-distance bound "not seen improvements in 60 years" ([PAGE 457]); (iii) the union-closed-sets constant, only recently advanced by Gilmer's entropy method ([PAGE 189]). These are the multi-decade sinks where contraction is *almost* tight but a gap survives.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND> (read as: self-referential proof moves, their traps, and the governing "clock")

The deepest recursive move in this text is reasoning about a **counterfactual world to bound the real one**. Fano/meta-converse (Remark 3.6, [PAGE 82]) introduces an *auxiliary* $Q_{X,\hat X}$ and transports a statement about $Q$ to $P$ via DPI — literally modeling an imagined agent's error to constrain the true one. The "counter-trap" is the recurrent failure of assuming a naive statistic applies: conditioning *reduces* Shannon entropy but *not* differential entropy ($h(f(X))\le h(X)$ fails under scaling, [PAGE 52]); the $\mathrm{Ber}(t^2)$ family defeats the naive Fisher formula ([PAGE 169]); and in directed information one must *not* assume $P_{Y^n|X^n}=\prod_j P_{Y_j|X_j}$ under feedback — the Markov chain $X_{t-1}\to X_t\to Y_t$ breaks it ([PAGE 472–475]). The "clock" is the asymptotic scaling that decides which term is real: $n$ (leading $nC$), $\sqrt n$ (dispersion penalty, killed by feedback per (23.20)), or $\log n$ (remainder). Reading "crisis-habit-regression vs counter-trap vs clock" as *default to the auxiliary-distribution reduction, guard against the illegitimate-statistic shortcut, and let the blocklength clock arbitrate which correction dominates* is the faithful, non-toy rendering.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION> (read as: cross-layer consistency & provable converse vetoes)

There is no physical/biomechanical layer here; the correct "layers" are operational vs. information quantities, and their *equalities are the vetoes*:

- **Operational = information capacity.** $C=C^{(I)}=\liminf_n \tfrac1n\sup_{P_{X^n}}I(X^n;Y^n)$ for information-stable channels (Theorem 20.9, [PAGE 424]). *Veto:* any coding scheme claiming throughput $>C$ at bounded error is inconsistent — enforced by the strong converse $R>C\Rightarrow\varepsilon\to1$ ([PAGE 448]).
- **Separation is tight only at the boundary.** Joint source–channel: reliable transmission iff $R>C/H$ (Theorem 19.23, [PAGE 418]); lossy: $R_{\mathrm{JSCC}}(D)=C/R(D)$ (Theorem 26.6, [PAGE 516]). *Veto:* "joint gains" beyond separation contradict $I(S;\hat S)\le I(X^n;Y^n)$ (Theorem 26.5 proof, [PAGE 541]).
- **Feedback cannot raise $C$.** $\vec I(X^n;Y^n)$ replaces $I(X^n;Y^n)$ but $\tfrac1n\vec I\to C$ (Ch. 23, [PAGE 450]). *Veto:* claims of capacity increase via feedback for stationary memoryless channels.
- **Contraction veto.** $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}<1$ strictly forbids information from surviving a subcritical cascade ($K\eta<1$).
