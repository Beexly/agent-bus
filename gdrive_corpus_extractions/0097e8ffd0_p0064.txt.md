\text{Duplication fails} \iff C_{\mathrm{channel}} < H_{\min}(\mathcal{A}),
\]
where \(C_{\mathrm{channel}}\) is channel capacity [bits/s] and \(H_{\min}(\mathcal{A})\) is the minimum entropy rate required to sustain the form \(\mathcal{A}\). Wiener's empirical limit—duplication possible "only over a short distance" [PAGE 109]—maps to finite \(C_{\mathrm{channel}}\) and non-zero propagation delay \(\tau > 0\).

Stone-like (matter-fixed) regime:
\[
\dot{m}(t) = 0,
\]
whereas flame-like (dissipative) regime satisfies \(\dot{m}(t) \neq 0\) and \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} \neq 0\), maintaining a non-equilibrium steady state through metabolic throughput [PAGE 109].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage in pp. 108–109 runs from Morton Prince's turn-of-the-century dissociative case studies through Wiener's 1950 cybernetics and forward into contemporary debates about digital identity. Prince's "girl, within whose body several better-or-worse-developed personalities seemed to succeed one another, and even to a certain extent to coexist" [PAGE 108] represented an early clinical observation of what would later be framed as dissociative identity disorder; Wiener notes that psychiatrists of his own day looked down on Prince's work and attributed the phenomenon to hysteria, which Wiener correctly identifies as a question-begging epithet [PAGE 108]. Wiener's own source discipline is the stored-program computing machine: "the retention of its earlier tapings and memory" [PAGE 109] directly echoes the von Neumann/Turing architecture in which program and data share the same addressable tape. The theological counterpoint is explicit: Wiener rejects "The mental identity necessary for the Church's view of the individuality of the soul" because no absolute mental identity exists in a form "acceptable to the Church" [PAGE 109]. Biologically, he invokes cell division and "one of the genes which carries our corporeal and mental birthright" being "split in order to make ready for a reduction division of a germ cell" [PAGE 109], drawing on Weismannian germ-plasm theory and meiosis. The flame-versus-stone metaphor reaches back to Heraclitean process philosophy but is stripped of Aristotelian essence; Wiener retains only form without substance [PAGE 109].

Multi-week traps: In public discourse, the concepts Wiener treats here—personality splitting, mind uploading, digital twins, and the alleged equivalence of symbolic and biological transmission—re-emerge in media cycles roughly every few weeks. Each resurgence typically treats "forking" a mind or cloning an AI as if it were trivial, ignoring Wiener's explicit constraint that duplication is presently possible "only over a short distance" [PAGE 109] and his denial of any absolute line between telegram and organism transmission without specifying channel capacity [PAGE 109]. The trap is to treat information as substrate-free; Wiener's actual argument is that information is substrate-dependent precisely because it is embodied in a dissipative metabolic process with high turnover [PAGE 109].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Wiener's claim that the mind continues "development along lines already laid out" [PAGE 109] implies a crisis-regression dynamic: under stress or novelty, an agent regresses to the highest-probability attractors encoded in its retained tape. This is the habit-regression mode. Recursively, Agent A models Agent B as likely to regress to B's laid-out lines; Agent A can therefore set a counter-trap by anticipating that regression and deviating from its own expected line. Because Wiener permits an individual to fork into two processes sharing a past but diverging [PAGE 109], the counter-trap itself becomes a new fork: Agent A's unexpected deviation creates a partial assembly that may achieve "a higher or lower degree of independence" from the predicted trajectory [PAGE 109].

The clock governing this recursion is set by metabolic turnover and neural plasticity. Wiener's documentation of the high turnover of "each and every component part" [PAGE 109] establishes a finite refresh rate \(\tau_{\mathrm{met}}\). If a crisis interval \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{crisis}}\) is shorter than the memory-consolidation time \(\tau_{\mathrm{mem}}\), regression to laid-out lines is effectively unavoidable; the agent cannot re-tape fast enough. If \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{crisis}} > \tau_{\mathrm{mem}}\), the agent has time to encode a counter-trap before the next crisis. The coupling operation—two machines becoming one [PAGE 109]—adds a social layer: recursive theory of mind scales from individual forking (Prince's case) to interpersonal coupling, where two agents may synchronize their tapes sufficiently to function as a single controller, or fail and remain "partial assemblies with a higher or lower degree of independence" [PAGE 109].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Metabolic turnover demonstrates that "The physical identity of an individual does not consist in the matter of which it is made" [PAGE 108–109]. If the replacement rate of molecular components exceeds the rate at which the process form can be re-constituted, the flame collapses into a stone. Veto condition: \(\tau_{\mathrm{replace}} < \tau_{\mathrm{repair}}\) forces loss of process continuity.

Biomechanical layer veto: An organism has "either a single central nervous system or a well-developed pair of separate brains" [PAGE 108]. Structural separation constrains coupling: two large machines may merge [PAGE 109], but only if anatomical or architectural pathways permit sufficient mutual information flow. Split-brain or partial-assembly configurations veto full coupling; the result is "partial assemblies with a higher or lower degree of independence" [PAGE 109].

Weather/environmental layer veto: Wiener equates, at least theoretically, telegram transmission and organismic transmission [PAGE 109]. Environmental noise acts as channel noise. If channel capacity \(C_{\mathrm{channel}}\) falls below the entropy rate of the transmitted form, the transmission vetoes itself: duplication remains limited to "a short distance" [PAGE 109]. Weather-like perturbations (thermal, vibrational, semantic noise) therefore bound the domain in which the flame metaphor holds.

Adjudication hierarchy: A physical-layer veto (metabolic failure) overrides biomechanical and informational layers, because without process continuity there is no form to couple or transmit. A biomechanical veto (CNS separation) overrides informational coupling proposals. Informational vetoes (channel-capacity failure) apply last. No layer permits the inference that mind is substrate-independent; Wiener's flame is a constrained dissipative process, not an abstract pattern floating above matter [PAGE 109].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1 (fork divergence veto). Condition: Two instances of a learning system are initialized from identical weights (a Wiener fork [PAGE 109]) and exposed to divergent data streams \(D_A\) and \(D_B\). Veto rule: if the output-distribution divergence satisfies \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_A \,\|\, p_B) > \theta\) within \(N\) update steps, the fork is vetoed as non-identical; the instances are classified as separate individuals rather than copies. Prediction: the threshold \(\theta\) scales as \(\log |\Sigma|\), where \(\Sigma\) is the output alphabet size; verification via held-out perplexity traces showing divergence crossing \(\theta\) at step \(N^*\).

Scenario 2 (coupling failure veto). Condition: Two autonomous agents attempt to couple into a single controller (Wiener's coupling operator [PAGE 109]). Veto rule: if post-coupling mutual information \(I(M_A; M_B) < C_{\min}\) after an adaptation window \(W\), the merge is vetoed and the agents remain partial assemblies. Prediction: successful coupling is verified only when \(I(M_A; M_B) > H_{\mathrm{overlap}}\), where \(H_{\mathrm{overlap}}\) is the joint entropy of shared state variables; failed mergers show persistent independent attractors.

Scenario 3 (turnover-driven identity veto). Condition: A biological or digital substrate undergoes component replacement at rate \(r_{\mathrm{replace}}\) while its memory system consolidates at rate \(r_{\mathrm{mem}}\). Veto rule: if \(r_{\mathrm{replace}} > r_{\mathrm{mem}}\), identity continuity is vetoed; the system reverts to stone-like matter-only persistence. Prediction: the half-life of an identity trace follows \(\tau_{1/2} = \ln 2 \, / \, (r_{\mathrm{replace}} - r_{\mathrm{mem}})\) when the denominator is positive; empirical verification by tagging metabolic or memory elements and measuring trace decay (Wiener's metabolic-turnover observation [PAGE 109]).
