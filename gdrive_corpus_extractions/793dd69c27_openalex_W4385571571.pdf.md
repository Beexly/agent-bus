$$q^t_{i,j} = [\text{CLS}] \circ P(w_i) \circ [\text{SEP}] \circ d^{t-1}_{i,j} \circ [\text{SEP}], \quad t > 1$$

**Operator 6 — Multi-round retrieval.** From [PAGE 4]:

$$T^t_i = \bigcup_{j} \Big(\operatorname*{Top-}k_{d \in \mathcal{D}} f(q^t_{i,j}, d)\Big), \quad T^t = \bigcup_{1 \le i \le c} T^t_i$$

k_t = 20 for t > 1 (sentiment), 10 (topic). Rounds T = 3 fixed (Table 11 [PAGE 17]). Singularity: as t increases, |{q^t_{i,j}}| grows ×k_{t-1} per round — combinatorial query explosion capped by |T̃^t| ≤ 3,000 per class.

**Operator 7 — Self-consistency filter.** From [PAGE 4]:

$$\widetilde{T}^t = \{(x^t, y^t) \in T^t \mid \arg\max p^{t-1}_\phi(x^t) = y^t\}$$

Units: retained-document count. Assumption: retriever-label yᵗ and classifier argmax are independent noise sources. Breakdown: correlated error (co-confident drift) violates independence — the singularity the paper's own filter does *not* address.

**Operator 8 — Weighted Jaccard (corpus–task similarity).** From [PAGE 18]:

$$J(T, \mathcal{D}) = \frac{\sum_k \min(C_k, T_k)}{\sum_k \max(C_k, T_k)} \in [0,1]$$

C_k and T_k are word frequencies. Table 14 [PAGE 18] reports J-values 0.296–0.714 with a monotone negative relation to the performance gap versus fully supervised.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs through five strata. **(1) Weak supervision via labeling functions** — Snorkel-style aggregation, formalized in WRENCH [PAGE 14, Zhang et al. 2021] and touched on in the paper's Related Work [PAGE 2]. **(2) Dense passage retrieval** — Lee et al. 2019 [PAGE 11] latent retrieval and Karpukhin et al. 2020 [PAGE 11] DPR are the direct ancestors of Eq. 1. **(3) Unsupervised retrieval geometry** — SimCSE (Gao et al. 2021b [PAGE 10]), Contriever (Izacard et al. 2022a [PAGE 11]), Condenser (Gao & Callan 2021 [PAGE 10]) are the retrievers the ablation [PAGE 7, Fig. 2] rejects. **(4) Zero-shot inference via prompting** — Schick & Schütze 2021a, NSP-BERT, KNN-Prompt. **(5) Dataset-generation without retrieval** — ZeroGen/ProGen (Ye et al. 2022a,b [PAGE 13]) and SuperGen (Meng et al. 2022 [PAGE 11]), plus regex-mining (van de Kar et al. 2022 [PAGE 13]). REGEN's specific novelty is the substitution of regex hard-match with contrastive dense retrieval plus progressive query augmentation — i.e., it retrofits retrieval-based PRF (Xu & Croft 2017 [PAGE 13]) into zero-shot dataset synthesis.

**Multi-week / long-horizon traps.**

*Trap 1 — "General domain" is actually near-target.* Table 10 [PAGE 16] shows D = WIKI + REALNEWS + REVIEWS. Every target task (AG News, Yahoo, NYT, IMDB, Amazon, Yelp, MR, SST-2, DBPedia) belongs to news/reviews/Wikipedia genres. The claim of "general-domain" is preserved only topically; the empirical advantage is partly an unmodeled genre-alignment prior. Multi-week consequence: practitioners deploying REGEN in biomedical (Cohan 2020 [PAGE 9]) get non-transfer unless they swap in PubMed. The paper anticipates this — the Discussion points out exactly this extension route.

*Trap 2 — Retriever self-consistency drift.* Fig. 2(b) [PAGE 7] shows gaps between REGEN and off-the-shelf retrievers narrowing across rounds on sentiment, consistent with the augmented query becoming increasingly retriever-native (Eq. 5 embeds prior documents in query space). Long-horizon consequence: systems that share REGEN's pretraining corpus but not its contrastive batch distribution will diverge from published numbers.

*Trap 3 — Verbalizer sensitivity decays slowly.* Table 5 [PAGE 7] group #1 on AG News: 82.7 versus 85.0. Multi-week implication: verbalizers are cheap but the marginal safety margin is 2–3 pp, not the 4.3 pp headline [PAGE 1] which is an average over the originals.

*Trap 4 — Efficiency amortization only at scale.* Table 6 [PAGE 8]. Fixed 29h (pretraining + indexing) means single-task productivity is worse than the cheaper baseline; multi-task suite N ≥ 5 makes REGEN dominant on time-to-result.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Reformulate the pipeline as two agents with recursive beliefs. Agent Rθ "believes" class i is the ball around Rθ(qᵢ) in ℝ^768; Agent Cϕ "believes" class i is {x : argmax p_ϕ(x) = i}. Eq. 7 [PAGE 4] is a *mutual-belief gate*: keep only cases where Cϕ's belief agrees with Rθ's assignment. This is powerful when errors are independent, but under co-confident drift (both wrong in the same direction) it can *lock in* mislabeled clusters, no matter how much data flows through.

**Crisis-habit regression.** The true "habit" is the zero-shot prompting fallback (no dataset curation). Table 3 [PAGE 6]: REMOVING data curation entirely drops AG News from 85.0 → 70.9. That is a 14.1-point regression and is the strongest evidence that any recursive self-belief (retriever↔classifier loop) is preferable to pure inference. Removing multi-step retrieval alone drops AG News 85.0 → 83.0 (2.0 pts); removing label smoothing drops 85.0 → 84.5 (0.5 pts). Decomposition of the total gap: curation ≈ 14.1, MSR ≈ 2.0, LS ≈ 0.5, filter ≈ residual. In order of load-bearing-ness: D-curation ≫ MSR ≫ LS.

**Counter-trap (label smoothing as safety valve).** Eq. 4 [PAGE 4] with α = 0.1 caps p_ϕ(x)_y at 1−α+α/c = 0.95 (c = 2). That is a hard ceiling on recursive self-confidence — the model cannot "double down" on one class. But the ceiling is soft in multi-class (c = 14, DBPedia → 0.906), which explains why DBPedia's label-noise story is less catastrophic than Yahoo's (Yahoo: 59.4, DBPedia: 87.6 [Table 2 PAGE 6]).

**Clock.** The clock is T = 3 [Table 11 PAGE 17], not more. [PAGE 6]: "We have also attempted to conduct more retrieval rounds, but do not observe significant performance gains." The belief system reaches its fixed point by ≈ round 3; beyond that, retriever↔classifier beliefs stabilize and further rounds are noise-not-signal. Fig. 1 [PAGE 6] visually corroborates: both the REGEN and w/o-Filtering curves plateau on sentiment and topic by round 2–3.

**Recursive prediction.** If T were pushed to 5 or 10, we'd predict (i) saturation of accuracy as observed, and (ii) growth of co-confident drift — a small *decline* in low-resource tasks (Yahoo, NYT) as belief-lock-in entrenches mislabeled clusters. The paper's own flat curve at T = 3 supports saturation without yet showing decline, so the safe inference is T* ≈ 3, with decay beyond.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Literal physical, biomechanical, and weather layers do not exist in an NLP paper; I map them rigorously to their analogues.

**Physical layer analogue — Representation geometry uniformity.** InfoNCE Eq. 2 [PAGE 3] explicitly promotes "the alignment of similar text sequences and the uniformity of unrelated text sequences" [PAGE 3]. Physical veto condition: if intra-corpus embedding variance collapses below a threshold (empirically, cluster count c̄ × σ_transverse ≪ σ_radial), Top-k returns sorted-but-random items. Under such collapse, every q_i maps into a single basin and T_i¹ ⊇ T_j¹ for i ≠ j, so the classifier receives identical data across classes → uniform prediction at accuracy ≈ 1/c. Yahoo (c = 10) lower-bound 10% vs REGEN 59.4% [PAGE 6, Table 2] shows we are well above the collapse floor but only 77% of fully-supervised 76.8.

**Biomechanical layer analogue — Model capacity.** [PAGE 17, D.3] Rθ = BERT-base 110M, Cϕ = RoBERTa-base 125M. Veto condition: if c exceeds effective capacity minus retrieval overhead, class discrimination fails. Empirical evidence that this is not the binding constraint: DBPedia (c = 14) 87.6 vs Yahoo (c = 10) 59.4 [Table 2 PAGE 6]. High class count alone does not cause failure; distributional overlap (Jaccard) is the load-bearing variable [Page 18 Table 14: Yahoo J = 0.362, DBPedia J = 0.566].

**Weather layer analogue — Distributional shift.** Eq. 8 [PAGE 18] is the predicted proxy for the shift. Table 14 gives the causal chain: Amazon J = 0.714 → 3.1 pp gap; Yahoo J = 0.362 → 17.4 pp gap. Veto proof: the residual gap versus fully supervised is monotone decreasing in J, currently citable for six of nine dataset correspondences (dataset entries are split across the two panels but the trend is coherent). So the actionable veto is: if J(T, D) < 0.4, expect ≥ 15 pp gap and REGEN's absolute accuracy may be below a naive baseline floor; if J ≥ 0.5, REGEN should be competitive within 5 pp.

**Composite veto.** All three analogues must hold simultaneously for provenance: (i) non-collapsed embedding (sim discrimination alive), (ii) classifier capacity respected (c not bookended by extreme class imbalance — NYT is explicitly F1 [PAGE 5]), (iii) J ≥ 0.4 domain overlap. Failure of any single layer invalidates the REGEN guarantee, though not the fine-tune itself — fine-tune degrades gracefully.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable verifier-reward (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a both-measurable prediction and both-pass/fail boundary.

**Scenario 1 — Verbalizer perturbation veto.**
Intervention: replace the canonical AG News verbalizer with group #1 "world, football, stock, science" [PAGE 17, Table 12].
Predicted outcome: 82.7 [PAGE 7, Table 5] versus 85.0 baseline. Δ = 2.3 pp, four-group σ = 1.2. An RLVR verifier with a 100-example-per-class held-out probe (binary correctness) has standard error √(0.85·0.15/100) ≈ 3.6e-2, so Δ / SE ≈ 0.64 — insufficient for a single-trial reject; but across 4 verbalizer draws the observed pooled σ=1.2 gives Δ/σ ≈ 1.9. Verdict: **conditional veto** — rejected if the verifier uses ≥ 4 verbalizer draws; accepted if a single canonical verbalizer is fixed. Prediction (falsifiable): sub-sampling 3 verbalizers should produce REGEN accuracies in [82.5, 85.2] on AG News.

**Scenario 2 — Corpus ablation veto.**
Intervention: restrict D to WIKI-only (remove REVIEWS, REALNEWS).
Predicted outcome: for AG News and sentiment, Fig. 5(a) [PAGE 8] shows WIKI bar at the lower end, with the exact gap largest for REVIEWS-dependent tasks. On IMDB, sentiment regression predicted to ~60–70 (versus 89.9 full-corpus). On Yelp, similar downward shift.
Verifier prediction: a labeled probe of 100 per class on IMDB will reject the WIKI-only pipeline at p < 0.01 (Δ ≈ 10–20 pp, much larger than sampling error ~3.6e-2). Verdict: **hard veto** — WIKI-only REGEN fails the reward threshold. Falsification check: if the observed IMDB WIKI-only accuracy were to stay above 80, the shift model is falsified.

**Scenario 3 — Round-count veto.**
Intervention: T = 1 (suppress multi-step) vs T = 3.
Predicted outcome: [PAGE 6, Table 3] AG News 83.0 → 85.0; SST-2 85.9 → 88.9; Yelp 90.9 → 93.0; DBPedia 83.6 → 87.6. Δ = 2.0–4.0 pp, larger than σ (0.4–1.1 from Table 2). Verdict: **moderate veto** — T=1 fails superiority but passes absolute floor; T=3 passes both.
Prediction (falsifiable): if T=1 is measured to be within 1 σ of T=3 across the nine datasets, the multi-step claim is overstated and the reward hypothesis is falsified.

**Bonus (soft-veto, worth reporting honestly).**
Label smoothing α = 0.1 vs α = 0 [PAGE 6, Table 3]: AG News 85.0 vs 84.5 (Δ = 0.5, below σ = 0.8), SST-2 88.9 vs 88.0 (Δ = 0.9, marginal). Verdict: **soft veto** — label smoothing does not clear a standard α = 0.05 reward threshold on AG News. Any RLVR criterion asserting label smoothing is "required" should be re-calibrated downward; the ablation supports "helpful" not "necessary."

**Joint RLVR decision rule.** A verifier combining all four tests would assign weights ≈ {0.15, 0.45, 0.30, 0.10} to Scenarios 1–3 + LS, mirroring the ablation magnitudes (14.1, 2.0, 0.5) with the curation baseline as the true crisis-regression reference. Any pipeline failing Scenario 2 (corpus) should be discarded regardless of Scenarios 1 and 3 outcomes, since corpus–task overlap dominates the empirical gap in Table 14 [PAGE 18].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
