The 13-position framework descends from a lineage of basketball role decomposition that predates Alagappan. The traditional five positions (point guard, shooting guard, small forward, power forward, center) emerged from early 20th-century positional conventions and were codified by coaching manuals and box-score stat lines (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks). The critique that positions do not reflect playing styles echoes earlier work by Dean Oliver’s “Four Factors” and the APBRmetrics community, which argued that positional labels obscure actual on-court contributions. Alagappan’s topological-data-analysis approach, executed at Ayasdi with Stanford affiliation, applies computational topology (Carlsson, Ghrist, Zomorodian) to sports: point-cloud data, filter functions, coverings, and nerve graphs to recover persistent clusters.
