Hull synthesized these streams by retrocausally projecting the effect of the reinforcer backward in time to stamp-in the preceding neural events—a concept he mathematically framed via the $V^r$ (incentive motivation) and the temporal decay factors. Early RL researchers adopted Hull’s delay-discounting intuition, formalizing it as the discount factor $\gamma$ in Temporal Difference (TD) learning. Furthermore, Hull’s reliance on free-choice data to validate strictly controlled theoretical derivations opened the door for the "behavioral momentum" critiques later championed by Nevin, arguing that the frequency of behavior, not just latency, dictates resistance to change, a nuance largely absent in 1943's purely temporal focus.
