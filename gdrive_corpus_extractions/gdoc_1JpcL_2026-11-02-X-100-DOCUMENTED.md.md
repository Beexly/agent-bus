(8) Drive-start EP monetization ([DOSSIER 7]). ΔEP_game ≈ (drive_start_edge_yards) × (drives_per_game) × (∂EP/∂x), ∂EP/∂x ≈ 0.05–0.06 EP/yd in midfield. Units: points per game. Assumption: first play of each drive carries yardline_100; end-of-half kneel drives excluded with filter stated. Breakdown regime: single week ≈ 10–13 drives, one takeaway swings mean by ~4 yards; turnover luck dominates and regresses.

(9) Binomial small-n check ([DOSSIER 10], [DOSSIER 36]). P(K=k | n, p) = C(n,k) p^k (1−p)^{n−k}. Butker 50–54 yd band 2/6 vs p ≈ 0.70 league baseline: P(K=2 | n=6, p=0.70) ≈ 0.264. Units: probability. Breakdown regime: n=6 is brutal small sample; band records ignore wind/altitude mix and end-of-half rushes.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudication order: (1) health/roster veto, (2) weather/venue physical veto, (3) data-provenance veto, (4) matchups/situations computation within the vetoed set.

Physical veto. A QB pressure-sensitivity split ([DOSSIER 75]; Penix clean ≈ +0.6 EPA/dropback vs pressured ≈ −0.7, gap ≈ 1.3–1.4) cannot override the health layer. A pressure-sensitive QB behind a downgraded offensive line is a multiplicative fade, not additive ([DOSSIER 75] §5; [DOSSIER 13] OL prior). If an OL starter is injured, the OL prior is swapped for the backup-unit prior and the pressure expectation re-simulated before any QB pressure-resilience number is consumed ([DOSSIER 13] §4).

Biomechanical veto. The catch rule requires (a) secure control before ground contact, (b) both feet or another body part down in bounds, (c) a football act or sufficient control time ([DOSSIER 24], citing NFL Rule 8 §1 Art. 3 via Eagles Football 101). Standard play-by-play records only complete/incomplete; it cannot adjudicate the third element. Eyeball step counts ("like double digit", [DOSSIER 24]) are estimates, not evidence. The engine therefore treats disputed catch rulings as rules-literacy content, not as graded features, unless NGS-class tracking (catch point, time to dislodgement, YAC before contact) is available.

Weather veto. Low combined aDOT games ([DOSSIER 38]; KC@DEN season-low verticality) may be wind- or altitude-driven rather than defensive quality. The venues-weather-refs layer must cross-check before crediting a defense for suppressing depth. Deep-throw accuracy ([DOSSIER 88]) collapses in wind; a deep-reliant QB in a 15+ mph wind game is vetoed from deep-threat matchup reasoning regardless of charted accuracy rank. Drive start ([DOSSIER 7]) and time of possession ([DOSSIER 5]) are game-script outcomes; raw TOP must never enter the model directly ([DOSSIER 5] §3).

Cross-layer example. The ATL@TB preview ([DOSSIER 73]) predicted Tampa's pass rush would force Penix into long third downs. This is admissible only if (i) Tampa's pass-rush health is intact, (ii) weather at the venue does not suppress the vertical game, and (iii) the pressure numbers derive from a stated charting vendor with a bracketed interval, not a single image-locked value. If any veto fires, the matchups layer falls back to PBP-native quantities (EPA/play, success rate, stuff rate) and the reasoning trail records the veto explicitly.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
