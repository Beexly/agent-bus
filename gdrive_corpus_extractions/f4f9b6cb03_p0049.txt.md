**Theorem B** (covering inequality): If \(F_0 = [a_0, b_0] \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n (a_i, b_i)\), then  
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n (b_i - a_i) \geq b_0 - a_0.
\]  
*Assumptions*: \(F_0\) is closed and bounded (compact), each \((a_i, b_i)\) is open and bounded.  
*Breakdown regime*: If \(F_0\) were not compact (e.g., an open interval), the inequality could fail because an open cover might require infinitely many intervals to achieve minimal total length. **Theorem C** (countable additivity): Let \(\{E_0, E_1, \dots\}\) be disjoint sets in \(\mathcal{P}\) (the ring generated by intervals) with \(E_0 = \bigcup_{i=1}^\infty E_i\). Then  
\[
\ell(E_0) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \ell(E_i).
\]  
*Assumptions*: \(E_i = [a_i, b_i)\) are half-open intervals; \(\mathcal{P}\) consists of finite unions of such intervals.  
*Breakdown regime*: If the sets were not disjoint, additivity fails due to overlap. Also, if the space lacked the Heine-Borel property (e.g., in infinite dimensions), the proof technique collapses.
