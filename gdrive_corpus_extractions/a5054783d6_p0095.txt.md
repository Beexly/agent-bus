The core theoretical construct is the **Immanent Expectation**, defined by Tolman as a mnemonic state \( E \) generated by preceding conditions \( C \), anticipating a specific goal-object type \( G_{exp} \) [PAGE 1]. We formalize this within a Bayesian predictive framework. Let \( \mathcal{G} \) be the space of possible goal objects. At time \( t \), given history \( H_{t-1} \), the agent maintains a posterior distribution over goal types:
\[
P(G_t | H_{t-1}) = \text{Expectation}(C_t),
\]
where \( C_t \) denotes the current cue context. Upon observation \( g_{obs} \in \mathcal{G} \), the prediction error is quantified by the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the expected distribution and the point mass of the observed goal:
\[
\Delta I = D_{KL}\left( \delta_{g_{obs}} \,\|\, P(G_t | H_{t-1}) \right) = -\log P(g_{obs} | H_{t-1}).
\]
This \(\Delta I\) represents the **informational surprise** (units: nats or bits). A larger surprise corresponds to lower probability of the observed goal under the expectation.

**Demand/Value Function**: Let \( V: \mathcal{G} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) denote the subjective demand (utility) of a goal object, with \( V(g) \) higher for more demanded goals. Tolman notes disruption occurs when \( V(g_{obs}) < V(G_{exp}) \) but not overtly when \( V(g_{obs}) > V(G_{exp}) \) [PAGE 1]. This asymmetry suggests a non-linear mapping from surprise \(\Delta I\) to observable behavioral disruption \( D \). We propose:
\[
D = f(\Delta I, \Delta V), \quad \Delta V = V(g_{obs}) - V(G_{exp}),
\]
with \( f \) being a function that saturates or becomes invisible for \( \Delta V > 0 \) due to rapid consumption masking subtle motor variations. For \( \Delta V < 0 \), \( f \) amplifies the effect, leading to hunting or rejection behaviors.

**Formal Assumptions**:
1. The agent computes \( P(G_t | H_{t-1}) \) instantaneously upon entering the goal box.
2. \( V(g) \) is pre-wired or learned through prior reinforcement history.
3. Behavioral disruption \( D \) is proportional to the product of surprise and a valence-dependent gain factor \( \gamma(\Delta V) \), where \( \gamma(\Delta V) \approx 0 \) for \( \Delta V \gg 0 \) and \( \gamma(\Delta V) > 0 \) for \( \Delta V \leq 0 \).

**Singularity/Breakdown Regimes**:
- **Perfect Prediction**: \( P(G_{exp} | H_{t-1}) = 1 \Rightarrow \Delta I = 0 \). No disruption regardless of \( \Delta V \), as there is no surprise.
- **Zero Demand Contrast**: \( V(g) \) is constant across \( \mathcal{G} \). Then \( \Delta V = 0 \), and disruption depends solely on \( \Delta I \); any substitution causes equal disruption, contradicting Tolman's observation of asymmetry. Thus, the model requires \( V \) to be non-constant.
- **Measurement Limit**: If observational resolution \( \rho \) is coarse, small \( D \) (from positive \( \Delta V \)) may fall below detection threshold, creating the illusion of "no overt disruption." Tolman's suggestion of motion pictures acknowledges this regime shift.

**Units**: \(\Delta I\) in nats (natural units of information); \( V \) in arbitrary utility units (e.g., calories, hedonic tone); \( D \) in normalized behavioral units (e.g., deviation from baseline speed/direction).
