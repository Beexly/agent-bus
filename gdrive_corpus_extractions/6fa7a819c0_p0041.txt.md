Modern analysis no longer asks "does sport hurt study?" but rather investigates the complex bidirectional relationship between physical exertion and cognitive function. Yet, this progression creates a multi-week trap for contemporary observers. The trap lies in assuming that because athletic participation is now broadly celebrated for its holistic benefits, the initial fears were completely unfounded. In reality, the concerns about physical injury (leading to concussions and career-ending trauma) and time displacement (from academics) have transformed rather than vanished. They manifest in modern debates over NIL (Name, Image, Likeness) agreements, athlete burnout, and the medicalization of sports injuries. Thus, the 1905 data does not simply validate football; it initiates a continuous dialectic between institutional utilitarianism (producing well-rounded leaders) and individual cost-benefit analysis, a conflict still unresolved a century later.
