Three verifiable RLVR (reinforcement learning via verifiable rewards) counterfactual veto scenarios with formal predictions.

Veto Scenario 1 — Null sign-gestalt-readiness.
Setup: Organism is in a drive state \(D = (\sigma, \Sigma)\) where \(\Sigma(s) = \emptyset\) (no means-object candidates are readied; “more or less vague” [PAGE 472] collapses to null). The goal-object \(g\) is present and energetically available.
Formal prediction: \[
\Pr(\text{directed commerce-with } m \in \mathcal{M} \mid \Sigma = \emptyset) \approx 0,
\]
while drive arousal (somatic indicators of \(\sigma\)) remains elevated.
Verification: In an animal model, selectively impair sensory-association cortex so that sign-gestalt-readiness cannot form, then present food. Prediction: hunger behaviors (approach orientation) occur without directed selection of the specific food object; activity is random or suppressed.

Veto Scenario 2 — Sub-threshold environmental disruption.
Setup: Behavior-act \(A\) reaches goal-object \(g\) via a long route \(L(A, s, g)\). The environment changes by \(\|s' - s\| < \epsilon_{\min}\) (minor alteration insufficient to trigger docility).
Formal prediction: \[
\Pr(\mathcal{D}(A, s', g) = B \mid \|s' - s\| < \epsilon_{\min}) < \alpha
\]
for small significance \(\alpha\); \(A\) persists despite suboptimal route length.
Verification: In a maze task, gradually shift the goal location by centimeters across trials. Prediction: rats do not switch to a shorter alternative route until the displacement exceeds \(\epsilon_{\min}\), consistent with “any major change” [PAGE 472] rather than any change.

Veto Scenario 3 — Second-order vs. first-order drive conflict under scarcity.
Setup: Second-order curiosity drive \(D^{(2)}_{\text{curiosity}}\) activates toward an unexplored unfamiliar object [PAGE 472], while first-order hunger drive \(D^{(1)}_{\text{hunger}}\) activates toward food. Energy budget \(E_{\text{budget}}\) is below critical threshold \(E_{\text{crit}}\).
Formal prediction: \[
E_{\text{budget}} < E_{\text{crit}} \implies \Sigma_{\text{curiosity}}(s) \text{ is suppressed}, \quad \Pr(\text{approach novelty}) \downarrow.
\]
Verification: Food-deprive animals to different levels, then expose them to a novel object in an open field. Prediction: high-deprivation animals show reduced investigation of the novel object relative to sated animals, because curiosity is ancillary to first-order ends [PAGE 472] and is vetoed by energy scarcity.

Each scenario is falsifiable: a null result (directed behavior despite \(\Sigma = \emptyset\), route switching under minor change, or curiosity dominance under starvation) falsifies the corresponding Tolman-derived constraint as formalized above.
