The fixed point is unstable because \(\mathcal{U}\) (umpire judgment) is uncalibrated [PAGE 97]; small changes in umpire strictness flip the payoff matrix. No betting lines are embedded in this recursion.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto layers.

Physical layer—pressure and projection. A projecting nail or iron plate on a shoe presents a small contact area \(A\) under load \(F\), generating localized pressure \(P = F/A\). SI units: \(P\) in pascals (Pa), \(F\) in newtons (N), \(A\) in square meters (\(\text{m}^2\)). Because \(A\) for a nail tip is orders of magnitude smaller than for a flat sole, \(P\) exceeds soft-tissue tolerance and creates puncture/laceration risk. The rule bans such projections outright [PAGE 97], which is a hard physical veto: no correction of material property can make a projecting nail safe; it must be removed. Leather cleats are permitted [PAGE 97], presumably because their \(A\) is larger and their shear failure mode is gradual rather than catastrophic.

Biomechanical layer—head protection. Head protectors cannot use "sole leather, papier mache, or other hard or unyielding substance" [PAGE 97]. In impact biomechanics, hard materials have high elastic modulus \(E\) and low strain-to-failure, transmitting acceleration to the skull rather than absorbing energy through foam compression. The rule's veto is material-class-based rather than threshold-based; it excludes entire substance classes but does not publish a maximum \(E\) or a Head Impact Criterion (HIC) limit. Because \(\mathcal{U}\) (umpire judgment) is the sole arbiter of padding adequacy [PAGE 97], the biomechanical veto is only as reliable as inter-umpire consistency. Breakdown regime: in low light, wet conditions, or high game speed, the umpire cannot reliably distinguish compliant padding from a thin hard shell, so the veto fails silently.

Weather layer—environmental modulation. Leather cleats are allowed [PAGE 97], but leather's coefficient of friction \(\mu\) and stiffness are functions of temperature and moisture. In rain, wet leather softens (\(\mu\) changes, \(E\) drops), altering traction and the effective projection of any embedded hardware. Iron plates, banned when dry [PAGE 97], become even more dangerous in wet conditions because sudden grip transition after a slip generates high shear impulse. The rule contains no weather-contingent clause; the veto is binary (allowed/banned) and does not scale with \(\mu(T,\text{humidity})\). Cross-layer adjudication: an umpire may veto a player's equipment under Section 3 if the projected surface poses danger in current weather, but the rule gives no procedure for revisiting a previously accepted cleat when weather shifts mid-game.

Veto proof summary: The two-minute suspension rule [PAGE 97] provides a temporal veto gate. If \(t_{\text{inspection}} - t_{\text{notice}} > 120\) s, \(\Sigma=1\) and the player is removed. This gate is robust only if the umpire's timepiece is synchronized to game operations; absent that, the veto is advisory. The discovery-spot enforcement switch [PAGE 97] provides a spatial veto: intervening play moves the penalty forward, which can be adjudicated by reviewing the sequence of downs. All three layers converge on the same singularity: uncalibrated human judgment \(\mathcal{U}\) with no SI tolerance, making the system veto-capable but not veto-proof.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios, each with formal verification conditions and predictions. RLVR here denotes reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards counterfactual auditing: each scenario specifies a falsifiable prediction that can be checked against game film, official spot records, or time-stamped umpire logs. No betting lines are used.

Scenario 1 — Discovery-spot veto after intervening play.
Setup: Team A makes an illegal substitution during down \(k\). One full play \(k+1\) occurs before the umpire discovers the infraction. The 1915 rule mandates enforcement from \(s_{\text{disc}}\), not \(s_{\text{down}}\) [PAGE 97].
Verification condition: Compare the official penalty-spot coordinate \(\mathbf{p}_{\text{recorded}}\) with \(\mathbf{s}_{\text{down}}\) and \(\mathbf{s}_{\text{disc}}\) extracted from film. Pass iff \(\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{recorded}} - \mathbf{s}_{\text{disc}}\| < \epsilon_{\text{spot}}\) and \(\|\mathbf{p}_{\text{recorded}} - \mathbf{s}_{\text{down}}\| > \delta_{\text{spot}}\), where \(\epsilon_{\text{spot}}\) and \(\delta_{\text{spot}}\) are measurement tolerances in meters.
Prediction: In games where play intervened, the enforced spot shifts by \(\Delta x = \|\mathbf{s}_{\text{disc}} - \mathbf{s}_{\text{down}}\| > 0\) yards (converted to meters) relative to the down spot. In games with zero intervening plays, \(\Delta x = 0\). Falsifiable via spot charts.

Scenario 2 — Two-minute correction-clock veto.
Setup: Umpire notices a projecting nail or illegal head-protector material at time \(t_0\) [PAGE 97]. The player is given \(\tau_{\text{corr}} = 120\) s to correct; failure yields suspension \(\Sigma=1\) [PAGE 97].
Verification condition: Time-stamp \(t_0\) (umpire notification) and \(t_c\) (correction completion or next snap). Pass iff suspension occurs exactly when \(t_c - t_0 > 120\) s and no suspension occurs when \(t_c - t_0 \le 120\) s.
Prediction: Players corrected within 120 s remain eligible; players not corrected by \(t_0+120\) s are suspended. The boundary at 120 s is sharp; a player corrected at 119 s plays, at 121 s sits. Falsifiable via sideline logs; breakdown if no stopwatch was assigned, in which case the prediction is conditioned on umpire testimony and is weaker.

Scenario 3 — Illegal head-protector material veto.
Setup: A player wears a head protector constructed with sole leather, papier mache, or another hard/unyielding substance [PAGE 97]. Umpire judgment \(\mathcal{U}\) deems the device dangerous.
Verification condition: Material sample \(m\) is tested for membership in banned set \(\mathcal{B}\) [PAGE 97]. Pass iff \(\mathcal{A}(\text{device}) = 0\) whenever material \(\in \mathcal{B}\), and \(\mathcal{A} = 1\) for compliant padded devices, subject to \(\mathcal{U} = \text{safe}\).
Prediction: Any head protector containing sole leather or papier mache is barred from the game regardless of padding thickness, because the rule prohibits the material class itself [PAGE 97]. A compliant device with soft padding passes. Falsifiable by equipment-room inspection records; breakdown regime is inter-umpire variance in \(\mathcal{U}\), which this page does not quantify [PAGE 97].

All three scenarios are verifiable without reference to odds, spreads, or moneylines.
