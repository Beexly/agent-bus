<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Galton’s methodological imperative directly descends from earlier attempts to quantify biological inheritance, notably Carl von Nägeli’s experiments on plant hybridization and Charles Darwin’s own observations in *The Variation of Animals and Plants Under Domestication*. Galton inverted the experimental biology approach by focusing on statistical aggregation in natural populations, thereby avoiding the ethical and practical constraints of human experimentation. This lineage established the "pedigree method" as a cornerstone of biometrical genetics, later formalized by Karl Pearson’s correlation coefficients and Ronald Fisher’s analysis of variance.

The "multi-week trap" emerges in contemporary contexts when sequential observational data (e.g., weekly sales figures, monthly health metrics) are misinterpreted through a static causal lens. For instance, in epidemic modeling, using case counts from week $t$ to predict week $t+1$ without accounting for changing public behavior (a form of "nurture" variation) can lead to severe underestimation of intervention effectiveness. Historical parallel: Early actuarial tables assumed mortality rates were constant across generations, ignoring improvements in sanitation and medicine—violating Galton’s "usual conditions" clause—and thus produced biased life expectancy forecasts.
