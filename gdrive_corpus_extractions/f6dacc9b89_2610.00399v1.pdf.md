generates a trajectory {ŷ₀, ŷ₁, …, ŷ_N} whose terminal energy values populate the IBL state space. Sample ŷ_{0,j} ∼ N(0, I);
for i = 0, …, N−1: ŷ_{i+1,j} ← ŷ_{i,j} − α∇_{ŷ_{i,j}}E_θ(x, ŷ_{i,j});
return ŷ* = argmin_j E_θ(x, ŷ_{N,j}).
Units: α is step size (dimensionless); N is step count (dimensionless integer); I is identity covariance over the candidate embedding space.

P_s(k) = exp(V(k)/τ_s) / Σ_{i∈A} exp(V(k_i)/τ_s).

V(k) = Σ_{i∈M} P_r(k_i) x_i.

The intellectual lineage of MERITED traces back through at least four historical layers, creating a multi-week trap structure in which early commitments to specific formalisms constrain later design degrees of freedom.

Layer 1: Energy-based learning. The paper cites Ackley, Hinton, and Sejnowski (1985) for Boltzmann machines [PAGE 2, 9], LeCun et al. (2006) for the energy-based learning tutorial [PAGE 3, 9], and Du and Mordatch (2019) for implicit generation via MCMC gradient flows [PAGE 2, 9]. The foundational trap here is the choice of the Boltzmann distribution p_θ(x) ∝ exp(−E_θ(x))/Z(θ). Because Z(θ) is intractable in high dimensions, the system must rely on MCMC approximation (Eq. 1) and Contrastive Divergence. This commits MERITED to a gradient-based optimization inner loop at inference time, which is computationally expensive and introduces the exact metacognitive problem the paper seeks to solve: how many MCMC steps are sufficient?

Layer 2: Cognitive architecture and IBLT. The paper integrates ACT-R (Anderson 1993; Anderson, Matessa, and Lebiere 1997) as the conceptual analogue for instance memory chunks [PAGE 1, 4, 9], and specifically Instance-Based Learning Theory from Gonzalez, Lerch, and Lebiere (2003) and Gonzalez and Dutt (2011) [PAGE 4, 9]. The IBL formalism assumes decisions are made by blending retrieved instances from experience, with activation governed by decay, noise, and similarity (Eq. 4). The multi-week trap: IBL was originally developed for human dynamic decision-making in repeated binary choice and sequential tasks (Lebiere, Gonzalez, and Martin 2007 [PAGE 9]; Bugbee and Gonzalez 2022 [PAGE 9]), not for allocating compute in differentiable neural architectures. Transposing it to EBT MCMC depth control requires assuming that human-like resource-rationality (Lieder and Griffiths 2020 [PAGE 3, 9]; Shenhav et al. 2017 [PAGE 1, 9]) transfers to silicon metacognition. This is an unproven cross-species analogy.

Layer 3: EBT architecture. Gladstone et al. (2025) introduced Energy-Based Transformers as scalable learners and thinkers [PAGE 1, 2, 3, 9]; Xu et al. (2025) extended energy-based diffusion to language [PAGE 9]. The EBT replaces direct autoregressive generation with iterative energy minimization (Algorithm 1). The trap: EBTs inherit the training instability of EBMs (Grathwohl et al. 2019 [PAGE 9]) and require careful Langevin noise scheduling and step-size scaling (τ=1.0, energy-scaled [PAGE 11]).

Layer 4: Prior metacognitive AI integration. CogRouter (Yang et al. 2026) integrates ACT-R into an LLM pipeline to predetermine reasoning depth based on uncertainty [PAGE 1, 6, 7, 9]. MERITED's distinguishing claim is that it relies on EBMs for uncertainty estimation and computation control rather than selecting discrete reasoning depth [PAGE 2]. However, the comparison to GPT-2-CogRouter is compromised because CogRouter "was not designed for the type of question answering response where a single next token is evaluated for accuracy" [PAGE 7], requiring ad hoc adjustments.

Multi-week traps in the experimental timeline: (1) The IBL model's optimal-stopping variant must handle delayed credit assignment because there are no immediate utility signals in stopping tasks; credit for the final stop decision must be assigned to previous "continue" instances, as in Nguyen, McDonald, and Gonzalez (2023) [PAGE 5, 10]. This temporal credit assignment problem mirrors multi-week reinforcement learning traps where immediate rewards are sparse and the true utility of an action is revealed only after a trajectory terminates. (2) The 450-configuration hyperparameter sweep on the same evaluation data creates a look-ahead bias: the reported p-values (depth choice p = 0.036; optimal stop p = 0.068 over GPT-2) are explicitly descriptive, not confirmatory [PAGE 7]. (3) ARC-Easy appears in both training and evaluation [PAGE 6], creating a temporal contamination trap where the model may have memorized validation-split structures during pretraining on ClimbMix.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
MERITED implements a second-order metacognitive loop: the EBT models the world (question answering), and the IBL model models the EBT's own computational behavior. This recursion generates three interacting dynamical regimes: crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

Crisis habit regression. Under uncertainty, humans and models face a choice between defaulting to a low-effort habit (shallow depth) or allocating additional resources. The IBL memory is prepopulated with outcomes where depth d ∈ {1,2,4,8,10} yielded +1 or −1 [PAGE 6]. If the current query's energy distribution p_d(q) and embedding e(q) are dissimilar to memory instances, retrieval probability P_r(k_i) (Eq. 4) collapses toward the noise floor σ = 0.5 [PAGE 4]. In that regime, the softmax action selection (Eq. 2) with τ_s → 0 becomes deterministic over the highest-valued retrieved instance, but if all retrieved values are near zero due to poor similarity, the policy regresses to a habit depth—likely the shallowest (d=1) because it is the least computationally costly default and was precomputed for all 20 memory questions. This is crisis habit regression: under metacognitive uncertainty, the system falls back to minimal compute rather than deeper reasoning.

Counter-trap. The optimal-stopping policy (Algorithm 3) queries IBL at every MCMC step and stops if c < τ [PAGE 5]. The counter-trap is that the IBL model may learn to stop prematurely on items that superficially resemble easy memory instances but are actually hard OpenBookQA items requiring multi-step reasoning [PAGE 6]. Because the memory contains only 360 instances and test questions do not update memory (history-less choices [PAGE 6]), the system cannot correct a premature-stop habit online. The OpenBookQA results bear this out: optimal stopping achieves only 28.4% with mean depth 1.95 [PAGE 7], barely above static d=1 (26.4%) [PAGE 8], suggesting the counter-trap of over-trusting shallow-depth similarity matches on hard items. The depth-choice policy avoids this by committing to mean depth 6.22 on OpenBookQA [PAGE 7], but pays 2.09 s versus 0.73 s.

Clock. The wall-clock constraint is the external forcing function. Static depth 10 costs 3.07 s per question [PAGE 8]; MERITED optimal stopping costs 0.73 s, a 76.1% reduction [PAGE 7]. The clock creates a Pareto frontier: depth choice yields the highest observed accuracy (58% ARC, 29.4% OB) at 2.09 s; optimal stopping matches ARC accuracy at 0.73 s but loses 1.0 point on OpenBookQA [PAGE 7]. The recursive theory of mind here is that the IBL model must model not only the EBT's accuracy landscape but also its own inference-time cost, and the two objectives (accuracy vs. time) are only partially aligned. When the clock is binding (e.g., real-time QA), the optimal policy regresses toward shallow stopping; when accuracy is binding, it commits to deeper one-shot selection. The system cannot simultaneously maximize both because the energy landscape does not guarantee that marginal MCMC steps improve every candidate (flat or non-convex regions [PAGE 5]).
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three cross-layer veto conditions can be adjudicated from the paper's formalism and hardware constraints.

Physical layer veto — flat energy landscape. If ∥∇_{ŷ}E_θ(x, ŷ)∥₂ ≈ 0 across the candidate space, the Langevin update (Eq. 1) produces negligible displacement per step: x̃_{i+1} ≈ x̃_i + ω_k. Additional MCMC steps consume wall-clock time without reducing energy, so the marginal benefit ∆_{i,j} ≈ 0. Veto: any further depth allocation beyond d=1 is physically wasteful. Evidence: optimal stopping on OpenBookQA terminates at mean depth 1.95 [PAGE 7], consistent with early flat-landscape vetoes. Breakdown: if the landscape is non-convex with a low narrow minimum separated by a high barrier, gradient descent from a random initialization ŷ₀ ∼ N(0, I) may never reach it within N steps, and the veto incorrectly halts search prematurely.

Biomechanical/cognitive layer veto — IBL memory saturation. The IBL retrieval probability depends on instance activation A(k) (Eq. 4), which includes decay D = Σ(t − t′)^{−d} with δ = 0.25 [PAGE 4]. With only 100 depth-choice instances and 360 optimal-stopping instances prepopulated at time zero [PAGE 6], and with test queries not updating memory [PAGE 6], the memory is static and small. Veto: when the maximum retrieval probability P_r(k_i) across memory falls below the noise floor induced by σ = 0.5, the IBL policy's utility estimate V(k) (Eq. 3) is dominated by noise rather than experience. The system should veto IBL control and revert to a static depth (e.g., d=2, the best static mean at 55% ARC [PAGE 8]). This veto is triggered by low cosine similarity between e(q) and memory embeddings in Eq. (8).

Environmental/infrastructure layer veto — hardware and backend constraints. All evaluation runs on the PyTorch MPS backend [PAGE 6], while training used 8×B200 GPUs [PAGE 11]. Veto: if the inference device lacks sufficient memory bandwidth to store the EBT's 1024-dimensional embeddings and energy distributions for multiple candidates simultaneously, the one-shot depth-choice policy (which requires computing depth-1 state, querying IBL, then running a separate trajectory when d > 1 [PAGE 6]) becomes infeasible. The optimal-stopping policy, which interleaves IBL queries with MCMC steps, imposes even higher per-step latency because it queries IBL after every depth [PAGE 5]. On a constrained device, the veto selects the cheapest feasible policy: static depth d=1 (0.35 s) or GPT-2 (0.07 s) [PAGE 8], sacrificing accuracy for deployability.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios, each with a formal prediction derived from the paper's equations and reported results.

Veto Scenario 1: High-entropy depth-1 energy distribution forces deep allocation. Verifiable condition: compute the depth-1 energy-induced distribution p_1(q) = softmax(−E_1(q)) over candidates [PAGE 6, Eq. 7]. If the Shannon entropy H(p_1) = −Σ_c p_1(c) log p_1(c) exceeds a threshold (e.g., H > ln(|Y|) × 0.8 for |Y| = 4 choices, indicating near-uniform uncertainty), veto shallow depths d ∈ {1,2} and force d ≥ 8. Prediction: On OpenBookQA items satisfying this entropy condition, MERITED depth-choice will select d ≥ 8 (consistent with its observed mean depth of 6.22 [PAGE 7]) and will outperform static d=1 (26.4% accuracy [PAGE 8]) by at least 3 percentage points, approaching static d=8 (27.2%) or better, while incurring the 2.45 s runtime cost. Verifiability: entropy is computable from the EBT's energy outputs without labels.

Veto Scenario 2: Zero marginal energy reduction triggers optimal-stop veto. Verifiable condition: at MCMC step i, compute ∆_{i,j} = E_θ(x, ŷ_{i,j}) − E_θ(x, ŷ_{i+1,j}) [PAGE 5]. If ∆_{i,j} < ε for a small ε (e.g., ε = 10⁻⁴ in arbitrary energy units) across all candidates j, veto continuation and break, accepting the current candidate. Prediction: On ARC-Easy items where this condition triggers before i = 4, MERITED optimal-stopping will match the 58% ARC accuracy of depth choice [PAGE 7] while reducing mean depth below its observed 2.69 [PAGE 7], because ARC-Easy items are easier and their energy landscapes settle rapidly [PAGE 5]. Runtime will fall below the observed 0.73 s [PAGE 8]. Verifiability: ∆_{i,j} is directly observable from the EBT's energy evaluations during inference.

Veto Scenario 3: IBL similarity mismatch forces static-depth fallback. Verifiable condition: compute the IBL state s_d(q) = (p_d(q), e(q)) [PAGE 6, Eq. 7] and its maximum weighted partial similarity S(s, s′) to memory instances [PAGE 6, Eq. 8]. If max_{i∈M} S(s, s_i) < 0.3 (indicating the query is dissimilar to all prepopulated memory instances), veto the IBL policy and fall back to static depth d=2 (the best static EBT depth at 55% ARC / 26.6% OB [PAGE 8]). Prediction: On OpenBookQA items triggering this fallback, MERITED depth-choice accuracy will collapse from its observed 29.4% [PAGE 8] toward the static d=2 baseline of 26.6%, because the thin 100-instance memory cannot generalize to dissimilar items [PAGE 6]. Runtime will match static d=2 (0.65 s) rather than depth-choice (2.09 s) [PAGE 8], since no separate deep trajectory is launched. Verifiability: similarity is computable from Eq. (8) using the EBT's embeddings and energy distributions; the memory contents are fixed at model initialization [PAGE 6].
