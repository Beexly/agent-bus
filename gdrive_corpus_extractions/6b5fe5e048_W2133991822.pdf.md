Crisis habit regression: When a player is fully occluded, the Kalman filter has no memory beyond its current Gaussian state; it continues purely on prediction until the 10-frame termination clock expires [PAGE 9], then habitually spawns a new track with a new identity rather than resurrecting the old one. This is the paper's observed 219 identity switches [PAGE 10], a direct signature of recursive online tracking's inability to maintain identity through crisis [PAGE 11].

Counter-trap: CEM exploits offline optimization both forward and backward in time, allowing it to connect broken trajectories and reduce identity switches to 51–60 while lengthening mean trajectories to 25.2–37.2 m versus Kalman's 11.5 m [PAGE 11]. However, this counter-trap is conditional on batch availability; it cannot operate in the online crisis moment because detections must be saved for the full batch before trajectory construction begins [PAGE 11].

Clock: The system was specified to run in real time at 33 fps [PAGE 2]. Kalman achieves 0.03 s per frame including detection [PAGE 10], satisfying the clock. CEM requires 0.12–0.32 s per frame excluding detection [PAGE 10], a 4–10× overrun relative to the 0.033 s frame budget, and its processing time might increase significantly with the number of targets [PAGE 11]. Thus the counter-trap is vetoed by the clock whenever real-time operation is required.

Multi-agent theory of mind is instantiated minimally in the Eexc mutual exclusion term, which encodes the physical constraint that two objects cannot occupy the same space simultaneously [PAGE 9]. Identity switches are therefore failures to model other players' intent and trajectory during physical interaction; the tracker treats other bodies as static obstacles rather than intentional agents, which explains why switch count explodes precisely in the high-interaction soccer context [PAGE 4].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The sensor suite consists of three cameras covering an indoor 20 m × 40 m field, rectified and stitched per frame into 1920 × 480 images [PAGE 4]. The background is a plain, relatively stable temperature, enabling threshold-based segmentation [PAGE 5]. However, the side-view camera angle produces a high degree of physical interaction and occlusions [PAGE 4].

Biomechanical layer: Players appear as grey blobs because thermal sensing captures no colour or texture [PAGE 5]. Loose or layered clothing creates colder body areas that split one person into multiple blobs [PAGE 7]. The paper's detection routines address this: tall blobs (one person behind another) are split horizontally via convex-hull convexity defects; wide blobs (people side by side) are split vertically from head-position minima on the upper edge; disconnected cold regions are reconnected using candidate rectangles of standard height and width one third of height [PAGE 6], [PAGE 7]. Full or severe occlusion cannot be solved by frame-basis detection [PAGE 5], constituting a hard biomechanical veto.

Weather/environmental layer: Thermal imaging is light-independent, works in dark environments, and is privacy-preserving [PAGE 2], [PAGE 3]. It requires that human temperature differ from background temperature [PAGE 11]; outdoor scenes are possible but performance depends entirely on detection quality [PAGE 11]. Mounting the camera above the scene to capture a top-view could significantly reduce occlusions [PAGE 11].

Exact veto/breakdown regimes: (i) A blob is discarded if its foreground white-pixel ratio is below 15% [PAGE 7]. (ii) Overlapping candidate rectangles above 45% overlap suppress all but the highest white-pixel-ratio candidate [PAGE 7]. (iii) A track is terminated after 10 frames without assigned detections [PAGE 9]. (iv) High measurement noise covariance R causes the system to rely less on measurements [PAGE 8]; with R fixed at 0.1, sudden measurement outliers are under-weighted. (v) The constant-velocity dynamic model Edyn in CEM breaks down under the abrupt, erratic motion characteristic of soccer [PAGE 4]. These are the singularity boundaries outside which the reported 70.36% MOTA and 33 fps real-time guarantees no longer hold.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
