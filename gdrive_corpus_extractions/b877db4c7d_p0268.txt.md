\]
so pooling N workers reduces variance not at all. The consolidation of uncertainties is vetoed by perfectly correlated environmental exposure.

Adjudication rule: An incorporation or syndicate arrangement survives only if expected consolidation benefit exceeds the credit-cost offset [PAGE 255] AND no common physical/biomechanical/weather veto shuts down all members simultaneously. If veto probability q > 0 and the payoff under veto is total capital loss -K (USD), the expected value contains a term -qK that can dominate any diversification gain.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions.

Scenario 1 — Professional syndicate physical veto. A lawyers' syndicate incorporates with limited liability and negligible capital [PAGE 255]. A county-wide courthouse closure imposes a physical veto, halting litigation work for four weeks. Prediction: Weekly billable output falls to zero for 100% of members simultaneously; limited liability transfers fixed obligations to creditors but cannot prevent operational cessation; because the credit-standing offset raises refinancing costs, the firm defaults despite limited liability. Verifiable metric: member billable hours = 0 for the veto window; missed debt service occurs within the same window.

Scenario 2 — Speculative specialization common-shock. A speculation market concentrates uncertainty-bearing in a small set of specialist dealers [PAGE 255]. A regulatory regime change occurs outside the support of every dealer's subjective prior Q_i (Knightian surprise). Prediction: Dealers' maxmin expected utility collapses; effective bid-ask spreads widen discontinuously; uncertainty is re-concentrated in the dealers' books rather than dissipated into the market. Verifiable metric: quote dispersion and effective spread spike while realized volatility of the underlying remains inside historically observed bounds, indicating a model-set breakdown rather than ordinary risk.

Scenario 3 — Limited-liability credit-offset. A firm incorporates and undertakes a high-uncertainty project, shifting tail losses beyond K to creditors [PAGE 255]. Prediction: Creditors price the transfer, so the post-incorporation cost of debt rises by approximately the expected transferred loss per dollar of debt:
\[
r_{\text{post}} - r_{\text{pre}} \approx \frac{\mathbb{E}[T(K,x)]}{D} \quad [\text{dimensionless}],
\]
where D is debt outstanding (USD) and \mathbb{E}[T] is in USD. Owner security gain is fully offset; firm value shows no abnormal positive jump. Verifiable metric: pre- versus post-incorporation debt interest rates and secondary-market firm-value multiples satisfy \Delta r \approx \mathbb{E}[T]/D with no corresponding Tobin's-q increase.
