<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let the template and test frame feature vectors be \(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{y}_j\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\), with \(d=52\) for 2D+3D, \(d=30\) for 2D, and \(d=22\) for 3D, following the paper’s feature count statement: “total count of features is 52, comprising 30 features in 2D and 22 features in 3D” [PAGE 9]. Coordinates are in pose-estimator image units or normalized pose units, not necessarily calibrated meters. Joint angles and angular features are in radians; the paper states “it is necessary to convert the non-radian numbers into radians” [PAGE 10]. The threshold \(t\), ratio \(q\), feature score \(S_i\), frame score \(\mathrm{Score}_{i,j}\), adaptive coefficient \(C\), participation counts \(N,P,Q\), path length \(l\), and final score \(FS\) are dimensionless or percentage-point quantities, as defined below.

The uniform per-feature budget is
\[
S_1=S_2=\cdots=S_n=\frac{100}{n},
\]
which allocates 100 percentage points across \(n\) features [PAGE 12, Eq. (4)]. The relative deviation is
\[
q=\frac{|x_i-y_i|}{x_i},
\]
using the paper’s exact template-denominator form [PAGE 12, Eq. (5)]. The tolerance-gated feature score is
\[
S_i=S_i \quad \text{for } q\leq t,
\]
\[
S_i=S_i(1-q+t) \quad \text{for } q\in(t,1],
\]
\[
S_i=0 \quad \text{for } q\in(1,\infty).
\]
The paper describes the first case as “if q is less than the threshold parameter t, it is considered an acceptable error, and the score remains unchanged” [PAGE 12]. The frame score is
\[
\mathrm{Score}_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_k,
\]
[PAGE 13, Eq. (7)], and the MED distance is
\[
\mathrm{MED}(i,j)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Score}_{i,j}}\times 100-1.
\]
This is the paper’s Eq. (8) [PAGE 13]. It is a decreasing transform: a perfect frame score of 100 gives MED 0; lower scores give larger positive distances. The final path score is
\[
FS=\frac{\sum_{i=0,j=0}^{n,n}\mathrm{Score}_{i,j}}{l},
\]
[PAGE 13, Eq. (9)], where \(l\) is the path length. The adaptive coefficient is
\[
C(x_{i,j})=\frac{2\max(a,b)}{a+b}N(x_{i,j}),
\]
[PAGE 14, Eq. (11)], where \(a,b\) are the two sequence lengths and \(N(x_{i,j})=P_{i,j}+Q_{i,j}\) is the participation count from the two reuse matrices [PAGE 15]. The recurrence is
\[
\mathrm{ACDTW}(1,1)=\mathrm{MED}(1,1),
\]
\[
\mathrm{ACDTW}(i,j)=\mathrm{MED}(i,j)+\min\left\{\mathrm{ACDTW}(i-1,j-1),\ \mathrm{ACDTW}(i-1,j)+C(x_{i-1,j})\mathrm{MED}(i,j),\ \mathrm{ACDTW}(i,j-1)+C(x_{i,j-1})\mathrm{MED}(i,j)\right\},
\]
[PAGE 15, Eq. (12)]. The diagonal move has no reuse penalty; vertical and horizontal moves add the adaptive penalty. The paper states “When the coefficient is zero, it corresponds to the standard DTW algorithm” [PAGE 15]. The evaluation metrics are
\[
\mathrm{Acc}=\frac{\text{Successful matching actions}}{\text{Number of actions}},
\]
\[
\mathrm{Rate80}=\frac{M+N}{\text{Number of action matches}},
\]
and
\[
\rho=1-\frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)},
\]
[PAGE 17, Eqs. (13)–(15)].

Breakdown and singularity regimes must be stated explicitly. First, Eq. (5) is singular when \(x_i=0\); \(q\) is undefined, and if \(x_i\to0\) with nonzero numerator, \(q\to\infty\), forcing the score to zero or producing a numerical overflow. The paper does not provide an epsilon guard. Second, Eq. (8) diverges as \(\mathrm{Score}_{i,j}\to0\); a zero frame score gives no finite MED distance. Third, if the feature dimension changes between template and test, Eq. (4) and Eq. (7) lose their \(100/n\) normalization, so scores are not comparable. Fourth, Eq. (11) is undefined for \(a+b=0\), i.e., empty sequences. Fifth, Eq. (9) is undefined for \(l=0\). Sixth, Eq. (15) is undefined for \(n=1\). Seventh, if \(\mathrm{MED}(i,j)=0\) everywhere, the recurrence has ties among zero-cost paths and the backtracking tie-break determines the path, so “optimal” is not unique. These are domain-exclusion conditions, not minor numerical details.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s lineage is retrocausal in the sense that a later unlabeled template-matching objective reinterprets earlier DTW constraints and earlier supervised action-quality regression. The DTW branch runs from classical dynamic time warping and shapeDTW to adaptive constrained DTW; the paper cites Li et al.’s “Adaptively constrained dynamic time warping for time series classification and clustering” [PAGE 26–27] and states that its adaptive penalty addresses “the specific challenge of multiple frames mapping to a single frame within an action time series” [PAGE 14]. The action-quality branch runs from Pirsiavash et al.’s regression on spatiotemporal pose features, through Parmar et al., I3D-MLP, I3D-DAE, and HGCN [PAGE 2–4]. The skeleton branch runs through MediaPipe and earlier Kinect/OpenPose-style feature construction [PAGE 5–6]. MED-ACDTW sits at the intersection: it keeps DTW’s monotone alignment, replaces Euclidean distance with a normalized multi-feature score, and replaces score labels with template videos.

Several multi-week traps follow from that genealogy. The first is accuracy drift: over successive tuning weeks, a team can select \(t=0.2\) because it gives 98.04% Accuracy, while overlooking that Rate80 falls to 65.00% [PAGE 19]. The second is false symmetry: because Eq. (5) uses \(x_i\) in the denominator, MED is template-referenced; swapping template and test is not an invariant operation. The third is over-penalization: Eq. (11) increases with \(|a-b|\), and the paper assumes that “when two video frames significantly differ in length, they are likely not from the same category of video” [PAGE 14]. That assumption can fail for legitimate tempo variation, fatigue, or camera frame-rate differences. The fourth is dataset-perspective leakage: the paper says the AQA dataset “often suffers from frequent camera switches, resulting in inconsistent perspectives” [PAGE 18], which is why BGym was constructed with a standardized perspective. The fifth is template leakage across splits: the paper’s second split holds out a character’s actions and reports Spearman 0.9427 for MED-ACDTW versus 0.8465 for I3D-DAE [PAGE 22–23], which is the more informative generalization test. The sixth is feature-fusion overclaim: 2D+3D improves Rate80 to 95.33%, but 3D alone already reaches 93.27%, so the gain is real but not a universal replacement for modality-specific validation [PAGE 20].

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when pose noise, occlusion, or perspective change makes the evaluator fall back to the old Euclidean-DTW habit or to raw visual impression. The paper’s own motivation is that Euclidean DTW “merely distinguishes the action without assigning it an appropriately low score” [PAGE 2], so regression to Euclidean distance is a known failure mode. In a crisis, a system that abandons MED and uses ED-ACDTW loses discrimination: Table 4 reports ED-ACDTW with 3D features at Rate80 16.99%, versus MED-ACDTW 3D at 93.27% [PAGE 20]. That is a measurable habit-regression signature. The counter-trap is optimizing the visible surrogate: a student or engineer can tune \(t\), template choice, or feature subsets to maximize Accuracy while degrading Rate80; the \(t=0.2\) row is the canonical example [PAGE 19]. Another counter-trap is template mimicry: because the method is unlabeled and template-referenced, a performer can match the template’s timing and limb positions without improving exercise quality. The algorithm’s recursive model of the performer treats deviation \(q\) as error, but the performer’s model of the algorithm treats the template as a target. The instructor’s model of the score treats a high score as competence, which closes the loop and can reward mimicry unless Rate80 and held-out-action tests are enforced.

The clock is the temporal layer. ACDTW’s \(P\) and \(Q\) matrices count how often a frame participates, and the penalty grows with reuse [PAGE 15–16]. This is a clock-sensitive mechanism: a pause, a slow repetition, or a frame-rate mismatch creates many-to-one or one-to-many mappings, which the penalty suppresses. That is desirable when the extra mappings are artifacts, but it can be undesirable when they are legitimate tempo changes. The paper’s three-second clips and four eight-beat movements [PAGE 18] impose a fixed clock, so the model assumes that a standard performance and a test performance can be aligned within that window. The counter-trap is to treat every clock mismatch as low quality. The correct recursive adjudication is to separate “reuse caused by artifact” from “reuse caused by legitimate timing,” which requires inspecting the \(P,Q\) path and the raw frame timing, not only the final score.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer veto is pose-extraction validity. The paper acknowledges that relying on one modality has failure modes: “such as inaccurate recognition due to occlusion in 2D or insufficient depth information in 3D” [PAGE 4]. If a required joint is occluded, outside the frame, or tracked with large reprojection error, then the feature vector entering Eq. (5) is not the same measurement object as the template feature vector. The score is a composition \(FS\) of path scores, and each path score is a sum over feature scores; therefore a missing or invalid feature changes both the \(100/n\) normalization and the path. The veto is domain exclusion: if the pose-validity predicate is false for any frame on the required path, the score is not interpretable as action quality. The biomechanical layer veto is feature-protocol validity. The method’s 52 features include joint angles, barycentric features, direction features, and upper-lower limb linkage features [PAGE 9]. If the exercise requires a joint or limb relation that the feature set does not encode, or if the foot/shoulder and wrist/shoulder tension features are not observable, the score cannot adjudicate that biomechanical requirement. The paper converts non-radian ratios into radians [PAGE 10], so unit consistency is also a veto condition. The weather layer is not a source variable in this indoor broadcast-gymnastics dataset; the paper provides no weather measurements. For outdoor extension, weather is a context-layer veto because rain, glare, clothing, or low illumination can cause keypoint dropout and thereby invalidate the physical layer. The proof obligation is causal: if the environment corrupts the skeleton stream, the observed \(q\) is a mixture of performance deviation and sensing deviation, and no monotone mapping from \(FS\) to quality is identified. Veto, do not score-adjust.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
1. Zero-template-feature veto. Intervention: set a template feature \(x_{i,k}=0\) while leaving the test feature and all other features unchanged. Prediction: Eq. (5) becomes undefined at that feature; if implemented without a guard, the pipeline emits NaN or infinity, and Eq. (8) cannot produce a finite MED distance. If implemented with an epsilon, the score shifts in a way not specified by the paper. Veto condition: any zero denominator without an explicit, tested guard. This is verifiable by a unit test on Eq. (5) and Eq. (8) [PAGE 12–13].

2. Accuracy-only reward hack. Intervention: tune \(t\) from 0.1 to 0.2 on the reported ablation. Prediction: Accuracy rises from 97.39% to 98.04%, but Rate80 collapses from 95.80% to 65.00% [PAGE 19]. A verifiable reward that uses only Accuracy will select the worse grading regime. Veto condition: reject any optimization that does not require both Accuracy and Rate80, and reject any claim that \(t=0.2\) is “best” without reporting the Rate80 collapse.

3. Adaptive-constraint removal. Intervention: set \(C=0\), or disable the \(P,Q\) reuse-count update, so ACDTW reduces to standard DTW. Prediction: the paper’s own reduction statement applies: “When the coefficient is zero, it corresponds to the standard DTW algorithm” [PAGE 15]. The measured signature is the MED-DTW 3D row at Rate80 65.00% versus MED-ACDTW 3D at 93.27%, a gap of about 28 percentage points [PAGE 22, Table 5]. Qualitatively, many-to-one path reuse reappears and dissimilar actions are not pushed below the intended threshold; the paper’s stretching-versus-other analysis shows the desired behavior only when the discriminative path is active [PAGE 24]. Veto condition: any implementation that reports “ACDTW” while \(C=0\) or while \(P,Q\) are not updated is mislabeled and should be rejected as a verifiable counterfactual failure.
