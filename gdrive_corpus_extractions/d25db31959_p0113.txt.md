Physical layer: Human stature is bounded by species biology; realistic adult statures lie approximately in \(0.5\text{ m} < X < 2.8\text{ m}\) (roughly 20 in to 110 in). Galton’s regression prediction \(\hat{X}_o = M + b(x - M)\) with \(0 \le b \le 1\) is a convex interpolation between M and parental stature x. If x and M are within biological bounds, \(\hat{X}_o\) cannot leave those bounds. Thus the regression equation itself triggers no physical veto unless b is estimated outside [0,1] or M is mis-specified outside the feasible stature range.

Biomechanical layer: Stature variance is bounded by total population variance and by developmental constraints (growth-plate closure, nutrition, disease). A subgroup’s conditional probable error cannot exceed the total population Q without implying that a subset is more dispersed than the whole, which is impossible under the law of total variance. Galton’s adopted Q = 1.5 in = 3.81 cm is physically plausible for stature. Veto condition: if any co-fraternal group’s measured Q_i exceeded the total-sample Q by a margin larger than sampling error (e.g., Q_i > 1.5 × Q_total), the constant-Q model is biomechanically inadmissible for that group. On [PAGE 113], Galton’s “trifle larger” for tall mid-parents is below any plausible veto threshold.

Weather layer: No meteorological variable appears on [PAGE 113]. Seasonal effects on conception, birth weight, or childhood nutrition could theoretically modulate stature variance, but they are absent from Galton’s operators. Veto rule: any model that imports weather-dependent modulation of Q or M without data from this source is over-specified and vetoed for lack of evidentiary support.

Veto adjudication summary: The constant-Q = 1.5 in model is cross-layer admissible: it respects physical stature bounds, does not violate variance constraints, and introduces no unsupported weather terms. The only admissible challenge is empirical heteroscedasticity—if future measurement shows \(Q_{\text{tall}} - Q_{\text{short}}\) materially above zero (say >0.5 in ≈ 1.27 cm), the constant-Q assumption fails the physical/statistical consistency test and must be replaced by a variance function Q(x). A social veto also applies: “mediocre” must not be read as eugenic decline; statistical regression toward M is not biological deterioration.
