<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

Its own introduction cites the Anderson and Varma discussions of the normal cuprate phase as motivation for NFL behavior [PAGE 1, PAGE 8 references]. It then distinguishes its fixed points from Luttinger liquids: Luttinger liquids have a c=1 bosonic charge sector and non-universal correlation exponents, while the chiral fluids have universal exponents fixed by anomaly matching and coset structure.

The CFT lineage is explicit. The paper cites Belavin-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov for the general classification of fixed points and Witten and Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov for WZW models [PAGE 8 references]. The QHE edge-state realization connects to Wen and Kane-Fisher [PAGE 8 references].

Retrocausally, the paper reads later QHE edge and NMR observables back into the CFT fixed-point data. Second trap: applying the Luttinger-liquid exponent formula to the spin sector. The spin sector is not a simple U(1) Luttinger sector; it is an SU(2) WZW or coset sector. The paper’s correlation functions on [PAGE 4] have different left and right exponents.
Third trap: treating f_R=f_L as a limiting chiral liquid. It is not: the spin sector gaps, and the specific heat drops [PAGE 6].
Fourth trap: missing the f_R-f_L=1 exception. The general NMR exponent α=4/(f_R-f_L+2) is explicitly replaced by α=2 when the imbalance is one [PAGE 8].
Fifth trap: assuming flavor anisotropy is equivalent to spin anisotropy. The paper says small spin exchange asymmetry is robust and expected irrelevant for f_R-f_L=1, while flavor anisotropy tends to be relevant and can produce sequences of fixed points [PAGE 4].
Sixth trap: ignoring channel anisotropy. The TBA equations with multiple mass scales produce intermediate plateaus and crossover peaks; a single high-T to low-T interpolation is insufficient [PAGE 7].
Seventh trap: confusing the Kac-Moody level k with the conformal central charge c. The formula c=(N^2-1)k/(k+N) [PAGE 2] shows they are distinct; equating them corrupts every central-charge count.
Eighth trap: forgetting the charge-sector umklapp route. The paper notes that an umklapp term can drive the charge sector to a chiral NFL fixed point as well [PAGE 5]. A reader who studies only spin exchange misses half the mechanism.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The “crisis” in this model is the breakdown of the Fermi-liquid habit under strong spin exchange. The habitual regression is to force a quasiparticle pole, to set Δ=1/2, or to apply the Luttinger-liquid formula even when the spin sector is gapped. The paper vetoes that regression in two ways. First, the single-particle Green’s function on [PAGE 2] is ⟨ψ(x_1)ψ^*(x_2)⟩=(x_1-x_2)^{-2Δ}, and “Δ≠1/2 directly implies NFL behavior.” Second, when f_R=f_L the spin sector develops a gap, so the correct low-energy description is not a gapless chiral liquid at all [PAGE 6]. The counter-trap is anomaly matching: because c_R-c_L and f_R-f_L are preserved, a nonzero chiral imbalance prevents the spin sector from trivializing. The c-theorem then selects the minimal central charge consistent with the preserved symmetry, giving the coset fixed point on [PAGE 3]. The recursive point is that the model’s “mind” is its effective field theory: under crisis it either regresses to FL assumptions or reorganizes around the anomaly.

The clock is the RG scale, represented experimentally by temperature and by the TBA masses m^l_L and m^r_R. The paper’s TBA rules on [PAGE 5] say the IR limit is obtained by taking the right mass infinitely heavy and truncating at the level where it was inserted, with an analogous rule for left movers; the UV limit is obtained by taking masses to zero. This gives three clock regimes. In the UV, T is much larger than the mass scales, and the free fixed point gives c_V/T=π/6(f_L+f_R). In the intermediate regime, if channel anisotropy is small, there can be a plateau with a different effective central charge. In the deep IR, T is much smaller than the lowest mass scale, and only the massless modes contribute. For f_R=2, f_L=1 the paper reports a high-T regime with three spinons, an intermediate regime with γ_spin=π/6, and a low-T regime where the left modes become massive and the right movers give γ_spin=π/12 [PAGE 7]. The recursive theory-of-mind lesson is that one must ask which clock regime an observer is in before interpreting a central-charge measurement. A low-T plateau is not a universal number independent of anisotropy; it is a clock-dependent fixed point.

The physical layer veto is mathematical, not metaphorical. The source preserves f_R-f_L and c_R-c_L by anomaly matching [PAGE 3]. Therefore any proposed cross-layer perturbation that would change the chiral imbalance without adding decoupled chiral degrees of freedom is vetoed. The c-theorem requires c=c_R+c_L to decrease under RG flow [PAGE 3], so any claimed flow to a higher-central-charge fixed point is vetoed unless extra degrees of freedom are explicitly included. The TBA driving terms provide a second physical veto: when left and right mass terms occur at the same level, the term m cosh ξ signals a mass gap [PAGE 5]. A claim of gapless chiral NFL behavior in that regime is vetoed by the TBA equations themselves.

The QHE realization supplies an experimental physical veto. The paper says the chiral fixed point determines behavior down to energy scales of order E_z, “below which the system will flow to more conventional fixed points” [PAGE 7]. Thus a claim that the chiral fixed point persists to zero temperature in a QHE sample is vetoed by the Zeeman scale, unless E_z is explicitly tuned or the model is altered.

The biomechanical layer is not present in the source. There are no strain, stress, fatigue, gait, or human-motion variables in H_0, the spin-exchange interaction, the coset fixed point, or the TBA equations. A biomechanical claim cannot be adjudicated by this corpus and is vetoed as a category error: no operator in Eqs. (1)-(11) maps onto a biomechanical variable. If someone proposed a biomechanical perturbation, the only legitimate bridge would be to show that it generates one of the symmetry currents S^i_L, S^i_R, or a charge-density operator with the correct quantum numbers. Absent that operator matching, the perturbation is outside the model.

The weather layer is similarly absent. Weather variables are not the thermodynamic temperature T of the CFT, nor the magnetic field h, nor the TBA mass scales. A weather claim would have to enter as an external stochastic field or boundary condition, and no such term appears in the source. The physical veto is therefore: any cross-layer variable that does not appear as a coupling in H=H_0+∑(g_s)_{rl}S^i_R S^i_L, or as a charge umklapp term, cannot alter the fixed-point exponents. The only allowed cross-layer statement is that the CFT’s T is an energy scale; weather cannot replace it. Biomechanical and weather layers are non-adjudicable here and are vetoed by absence of operators, symmetries, and units.
