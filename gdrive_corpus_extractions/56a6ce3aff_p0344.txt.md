Scenario 1 (combustion veto). Claim: a sheet is essentially "paper for writing"; combustibility is accidental [PAGE 334]. Intervention: apply radiative heat flux \(q''\) sufficient to exceed ignition temperature \(T_{\text{ign}}\). Veto condition: observed mass loss \(\Delta m > \delta_m\) above sensor noise and exothermic signature \(Q > 0\) in joules. Prediction: ignition occurs, falsifying "just paper and nothing else" because \(\mu_o(\Omega_o \setminus S_n) \ge \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon > 0\). Verifiable by calorimetry and gravimetry.

Scenario 2 (attentional-essence veto). Claim: emphasis and selection are the fixed essence of mind [PAGE 334]. Intervention: present two concurrent purposes \(p_1, p_2\) requiring mutually exclusive selections. Veto condition: gaze distribution \(P(g \mid p_1, p_2)\) becomes multimodal and
\[
H(G \mid p_1, p_2) > \max\{H(G \mid p_1), H(G \mid p_2)\} + \delta_H \text{ bits}.
\]
Prediction: selection switches or splits rather than manifesting one stable essence; verifiable by eye-tracking entropy.

Scenario 3 (cross-purpose naming veto). Claim: the vulgar name connotes the exclusive essence across contexts [PAGE 334]. Intervention: elicit property-relevance weights \(w_j(p_u)\) for user-purpose \(p_u\) (writing) and \(w_j(p_m)\) for manufacturer-purpose \(p_m\). Veto condition:
\[
\|w(p_u) - w(p_m)\|_1 > \delta_w
\]
beyond test-retest reliability. Prediction: "rectangular" is weighted high under \(p_m\), low under \(p_u\); "writable surface" shows the reverse; no single \(S_n\) spans both purposes. Verifiable by ranking tasks and by \(I(N; X \mid p)\) varying with \(p\). Assumption: purpose labels are stable during each block; breakdown if weights fail to exceed measurement noise.
