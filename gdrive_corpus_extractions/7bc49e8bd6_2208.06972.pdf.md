The source says it conducts “six two sample t-tests for the difference between two means ... through two pooled data tests and four non-pooled data tests” [PAGE 6]. For a one-sided test \(H_0:\mu_1=\mu_2\) versus \(H_a:\mu_1>\mu_2\) [PAGE 6], the pooled statistic is
\[
t_{\text{pooled}}
=
\frac{\bar Y_1-\bar Y_2}
{s_p\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}},
\qquad
s_p^2=
\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2+(n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2},
\]
with \(\nu=n_1+n_2-2\). The non-pooled Welch statistic is
\[
t_{\text{Welch}}
=
\frac{\bar Y_1-\bar Y_2}
{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}},
\qquad
\nu=
\frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}\right)^2}
{\frac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1}+\frac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}.
\]
The p-value is
\[
p=\Pr\!\left(T_\nu\ge t_{\text{obs}}\mid H_0\right),
\]
dimensionless in \([0,1]\). The reported p-values are “0.153, 0.252, 0.161, 0.012, 0.067, and 0.016” [PAGE 7]. Only 0.012 and 0.016 are below 0.05; none is below the Bonferroni threshold \(0.05/6\approx 0.00833\). The source does not report confidence intervals, variance-equality diagnostics, or multiplicity correction.
