\[

\[
\mathbf{b}(t) = \bigl(\mathbf{p}_b(t),\,\mathbf{v}_b(t),\,\boldsymbol{\omega}_b(t)\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R},

with “rotation of the ball” = “rotácia lopty” [PAGE 10 entry 470] and “backspin” = “spätná rotácia” [PAGE 9 entry 325]. The source gives no mass, moment of inertia, or coefficient of restitution, so \boldsymbol{\omega}_b cannot be linked to aerodynamic torque.

\[

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The dictionary’s publication lineage begins with the Comenius University Faculty of Physical Education and Sports project (2001–2003) and culminates in the 2005 first edition of 106 pages [PAGE 2]. Its scholarly ancestry, listed in the bibliography [PAGE 105], includes Barnett’s *Inside Basketball* glossary (Chicago, 1971, pp. 80–87), Clark’s *Illustrated Basketball Dictionary for Young People* (1977), Hartányi’s *Basketball Dictionary in Pictures* (Budapest, 1992; Munich/FIBA edition, 2001), and Limbecková & Herrmann’s *Anglicko-slovenský odborný basketbalový slovník* (Bratislava, 1980). This is a retrocausal genealogy: contemporary Slovak coaching terminology is reachable only by back-projecting through decades of bilingual lexicographic revision, and each earlier text imposes semantic drift on current usage.

Multi-week trap: over a standard microcycle of practice planning, the English head “drive” maps to both “únik” and “uniknúť” [PAGE 32 entries 1519–1521], while “drive to the basket” maps specifically to “uniknúť ku košu” [PAGE 32 entry 1521]. A separate entry, “break towards the basket,” maps to “vyraziť ku košu” [PAGE 9 entry 530]. Because these near-synonyms are not discriminated by defensive pressure or lane geometry in the source, a coach scripting a week of penetrating drills risks conflating a lane cut with a basket drive. The untranslated Slovak term “stack” in entries 4417–4425 [PAGE 89] compounds the trap: an international staff importing the offensive system “útočný systém stack” [PAGE 89 entry 4422] must reconstruct its meaning ex nihilo each week, because no Slovak operational definition exists in the lexicon. The retrocausal hazard is that session plans authored before the terminology was standardized retroactively inherit ambiguity when revisited in later microcycles.
