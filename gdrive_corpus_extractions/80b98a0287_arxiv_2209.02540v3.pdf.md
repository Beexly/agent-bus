The source is a tracking-by-detection 3D multi-object tracking system. Its causal chain has four layers. Layer 1 is the detector: a 3D detector turns a point cloud into detections \(D_t\), and the paper states that “camera-based methods suffer in the case of occlusions” while LiDAR-only methods can fail under “irregular motion” or “large inter-frame displacements” [PAGE 1]. Layer 2 is appearance: the O2S-OAM module extracts image features, estimates an occlusion state \(Occ_s\), and uses that state to select the best appearance feature over time; the paper says “The term Occs is then generated for each object and used to determine its occlusion state” [PAGE 4]. Layer 3 is motion: the CS-MM module runs a Kalman prediction, computes a 3D generalized IoU cost, and penalizes low-confidence detections; the paper states “Objects with lower confidence scores have higher costs and are more challenging to track continuously” [PAGE 5]. Layer 4 is association: M2-FAM adds a category loss and performs motion-first association followed by appearance association on the remaining detections and trajectories; the paper states “We first associate trajectories based on Mot to acquire the corresponding detections” and then uses \(App_t\) on the remainder [PAGE 5]. The resulting tracker is therefore not a single similarity score but a cascade of vetoes: class veto, motion veto, and appearance veto.

The multi-hop derivation is as follows. First, a detection \(i\) and a predicted trajectory \(j\) are compared by motion. The motion cost is \(C^{i,j}_{mo}=M^{i,j}_t/\gamma^j_t\), where \(M_t\) is a gIoU3D cost and \(\gamma_t\) is a confidence-derived state score. If \(C^{i,j}_{mo}\leq \theta_{mo}\), the pair can be associated in the first stage. If the pair remains unmatched, appearance association is attempted with \(App_t=C_{app}+Cls_t\) and threshold \(\theta_{App}\). If the classes differ, the category loss is set to \(10^5\), which is far above the published thresholds \(\theta_{mo}=0.01\) and \(\theta_{App}=1.4\) for KITTI, and \(\theta_{mo}=0.02\) and \(\theta_{App}=1.4\) for nuScenes [PAGE 6]. Thus cross-class association is mathematically vetoed unless the threshold is changed to exceed \(10^5\). This is a hard constraint, not a learned soft penalty.

The gray-zone audit concerns cases where the two modalities disagree. Under occlusion, appearance features can become unreliable, so the system should prefer motion or a historical feature with low occlusion state. Under irregular pedestrian motion or 2 Hz nuScenes sampling, motion prediction can become unreliable, so the system should prefer appearance or category-consistent association. The paper’s own figures show the failure modes: Fig. 1(a) shows an ID change under occlusion, and Fig. 1(b) shows an ID change under large LiDAR displacement [PAGE 1]. The ablation tables quantify the trade-off: for Car, AP-only gives HOTA 60.15 and IDS 39, MO-only gives HOTA 77.02 and IDS 68, AP+MO gives HOTA 79.28 and IDS 22, and AP+MO+OCC gives HOTA 80.74 and IDS 6 [PAGE 9]. For Pedestrian, AP-only gives HOTA 36.07 and IDS 148, MO-only gives HOTA 48.27 and IDS 100, AP+MO gives HOTA 49.76 and IDS 96, and AP+MO+OCC gives HOTA 50.24 and IDS 88 [PAGE 9]. The occlusion head therefore does not merely add a feature; it changes the feature-selection policy over time, reducing identity switches.

The audit also exposes gray zones the paper does not fully close. The occlusion head is trained using ground-truth multi-object bounding boxes \(B_t\) according to the method description [PAGE 4], while inference uses detector boxes. If the detector jitters or misses an object, the occlusion-state input distribution shifts. The matching head uses a reconstruction tensor of size \(R^{(C\times M)\times N_{max}\times N_{max}}\) and zero-padding for fewer than \(N\) objects [PAGE 4]; if \(N_{max}\) is too small for dense scenes, detections or trajectories are truncated. The gIoU3D cost is undefined when the union or convex-hull volume is zero, and the Kalman filter assumes a linear Gaussian state model with a valid covariance matrix. The category loss is robust only if the constant \(10^5\) remains larger than any possible motion or appearance cost; if cost normalization changes, the hard veto can become a soft preference. Finally, the paper reports “the lowest identity switches (IDS) value (23 for Car and 137 for Pedestrian)” on KITTI [PAGE 1], but low IDS can coexist with higher false negatives or false positives; the tracker’s safety claim depends on which error is more costly for the deployment. These are the exact breakdown regimes that a frontier audit must track.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The source defines the tracker as a tracking-by-detection system with three modules: O2S-OAM, CS-MM, and M2-FAM [PAGE 2, PAGE 3]. The following equations are the formal operators needed to reproduce the decision logic. Units are stated explicitly; where the source gives no unit, the quantity is dimensionless.

1. Kalman prediction operator. The source writes
\[
\hat Z^j_t = A Z^j_{t-1},
\]
\[
\hat S^j_t = A S^j_{t-1} A^{T}+Q,
\]
with state-transition matrix
\[
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
E_{3\times 3} & \sigma E_{3\times 3} & \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 E_{3\times 3}\\
O_{3\times 3} & E_{3\times 3} & \sigma E_{3\times 3}\\
O_{3\times n} & O_{3\times 3} & E_{3\times 3}\\
O_{n\times 9} & & E_{n\times n}
\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \(E\) and \(O\) are identity and zero matrices, \(\sigma\) is the LiDAR scan interval in seconds, and \(n=4+k\), with \(k\) covering position, velocity, and acceleration [PAGE 5]. The state entries carry SI units: position \(x,y,z\) in metres, velocity \(v_x,v_y,v_z\) in metres per second, acceleration \(a_x,a_y,a_z\) in metres per second squared, and size \(l,w,h\) in metres; rotation angle \(\alpha\) is in radians. The covariance \(S\) has units of the outer product of the state units, for example \(m^2\) for position-position blocks and \(m^2/s^2\) for velocity-velocity blocks. Assumptions: linear Gaussian dynamics, known scan interval, and positive-semidefinite \(Q\). Breakdown regimes: \(\sigma=0\) removes velocity/acceleration propagation; a singular or non-positive-semidefinite \(Q\) makes the covariance update invalid; missing velocity in a dataset changes the effective state dimension and can destabilize the prediction.

2. gIoU3D motion cost. The source defines
\[
M^{i,j}_t
=1-\mathrm{gIoU3D}(D^i_t,\hat T^j_t)
=2-\frac{V(D^i_t\cap \hat T^j_t)}{V(D^i_t\cup \hat T^j_t)}
-\frac{V(D^i_t\cup \hat T^j_t)}{V_{\mathrm{hull}}(D^i_t,\hat T^j_t)},
\]
and then
\[
C_{mo}=
\begin{bmatrix}
M^{1,1}_t/\gamma^1_t & \cdots & M^{1,N^Y_{t-1}}_t/\gamma^{N^Y_{t-1}}_t\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots\\
M^{N^X_t,1}_t/\gamma^1_t & \cdots & M^{N^X_t,N^Y_{t-1}}_t/\gamma^{N^Y_{t-1}}_t
\end{bmatrix},
\]
with a confidence-state score \(\gamma^\sigma_t\) and decay factor \(\theta\) [PAGE 5]. The volumes \(V(\cdot)\) are in cubic metres, so \(M_t\) and \(C_{mo}\) are dimensionless cost ratios. The paper states “the motion cost matrix is divided by the state score” [PAGE 5]. Assumptions: each detection and trajectory is a valid 3D box with nonzero volume; the convex hull is well defined; \(\gamma_t\neq 0\). Exact singularities: if \(V(D^i_t\cup \hat T^j_t)=0\), the first ratio is undefined; if \(V_{\mathrm{hull}}=0\), the second ratio is undefined; if both boxes are degenerate, the cost is undefined. The OCR of Eq. 6 is ambiguous, but the operative prose is clear: lower confidence must produce higher cost. If the implementation instead sets \(\gamma_t=0\), the division is singular. The source’s own thresholds are \(\theta_{mo}=0.01\) for KITTI and \(\theta_{mo}=0.02\) for nuScenes [PAGE 6].

3. Category veto. The source defines
\[
Dis^{i,j}_t=D^j_t(cls)-T^j_{t-1}(cls),
\]
\[
Cls^{i,j}_t=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
10^5,\ \mathrm{if}\ Dis^{i,j}_t\neq 0,\\
0,\ \mathrm{otherwise},
\end{array}
\right.
\]
and then
\[
Mot=C_{mo}+Cls_t,\qquad App_t=C_{app}+Cls_t.
\]
The paper states that “a large gap (e.g. 105) is included between the constants to ensure no inter-class interference is present in subsequent associations” [PAGE 5]; the intended value is \(10^5\). \(Cls_t\) is dimensionless. Assumptions: class labels are discrete and the constant is larger than the largest admissible motion or appearance cost. Breakdown regime: if the cost normalization or threshold exceeds \(10^5\), cross-class association is no longer hard-vetoed; if the class set changes continuously, the constant scheme must be revisited.

4. Two-stage association objective. The source writes
\[
\min \sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Mot^{i,j}_t
+\sum_{i=1}^{M'}\sum_{j=1}^{N'}App^{i,j}_t,
\]
subject to
\[
Mot^{i,j}_t\leq \theta_{mo},\qquad App^{i,j}_t\leq \theta_{App},
\]
where \(N,M\) are detections and trajectories at time \(t\), and \(N',M'\) are the unmatched sets [PAGE 5]. The objective is dimensionless. Assumptions: the assignment problem is solved by a Hungarian-style matcher; the thresholds are fixed. Breakdown regime: if \(\theta_{mo}\) or \(\theta_{App}\) is too large, false detections and cross-class pairs can be admitted; if too small, true matches are rejected and trajectories fragment.

5. Occlusion and matching losses. The occlusion head uses ResNet18 and cross-entropy:
\[
L_{\mathrm{occlusion}}
=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{c=1}^{4}y_{ic}\log p_{ic},
\]
where \(y\) is the label and \(p\) is the probability vector [PAGE 6]. The loss is in nats if the logarithm is natural and in bits if base 2; \(p_{ic}\in[0,1]\). Assumptions: four occlusion classes and one-hot labels. Exact singularity: if \(y_{ic}=1\) and \(p_{ic}=0\), the loss is infinite, so label smoothing or clipping is required. The matching loss is
\[
L_{\mathrm{match}}
=\frac{L_{\mathrm{match}_t}+L_{\mathrm{match}_{t-n}}}{2(N_t+N_{t-n})},
\]
with softmax-normalized association matrices and a ground-truth association matrix \(M_g\) [PAGE 6]. The total loss is
\[
L_{\mathrm{join}}
=\frac{1}{e^{\lambda_1}}L_{\mathrm{detect}}
+\frac{1}{e^{\lambda_2}}(L_{\mathrm{match}}+L_{\mathrm{occlusion}})
+\lambda_1+\lambda_2,
\]
where \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) are learnable weights [PAGE 6]. Units depend on the loss terms; \(\lambda\) terms are dimensionless. Breakdown regime: if a softmax row is exactly zero, the logarithm is undefined; if \(\lambda\) weights diverge, one branch dominates.

6. Evaluation operators. HOTA is
\[
\mathrm{HOTA}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{c\in TPA(c)}}{\lvert TP\rvert+\lvert FN\rvert+\lvert FP\rvert}},
\]
and MOTA is
\[
\mathrm{MOTA}=1-\frac{\sum_t(F_t+N_t+IDS_t)}{\sum_t G_t}.
\]
AMOTA is
\[
\mathrm{AMOTA}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{r\in\{1/L,\dots,1\}}\mathrm{MOTA}_r,
\]
with scaling
\[
sMOTA_r=\max\left(0,\frac{\mathrm{MOTA}_r}{r}\right).
\]
The paper notes “AMOTA in the official nuScenes tracking benchmark is in fact the sAMOTA in [17]” [PAGE 7]. All metrics are dimensionless percentages. Breakdown regime: AMOTA is recall-threshold dependent; comparing values across different scaling conventions is invalid.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from tracking-by-detection to deep appearance association to multi-modal 3D MOT. The paper traces 2D MOT through DeepSORT, the Deep Affinity Network, Tracktor, JDE, FairMOT, ByteTrack, TransTrack, and MOTR [PAGE 2]. It traces 3D MOT through LSTM state estimators, AB3DMOT, monocular 3D MOT, EagerMOT, MmMOT, JMODT, and PnPNet [PAGE 3]. The retrocausal point is that later benchmark metrics shape earlier architectural choices. KITTI 2D MOT uses HOTA as the primary metric, KITTI 3D MOT follows AMOTA, and nuScenes uses AMOTA [PAGE 6, PAGE 7]. This explains why the paper emphasizes IDS: it reports “the lowest identity switches (IDS) value (23 for Car and 137 for Pedestrian)” [PAGE 1]. It also explains why the nuScenes result is stated as “75.3% AMOTA” [PAGE 1] and why the method fuses BEVFusion and FocalsConv according to detection class on the test set [PAGE 9].

Multi-week traps are real. First, the KITTI validation split is a fixed set of sequences: 1, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, and 19 [PAGE 6]. Hyperparameters are chosen by best HOTA on that validation set [PAGE 6]. Over several weeks, a team can overfit to those eleven sequences and mistake sequence-specific gains for general occlusion robustness. Second, the category-loss constant \(10^5\) is a brittle design. It works because the published thresholds are \(\theta_{mo}=0.01\) and \(\theta_{App}=1.4\) [PAGE 6], but if a later detector or cost normalization changes the cost scale, the hard veto becomes a soft penalty. Third, the nuScenes sampling rate is 2 Hz, and the paper explicitly says this “is not favorable to the precise prediction of the motion model and introduces a significant inter-frame displacement” [PAGE 6]. A tracker tuned on KITTI at 10 Hz can look excellent for weeks and then fail on nuScenes because the motion model’s linear-Gaussian assumption is stressed. Fourth, the occlusion head is trained with image pairs separated by a random number of frames \(1\leq n\leq n_{max}\) [PAGE 6]; if \(n_{max}\) is set to the benchmark’s frame gap, the head may not generalize to a different sensor cadence. Fifth, detector coupling is a hidden confounder. EagerMOT is compared with the same PointGNN detector [PAGE 8], while the nuScenes result uses BEVFusion and FocalsConv [PAGE 9]. A multi-week experiment that swaps detectors without re-tuning thresholds can attribute detector gains to the tracker. Sixth, the paper’s own ablation shows that OCC is better than LTF+OCC for Car: OCC gives HOTA 80.74 and IDS 6, while LTF+OCC gives HOTA 80.71 and IDS 7 [PAGE 10]. A team that keeps adding temporal feature averaging can regress because occluded frames inject interference. These traps are genealogical: they come from the benchmark’s metric design, the sensor cadence, and the detector lineage, not from a single bad implementation choice.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The tracker’s recursive theory of mind is its internal model of object visibility, object motion, and its own uncertainty. It maintains an occlusion state \(Occ_s\), a confidence score \(c_t\), a predicted state \(\hat Z_t\), and a trajectory age. The crisis habit regression is appearance over-trust. When an object is occluded, the camera feature contains information outside the object itself; the paper warns that “occlusion features that contain information other than the object itself are not reliable” [PAGE 5]. A naive tracker regresses to the habit of nearest-appearance matching and switches identity. The paper’s countermeasure is to keep appearance features and occlusion states for each moment and “select the optimal appearance features among a lower occlusion state among multiple trajectory moments” [PAGE 5]. The ablation confirms the counter-trap: for Car, AP+MO gives IDS 22, while AP+MO+OCC gives IDS 6; for Pedestrian, AP+MO gives IDS 96, while AP+MO+OCC gives IDS 88 [PAGE 9]. The recursive model therefore does not merely ask “does this look like the same object?” It asks “was this object visible enough at some earlier time for its appearance to be trustworthy?”

The second crisis habit is motion over-trust. A constant-velocity Kalman filter is a strong prior, but pedestrians articulate and vehicles turn. The paper shows that LiDAR-only tracking can fail “when an object is considered a new object” after large inter-frame displacement [PAGE 1]. The counter-trap is the second appearance association: motion-first association handles the easy cases, and appearance association rescues the remaining detections and trajectories [PAGE 5]. The clock is the trajectory lifetime. The paper discards a trajectory if it has not been updated in the last 15 frames [PAGE 6], and on nuScenes it outputs up to 2 frames of predicted trajectory state during a mismatch period, with the predicted state’s tracking score set to 0.05 times the most recent score [PAGE 6]. This clock prevents an occluded object from being kept alive forever, but it also creates a gray zone: a pedestrian occluded for 16 frames is deleted, while a false detection that persists for 15 frames can be promoted to a trajectory. The recursive theory of mind must therefore balance three beliefs: the object still exists, the object is visible, and the object’s motion is predictable. The paper’s qualitative results show the intended behavior: under occlusion, DEFT and EagerMOT switch IDs, while CAMO-MOT remains stable; under illumination change, JMODT switches ID, while CAMO-MOT remains stable [PAGE 10, PAGE 12]. The residual counter-trap is that the tracker can become overconfident in its own history. If the occlusion head misclassifies a fully occluded object as partly visible, the appearance feature can be stale but accepted; if the confidence score is low but nonzero, the motion cost can still admit a false detection. The clock and the category veto are the final guards, but they do not prove correctness.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto can be written as a conjunction. For a detection \(i\) and trajectory \(j\), category veto is
\[
V_{\mathrm{class}}(i,j)=1\ \mathrm{if}\ cls_i\neq cls_j,
\]
and because \(Cls_t=10^5\) while \(\theta_{mo}\ll 10^5\) and \(\theta_{App}\ll 10^5\), the association is vetoed [PAGE 5, PAGE 6]. Motion veto is
\[
V_{\mathrm{motion}}(i,j)=1\ \mathrm{if}\ V(D^i_t\cup \hat T^j_t)=0
\ \mathrm{or}\ V_{\mathrm{hull}}(D^i_t,\hat T^j_t)=0
\ \mathrm{or}\ C^{i,j}_{mo}>\theta_{mo}.
\]
Appearance veto is
\[
V_{\mathrm{appearance}}(i,j)=1\ \mathrm{if}\ C^{i,j}_{app}>\theta_{App}.
\]
The accepted association is the first-stage motion match or, for the remaining pairs, the second-stage appearance match [PAGE 5]. This is the formal proof that the system is a multi-layer veto rather than a single score.

Physical veto: LiDAR geometry is the hard physical layer. A box with zero volume, a convex hull with zero volume, or a predicted state that is inconsistent with the scan interval \(\sigma\) makes gIoU3D undefined or large. The source’s gIoU3D uses intersection volume, union volume, and convex-hull volume [PAGE 5]. Therefore a physically impossible detection cannot be rescued by appearance, because the motion cost is either undefined or above \(\theta_{mo}\). This is the correct adjudication for a sensor fault or a degenerate bounding box.

Biomechanical veto: pedestrians are the stress case. The paper reports Pedestrian AP-only HOTA 36.07 and IDS 148, MO-only HOTA 48.27 and IDS 100, AP+MO HOTA 49.76 and IDS 96, and AP+MO+OCC HOTA 50.24 and IDS 88 [PAGE 9]. The motion model assumes a linear state transition; a sudden acceleration or turn is a biomechanical violation of that assumption. In that regime, the appearance and occlusion layers should adjudicate because the motion prediction is less trustworthy. The paper’s visualization says that when an object suddenly accelerates or turns, single-modality trackers consider it a new object, while the fusion method remains stable [PAGE 10]. The veto rule is: if the predicted motion residual exceeds the Kalman gate and the occlusion state is low, allow appearance association; if the occlusion state is high, do not allow appearance association and let the trajectory age out.

Weather and illumination veto: the source does not run a weather ablation, so this is a gray-zone extension. The paper does show illumination sensitivity: “When the illumination changes, the multi-modal fusion method JMODT recognizes the tracking object as a new one while our method still maintains stable tracking” [PAGE 10], and Fig. 5 shows “JMODT shows the wrong ID switch (ID 9→ID 19)” under illumination change [PAGE 12]. The adjudication is: if camera appearance drifts because of illumination, the occlusion head should raise \(Occ_s\), and the appearance cost should be down-weighted or vetoed; if the occlusion head fails to flag the drift, the motion layer must veto the appearance match. If both layers disagree and neither is confident, the category veto and the trajectory clock are the last guards. The proof is by cost inequality: a false appearance match must have \(C_{app}\leq\theta_{App}\), a false motion match must have \(C_{mo}\leq\theta_{mo}\), and a cross-class match must have \(Cls_t=0\). If any inequality fails, the pair is rejected. The remaining risk is a pair that satisfies all inequalities but is still wrong; that is the irreducible gray zone of tracking-by-detection.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
