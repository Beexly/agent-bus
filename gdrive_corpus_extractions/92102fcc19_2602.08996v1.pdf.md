Testing each against the OOD-Feedback baseline (1.30 / 11.45 / 17.30 / 25.4): BLEU (2.68−1.30)/1.30 = 106.2% ✓; METEOR (15.59−11.45)/11.45 = 36.2% ✓; ROUGE (24.01−17.30)/17.30 = 38.8% ≈ 39% ✓; but BERT (37.9−25.4)/25.4 = 49.2%, not 25%. Recomputing BERT against Zero-Shot (30.3): (37.9−30.3)/30.3 = 25.1% ✓. So three of the four percentages use the OOD baseline while the BERTScore percentage silently switches to the zero-shot baseline. That is an inconsistent baseline inside a single sentence — a real methodological gray zone, not a rounding artifact.

Hop 3 — the "upper bound" leak. Table 2 labels in-domain (ID) feedback as "an upper bound." Yet Ours (37.9) exceeds ID (37.3) on BERTScore while remaining below ID on BLEU-4 (2.68 < 3.31), METEOR (15.59 < 20.94), and ROUGE (24.01 < 25.91). Therefore ID is not a genuine upper bound on the semantic-similarity axis; auxiliary data lifts semantic similarity past in-domain supervision even as lexical overlap lags. This is the paper's most interesting and under-stated result.

Hop 4 — ablation decomposition. Table 5 (PAGE 12) isolates the pipeline stages: adding OOD feedback to raw commentary (1.06→1.62 BLEU), then refinement (→1.93), then precise localization (→2.67) — the largest single jumps come from refinement and localization, consistent with the text's claim that "the most significant improvements come from refinement and the precise localization stages." Table 3 (PAGE 8) then shows the complementary book-text effect: text alone barely moves lexical metrics (15.08→15.22 METEOR) but, per Fig. 3, produces "notable gains in actionability," which lexical metrics cannot see. This is the crux: the two new metrics reveal gains that BLEU/METEOR/ROUGE are structurally blind to.

Hop 5 — consistency of the funnel. 14,855 train + 3,760 validate = 18,615, exactly the collected "18,615 video clip-commentary pairs" (PAGE 3). Good. But the evaluator reliability is thin: weighted Cohen's κ = 0.28 (specificity, n=24) and 0.60 (actionability, n=14) (PAGE 7). A κ of 0.28 is only "fair" agreement; the specificity axis — the one most dependent on visual grounding — has the weakest human ceiling, so LLM-as-judge specificity scores inherit that noise floor. I flag this as the dominant measurement risk.

Gray-zone audit conclusion: the pipeline is internally consistent on counts, but (a) mixes baselines in the headline %, (b) mislabels the ID bound, and (c) rests specificity on low inter-annotator agreement. These are the falsifiable seams I carry into the veto sections.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All operators below are transcribed from the source or defined from standard, cited metric formulas; where I introduce a derived quantity I state its assumptions and its breakdown regime.

1) Training operator — autoregressive NTP (PAGE 5, Equation 1, verbatim):
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}} = \frac{1}{\,n-1\,}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\text{CrossEntropyLoss}(\hat{y}_i,\,y_i)$$
Units: with natural-log cross-entropy, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{NTP}}$ is mean negative log-likelihood in nats/token; with $\log_2$, bits/token. Task (PAGE 5): given $D=\{(v_i,t_i)\}$ with 4-second clips $v_i$ and feedback $t_i$, learn $\hat{t}_i$. Each token distribution $\hat{y}_i,y_i\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|-1}$. For one-hot targets, $\text{CrossEntropyLoss}(\hat{y}_i,y_i)=-\log \hat{y}_i(x_{i+1})$.
Assumptions / breakdown regimes:
- Singularity at $n=1$: the prefactor $\tfrac{1}{n-1}\to\infty$ and the sum is empty (0/0) — single-token sequences are undefined; any real input must have $n\ge 2$.
- The paper states visual tokens are "padded by <image> tokens" (PAGE 5) but does not specify loss masking. Standard practice masks non-text positions; if unmasked, the model would be trained to predict visual-token IDs, which is degenerate. I flag this as an under-specification, not a stated fact.
- Text-only sequences ("or only text tokens," PAGE 5) share the identical loss, which is what permits the coaching-manual data to enter the same objective.

2) Refinement as a Bernoulli information filter (PAGE 4, verbatim: "The LLM filters out 80% of narrations, leaving 75K narrations"). Model keep/drop as iid Bernoulli with retention $p=\tfrac{75\text{K}}{\approx375\text{K}}=0.20$. Per-narration decision entropy:
$$H(p) = -p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p),\qquad H(0.2)=0.722\ \text{bits/narration}.$$
Units: bits/narration. Assumption: independent, memoryless keep/drop. Breakdown: if filtering is correlated (e.g., whole commentary runs dropped together), the aggregate retained entropy is far below $\sum H$, and the 0.722 bit/narration figure overstates true information reduction.

3) Precise temporal localization (PAGE 12 prompt, verbatim: "localize each commentary segment to a short (1–4 second) time span"). Define compression ratio
$$\rho=\frac{\Delta t_{\text{ASR}}}{\Delta t_{\text{refined}}},\qquad \Delta t_{\text{refined}}\in[1,4]\,\text{s}.$$
Units: dimensionless. $\rho>1$ quantifies the tightening over the coarse ASR chunk. Breakdown: for actions that anticipate or precede speech (PAGE 4 problem (2): "narration may be in anticipation of/reaction to an action"), the offset error is unbounded; the paper concedes "there still can exist temporal misalignment" (PAGE 10) and argues it is small only because "climbers often remain in position for extended periods" (PAGE 11). For fast sports this operator's error diverges.

4) Legacy metrics (real forms; table values on a 0–100 scale).
BLEU-4 (Papineni et al. [22]): $\text{BLEU}=BP\cdot\exp\!\big(\tfrac14\sum_{k=1}^{4}\log p_k\big)$, brevity penalty $BP=\min\!\big(1,e^{1-r/c}\big)$; $c$ candidate length, $r$ reference length, both in tokens; dimensionless. Breakdown: any $p_k=0\Rightarrow\text{BLEU}=0$; with one-sentence feedback, 4-gram overlap is near-absent, explaining the tiny magnitudes (Ours 2.68 ⇒ 0.0268).
METEOR (Banerjee & Lavie [4]): harmonic $F_{\text{mean}}=\tfrac{PR}{\alpha P+(1-\alpha)R}$ (typ. $\alpha=0.9$) times fragmentation penalty; allows synonym/stem matches (PAGE 6); dimensionless.
ROUGE-L (Lin [14]): $F$-measure over longest common subsequence; dimensionless.
BERTScore (Zhang et al. [36]): IDF-weighted greedy cosine $\tfrac{1}{|x|}\sum_{x_i}\max_{\hat{x}_j} x_i^\top\hat{x}_j$ on normalized contextual embeddings. Gray zone: canonical BERTScore is $O(0.1\text{–}0.9)$ on a 0–1 scale; the reported 37.9 implies a $\times100$ rescaling or an unstated variant — the paper never states the scale, so absolute BERTScore values are ambiguous.

5) Evaluator reliability — weighted Cohen's κ (PAGE 7):
$$\kappa = 1-\frac{\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}O_{ij}}{\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}E_{ij}},$$
dimensionless, $\kappa\in(-\infty,1]$; reported $\kappa=0.28$ (specificity, $n=24$), $\kappa=0.60$ (actionability, $n=14$). Breakdown: Landis–Koch bands put 0.28 in "fair" (0.21–0.40); with $n=14$–$24$ the standard error of $\kappa$ is large, so the specificity ceiling is statistically soft. LLM-judge accuracies (Table 1, PAGE 7): GPT-4o-mini 70.8%/85.7%, Phi-4 14B 37.5%/64.2% (spec./act.).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage is traceable across three converging streams. Stream A — motor-learning theory: the metrics are grounded in Schmidt, Lee, Winstein, Wulf & Zelaznik [27] (PAGE 2, 6), specifically the Knowledge-of-Performance vs Knowledge-of-Results distinction. This descends from Thorndike's "knowledge of results," through Schmidt's schema theory, into the augmented-feedback literature; the paper's novelty is operationalizing descriptive-KP as "specificity" and prescriptive-KP as "actionability" (Definition 1–2, PAGE 6). Stream B — noisy video-text learning: HowTo100M [18] → VideoCLIP [33] → LLM-captioning repairs LaViLa/HowToCaption [37, 29] → this paper's ASR-refinement, which differs by extracting rather than augmenting (PAGE 3, verbatim: "extract concise, feedback-relevant commentary … rather than augment the caption with new information"). Stream C — sports feedback datasets: Ego-Exo4D [9] → ExpertAF [2] → this work's cross-domain transfer to an unseen target. The evaluation lineage (BLEU [22] 2002 → ROUGE [14] 2004 → METEOR [4] 2005 → BERTScore [36] 2020 → GPTScore [8] / LLM-Acc [21]) culminates in the paper's reference-free, aspect-level metrics. Whisper [25] and Phi-4 [1] are the tooling layer.

Multi-week traps embedded in this lineage: (i) The temporal-alignment assumption quietly imports procedural-video priors (consistent step order, single-step-per-video, PAGE 3, refs [6, 11]) that competition footage violates — debugging "why is commentary mismatched?" can consume weeks before one realizes the localization operator (Hop 3) is the culprit, not the encoder. (ii) The descriptive-vs-prescriptive style bias (PAGE 2: "expert commentators are more descriptive, rather than prescriptive … this can bias the style of the feedback") means a model that looks good on lexical metrics may silently regress to commentary style, invisible until actionability is measured. (iii) The scale ambiguity of BERTScore plus the baseline switch in the headline % can mislead an entire replication effort for weeks.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Biomechanical veto (in-scope; the paper explicitly grounds transfer in mechanics — PAGE 2: "hip flexion and explosive leg drive used in basketball jumping applies similarly to power generation for upward movement in bouldering").

Static-equilibrium operators. For quasi-static climbing ($\vec{a}\approx 0$):
$$\sum \vec{F}=m\vec{a}=0,\qquad \sum \vec{\tau}=\vec{0}.$$
Base of support $\text{BoS}=\text{conv}\{\text{contact points (hands+feet on holds)}\}$. Static-stability veto: the vertical projection of the center of mass must satisfy $\Pi_{\text{COM}}\in\text{BoS}$; violation ⇒ toppling. Coulomb friction at each hold $h$: $\|F^{\parallel}_h\|\le \mu_h F^{\perp}_h$; violation ⇒ slip ⇒ fall. This adjudicates real feedback in the corpus: "a drop knee with the right leg will significantly broaden the base so that the center remains within the base for the entire move" (PAGE 8) — correct, it enlarges BoS; and "A fall becomes more likely because your center is actually pulling you off the rock" (PAGE 8) — correct, it pushes $\Pi_{\text{COM}}\to\partial\text{BoS}$. Veto rule: any generated correction that would move $\Pi_{\text{COM}}$ outside BoS or demand $\|F^{\parallel}\|>\mu F^{\perp}$ is physically unsound and should be rejected regardless of its BLEU/BERTScore.
Breakdown regime: the operators are quasi-static; for a dynamic move (a "deadpoint"/lunge) $\sum\vec F=m\vec a\neq0$ and momentum $m\Delta v=\int F\,dt$ dominates, so the static veto under-adjudicates explosive moves — precisely the basketball-to-bouldering transfer the paper invokes.

Weather veto (out-of-scope, honestly adjudicated as null). Outdoor climbing friction is strongly $\mu(T,\text{RH})$-dependent (rock friction falls with heat/humidity), a genuine physical factor. But the model's inputs are only "the best exocentric view," "16 frames," "Pose data is also excluded" (PAGE 6) — no temperature, humidity, or rock-type channel exists. Therefore no weather veto can be issued by this system; it is a null projection, and any weather-sensitive recommendation would be an extrapolation the model is not equipped to verify. Stating this is preferable to inventing a coupling the data cannot support.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
