Crisis habit regression. Under prolonged stability, agents regress to the habit of treating capitalized value as an immediate utility [PAGE 330], effectively setting \(S(t) = 1\) and treating \(r\) as a known constant. This is the regression described by Knight: the psychological school's error of conflating the fund of value transferred in a loan with the capitalized value of productive property. When a crisis arrives, the habit reverses asymmetrically: agents suddenly overweight footnote 2, treating income permanence as highly uncertain, and the capitalized value collapses not because \(Y_t\) has fallen, but because the implicit \(S(t)\) is revised toward zero.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is the owner who declares no intention to sell [PAGE 330] and therefore believes the capitalization signal is irrelevant to his decisions. This is false because the imputed sale value enters collateral constraints, borrowing capacity, and the opportunity cost of retaining control. The owner’s recursive theory of mind runs: "I believe the market thinks the capitalized value is high; therefore my lenders will lend against it; therefore I can expand as if I had liquid funds." When the market revises \(r\) or \(S(t)\), the collateral value evaporates, and the owner faces a forced contraction despite never having intended to liquidate. The counter-trap is that non-sellers are not insulated from capitalization cycles.

Clock. Two clocks govern the system. The operational clock measures the arrival of physical yield \(Y_t\) from the enterprise. The capitalization clock discounts that yield at the current rate of interest and updates continuously. When the capitalization clock runs faster than the operational clock—when \(r\) shifts or sentiment over \(S(t)\) changes in days while the plant produces over years—the present-worth calculation on [PAGE 330] generates price movements that have no counterpart in the physical productive process. The recursive failure occurs when agents synchronize their behavior to the fast clock, abandoning the slow clock of actual yield, thereby converting Knightian uncertainty into a self-fulfilling liquidity event.</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
