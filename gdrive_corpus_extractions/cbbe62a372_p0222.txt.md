<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto-proof adjudication requires evaluating three nested layers before accepting the [PAGE 213] summation prediction.

Physical layer (transduction): The weak light must exceed photoreceptor threshold despite ambient illumination; the cutaneous vibrator must exceed mechanoreceptor threshold despite ambient sound, vibration, and temperature. If ambient room illuminance raises the light’s absolute threshold above the stimulus luminance, or if 80 dB broadband noise masks the vibrator, the effective habit strength of that aggregate is reduced toward zero regardless of its prior reinforcement history. Verification: psychometric detection thresholds measured immediately before and after compound exposure.

Biomechanical layer (effector execution): Even if \(H_{\text{joint}} = 65.13\) habs is computed, the monotonic habit-reaction map \(g(H)\) [PAGE 213, p. 326 ff.] is bounded by the effector’s maximum response amplitude and refractory period. Muscle fatigue, tendon compliance, or response topography constraints impose a ceiling \(R_{\max}\). If \(g(65.13\text{ habs}) > R_{\max}\), the observed response saturates and cannot express the full joint habit strength.

Environmental / weather layer: Temperature, humidity, and barometric pressure alter cutaneous receptor transduction velocity and light scattering; wind or precipitation can attenuate a vibrator’s mechanical coupling to the skin. These are not metaphorical “odds” but physical parameters that shift the effective stimulus intensity at the receptor.

Composite veto operator: Let \(V_{\text{phys}}, V_{\text{bio}}, V_{\text{env}} \in \{0,1\}\). Then
\[
H_{\text{effective}} = V_{\text{phys}} \cdot V_{\text{bio}} \cdot V_{\text{env}} \cdot H_{\text{joint}}.
\]
If any layer vetoes (\(V=0\)), the compound summation is blocked or reduced to the unmasked aggregate alone. This is a logical AND gate: all layers must pass for the [PAGE 213] prediction to hold.
