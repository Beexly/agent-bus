This is not monotone in the naive sense because LSTM-only (−17.8) beats GCNN-only (−23.0), yet the combination (−16.2) is best. The non-additivity is a genuine signal, not a toy ratio: it implies the two modules encode partially redundant information, and the fully connected fusion layers learn a weighted compromise. The gray zone is the vehicle dataset, where the ordering *inverts*: "AerialMPTNetLSTM and AerialMPTNetGCNN outperform AerialMPTNet itself in terms of MOTA by 1.3 and 0.8 points" [PAGE 73]. The author attributes this to "the distance threshold for neighbor modeling, where only objects within a distance of 50 pixels from the target object were considered. For vehicle tracking, such settings differ" [PAGE 65]. This is an honest admission of a hyperparameter-induced breakdown regime, and it bounds the claim: the method is tuned for pedestrians, not a general MOT solver.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

All operators below are cited verbatim from the source with page markers. Units are SI-adjacent as used in the thesis: pixels (px), centimeters (cm), hertz (Hz), meters (m).

**(1) MOTA — Multiple Object Tracker Accuracy** [PAGE 31, Eq. 3.2]:
$$\text{MOTA} = 1 - \frac{\sum_t (FN_t + FP_t + ID_t)}{\sum_t GT_t}$$
Dimensionless. Range: unbounded below (can be arbitrarily negative). Breakdown regime: MOTA → −∞ as FN rate → ∞; MOTA is dominated by FN for sub-5-px targets.

**(2) MOTP — Multiple Object Tracker Precision** [PAGE 31, Eq. 3.1]:
$$\text{MOTP} = \frac{\sum_{t,i} d_{t,i}}{\sum_t c_t}$$
Units of $d_{t,i}$: pixels (distance between matched object and ground truth). Breakdown: MOTP is computed *only over matched pairs*, so a tracker that matches few objects can post a spuriously high MOTP. The thesis flags this explicitly [PAGE 74]: "the MOTP score solely is not a good performance indicator."

**(3) MOTAL — log-weighted ID switch penalty** [PAGE 32, Eq. 3.3]:
$$\text{MOTAL} = 1 - \frac{\sum (FN_t + FP_t + \log_{10}(ID_t + 1))}{\sum GT_t}$$
Dimensionless; the $\log_{10}$ compresses ID-switch severity relative to MOTA.

**(4) FAR, Recall, Precision** [PAGE 32, Eqs. 3.4–3.6]:
$$\text{FAR} = \frac{\sum FP_t}{f}, \quad \text{Rcll} = \frac{\sum TP_t}{\sum (TP_t + FN_t)}, \quad \text{Prcn} = \frac{\sum TP_t}{\sum (TP_t + FP_t)}$$
FAR units: false positives per frame (frame$^{-1}$). TP criterion: IoU > 0.5 [PAGE 31].

**(5) Linear motion prior (Stacked DCFNet baseline)** [PAGE 35, Eq. 4.1]:
$$p_{\text{est}}(x,y) = p(x,y) + k \cdot v_t(x,y)$$
$k$ = dimensionless hyperparameter; $v_t$ = last movement vector (px).

**(6) EOT Euclidean association cost** [PAGE 40, Eq. 4.2]:
$$D_{i,j} = \sqrt{(x_i - x_j)^2 + (y_i - y_j)^2} \cdot \text{GSD}$$
Units: cm (GSD in cm/px × distance in px). Threshold: $u = 17 \cdot \text{GSD}$ [PAGE 40]. Singular regime: if $u \to \infty$, the Hungarian assignment propagates error across all tracklets when objects enter/leave; the finite $u$ is a hard gate that prevents error propagation.

**(7) LSTM movement-prediction error** [PAGE 49]: "capable of predicting the next movement vector to as accurate as 3.6 pixels, representing 0.43 m." Unit conversion: 3.6 px × GSD ≈ 0.43 m implies GSD ≈ 12 cm/px, consistent with the KIT AIS range of 12–15 cm [PAGE 24].

**Assumptions and breakdown regimes:**
- GSD varies 8–15 cm across sequences ([PAGE 9], [PAGE 24], [PAGE 27]); the fixed 17·GSD threshold in EOT and the 7.5 m / 50-px neighbor gate in GraphCNN ([PAGE 49], [PAGE 65]) are *scale-sensitive* and break when GSD is non-uniform.
- Frame rate fixed at 2 Hz ([PAGE 23]). At 2 Hz, a pedestrian at 1.5 m/s moves 0.75 m = ~62 px between frames at 12 cm/px — far exceeding a 16-px search crop scaled by context factor 4 ([PAGE 51]). This is the formal origin of the "objects move out of their search window" failure [PAGE 64].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The scientific lineage is a strict descent from correlation-filter and Kalman paradigms into end-to-end regression. MOSSE (2010) [PAGE 19] established single-frame-initialized correlation filters; KCF (2014) [PAGE 19] added HOG and non-linear kernels; CSRT [PAGE 19] added channel and spatial reliability — and CSRT is the best *traditional* method in the final comparison (MOTA −55.9 KIT AIS, −64.6 AerialMPT) [PAGE 73]. The DCF lineage then migrated into networks via DCFNet [PAGE 34], where the DCF is "implemented as part of a DNN." In parallel, SORT [PAGE 20] (Kalman + Hungarian + IoU) and DeepSORT [PAGE 20] (adding Mahalanobis + cosine appearance distance) represent the tracking-by-detection branch. GOTURN [PAGE 19] introduced the offline-trained Siamese regression tracker at 100 fps, and SMSOT-CNN [PAGE 22] stacked GOTURN to achieve MOT without exponential complexity. AerialMPTNet is the direct descendant: it retains the SMSOT-CNN SNN stem "as a baseline" [PAGE 47] and replaces the hand-designed Kalman motion model with a learned LSTM, and the absent neighbor model with a GraphCNN.

The multi-week traps embedded in this lineage are two. **Trap 1 — the IoU-overlap trap.** DeepSORT's default IoU threshold of 0.5 is calibrated for pedestrians tens of pixels tall; at 4×4 px it produces 8,627 ID switches [PAGE 37]. The thesis documents the escape: doubling the box (5,073 switches) then setting IoU = 0.99 (738 switches) [PAGE 38]. But the author correctly refuses this as a final fix: "enlarging the bounding boxes sizes can not be a final solution, leading to a decreased localization accuracy" [PAGE 32]. **Trap 2 — the appearance-discriminability trap.** Fine-tuning the re-identification network reduced switches only from 738 to 734 [PAGE 39], because "the appearance features of different persons are often not discriminative enough" [PAGE 39] and "the extracted features do not necessarily correspond to the object, but in large parts to the background" [PAGE 39]. The genealogy's lesson is that appearance-only and motion-only trackers both fail; the fusion is the escape hatch.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

"Crisis habit regression" maps to how AerialMPTNet behaves when pushed out of its comfort distribution. Under crowded conditions (Bauma3: 609 pedestrians, 549 annotations/frame [PAGE 27]), the network regresses to its learned neighbor-graph prior: the GraphCNN encodes "the eight closest neighbors in a distance of 7.5 m" [PAGE 49], and the LSTM predicts motion from up to five prior vectors. The recursive self-model is the feedback loop described on [PAGE 50]: "the object is left in the batch as long as the network tracks it successfully; consequently, target and search crop are based on the network predictions." The network thus *trains against its own predictions*, a closed loop that can compound drift when a prediction is wrong.

"Counter-trap" is the appearance-confusion failure. The canonical case is the ground line in AA_Walking_02: "the tracker mistakes the line on the ground with the people walking across this line and loses both objects" [PAGE 35], and the activation map [PAGE 45] confirms the last conv layer "also highlights the line on the ground in a similar way." AerialMPTNet's counter-trap defense is that the LSTM/GCNN override pure appearance when the line is static but the pedestrian is moving — Figure 6.3 ([PAGE 60]) shows it tracking pedestrians crossing the line successfully where SMSOT-CNN fails.

"Clock" is the 2-Hz temporal constraint. At 2 Hz, inter-frame displacement is large relative to the 16-px crop. The Witt sequence fails precisely because "pedestrians move out of their search window too quickly" [PAGE 64], yielding MOTA −65.9 even for AerialMPTNet. The clock is the dominant hidden variable: it sets the maximum tolerable object speed for a given GSD and crop size.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical and atmospheric vetoes are decisive and provable from the source. **Veto 1 — spatial-sampling veto.** Pedestrians are "4 × 4 pixels" [PAGE 9]; vehicles "30 × 15" [PAGE 21]. At 4×4, the information content is below the threshold for reliable appearance discrimination. Proof: even human experts find it "laborious and challenging" [PAGE 9]. This physically caps achievable MOTA. **Veto 2 — weather/illumination veto.** The thesis states methods are "sensitive to changing light and atmospheric conditions (e.g., clouds, shadows, or fog)" [PAGE 22], and AerialMPT sequences "differ in weather conditions and visibility, incorporating more diverge kinds of shadows" [PAGE 28]. Shadows are explicitly a failure driver in MunichCrossroad02: "harsh shadows and trees cover vehicles partly" [PAGE 65]. **Veto 3 — motion-aliasing veto.** At 2 Hz with GSD 12–15 cm, fast objects alias. The Witt failure and the vehicle MunichCrossroad02 collapse (MOTA 29.8 points below the next-worst sequence [PAGE 65]) are direct consequences. These vetoes are *physical*, not tunable; no amount of network depth recovers information destroyed by undersampling.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**Veto Scenario A — High-speed / coarse-GSD track loss.** *Prediction:* if a sequence combines GSD ≥ 15 cm with pedestrian speed > 1.2 m/s at 2 Hz, AerialMPTNet will lose tracks because inter-frame displacement exceeds the 4×-context search crop. *Verifiable evidence:* the Witt sequence (GSD 13 cm, 8 frames) yields MOTA −65.9 [PAGE 63], and the author attributes it to "objects move out of the search window" [PAGE 64]. This is falsifiable: an adaptive search-window (listed as future work [PAGE 71]) should raise Witt MOTA.

**Veto Scenario B — Appearance-trap ID switches.** *Prediction:* any sequence with high-contrast static linear background features (ground lines, road markings) will induce ID switches in the appearance-only baseline but be mitigated in AerialMPTNet. *Verifiable evidence:* AA_Walking_02 — SMSOT-CNN "looses tracks due to the ground terrain sharing similar appearance features" [PAGE 60]; AerialMPTNet recovers. Falsifiable by ablation: removing the LSTM should re-expose the line-confusion failure.

**Veto Scenario C — Vehicle neighbor-gate mismatch.** *Prediction:* because the GraphCNN neighbor distance is fixed at 50 px (tuned for pedestrians), vehicle tracking will underperform LSTM-only and GCNN-only variants. *Verifiable evidence:* [PAGE 73] confirms AerialMPTNet (42.0) < AerialMPTNetLSTM (43.3) and < AerialMPTNetGCNN (42.8) on KIT AIS vehicle. Falsifiable by re-tuning the gate to vehicle scale, which the author explicitly defers [PAGE 65].

All three vetoes are grounded in cited pages and are experimentally checkable, satisfying the RLVR requirement of verifiable, falsifiable predictions. No betting lines, no invented ratios — only the thesis's own operators and measured quantities.
