The source uses a pinhole camera operator and a multi-term regression loss. Let the camera parameter vector be  
\[
\theta = (x, y, z, \text{pan}, \text{roll}, \text{tilt}, \text{FOV}),
\]  
where \(x,y,z\) are camera position in meters, pan/roll/tilt are orientation angles in radians for SI analysis, and FOV is an angle in radians. The paper’s Figure 7 examples use scalar values consistent with angular parameters, but the main text does not always specify radians versus degrees; for formal SI treatment, radians are preferred [PAGE 3, PAGE 15].

The forward mapping from world coordinates to normalized camera coordinates is given as  
\[
x_{\text{camera}} = P(X) = I R (X - t),
\]  
where \(X \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is a world point in meters, \(t \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is translation in meters, \(R \in SO(3)\) is an orthogonal rotation matrix, and \(I\) is an intrinsic/NDC operator. The inverse mapping is explicitly derived:  
\[
X = P^{-1}(x_{\text{camera}}) = R^{T} I^{-1} x_{\text{camera}} + t.
\]  
This is stated verbatim in the supplementary derivation: “Thus, our inverse mapping is: \(P inv(xcamera) = RT I−1xcamera + t\)” [PAGE 12]. The dimensional structure is: \(I^{-1}x_{\text{camera}}\) returns a metric displacement when \(I\) is properly scaled; \(R^T\) rotates it back to world axes; \(t\) adds metric translation. Breakdown occurs if \(I\) is singular, if \(R\) loses orthogonality due to unconstrained parameter regression, or if \(t\) is estimated outside the physical camera-placement manifold.

The primary loss is Eq. (1):  
\[
L = w_1 \cdot L^2_{\text{world}} + w_2 \cdot L^2_{\text{camera}} + w_3 \cdot L^1_{\text{parameters}} + w_4 \cdot L^2_{\text{heatmap}}.
\]  
The world-space term is  
\[
L^2_{\text{world}} = \sum_x \left\| P^{-1}_{\text{pred}}(P_{\text{gt}}(x_{\text{world}})) - x_{\text{world}} \right\|_2^2,
\]  
with units of square meters if \(x_{\text{world}}\) is in meters [PAGE 3]. The camera-space term is  
\[
L^2_{\text{camera}} = \sum_x \left\| P_{\text{pred}}(x_{\text{world}}) - P_{\text{gt}}(x_{\text{world}}) \right\|_2^2,
\]  
with units of squared NDC coordinates, effectively dimensionless after NDC normalization [PAGE 4]. The parameter term is  
\[
L^1_{\text{parameters}} = \sum_y \left\| y_{\text{pred}} - y_{\text{gt}} \right\|_1,
\]  
where \(y\) denotes camera parameters; this term mixes meters for position and angular units for orientation, so it is not a pure SI-homogeneous norm unless each parameter is independently normalized [PAGE 4]. The heatmap term is  
\[
L^2_{\text{heatmap}} = \sum_{i,j} \left\| UV_{\text{pred}}(i,j) - UV_{\text{gt}}(i,j) \right\|_2^2,
\]  
where \(i,j\) are heatmap pixel coordinates and the predicted UV values are normalized image-plane coordinates [PAGE 4].

The keypoint refinement operator can be written formally as a discrete search:  
\[
\theta^* = \arg\min_{\delta \in \Delta} \sum_{\ell} d_{\mathbb{R}}\!\left( \text{Line}_\ell(H(\theta + \delta)K), M_\ell \right)^2,
\]  
where \(K\) are detected keypoints, \(H(\theta + \delta)\) is the homography induced by corrected camera parameters, \(M_\ell\) are ideal pitch-model lines, and \(d_{\mathbb{R}}\) is planar distance in meters. The paper states that “the L2 distance between these planar lines and the ideal pitch model is calculated” and “The optimal camera parameters are determined by selecting the set with the smallest L2 difference” [PAGE 4]. The delta set is “such as [−0.15, −0.10, . . . , 0.15]” [PAGE 4]. If these deltas are angular in radians, the range spans approximately \(\pm 8.6^\circ\); if degrees, it spans \(\pm 0.15^\circ\). The source does not explicitly resolve this unit ambiguity, which is a reproducibility gray zone.

The temporal smoothing operator is a bounded Savitzky-Golay correction. If \(\hat{\theta}_t\) is the raw predicted camera state and \(\tilde{\theta}_t\) is the smoothed state, the source specifies a correction envelope:  
\[
\left|\tilde{\theta}_{t,\text{angle}} - \hat{\theta}_{t,\text{angle}}\right| \leq 2^\circ = 0.0349066\ \text{rad},
\]  
\[
\left|\tilde{\theta}_{t,\text{position}} - \hat{\theta}_{t,\text{position}}\right| \leq 2\ \text{m}.
\]  
This is stated verbatim: “maximum adjustment range of ±2 degrees for angles and ±2 meters for positions” [PAGE 4]. The filter uses delayed values: “predictions are delayed by 15 frames (0.5 sec)” [PAGE 4]. Breakdown occurs when the true correction required exceeds the envelope, especially across cuts or rapid camera motion.

The team assignment operator uses cosine distance. The source says athletes are assigned by “minimum cosine distance to the estimated clusters” [PAGE 5]. For embedding \(f_i\) and cluster centroid \(\mu_c\),  
\[
d_{\cos}(f_i, \mu_c) = 1 - \frac{f_i^\top \mu_c}{\|f_i\|\,\|\mu_c\|},
\]  
with \(d_{\cos} \in [0,2]\) and dimensionless units. The cluster set contains “left/right team athletes, referees, and left/right goalkeepers” [PAGE 5]. The spatial query uses “X-axis distance is less than 30 meters from the pitch center” [PAGE 5], a SI length condition.

The tracklet-merge operator is constrained by temporal non-overlap, attribute consistency, and physical feasibility. The source explicitly lists “Temporal Non-Overlap”, “Consistent Team IDs”, and “Physical Feasibility” [PAGE 5]. Formally, for two tracklets ending at \(p_1\) and starting at \(p_2\), with time gap \(\Delta t\),  
\[
\|p_2 - p_1\|_2 \leq v_{\max}\Delta t + \varepsilon,
\]  
where \(v_{\max}\) is a maximum plausible player speed in \(\text{m/s}\), \(\Delta t\) is in seconds, and \(\varepsilon\) is a measurement tolerance in meters. The paper does not give a numeric \(v_{\max}\); it only states that the distance “must be physically traversable by a player within the time gap” [PAGE 5]. Therefore any numerical threshold must be treated as an assumption, not a source-defined constant.

ReID matching uses two operators. First, mean-vector cosine comparison. Second, a cross-multiplication matrix. The source says: “we construct an N × M matrix where each entry represents the cosine similarity between a pair of ReID vectors” and “By identifying the maximum value in this matrix … we ensure that even noisy or inconsistent embeddings do not hinder accurate matching” [PAGE 6]. Formally, for embeddings \(a_i\) and \(b_j\),  
\[
S_{ij} = \cos(a_i, b_j),
\]  
and merging may require \(\max_{i,j} S_{ij} > \tau\), where \(\tau\) is a threshold not specified in the source.

The source states: “Localization Similarity (LocSim) – Measures Euclidean distance in pitch coordinates, smoothed by a Gaussian kernel with a 5-meter tolerance” and “Identification Similarity (IdSim) – Requires exact matches for role, team, and jersey number; mismatches are treated as false positives” [PAGE 8]. Thus, positional error has a metric tolerance around 5 m, while attribute error is gate-like. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The system is not only a tracker; it is an inference machine over multiple agents: players, referees, goalkeepers, the broadcast camera operator, and the evaluator. The source frames GSR as requiring “roles (e.g., player, goalkeeper, referee), jersey numbers, and team affiliations (left or right relative to the camera viewpoint)” [PAGE 1]. This requires a recursive model of appearance, position, role, and temporal continuity.

The first recursive inference is left/right team assignment. The source uses voting across frames: “For each processed frame, we calculate the mean X-coordinate of athletes belonging to one of the two largest clusters and collect votes indicating whether the cluster is positioned more to the left or right” [PAGE 5]. This counter-traps against a naive mean-X failure: “This voting mechanism is more robust than simply calculating the mean X-coordinate across all frames, which can fail in very short clips or scenarios where only part of the football field is visible” [PAGE 5]. In other words, the model anticipates that a single frame or short clip may misrepresent team side because of partial field visibility.

The second recursive inference is goalkeeper role assignment. The source separates goalkeepers: “we query athletes positioned inside the penalty area” and then “Athletes near previously detected clusters are filtered out based on cosine distance to avoid overlap” [PAGE 5]. This assumes that goalkeeper embedding clusters are spatially anchored. The counter-trap is that goalkeepers may leave the penalty area. The source explicitly notes this: “goalkeepers occasionally leave their designated areas” [PAGE 5]. If the goalkeeper is outside the penalty area during a short clip, the model’s prior regresses to the wrong cluster, causing role or team confusion.

The third recursive inference is motion prediction under camera movement. DeepSORT is adapted to operate in world coordinates: “DeepSORT-based algorithm [52] is used for preliminary tracking and operates in real-world pitch coordinates derived from the camera parameters instead of pixel coordinates” [PAGE 4]. This is a counter-trap against pixel-space motion discontinuities caused by camera pan and zoom. However, the tracker still inherits Kalman-style smoothing assumptions, which the paper acknowledges as limited: “Kalman filter limitations in predicting non-linear motion” [PAGE 2]. The orientation constraint is another counter-trap: “restrictions based on player orientation (e.g., preventing 180-degree direction changes in one frame) and TeamID Embeddings are applied” [PAGE 4]. This helps when a player is briefly occluded and the tracker might otherwise associate with an athlete moving in the opposite direction.

The clock layer is explicit. The Savitzky-Golay filter uses delayed predictions: “predictions are delayed by 15 frames (0.5 sec)” [PAGE 4]. This is a temporal belief-formation mechanism: the model trades latency for smoother future-aware correction. The clock also appears in post-processing: tracklet merging requires “Temporal Non-Overlap” and a physical time gap constraint [PAGE 5]. If the gap is too large, interpolation or merging becomes speculative. The evaluation set is also clock-bounded: “200 video sequences (30 seconds each)” [PAGE 7]. Short clips amplify failure because team-side voting, cluster formation, and post-processing have fewer observations.

The crisis habit regression is that the model tends to assume continuity when the broadcast cuts, occlusions occur, or players cluster. The counter-traps are: split tracklets by jersey/team, merge only under physical feasibility, use orientation constraints, use team embeddings, and use ReID cross-similarity. The residual clock risk is that post-processing may fill gaps with linearly interpolated positions that are physically plausible but identity-incorrect. In recursive terms, the system believes a tracklet identity persists across a gap because the gap is short and the endpoint geometry is traversable; however, if two players exchange screen positions during occlusion, the belief becomes false. The source’s strict IdSim rule magnifies this risk: “Requires exact matches for role, team, and jersey number; mismatches are treated as false positives” [PAGE 8]. Therefore, the model’s theory of mind must be conservative: identity continuity is only credible when attribute continuity and physical traversability jointly support it.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto analysis separates physical geometry, biomechanics, and appearance/weather conditions. A veto is not a preference; it is a proof that a proposed reconstruction or merge cannot be certified under the source’s own constraints.

Physical-layer veto: camera-to-pitch mapping validity. The source requires accurate mapping: “enabling the accurate mapping of detected objects from image-space to real-world pitch coordinates” [PAGE 1]. The refinement objective minimizes reprojection error: “minimizing reprojection errors” [PAGE 1] and computes “L2 distance between these planar lines and the ideal pitch model” [PAGE 4]. If the visible keypoint set \(K_t\) does not constrain the homography, the mapping is non-identifiable. Formally, let \(r(\theta) = \sum_\ell d(\text{Line}_\ell(H(\theta)K_t), M_\ell)^2\). If \(\min_\theta r(\theta) > r_{\text{tol}}\), the camera state is vetoed. The source provides a metric tolerance for evaluation: LocSim uses a “5-meter tolerance” [PAGE 8]. If the induced player-position error exceeds that tolerance, the reconstruction cannot satisfy the intended localization quality. Therefore, in a frame with insufficient field markings, any player coordinate derived from that frame should be treated as low-confidence or vetoed for downstream identity merging.

Biomechanical veto: impossible tracklet merge. The source explicitly requires “Physical Feasibility : The distance between the end position of one tracklet and the start position of another must be physically traversable by a player within the time gap between the two tracklets” [PAGE 5]. Let \(p_{\text{end}}\) and \(p_{\text{start}}\) be world coordinates in meters, and \(\Delta t\) be the temporal gap in seconds. A merge is biomechanically valid only if  
\[
\|p_{\text{start}} - p_{\text{end}}\|_2 \leq v_{\max}\Delta t + \varepsilon.
\]  
If the inequality fails, the merge is vetoed regardless of ReID similarity or jersey similarity. This is a hard veto because no appearance evidence can make a non-traversable jump physically valid. The source does not specify \(v_{\max}\), so the threshold must be treated as an assumption; however, the inequality structure is directly supported by the paper.

Role-geometry veto: goalkeeper and referee clustering. The team detection stage uses spatial priors: athletes within 30 m of pitch center are clustered into two team clusters and a referee cluster [PAGE 5]. Goalkeepers are detected inside penalty areas [PAGE 5]. The source warns that “goalkeepers occasionally leave their designated areas” [PAGE 5]. Therefore, if a clip shows only one penalty area and the goalkeeper is outside it, the fallback rule—“select all athletes outside the penalty area”—can misclassify the goalkeeper as an outfield player or contaminate the referee cluster. This is a role-layer veto: the spatial prior is invalidated by observed game state. The prediction is increased role/team error in clips where the goalkeeper is outside the penalty area, especially when the clip is short.

Appearance-layer veto: jersey and ReID ambiguity. The source notes that ReID in football is degraded by “uniform similarity, occlusions, and drastic pose variations” [PAGE 2]. It also states that post-processing depends on “video resolution, weather conditions, and scene complexity” [PAGE 6]. If jersey numbers are occluded, the exact-match requirement of IdSim becomes fragile: “Requires exact matches for role, team, and jersey number” [PAGE 8]. If ReID embeddings from different teammates are closer than embeddings from the same player across frames, the merge criterion can select the wrong identity. Formally, let \(d_{\text{intra}}\) be intra-player embedding distance and \(d_{\text{inter}}\) be inter-player embedding distance. A veto condition is  
\[
d_{\text{inter}} < d_{\text{intra}} + \delta
\]  
for a margin \(\delta\), because the appearance manifold no longer separates identities. In that regime, merging by ReID alone is not certifiable.

Weather/scene veto: degraded observation channel. The source does not provide a weather model, but it acknowledges that quality depends on “weather conditions” [PAGE 6]. In information-theoretic terms, weather reduces mutual information between image crops and latent identities. If the probability of correct jersey classification \(p(J_{\text{correct}})\) falls below the threshold required for exact IdSim matching, attribute reconstruction is vetoed. The source does not specify \(p_{\text{min}}\), but the qualitative mechanism is supported: poor resolution, weather, and scene complexity reduce crop quality, which degrades jersey and ReID features.

Cross-layer proof sketch:  
1. If camera residual \(r(\theta)\) is large, reject coordinate frame.  
2. If coordinate error exceeds LocSim tolerance, reject localization contribution.  
3. If tracklet distance exceeds traversability, reject merge.  
4. If goalkeeper spatial prior is violated, reject role cluster assignment.  
5. If jersey probability or ReID separability fails, reject identity merge.  
These vetoes are cumulative: a frame or tracklet may be physically plausible but still fail attribute certification, and therefore cannot produce a high-confidence GS-HOTA contribution.

The following are three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. In a reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards setting, each veto defines a condition under which the predicted game-state trajectory should receive reduced reward or be marked unverifiable.

Veto Scenario 1: Hard camera cut or rapid pan exceeding smoothing authority.  
Setup: take a valid SoccerNet clip and insert a hard cut or simulate a rapid pan such that the true camera parameter change between consecutive frames exceeds the Savitzky-Golay correction envelope. The source limits correction to “±2 degrees for angles and ±2 meters for positions” [PAGE 4] and uses a delayed window of “15 frames (0.5 sec)” [PAGE 4].  
Formal veto condition:  
\[
|\Delta \theta_{\text{true}}| > \Delta\theta_{\text{filter}}
\]  
for any angular parameter, or  
\[
\|\Delta t_{\text{true}}\|_2 > 2\ \text{m}
\]  
within the smoothing window.  
Prediction: GS-DetA decreases because LocSim penalizes Euclidean pitch-coordinate error; GS-HOTA decreases through the localization term. The measurable signature is a spike in reprojection residual and a jump in inferred player world positions immediately after the cut. Verification: compare predicted camera parameters against ground truth or against a manually annotated cut frame; measure whether residual exceeds the filter envelope. If the veto condition holds, the pipeline’s smoothed trajectory is not causally valid.

Veto Scenario 2: Short partial-field clip with goalkeeper outside the penalty area.  
Setup: select a short clip where the camera shows only one penalty area or a partial field, and the goalkeeper has left the penalty area. The source’s fallback says: “If the input video contains only the left or right penalty area … we instead select all athletes outside the penalty area” [PAGE 5]. The source also warns: “goalkeepers occasionally leave their designated areas” [PAGE 5].  
Formal veto condition:  
\[
\text{GK outside penalty area} \land \text{clip length} < L_{\text{crit}} \land \text{partial field visibility}.
\]  
The exact \(L_{\text{crit}}\) is not source-specified, but the condition is structurally verifiable.  
Prediction: goalkeeper role errors increase; team or referee clusters may absorb the goalkeeper embedding; GS-HOTA decreases because IdSim requires exact role and team matching [PAGE 8]. Verification: annotate goalkeeper positions in test clips, filter for goalkeepers outside the penalty area, and compare role accuracy before and after applying the fallback rule. If the veto condition holds, the cluster assignment is not identifiable from the visible spatial prior.

Veto Scenario 3: Occluded jersey numbers with high ReID confusion among teammates.  
Setup: synthetically occlude jersey-number regions in clips where two same-team players have visually similar uniforms. The source acknowledges that ReID suffers from “uniform similarity, occlusions, and drastic pose variations” [PAGE 2]. The post-processing merge can use ReID when jersey numbers are missing [PAGE 6].  
Formal veto condition:  
\[
p(J_{\text{visible}}) < p_{\text{min}}
\]  
and  
\[
d_{\cos}(f_{\text{player A}}, f_{\text{player B}}) < d_{\cos}(f_{\text{A,frame1}}, f_{\text{A,frame2}}) + \delta.
\]  
Here \(p_{\text{min}}\) is a required jersey-visibility probability and \(\delta\) is an embedding separation margin. The source does not specify these thresholds, but the inequality formalizes the failure regime: appearance evidence cannot distinguish identities.  
Prediction: tracklet fragmentation may decrease falsely while GS-AssA and GS-HOTA decrease due to identity swaps; IdSim penalizes mismatches as false positives [PAGE 8]. Verification: measure GS-AssA and GS-HOTA on clips with jersey occlusion masks, and compare with unoccluded controls. If the veto condition holds, ReID-only merging is not certifiable.

These three vetoes are verifiable because they depend on observable conditions: camera discontinuity, goalkeeper position relative to the penalty area, and jersey/ReID separability. They are also predictive: each condition should cause a measurable decrease in the relevant GS-HOTA component, especially GS-DetA for camera error, role/team accuracy for goalkeeper spatial-prior violation, and GS-AssA for ReID/jersey identity confusion.
