**2. Tchebycheff's Inequality**
*Statement:* If $f$ is a measurable function with finite variance (i.e., $f \in L^2(\mu)$ and $\mathbb{E}[f^2] < \infty$), then for every $\epsilon > 0$:

**3. Kolmogoroff's Inequality**
*Context:* A refinement for sums of independent random variables. Let $X_1, \dots, X_n$ be independent with $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = 0$ and $\text{Var}(X_i) = \sigma_i^2$. Let $S_k = \sum_{i=1}^k X_i$.
*Kolmogoroff's Maximal Inequality:*
$$ P\left( \max_{1 \le k \le n} |S_k| \ge \lambda \right) \le \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2}{\lambda^2} $$
*Relation to Source:* The text notes that applying Tchebycheff separately to each $S_k$ yields $P(|S_k| \ge \lambda) \le \frac{\text{Var}(S_k)}{\lambda^2} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i^2}{\lambda^2}$. Summing these probabilities (Union Bound) gives a looser bound for the maximum. Kolmogoroff's result improves this by exploiting orthogonality (independence) to bound the trajectory maximum directly by the total variance, avoiding the factor of $n$ loss from the union bound.
