Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week traps.

Lineage: Reep and Benjamin (1968) used negative binomial distributions for football scores and concluded matches could not be predicted reliably [PAGE 2]. Karlis and Ntzoufras (2003) introduced bivariate Poisson models for team goals [PAGE 3]. Baio and Blangiardo (2010) proposed a Bayesian hierarchical model for Serie A outcomes [PAGE 3]. Dixon and Coles (1997) showed that predicting well-performed teams is easier than predicting individual games [PAGE 4]. Rue and Salvesen (2000) built a time-dependent Bayesian dynamic model for attack and defense strength [PAGE 2]. Joseph, Fenton, and Neil (2006) compared expert Bayesian networks to machine-learning classifiers for Tottenham Hotspur, reporting accuracy variation between 38% and 59% [PAGE 2]. Timmaraju et al. (2013) achieved 60% accuracy with RBF-SVM using a one-season train/test split [PAGE 2]. Steffen (2017) reported over 72% accuracy on the final games of the 2015/16 EPL season, noting late-season matches are easier to predict [PAGE 3]. Zaveri et al. (2018) reached 71.63% accuracy with logistic regression on five La Liga seasons [PAGE 3]. Elo (1978) provided the chess rating framework later adapted to football [PAGE 4]. Huang and Chen (2011) used multilayer perceptrons to reach 75% accuracy on World Cup games when draws were excluded [PAGE 4].

Multi-week traps: Hamadani (2005) found that feature significance rotates across seasons, implying either an unstable game or a non-stationary optimal feature set [PAGE 3]. The present paper’s fixed feature list cannot rotate with regime shifts. Relegation and promotion create cold-start vertices in the team graph; the authors’ zero-imputation for missing teams creates phantom draws that corrupt λ_j^i and GD_k [PAGE 4]. Late-season predictability noted by Steffen [PAGE 3] is never tested in this study, which evaluates only the full 2018/19 hold-out season.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression: under squad stress—injuries, red cards, suspensions—the model lacks an acute-state input and therefore regresses to its static prior, namely the rating differential ΔR and the home-win base rate. It tracks long-run hierarchy rather than current match state, so its predictions systematically underweight crisis-driven deviations.

Counter-trap: because the classifier is deterministic given ΔR and rolling averages, an adversary who knows the model can exploit contexts where rating differentials compress (derbies, cup matches, matches involving teams with depleted squads). The model offers no adversarial robustness layer and no mechanism to down-weight over-favored high-ΔR sides when situational factors dominate.

Clock: Equation (3) weights all k previous matches equally, so a performance from six weeks ago carries the same mass as the most recent match. The fifaindex.com overall rating is effectively an annual snapshot [PAGE 5], while intra-season transfers, injuries, and fatigue change physical team strength continuously. Consequently, model accuracy should decay as the season progresses away from the rating snapshot date, with the largest degradation during festive fixture congestion and post-transfer-window windows.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Physical layer: Association football is played 11-a-side under fixed Laws of the Game; a red card removes a player and changes passing lanes, defensive coverage, and expected goal geometry nonlinearly. None of the 64 raw columns or the selected engineered features encode player count changes [PAGE 4–5].

Biomechanical layer: Player workload, muscular readiness, sprint capacity, and injury status are absent. The fifaindex.com overall rating is a static scalar aggregate [PAGE 5] and cannot capture acute fatigue or musculoskeletal degradation.

Weather layer: EPL matches are played in rain, wind, and low temperature, which alter ball roll, passing accuracy, and aerial contest physics. No weather column exists in the public CSV or in the engineered feature set [PAGE 4–5].

Cross-layer veto adjudication: any match presenting (i) a starting goalkeeper or defender injury, (ii) suspension of a top-three attacker, or (iii) extreme weather invalidates the assumption that ΔR and rolling λ averages describe the current match state. Because the model contains no override input, its output is physically ungrounded under these conditions. A cross-layer veto must suppress the prediction and revert to base rates or abstain rather than emit a stale static-rating classification.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios and predictions.

Scenario 1 — Injury veto. Counterfactual: a hold-out match in which the home team loses two or more starting defenders but ΔR remains unchanged because fifaindex.com ratings were not updated. Veto rule: if verified injury count exceeds threshold, suppress model output. Prediction: accuracy on injured-home matches collapses to ≤0.40, below the ≈0.45 home-win base rate. Verifiability: merge Transfermarkt or PhysioRoom injury logs with the 2018/19 hold-out fixtures and recompute the confusion matrix.

Scenario 2 — Promotion zero-imputation veto. Counterfactual: a match involving a promoted club within its first eight fixtures. Veto rule: if either team has a zero-imputed rating due to absence from the prior-season fifaindex.com dataset [PAGE 4], abstain. Prediction: accuracy on promoted-team matches ≤ baseline (~0.45), not 0.543, because phantom draw history corrupts λ averages and GD_k. Verifiability: identify promoted clubs in the hold-out season and isolate their matches.

Scenario 3 — Fixture-congestion clock veto. Counterfactual: a match where the home team played fewer than 72 hours previously. Veto rule: if inter-match interval < 3 days, apply exponential decay or abstain, because Equation (3) weights old and recent matches equally [PAGE 5–6]. Prediction: accuracy in congested weeks falls to ≈0.48–0.50 (near random forest’s 0.498), while non-congested matches hold ≈0.54–0.56. Verifiability: compute inter-match intervals from the fixture list and stratify hold-out accuracy by congestion bin.

All three scenarios are verifiable with public injury records, promotion status, and fixture dates; none requires proprietary spreads, totals, moneylines, or odds.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
