Verbatim lineage example [PAGE 10]: “if a diamond is worth 2 circles, a circle is worth 4 ETH, and a star is worth 2 ETH, a diamond is worth 8 ETH, and star is worth 2 ETH. <RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The patent lineage traces from Szabo’s bit gold and Buterin’s Ethereum white paper through the Bancor protocol (Bprotocol) smart-contract family, specifically US 11,107,049 B2. The core intellectual contribution is an on-chain bonding curve that replaces an order book with a reserve-backed spot price \(P = T_R/(R_r T_t)\), historically motivated by the problem of continuous liquidity for long-tail tokens. The “mutual ancestor” construct is a formalization of currency triangulation: just as cross rates in foreign exchange are computed through a numéraire (historically gold, then USD), here every token type reduces to a common ancestor token, ultimately ETH in the described implementations [PAGE 9–10].

Multi-week traps arise from the tension between the static parameterization at issuance and dynamic market reality. At issuance, \(T_R = R_r T_t\) fixes spot price at one underlying unit per token; however the text says the ratio is “maintained” without specifying an oracle, rebalancing transaction, or administrative trigger [PAGE 9]. Over weeks, if external market price diverges from the bonding-curve spot price, arbitrageurs drain or replenish the reserve, changing \(T_R\) and \(T_t\) and thereby moving \(P\) along the curve. Because the reserve ratio is a constant parameter while supply fluctuates, the system does not guarantee price stability; it guarantees only that the reserve covers a fixed fraction of outstanding tokens at the pricing formula’s evaluation instant. A second multi-week trap is the recursive redemption path: holders of descendant tokens can exchange down through underlying tokens to a mutual ancestor (steps 510–532). In a stressed market over a multi-week horizon, rational actors anticipate reserve depletion and accelerate redemptions, creating a bank-run dynamic that the contract does not pause or rate-limit. No circuit-breaker, time-lock, or emergency shutdown is disclosed in the provided pages.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under market stress, token holders regress to the most liquid exit available—exchanging held tokens for their underlying token and iterating until a requested ancestor or reserve asset is reached (steps 510, 520, 531) [PAGE 10]. Because each iteration reads live blockchain state (step 503/602), a self-fulfilling redemption cascade forms: each burn reduces \(T_t\), which under \(P = T_R/(R_r T_t)\) raises the spot price for remaining holders if \(T_R\) is unchanged, encouraging earlier exit by others who fear dilution of reserve coverage. Participants recursively model this: “If I believe others will redeem star for ETH first, I should redeem my descendant token for star now to avoid the ancestor bottleneck.” This is a second-order theory-of-mind loop over the contract’s state machine.

Counter-trap: an adversarial participant can front-run the mutual-ancestor resolution by submitting a transaction that exchanges held tokens for the underlying reserve before a large redemption transaction updates \(T_R\) and \(T_t\), thereby capturing a more favorable spot price. Because the exchange method does not disclose a commitment-reveal or batch-auction mechanism, the counter-trap is simply priority-based transaction ordering on the Ethereum mempool.

Clock: the binding constraint is block finality latency versus recursive depth. Each exchange leg requires a blockchain read and state mutation; if the ancestor chain length \(n\) times the per-leg gas cost exceeds the block gas limit, the transaction fails. Moreover, if the oracle or off-chain keeper that “maintains” the reserve ratio updates at a cadence slower than the redemption clock (e.g., daily rebalance versus intraday redemptions), the on-chain \(R_r\) becomes stale, and agents trade against a mispriced bonding curve. The clock therefore favors the first redeemer in each block and punishes late recursive resolvers.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: the contract’s correctness depends on Ethereum node hardware and network finality. Extreme weather events (heatwaves, floods, storms) that regionalize validator or mining operator downtime can increase block times or cause temporary chain finality delays. If a state retrieval in step 503 returns a block that does not yet include the latest reserve transfer, the price operator \(P = T_R/(R_r T_t)\) evaluates against stale \(T_R\), and the exchange settles at an economically invalid rate. Veto condition: if latest finalized block timestamp minus local system clock exceeds threshold \(\Delta_{\text{finality}}\), veto the exchange.

Biomechanical/human layer: admin or multisig keys that can alter token-type parameters (identifier, underlying reserve token, reserve ratio constant) are operated by humans subject to fatigue, social engineering, and coercion. A compromised key can set \(R_r=0\) or \(T_R=0\), triggering the division-by-zero singularity, or can drain the reserve directly. Veto condition: any parameter change must be time-locked and multi-signed; a single-signature parameter mutation is a biomechanical veto trigger.

Weather/environmental veto proof: because the system assumes continuous Ethereum finality to read “Total Tokens in Circulation, Total Reserve, and Reserve Ratio Constant” [PAGE 10], a prolonged network partition caused by regional infrastructure loss (e.g., undersea cable damage, data-center cooling failure) invalidates the liveness assumption. The contract has no fallback price source; therefore exchange operations must be vetoed until finality is restored. This is a physical veto, not a financial one: it does not reference spreads, moneylines, or odds.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
