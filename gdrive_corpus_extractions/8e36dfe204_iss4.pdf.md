<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Level 0 (applicant's naive model):** "I need a number; any channel works." The document refutes this with a partition, not a ranking [PAGE 1–2].

**Level 1 (applicant models the queue):** chooses channel by latency. This yields the correct first-order rule — online if U.S./territory nexus and a valid TIN exist; otherwise fax for speed; mail only with ≥4–5 weeks of slack [PAGE 1–2].

**Level 2 (the IRS models the applicant):** the agency's countermeasures reveal an adversary model — duplicate applications are expected, hence the one-method rule and the per-party daily cap [PAGE 1]; designee overreach is expected, hence automatic authority termination at release [PAGE 1] and the forced paper/fax path when designee contact data matches the taxpayer's [PAGE 6]; "N/A" abuse is expected, hence "Don't enter 'N/A.' A selection is required" on line 10 [PAGE 5] and "An entry is required" on line 17 [PAGE 6].

**Level 3 (applicant models the IRS modeling them):** the sophisticated applicant pre-commits to a single channel, avoids the shared-contact designee configuration, and pre-registers the fallback annotation. The residual risk at Level 3 is *transmission*, not *policy* — and the document provides no explicit remedy for a fax that never lands, only "Call [PHONE] to verify a number or to ask about the status of an application by mail" [PAGE 2], which is scoped to mail.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under deadline compression, applicants regress to three documented failure modes: (i) *shotgunning* — submitting online and by fax "just in case," which violates the cardinality constraint and risks "more than one EIN for an entity" [PAGE 1]; (ii) *identifier substitution* — "Don't use your EIN in place of your social security number (SSN)" [PAGE 1] and "Don't show your SSN as an EIN on returns" [PAGE 1], habits formed because both are 9-digit strings; (iii) *blank-field panic* — leaving line 10 or line 9a incomplete, which the document pre-empts with mandatory-entry language [PAGE 4–5].

**Counter-trap.** The correct counter-move is not speed but *classification before submission*: determine entity type (O10), determine whether an EIN already exists, then pick exactly one channel. The document's own sequencing supports this: "Follow the instructions for each line to expedite processing and to avoid unnecessary IRS requests for additional information" [PAGE 2].

**Clock hierarchy.** Three clocks compete and must be ordered: the *need clock* $t_{\text{need}}$, the *channel clock* $L_c$, and the *service-window clock* $\mathcal{W}$ (06:00–23:00 ET, Mon–Fri, telephone only) [PAGE 1]. The applicant's urgency is not a clock at all; it enters only through $S$. The dominant error is treating $t_{\text{need}}$ as binding while ignoring that $L_{\text{mail}}$ has zero margin against its own recommendation [PAGE 2].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Adjudicate five layers; each is a Boolean gate, and a gate failure cannot be compensated by performance in another layer.

**L0 — Physical transport (postal carrier, fax telephony, voice network).** Fax-TIN "is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week" [PAGE 2], so the fax *gate* is nominally open continuously; mail has no stated availability constraint. **Veto proof:** "Be sure to provide your fax number so the IRS can fax the EIN back to you" [PAGE 2] — an omitted return-fax number is a hard veto on *delivery of the result*, even if the application itself is perfect. Weather/carrier disruption acts here and is *not* mitigated anywhere in the document; the only stated recovery is a status call, scoped to mail [PAGE 2].

**L1 — Credential layer.** Online requires a valid TIN held by the principal officer/general partner/grantor/owner/trustor [PAGE 1]. **Veto proof:** "If you have NO legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the United States or U.S. territories, you can't use the online application to obtain an EIN" [PAGE 1]. This is a categorical, non-probabilistic veto — no amount of preparation cures it; the applicant must switch layers.

**L2 — Legal-form and signature layer (the "biomechanical" layer: a human hand must sign).** "You must complete the signature area for the authorization to be valid" [PAGE 1]; the signature must come from "(a) the individual… (b) the president, vice president, or other principal officer… (c) a responsible and duly authorized member or officer having knowledge of its affairs… or (d) the fiduciary" [PAGE 6]. **Veto proof:** an unsigned application cannot be validated regardless of data quality; a signatory outside classes (a)–(d) voids the instrument. For foreign applicants the class widens to "any duly authorized person (for example, division manager)" [PAGE 6] — a jurisdictional relaxation, not a removal.

**L3 — Temporal layer.** Telephone window $[06{:}00,23{:}00]$ ET Mon–Fri [PAGE 1]; 24-hour faxback turnaround if requested [PAGE 1]; 24-hour Form 8871 [PAGE 4]; 15th-day-of-3rd-month Form 2553 [PAGE 4]; 60-day Form 8822-B [PAGE 1, PAGE 3]. **Veto proof:** missing the 2553 window changes the default classification ("you will be considered a Form 1120 filer" [PAGE 4]) — the veto operates on *status*, not on the application.

**L4 — Administrative layer.** Per-party daily cap and one-method rule [PAGE 1]. **Veto proof:** a second same-day submission by the same responsible party is refused by construction, no matter how correct the paperwork. Note the domain singularity flagged in O2: "per day" is undefined in time zone, so the veto's boundary is formally indeterminate.

**Cross-layer conflict (the key adjudication).** L0 failure (a fax that never arrives) collides with L4 (one method per entity). The document resolves *duplicate success*, not *transmission failure*: it says "Use only one method for each entity so you don't receive more than one EIN for an entity" [PAGE 1] — the stated harm is duplicate issuance, which presupposes the first submission succeeded. **Adjudication:** re-submission after verified non-receipt is not the harm the rule targets, but the document never grants that permission explicitly, and the only verification channel it names is scoped to mail [PAGE 2]. This is an unresolved gray zone; the defensible reading is that the constraint binds on *issued* EINs (which the daily cap also polices), not on *attempted* transmissions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
