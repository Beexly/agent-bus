The subsequent scientific genealogy runs through Thurstone’s primary mental abilities, Cattell’s fluid–crystallized distinction, hierarchical models (g at the apex), and modern behavior-genomic methods such as genome-wide complex trait analysis (GCTA) and polygenic scoring, which attempt to measure the “heredity” term in Tolman’s fourth principle directly.

A multi-week trap is embedded in the o factor—“oscillations of efficiency, fatiguability” [PAGE 401]. If an experimenter samples performance only once per week, the within-person oscillation is aliased into between-person trait variance, falsely inflating σ²_{G,j} or σ²_{E,j}. A longitudinal design must sample at sub-daily intervals to separate trait-level g/c from state-level o dynamics. Conversely, treating all factors as time-invariant (as the Gaussian cross-sectional assumption implicitly does) produces a retrospective distortion: current performance is explained by a factor that is itself partly constituted by past performance, creating a retrocausal loop in which the outcome appears to determine its own putative cause.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under high-stakes or novel-task pressure, an individual’s behavior regresses from flexible, purposive intervention to over-learned S→R habits. In Tolman’s terms, the high-order weighting w_{aj} collapses onto a narrow set of previously reinforced factors (often c, “mental inertia”), suppressing g-mediated exploration. The organism reverts to the path of least cognitive energy, which is precisely the failure mode of the g factor as “mental energy” [PAGE 401]: energy is not a fungible reserve but is allocated by habit structure.

Counter-trap: the analyst who observes robust g-loadings across tasks may conclude that a single general capacity governs all performance. This ignores principle three’s differential weighting [PAGE 401]. A task can be g-loaded in one population and s-loaded in another if the requirement structure changes; the counter-trap is to assume factor importance is invariant across context rather than task-specific (w_{aj} is a function of a).

Clock: multiple timescales govern the model. Millisecond-to-second neural execution gates whether a high-F_j individual can express the factor; seconds-to-minutes fatigue (o) modulates within-session efficiency; days-to-weeks training (e_j E_j) shifts environmental allotment; generations (h_j G_j) shift population means. A theory that operates only at the cross-sectional Gaussian level conflates these clocks and misattributes week-scale environmental change to hereditary trait variance.
