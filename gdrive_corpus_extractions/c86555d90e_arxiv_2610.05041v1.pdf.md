<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Game and measurement primitives (population counts n_t, m_t are dimensionless integers; day index t ∈ ℕ):
- Blind lynch hit probability: P(hit mafia | roles) = m_t / n_t, the living mafia population share [PAGE 2].
- Tilt model: logit(q_t) = logit(m_t / n_t) + θ, where θ ∈ ℝ is per-lynch tilt in log-probability-ratio units; positive θ favors town [PAGE 2].
- Exact partial likelihood: L(θ) = ∏_t q_t^{h_t} (1 − q_t)^{1 − h_t}, exact because night kills remove only citizens so (n_t, m_t) and stopping time are functions of the lynch sequence [PAGE 2].
- Estimator: θ̂ maximizes Σ_t [ h_t log q_t + (1 − h_t) log(1 − q_t) ] over an arm’s lynches [PAGE 2].
- Zero-information mafia win probability w(n, m) in closed form [17]; examples w(16, 3) = 0.576, w(100, 7) = 0.530 [PAGE 2].

Reference surface and fair line (n dimensionless; x = m/√n dimensionless; Φ standard normal CDF; φ standard normal PDF):
- Large-n limit at fixed density: w(n, x√n) → 2Φ(x) − 1 as n → ∞; first correction +x²φ(x)/√n [PAGE 3].
- Fair mafia count m*(n) where w = 1/2: m*/√n runs 0.646–0.657 over tested sizes (≈ 0.65√n) and tends to Φ^{-1}(3/4) = 0.6745 as 0.6745 − 0.2275/√n [PAGE 3].
- Mean mafia trajectory: m_t ≈ M √(1 − τ), τ = 2t/n, within 0.2–1.5% at mid-game [PAGE 3].
- Win-rate sensitivity at θ = 0: d(town)/dθ = 0.49 at (16,3), 0.66 at (46,5), 0.79 at (100,7) [PAGE 3].

Headline statics (θ̂ in log-probability-ratio units; 95% CIs):
- Broadcast − silent: Δθ = +0.62 [+0.44, +0.81] at (46,5) over 120 paired castings; town rate 0.70 vs. 0.29 [PAGE 4].
- Generation-1 broadcast − silent: +1.25 [+0.54, +2.22] on 23 matched castings [PAGE 4].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The paper’s theoretical ancestry runs from classical hidden-role game theory to contemporary multi-agent LLM evaluation. Braverman, Etesami, and Mossel [5] established the Θ(√R) fair line for town play under simultaneously announced random numbers; Yao [32] provided recursion-based bounds; Migdał [17] supplied the exact closed-form zero-information solution used here as the fixed ruler; Tamashiro [25] extended information-zero periodic random-elimination chains [PAGES 1, 8]. The experimental design—societies of 8–100 claude-haiku-4-5 agents adapting across generations by rewriting private strategy notes—sits in a lineage of LLM social-deduction work including Werewolf Arena [2], Hoodwinked [19], ChatEval [6], and asynchronous Mafia chat [11], but departs by anchoring multi-day societies to Mafia’s own exact theory rather than fitting capability models to four-player, single-day variants [8] [PAGE 1].

A distinct lineage concerns cultural transmission versus selection [21, 26]. The paper explicitly rejects selection/recombination: lineages accumulate and inherit experience through self-authored notes, nothing is scored or recombined [PAGE 4]. This creates a multi-week trap visible across 60-generation chains: the notes keep changing (≈0.89 rewritten per generation, length doubling 420 → 897 words) while outcomes show no systematic trend in level (last third vs. middle third: 0 of 30 cells; early-vs-late tilt: 3 of 34 arms, both directions) [PAGE 7]. The trap is that adaptation’s fastest and most uniform act is destructive: citizens un-learn the generation-1 claim convention by generation 2, and the inherited notes carry that destruction into frozen redeployment, where evolved notes score below blank notes [PAGE 6]. Another trap is scale: limited talk is worth less at 70 players than at 46, and full broadcast does not fit the context window at 100, so the value of communication collapses precisely as the population scales beyond the architecture’s memory bandwidth [PAGES 4–5]. A third trap is temporal misalignment: the insight that claims are informative is written into notes only in 9 of 56 chains, with median first writing at generation 19, while the convention collapses at generation 2 [PAGE 6]. The written culture therefore lags the behavioral regime shift it purports to encode, and “analysts get killed” rises from 27.8% to 73.3% of broadcast notes even as the first-day cue evaporates [PAGES 7–8].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Untrained societies arrive with an honest separating convention: citizens open with explicit “I am a citizen” claims (91% of 351 statements) while mafia mostly refrain (30%) [PAGE 6]. Under the pressure of night kills—citizens who claim draw the night kill at O/E 1.37 [1.21, 1.56], and a self-announced detective is killed the following night 40 of 46 times (O/E 12.6)—this convention regresses [PAGE 7]. Broadcast citizen claims fall 91% → 21% by generations 2–3 and settle at 13%; mafia claims drop to ≤0.6% and never recover [PAGE 6]. This is a crisis-induced abandonment of the most informative first-day cue: with 87% of citizens silent about role, non-claiming carries almost no information (lift 3.53 → 1.12) [PAGE 6].

Counter-trap. Under turn-taking, the recursive mind-reading loop inverts: mafia who speak after claimers copy the claim (claim probability jumps from 0.005 to 0.41 for citizens and 0.0003 to 0.24 for mafia after an earlier claim; 220 of 221 later-position day-1 mafia lies follow one) [PAGE 7]. The channel then carries exposure and imitation rather than information: landslides are as frequent as broadcast but land on mafia near chance (1.08× vs. 2.53×), and the first speaker—usually a citizen—is lynched at 1.66× [1.58, 1.73] the position-blind rate [PAGES 7–8]. Mafia discipline completes the counter-trap: partner ballots fall far below chance (0.61% vs. 16.1% at k = 0), and accusations run at 0.30× the citizen rate [PAGE 7].

Clock. The adaptation clock runs faster than the written culture. The first-day accuracy edge decays in step with claim collapse: 3.0× → 1.7× → 1.2× chance [PAGE 6]. Frozen cross-deployment shows that generation-60 notes suppress claims even when they do not mention them; the insight is written early in only 9 of 56 chains (median first writing: generation 19), yet generation-2 citizens whose notes say nothing about claims still fall to 18.8% claiming [PAGE 6]. Over sixty generations the notes keep changing while outcomes show no detected trend of level; what broadcast changes is the coupling—generations cohere and families individuate [PAGE 7]. The scalar tilt also mispredicts talking-arm wins at (70,6) (observed-minus-predicted up to +0.23 at k = 12), indicating the per-lynch clock of θ does not capture endgame dynamics where θ_end − θ_bulk = +1.33 [PAGE 5].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The following hard constraints act as vetoes that falsify broad claims about communication value, independent of statistical significance:

1. Context-window veto (physical/architectural). Full broadcast at n = 100 cannot run in this stack: a measured k = 20 game overflowed a surviving seat’s 200k-token context by day 38, projecting ~330k tokens required [PAGE 5]. Any claim that “more communication is better at scale” is vetoed by token exhaustion before endgame.

2. Memory-defect veto (data integrity). In a 15-day window, machine memory pressure dropped persisted turns in 549 games (11.8% of chain games; 0.12% of turns) [PAGE 4]. Excluding them preserves broadcast–silent resolution in 9/9 cells but causes two secondary contrasts to lose resolution, so those secondary claims are vetoed as non-robust.

3. Scale veto (bandwidth × population). At (70,6), k = 8 sits below silence (−0.54 vs. −0.07), and the cross-size interaction vs. (46,5) is resolved at −0.70 for k = 8 [PAGE 5]. The hypothesis “limited talk adds value monotonically with bandwidth” is vetoed at n = 70.

4. Blank-notes veto (adaptation subtracts value). On 40 identical castings at (16,3), every generation-60 note set scores below blank notes, resolved for five of six families (broadcast-trained evolved − blank = −1.37 [−1.97, −0.83]) [PAGE 6]. The claim “sixty generations of experience improve play” is vetoed in frozen redeployment.

5. Detective veto (disclosure is costly). The detective variant at (26,4) did not resolvably raise town win rate (tilt lift +0.16 [−0.12, +0.45]) and stays below the exact benchmark 0.770 (binomial p = 0.003); the detective is killed the following night 40 of 46 times [PAGE 5]. The claim “informed minority disclosure helps town” is vetoed.

6. Sequential veto (reaction-first erases information). Turn-taking trails broadcast in 7 of 9 cells; its landslides land on mafia near chance (1.08×) [PAGES 4, 7]. The claim “any talk helps” is vetoed for sequential protocols.

7. Silence veto (minority exploits residual channels). Silent societies sit below the reference in six cells, reaching θ̂ = −0.84 (town 0.028) at (16,5), because the minority steers elimination through ballots and night kills [PAGE 4]. The claim “silence is neutral” is vetoed; silence favors the informed minority.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions:

Scenario 1 — Context-overflow replication at n = 100. Deploy full simultaneous broadcast (k = n − 1 = 99) at n = 100 with no summarization or output caps. Veto condition: if the transcript overflows the surviving seats’ context before endgame, the partial-likelihood conditioning set is truncated and the per-lynch tilt θ is no longer identified over the full lynch sequence. Prediction: context overflow occurs by day ≤ 40 (extrapolating from the k = 20 overflow at day 38 [PAGE 5]), and the measured town win rate will collapse toward the silent reference because later lynches occur without access to earlier statements.

Scenario 2 — Blank-notes redeployment at (16,3). Take generation-60 notes from each broadcast-trained family and redeploy them on 40 fresh matched castings with learning off, against blank-notes controls. Veto condition: if evolved notes outperform blank notes, the paper’s claim that adaptation subtracts value is falsified. Prediction: θ̂_evolved < θ̂_blank with the difference CI excluding 0 for at least five of six families, replicating broadcast-trained evolved − blank = −1.37 [−1.97, −0.83] [PAGE 6], because the notes encode suppression of the generation-1 claim cue rather than improved inference.

Scenario 3 — Cross-size bandwidth inversion. Replicate the (46,5) k = 8 arm at n = 70 using castings matched to the silent (70,6) chain, run for 20 generations. Veto condition: if [k − silent](70) ≥ [k − silent](46), the claim that limited talk is worth less at larger n is falsified. Prediction: [k − silent](70) − [k − silent](46) < 0 with CI excluding 0, reproducing the resolved interaction −0.70 [−1.15, −0.24] at k = 8 [PAGE 5], because read-eight-statements-per-day at 70 players sits below silence while the same bandwidth adds value at 46.
