<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive model has three agents: the tracker’s belief about identity group \(Q_i(t)\), the annotator’s ground-truth identity, and the metric’s model of identity error. The tracker does not directly know a person’s name or player number. It maintains \(\phi_i^l(t)\), the probability that an occupied location belongs to group \(l\) [PAGE 8]. When appearance is uninformative, it sets \(\phi_i^l(t)=1/L\) [PAGE 15]. When the LP relaxation is fractional, the paper says the non-integer results “can be interpreted as an uncertainty about identity assignment by our algorithm” [PAGE 8]. This is the formal recursive belief: the algorithm represents its own ambiguity through fractional flow mass.

Crisis habit regression: under crowding or occlusion, the system’s appearance channel collapses toward a uniform prior. The paper’s designed response is not to hallucinate appearance but to fall back to geometry and global flow. The method “first ignore appearance and run the K-Shortest Path algorithm” [PAGE 9], then prune, then apply MCNF. This is a regression to the appearance-free habit, but it is a controlled regression. The danger is that an operator might interpret the fallback as full identity knowledge. The source warns against that: appearance models are “most needed when the players are bunched together, and it is precisely then that they are the least reliable” [PAGE 2].

Counter-trap: over-trusting appearance during a crisis. If two people are close, the color histogram may be contaminated by segmentation error [PAGE 15], the number may be unreadable [PAGE 17], or the face may not be detected [PAGE 17]. A naive system might increase appearance weight exactly when appearance is worst. The source’s counter-trap is to keep the appearance term sparse and to let whole-trajectory constraints resolve identity. The paper says appearance “needs only be discriminative in a very limited number of frames” [PAGE 2], and that reasoning is over “whole trajectories” [PAGE 15]. The correct recursive move is to wait for a high-confidence cue, not to force a low-confidence cue.

Clock: the system’s causal update is batched. The paper reports a “constant 4-second delay” from 100-frame batches [PAGE 6] and a “constant 3.4s delay” from 50-frame batches at 15 Hz [PAGE 17]. This clock matters for theory of mind because the tracker’s belief is not instantaneous; it is a delayed posterior over a batch. A human observer watching live may know an identity switch before the batch output does. Conversely, the batch can use future frames within the batch to revise identity, which is a form of limited look-ahead. The paper’s real-time claim is therefore not “zero-latency inference”; it is “bounded-latency batch inference.”

The recursive failure mode is metric confusion: the tracker’s internal fractional uncertainty, the annotator’s discrete identity, and the metric’s switch count are three different representations. MOTA’s \(mme_t\) counts instantaneous switches [PAGE 18], while GMOTA’s \(gmme_t\) counts all times identity is wrong [PAGE 18]. A tracker can look good under one representation and bad under another. The correct audit is to compare the tracker’s uncertainty, the ground-truth identity, and the persistent-error metric simultaneously. The paper’s strongest identity-preservation evidence is therefore GMOTA, not MOTA, and the strongest uncertainty evidence is fractional flow, not rounded output.

The paper’s admissible layers are sensor geometry, occupancy probability, graph flow, appearance classification, illumination, resolution, camera motion, and optimization integrality. The paper’s Table 1 variables [PAGE 5] contain no weather variable, no temperature, no wind, no precipitation, no biomechanical force, no joint torque, no fatigue, no physiological load, and no ground-reaction variable. Therefore no physical, biomechanical, or weather veto can be proven from this PDF. Any such veto is out-of-source and must be marked unadjudicated by this document.

Sensor-layer vetoes are proven. Close proximity and occlusion make color extraction “unreliable” [PAGE 15]. When no appearance cue is available, the method sets \(\phi_i^l(t)=1/L\) [PAGE 15], so identity is not appearance-vetoed; it is appearance-blinded. APIDIS is challenged by “strong specular reflections and poor lighting” [PAGE 18]. Low-resolution ISSIA makes “the shirt numbers unreadable” [PAGE 17]. Moving-camera FIBA is handled by DPM rather than static background subtraction [PAGE 12–13], but the original DPM is “insufficient” for basketball’s “large amplitude motions” without retraining [PAGE 12]. These are source-layer vetoes: they constrain what identity evidence is available.

Geometry-layer vetoes are proven. The graph discretizes the ground plane into \(K\) locations and restricts motion to \(N(i)\) [PAGE 4–5]. Occupancy is at most one person per location in Eq. 2 [PAGE 6], and at most one person across groups in Eq. 4 [PAGE 8]. If a proposed identity claim violates those constraints, the source-layer veto wins. The paper’s pruning step also vetoes unnecessary nodes: it keeps trajectories found by KSP plus connectors [PAGE 9]. Tracklets further reduce the graph [PAGE 9–10]. A claim that full MCNF can be solved directly at scale is vetoed by the paper’s statement that the graph is too large and “cannot be solved anymore using the efficient K-Shortest Paths algorithm” [PAGE 9].

Optimization-layer vetoes are proven. Eq. 2 is exact under total unimodularity [PAGE 6]. Eq. 4 is not totally unimodular [PAGE 8], so fractional solutions are possible, “typically when two or more targets are moving so close to each other that appearance information is unable to disambiguate their respective identities” [PAGE 8]. Rounding is used in experiments [PAGE 8]. A claim that MCNF always returns integral identity assignments is vetoed by this source.

Biomechanical-layer adjudication: the only relevant phrase is that basketball players “tend to perform large amplitude motions” [PAGE 12]. That is a detector-training rationale, not a biomechanical model. It provides no force, torque, velocity, acceleration, fatigue, or injury variable. A biomechanical veto cannot be derived from it. Weather-layer adjudication: the source contains no weather measurement or weather equation. Illumination and specular reflection are not weather; they are image-formation conditions [PAGE 18]. A weather veto is therefore not merely weak in this source; it is absent. Physical-layer adjudication: occupancy, flow, and appearance are the only physical-world proxies. They are about people’s locations and identities, not about physical performance. Any claim that this paper proves a physical-performance veto is unsupported by [PAGE 1–22].

The cross-layer verdict is therefore: source-layer vetoes from occlusion, crowding, low resolution, camera motion, illumination, graph constraints, and LP integrality are admissible. Physical, biomechanical, and weather vetoes are not admissible from this PDF. If an external layer is introduced, it must be supplied with its own equations, units, and validation data; it cannot borrow authority from this tracking paper.
