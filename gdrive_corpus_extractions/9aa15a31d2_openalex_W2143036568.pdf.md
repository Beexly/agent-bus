<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The retrocausal lineage runs from 1990s–2000s compressed-domain video analysis into modern multimodal event spotting. The paper is an ancestor of “cheap feature, temporal window, classifier” pipelines: MPEG motion vectors, DCT coefficient counts, audio scalefactors, static graphic tracking, Hough geometry, and an SVM. It explicitly notes an alternative temporal model: “Alternative classification schemes such as hidden Markov models are likely to be more appropriate in video indexing applications, where continuous knowledge of past and present states is desired” [PAGE 7]. Read forward, that sentence anticipates sequence models and temporal event detection; read backward, the SVM is a static per-shot classifier sitting on hand-designed temporal maxima. The paper’s own future work asks for feature-contribution analysis and threshold-selection analysis [PAGE 8], which modern ablation and calibration studies would supply.

The first multi-week trap is threshold drift. The close-up threshold \(0.1\), the motion “zero” threshold \(50\), the \(5^\circ\)–\(25^\circ\) marking range, and the 25-s window are all tuned to a 2005 multi-genre corpus. Over multiple weeks or seasons, broadcaster graphics change, camera rigs change, field colors change, and production styles change. A threshold that was stable in the training corpus can become a systematic veto on true events. The paper already flags one such fragility: replay detection based on slow-motion frame repetition is “vulnerable to breakdown” with “the onset of high-speed camera technology” [PAGE 2]. The same logic applies to all hard-coded production cues.

The second trap is annotation and event-definition drift. The paper’s “event” is a successful scoring incident [PAGE 2], not every highlight, foul, injury, substitution, or momentum shift. A system trained on scoring incidents can be evaluated on a different target and appear to fail or succeed for the wrong reason. Ground truth is manually annotated [PAGE 2], but annotation conventions can drift across leagues and broadcasters. The third trap is broadcast-style confounding: the paper attributes genre differences partly to pace, noting “the faster paced games, i.e., soccer and hockey, which outperformed the more slowly paced games of Gaelic football and rugby” [PAGE 8]. Slow games contain more contextual content—close-ups, crowd shots, replays, dissolves—so the feature prevalence changes even when the event definition is fixed.

The fourth trap is benchmark leakage and ceiling effects. The preprocessor rejects content before classification, and reported maxima are limited by that rejection [PAGE 7]. If a later evaluation changes the preprocessor or the advertisement detector, the ERR/CRR comparison is no longer like-for-like. The fifth trap is interpretability overreach: because the paper did not report individual feature contributions [PAGE 8], a later reader cannot assume that crowd detection or audio energy is the decisive cue. The sixth trap is temporal nonstationarity across weeks: a detector tuned to “reaction-phase” assumes a director’s habit that may regress under remote production, neutral crowd sound, or changed replay policy. The genealogy therefore is not a simple progress line; it is a chain of assumptions that must be revalidated whenever the broadcast-production regime changes.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The paper’s feature design embeds an implicit recursive theory of mind. The detector models a director who models an audience: the director expects the audience to want the players’ and crowd’s reaction, so the director cuts to a close-up or crowd shot after a score. The paper states that “the director utilizes this reaction-phase to capture the responses of players and/or crowds to the significance of the event” [PAGE 2]. The detector also models a commentator who raises voice-band energy when excited, a crowd that cheers, a scoreboard operator who updates the graphic, and a camera operator who follows the scorer. These are agent models, not physical measurements. The SVM has no explicit mental-state variables, but its feature vector is a compressed representation of those production agents’ habits.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the production regime is stressed and the agents fall back to generic habits. If a director has no replay, no close-up, or a delayed cut, the detector’s close-up retention rule may reject the shot before classification. If a commentator is absent, remote, or speaking a different language, the speech-band feature loses its intended meaning. If the crowd is neutral or absent, the crowd-image detector still detects texture, but the texture no longer carries the same causal relation to scoring. The detector regresses toward its training prior: it treats the presence of a familiar production pattern as evidence, even when the underlying event is absent or when the pattern is present for a non-event reason.

The counter-trap is the inverse: a non-event can generate event-like production behavior. An injury, substitution, controversial call, crowd wave, or sponsor segment can produce close-ups, crowd shots, audio energy, graphic changes, and motion within the 25-s window. Because the features are maxima within the window, any unrelated spike inside that clock can contribute. The counter-trap is especially dangerous when the director deliberately creates suspense or when the broadcaster uses the same close-up grammar for non-scoring moments. The system has no explicit “why” variable to distinguish celebration from concern, so the recursive agent model is underdetermined.

The clock is the 25-s critical-seek-window, with mode 18 s and 16% of events at that mode [PAGE 2]. The clock is both a causal assumption and a failure surface. If the reaction is early, late, or split across a commercial break, the maxima may fall outside the window. If the scoreboard update is delayed beyond the window, the graphic feature is lost. If the director cuts to a replay before the close-up, the preprocessor may or may not retain the shot. Thus the recursive theory of mind must be time-indexed: the detector is not asking “is this an event?” in isolation; it is asking “did the production agents behave as if an event just occurred, within the clock?” When that question is answered under a different production culture, habit regression and counter-traps dominate.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Cross-layer veto adjudication treats a feature as valid evidence only if its necessary physical, biomechanical, acoustic, and broadcast-layer assumptions hold. Let \(C_i(s)\in[0,1]\) be the confidence from feature \(i\), and \(V_i(s)\in\{0,1\}\) be a veto indicator for that feature. The adjudicated evidence is \(C_i(s)(1-V_i(s))\). A veto does not prove that no event occurred; it proves that the feature cannot be used as positive evidence in that regime.

Physical and optical veto for close-up and field-marking features: the close-up model requires a face in Region-1, a monochromatic jersey in Region-2, and background occlusion in Region-3 [PAGE 3]. The field-marking model requires bright markings separated from a darker field after luminance binarization and Hough extraction [PAGE 6]. If illumination is extreme, if rain or snow lowers contrast, if the field is not grass-like, or if the markings are not white, the luminance distributions of marking and field can overlap. Then the binarization threshold—equivalent to the mode greyscale of field pixels—cannot separate the two classes. The Hough input loses the marking, and \(F_5\) becomes zero or unstable. This is a physical veto: the operator’s necessary contrast condition is violated, so a low \(F_5\) is not evidence against an event.

Biomechanical and camera veto for motion activity: \(F_4\) counts macroblocks whose motion-vector length exceeds a “zero” threshold [PAGE 5]. The observed motion field is a superposition of camera motion, player motion, crowd motion, and compression noise. If the camera pans or zooms during a close-up, camera-induced vectors can exceed the threshold and inflate \(F_4\) without any celebratory player biomechanics. Conversely, a static wide shot of a distant scorer can have low near-field motion even during a genuine event. The motion feature therefore cannot identify player biomechanics unless camera motion is separated or the shot type is constrained. This is a biomechanical veto: the measured quantity is not the intended quantity.

Acoustic veto for speech-band activity: \(F_2\) is a scalefactor-energy proxy for speech-band activity in subbands 2–7 over 0.5-s intervals [PAGE 4]. If wind, crowd noise, music, public address, or a different audio mix dominates the speech band, a peak in \(F_2\) is not commentator enthusiasm. If there is no commentator, the feature is systematically absent even for a true event. The necessary assumption is that commentator vocal energy is a separable component of the broadcast audio. When that assumption fails, \(F_2\) is vetoed as event evidence.

Graphic-layer veto: \(F_3\) requires a static scoreboard graphic with a temporal mode luminance and a temporary disappearance during scoreboard update [PAGE 5]. If the graphic is permanent, absent, animated, or reformatted, the mode SAD is not a valid absence signal. The paper’s own prevalence figure is only 61% for temporary removal [PAGE 2], so even in the training regime the feature is conditional. A permanent graphic vetoes \(F_3\) without implying that no score occurred.

Adjudication rule: if a feature has a layer veto, its confidence is set to zero for that shot. If multiple independent layers veto the same positive decision, the event hypothesis is rejected unless at least one non-vetoed layer carries strong evidence. If the close-up preprocessor vetoes the shot before SVM classification, no downstream adjudication can recover it. This is why the preprocessor limit is a hard ceiling: a close-up retention failure is not a soft feature disagreement; it is a gate.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

The reward vector must be measured, not asserted: \(\mathbf{R}=(\mathrm{ERR},\mathrm{CRR},\mathrm{retention},\mathrm{feature\ validity})\), with ERR and CRR in \(\%\), retention dimensionless, and feature validity a binary or proportion score. The three counterfactual vetoes below are do-interventions on the source pipeline. They are verifiable because each has a measurable outcome in a replayable corpus.
