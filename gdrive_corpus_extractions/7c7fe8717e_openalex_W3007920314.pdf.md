Biomechanical layer veto: The sit-and-reach test shows better flexibility associated with marginally higher injury-risk classification [PAGE 14], explained by the scarcity of non-contact muscle strains in this youth sample [PAGE 14]. Veto condition: if this model is ported to adult or senior elite football, where lack of flexibility is an established risk factor for muscle injury [PAGE 21 ref 35, Witvrouw et al. 2003], the sit-and-reach coefficient must be inverted or retrained; applying the youth-derived sign convention to adults would invert the flexibility risk signal and produce systematic misclassification. Additionally, CMJ and SBJ are best-of-three attempts [PAGE 9]; if a player performs sub-maximally due to minor pain not yet classified as injury, the power features are downward-biased, distorting the SHAP contribution of strength variables.

Weather/exposure layer veto: The YoYo IR1 was conducted on artificial turf in dry weather per Krustrup et al. guidelines [PAGE 9]. Veto condition: any endurance assessment performed on natural grass, in wet/cold conditions, or indoors violates the protocol under which the feature was validated; the YoYo distance feature should be dropped or imputed before inference, because cardiorespiratory endurance is among the top 20 variables [PAGE 12] and its distribution shift would degrade the 85% recall. Separately, individual match/training exposure was recorded by coaches via monitoring systems or Excel sheets [PAGE 10]; if a team fails to log exposure hours consistently, the injury incidence denominator (129,206 h) is unreliable, vetoing any incidence-rate normalisation of model outputs.
