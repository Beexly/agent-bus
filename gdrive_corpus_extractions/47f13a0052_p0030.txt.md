Verifiable prediction: tracked-shot coverage falls, but the conditional distribution of emitted xG values remains calibrated because no hallucinated geometry enters f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}. If the veto is disabled, the system will impute positions and produce overconfident xG estimates with elevated Brier score on occluded subsets.

Scenario 2 (Biomechanical impossibility veto). Counterfactual: computed ball speed from successive tracking frames is v_{ball}=45 m/s, above the empirical elite soccer striking ceiling (~36 m/s). Veto rule: if \|\mathbf{v}_{b}\|_2 > v_{\max} or \|\mathbf{a}_{i}\|_2 > a_{\max} (human acceleration limit), the input tuple is flagged as sensor error and excluded from the training/inference batch. Verifiable prediction: the model never outputs xG > 1 or negative probabilities caused by physically impossible feature combinations; disabling the veto produces spurious high-xG predictions on outlier-speed frames and degrades precision at high recall thresholds.

Scenario 3 (Penalty and own-goal domain veto). Counterfactual: the action type is a penalty kick or an own goal. Veto rule: if s=\text{penalty}, bypass f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} and assign xG=0.7884; if s=\text{own goal}, exclude from model output entirely [PAGE 12]. Verifiable prediction: penalty xG is constant across all goalkeeper positioning contexts, preventing overfitting to rare penalty-specific defender-pressure features; if the veto is removed and penalties are fed into the general model, the constant 0.7884 calibration is lost and penalty predictions become sensitive to noisy, low-sample positional features, increasing variance without improving accuracy.
