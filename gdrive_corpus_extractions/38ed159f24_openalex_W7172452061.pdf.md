<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the interaction as a recursive theory-of-mind stack. Level 0: the user holds an intent \( I_u \) (e.g., “identify the passer on the scoring play”). Level 1: the system’s ambiguity handler produces an interpretation \( I_s \) via GPT-4o over Whisper transcription and match context [PAGE 6]. Level 2: the user constructs a model \( \hat{I}_s \) of the system’s interpretation from externalized cues (badges, zone overlays, timeline thumbnails, scope panels) [PAGE 5-6]. Level 3: the system anticipates \( \hat{I}_s \) and adjusts its next response based on repair language detected through the keyword classifier [PAGE 8].

Crisis habit regression enters because XR sports viewing is fast-paced and cognitively demanding: viewers track multiple players, evolving play, and shifting attention, leaving limited capacity to formulate precise queries [PAGE 1]. Under temporal pressure—clips loop every 15–30s and live play never pauses—users regress to habitual acceptance of the system’s first output rather than constructing costly verbal repairs. The paper confirms this at the behavioral level: confirmation time did not increase under externalization (\( \approx 25.8\text{s} \) vs. \( 25.9\text{s} \), NS), indicating users did not spend more time evaluating responses [PAGE 14, Table 10].

The counter-trap is anchoring to visible system outputs. When externalization surfaces a wrong but plausible referent (e.g., a badge on the wrong passer), users may treat the visual commitment as authoritative rather than erroneous, consistent with Nourani et al. [50] on anchoring bias in XAI [PAGE 8]. The referential misaligned repair rate was numerically lower under externalization (9.9%) than baseline (11.2%), though not significantly so (\( p = .611 \)) [PAGE 13, Table 9], suggesting externalization can entrench incorrect interpretations when the verbal repair cost is high.

The clock is the real-time match timeline. System latency averages 2.77s [PAGE 6]; event data arrive at 25Hz [PAGE 6]; and the user must decide whether to repair before the next play unfolds. The recursive loop closes only when Level 2 verification translates into Level 0 repair. The 62% visibility-action gap [PAGE 9] is precisely the failure of this closure: users reach Level 2 (they see the assumption) but do not emit Level 0 repair utterances because speech-only correction imposes a construction cost externalization alone cannot remove [PAGE 8, Section 8.2]. A working system must therefore collapse Levels 2 and 0 through direct manipulation—tapping a candidate badge, scrubbing a timeline, toggling a metric scope—so the recursive loop completes within the clock of live play.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer. The system runs on Unity 6000.2.6f2 deployed on Meta Quest 3 [PAGE 6]. Prior work cited in the paper establishes that mid-air text entry is slow and error-prone [26,28] and sustained gestural interaction causes physical fatigue [21,24] [PAGE 1]. The system mitigates this with push-to-talk (controller “B” button) rather than continuous listening, trading a biomechanical initiation cost for utterance segmentation reliability and lower latency [PAGE 4, PAGE 6].

Biomechanical layer. Repair requires precise verbal specification under time pressure. The paper identifies this as the binding constraint: externalization raised inspectability but did not reliably increase correction because users must reformulate queries verbally [PAGE 8-9]. The biomechanical veto is therefore any correction pathway that demands novel, multi-word verbal construction under a 2.77s system latency plus ongoing match clock [PAGE 6]. Direct manipulation (tap candidate, scrub timeline, toggle scope) shortens this path; speech-only repair does not [PAGE 10].

Weather/environmental layer. The source contains no meteorological variables because all data are pre-recorded Bundesliga match reconstructions viewed in a controlled lab setting [PAGE 7, Section 8.2]. Consequently, no weather veto can be derived from this paper alone. However, the controlled setting itself is an environmental limitation: live broadcast viewing would introduce variable acoustics, crowd noise, lighting, network jitter, and uninterrupted play clocks absent from the 15–30s looped clips [PAGE 7]. A veto condition for deployment is any environment where the \( M = 2.77\text{s} \) latency or the verbal repair cost cannot be absorbed within the inter-play interval.

Veto proof. A configuration is vetoed if it (a) relies on voice-only correction as the sole repair channel, (b) assumes transparency implies correction without measuring the visibility-action gap, or (c) deploys in live contexts without validating latency and environmental robustness beyond pre-recorded clips. The paper’s null findings for trust and clutter (\( p > .05 \)) indicate externalization does not create an attentional burden, but also does not build generalized trust [PAGE 8, PAGE 14], so trust cannot be leveraged to override the correction-cost veto.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Veto Scenario 1 — Voice-only production default. Counterfactual: deploy the XR sports system without visual externalization, using the voice-only baseline as the default interface. RLVR veto signal: the study found externalization significantly increased inspectability on three of four items and raised repair utterance rate from 3.3% to 5.6% (\( p = .039, r = .57 \)) [PAGE 8]. Prediction: in a live A/B deployment, the voice-only condition will leave the 62% visibility-action gap entirely unmitigated; users will observe incorrect interpretations but lack cues to inspect them, producing near-zero explicit repair for misaligned referential, temporal, and metric queries. Verifiable metric: repair utterance rate per 100 queries; expect baseline \(\leq\) 4% and externalization \(\geq\) 5.5% with \( p < .05 \) under matched traffic.

Veto Scenario 2 — Externalization without multimodal correction channels. Counterfactual: implement situated visual cues (badges, overlays, timelines) but route all corrections exclusively through speech, prohibiting tap, scrub, or toggle affordances. RLVR veto signal: the paper’s central finding is that transparency and correction affordance are orthogonal axes; repair occurred in only 38% of misaligned externalization trials, a 62% gap, because precise verbal reformulation is costly [PAGE 9]. Prediction: users will reach high inspectability ratings (approximately \( M \geq 5.9 \) on player-referent and moment items) but repair behavior will remain flat; temporal and metric misaligned trials will show large effect sizes (\( r \approx .64 \) and \( r \approx .91 \)) that fail significance only due to sample size, not absence of effect [PAGE 13, Table 9]. Verifiable metric: ratio of noticed-to-acted mismatches; predict \( > 0.6 \) mismatches noticed but unrepaired under speech-only correction.

Veto Scenario 3 — Non-deterministic LLM interpretation without within-session consistency checks. Counterfactual: allow GPT-4o to generate externalizations with default sampling (non-deterministic decoding) rather than the constrained JSON schema used in the study. RLVR veto signal: the paper explicitly states that within-session consistency of the full interpretation pipeline was not measured, and LLM variability could make identical queries produce different externalized commitments across repetitions [PAGE 9, Section 8.2]. Grounding theory requires stable commitments for ratification [12] [PAGE 2]. Prediction: repeated identical queries will yield divergent candidate badges, zone rankings, or timeline selections; users will fail to build a stable model \( \hat{I}_s \), causing inspectability ratings to collapse toward baseline and trust to decrease over sessions. Verifiable metric: candidate-ID consistency across 100 repetitions of the same query under identical match state; predict \( < 100\% \) consistency, violating the grounding assumption of a fixed externalized commitment.
