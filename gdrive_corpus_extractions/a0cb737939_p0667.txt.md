The first exclusive axiom concerns marks of identity: "Straight lines which have two points, planes which have three points, parallels to a given line which have one point, in common, coalesce throughout" [PAGE 657]. James explicitly rejects two standard explanations for our certainty in these axioms. It is neither repeated experience of their truth nor intuitive acquaintance with the properties of space [PAGE 657]. His alternative is constructive and faculty-based: we possess a definite idea of each term, and when something different is offered, we see the difference. Geometric straight lines, planes, and parallels are "mere inventions of our faculty for apprehending serial increase" [PAGE 657].

The derivation proceeds in hops. Hop 1: empirical lines are heterogeneous; we experience lines passing through two points that separate again, but we refuse to call them straight [PAGE 657]. Hop 2: the term "straight" is therefore a normative filter, not an empirical generalization. Hop 3: the faculty for serial increase stipulates that the farther continuations of these forms bear the same relation to their last visible parts as those parts did to still earlier parts. Hop 4: from the "axiom of skipped intermediaries which obtains in all regular series," parts separated by other parts must agree in direction just as contiguous parts do [PAGE 657]. Hop 5: uniformity of direction throughout is the sole property that confers beauty, fixed conception, and cognitive salience on these forms.

Gray-zone audit: The boundary between an empirical line and an ideal straight line is not drawn by observation but by our refusal to extend the label. The text states the breakdown condition with precision: "if two lines, or two planes, with a common segment, were to part company beyond the segment, it could only be because the direction of at least one of them had changed" [PAGE 657]. This is the singularity regime: divergence beyond a common segment is analytically equivalent to a change in direction. It does not falsify the axiom; it falsifies the classification of one object as straight. The gray zone lies in the psychophysical transition where a line is straight up to measurement resolution but curves beyond it; James's stipulation says we will not call it straight, yet our perceptual system may initially register it as such. A second gray zone concerns the cross-reference chain: if the axioms of number are contingent on numerability and logic on forms resembling and differing, then the geometric axioms here inherit a second-order status—necessary only within the closed system of the invented form. Whether that necessity is psychological (we cannot think otherwise) or ontological (the form cannot be otherwise) is left unstable, though James locates its source in the faculty, rendering it psychologically necessary but historically contingent. A third gray zone is intersubjective: the phrase "we won't call them straight" implies a community of language users enforcing the label, not an isolated intuition.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(\mathcal{L}\) denote the class of ideal straight lines, \(\mathcal{P}\) the class of planes, and \(\mathcal{G}\) the class of parallels to a fixed line \(g\). Define the intersection-cardinality operator \(\kappa(x,y) = |x \cap y|\). The identity/coalescence axioms stated on [PAGE 657] are:

\[
\forall l_1,l_2 \in \mathcal{L}: \quad \kappa(l_1,l_2) \ge 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; l_1 \equiv l_2 ,
\]
\[
\forall p_1,p_2 \in \mathcal{P}: \quad \kappa(p_1,p_2) \ge 3 \;\Longrightarrow\; p_1 \equiv p_2 ,
\]
\[
\forall q_1,q_2 \in \mathcal{G}: \quad \kappa(q_1,q_2) \ge 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; q_1 \equiv q_2 .
\]

These formalize the verbatim claim: "Straight lines which have two points, planes which have three points, parallels to a given line which have one point, in common, coalesce throughout" [PAGE 657].

For the serial structure, let \(S = (s_i)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\) be a regular series of spatial parts, and let the direction field be \(D(s_i) \in \mathbb{S}^2\) for oriented lines or \(D(s_i) \in \mathbb{RP}^2\) for unoriented axes. Direction is measured in radians (rad), a dimensionless SI unit; projective direction is dimensionless. The axiom of skipped intermediaries, which James says "obtains in all regular series" [PAGE 657], is rendered as:

\[
\forall i<j: \quad D(s_i) = D(s_j)
\quad \text{whenever} \quad
D(s_k) = D(s_{k+1}) \;\; \forall k \in [i,\,j-1] .
\]

Equivalently, local directional uniformity between contiguous parts implies global directional uniformity between non-contiguous parts separated by intermediaries. James identifies the consequence verbatim: "parts of these figures separated by other parts must agree in direction, just as contiguous parts do" [PAGE 657].

The exact breakdown or singularity regime is specified by James: "if two lines, or two planes, with a common segment, were to part company beyond the segment, it could only be because the direction of at least one of them had changed" [PAGE 657]. Formally, let \(\sigma = l_1 \cap l_2\) be a common segment. If beyond \(\sigma\) the entities part such that \(l_1 \cap l_2 = \sigma\) only, then

\[
\exists m \in \{l_1,l_2\}: \quad D(m|_{\sigma}) \neq D(m|_{\text{beyond }\sigma}) .
\]

Parting company is analytically equivalent to directional discontinuity; at least one entity ceases to belong to \(\mathcal{L}\) or \(\mathcal{P}\).

Units and assumptions: The stipulation that an empirical line is "not straight" carries an information cost \(H_{\text{stip}}\) measured in bits (Shannon). The probability of misclassifying a curved empirical segment as straight under repeated observation is \(P_{\text{err}} \in [0,1]\), dimensionless. The verbatim basis for this refusal is: "We experience lines enough which pass through two points only to separate again, only we won't call them straight" [PAGE 657]. The faculty-based conclusion is: "Straight lines, planes, and parallels, as they figure in geometry, are mere inventions of our faculty for apprehending serial increase" [PAGE 657]. James's rejection of the two rival accounts is: "It is neither" [PAGE 657], referring to repeated experience and intuitive acquaintance. Antecedent cross-citations are [PAGE 645] (axiom of constant results; axioms of mediate comparison), [PAGE 648] (axioms of logic), and [PAGE 654] (axioms of number).

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The historical-scientific lineage of p. 657 runs through three major strata. First, Euclidean geometry: the identity marks James lists are restatements of Euclidean incidence axioms—through any two points there passes a unique straight line, through any three non-collinear points a unique plane, and the uniqueness of parallels. Second, Kantian transcendental idealism: in the *Critique of Pure Reason*, space is a pure form of sensible intuition and geometric axioms are synthetic a priori. James explicitly rejects the "intuitive acquaintance" horn of the dilemma [PAGE 657], thereby psychologizing Kant's a priori into a faculty for apprehending serial increase rather than a pure form of intuition. Third, the Helmholtzian tradition of the 1860s–70s, which questioned whether empirical space must be Euclidean and treated geometric axioms as empirically revisable conventions. James's observation that we experience lines passing through two points only to separate again [PAGE 657] anticipates Helmholtz's point that experience alone cannot secure ideal straightness.

The multi-week traps embedded in this lineage are specific. Trap A (reification): in a longitudinal study of spatial cognition, a researcher may treat a participant's stable verbal assent to "straight lines coalesce" across weeks as evidence of an innate geometric module, when the stability may merely reflect a stable linguistic habit enforced by repeated testing. Trap B (stationarity of serial apprehension): assuming the axiom of skipped intermediaries holds across weekly sessions. If the participant's faculty for serial apprehension drifts due to learning, fatigue, or strategy change between weeks, the uniformity of direction may fail between sessions even when within-session performance is stable. Trap C (conflation of necessities): confusing psychological necessity—"we cannot conceive of two straight lines parting after a common segment"—with logical necessity, leading to false claims that spatial judgments are invariant over multi-week developmental intervals. James's position blocks Trap C by locating necessity in the faculty, which is historically contingent and therefore not guaranteed to remain fixed across weeks of testing.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates as follows: when a subject faces high cognitive load, time pressure, or contradictory perceptual evidence, they regress from the ideal geometric stipulation to an empirical segment-based classification. The ideal "straight line" collapses into a sequence of locally straight segments; the subject applies the criterion "it separated after two points, so I won't call it straight" [PAGE 657] rather than the idealized identity axiom.

The counter-trap is for the observer-theorist to misread this regression as irrationality or failure to grasp the axiom. The correct recursive reading is that the subject is rationally applying the empirical criterion while the theorist insists on the idealized criterion. Each party models the other: the subject models the theorist's expectation of geometric necessity; the theorist models the subject's supposed regression. Under crisis, both fixate on different layers—ideal versus empirical—producing a recursive misalignment in which neither recognizes that the disagreement is about layer jurisdiction, not about logical competence.

The clock is the temporal substrate of the faculty for apprehending serial increase. Each serial step is a tick; the relation between non-contiguous parts depends on the continuity of that clock. If the clock is desynchronized by divided attention, interleaved distractors, or an inter-stimulus gap exceeding neural persistence, the axiom of skipped intermediaries fails phenomenologically: separated parts are judged to disagree in direction even when contiguous parts agree. The subject then experiences two segments rather than one line, and the uniformity of direction [PAGE 657] is broken not by a change in the object but by a change in the apprehension rhythm.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The geometric identity axiom is not a pure psychological given; it is a cross-layer stable state. Three layers can veto it.

Layer 1—Physical/Neural: Orientation-tuned neurons in primary visual cortex exhibit tuning width \(\Delta\theta_{\text{neural}}\), measured in radians. Two lines with angular separation \(\delta\theta < \Delta\theta_{\text{neural}}\) cannot be resolved as distinct directions. Veto condition: if empirical lines differ by less than \(\Delta\theta_{\text{neural}}\), the neural layer enforces a "same direction" percept, blocking the subject from applying James's distinction between straight and non-straight lines.

Layer 2—Biomechanical: Oculomotor pursuit or manual tracing introduces path-length error \(\epsilon_{\text{trace}}\) in meters over trace length \(L\) in meters. Under the small-angle approximation, angular error is \(\delta_{\text{bio}} \approx \epsilon_{\text{trace}}/L\) radians. If \(\delta_{\text{bio}}\) exceeds the just-noticeable difference for collinearity, the biomechanical layer reports parting company even when the intended mark was a single straight line.

Layer 3—Environmental/weather-analog noise: Ambient luminance contrast \(C\), scattering, and surface texture contribute additive noise \(n(t)\) to edge position. Define signal-to-noise ratio \(\mathrm{SNR} = \sigma_{\text{signal}}^{2}/\sigma_{n}^{2}\), dimensionless. If \(\mathrm{SNR} < \tau_{\text{env}}\), the edge becomes discontinuous and the serial apprehension faculty receives broken inputs, preventing the formation of a single serial object.

Adjudication proof: The geometric identity axiom is behaviorally expressed only if all three conditions hold simultaneously: (1) \(\delta\theta \ge \Delta\theta_{\text{neural}}\) (neural discriminability); (2) \(\delta_{\text{bio}} < \mathrm{JND}_{\text{collinear}}\) (biomechanical trace stability); (3) \(\mathrm{SNR} \ge \tau_{\text{env}}\) (environmental integrity). Failure of any layer vetoes the axiom: the subject either merges distinct lines (physical veto), splits one line into two (biomechanical veto), or fails to constitute a serial object at all (environmental veto). The "straight line" is therefore an engineered cross-layer alignment, not an immediate deliverance of intuition or experience [PAGE 657].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
