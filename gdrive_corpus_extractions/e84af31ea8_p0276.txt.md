The mathematical elegance of Figure 60 rests upon a lineage stretching back to Edward Thorndike’s Law of Effect (1898) but diverging sharply into Clark Hull’s rigid algebraic drive-reductionism. Hull sought to purify psychology of "mentalism," demanding that every behavioral quirk be expressed as a differential equation of habit strength ($sEr$) and conditioned reflexes ($sFr$). The 1943 text explicitly credits Kenneth Spence (footnote 1, [PAGE 276]) for refining this approach. Spence introduced the critical concept that secondary drives (frustration generated by blocked responses) themselves obey generalization gradients.

It framed learning not as a sudden realization or "insight" (rejecting Köhler’s Gestalt psychology), but as a slow, calculable accumulation of scalar vectors.

Modern systems neuroscience has proven that repeated exposure to discriminative boundaries causes cortical remapping (e.g., in auditory cortex during frequency discrimination tasks), dynamically shrinking $\sigma_g$ and expanding the margin of separation. The 1943 gradient model traps the learner in a fixed perceptual space, whereas biological brains continuously reshape the metric space itself to maximize classification margins—a feat the Hullian arithmetic cannot natively account for.
