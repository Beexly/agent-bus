The pipeline contains a recursive theory of mind: it models the broadcaster's camera choices, the spectator's presence in frame, and its own prior errors. Under crisis conditions—high occlusion, rapid camera pans, or missing limbs—the denoiser regresses to a statistical habit: replacing abnormal limb or torso length ratios with a "normal length ratio" [PAGE 19]. This is a crisis habit regression: when sensory evidence is weak, the system defaults to the population mean rather than preserving outlier but valid athletic poses (e.g., a fully extended dunk or a contorted defensive slide). The regression error is not random; it is biased toward anthropometric averages.

The counter-trap arises from the refiner's spectator filtering and the denoiser's correction. If the broadcast camera frames spectators adjacent to the court, refiner 120 must distinguish spectator body poses from player body poses [PAGE 19]. A spectator raising arms during a celebration can resemble a player in a shooting pose. If the refiner over-filters, it removes real players; if it under-filters, spectators enter the game file 124 and contaminate downstream 3D projection [PAGE 19]. Adversarially, consistent camera angles that repeatedly include spectators in predictable locations can train the refiner to treat those image regions as spectator-only, so that a player entering that same region (e.g., diving into the stands) is systematically filtered out—a trap where the system's model of "what is a spectator" causes it to erase the player precisely when the play is most dramatic.

The clock is the deployment/training boundary. Phase 202 training occurs offline on labeled and unlabeled data [PAGE 19; FIG. 2], while phase 204 deployment must process raw body pose data 210 in real time from a live broadcast [PAGE 19]. Broadcast latency, frame rate, and camera cuts impose a hard wall-clock constraint: the 3D projector 132 must deliver court-space poses before the broadcast moves to the next play or graphic [PAGE 19]. The denoiser-refiner interchangeability [PAGE 19] introduces a recursive loop: the system's belief about a player's location influences how it denoises the pose, and the denoised pose reinforces the location belief. Without a fixed correction order (because the modules are interchangeable), this loop has no guaranteed fixed point; under clock pressure, the system may output the first convergent estimate rather than the most accurate one.
