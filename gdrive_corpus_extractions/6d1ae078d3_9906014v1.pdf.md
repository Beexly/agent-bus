The source contains formal-sounding physics but few explicit equations. I reconstruct only operators explicitly licensed by the text and mark every assumption and breakdown regime.

1. Operational measurement premise. Noyes treats agreed quantitative measurement as primary and mathematical language as secondary [PAGE 2]. In formal terms, an observable \(O\) has recorded value \(o_i\) in units proper to \(O\), with an associated protocol \( \mathcal{P} \) specifying apparatus, calibration, blinding, and error budget. This is not a numerical equation; it is a validity condition. Breakdown occurs when protocol is not mutually agreed or when the error budget is unspecified. The source’s criterion is that systematic error must be shown to vary with experimental conditions and brought below statistical error [PAGE 4].

2. Born rule. Noyes cites “Born’s probability rule that gives probabilities as the squares of ‘amplitudes’” [PAGE 8]. Formally,
\[
P(a)=\left|\langle a|\psi\rangle\right|^2,\qquad \sum_a P(a)=1,
\]
where \(P(a)\) is dimensionless probability, \(|\psi\rangle\) is a normalized Hilbert-space state, and \(\langle a|\) is a measurement basis. Assumptions: projective measurement, normalized state, and a specified basis. Units: probability dimensionless; amplitude dimensionless. Breakdown: the rule gives probabilities, not a dynamical explanation of measurement; open-system decoherence and apparatus models are required for actual measurement chains. The source does not supply those for paranormal claims.

3. Unitary evolution. Noyes cites “the unitary evolution of the quantum state called Schroedinger’s equation” [PAGE 8]. Formally,
\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\psi(t)\rangle=\hat H|\psi(t)\rangle,
\]
with \(\hbar=1.054571817\ldots\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\), \(\hat H\) in joules, and \(t\) in seconds. Assumptions: closed quantum system, Hermitian Hamiltonian, reversible evolution. Breakdown: measurement, decoherence, and open-system dynamics are not described by this closed unitary alone. The source uses this only to contrast time-reversible quantum evolution with irreversible Markov chains [PAGE 8].

4. Markov-chain boundary-condition formalism. Noyes says Etter formalizes “Markov chains (which are irreversible) with either past or future boundary conditions” [PAGE 8]. A standard finite Markov chain has
\[
P(x_{0:N})=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}T_{i+1,i}(x_{i+1}\mid x_i),
\]
where \(P\) and transition kernels \(T\) are dimensionless. A future-boundary-conditioned version is
\[
P(x_{0:N}\mid x_N)=\frac{P(x_{0:N},x_N)}{P(x_N)},
\]
with \(P(x_N)>0\). Assumptions: Markov property, finite or well-defined state space, and a specified boundary condition. Units: dimensionless probabilities. Breakdown: if hidden memory, non-Markovian noise, or undefined boundary conditions exist, the formalism is descriptive only. Noyes explicitly says “at this stage no new predictions are made” [PAGE 8]. It is “not tied to Planck’s constant” and only “provides for the possibility of macroscopic acausality” [PAGE 8].

5. Three-body nonlocality / reduced-state operator. Noyes states that with two quantum particles interacting via short-range forces, adding a third with similar interactions changes the pair’s behavior “no matter how far away the third particle is” [PAGE 5]. For total density operator \(\rho_{123}\), the pair reduced state is
\[
\rho_{12}=\mathrm{Tr}_{3}\,\rho_{123}.
\]
Here \(\rho\) is dimensionless and the trace is over particle 3’s Hilbert space. Assumptions: quantum coherence, finite-range interactions \(V_{13}\) and \(V_{23}\), and a specified global state. Breakdown: if the third particle is classically decoupled, if interactions vanish, or if the pair is already separable, no such reduced-state change is implied. Crucially, no-signalling is not violated: local operations on 3 cannot change the marginal \(\mathrm{Tr}_{12}\rho_{123}\) in a controllable signalling way. Noyes cites “supraluminal correlation without supraluminal signalling over distances of 20 kilometers” [PAGE 9].

6. Systematic-error model. Noyes demands that systematic error vary systematically with conditions and be reduced below statistical error [PAGE 4]. A minimal additive model is
\[
O_i = T_i + S(\mathbf{c}_i) + \epsilon_i,
\]
where \(O_i\) is the recorded observable in its own units, \(T_i\) is the target effect, \(S(\mathbf{c}_i)\) is a systematic function of condition vector \(\mathbf{c}_i\) in the same units, and \(\epsilon_i\) is random error in the same units. Assumptions: additivity and a complete condition vector. Breakdown: unknown confounders, non-additive interactions, and unmeasured “emotional state” invalidate separation. Noyes flags the “emotional state” of subject and experimenter as crying out for “quantitative assessment and investigation in this ﬁeld — perhaps an impossible task?” [PAGE 6].

7. No explicit paranormal operator. The source never gives a quantitative equation for telepathy, precognition, telekinesis, or macroscopic acausality. Noyes says quantum mechanics does not “explain” paranormal phenomena by such a route [PAGE 6], and Etter’s framework makes “no new predictions” [PAGE 8]. That absence is itself a formal veto condition: no calibrated operator, no units, no singularity analysis, no cross-layer mapping.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The genealogical chain runs from operational physics to historical sciences to a cautious analogy for paranormal reports. Noyes begins with the Galilean assumption that local processes repeat elsewhere until evidence says otherwise, the limiting velocity \(c\) for unimpeded light, and the Friedman-Robertson-Walker metric on large scales [PAGE 2]. He then extends to a 13-billion-year cosmic description [PAGE 3], while insisting that this picture refers only to measurable phenomena “here and now” and does not establish the deep “reality” of space and time [PAGE 3]. The ANPA/Program Universe lineage is explicit: the framework can be reached “from the combinatorial hierarchy construction via program universe” without postulating a priori spacetime [PAGE 3]. This is the retrocausal genealogy in the source: later measurement frameworks are read back into earlier cosmological and operational assumptions, but Noyes warns against reifying the resulting spacetime.

The immediate scientific lineage is Etter and Shoup’s new theoretical framework [PAGE 4, PAGE 7], Etter’s older web paper [PAGE 7, PAGE 9], Galison’s material culture of particle physics [PAGE 2, PAGE 10], Deacon on language/brain co-evolution [PAGE 3, PAGE 9], and Bastin’s proposed definition of the paranormal [PAGE 8]. The conference setting is ANPA 20, Wesley House, Cambridge, September 7–8, 1998, published as an appendix to the proceedings [PAGE 1]. The shaman anecdote is a 30-year-old secondhand report [PAGE 4–5]; Noyes uses it as a prompt for the eternal triangle analogy, not as proof. The historical trap is multi-week and institutional: the anthropologist delayed publication for twenty years “for fear it would damage his professional reputation” [PAGE 5]. That creates publication bias and retrospective selection. A second multi-week trap is theory drift: a descriptive future-boundary formalism can be stretched week after week to “explain” new anecdotes, while Noyes says it “does not really explain anything” [PAGE 6] and makes “no new predictions” [PAGE 8]. A third multi-week trap is conflating nonlocality with signalling; Noyes’s 20-kilometer example explicitly separates “supraluminal correlation” from “supraluminal signalling” [PAGE 9]. A fourth is treating Bastin’s “no dependence on space and time” as settled; Noyes rejects the premise that physics must begin with space and time [PAGE 8–9]. The correct lineage audit is therefore: operational measurement first, quantum analogy second, future-causation formalism third, and no evidential promotion without independent, pre-registered, systematic-error-controlled prediction.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive observer problem is central to Noyes’s shaman story. The anthropologist and shaman had “reached a mutual level of conﬁdence and trust” and could “share each other’s thoughts” to some extent [PAGE 5]. The anthropologist also knew his distant friend and might unconsciously anticipate what the friend was doing; the shaman might “pick up” that knowledge through non-verbal communication [PAGE 5–6]. This is nested modelling: A models B, B models A, and A models C through B. The source’s own warning is that this “framework” merely replaces one problem with another: how does the mind make such accurate unconscious deductions? [PAGE 6]. The recursive theory-of-mind audit has three modes.

First, crisis habit regression. Under an unresolved anomaly, a community may regress to prior habits: dismiss as chance, dismiss as fraud, or accept a facile explanation. Noyes criticizes “all too many ‘scientists’” who “grasp at facile explanations or rejections” [PAGE 7]. The regression is not a logical inference; it is a stress response to uncertainty. Noyes’s counter-virtue is “the true scientist lives with uncertainty as his constant companion” [PAGE 7]. In practice, crisis regression appears when a small statistical deviation is either overinterpreted as paranormal or underinterpreted as noise without a systematic-error theory. Both are habits, not adjudications.

Second, counter-trap. The counter-trap is to invoke unconscious inference, telepathy, precognition, or predictive telekinesis as an all-purpose explanation. Noyes says this is “only to replace one problem with another” [PAGE 6]. The trap is recursive because each new anecdote can be absorbed by widening the hypothesized unconscious computation. The only escape is to require a quantitative theory of the phenomenon and a quantitative theory of interference [PAGE 4]. Without that, the counter-trap is unfalsifiable and therefore not an adjudication.

Third, clock. The clock is the operational time axis: when was the protocol fixed, when were conditions randomized, when was the outcome recorded, and what past events are included? Noyes’s quantum claim requires “the inclusion (in principle) of all past events in the discussion of the current situation” [PAGE 7]. Etter’s formalism allows past or future boundary conditions [PAGE 8], but Noyes does not license retroactive editing of the evidence. The clock discipline is: pre-register conditions, timestamp observations, blind the analyst, and model emotional state as a time-varying covariate rather than a post hoc excuse. Noyes flags “emotional state” of both subject and experimenter as needing quantitative assessment, “perhaps an impossible task?” [PAGE 6]. The recursive audit therefore rejects both crisis regression and counter-trap, and retains only clock-governed, condition-controlled inference.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto proof by layer:

Physical layer. The source supplies Born-rule and unitary-evolution mathematics [PAGE 8], a three-body nonlocality analogy [PAGE 5], and a no-signalling caution [PAGE 9]. It does not supply a macroscopic acausality equation, a coupling constant, a boundary-condition estimator, or an experimental target. Noyes explicitly says Etter’s framework makes “no new predictions” [PAGE 8] and is only a “possibility” for macroscopic acausality. Therefore any physical-layer claim about a specific event is vetoed: there is no calibrated operator and no uncertainty budget. The source’s own standard requires a quantitative theory of the phenomenon and of systematic error [PAGE 4]; neither exists here for an event outcome.

Biomechanical layer. The source contains no force, torque, energy expenditure, muscle activation, gait, injury, fatigue, or kinematic operator. It contains no SI biomechanical units and no subject-specific measurements. The only bodily variable mentioned is “emotional state,” and Noyes says it “cries out for quantitative assessment” while possibly being an impossible task [PAGE 6]. That is an admission of missing measurement, not a biomechanical model. Any biomechanical inference is therefore vetoed.

Weather layer. The source contains no temperature, pressure, wind, precipitation, humidity, boundary-layer, or forecast operator. It contains no meteorological data and no time-series model. Any weather-layer inference is vetoed.

Cross-layer coupling. A valid cross-layer adjudication would require a mapping \(F:\) physical state \(\to\) biomechanical state \(\to\) weather state \(\to\) outcome, with units and calibration at each edge. The source provides none. Noyes says: “Having a theory is useless, except as an aid to imagination, until a way is found to fit experimental results into the theoretical framework” [PAGE 8]. That is the veto rule. The paper’s “explanatory framework” is analogical, not technological. It cannot adjudicate physical, biomechanical, or weather layers, and it cannot combine them. The cross-layer verdict is therefore a hard veto, not a probabilistic downgrade.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: Blinded systematic-error challenge. Prediction: if an anomalous statistical deviation is due to a condition-dependent systematic, then randomizing and stratifying by experimental conditions will make the effect track those conditions, and a blinded control will reduce the residual to the statistical-error band. Verifiable test: pre-register the condition vector \(\mathbf{c}_i\), blind the analyst to condition labels, and estimate \(S(\mathbf{c}_i)\) in the additive model \(O_i=T_i+S(\mathbf{c}_i)+\epsilon_i\). Veto condition: if the effect disappears or becomes condition-correlated after controls, the paranormal interpretation is vetoed. This follows Noyes’s demand to show systematic error varies with conditions and bring it below statistical error [PAGE 4].

Scenario 2: Eternal-triangle no-signalling test. Prediction: changing a distant third particle’s measurement basis or local operation can change tripartite correlations, but cannot change the pair’s marginal distribution in a controllable signalling way. Verifiable test: prepare the three-body finite-range interaction state, vary operations on particle 3 at separation, and compare the pair coincidence distribution and its marginal. Veto condition: a claimed superluminal message or controllable pair-marginal shift from a local operation on 3 is vetoed by the source’s own “supraluminal correlation without supraluminal signalling” constraint [PAGE 9] and the reduced-state structure of the eternal triangle effect [PAGE 5].

Scenario 3: Emotional-state covariate and future-boundary model. Prediction: if observer/subject “emotional state” is a confounder, then measuring it as a time-stamped covariate and blinding the analyst will reduce the residual anecdotal correlation; if a future-boundary Markov model is merely descriptive, it will produce no out-of-sample prediction beyond standard quantum/statistical predictions. Verifiable test: pre-register emotional-state instruments, timestamp all observations, fit the future-boundary model with no extra free parameter, and evaluate on held-out data. Veto condition: if the effect survives only when emotional state is unmeasured, or if the future-boundary model adds no out-of-sample prediction, the paranormal/future-causation claim is vetoed. This follows Noyes’s warning that the framework “does not really explain anything” [PAGE 6] and that “at this stage no new predictions are made” [PAGE 8].
