<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when an anomalous non-Fermi-liquid signature is automatically assigned to disorder because the unannealed sample is disordered. Temperature is a clock: \(T_N\approx170\,\mathrm{mK}\) in unannealed, an “inkling” near 140 mK in 7-day, and no transition above 0.08 K in 14-day [PAGE 5]. Measurement frequency is a clock: 95 Hz versus 995 Hz distinguishes spin-glass-like freezing from a frequency-independent maximum [PAGE 6]. Recursive theory-of-mind here means asking what an alternative explanation would predict before accepting the first fit: if impurity phases caused the resistivity change, diffraction and specific heat should see them; if Kondo disorder caused both channels, annealing should remove both signatures; if Griffiths behavior caused the specific heat, the exponent should prefer \(\lambda \lt 1\). Counterfactual prediction: if the resistivity change after annealing were caused by an impurity phase, strain, or sample inhomogeneity rather than crystallographic order, then diffraction or thermodynamic probes should detect impurity phases above the stated 1% sensitivity, and the X-ray FWHM should not decrease monotonically with annealing. Verifiable observed outcome: the paper reports single-phase AuBe5 structure, “no impurity phases like UCu5 or UPd3 were detected within 1% accuracy,” no annealed weight loss within 0.1%, and FWHM values \(0.39^\circ,0.31^\circ,0.27^\circ,0.25^\circ\) in the splat, unannealed, 7-day, and 14-day sequence [PAGE 4, PAGE 8]. Veto condition: a neutron-diffraction or µSR measurement on the same specimens that finds a correlated impurity or strain fraction exceeding the reported sensitivity would weaken the order interpretation; absent that, the artifact hypothesis is vetoed by the phase-purity and FWHM data.
