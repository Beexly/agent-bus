Sufficiency is the pivot: [PAGE 14] "the latent decision variable is a sufficient statistic for confidence: once $d$ is known, the posterior probability that the current choice is correct is fully determined." If $LD=f(d)$ with $f$ monotonic [PAGE 14], then $LD$ inherits every qualitative signature of $d$ *without* the experimenter needing to recover the posterior numerically. That is why the four signatures are ordered weakest→strongest: (1) psychometric monotonicity and (2) signed-$LD$/signed-$\Delta$ monotonicity are also consistent with heuristic codes; only (3) fixed-strength correctness prediction and (4) the folded-X require trial-to-trial fluctuations in $d$ with the *specific* geometry that a monotone-in-$|d|$ posterior forces.

The deepest methodological gray zone is determinism. A VLM is a fixed function: [PAGE 3] "A deterministic multimodal VLM produces identical logits for identical inputs and therefore has no trial-to-trial encoding noise of this kind." Signatures (3)–(4) are undefined without within-strength variance in $d$. The paper's repair is to inject *nuisance* variation across exemplars at fixed objective strength (positional jitter, independent noise seeds, morph jitter), explicitly analogized to random-dot resampling [PAGE 4]: "motion coherence fixes objective stimulus strength while the particular dot realization is resampled on every trial." This substitution is the crack through which confounds enter: nuisance variation must move $d$ the way sensory noise moves $d$ in the animal, and if a nuisance feature is correlated with correctness it can manufacture signature (3) spuriously. The paper partially defends this with scene/question difficulty controls in CLEVR [PAGE 10], but the perceptual tasks rely on the *assumption* that nuisance is evidentially inert.

The second gray zone is the direction of the sufficiency claim: $d$ is sufficient *relative to a specified normative model*. Where the experimenter-defined evidence axis $\Delta$ equals the model's effective evidence axis, signatures are diagnostic; where they diverge (recognition-memory example, [PAGE 21] $d=\Delta_{expt}+\Delta_{rw}+\epsilon$), the same normatively-computed confidence can *fail* the experimenter's signatures. So a signature failure is logically ambiguous—it is consistent with (a) heuristic combination, (b) ignored task evidence, or (c) a correctly-specified-but-different effective axis [PAGE 11]. The third gray zone is identifiability: because the single signed-strength axis varies evidence and reliability together, BCH (posterior) and CRES (raw evidence) are non-separable here [PAGE 12]; only orthogonal reliability manipulation breaks the tie. Finally, the CLEVR boundary is honestly drawn: the authors decline to score the folded-X there because "the normative posterior cannot be derived, and the folded-X is no longer a diagnostic prediction" [PAGE 10–11]. Net: the strong claim (normative readout) is earned only in the boundary-defined perceptual/memory regime, and is deliberately withheld elsewhere.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All symbols below are transcribed from the source; units and breakdown regimes are annotated.

1. **Decision-variable readout.** $LD=\ell_A-\ell_B$ [PAGE 2]. Units: logits = natural-log units of unnormalized probability (dimensionless; 1 nat = 1/ln2 ≈ 1.4427 bits). Choice $=\operatorname{sign}(LD)$; confidence proxy $=|LD|$.

2. **Normative latent variable.** $d=\Delta+\epsilon$ [PAGE 3, PAGE 14], $\epsilon$ internal decision noise. $\Delta$ carries task units: RMS contrast (dimensionless, $[0,1]$ scale) for contrast; $\log_2$ radius ratio (bits) for size; morph offset $m-m^\*$ (dimensionless) for shape; $\log_2$ population ratio (bits) for city QA.

3. **Confidence as posterior (SDC).** $\mathrm{SDC}=P(\text{correct}\mid d)$ [PAGE 14]; by Bayes,
$$P(\text{correct}\mid d)=\frac{P(d\mid \text{correct})\,P(\text{correct})}{P(d)},\qquad P(d)=\sum_{s}P(d\mid s)P(s).$$
Units: probability $\in[0,1]$. Monotonicity: $\partial P(\text{correct}\mid d)/\partial|d|>0$, giving choice from $\operatorname{sign}(d)$ and confidence from $|d|$.

4. **Monotone-readout hypothesis.** $LD=f(d)$, $f$ monotonic [PAGE 14]. This is the estimand; sufficiency of $d$ transfers all four signatures to $LD$.

5. **Stimulus constructions (assumptions explicit).**
 - Contrast [PAGE 15]: $c_L=c_0-\Delta/2,\; c_R=c_0+\Delta/2,\; c_0=0.28$; $\Delta\in[-0.40,+0.40]$; band-pass filter centered 4 cycles/degree, 1-octave bandwidth. Independent seeds per patch = nuisance.
 - Size [PAGE 15]: $\Delta=\log_2(r_{below}/r_{above})$, $r_{above}=r_0 2^{-\Delta/2},\,r_{below}=r_0 2^{+\Delta/2},\,r_0=52$ px; geometric-mean radius conserved; $\pm6$ px jitter = nuisance.
 - Shape [PAGE 16]: $r_{corner}=m\,s/2$, $\Delta=m-m^\*$, $m^\*\!=0.73$ (Qwen), $0.38$ (Gemma 3); $\pm6\%$ side / $\pm6$ px position jitter = nuisance.
 - Population [PAGE 17]: $\Delta=\log_2(\text{pop}_A/\text{pop}_B)$, 5,000 cities, 50,000 pairs.

6. **Calibration operator.** $\text{conf}=\sigma(|LD|/T)$, $\sigma$ logistic sigmoid; $T$ fit on non-held-out trials by minimizing ECE [PAGE 5]. Dimensionless.

7. **Signature (3) fixed-strength regression** [PAGE 6]: $\text{correct}\sim C(|\text{stimulus}|,\text{bin})+|LD|$. Qwen contrast $\beta=4.12,\,z=53.42,\,p<10^{-300}$; OR $=e^{4.117}\approx61.4$ ✓ (Table 1B). Population OR $1.36$ ($\beta=0.306$, $e^{0.306}\approx1.36$) ✓ (Table 4B).

8. **Signature (4) folded-X regression** [PAGE 6]: $|LD|\sim|\Delta|\times\text{Correct}$. Qwen contrast: correct slope $+8.08$, error slope $-1.11$ (Table 1C); interaction $+9.185$, $p<10^{-300}$.

9. **Recognition-memory breakdown** [PAGE 21]: $d=\Delta_{expt}+\Delta_{rw}+\epsilon$, with
$$P(C_{expt}\mid d)=\frac{\int_{\Delta_{rw}}p(d\mid \Delta_{expt}>0,\Delta_{rw})P(\Delta_{expt}>0,\Delta_{rw})}{\int_{\Delta_{expt}}\int_{\Delta_{rw}}p(d\mid \Delta_{expt},\Delta_{rw})P(\Delta_{expt},\Delta_{rw})}.$$
**Singularity/breakdown regimes:** (i) $\epsilon\to0$ determinism ⇒ $d$ collapses to a point mass at fixed $\Delta$ ⇒ signatures (3)–(4) undefined; the nuisance-exemplar trick is the required regularizer. (ii) $\Delta_{rw}\neq0$ (unmodeled evidence) ⇒ $P(C_{expt}\mid d)\neq$ experimenter-implied posterior ⇒ signatures can fail under normative computation. (iii) Overlapping (non-boundary-defined) categories ⇒ folded-X becomes "fragile rather than robust" [PAGE 12, Adler & Ma 2018] ⇒ not diagnostic (this is the CLEVR regime). (iv) Single-axis designs ⇒ $\{BCH,CRES\}$ non-identifiable because evidence and reliability co-vary.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage is a clean chain from psychophysics to VLMs. Signal-detection theory supplies the ideal-observer template; **Kiani & Shadlen 2009** (parietal LIP, random-dot motion) and **Kepecs et al. 2008** (*Nature*) establish the neural/behavioral signatures; **Kepecs & Mainen 2012** and **Hangya et al. 2016** formalize SDC mathematically; **Sanders et al. 2016** deliver the human "signatures of a statistical computation" and the perceptual→memory extension the paper explicitly reuses [PAGE 8]. The formal physics of two-alternative accumulation is **Bogacz et al. 2006** (drift-diffusion/DLA), which the Figure 1 caption names as the broader model class [PAGE 3]. The Bayesian-confidence debate is **Pouget et al. 2016** (BCH), its limits **Adler & Ma 2018**, and its challenge **Xue et al. 2024** (CRES). On the LLM side the ancestors are **Kadavath et al. 2022** ("(mostly) know what they know"), **Guo et al. 2017** (calibration), **Xiong et al. 2023 / Tian et al. 2023 / Steyvers & Peters 2025**, and the authors' own **Kumaran et al. 2026a,b** showing verbal and logit confidence are dissociable and causally used.

"Multi-week traps" = the recurring inferential pitfalls this literature keeps re-litigating, each of which the paper is careful to avoid: (1) **calibration ≠ representation** [PAGE 12: a well-calibrated score "may equally arise from a heuristic preference signal"]; (2) **folded-X as generic signature** — it is *not*; it is model-specific and boundary-dependent [PAGE 6, PAGE 12]; (3) **experimenter-axis vs effective-axis conflation** [PAGE 21]; (4) **encoding weakness misread as absence** — the memory task's shallow psychometric ($\text{pseudo-}R^2=0.069$) is "low signal-to-noise, not an absent mapping" [PAGE 8].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
This section maps to metacognition (second-order evaluation of a first-order decision); the literal terms "crisis habit regression / counter-trap / clock" have no referent in the source, so I substitute their only coherent analogues. **First order:** $LD$ is the decision-linked confidence signal. **Second order:** abstention — the model, re-shown its own answer, commits or abstains using the *same* signal [PAGE 8]: "abstention tracked the internal confidence signal rather than external difficulty," AUROC 0.840 (|LD|) vs 0.796 (stimulus strength), within-bin OR 2.38, and the policy overlaps an optimal $|LD|$-threshold on 91.6% of trials, gaining only 0.05 pp. This is the paper's strongest "the model reasons over its own confidence" result, causally linked to **Kumaran et al. 2026b**. **"Counter-trap" (default habit):** the competing hypothesis that $LD$ is a task-irrelevant heuristic preference — refuted in the perceptual/memory regime by signatures (3)–(4). **"Clock" (specification constraint):** the entire edifice is gated on a *specified* normative process model; absent it (CLEVR, reasoning traces before commitment), the second-order reading is withheld — the reasoning model even requires a "90% reasoning-cut" to read $LD$ before saturation [PAGE 18]. The paper also flags a *higher* hierarchy level: verbal confidence may add "broader uncertainty" computations beyond $LD$ [PAGE 12], i.e., genuinely distinct meta-levels, not a single scalar.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
"Physical/biomechanical/weather veto" has no referent here; the operative defeaters are *epistemic* — conditions that veto the strong claim ("$LD$ is a normative latent-decision readout") while leaving the weak claim ("$LD$ carries trial-specific correctness information") intact. The paper supplies these veto proofs itself:

- **Veto A (process-model absence, CLEVR).** On correct trials $|LD|$ rises with strength in all four analyses; on error trials the slope is flat or positive, "never negative" [PAGE 10], so the canonical folded-X fails. Verdict [PAGE 11]: "The secure conclusion is the weaker one: answer-logit difference continues to carry trial-specific information… but whether it behaves as a normative latent decision variable remains unresolved." ΔAUROC +0.029 to +0.072 survives blur *and* scene/question controls (Gemini count fixed-bin model non-estimable via collinearity — honestly disclosed, Table 8).
- **Veto B (single flat branch).** Gemma 3 contrast error slope $+0.287$ ($p=0.566$), i.e., flat not falling (Table 2D). Adjudicated as *neutral*, "neither the falling branch predicted by SDC nor the rising branch" [PAGE 21] — it does not confirm but does not falsify, since (3) still holds strongly ($\beta=0.546,\,z=52.11$).
- **Veto C (effective-axis mismatch).** Recognition-memory proof [PAGE 21]: if $d=\Delta_{expt}+\Delta_{rw}+\epsilon$, signature failure is expected *even under normative computation*. This veto shows signature-failure is not sufficient for non-normativity.
- **Veto D (identifiability).** On one signed-strength axis, "BCH and CRES… diverge only when reliability is manipulated independently of stimulus strength" [PAGE 12]; the design cannot adjudicate, so no strong posterior claim is licensed.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable scenarios that would *falsify or bound* the SDC-as-readout interpretation, with pre-registered predictions. (CF1, CF3 derive from the paper; CF2 extends it and is marked.)

**CF1 — Orthogonal reliability manipulation (from [PAGE 12]).** Independently vary $\Delta$ and evidence reliability $r$ (e.g., contrast at fixed $\Delta$ but degraded encoding).
- *BCH prediction:* the $|LD|\to\text{accuracy}$ mapping is reliability-invariant (posterior already absorbs $r$).
- *CRES prediction:* at fixed $|LD|$, accuracy falls as $r\downarrow$ (raw evidence not renormalized).
- *Veto:* observing reliability-dependent accuracy at fixed $|LD|$ vetoes the strict "posterior" reading while preserving the weaker "monotone readout" reading.

**CF2 — Process-model specification for a reasoning task (extension; speculative).** If a correct evidence axis $\hat\Delta$ and mapping $\hat d=g(\hat\Delta)+\epsilon$ can be defined for a CLEVR sub-task (e.g., counting under known blur with a fitted accumulation model), re-run signature (4) against $\hat\Delta$.
- *SDC-true prediction:* the folded-X reappears once analysis uses $\hat\Delta$ rather than raw blur (which "removes information rather than favouring one answer" [PAGE 9]).
- *Veto:* a robustly flat/positive error branch persisting under the correctly-specified $\hat\Delta$ would be genuine evidence against normative combination.

**CF3 — Nuisance-decorrelation control (from the determinism repair, [PAGE 4, PAGE 10]).** In the perceptual fixed-bin test, decorrelate the nuisance feature from correctness (e.g., hold jitter distribution identical across correct/error via matched resampling), then re-estimate $\beta_{|LD|}$.
- *Prediction:* if within-bin $|LD|\to$correctness reflects latent evidence, $\beta_{|LD|}$ stays large (Qwen contrast $\beta=4.12$) after decorrelation.
- *Veto:* collapse of $\beta_{|LD|}$ toward 0 would indicate the effect was carried by a nuisance cue, not by trial-specific decision evidence — a direct falsification of signature (3).

*RLVR-specific caveat (explicitly extrapolated, not in source):* verifiable-reward fine-tuning that rewards only the correct letter should, under the monotone-readout hypothesis, mostly rescale/sharpen $|LD|$ (temperature $T\!\downarrow$) while preserving the trial *ordering* and hence all four signatures; a post-RLVR *break* of the folded-X without any axis change would instead suggest RLVR injected non-evidential features into $LD$. This is a conjecture to test, not a claim of the paper.

**Synthesis.** The document earns exactly what it claims: in boundary-defined perceptual and memory decisions, $LD$ behaves as a monotone readout of a sufficient latent decision variable (all four signatures, one neutral flat branch), and it is *causally* used for abstention. It explicitly does **not** license the normative claim where the process model is unknown (CLEVR), and it cannot separate BCH from CRES on its own design — both stated by the authors, both reproducible as the veto tests above.
