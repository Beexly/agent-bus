Physical layer (sensor and sampling). The data originate from 10 Hz Catapult S5 GPS units [PAGE 4]. The Nyquist limit of 10 Hz sampling is 5 Hz, meaning accelerative or directional events with spectral content above 5 Hz are aliased or missed entirely. Sharp cuts, foot-plant transients, and collision-induced decelerations can contain sub-100 ms dynamics that fall between samples, causing the discretization operator M: (v, a, θ) → c_t to assign a single movement-unit character to a biomechanically non-uniform interval. Veto condition: if a player executes a sub-0.1 s direction reversal that is averaged into a "Straight" or "Acute-change" bin, the resulting movement sequence loses the very motor signature that separates elite positional movement. Adjudication: despite this physical limitation, LCCspm still achieved MLP accuracy of 91.02% [PAGE 1, PAGE 13], indicating that the veto was not triggered strongly enough to destroy separability between hookers and wingers under the observed conditions.

Biomechanical layer (mass and load). Hookers and wingers were selected partly because they share nearly similar average body weight yet perform distinct tactical roles [PAGE 3, citing study 24]. Because the classification vectors X_i contain only presence/absence of movement patterns and not body mass, collision load, or tackle count, any veto based on mass confounding is structurally bypassed: the model cannot use mass as a feature. However, high-speed running volume differs markedly (wingers 626 m vs. hookers 285 m at > 5.00 m·s⁻¹ [PAGE 15]), and LCCspm uniquely captures the hooker’s 19-item run/sprint straight sequence versus the winger’s alternating acute-change sprint sequence [PAGE 15]. Biomechanically, this is coherent: wingers execute repeated high-speed accelerations with acute direction changes in open-field kick returns, whereas hookers reposition behind the play-the-ball with lower high-speed volume [PAGE 15]. Veto condition: if future data show that hookers and wingers converge in high-speed volume due to tactical role blurring, the movement-pattern separation would degrade; no such convergence is evidenced in the source.

Weather layer. The source contains no weather, pitch-condition, or precipitation variables [PAGE 4–PAGE 6]. Table 2’s turning-angle and velocity thresholds are treated as context-invariant [PAGE 5]. A formal weather veto would trigger if rain alters pitch friction sufficiently to shift the distribution of θ_t such that the "Straight" (< 10°) and "Acute-change" (10°–45°) bins no longer correspond to the same motor intents, or if wind/slip conditions reduce maximum attainable velocity below the 5.00 m·s⁻¹ Sprint threshold for sustained periods, collapsing the Sprint bin. Because weather is absent from the operationalization, this veto cannot be adjudicated internally; it is an external validity hole. Any deployment of LCCspm in match environments with materially different weather from the 2019–2020 Super League fixtures requires re-calibration of Table 2 thresholds before the pattern vocabulary—and hence the classifier—remains valid.

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-verifiable (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1: Player-level cross-validation leakage. The reported 10-fold cross-validation randomly shuffles the 1,036 player-per-fixture observations (random_state = 10, shuffle = True) [PAGE 7]. Because multiple fixtures belong to the same player, standard k-fold validation can place observations from the same individual in both training and test partitions, violating independence. Counterfactual intervention: re-run all five classifiers using GroupKFold grouped by player ID so that no player appears in both train and test folds. Prediction: the MLP accuracy on LCCspm patterns will fall materially below the reported 91.02%, plausibly into the 70–80% range, because position-invariant signal must be separated from player-specific idiosyncratic movement signatures. Verifiability: reimplementation with the public GitHub code [PAGE 7, ref. 37] and player identifiers from the non-public GPS dataset.

Veto scenario 2: Support threshold inflation. The study fixes support at 5 % for LCCspm and AprioriClose, yielding a minimum absolute occurrence count of 52 sequences out of 1,036 and producing 3,881 LCCspm patterns [PAGE 6, PAGE 8]. Counterfactual intervention: raise support to 20 % (minimum 208 occurrences). Prediction: the LCCspm vocabulary will shrink by more than half, pruning rare but discriminative patterns such as the hooker’s 19-item "GGGGGGGGGGGGSSSSSSS" and the winger’s "TSSTSTTSST" [PAGE 15]. Consequently, MLP accuracy on the LCCspm dataset will fall below 85%, likely toward 75–80%, because position separation in this study relies partly on not-too-frequent and position-unique patterns rather than the most frequent-50 overlaps [PAGE 15]. Verifiability: re-mine with supp = 0.20 and re-evaluate Table 5 classifiers.

Veto scenario 3: Temporal window contraction. LCCspm’s length parameter is set to 20 items, corresponding to a 2-second contiguous window [PAGE 6]. Counterfactual intervention: reduce length to 5 items (0.5 seconds). Prediction: short contiguous patterns will overlap more heavily with LCS (raising the LCCspm–LCS Jaccard score above 0.19), but classification accuracy for separating hookers and wingers will fall below 85% because longer tactical sequences that encode positional role—such as the winger’s alternating sprint acute-change/straight motif—will be truncated and rendered indistinguishable across positions. Verifiability: re-run LCCspm with length = 5, regenerate the binary classification matrix, and reproduce Table 5 accuracy outcomes.
