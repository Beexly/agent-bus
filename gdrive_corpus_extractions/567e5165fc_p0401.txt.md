Breakdown / singularity regimes. (i) Afferent interaction dominance: when receptor-channel cross-terms I_ij ≠ 0 are nonzero, Φ must become Φ(𝒓) + Σ_{i≠j} I_ij r_i r_j, invalidating the interaction-free law [PAGE 401]. (ii) Perfect redundancy: if s_i = s_j, information saturates and Φ → max_i r_i rather than accumulation. (iii) Zero-reinforcement novelty: if all r_i = 0, then R_compound = 0 and P_evoke = σ(−θ), collapsing to baseline prior. (iv) Saturation: as R_compound → R_max, ∂_{r_i}Φ → 0, so additional elements produce no further increase in response probability.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage. Hull's compound summation descends from Thorndike's law of effect and Pavlovian excitatory generalization, systematized in Principles of Behavior [PAGE 401] as a neo-behaviorist homing mechanism: present response probability is organized by the anticipated future consequence (need reduction), making past reinforcement history retrocausally effective at stimulus presentation. The subadditive Φ anticipates later associative architectures: it mirrors the diminishing prediction-error term in Rescorla-Wagner and the saturating value function in temporal-difference learning, where redundant cues block further association (cue competition / overshadowing).

Multi-week traps. After weeks of daily reinforcement of a stable compound, the habit-strength load r_i becomes entrenched. The organism exhibits stimulus rigidity and continues to evoke the response even after the need-reduction contingency has changed, because the probability gauge still registers high similarity to the original context. This produces a behavioral trap in which extinction requires far more trials than acquisition, and partial-reinforcement schedules generate persistent responding well beyond the point at which a rational Bayesian updater would abandon the response. The trap is especially severe when the compound is "repeated on a subsequent occasion" under conditions that superficially resemble the original reinforced context but differ in the critical contingency [PAGE 401].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression. Under acute stress or need deprivation, the organism regresses to the compound carrying the highest historical habit-strength load, bypassing fine-grained discrimination. The evoked response is the one previously paired with the most urgent need reduction, not necessarily the currently optimal response.

Counter-trap. Discrimination training that differentially reinforces compound elements presented separately forces Φ to weight unique features, breaking the subadditive lock on the full compound. This opponent-process intervention recovers sensitivity to elemental contingencies and prevents the organism from being captured by a historically dominant compound.

Clock. Time since last reinforcement, Δt, acts as a decay variable on reaction potential: r_i(t) = r_i(0)e^{−λΔt}, with decay rate λ > 0. As Δt grows, R_compound falls below the threshold θ/β, and response probability collapses even for a fully trained compound. The interaction of clock and crisis regression produces a hysteresis loop: after a long inter-reinforcement interval, a crisis triggers the oldest high-load habit rather than the most recently adaptive response.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto. Receptor loss or peripheral nerve conduction block sets r_i = 0 for affected channels regardless of habit strength; if all receptors for a compound are damaged, R_compound = 0 and the summation equation is vetoed at the input layer.

Biomechanical veto. Even when R_compound is maximal and P_evoke ≈ 1, motor execution failure (muscle fatigue, joint injury, paralysis) prevents the response. The behavioral output layer overrides central reaction potential.

Weather veto. Environmental degradation—heavy rain attenuating visual stimuli, high noise masking auditory stimuli—reduces effective receptor discharge fidelity by scaling r_i with an environmental transmission factor η ∈ [0,1]: r_i^eff = η r_i. When η drops below the critical value η_c = θ/(β·Σr_i), response probability falls to baseline. These vetoes are necessary conditions: no amount of habit-strength history can compensate for a zero at any veto layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Subadditivity test). Train two equal-load aggregates A and B to the same response, then present the compound AB. Prediction from [PAGE 401]: P_evoke(AB) < P_evoke(A) + P_evoke(B). Veto condition: if measured compound probability is superadditive or exactly additive, Hull's diminishing-returns operator Φ is falsified for this modality.

Scenario 2 (Withdrawal test). After AB training, withdraw B and present A alone. Prediction: P_evoke(A) > ½P_evoke(AB). Veto condition: if P_evoke(A) ≤ ½P_evoke(AB), the "more than half remains" claim fails, indicating linear or superadditive summation rather than Hullian diminishing returns.

Scenario 3 (Novelty test). Present a never-reinforced compound C composed of familiar elements in a new configuration. Prediction: R_compound ≈ 0, so P_evoke(C) ≈ σ(−θ), i.e. baseline prior. Veto condition: if a novel compound evokes above-baseline responding without direct reinforcement history, the instant probability gauge cannot rest solely on summed habit strength; configural or pattern-level encoding is required. All three scenarios are verifiable with standard response-frequency recording and invoke no betting-line quantities.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
