Any model asserting a single-valued Euclidean apparent metric across the whole body is vetoed by the wrist bifurcation and the facial ellipse [PAGE 141]. Any model asserting modality invariance is vetoed by the tongue/finger comparison [PAGE 141]. Environmental veto: if temperature \( T \) changes such that skin compliance \( \kappa(T) \) alters the card-edge contact width, then \( \hat{d}(p_1,p_2;\text{card-edge},T) \) is not invariant across \( T \); a model ignoring \( \kappa(T) \) fails under thermal variation. The veto is cross-layer because it requires physical receptor distribution, biomechanical contour, and environmental modulation to be simulated jointly; a single-layer model cannot reproduce the card-versus-compass asymmetry at the same endpoints [PAGE 141].

Scenario 1 (site dependence): Counterfactual—apply a fixed 15 mm compass-point separation to the volar forearm and to the distal fingertip of the same subject. Verifiable prediction: \( \hat{d}_{\text{fingertip}} > \hat{d}_{\text{forearm}} \) and the two-point threshold \( \tau_{\text{fingertip}} < \tau_{\text{forearm}} \). Veto clause: if a model predicts equal apparent separation or equal threshold for both sites, it is vetoed, contradicting James's report that apparent distance varies by body location and that lines "become more widely separated below the wrist... towards the finger-tips" [PAGE 141].

Scenario 2 (stimulus morphology): Counterfactual—press a card edge and a pair of compass tips against the same two skin terminals separated by 20 mm. Verifiable prediction: \( \hat{d}_{\text{card}} < \hat{d}_{\text{compass}} \). Veto clause: if a model predicts \( \hat{d}_{\text{card}} \ge \hat{d}_{\text{compass}} \), it is vetoed, contradicting "the distance between its extremities will seem shorter than that between two compass-tips touching the same terminal points" [PAGE 141].

Scenario 3 (dynamic trajectory bifurcation): Counterfactual—draw two compass points 15 mm apart slowly down the forearm, across the wrist, over the palm, and split onto adjacent fingers while recording continuous apparent-path reports. Verifiable prediction: the apparent path is single proximal to the wrist, bifurcates into two branches at \( w \), widens distal to the wrist, contracts over the palm, and diverges again at the finger-tips. Veto clause: if a model predicts a single uniform parallel path matching the dotted true path throughout, or if it predicts no bifurcation at \( w \), it is vetoed, contradicting the description of true versus full lines in Figs. 51 and 52 [PAGE 141]. Each scenario is verifiable with kinematic tracking and forced-choice apparent-separation reports; the vetoes are falsifiable because they specify exact directional inequalities and topological outcomes grounded in the source text [PAGE 141].
