However, a multi-week trap exists for those studying the genealogy of avoidance learning: the conflation of Hullian reactive inhibition with later Pavlovian "conditioned inhibition" ($I_\alpha$ and $I_\delta$). Recognizing this divergence is crucial; conflating them leads to flawed predictions about extinction curves. Furthermore, early interpretations often missed the retrocausal aspect of the "gradient of reinforcement"—that the *future* relief (cessation of pain) dictates the *past* reinforcement allocation. Hull correctly identified that the moment of relief is temporally closest to the state of rest, thereby reinforcing rest over action. This establishes a deep historical precedent for understanding how future-oriented relief shapes present-day inhibitory structures, a concept later echoed in temporal difference learning algorithms but grounded here in rigorous behavioral postulates.
