\]

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The method's lineage is a retrocausal chain in which each older operator was originally selected for a different failure mode and is re-selected here by the narrow-FOV constraint. The homography/DLT tradition (Hayet et al. 2004; Dubrofsky & Woodham 2008) supplies the 8-constraint baseline that the paper explicitly declares insufficient: "Estimating the homography requires at least 8 constraints" [PAGE 1]. The absolute-conic line of work (Triggs; Hartley & Zisserman) supplies Eq. (3) and the IAC \(\omega=K^{-T}K^{-1}\). Gedikli's 2009 thesis supplies the two-point focal-length quadratic; Li et al. 2015 supplies the single-point PT calibration. The paper is candid about the assembly: "The components of the method have been proposed by Gedikli et al. [11] and Li et al. [19], separately. However, to the best of our knowledge, we are the first to integrate them and apply them to sports camera calibration" [PAGE 3]. Thomas 2007 supplies the fixed-base PTZ prior; Okuma et al. 2004 and Thomas supply the line-based robustness lineage; Cavallari et al. 2017 and Meng et al. 2017 supply the regression-forest camera-relocalization precedent that the pan-tilt forest adapts.

The retrocausal reading is that the narrow-FOV requirement acts as a selection pressure running backwards through the literature: it discards the four-point homography as over-parameterized, revives the two-point focal quadratic as the minimal identifiable core, and promotes the random forest from a relocalization tool to a prior-encoding device. The deep structured model of Homayounfar et al. 2017 and the top-view dictionary of Sharma et al. 2017 are the two contemporaneous alternatives; the paper positions itself between them — more automatic than CalibMe, less dependent on semantic segmentation than Homayounfar.

Multi-week traps are the temporal failure modes that a single-game evaluation cannot expose. Trap one: base drift. The prior block \(S[I\mid -C]\) is estimated per venue and per game; a stadium re-mount, a camera-platform maintenance event, or a broadcaster relocating the gantry between matchweeks invalidates it, and the paper warns "these parameters may change from one game to another" [PAGE 8]. Trap two: pan-envelope drift. A new operator or a new directorial mandate widens the pan range beyond the training coverage, which is precisely the Seq3 failure. Trap three: seasonal radiometry. Winter light, floodlight colour temperature, rain sheen, and shadow lines alter SIFT repeatability; the SURF substitution drop from 0.83 to 0.74 IoU [PAGE 7] is a lower bound on descriptor sensitivity. Trap four: lens-distortion accumulation. The model omits distortion entirely, and the error is focal-length-dependent, so a zoom regime change across weeks silently biases the calibration. Trap five: pitch-geometry variation. The world model assumes a standard soccer field; venues with non-standard dimensions or temporary markings break the 3D–2D correspondence assumptions. Each trap has a detectable signature in the paper's own metrics — falling IoU, rising rotation-error CDF mass above 1°, or an inlier-ratio collapse below the 0.33 thresholded value.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The "theory of mind" layer here is the pan-tilt forest's implicit model of the human camera operator. The forest is trained on \((x_p,r_p)\) pairs, where \(r_p\) is the ray the operator chose to place at pixel \(p\). Learning \(h(\cdot)\) is therefore learning the operator's pointing policy: given local appearance, where would the operator have aimed? The paper frames this as regression, but the recursive content is that the model must infer intent from a patch, not from a global scene. This is why the method generalizes across images without image-to-image matching: it has internalized a policy, not a database.

Crisis habit regression. The system's "habit" is to trust SIFT descriptors on pitch markings and surrounding texture. When the crisis arrives — a narrow-FOV crop with "a very small texture-rich area" [PAGE 6] — the habit regresses: the forest still emits predictions, but they are outlier-dominated. Table 4 quantifies the regression: without thresholding, the inlier fraction at 0.5° is 0.09 and mean IoU is 0.53 [PAGE 7]. The habit does not fail gracefully; it produces confident wrong rays.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is the feature-distance gate \(\lVert \bar x_l-x\rVert_2^2<\tau\) described on [PAGE 4] as a way "to remove outliers (e.g. features on players)." This is a deliberate refusal to recognize familiar-looking patches when their descriptor is far from the leaf mean. It raises the 0.5° inlier fraction from 0.09 to 0.33 and mean IoU from 0.53 to 0.83 — a +0.24 inlier gain and +0.30 IoU gain, both reported in Table 4 [PAGE 7]. The counter-trap is strictly more valuable than the descriptor swap: replacing SIFT with SURF costs only 0.09 IoU, whereas removing the gate costs 0.30 IoU. The gate, not the descriptor, is the dominant control.

Clock. The clock pressure is explicit: 0.2 s/frame for SIFT plus 0.1 s/frame for prediction and optimization, total 0.3 s/frame on a 3.0 GHz CPU [PAGE 7]. At the dataset's 6 FPS capture rate [PAGE 4], real-time would require ≤0.167 s/frame, so the current implementation runs at roughly 3.3 FPS — about 1.8× too slow for live use. The clock also governs the RANSAC budget: two-point minimal sets cut iterations from 71 to 16 at 50% outliers [PAGE 4], and the forest ensemble is capped at 5 trees because "adding more trees also improves the performance at the cost of running time" [PAGE 7]. The recursive trade is that the counter-trap (thresholding) and the clock (tree count, RANSAC budget) are coupled: tightening the gate raises inliers, which lowers the RANSAC iteration count, which buys back the time spent on extra trees.

The paper's own sensitivity result is the proof: "the rotation error of our method is sensitive to camera base rotation uncertainties. It is not unexpected as the base rotation error can be directly propagated from the 'prior' part to the 'PTZ' part in (1)" [PAGE 5]. Figure 5(d) plots rotation error against base-rotation noise over 0–1°, and the propagation is order-unity: a base error of ~1° produces a comparable rotation error, whereas Figure 5(c) shows location noise over 0–0.5 m is comparatively benign because "the movement of camera location is relatively small compared to the spatial extent of the stadium" [PAGE 5]. The veto condition is therefore: any physical event that changes \(S\) or \(C\) — remount, platform work, venue change, structural vibration — invalidates the prior and must trigger re-estimation before the two-point solver is trusted.

**Layer 3 — Weather/radiometric veto.** Weather does not enter the optics; it enters the feature extractor. Rain, snow cover, glare, floodlight flicker, and hard shadow boundaries reduce local-patch distinctiveness, which lowers the effective inlier ratio \(w\) and therefore raises the RANSAC iteration count as \(N=\log(1-P_s)/\log(1-w^s)\). The empirical bound on descriptor sensitivity is the SURF substitution: "if we replace the SIFT descriptor with the SURF descriptor [3], the mean IoU score drops from 0.83 to 0.74" [PAGE 7]. The weather veto is thus soft but real: it does not break Eq. (2), it starves the forest, and Table 4 shows the starved regime collapses to 0.53 IoU.

**Layer 4 — Biomechanical/occlusion veto.** The operator's head–eye–hand loop sets the pan/tilt trajectory, and the forest learns that loop. A change of operator or directorial style is an off-policy shift. Separately, players occlude the very markings the method needs; the paper's own outlier example is "features on players" [PAGE 4], and the feature-distance gate exists specifically to reject them. If players cover the two visible model points, the manual annotation path has no input and the automatic path has no inlier geometry. The paper does not quantify occlusion rate, so this veto is qualitative but mechanically unavoidable.

**Layer 5 — Model-error veto.** Lens distortion is excluded by construction and conceded: "our camera model does not consider lens distortion which may be significant in some games" [PAGE 8]. Because distortion grows with focal length and off-axis distance, the narrow-FOV regime that the method targets is also the regime where the omitted term is largest. This is a silent veto: it does not produce an obvious failure, it biases \(f\) and the rotation angles inside the LM residual.

**Veto 1 — FOV floor.** Counterfactual: hold the camera base and the two-point geometry fixed, sweep \(\mathrm{FOV}_h\) from 45° down to 10°. Prediction: the red-cross rate (IoU ≤ 0.6) is zero for \(\mathrm{FOV}_h\gtrsim 40^\circ\), becomes non-zero somewhere in \(25^\circ\)–\(40^\circ\), and rises monotonically below 25°. Verifiable directly against Figure 8 [PAGE 7] and against the quoted sentences "smaller than about 25° ... more incorrect estimates" and "larger than about 40° ... no incorrect estimates." Falsification condition: any red cross above 40° or a clean pass below 20° would refute the veto.

**Veto 2 — Base-rotation propagation.** Counterfactual: perturb only the base rotation \(S\) by \(\delta\in[0,1]^\circ\) while holding feature noise at zero. Prediction: the estimated rotation error scales approximately as \(O(\delta)\), with slope near unity, while an equal-magnitude perturbation of \(C\) over 0–0.5 m produces a much smaller rotation error. This is verifiable from Figure 5(c,d) [PAGE 5] and from the stated propagation mechanism through Eq. (1). A stronger, exact prediction: because the error enters through the prior block multiplicatively, the induced pan/tilt error is not attenuated by adding more training data or more trees — it is a bias, not a variance. Falsification condition: demonstrating that base-rotation error shrinks with more trees would refute the bias claim.

**Veto 3 — Gate versus descriptor.** Counterfactual: two independent ablations on the same test set. (a) Remove the feature-distance threshold: predicted inlier@0.5° = 0.09, mean IoU = 0.53. (b) Keep the gate but swap SIFT for SURF: predicted mean IoU = 0.74. The ordering \(0.53<0.74<0.83\) is the falsifiable claim, and it asserts that the gate is the dominant operator (Δ = 0.30) over the descriptor choice (Δ = 0.09). These are the exact numbers in Table 4 and the SURF sentence [PAGE 7]. A replication that finds the reverse ordering would refute the counter-trap thesis.

**Veto 4 — RANSAC budget under outlier inflation.** Counterfactual: raise the outlier ratio from 50% to 70% at fixed 99% success. Prediction from \(N=\lceil\log(0.01)/\log(1-w^s)\rceil\): two-point minimal set requires ~49 iterations, four-point requires ~566, a factor of ~11.6; at 90% outliers the two-point requirement rises to ~458. The 50% row is verified exactly by Table 1 (71/16/7). Falsification condition: measuring a two-point iteration count materially below 49 at 70% outliers would indicate that the RANSAC implementation is exploiting additional structure not present in the paper's stated assumptions.
