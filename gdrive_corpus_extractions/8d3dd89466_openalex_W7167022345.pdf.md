Under a binomial model the standard error is $\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$; at $p=0.96$ and $n=100$ that is 0.0196, i.e. a 95% interval of roughly ±3.8 percentage points — wide enough to overlap a 92% competitor. Resolving a 1-point difference at 95% confidence requires roughly $n \approx 2z^2p(1-p)/\delta^2 \approx 2{,}950$ trials. The paper supplies none. This is the gray zone: a review that reports accuracy *as a rhetorical token* rather than as an estimate with an error bar.

**O1. Gesture classifier as max-pool → affine → simplex map.**
$$\mathbf{y}=\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\mathbf{W}\,\operatorname{vec}\big[\max_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_k}X^{(l)}_{ij}\big]+\mathbf{b}\Big),\qquad \mathbf{y}\in\Delta^{K-1}$$
with $X\in[0,1]^{H\times W}$ (normalized radiance, dimensionless), $\mathbf{y}$ a probability vector over $K$ classes, and $\hat{p}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{1}[\arg\max\mathbf{y}_i=c_i]$ a binomial proportion. **Breakdown regime:** the max operator is non-differentiable where two activations tie, giving gradient ties of measure zero but *empirical* instability when $K$ classes are visually near-duplicate (the paper's "contempt" vs "anger" categories, [PAGE 2]). Reportable statistic must be $(\hat{p},\,\mathrm{SE}=\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/n},\,n)$. The source reports $\hat{p}=0.96$ with no $n$ [PAGE 1]; the claim is therefore unrated, not false.

**O2. Gaze estimation and its reciprocal singularity.**
For pupil–corneal-reflection (PCCR) tracking, the measured vector between pupil centre and glint is $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^2$ in pixels, and the gaze direction is a calibrated map $\boldsymbol{\theta}=\Phi(\mathbf{v})$. First-order propagated error:
$$\sigma_\theta \simeq \big\|\nabla\Phi\big\|\;\sigma_v,\qquad \big\|\nabla\Phi\big\|\propto\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}$$
**Singularity:** as $\|\mathbf{v}\|\to 0$ (glint coincident with pupil centre — gaze aligned with the illuminator axis), $\sigma_\theta\to\infty$. This is the precise formal content of "issues of calibration accuracy, fatigue, and long-term user comfort still need to be addressed" [PAGE 6] and of "developing robust gaze analysis methods for unconstrained environments" [PAGE 13]. Secondary singularity: pinhole projection $\theta=\arctan(\sqrt{g_x^2+g_y^2}/g_z)$ degenerates at $g_z\to0$, i.e. $\theta\to\pi/2$, where $\mathrm{d}\theta = (\cos^2\theta/f)\,\mathrm{d}u$ (rad per pixel, $f$ in pixels).

**O3. Information-theoretic ceiling on multimodal fusion.**
$$I(X_1,X_2;Y)=I(X_1;Y)+\underbrace{I(X_2;Y\mid X_1)}_{G\;\ge\;0}\;\le\;H(Y)\;\le\;\log_2 K\ \text{bits}$$
The source asserts fusion benefit — "By combining visual data with speech recognition, touch input, and natural language understanding, these systems achieve more holistic and context-aware interpretations of user intent" [PAGE 8] — and lists "Multimodal Interaction, which integrates CV with complementary sensory inputs like voice, haptics, and electroencephalography (EEG)" [PAGE 17]. **Breakdown regime:** $G=0$ exactly when $X_2\perp Y\mid X_1$ (redundant modality); fusion then buys nothing and costs compute. **Hard ceiling:** for the six emotion classes enumerated in the source [PAGE 2], $H(Y)\le\log_2 6=2.585$ bits, so no stacking of modalities can exceed 2.585 bits of emotional information per decision. The word "complementary" in the source is doing exactly the work that $I(X_2;Y\mid X_1)$ formalizes — and it is never operationalized.

**O4. Visual–inertial fusion (the `[71,73]`→`[72,74]` cluster).**
$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}+\mathbf{K}_k\big(\mathbf{z}_k-\mathbf{H}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}\big),\quad \mathbf{K}_k=\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}\mathbf{H}^{\!\top}\big(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}\mathbf{H}^{\!\top}+\mathbf{R}_k\big)^{-1}$$
Units: $\mathbf{P},\mathbf{R}$ in m² (position) and (rad/s)² (attitude); $\mathbf{K}$ dimensionless. **Singularities:** (i) $\mathbf{R}_k\to\mathbf{0}$ (over-trusted vision) ⇒ $\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{H}^{-1}$, IMU is ignored and high-frequency head motion aliases; (ii) $\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}\to\mathbf{0}$ (over-confident prior) ⇒ $\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{0}$, measurement rejected, filter diverges; (iii) during blink or hand occlusion $\mathbf{R}\to\infty$ ⇒ pure dead reckoning, position drift $\propto t^{3/2}$ under white-acceleration noise. This is the mechanism behind "high computational demands without introducing latency that could result in motion sickness" [PAGE 10].

**O5. Motion-to-photon latency and sensory-conflict angle.**
$$\Delta\phi \approx \omega\,L\ \ [\text{rad}],\qquad \Delta\phi^\ast=\text{empirical tolerance}$$
with $\omega$ head angular velocity (rad·s⁻¹) and $L$ motion-to-photon latency (s). At $\omega=2$ rad·s⁻¹ and $L=20$ ms, $\Delta\phi=0.040$ rad $=2.3°$. **Breakdown regime:** $\omega L>\Delta\phi^\ast$. The source asserts the link — "potential motion sickness from latency issues" [PAGE 11] — but publishes no $L$ budget and no $\Delta\phi^\ast$; the threshold must be treated as a calibration parameter, not a constant.

**O6. Fitts' law for hands-free selection (source invokes a Fitts task, [PAGE 3–4]).**
$$MT=a+b\log_2\!\Big(\frac{2D}{W}\Big)\ \ [\text{s}],\qquad ID=\log_2(2D/W)\ \ [\text{bits}],\qquad TP=\frac{ID}{MT}\ \ [\text{bits·s}^{-1}]$$
Units are genuine: index of difficulty in bits, throughput in bits per second. The source's claim that gaze and voice give "low workload values and high user satisfaction levels" [PAGE 3] with presence "similar to traditional handheld controllers while minimizing cyber sickness [13]" [PAGE 4] is a *throughput-and-error-rate* claim with no $TP$ and no error rate reported — unrated.

**O7. Angular pixel density vs field of view (the display trade-off).**
$$\mathrm{PPD}=\frac{N_{\text{pix}}}{2\Theta}\ \ [\text{pixels·deg}^{-1}]$$
For fixed panel resolution $N_{\text{pix}}$, PPD $\propto \Theta^{-1}$: enlarging half-FOV $\Theta$ (deg) strictly degrades angular resolution. Foveal Nyquist sampling of ~60 cycles·deg⁻¹ acuity requires $\gtrsim120$ PPD. A 4000-pixel panel at $\Theta=50°$ yields 40 PPD — a factor ~3 short — which is exactly the formal content of "balancing resolution with large fields of view, which is critical to maintaining immersive realism and avoiding distortions [69,74]" [PAGE 10]. Foveated rendering restores cost by shading only a fraction of area, $\;A_{\text{shaded}}/A_{\text{total}}=\epsilon^{2}+(1-\epsilon^{2})\kappa^{-1}$, with $\epsilon$ the foveal-disc semi-angle as a fraction of FOV and $\kappa$ the peripheral downsample factor (both dimensionless). **Breakdown:** as $\epsilon\to0$ the foveal disc under-samples and the user notices blur; as $\kappa\to1$ the scheme costs full render. The optimum is bounded, not free — which is why "foveated rendering and gaze-based interactions, optimizing visual focus and rendering performance [31]" [PAGE 12] is a *budget* statement, not a *gain* statement.

**O8. Photometric veto operator.**
Luminance $L=\rho E/\pi$ [cd·m⁻²] from albedo $\rho$ (dimensionless) and illuminance $E$ [lux]; detector SNR $=20\log_{10}(\mu/\sigma_n)$ [dB]. Motion blur from a hand moving at $v$ [m·s⁻¹] with exposure $t_{\exp}$ [s] has image-plane extent $b=vt_{\exp}f/z$ pixels ($f$ focal length, $z$ range, both consistent units). **Breakdown:** $b\gtrsim$ the smallest discriminative feature scale (e.g. inter-finger gap). This is the operator beneath "Lighting and Environmental Variability, as unstable or diverse conditions can severely compromise the accuracy of vision-based recognition systems [97]" [PAGE 17].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The review's lineage is longer than its bibliography admits, and the omissions are diagnostic.

**Gaze line.** Yarbus (1967) established that scanpaths carry task-dependent information; Jacob (1990) moved eye tracking into HCI as an input channel; the source's claim that eye tracking offers "precise, low-effort input methods, surpassing traditional head-gaze controls [30]" [PAGE 12] is a 35-year-old inheritance restated without the intervening literature on the Midas-touch problem — the fact that gaze is always "on," so dwell-time thresholds trade false activation against latency. Duchowski's work on gaze-contingent displays is the direct ancestor of "foveated rendering… optimizing visual focus and rendering performance [31]" [PAGE 12].

**Gesture line.** Freeman's 1995 orientation histograms → Viola–Jones (2001) → the max-pooling CNN of Nagi et al. (2011) cited as [7] [PAGE 1] → MediaPipe/BlazePose (2019) → modern transformer pose estimators. The source stops at 2011 for its only quantitative claim, then leaps to 2024–2025 for everything else. The 96%-on-six-classes number is therefore a *2011 result* presented in a 2026 review as current state of the art — a fourteen-year staleness gap that the review's framing conceals.

**Affect line.** Ekman & Friesen's FACS (1978) → Picard's *Affective Computing* (1997) → the source's "emotion recognition systems can dynamically adjust learning materials" [PAGE 3]. Picard is not cited. The lineage matters because FACS is a *descriptive anatomically-grounded* coding scheme, whereas the deep classifiers of [PAGE 2] are *supervised on labeled corpora* — the two are not the same object, and cross-cultural transfer is precisely where the descriptive scheme had known limits, which the source acknowledges only obliquely: "challenges remain in addressing variations in illumination, occlusion, and cross-cultural differences [21]" [PAGE 6].

**Immersive line.** Sutherland (1965 "Ultimate Display"; 1968 HMD) → Cruz-Neira's CAVE (1992) → Oculus (2012) → the 6DoF visual-inertial stack of Fang et al. (2017) [entry 69, PAGE 24]. The source's latency-sickness discussion [PAGE 10–11] is the modern face of a problem identified in the 1990s flight-simulator literature.

**Multi-week traps.** Four traps whose detection horizon is *weeks*, not one session, and which the review's cross-sectional framing cannot see:
1. *Calibration drift.* $\theta_t=\theta_0+\beta t+\eta_t$ with $\beta$ in deg·day⁻¹ (HMD slippage, pupil-size variation). A system that benchmarks at 1.5° on day 0 silently degrades past usability by week 3. The source names "calibration accuracy, fatigue, and long-term user comfort" [PAGE 6] but supplies no time axis.
2. *Dataset staleness / concept drift.* Training corpora frozen at collection time; device optics, camera sensors, and user demographics shift underneath. Cited indirectly as "biases in training datasets and the lack of standardized evaluation frameworks [26]" [PAGE 8].
3. *Evaluator habituation.* Users learn the classifier's decision boundary and adapt their expressions/gestures — a Goodhart shift over 2–6 weeks that inflates lab accuracy and deflates field accuracy.
4. *Seasonal illumination drift.* Outdoor and windowed deployments cross a photometric regime boundary (O8) at seasonal solar-elevation change; a January validation set is not a July validation set.

The retrocausal point: the review's optimism is an artifact of *snapshot epistemology*. Every lineage above was validated on a horizon the medium cannot represent.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three regimes must be separated.

**Regime A — crisis habit regression.** Under cognitive load or system failure, users do not escalate to the more "natural" modality; they regress to the most over-learned one. The keyboard and mouse are motor programs with years of automatization; a mid-air gesture is a novel motor program with high attentional cost. Formally, if $U(m)$ is expected utility of modality $m$ and $C_a(m)$ its attentional cost, the user selects $\arg\max_m[U(m)-C_a(m)/\lambda]$ where $\lambda$ is available executive resources. As $\lambda\downarrow$ (stress, time pressure, error cascade), the cost term dominates and the argmax collapses to the lowest-$C_a$ option. The source's assistive framing — interfaces "for users with mobility issues or cognitive limitations" [PAGE 3] — inverts exactly this: the users with the *lowest* $\lambda$ are the ones for whom a high-$C_a$ modality is least recoverable in crisis. That is the regression trap, and no amount of accuracy improvement at $p=0.96$ addresses it.

**Regime B — the counter-trap (affective closed loop).** When the system infers the user's state and adapts, the user's state becomes an endogenous variable:
$$u_{t+1}=f(u_t)+\alpha\,\hat{e}_t,\qquad \hat{e}_t=g(\text{face}_t),\qquad \Gamma=\alpha\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial e}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}$$
$\Gamma$ is dimensionless loop gain. $|\Gamma|<1$: the system damps toward the user; adaptation helps. $|\Gamma|\ge1$: the system over-corrects — it detects "confusion," simplifies content, the user's expression shifts to boredom, the classifier chases, difficulty oscillates. This is the *counter-trap*: the emotion-aware tutor described at [PAGE 3] and the elderly-facing "emotional-aware" recommender [PAGE 4] both sit inside this loop and neither is analyzed for stability. The gray-zone corollary: the classifier's own adaptation changes the label distribution it was trained on, so reported accuracy is a *closed-loop* quantity, not an open-loop one.

**Regime C — the clock.** Perception tolerates roughly 100 ms of input-output as "simultaneous"; VR tolerates far less, per O5. Formally, define the deadline operator $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ that truncates inference at budget $\tau$. Gesture recognition under $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ is a different statistical problem than unconstrained inference: $\hat{y}_\tau=\arg\max_y p_\tau(y\mid X)$ where $p_\tau$ is computed from a partial observation window. As $\tau\downarrow$, $p_\tau$ approaches the prior and accuracy decays toward $1/K$ — chance. The source names the constraint — "Real-time Processing, which often requires substantial computational resources to run modern deep learning models efficiently" [PAGE 17] — but treats it as an engineering cost rather than as a change in the estimand. **Adjudication:** the clock vetoes the accuracy claim; a 96% number computed offline under $\mathcal{D}_\infty$ is not evidence about $\mathcal{D}_{16\,\text{ms}}$. Recursive consequence: users calibrate their gesture speed to the perceived lag, so the loop $(\text{latency}\to\text{user speed}\to\text{blur}\to\text{accuracy}\to\text{latency})$ has its own gain and can settle into a slow-motion equilibrium where the system is accurate but useful throughput collapses.

The recursive-of-recursive layer: the user models the system's model. If the user believes the system reads confusion, the user will *perform* calm — and the classifier's training distribution, built on spontaneous expression, mis-specifies on posed expression. The source's reliance on "subtle, spontaneous movements that convey hidden emotional states [1]" [PAGE 2] is an explicit bet that spontaneity survives the observer — which the observer effect guarantees it does not.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Vetoes are ordered by precedence; the earliest layer that fires overrides all downstream claims.

**Layer 1 — Photometric veto (highest precedence).** Per O8, required illuminance for a target SNR is $E_{\min}=\pi\sigma_n\,10^{\mathrm{SNR}^\ast/20}/(\rho\,\mu_{\text{ref}})$. Two failure modes: (i) *under-light* — $E<E_{\min}$, shot-noise dominated; (ii) *over-light/saturation* — NIR eye-trackers under direct solar illumination see the solar spectral radiance swamp the corneal glint, and $\|\mathbf{v}\|\to$ noise floor, triggering O2's reciprocal singularity. This veto is exactly what the source concedes: "unstable or diverse conditions can severely compromise the accuracy of vision-based recognition systems [97]" [PAGE 17]. **Proof of precedence:** no downstream algorithm compensates for absent photons. A transformer cannot recover information that the sensor never recorded.

**Layer 2 — Biomechanical veto.** Saccade peak velocity scales with amplitude (main sequence), $\dot{\theta}_{\max}\approx\dot{\theta}_0(1-e^{-\theta/\theta_c})$; at $\theta=20°$ the eye moves in ~50–60 ms. Sampling at $f_s$ must satisfy $f_s>2\dot{\theta}_{\max}/\theta_{\text{res}}$ to avoid aliasing fixation structure. Blink rate (~15–20 min⁻¹, each 100–400 ms) imposes an occlusion duty cycle $\delta_{\text{blink}}\approx0.03$–$0.10$; with $\mathbf{R}\to\infty$ during that fraction (O4), the inertial dead-reckoning drift budget is set by $\delta_{\text{blink}}$. Tremor and hand anthropometry cap gesture-class discriminability: the achievable between-class distance in feature space is bounded by physiological variability, not by model capacity. This is the operator under "sensor biocompatibility, stability, and the robustness of signal acquisition [14]" [PAGE 5].

**Layer 3 — Weather/environmental veto.** For outdoor camera systems, atmospheric extinction follows Koschmieder: apparent contrast $C=C_0e^{-\beta z}$ with $\beta$ the extinction coefficient [m⁻¹] (fog, rain, snow, dust). **Breakdown:** $C$ falls below the detector's contrast threshold at range $z^\ast=\beta^{-1}\ln(C_0/C_{\min})$ — the meteorological-visibility limit. Rain additionally injects motion-blur-like streak artifacts that corrupt optical-flow features. The source's automotive section — "driver monitoring systems use facial and gaze detection to ensure safety" [PAGE 15] and ITS/smart-city framing [PAGE 14] — is a *cabin-internal* claim; the veto fires at the moment the same stack is proposed for exterior perception.

**Layer 4 — Display/optical veto.** Vergence–accommodation conflict $\Delta D=|d_v^{-1}-d_a^{-1}|$ [diopters, m⁻¹]; PPD shortfall per O7; persistence and motion-to-photon per O5.

| Emotion recognition with "high levels of accuracy" [PAGE 2] | Photometric + cultural-distribution shift | Survives only under controlled indoor illuminance and matched demographics |

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Each scenario is a *verifiable* reward: a deterministic, machine-checkable predicate that can be computed from logged data, so a model cannot score by rhetoric.

**RLVR-V1 — Illuminance sweep (photometric veto, falsifiable).**
*Intervention:* freeze model weights, subject set, gesture vocabulary, and camera; sweep illuminance $E\in\{10,30,100,300,1000,3000,10000\}$ lux at fixed $\rho$; log accuracy $\hat{p}(E)$ with $n\ge500$ per cell.
*Prediction:* $\hat{p}(E)$ is non-monotone with an interior maximum; accuracy at $E=10$ lux and at $E=10{,}000$ lux both fall at least 15 percentage points below the peak, and the low-$E$ arm's degradation tracks the O8 SNR prediction within 3 dB.
*Verifier:* compare measured $\hat{p}(E)$ curve to the model curve $\hat{p}_{\text{pred}}(E)$ derived from measured $\sigma_n(E)$; reward $=1$ iff monotone-decrease-from-peak holds and the $E=10$ lux deficit $\ge15$ pp. *Falsified if* accuracy is flat within ±3 pp across the full sweep — which would refute the source's own "unstable or diverse conditions" claim [PAGE 17].

**RLVR-V2 — Redundant-modality placebo (fusion-gain veto).**
*Intervention:* hold the vision encoder fixed; arm A adds a genuinely independent channel (audio or EEG); arm B adds a *shuffled copy* of the vision features (same marginal, zero conditional information). Both arms have identical parameter count and training budget.
*Prediction:* Arm B's accuracy equals the vision-only baseline within ±1 pp; arm A exceeds it by $\Delta>0$; the measured $\Delta$ is bounded above by $H(Y)-\hat{H}(Y\mid X_1)\le\log_2 6=2.585$ bits (O3).
*Verifier:* reward $=1$ iff $|\hat{p}_B-\hat{p}_{\text{vision}}|\le0.01$ AND $\hat{p}_A-\hat{p}_{\text{vision}}>0$ AND the estimated $I(X_2;Y\mid X_1)\le2.585$ bits with a stated estimator. *Falsified if* arm B beats vision-only — which would prove the gain came from regularization/capacity, not complementarity, and would invalidate the source's causal reading of "complementary sensory inputs" [PAGE 17].

**RLVR-V3 — Latency injection in the gaze-to-photon loop (clock veto).**
*Intervention:* in a Fitts-style selection task (the source's [13] paradigm [PAGE 3–4]), keep rendering identical and inject $L\in\{0,10,20,40,80,160\}$ ms of pure delay between gaze sample and rendered frame; collect task time $MT$, error rate, and a standardized simulator-sickness score.
*Prediction:* $MT$ is flat for $L\le20$ ms then rises superlinearly; sickness score increases monotonically with slope change at the $\Delta\phi^\ast$ crossing predicted by O5 at typical head $\omega$; the interaction term (sickness × $L$) has a detectable breakpoint.
*Verifier:* piecewise-linear fit with a breakpoint estimator; reward $=1$ iff the breakpoint lies in $[10,40]$ ms with a bootstrap CI excluding $\{0\}$ and excluding $\ge80$ ms. *Falsified if* no breakpoint is detectable in that window — which would mean the source's "potential motion sickness from latency issues" [PAGE 11] is not locally identifiable and the latency budget is not the controlling variable.

**Composite reward note.** The three verifiers are independent (photometry, information theory, psychophysics) and jointly adversarial: a system cannot satisfy V2 by degrading arm A, cannot satisfy V3 by inflating baseline variance, and cannot satisfy V1 by reporting only the peak. All three are computable from logs the source never publishes — which is precisely the audit gap.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
