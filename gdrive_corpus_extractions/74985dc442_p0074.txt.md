This is a habit regression to the physical layer because material value is intersubjectively stable while aesthetic value requires recursive belief chains ([PAGE 129]).

Counter-trap: A sophisticated agent may speculate that a depressed collectible’s façon will rebound when a new craze emerges, buying illiquid objects at pawn-level material prices. This counter-trap fails if the buyer cohort has permanently migrated or fallen below critical mass; Wiener’s “bottom may drop out of the market” describes a coordination failure in which no agent expects another to value the workmanship, so none bids ([PAGE 129]).

Clock: Wiener implicitly contrasts two clocks. The thermodynamic clock runs one way—entropy increases, information decreases—and is non-cyclical. The fashion clock runs cyclically, with crazes rising and supplanting one another, but its cycles are not conserved and carry no guarantee of return ([PAGE 128–129]). Recursive theory of mind: Agent A purchases for façon because A believes B values it; B believes C values it; C believes a museum or future collector values it. This common-knowledge chain collapses if any node defects to material-only valuation. Crisis accelerates the regression; the clock of buyer-cohort attrition (\(N \to N/4\) or \(N/5\)) determines whether the recursive belief chain repairs itself or reaches the absorbing state of no competitive purchaser.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Wiener’s transmission-line analogy treats electrical energy as a near-conserved commodity, but information is physical. Any model that treats information as an immaterial, perfectly conserved commodity is vetoed by thermodynamics: bit manipulation and erasure incur Landauer costs (\(kT \ln 2\) joules per bit), and communication channels are bounded by noise and capacity, not by conservation. Wiener’s own “minute losses” caveat ([PAGE 128]) already admits that physical transmission breaks exact additivity.

Biomechanical layer veto: The “active group of buyers” is bounded by human attention, appraisal expertise, and social-network reach. Wiener’s market collapse depends on the cognitive limit of \(N_{\text{buyers}}\); a depression dividing the group by four or five is not merely a price shift but a reduction in the number of agents capable of verifying façon, authenticity, and historical value ([PAGE 129]).

Weather layer veto: Although Wiener does not discuss meteorology, his transmission-line and market examples are exposed to exogenous shocks. Electrical transmission suffers environmental losses; collector markets suffer “depression” events that fragment buyer cohorts. A weather or macroeconomic shock that removes competitive purchasers triggers the same \(V_{\text{façon}} \to 0\) singularity as a line fault removes delivered energy.

Adjudication: Any valuation model asserting perfect additivity, conservation, or transfer-value retention for information, fashion, or collector goods is vetoed at the physical layer by non-conservation of entropy/information and at the market layer by buyer-cohort bifurcation. Wiener’s framework is valid only for the conserved material substrate (gold, matter, approximately energy) and invalid for the façon/information superstructure once \(N_{\text{buyers}}\) approaches the singularity.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Digital scarcity token: A non-fungible digital artifact is marketed as a conserved commodity because its ledger entry is unique. Veto: Information is non-conserved (\(dI/dt \le 0\)) and copyable; scarcity is enforced artificially, not by physics. Prediction: If the cost of perfect verification falls to zero and the active buyer cohort fragments by a Wiener factor \(k \in \{4,5\}\), the artifact’s price converges to its material substrate cost (near zero for purely digital goods), independent of claimed rarity.

Scenario 2 — Vintage instrument collector market: A class of mid-century audio equipment is held as a façon asset. Veto: \(V_{\text{façon}}\) is not extensive in the buyer population; demographic aging that divides \(N_{\text{buyers}}\) by four or fifty-two weeks produces a liquidity singularity. Prediction: If new-entrant buyer count falls below replacement for two consecutive quarters, median resale value declines by more than half within twelve months, even for objects with unchanged physical condition.

Scenario 3 — Kilowatt-hour energy derivative: A financial instrument assumes electrical energy is perfectly additive across space and time, so one unit at node A equals one unit at node B. Veto: Transmission losses and peak/off-peak non-fungibility violate exact conservation and additivity ([PAGE 128]). Prediction: The price differential between generation and delivery nodes equals marginal transmission loss plus congestion cost; any model assuming exact equality across nodes is physically false and will fail under load variations.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
