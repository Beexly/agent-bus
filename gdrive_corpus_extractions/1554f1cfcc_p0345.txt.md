<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage of this formalism traces back to Edward L. Thorndike's Law of Effect (1898), which posited that responses followed by satisfying states become strengthened. Clark L. Hull synthesized this into a neobehaviorist system, introducing mediating variables ($sH_r$, $D$, $V_s$) to make psychology a deductive-nomological science. Figure 77 is a quintessential artifact of this era, attempting to graphically resolve the "learning curve" problem by separating acquisition (upper curve) from resistance to extinction (lower curve).

However, a retrocausal analysis reveals hidden traps spanning decades:
1.  **The Drive Reduction Trap (1940s-1960s):** Hull assumed all reinforcement was primary (biologically significant). The discovery of secondary reinforcement (Mowrer, 1940) and later cognitive maps (Tolman, 1948) showed that $E_r$ could be high even with $D \approx 0$ if $V_s$ was strong. This broke the direct $D$-$E_r$ coupling.
2.  **The Linear Assumption Trap (1970s-1990s):** Rescorla-Wagner (1972) demonstrated that learning is delta-rule based ($\Delta V = \alpha \beta (\lambda - \sum V)$), not cumulative. The "number of reinforcements $N$" is less important than the *surprise* or prediction error at each step. Hull's smooth, monotonic $sH_r(N)$ curve fails to capture sudden shifts in associative strength when cues become redundant.
3.  **The Information-Theoretic Blind Spot (2000s-Present):** Modern neuroscience frames habits as policies optimized for expected reward, encoded in basal ganglia circuits. The "habit strength" is a Q-value, not a static bond. The "number of unreinforced trials $n$" reflects the time to update the value function, which depends on learning rate and exploration-exploitation trade-offs, not just initial $E_r$.

A multi-week trap emerges in contemporary AI safety: Over-reliance on reinforcement learning (RL) agents trained in simulated environments may create "Hullian" agents that persist in outdated behaviors ($n$ is too high) when deployed in reality, because they lack meta-learning to detect distribution shifts. Their $V_s$ remains high for stimuli that no longer predict reward, leading to catastrophic failure—a direct violation of the "constant conditions" assumption.</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
