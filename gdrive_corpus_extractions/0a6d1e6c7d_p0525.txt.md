Breakdown/singularity regimes: (i) afferent blockade — \(\Psi(W) \to 0\) and \(d' \to 0\); (ii) rubber-band force exceeds finger force ceiling \(F_{\text{required}} \ge F_{\max}\), task failure; (iii) screen non-planarity or \(\theta\) deviating from \(90^\circ\) makes "corresponding spots" ill-defined; (iv) multi-week motor learning or rubber stress relaxation changes \(e\) or \(k_r\) independently of \(I\), contaminating the null.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week traps.

Lineage on [PAGE 615]: James (1890), Principles of Psychology Vol. II, "WILL," p. 615, cites M. Gley quoting Dr. Bloch; Gley's reference is "Ibid. xx. 604" and "Revue Philosophique, xxıII. 442." James also invokes Herr Sternberg (Pfüger's Archiv, xxxvı, p. 1) and his own earlier discussion on pp. 105–6, citing Exner's hard-rubber bite paradox. The deeper genealogy is the Helmholtzian/Wundtean debate over the "feeling of innervation" (innervationsgefühl) versus the "feeling of resistance": Exner's dental illusion (front teeth seem to approach although hard rubber physically prevents it) already dissociates spatial kinaesthetic report from mechanical reality; Bloch extends this by showing spatial position matching survives passive carriage and increased innervation current; James uses Bloch to undercut Sternberg's claim that willing a movement generally produces the thought that it is made. Thus the chain runs Exner's proprioceptive mismatch → Sternberg's agency inference → Bloch's controlled screen/pressing experiments → Gley's synthesis → James's Vol. II argument that consciousness of innervation adds nothing to spatial position sense.

Multi-week traps: (1) Practice and cerebellar adaptation: repeated screen matching across sessions lowers \(e\) through motor learning independently of innervation, so a later emergent active/passive difference could reflect learning history rather than efference. (2) Suggestion decay: the somnambulist's "no weight" report on [PAGE 615] is state-dependent; hypnotic depth and affective set may drift over weeks, causing the weight-perception singularity to appear and disappear. (3) Viscoelastic drift: India-rubber bands exhibit stress relaxation and creep, so \(k_r(T,t)\) changes over days to weeks even if the nominal band is unchanged; the "physiologically much greater innervation-current" manipulation drifts unless \(k_r\) is re-calibrated each session. (4) Retrospective confabulation (the Sternberg/James trap): across weeks, subjects develop post hoc narratives "I felt the movement made," which corrupts the very conscious efference signal James and Bloch try to isolate; the longer the series, the more the subject's theory of mind about their own agency contaminates the raw measure.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and the clock.

Crisis habit regression: under perceptual uncertainty (eyes bandaged; fingers mechanically opposed by rubber bands; one hand passively moved), subjects regress to the commonsense model that effort, tension, or the "feeling of innervation" signals limb position and resistance. On [PAGE 615] this is exactly the folk psychology Bloch targets: the assumption that greater innervation current ought to alter the consciousness of the movement. Counter-trap: Bloch's design holds the spatial target \(S\) fixed while varying \(I\); because \(e_{\text{active}} \approx e_{\text{passive}}\) and \(e_{\text{rubber}} \approx e_{\text{normal}}\), the regression to "effort equals position" is behaviorally falsified for spatial matching. Recursive theory of mind layer: the experimenter models the subject as using proprioception; the subject models their own matching as voluntary; when one hand is passively carried by an assistant, the subject may still impute agency to that hand. Bloch's result decouples the subject's belief in voluntary control from proprioceptive accuracy: the subject can be wrong about agency while remaining accurate about spatial correspondence.

The clock: define search/matching time \(T_{\text{search}} = t_{\text{endpoint}} - t_{\text{start}}\). The passive hand arrives at an assistant-controlled \(t_{\text{passive}}\), while the active hand seeks. If spatial correspondence is achieved with little extra delay despite passive initiation, the clock shows that proprioceptive target registration can precede or operate independently of efference copy. Crisis-clock interaction: under a novel load (new screen angle, fresh rubber band), \(T_{\text{search}}\) increases as the subject regresses to effort-based search; over repeated trials \(T_{\text{search}}\) ticks down as the subject switches to a proprioceptive feedforward policy. The regression resurfaces only under novel perturbations, and the clock measures the latency of that relapse versus recovery.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Physical layer: [PAGE 615] specifies screen sides at "an angle of about 90°" with spots marked in squares. The geometry imposes a Euclidean constraint \(\lVert \mathbf{s}_L - \mathbf{s}_R \rVert_2\) minimized at the symmetry plane. Veto: if the screen is warped or \(\theta\) deviates materially from \(90^\circ\), "corresponding spots" is ill-defined and the error measure \(e\) loses its reference; Bloch's "about" admits this breakdown regime. The mercury bottle has invariant physical mass \(W = mg > 0\); the subject's report of "no weight" is a perceptual veto (afferent block), not a physical one.

Biomechanical layer: India-rubber bands impose \(F_{\text{band}} = k_r \Delta x\) (N), altering muscle-spindle stretch and Golgi-tendon tension signals while the spatial page-count match is preserved. This vetoes the claim that tension/innervation magnitude is required for spatial position. Breakdown singularity: if \(F_{\text{required}} \ge F_{\max}\) of the finger flexors, the pages cannot be pressed and the task fails; if bands slip or exceed elastic limit, the force-spatial coupling re-enters.

Weather veto: the experiment is indoor psychophysics; no direct meteorological variable enters the screen or book tasks on [PAGE 615]. However, across multi-week seasonal swings, rubber elastic modulus is temperature-dependent, approximately \(k_r(T) = k_{r0}[1 - \alpha (T - T_0)]\), and screen material undergoes thermal expansion \(\Delta L = L\alpha_{\text{mat}}\Delta T\). For a tabletop screen \(L \sim 0.5\ \mathrm{m}\), \(\alpha_{\text{mat}} \sim 5\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}\), and \(\Delta T \sim 10\ \mathrm{K}\), \(\Delta L \sim 25\ \mu\mathrm{m}\)—negligible relative to square size—but rubber tension drift could be perceptible. Within a single session weather is effectively constant, so the veto holds; across weeks, temperature must be logged as a covariate or \(k_r\) re-calibrated, otherwise the biomechanical layer drifts and the Bloch comparison is confounded.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR-style veto scenarios with predictions.

Veto scenario 1 (afferent-channel veto): Repeat Bloch's screen matching with the passively carried hand's proprioceptive afferents blocked (regional anesthesia) while the other hand actively matches. Verifiable metric: \(e = \lVert \mathbf{s}_L - \mathbf{s}_R \rVert_2\) in cm on square grid. Prediction under Bloch's null (position independent of innervation): \(e_{\text{blocked}} \approx e_{\text{normal}}\) within measurement resolution (equivalence bound \(\pm 0.5\ \mathrm{cm}\), \(p > 0.05\)). Veto condition: if blocking afferents significantly raises \(e\), then spatial position depends on afferent proprioception; this does not rescue innervation but refines the channel and verifies that Bloch's null depends on intact afferent signalling.

Veto scenario 2 (efference-load veto): Add a calibrated wrist mass \(m_{\text{cuff}}\) (kg) to the actively matching hand, raising required innervation current \(I\) while the target squares remain unchanged. Verifiable metric: slope of \(e\) versus \(m_{\text{cuff}}\) in cm kg\(^{-1}\). Prediction under Bloch: \(de/dm \approx 0\) within error; spatial match is invariant to load. Veto: a statistically significant positive slope (e.g., \(de/dm > 0.1\ \mathrm{cm\,kg^{-1}}\), \(p < 0.01\)) falsifies the independence of spatial position from innervation/effort and overturns Bloch's specific conclusion.

Veto scenario 3 (efference-correlation/prism veto): Rotate one screen side by angle \(\phi\) (radians) relative to the other while keeping square labels fixed to the surface, or impose a visuomotor rotation. Verifiable metrics: endpoint angular error \(\delta\theta\) vs \(\phi\), and trial-by-trial correlation of \(\delta\theta\) with an efference proxy such as EMG amplitude. Prediction under a pure proprioceptive spatial channel: offset tracks the physical rotation of the screened surface and is uncorrelated with trial-by-trial motor-command variance. Veto: if \(\delta\theta\) scales with EMG/efference variability rather than with \(\phi\), then the conscious feeling of innervation is contributing to the spatial report after all, falsifying Bloch's separation of innervation feeling from limb-position sense.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
