<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage, plus multi-week observational traps.

Tolman's 1932 glossary sits at a causal crossroads in behavior theory. Looking backward, the means-end field inherits from Gestalt psychology (Köhler, 1917/1925) the insight that organisms perceive relations and configurations, not merely isolated stimuli; the sign-gestalt emphasis on both termini echoes Gestalt figure–ground and structural wholes. The chain succession formalizes Thorndike's (1898/1911) trial-and-error chaining and the law of effect: commerce with one means-object brings about the next, a successive-linking of acts reinforced by outcomes. The lever/tool succession directly engages Köhler's ape tool-use experiments, where inserting a stick or stacking boxes creates a coupled means-ends structure in which one manipulation simultaneously affects another object relation.

Looking forward, Tolman's field anticipates the cognitive map (Tolman, 1948), latent learning, and expectancy-based accounts of purposive behavior. The typed relation set \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{type}}\) prefigures successor representations in reinforcement learning, where states are valued by their predictive occupancy of future states. The capacity clause—formal and dimensional limits on the field—foreshadows bounded rationality and, much later, active inference and the free-energy principle, in which organisms maintain generative models of environmental structure within finite computational and energetic bounds. Rescorla–Wagner (1972) can be read as a local instantiation of \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ME}}\): the means–goal expectancy is updated by prediction error. Modern predictive coding treats \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SG}}\) as the binding of sensory sign to latent cause under a generative model.

Multi-week traps. A short observation window (days) cannot establish that a full means-end field determines behavior; Tolman's determinacy condition requires demonstrating that the total complex, including rejected means-objects and all relations, governs the sequence. Over one to two weeks, an observer may mistake a short chain for a complete field, or conflate a lever succession with a chain because the simultaneity of tool ends is not resolved at the sampling resolution. Readiness \(\rho\) may be misread as motivation or drive when it is actually a capacity–field match threshold. Finally, because field complexity depends on both environment and organism capacities [PAGE 450], a two-week field map in one environment does not transfer; changing the objective situation can reveal previously latent relations (reverse ends, multiple trackness) and collapse the earlier inference. Longitudinal, multi-environment observation across weeks is therefore required to avoid the trap of overfitting a local act sequence to a nonexistent global field.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Under crisis—high stakes, time pressure, or environmental perturbation—the organism's means-end field undergoes habit regression: dimensionality collapses. High-complexity lever abstractions and multi-track fields are abandoned in favor of proximal chain successions, each link tied to an immediately available sign-object. The readiness threshold \(\theta_{\mathrm{org}}\) effectively rises; only high-certainty \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ME}}\) links fire. Behavior becomes serial, local, and repetitive: a regress to the lowest-complexity subgraph of \(\mathbb{F}\) that still offers a perceived path to \(g^*\). If no such subgraph exists, the null-path singularity triggers freezing or displacement activity.

The counter-trap is the observer's inverse error: assuming the organism is using a lever succession when it has regressed to a chain, or assuming a chain when the organism is maintaining a coupled tool field. In experimental design, this produces false inference about the subject's cognitive architecture. The researcher models the subject's field \(\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{model}}\) while the subject's actual field is \(\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{subject}}\); when crisis forces \(\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{subject}}\) into a low-dimensional chain, \(\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{model}}\) retains lever couplings that no longer obtain, and predictions fail systematically.

The clock introduces temporal-scale separation. Lever successions operate on fast time-scales (\(\Delta t < \tau_{\mathrm{coupling}}\)); chain successions unfold serially over longer durations. Under recursive theory of mind, observer \(O_1\) models subject \(S\)'s means-end field, while \(S\) models \(O_1\)'s field as part of its own environment. If \(O_1\) changes its field (e.g., blocks a path, alters a tool), \(S\) must update \(\mathbb{F}_{S}\). Under crisis, \(S\) cannot sustain the recursive model and regresses to habit. The clock thus bounds the depth of recursive modeling: each recursion level consumes temporal and dimensional capacity; past a critical recursion depth \(d^*\), the field exceeds \(C_{\mathrm{formal}}^{\max}\), and the system collapses to zero-order habit (chain of length one or direct sign–act reflex). Crisis habit regression, counter-trap misidentification, and clock-bound recursion depth are the three failure modes of interpreting Tolmanian fields in live subjects.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proofs.

A means-end field inference is admissible only if it survives veto across physical, biomechanical, and environmental layers.

**Physical veto.** Maintaining a field representation \(\mathbb{F}\) and computing \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ME}}\), \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SG}}\) across relations consumes energy and neural bandwidth. Let \(E_{\mathrm{rep}}(\mathbb{F})\) be the representational cost and \(E_{\mathrm{budget}}\) the organism's available metabolic allocation for the behavior epoch. Veto condition:
\[
E_{\mathrm{rep}}(\mathbb{F}) > E_{\mathrm{budget}} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{reject global-field inference; collapse to local readiness } \rho.
\]
If the goal-object \(g^*\) is physically unreachable (gravity, friction, distance beyond locomotor range), the null-path singularity applies and the field is vetoed regardless of expectancy strength.

**Biomechanical veto.** A lever succession presupposes rigid or semi-rigid coupling between means-objects \(m_a, m_b\) such that \(|\Delta t_{ab}| < \tau_{\mathrm{coupling}}\). Tendon compliance, muscle fatigue, or tool deformation increases effective coupling delay. Veto condition:
\[
|\Delta t_{ab}|_{\mathrm{measured}} \ge \tau_{\mathrm{coupling}} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{reject lever model; reclassify succession as chain.}
\]
Serial engagement with separated tool ends under load is chain behavior, not tool behavior, even if the apparatus is nominally a single lever.

\[
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SG}}(s \leftrightarrow \sigma \mid \mathcal{H}_t) < \epsilon_{\mathrm{SG}} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{reject sign-gestalt binding; field collapses to proximal means-end links only.}
\]
Fog, rain, or wind that breaks the sensory contingency between sign and signified invalidates global field determinacy [PAGE 450] because the objective environmental situation no longer supports the relation set \(\mathcal{R}\).

**Adjudication rule.** An inference that a behavior sequence is determined by a means-end field is accepted only if all three veto conditions are false: energy sufficient, biomechanical coupling intact, and sign–signified binding above noise floor. Otherwise the explanation must be downshifted—field to chain, chain to readiness, readiness to reflex—until a layer survives the veto.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios with predictions.

**Scenario 1: Blocked-lever detour in a rat T-maze.** A rat is trained that pulling a lever (means-object \(m_a\)) raises a gate to goal \(g^*\). In the probe, the lever is mechanically disconnected from the gate but a second, chain-like route (series of two doors) remains. Prediction under Tolmanian field logic: if the rat represents the lever as part of a tool/lever succession, it will show a single, continuous attempt to depress \(m_a\) expecting simultaneous gate opening; when that fails, it should either abandon the field or switch to the chain route. Verifiable veto: if the rat serially re-engages \(m_a\) repeatedly (head-sweeps, repeated presses) with inter-press intervals \(> \tau_{\mathrm{coupling}}\), the lever model is falsified and chain regression is confirmed. RLVR metric: press recurrence rate \(> 0.5\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) for \(>30\,\mathrm{s}\) vetoes the lever succession and predicts chain-switch within 60 s.

**Scenario 2: Visibly decoupled tool in chimpanzee extractive foraging.** A chimpanzee is given a rake-like tool with two handles separated by a visible slack joint such that moving handle A does not move handle B. Prediction: a lever succession predicts \(|\Delta t_{AB}| < 1\,\mathrm{s}\) (pari passu commerce [PAGE 450]); a chain succession predicts sequential grasping with \(|\Delta t_{AB}| \gg 1\,\mathrm{s}\). Verifiable veto: high-speed video measurement of handle engagement latencies. If median \(|\Delta t_{AB}| > 5\,\mathrm{s}\), the field relation is chain, not lever; the inference that the rake is a single coupled means-object is vetoed. Prediction of behavior after veto: the subject will treat handle A and handle B as separate superordinate/subordinate means-objects in a chain, attempting each sequentially rather than simultaneously.

**Scenario 3: Human navigator in dense fog (visibility < 5 m).** A human attempts to navigate to a known goal in an outdoor terrain under zero-visibility fog, removing distal sign-objects. Prediction: the means-end field loses distant relations (direction, distance to goal); \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SG}}\) for global sign–signified pairs drops below \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{SG}}\). Verifiable veto: GPS/telemetry shows path tortuosity increasing and directness index (straight-line distance / path length) falling below 0.4, while local obstacle-avoidance chains persist. The global field is vetoed by the environmental layer; behavior collapses to local sign-gestalts (immediate tactile/ proximal visual signs) with readiness \(\rho\) gating each step. Prediction after veto: upon fog lifting, directness index recovers within one decision epoch, demonstrating field re-instatement rather than permanent capacity loss.

All three scenarios are falsifiable: if the measured latency, tortuosity, or recurrence remains within the predicted bounds of the intact model, the veto condition is not triggered and the Tolmanian field inference stands; if the thresholds are crossed, the corresponding layer vetoes the model and forces a downshift in explanatory complexity.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
