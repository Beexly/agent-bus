<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression appears when staffs under injury pressure revert to personally trusted but sub-optimally positioned players. Chattanooga moved Woodworth, "the star quarter," to half and inserted Scott at quarter [PAGE 237]. This is a regression to a known ball-handler under stress. The counter-trap is opponent adaptation: if Cumberland or future opponents key on Woodworth as a quarter, his presence at half changes the pre-snap read; conversely, if they anticipate the shift, Scott's inexperience at quarter becomes the exploitable node. The source notes Scott "played a great game on Thanksgiving Day against Cumberland," which resolves the immediate counter-trap but does not establish scheme stability [PAGE 237].

Recursive belief modeling applies to Chicago's Minnesota game. Minnesota knew Gray was out entirely; Illinois-style logic suggests Minnesota might key on Russell's hip passes, yet Chicago "made a wonderful showing" until the last quarter [PAGE 237]. The recursive layer is: Chicago believes Minnesota expects a degraded passing game; Minnesota believes Chicago believes that; Chicago runs anyway with Russell throwing from the hip. The clock intervenes through Des Jardien's two-week hospital stay [PAGE 237]. If \(t_M\) is the week of the Minnesota game, Des Jardien's return clock is \(t_M+2\) weeks; because the decisive injury occurred in the last quarter, the clock did not permit reintegration within the game, and the captaincy/physical node remained vacant during the Gophers' winning score [PAGE 237].

Spencer's one-third-season availability [PAGE 237] creates a fractional reintegration clock: if Chattanooga regresses to him after two-thirds of the season, timing chemistry with Dexheimer and the backfield may have decayed, raising re-injury or execution-failure risk. Georgia's recursion is inverse: opponents, knowing only two veterans returned and freshmen were barred, scheduled difficult games early; Georgia survived until late November, then improved, causing 1915 opponents to potentially underestimate a Cunningham-system team regaining depth [PAGE 237]. The governing variable is the clock relative to the crisis: crisis habit regression is viable only if the recovery clock permits reinsertion before critical games; otherwise the team must commit to replacements and accept permanent scheme degradation for that season.
