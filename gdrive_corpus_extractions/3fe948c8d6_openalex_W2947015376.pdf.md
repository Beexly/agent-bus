**1. NLP and KKT (PAGE 20, 22).**
$$\min_{z} L(z)\ \text{s.t.}\ f_i(z)\le 0,\ g_j(z)=0,\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{1,p},\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{1,m}\tag{2.1}$$
Stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity:
$$\nabla_z \mathcal{L}(z^*,\lambda^*,\nu^*)=0,\quad \lambda_i^* f_i(z^*)=0,\quad \lambda_i^*\ge 0\tag{2.5}$$
Breakdown: KKT is only *necessary*; sufficiency requires convexity or the second-order condition (2.7) over the critical cone (2.8) [PAGE 23].

**2. SQP subproblem (PAGE 24).**
$$\min_{p_k}\ \tfrac12 p_k^\top B_k p_k + \nabla L(z_k)^\top p_k\ \text{s.t.}\ \nabla f^\top p_k+f\preceq 0,\ \nabla g^\top p_k+g=0\tag{2.9}$$
$B_k=\nabla^2_{zz}\mathcal{L}_k$ for exact Newton.

**3. Continuous OCP (PAGE 28).**
$$\min_{x(\cdot),u(\cdot),t_f}\ \Phi(x(t_f))+\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\ell(x,u)\,dt\ \text{s.t.}\ \dot x=f(x,u),\ c(x,u)\preceq 0,\ \Psi(x(t_f))=x_{\text{term}}\tag{2.19}$$
Units: $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $u\in\mathbb{R}^m$, $\ell$ in cost-per-second (for path-length cost, m·s⁻¹ integrated over s gives m).

**4. Motion-planning OCP (PAGE 33).**
$$\min\ J=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\ell(x,u)\,dt\ \text{s.t.}\ x(t)\in X_{\text{free}}(t)\cap X_{\text{valid}},\ u(t)\in U\tag{2.29}$$

**5. Homotopy map (PAGE 31).**
$$H(z,\gamma)=\gamma F(z)+(1-\gamma)G(z),\qquad \gamma\in[0,1]\tag{3.1}$$
**Breakdown regime:** if $h$ is not a homeomorphism in the sense of Definition 3.1 [PAGE 32], homotopy classes are created or destroyed and the continuation path no longer tracks the original problem; Figure 3.1 [PAGE 33] shows the punched-sheet counterexample.

**6. SHQP subproblem (PAGE 35).**
$$\min_{p_k}\ \tfrac12 p_k^\top B_k p_k+\nabla L(z_k)^\top p_k\ \text{s.t.}\ \nabla F^\top p_k+F=0,\ \nabla G_c^\top p_k+G_c\preceq 0,\ \nabla H^\top p_k+H(z_k,\gamma)\preceq 0\tag{3.4}$$

**7. Monotone cost under tightening (PAGE 34).**
$$J(x_k^*,u_k^*,\gamma_k)\ \ge\ J(x_{k-1}^*,u_{k-1}^*,\gamma_{k-1}),\qquad \gamma_k>\gamma_{k-1}\tag{3.3}$$
with units of the chosen objective (meters for path length). This holds *only* if the homotopy is a monotone relaxation; it is an assumption, not a theorem, in the general case.

**8. Super-ellipsoid obstacle (PAGE 38).**
$$\left(\frac{x-x_c}{r_x}\right)^{k}+\left(\frac{y-y_c}{r_y}\right)^{k}+\left(\frac{z-z_c}{r_z}\right)^{k}-1\ \ge\ 0,\quad k=2N\tag{3.5}$$

Invariant: $R+r$ constant, so the outer silhouette never moves; only the hole shrinks to zero at $\gamma=1$.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under nonconvexity, a naive SQP/IP invocation regresses to its habitual default — linear interpolation between endpoints — which is exactly the behavior the thesis diagnoses at [PAGE 20] and demonstrates as catastrophic in Figure 2.3 [PAGE 21], where the simplified-model initialization is "not homotopic to a feasible solution which will make it impossible for the nlp solver to converge." The habit is not a bug in the solver; it is the solver faithfully doing what it was told with an uninformative prior.

**Counter-trap.** The homotopy schedule is the counter-trap: it removes the temptation to solve the hard problem directly by *constructing* an easy problem whose solution is a valid starting point. The homeomorphism constraint at [PAGE 32] is the guardrail that prevents the counter-trap from becoming a new trap — i.e., prevents the relaxation from quietly deleting the obstacle. The second counter-trap is the warm-start from the lattice planner of Chapter 5, which is "not only feasible, but also optimized using the same objective function as in the improvement step" [PAGE 65].

**Clock.** The controlling clock is Δγ. Figure 3.3 [PAGE 38] is an explicit two-panel demonstration that the same algorithm, same obstacle, same problem succeeds under a small step and fails under a large one. The chosen values — Δγ=0.02 for P1 and P3, Δγ=0.01 for P2 and P4 [PAGE 41] — track problem hardness (maze and torus get the finer clock). The second clock is the horizon $N$ (30 for P1, 40 for P2–P4). A third, subtler clock is the usage-weight schedule $\mathbb{E}(k_i)$ in (4.10) [PAGE 54], which lets the primitive set be re-optimized as online statistics accumulate — a slow loop over weeks of deployment, not over milliseconds.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Four veto layers are physically non-negotiable and cannot be optimized away by any objective function.

**Layer 1 — kinematics/nonholonomy.** The car "is unable to move sideways or rotate in place" [PAGE 18]; the constraint set is nonholonomic, so no amount of homotopy makes a lateral slip feasible. This is why the steering angle bound $|\alpha|\le\pi/4$ [PAGE 40] bites: it caps curvature at $\tan(\pi/4)/L_1$, so a truck with $L_1=4.66$ m cannot execute the same heading change as a car with $L_1=2.5$ m within the same lateral footprint. Table 4.3 [PAGE 76] quantifies the cross-layer veto directly: using car-optimized connectivity on a truck gives 1.14× cost; on truck-and-trailer the primitive generation *fails outright* by infeasibility.

**Layer 2 — instability (truck-trailer backward).** Verbatim [PAGE 56]: "in backward motion the dynamics for the joint angles $\beta_3$ and $\beta_2$ are unstable, while they are stable in forward motion." This is a Lyapunov-type veto on the numerical procedure: shooting backward through an unstable vector field amplifies initial-condition error exponentially, so backward primitives *must* be computed forward from the reversed endpoint under (4.15). The jackknife penalty in (4.17) [PAGE 58] is the soft version of the same veto.

**Layer 3 — geometric homotopy class.** The torus of Figure 3.4(d) [PAGE 43] has "a minimum radius of 0.5 meter" — the only admissible route is through the hole. This is a topological veto, and it explains the 13% SST success rate [PAGE 57]: random sampling has near-zero probability of threading a 0.5 m gap in a 3D state space with seven dimensions. SHQP's 100% is a consequence of the homeomorphic radial homotopy (3.10), which shrinks the hole continuously rather than asking the sampler to find it.

**Layer 4 — numerical conditioning.** Higher super-ellipsoid exponent $k$ sharpens edges but "cause[s] the linear approximation of the constraints used in the sqp iterations to be more numerically sensitive" [PAGE 38]. The thesis's mitigation is to keep $k=4$ in the numerical examples. This is a *self-imposed* veto on expressiveness in exchange for conditioning — an honest engineering trade, not a theorem.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
