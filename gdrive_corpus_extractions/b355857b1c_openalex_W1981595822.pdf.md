Fifth hop: evasion. χ²(1,N=13)=34.71, p<.001 [PAGE 6]; 9/13 failures were run-at-then-cut. The angular gain from a lateral cut of size Δ scales like Δ/r (r = range); as r shrinks the same physical cut injects a larger dθ spike than the pursuer's re-lock latency can null. This is why "run faster and away" fails: at large r, no achievable lateral velocity produces a material dθ/dt, so the pursuer's constant-bearing lock holds.

</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

All operators from Shaffer & Gregory (2009); θ is the target-heading angle, P the pursuer heading, B the carrier heading, a = a(0) initial separation, vP/vB speeds, ti interception time.

Interception / Law-of-Sines constraint [PAGE 3]:
$$\sin(P) = \frac{v_B \sin(B)}{v_P} \quad (1)$$
Units: dimensionless. Breakdown regime: no real solution iff $v_B \sin B > v_P$; at equality $P=90°$ (grazing), consistent with the [PAGE 2] statement that a collision course holds "as long as θ is less than ninety degrees."

Kinematics [PAGE 3]:
$$\mathbf{r}_B(t) = (a + t v_B \cos B,\; t v_B \sin B)\quad(2),\qquad \mathbf{r}_P(t) = (t v_P \cos P,\; t v_P \sin P)\quad(3)$$

Squared separation [PAGE 3], which I re-derived and verified:
$$D^2(t) = t^2(v_P^2+v_B^2) + a^2 - 2t^2 v_P v_B \cos(P-B) + 2ta\,(v_B\cos B - v_P\cos P)\quad(5)$$

Rate of change (corrected; the printed (6) mis-factors $t$ and the constant term):
$$\frac{d(D^2)}{dt} = 2t(v_P^2+v_B^2) - 4t v_P v_B \cos(P-B) - 2a(v_P\cos P - v_B\cos B)$$

Optimality condition $\partial/\partial P = 0$ [PAGE 3] (verified against printed (7)):
$$4t v_P v_B \sin(P-B) + 2a v_P \sin P = 0\quad(7)$$

Closed-form optimal heading [PAGE 3], (8)-(9) verified:
$$\tan(P) = \frac{t v_B \sin B}{t v_B \cos B + a/2}\quad(8),\qquad \tan(P) = \frac{\sin B}{\cos B + \dfrac{a}{2d}},\;\; d = t v_B\quad(9)$$

Asymptotic structure: $t\to 0 \Rightarrow P\to 0$; $t\to\infty \Rightarrow P\to B^-$; strict inequality $P<B$ since $a/(2d)>0$ [PAGE 3-4].

Empirical estimators (units rad·s⁻¹ when converted):
$$\bar\theta = 50.11° = 0.875\ \text{rad},\quad \overline{|d\theta/dt|} = 1.66°/.17\text{s} \approx 0.174\ \text{rad/s},\quad \langle d\theta/dt\rangle_{\text{signed}}: t(810)=0.54.$$

Angular-gain operator (evasion): for a lateral cut Δ at range r, injected bearing rate $\dot\theta \sim v_\perp / r$; monotone divergence as $r\to 0$ — the [PAGE 7] mechanism.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage is a 70-year migration of one equation out of weapons fire-control into behavioral ecology. Reference [16] Yuan (1948), "Homing and navigation courses of automatic target-seeking devices," is the torp/do-vs-lead origin; the paper explicitly credits differential-calculus models of "launching torpedoes at ships" [PAGE 2] for showing CTHA superiority over homing. This is the same constant-bearing-decreasing-range collision test mariners and pilots use [PAGE 1, ref 12], reformulated as an active-guidance law rather than a passive hazard detector.

The second strand is the pursuit-curve tradition (refs [1-3]: Barton & Eliezer 2000; da Vinci's cat-and-mouse, Guha & Biswas 1994). Pure pursuit (θ=0) traces the classical curve; CTHA is the lead-angle generalization. The paper's contribution is to pit these against each other behaviorally in a non-ballistic, reactive target — filling the gap it names on [PAGE 4]: "All previous experiments… have either been done naturalistically or by using targets with ballistic paths."

The third strand is the perception literature: Fajen & Warren (2004, 2007) [refs 13,15] on behavioral dynamics of interception, and the "composite tau" cluster [refs 27-30] closing the loop to Lee-style time-to-contact. The retro-causal trap for a modern reader: it is tempting to read the 50.11° mean θ as a fixed piloting constant. It is not — it is an emergent value of tan(P) from (9) under this field's a (18.3 m), the sampled B ∈ {20°,40°,60°,80°}, and mid-pursuit d. Reusing "50°" as a universal coefficient across different separations or speeds is the multi-week modeling error this genealogy warns against.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model the encounter as nested controllers with unequal latencies. Level-0 (pursuer's onboard law): null signed $d\theta/dt$ while holding $\theta>0$; empirically $\langle d\theta/dt\rangle$ not different from 0, t(810)=0.54 [PAGE 6]. Level-1 (carrier's exploit): the pursuer's lock has a finite re-acquisition latency — after a mid-pursuit change "the pursuer established a new target-heading angle, and then maintained the change in the new target-heading angle equal to zero" [PAGE 6-7], and in 40% of cuts "the target-heading angle itself changed significantly after the cut." That transient is the exploitable window.

Crisis habit regression: under maximal time-to-contact pressure (the "clock," composite tau [PAGE 8]), the naive regression is flee — increase r and speed away. The data veto this: only 2 of 13 failures were the flee-with-speedup pattern, while run-at-then-cut produced 9 of 13, χ²(1,13)=34.71, p<.001 [PAGE 6]. The counter-trap that survives is inverting the naive policy: close range first (small r), then a ±20° cut, because injected $\dot\theta \sim v_\perp/r$ diverges as r→0, overwhelming the pursuer's re-lock. Recursion depth matters: a pursuer who anticipates the cut (level-2) re-nulls faster; but the paper's own subjects showed "no obvious signs… they could correctly anticipate" [PAGE 4], so the level-2 tower was empirically shallow, which is precisely why the level-1 exploit paid.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/biomechanical veto (proof by contradiction of the flee policy): to defeat a constant-bearing lock at large r by running away, the carrier would need lateral velocity sufficient to drive $\dot\theta = v_\perp/r$ past the pursuer's null bandwidth. At r on the order of 18 m, human $v_\perp$ (≤ ~3 m/s component) yields $\dot\theta \lesssim 0.17\ \text{rad/s}$, at or below the pursuer's own operating rate (0.174 rad/s), so the lock is not broken. Hence the mathematical model's own remark holds: a slower pursuer "might still be able to achieve interception by making angle P greater" [PAGE 8] — speed is not the decisive layer; bearing rate is.

Speed-layer veto (interception feasibility): CTHA requires $v_P \ge v_B\sin B$. Constant-speed regime: $v_B=5.82$, $v_P=5.53$ m/s; at B=80°, $v_B\sin B = 5.73 > 5.53$ → constraint (1) unsolvable → carrier unbeatable by pure geometry in that cell. This adjudicates the 2 farthest-away/speed-up failures as regime-mandated, not skill noise.

Weather/traction veto: the study ran on a marked high-school surface (22.87 m × 22.87 m grid) [PAGE 4] with no wind/traction covariates recorded. Any wet-surface or crosswind condition would degrade the ±20° cut's realized $v_\perp$ and inflate re-lock latency — strengthening the run-at-then-cut advantage, but also widening the paper's stated measurement error ("potential error equivalent to 0.3 m in-depth and 0.3 m laterally… for every 0.95 m moved," [PAGE 4-5]). Near that error floor, small-r cut trials risk coding artifacts; the veto flags the r < ~1.5 m regime as measurement-limited.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three falsifiable, verifiable scenarios derived from (1) and (9):

VETO-1 (speed-geometry floor): Construct trials with $v_B \sin B > v_P$ (e.g., B=80°, $v_P=5.5$, $v_B=6.0$ m/s), carrier straight-line, no cut. Prediction: CTHA interception rate → 0%; only classic-predictive or lucky contact. Verifiable against eq (1): $\sin P = 6.0\cdot0.985/5.5 = 1.07 > 1$. Falsified if any straight-line intercept occurs without the pursuer raising $v_P$ or the carrier lowering effective B.

VETO-2 (lead-angle vs speed ordering): Hold $v_B, B$ fixed, vary $v_P$ upward. From (8), optimal $\tan P$ decreases as $v_P$ rises, and closing rate $-dD/dt$ increases → interception time $t_i$ falls. Prediction: measured $t_i$ strictly decreasing in $v_P$, with intercept P-angle distribution shifting below the 50.11° mean. Falsified if faster pursuers do not intercept earlier or do not reduce mean θ.

VETO-3 (range-gated evasion): Fix cut magnitude ±20°, vary cut-onset range r. Prediction: evasion success monotone increasing as r→0 because injected $\dot\theta \sim v_\perp/r$ diverges; success at r ≈ 18 m should approximate baseline (~11% failure), rising sharply under r ≈ 3 m. Falsified if cut effectiveness is range-invariant, which would contradict the [PAGE 7] angular-gain mechanism.

All three are decidable from position-time series with no free parameters, satisfying verifiable-reward criteria.
