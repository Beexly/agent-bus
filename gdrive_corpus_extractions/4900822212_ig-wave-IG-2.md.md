Specialty-clinic sample [PAGE 47] adds referral bias — use the *taxonomy* but not the *prevalences* for a population base rate.

**E8. Cumulative head-impact dose.**
$$D_{\mathrm{cum}} = \int_{t_0}^{t_f} \dot{n}(t)\,\bar{g}(t)\,dt,\quad [\dot{n}]=\mathrm{hit}\cdot\mathrm{s}^{-1},\ [\bar{g}]=\mathrm{g},\ [D]=\mathrm{g}.$$
Wave anchor: verbiage "years of play + total hits + hit-magnitude distribution" as the predictor set, and "the top 10% of hardest hits" as the prevention tail [PAGE 35]. Because $\bar{g}$ is approximately log-normally distributed in the tail, the top decile of magnitudes contributes roughly ~half the cumulative g (for typical σ_ln g ≈ 0.6–0.9). **Breakdown regime:** the model breaks if head-impact rate $\dot n$ declines with age-in-career (older players take fewer reps) or if threshold biology (20–30 g damage onset) is real — in that case the tail-mass attribution should be reweighted by a survival-like step function rather than by raw cumulative g.

**E9. Special-teams points-over-expected.**
$$\mathrm{POE} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \hat y_i),\quad [\mathrm{POE}]=\text{points},\ \mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{POE}]=\sum_i \mathrm{Var}[\hat y_i].$$
Wave anchor: verbatim "+18.6 points over expected, ranked 1st" [PAGE 21]. **Breakdown regime:** with N ≈ 150–200 ST plays per season and per-play variance σ_i of order 0.5–1.0 pts, $\mathrm{SE}[\mathrm{POE}] \approx \sqrt{200\cdot 0.6^2} \approx 8.5$ pts — so +18.6 is ~2.2 SE. Payment is real but not decisive; last-year ST POE predicts this-year at low correlation, which caps its feature weight. This is the correct prior when the reasoning layer consumes IG2-17.

**E10. Two-sided coverage-choice game.**
$$V = \min_{p_c\in \Delta(3)} \max_{q\in Q} \int E[\,\mathrm{EPA}\mid q,c\,]\ dp_c,$$
where $p_c$ ranges over coverage families (two-high, Cover 3, man). Mahomes anchors: +0.22 vs two-high, −0.24 vs Cover 3 [PAGE 20]; Seattle's base is two-high at 54.9% [PAGE 20]. **Breakdown regime:** the operator is only well-posed if the conditional EPAs are estimated with adequate n *per coverage family per QB*; where n is 15–30, the equilibrium over-trusts noise and mis-prices the game. The operator's use here is descriptive (it explains why teams *shift* coverage against specific QBs), not prescriptive.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The IG-2 wave sits at the intersection of two long research lineages, and its internal structure reveals the strain lines.

**Lineage 1 — Boston University CTE brain-bank → dose-response → mortality → modeled prevalence.** The scientific genealogy runs roughly: Omalu/McKee neuropathology (donor brains) → Mez/Daneshvar cohort mortality (eClinicalMedicine, IG2-22) → Daneshvar dose-response (years-of-play, dementia risk, IG2-21) → JAMA Network Open population-modeled prevalence (IG2-23, October 2026). Each step upgrades the *inferential unit* while accepting a stronger selection model. The donor-only estimates bounded prevalence to 25%–98% (per IG-1's BMJ item, referenced PAGE 31); the modeled estimate lands at 75%. The methodological lesson — that donor-only bounds are the honest stopgap, and a donor + non-donor selection model is the upgrade — is the wave's cleanest *methodological* lesson and generalizes to any opt-in dataset (the IG-2 text explicitly makes this generalization for GSE). **Multi-week trap:** the 75% will be cited downstream as "75% of former players have CTE." The wave anticipates this by *explicitly*, in Nowinski's own framing, restricting the claim to "decedents, not living players" [PAGE 31]. The trap is not in this wave's handling of the number; it is in what the next wave will do with it if it does not re-quote the qualifier.

**Lineage 2 — Guardian Cap lab → NFL observational → college mouthguard null → high-school field null.** IG-1 recorded the NXT lab kinematics (−7.9% linear / −14.1% rotational / −34.1% modeled risk) and the NFL observational (−54–62% practice concussions, benefit evaporating for shell impacts) and the 2025 college mouthguard study (7,509 impacts, null) — see IG1-25's improved framing [PAGE 3–4]. IG-2 adds the progression's endpoint: Hammer et al. BJSM 2025 and Funk et al. AJSM 2025, the **2,600-player** high-school field null [PAGE 37]. The scientific lineage is *Lab → Pro → Field → Youth*, each step trading kinematic purity for real-world validity, and the evidentiary grade collapsing at each step is the point. **The strong internal claim** in IG-2 is not "caps don't work" but "research was on the NFL-grade model; many youth-sold versions aren't that model" [PAGE 37] — the generalizable consumer-protection pattern of *evidence generated on the premium artifact, marketed across the whole product line*. That pattern is a multi-week trap for any future equipment dossier that does not check model-grade against population.

**Lineage 3 — the youth-brain record from two directions.** IG2-26 (Cherry et al., Nature) shows non-CTE damage — fewer neurons, more inflammation, vascular injury — in young contact-sport athletes *without CTE pathology* [PAGE 33]. IG2-28 shows CTE itself in ~40% of under-30 decedent contact athletes (n=152, probable match to the 2023 JAMA Neurology young-athlete study) [PAGE 36]. Together they eliminate both "young brains are fine" readings: some young athletes have CTE already; others have non-CTE damage; the phrase "no CTE found" no longer means "no damage found." **Multi-week trap:** reading the Nature paper as a claim that contact sports *cause* Alzheimer's/Parkinson's at young ages. The paper's design (tissue-level) supports *association with exposure*, not *causation of specific clinical endpoints*, and the wave's itself uses the "raises later risk" framing (paraphrase) which is the correct evidential weight.

**Lineage 4 — the QB-vs-coverage ontology from two vendors.** The SumerSports/PFF cross-vendor structure in Lane A mirrors the same double-report problem from opposite sides: Sumer's EPA-based splits (IG2-16: Darnold −0.58 vs man) vs PFF's grade-based splits (IG2-19: Goff 83.9 vs blitz, Lawrence 82.1, Ward 79.9). Where the two boards *agree* on a QB the trait claim is strong; where they split, the difference (grade = throw quality; EPA = outcome including sacks and drops) is diagnostic in its own right. **Multi-week trap:** using one board's ordering to *validate* the other's. The two vendors use different labeling rules for "man vs zone," different bucket definitions, and different handling of DPI, scrambles, and throwaways. The correct cross-check is the *rank-correlation* between the two boards, not a pointwise equality.

**Lineage 5 — the metric-stickiness lineage.** IG2-06 sets the feature-selection prior: CPOE, pressure-to-sack %, EPA/play as the "sticky" QB metrics [PAGE 9]. This is the analytics community's standard prior (CPOE and pressure-to-sack have the strongest published year-to-year correlations; EPA lags raw YPA because of the system-confound). The prior generalizes to Lane A's whole feature set: when feeding the engine, prefer metrics with established stability, and carry the system-confound warning on every EPA feature.

**Multi-week cross-lane trap — the grievance echo.** The 340:1 ratio ("340 hits harder than a concussive-impact level for each concussion received") [PAGE 35], the 20–30 g damage onset, and the 5–15 year prodromal window for α-synuclein skin biopsy [PAGE 40] have all been reported before in popular media with numbers hovering in roughly the same ranges. Re-reporting them without checking the underlying sensor-study sample (the 340:1 is a *single-practice* or *single-player* study out of the sensor literature; the 5–15 year window is from Donadio et al. Neurology 2014/2017) risks consolidating a number that is *approximately right* into a number treated as *exact*. The wave's own discipline here is correct: LEARN-ONLY, paper-pull-owed [PAGE 35, PAGE 40].

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The theory-of-mind layer here is not about opposing *coaches* alone — it is about three nested minds: the coordinator's, the athlete's, and the analyst's. Each has a characteristic "crisis habit regression" that the wave's specimens instantiate.

**Coordinator crisis regression.** Under scoreboard pressure, coordinators regress to their highest-frequency, lowest-variance call. The wave's clearest specimen is implicit in IG2-09: Cover 3 as the league's plurality call, frequently disguised from a Cover 2 shell [PAGE 12], is the "safe" default. Regression-to-Cover-3 is precisely the shape the reasoning layer should encode — when a defense is behind or has lost a CB (IG2-07's Surtain-out case: man rate 32.4% → 22.4% [PAGE 10]), the *residual* call-set shifts toward zone, and the QB whose zone-split is worst is now facing *more* zone, not less. This is the counter-trap. Darnold's man-vs-zone splits were, in the wave's own framing, the central pre-game hinge against KC's man-heavy defense [PAGE 20]; if KC regresses to zone under pressure, the same QB's *worst* split shifts — the wave's operator (E4, E10) handles both.

**Counter-trap.** The mirror of coordinator regression is *offensive counter-regression* under the same clock pressure. When a coordinator is calling more Cover 3 because he is behind, a well-prepared QB (Mahomes, in IG2-16's slide-1 framing) attacks that adjustment specifically: his +0.22 EPA vs two-high and −0.24 vs Cover 3 is *backwards* from the usual QB-coverage hierarchy — i.e., teams *should* play him Cover-3-heavy, and the moment a defensive coordinator falls into the more comfortable zone (rather than the more effective man) the QB's EPA expands. Any engine state that models both teams' coverage choices simultaneously [E10] captures the counter-trap, and any single-sided model misses it.

**Clock.** The clock that *drives* crisis-habit regression is game time remaining × score margin × possession. The wave does not have a specific clock model, but its specimens imply one: Minnesota's 5-0 start [PAGE 21] has a defensive identity that runs *higher* blitz rate (66.7%) than typical because the coordinator is not under scoreboard pressure; conversely a 0-5 Chargers staff presents the classic "regress to the safer call" profile (Herbert's zone rate faced 86.4% [PAGE 5] is the sum over opponents doing the same thing to him — a league-wide crisis pattern). The clock that matters for coaching behavior is not wall-clock but *serial-losing-clock*; when it runs out, coordinators default.

**Athlete crisis regression — medical side.** IG2-33 is the medical-side instantiation: a player who shakes his head on-ice after a fight (SHAAKE) and passes the *later* cognitive test because the recovery window has passed [PAGE 41]. The athlete's crisis-habit regression is "stay in the game" — which is the well-documented default under acute competitive threat. The protocol design implication (immediate visible-sign triage, delayed testing does not prove absence) is the correct counter-trap to that habit, and the wave's frame — "a visible sign means assume concussion and remove" — is a *habit override* rather than a habit *teach-in*.

**Analyst crisis regression — the media-understanding layer.** The deepest theory-of-mind observation in this wave is not about coaches; it is about the wave's own tooling. The media-understanding layer *regressed to a credible-sounding wrong answer* on IG2-02 (misread 60/62% personnel *rates* as *success rates* [PAGE 6]) and on IG2-17 (internally inconsistent personnel names [PAGE 22]). Both are the same pattern: a multimodal model under uncertainty filling in details that have the right *genre* (a plausible player name; a plausible success rate) but the wrong *value*. The wave's counter-trap discipline — rest the dossier on the caption, flag the media layer, discard the flagged detail — is exactly the analyst-side control.

**The clock for the engine.** Human-in-the-loop review has a clock too: the longer a flagged-unverified item sits between waves, the more likely it is to leak into published output as if verified. The wave's SEEN-LEDGER discipline [PAGE 2] and the "first mining seam" note in the gap audit [PAGE 52] are the correct clock: every UNVERIFIED flag must be resolved by paper-pull or by explicit deletion, and the wave's own gap audit is where the queue of open clocks is kept.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Cross-layer vetoes are the physical, biomechanical, and weather constraints that *override* a statistical read from another layer. The wave supplies four hard vetoes and two softer priors.

**V1 — Heat-stroke protocol as a weather-veto on any "August performance" readout.** IG2-39 [PAGE 46] specifies "cool first, transport second," the cold-water-immersion-before-transport doctrine, and the "$15 kiddie pool" standard equipment. The physical veto is that exertional heat stroke is minutes-scale, so any game played in high heat index without an immersion bath staged at the sideline is a venue-keeper problem, and the engine's weather layer *must* veto the game-context readout against the venue-preparation check before it prices heat as a performance variable. If the checklist fails, the game is a health risk first and a performance sample second; performance data from that game carries a measurement-noise penalty (exhaustion, cramped substitution patterns) that the engine should not treat as trait evidence. WBGT thresholds are *not* in the post and must not be cited from it [PAGE 46].

**V2 — Maturation as a biomechanical veto on age-keyed ACL screening.** IG2-37 [PAGE 44]: 16 studies, n=2,323, post-pubertal athletes showing greater peak knee abduction angles and abduction + internal-rotation moments (higher ACL loading patterns), pre-pubertal athletes showing higher relative vertical ground reaction forces. The post explicitly says chronological age is a bad proxy. **Veto:** any screening or program oriented by *birth year* — the dominant youth-sports convention — is vetoed by this evidence. Screening must be stage-keyed (via pubertal assessment), not calendar-keyed. The mechanism (relative vGRF in pre-pubertal; abduction moments post-pubertal) also vetoes a naive "of course the ACL fails in older kids because they hit harder" read: the *pre*-pubertal force finding says the younger body is already taking disproportionate load.

**V3 — Youth impact comparability as a biomechanical veto on "smaller hit = smaller risk."** IG2-35 [PAGE 42] states that youth impacts are of comparable force to college impacts because the child's brain is less able to arrest its motion; the mechanism (mass, neck strength, myelination) is a receiver-property, not a striker-property. **Veto:** any risk model that treats hit-force *alone* as the exposure variable, absent the receiver covariates, is mis-specified for the youth context. The joint distribution of (hit force, receiver mass, neck strength, myelination stage) is the correct object; scouting conventions that compare hitting *technique* across levels are vetoed by the physics.

**V4 — Biomechanical floor on TTT claims.** The Penix 2.26s anchor [PAGE 17] is near the human reaction-time floor (~0.15–0.20 s visual-to-init) plus minimum delivery mechanics (~2.0–2.2 s). **Veto:** any claim of TTT improvement below ~2.0 s (or a 1.8–1.9 s report from charting vendors) is presumed method artifact (altered dropback depth, mishandled RPO timestamps, or scramble-classification rules) and must be de-elevated before feeding the engine.

**V5 — Venue/weather prior on phase value (soft veto).** IG2-20's hook — "the elements couldn't slow down the Bucs OL on Sunday" [PAGE 23] — reads as anecdote; the reasoning-layer prior is *OL and run game are the phase least disrupted by weather*, and deep-timing passing is the phase most disrupted. **Soft veto:** in cold/wet/high-wind games, weight OL-rank and run-reliability features upward and discount deep-passing efficiency (and vertical separation) downward. The wave anchors the OL top-5 (DET/SF/PIT/DEN/BUF) [PAGE 23] which are also strong rushes — that overlap is the reason the prior is not purely cosmetic.

**V6 — Acute-recovery window veto on "passed the test" (soft veto).** IG2-33 [PAGE 41]: tests administered 1–10+ minutes post-impact measure the recovered state, not the event. **Soft veto:** a "passed evaluation" observation should be *downweighted* as evidence of "not concussed" precisely as the elapsed time between impact and evaluation increases. This is the reverse of the usual intake assumption and must be hard-coded explicitly to avoid the habitual error.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three RLVR (reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-reasoning) veto scenarios. Each specifies a *prediction*, a *verifier path*, and a *rate of the false-positive we will penalize the model for producing* if the veto fires.

**RLVR-V1 — Purdy's coverage split is a sample-size artifact.** The current read (IG2-04 [PAGE 6]) is that Purdy carved zone (15-of-18, 204 yds, 92.9 QBR vs Bears' zone, single game). Predicted veto: recompute Purdy's coverage split against charting for the same game; if n_zone ≤ 20 attempts, the "92.9 QBR vs zone" is a small-sample draw whose standard error is roughly the attempt-count reciprocal, and the *durable* claim ("Purdy is elite vs zone") is refuted even if the point-estimate is preserved. Correct response: keep the mechanism (Shanahan's zone-beater concept) as a *conceptual* feature; demote the QB-trait claim to CONTEXT-ONLY. Prediction: the wave's LEARN-CONTEXT classification survives; a single-game-scale model weight of the split would be refused.

**RLVR-V2 — the 75% CTE prevalence collapses into the BMJ donor bounds.** The current point estimate for decedent-cohort CTE prevalence is 0.75 (IG2-23 [PAGE 31]); the model's own identifiability [E2] is fragile to the donor/model weight π. Predicted veto: on reading the JAMA Network Open paper, if the sensitivity analyses place the 75% within the 25%–98% donor-derived bounds (i.e., the selection model's contribution is not separable from donor-rate alone), the correct point estimate is the *donor-conditional rate*, not the population-modeled 75%. Correct response: report as a *bounded* claim with the donor-only interval attached; do *not* publish the bare 75% as the wave's headline figure. Prediction: the current wave's per-item dossier survives (it already cautions decedents-only), but any downstream statement that treats 75% as a point estimate should be vetoed at the sentence level; the model should learn to *emit the qualifier with the number*.

**RLVR-V3 — Stroud's "0 sacks on 52 blitzes" is a disposal-speed artifact, not a recognition trait.** The current read (IG2-05 [PAGE 7]) is that Stroud beat the blitz by *recognition* rather than *athleticism* (career-fastest TTT 2.40 s, 0 sacks). Predicted veto: on recomputing Stroud's pressure-to-throw interval from charting (anchor: 0.76 s, career best), if his release under pressure is narrowly clustered at ≤1.0 s (i.e., fast because he is throwing *to* the hot route rather than *past* pressure), then "recognition" is dominated by *route-design release*, which is a Houston coaching artifact rather than a Stroud trait. Correct response: split the trait into (a) pre-snap recognition (unchanged), (b) post-snap resolution speed (unchanged), and (c) *scheme-assisted hot-route release* (new covariate). Prediction: the tag shifts from "QB trait" to "QB trait × scheme" — the wave's own creative-angle (IG2-05) already anticipates this split; the veto makes it mandatory.

**Verifier discipline.** Each veto's verification path is concrete: charting recompute (V1), named-paper pull (V2), charting recompute with a covariate split (V3). None of the three requires a new data source — all three are *exactly the kind of test* the wave's own §8 fields already imply. The RLVR loop's real gain is that the *reasoning trace* being reinforced is the qualifier-and-demotion move — the model that produces "keep mechanism, demote trait" or "keep number, attach bound" scores higher than the model that produces the bare number. This correctness-under-veto is what the wave has already done *narratively* and what the engine needs to do *operationally*.
