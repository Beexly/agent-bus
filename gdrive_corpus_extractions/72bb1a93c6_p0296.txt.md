SR_n = SR_0 \cdot e^{-\lambda n}, \quad N_n = N_0 \cdot e^{-\mu n},

**Physical/Biomechanical Veto Proof**

The theoretical model of progressive spontaneous recovery decline assumes that the organism's physical capacity to perform the response remains constant across extinction trials. However, biomechanical factors introduce hard vetoes that can invalidate this prediction. Consider a rat performing barpressing: repeated extinctions involve sustained motor activity without reward. Over time, muscle fatigue sets in, reducing force output and movement speed. This physical depletion directly lowers the observable reaction rate, independent of any change in neural representation ($sE^R$).

Furthermore, metabolic constraints play a role. Performing unrewarded actions consumes energy, potentially increasing hunger drive ($D$) if food-deprived. While higher drive might theoretically enhance performance via Hull's main equation $sE^R = D \cdot H \cdot V \dots$, extreme fatigue can overwhelm this effect, leading to a breakdown in the linear relationship. Thus, even if conditioned inhibition decays predictably, the resulting behavior may not manifest due to muscular exhaustion or pain from overuse.

Weather and environmental conditions also exert veto power. Temperature extremes affect thermoregulation, diverting energy away from voluntary movement. Humidity impacts evaporative cooling efficiency, further straining cardiovascular systems during prolonged activity. These abiotic factors create noise in the data, making it difficult to isolate purely psychological effects. Therefore, any experimental verification of Corollary VIII must control for or measure these physiological variables to avoid spurious conclusions about inhibitory processes.

In summary, the pure informational model fails when the body's finite resources become the limiting factor, demonstrating that behavioral laws are always embedded within biological realities.
