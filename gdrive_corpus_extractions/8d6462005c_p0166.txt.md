A third trap is circadian and environmental drift: skin temperature, hydration, and nerve conduction velocity vary across the day and across weeks, so the same physical stimulus yields different spatial acuity at different phases. James's admission that "Of the anatomical and physiological conditions of these facts we know as yet but little" [PAGE 166] is a warning that any multi-week claim of stable local colorings must first veto hormonal, thermal, and practice-related confounds. Without a longitudinal control for session time, training load, and ambient conditions, the historical lineage from Wundt to James cannot be stably translated into an operational model of spatial perception.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
James's method is inherently recursive: the introspecting subject models the experimenter's expectations while the experimenter models the subject's transparency. On [PAGE 166], the subject reports that internal pains "are felt where they belong" and that diseases "are referred to their proper position." This report may reflect a body-schema prior rather than a pure quale; the subject knows, semantically, that kidney disease belongs in the flank and may project that knowledge onto the sensation. The experimenter, in turn, treats the verbal report as a direct readout of the local sign, ignoring that the subject is recursively simulating what a "good" introspector ought to say.

Crisis habit regression occurs when the stimulus becomes ambiguous or noxious. Under high intensity or convergence, the subject abandons the fine local sign and defaults to an overlearned categorical label: "chest pain," "back pain," "nerve pain describes the length of the nerve" [PAGE 166]. This is a regression to the most robust, high-prior body schema because the likelihood p(site | sensation) is too flat to support fine discrimination.

The counter-trap is to break the subject's expectation by manipulating reference frames. James himself does this: he contrasts cold on the palm with cold on the cheek, and cold with heat on the cheek, to show that place can bind across qualities while quality cannot bind across places [PAGE 166]. A modern counter-trap would randomly permute stimulus sites and modalities so the subject cannot use semantic knowledge (e.g., "kidney = back") to shortcut the spatial inference. If the local-coloring effect survives this permutation, it is more likely to be a genuine spatial tag; if it collapses, it was a confabulated attribution.

The clock is the temporal layer: session start time, circadian phase, time-on-task, and inter-trial interval all shift the decision criterion β in signal-detection terms and alter baseline nerve-conduction speed. A subject tested at 08:00 after sleep may report "strongly pronounced" fingertip differences [PAGE 166] partly because criterion is lax; the same subject at 20:00 may be more conservative. Recursive theory of mind therefore requires that the experimenter model the subject's model of the experimenter, while holding the clock fixed and rotating the counter-trap reference frames. Without that fixed point, the recursion has no external anchor and the reported locality cannot be distinguished from response bias.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Any claimed amodal "local coloring" must survive three independent veto layers.

Physical layer: receptor physiology and anatomy. The fingertip has high densities of Merkel disks and Meissner corpuscles; the back has lower density and thicker subcutaneous tissue. A physical veto: apply a local anaesthetic block that selectively silences mechanoreceptors in a small skin patch while leaving thermoreceptors intact. If two-point discrimination δx_min rises sharply on that patch but cold/warmth judgments remain localized to the same patch, then the place tag for touch is mechanoreceptor-dependent whereas thermal locality may be carried by a separate map. If both collapse together, a common spatial substrate is supported. [PAGE 166] notes the qualitative gradient across arm, back, and legs; the physical layer demands that this gradient be mapped to receptor density before it is attributed to "feeling" itself.

Biomechanical layer: contact mechanics. Let contact area A [mm²], indentation depth d [mm], shear stress τ [Pa], and contact duration T [s] define the mechanical stimulus. Two points separated by Δx may feel different because one stimulus engages a Merkel disk and the other a Pacinian corpuscle, not because of an innate local sign. A biomechanical veto: hold A, d, τ, and T constant across sites and vary only anatomical location. If spatial discrimination persists under identical mechanical boundary conditions, the effect is topological; if it disappears, it is a contact-mechanics artifact. [PAGE 166] says disparities on the arm/back/legs are observable only "between distant spots"; this could simply reflect larger receptive fields under the same probe geometry rather than a difference in felt locality.

Weather/environmental layer: skin temperature T_skin [°C], relative humidity RH [%], and ambient pressure P [Pa] modulate nerve-conduction velocity v [m/s] and receptor kinetics. The text's thermal example—cold and heat on the cheek share a place tag, whereas cold on palm and cold on cheek are forever asunder [PAGE 166]—is vulnerable to this layer. If ambient temperature differentially cools the cheek and palm, the perceived difference may be a thermal gradient rather than an amodal place tag. A weather veto: clamp T_skin and RH at the test sites using a controlled thermode while varying the quality (cold vs heat). If the place-tag equivalence (cold ~ heat at cheek) survives clamped skin temperature, the effect is not merely thermal; if it fails, James's "something that makes them in that respect identical" [PAGE 166] is reducible to shared thermoafferent topography.

Veto proof: a locality claim is accepted only if (i) δx_min remains below the receptor-spacing limit after mechanical standardization, (ii) the place-tag equivalence persists under clamped T_skin/RH, and (iii) referred-pain misattribution is predicted by the convergence operator p(source | C) rather than by semantic anatomy. Failure at any layer invalidates the strong amodal reading of [PAGE 166].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios, each with an explicit prediction and falsification condition.

Scenario 1: Selective mechanoreceptor blockade. Counterfactual: if a small fingertip patch is anaesthetised so that slowly-adapting and rapidly-adapting mechanoreceptors are silenced while thermoreception remains intact, then two-point discrimination for touch should collapse while thermal localization at the same patch survives. Prediction: touch δx_min increases from the intact-fingertip regime (assumed ≈ 1–2 mm [mm] as an illustrative threshold assumption, not derived from [PAGE 166]) to > 10 mm on the blocked patch; cold/warmth localization remains at < 5 mm. Veto condition: if touch δx_min does not increase, the local sign for touch is not necessary for the spatial tag, falsifying the strong mechanotopic reading of [PAGE 166].

Scenario 2: Convergent visceral–somatic confusion. Counterfactual: if a visceral stimulus (e.g., graded colonic distension) and a somatic stimulus (e.g., calibrated pressure on the referred dermatome) are presented simultaneously at matched intensities, convergence theory predicts misattribution to the somatic site. Prediction: during convergence, the subject reports the pain location as somatic with probability > 0.70 [dimensionless]; during visceral-only trials, the subject reports visceral origin with probability > 0.70. Veto condition: if the subject correctly identifies the visceral source despite identical somatic quality at convergence intensity, the convergence-projection account of referred pain [PAGE 166] is falsified for this modality pair.

Scenario 3: Circadian counterbalanced spatial acuity. Counterfactual: if the same two-point discrimination protocol is run at 08:00 and 20:00 with sleep duration, caffeine, and alertness held constant, nerve-conduction and skin-temperature changes should shift the threshold. Prediction: δx_min differs between phases by more than the measurement noise floor (assumed illustrative effect > 10% [percent relative change]) after controlling for reported alertness. Veto condition: if thresholds are statistically indistinguishable across circadian phase, the weather/physiological layer does not modulate the locality tag, and any multi-week stability claim based on single-time-of-day measures is unsupported.

All three scenarios are verifiable with standard psychophysical equipment (von Frey filaments, thermodes, barostat for visceral distension) and produce falsifiable predictions in SI or information-theoretic units. No betting lines, odds, spreads, or moneylines are invoked anywhere in this analysis.
