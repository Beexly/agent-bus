<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
The detector loss is binary cross-entropy on [PAGE 5]:

\[
\mathrm{BCELoss}
= -\frac{1}{n}\sum_{n}\left[y_n\ln x_n+(1-y_n)\ln(1-x_n)\right],
\]

where \(y_n\in\{0,1\}\) is the binary label and \(x_n\in(0,1)\) is the predicted probability. Units: nats per sample when the logarithm is natural; bits per sample if base-2. Assumption: independent binary labels. Exact breakdown: if \(x_n=0\) and \(y_n=1\), or \(x_n=1\) and \(y_n=0\), the term is infinite; implementations must clip \(x_n\) to \([\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\). The paper does not state a clipping value.

The activation is SiLU on [PAGE 5]:

\[
\mathrm{SiLU}(x)=x\cdot\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}},
\]

with dimensionless input/output. It has no finite singularity, but for large negative \(x\) the gate tends to zero and gradients vanish; for large positive \(x\) it behaves approximately linearly. This matters under low-light or blurred frames where activations can saturate.

The depthwise-separable cost comparison is on [PAGE 6]. The paper writes ordinary convolution cost as

\[
C_{\mathrm{cost}}=D_K\cdot D_K\cdot M\cdot N\cdot D_F\cdot D_F,
\]

and separable convolution as

\[
C_{\mathrm{cost-S}}
=D_K\cdot D_K\cdot M\cdot D_F\cdot D_F
+M\cdot N\cdot D_F\cdot D_F.
\]

Here \(D_K\) is kernel spatial size, \(M\) input channels, \(N\) output channels, and \(D_F\) feature-map spatial size. Units: multiply-accumulate operations or FLOPs. Under the paper’s same-spatial approximation, the ratio is

\[
\frac{C_{\mathrm{cost-S}}}{C_{\mathrm{cost}}}
=\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{D_K^2}.
\]

This ratio is valid only for stride 1 and the paper’s same \(D_F\) convention; with different output spatial size \(D_F'\), the correct expression becomes

\[
\frac{C_{\mathrm{cost-S}}}{C_{\mathrm{cost}}}
=\left(\frac{D_F'}{D_F}\right)^2
\left(\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{D_K^2}\right).
\]

Breakdown: \(N=0\), \(D_K=0\), or mismatched \(D_F,D_F'\) makes the formula meaningless. The paper’s claim that “the DSC speed is proportional to the square of the convolutional kernel size” [PAGE 6] is therefore a same-spatial, same-stride approximation, not a universal law.

The weighted BiFPN fusion is Eq. (5) on [PAGE 7]:

\[
\begin{cases}
T_6^m
=\mathrm{Conv}\!\left(
\dfrac{w_1T_6^{in}+w_2R(T_7^{in})}
{w_1+w_2+\lambda}
\right),\\[6pt]
T_6^{out}
=\mathrm{Conv}\!\left(
\dfrac{w_1'T_6^{in}+w_2'T_6^m+w_3'R(T_5^{out})}
{w_1'+w_2'+w_3'+\lambda}
\right).
\end{cases}
\]

The tensors are feature maps with dimensionless activation values; \(R(\cdot)\) is a resolution or resampling operator; \(\lambda\) is a dimensionless resolution-adjustment parameter. Breakdown: any denominator equal to zero makes the fusion undefined; negative or unbounded learned weights can make the normalized sum unstable. The paper does not constrain \(w_i\) or \(\lambda\) in the excerpt.

CBAM is given by Eq. (6) and Eq. (7) on [PAGE 7]:

\[
M_c(I)=\sigma(WF),
\]

\[
M_s(I)=\sigma\!\left(f^{7\times7}\left([F_{\mathrm{avg}}^s;F_{\mathrm{max}}^s]\right)\right).
\]

Here \(\sigma\) is an activation function, \(W\) a multilayer-perceptron weight, \(F\) a pooling aggregation, \(F_{\mathrm{avg}}^s\) and \(F_{\mathrm{max}}^s\) spatial average/max pooling, and \(f^{7\times7}\) a \(7\times7\) convolution. Units: dimensionless attention weights. Breakdown: channel and spatial tensor dimensions must match; a \(7\times7\) kernel on a feature map smaller than \(7\times7\) requires padding or it is undefined. The paper says CBAM is used “considering the interference of billboard location and audience background” [PAGE 7], but does not report a separate background-only ablation.

The Kalman state and observation equations are Eq. (8) on [PAGE 8]:

\[
\begin{cases}
x_{k+1}=A_{k,k+1}x_k+W_k,\\
z_k=H_kx_k+V_k.
\end{cases}
\]

If \(x_k\) contains image coordinates and velocities, its units are pixels and pixels/frame or pixels/s; \(A\) is dimensionless per-step transition; \(H\) maps state to observation; \(W_k\) and \(V_k\) are noise terms with covariance in pixels² or velocity². Breakdown: the standard Kalman filter assumes linear dynamics and observation. The paper explicitly notes that “The standard Kalman filter assumes that both the dynamic model and the observation model of the system are linear” [PAGE 8]. Under rapid direction change or acceleration, that assumption is violated.

The unscented transform is Eq. (9)–Eq. (12) on [PAGE 9]:

\[
S=\{s_0,s_1,s_2,\ldots,s_m\},
\]

\[
x_i=f(s_i),\qquad i=0,1,2,\ldots,2L,
\]

\[
\begin{cases}
\hat{x}_{k|k-1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{2L}W_i^a x_j,\\[4pt]
P_{k|k-1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{2L}W_i^c
\left(x_j-\hat{x}_{k|k-1}\right)
\left(x_j-\hat{x}_{k|k-1}\right)^T
+Q_k,
\end{cases}
\]

\[
\begin{cases}
\hat{x}_{k|k}
=\hat{x}_{k|k-1}+K_k(z_k-\hat{z}),\\[4pt]
P_{k|k}
=P_{k|k-1}-K_k\hat{s}_kK_k^T.
\end{cases}
\]

Here \(W_i^a\) and \(W_i^c\) are first- and second-order weights, \(Q_k\) is process-noise covariance, \(K_k\) is Kalman gain, and \(P\) is state covariance. Units follow the state vector: pixels² for position covariance, (pixels/frame)² for velocity covariance. Breakdown: sigma-point generation requires a matrix square root of \(P_{k-1}\); if \(P_{k-1}\) is not positive semidefinite, Cholesky factorization fails. Negative UKF weights can also produce non-positive covariance. The subtractive update for \(P_{k|k}\) can lose positive semidefiniteness under ill-conditioning, so numerical stabilization is required and is not reported.

The association similarity is Eq. (13) on [PAGE 9]:

\[
\mathrm{IIoU}
=\frac{A\cap B}{A\cup B}-\frac{d^2}{c^2},
\]

where \(A,B\) are box regions, \(d\) is center distance, and \(c\) is the diagonal of the smallest enclosing rectangle. Units: areas in pixels², \(d\) and \(c\) in pixels, so IIoU is dimensionless. Breakdown: \(A\cup B=0\) or \(c=0\) makes the expression undefined. The subtracted term can make IIoU negative, so a zero threshold is not automatically a valid association threshold; the paper does not give the threshold used.

The reported metrics use mixed units: mAP, MOTA, HOTA, DetA, AssA, MOTP, precision, recall, and tracking-success rate are percentages; FPS is img/s; system processing speed is ms; power is W; parameter quantity is M; AUC and rank are dimensionless. The paper’s Table 1 on [PAGE 11], Table 2 on [PAGE 11], Table 3 on [PAGE 12], and Table 4 on [PAGE 13] provide the values, but the conclusion on [PAGE 15] repeats different tracking values from Table 4. A metric table without a fixed split, fixed confidence threshold, and fixed association threshold is not a reproducible operator definition.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Let the tracker’s belief over the target state be \(b_t(x_t)\). A Bayes-filter recursion is

\[
b_t(x_t)\propto p(z_t\mid x_t)\int p(x_t\mid x_{t-1})b_{t-1}(x_{t-1})\,dx_{t-1}.
\]

The paper’s UKF is an approximate Gaussian implementation of this recursion, with mean and covariance in Eq. (11)–Eq. (12) on [PAGE 9]. The recursive theory-of-mind question is not “does the model see the player?” but “what does the model believe about its own uncertainty?” If \(P_{k|k}\) is underreported, the gain \(K_k\) and the association gate become overconfident; correct detections can be rejected because they disagree with the prior. If \(P_{k|k}\) is overreported, the tracker chases background clutter.

Crisis habit regression appears when the likelihood \(p(z_t\mid x_t)\) becomes flat because of occlusion, shadow, or motion blur. The posterior is then dominated by the prior \(\int p(x_t\mid x_{t-1})b_{t-1}\). The tracker “habitually” continues the last velocity, which is useful for short occlusion but dangerous for identity switches. The paper acknowledges the failure mode: “the Kalman filter has trouble anticipating nonlinear target motion in intricate settings” [PAGE 8], and football players “flexibly adjust their running position and moving route based on the strategic deployment, which makes the target movement show greater randomness” [PAGE 8]. UKF reduces linearization error, but it cannot eliminate prior lock if the process-noise covariance \(Q_k\) is too small relative to true acceleration.

The counter-trap is that added attention and re-identification can overcorrect. CBAM is introduced “considering the interference of billboard location and audience background” [PAGE 7], but attention can lock onto a salient billboard, referee, or sideline logo. A tracker that is too aggressive in re-identification may hallucinate a match after occlusion. The safe counter-trap is a counterfactual occlusion probe: mask the suspected distractor and test whether the identity association survives. The paper reports Scenario 3 and Scenario 4 success rates [PAGE 13]–[PAGE 14], but it does not report a distractor-only or background-only counterfactual.

The clock is the frame budget. If the required rate is \(f_{\mathrm{req}}\), then \(T_{\mathrm{budget}}=1/f_{\mathrm{req}}\). Table 2 on [PAGE 11] gives I3-YOLOv5 on Jetson Nano at 8.47 img/s, i.e. about 118 ms/frame; Table 4 on [PAGE 13] gives the research tracker at 17.76 img/s, about 56.3 ms/frame; the conclusion on [PAGE 15] gives 15.6, about 64.1 ms/frame. The paper also says system processing speed is “less than 20 ms” [PAGE 12]. These are different clocks. A recursive self-model must specify which clock it is optimizing: detector-only latency, tracker-only latency, or full pipeline. Without that specification, “real-time” is a belief, not a measured property.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The physical layer veto is occlusion and optics. The paper reports that in Scenario 3 the proposed algorithm has a success rate of “86.12%” when “the occlusion overlap lies in the interval of 0 to 0.4” [PAGE 13], and that the gap “is more obvious after the overlap degree exceeds 0.4” [PAGE 14]. Adjudication: the paper supports a narrow claim inside 0–0.4 overlap; it does not support uniform 90%+ robustness beyond 0.4. A standard motion-blur veto is

\[
b=\frac{v\tau}{s_p},
\]

where \(v\) is target image speed in m/s, \(\tau\) exposure time in s, and \(s_p\) is the pixel scale in m/px; \(b\) is blur length in pixels. If \(b\) is comparable to the target box width, detection confidence and association both degrade. The paper’s “uneven lighting and shadows” condition [PAGE 1] is exactly this optical regime, and Table 3’s mAP of 89.25 [PAGE 12] is below the abstract’s “exceeded 90%” wording.

The biomechanical layer veto is acceleration and deformation. Football players change direction, collide, and occlude each other; the paper itself lists “target deformation, occlusion, dense distribution” [PAGE 2]. A kinematic veto is \(a\Delta t^2 > 3\sigma_p\), where \(a\) is acceleration, \(\Delta t\) the frame interval, and \(\sigma_p\) the position uncertainty. When true acceleration exceeds the UKF’s process-noise model, the sigma points do not cover the state, and the tracker either lags or switches identity. The paper’s UKF is an improvement over linear KF, but Eq. (11)’s \(Q_k\) must be tuned to the actual acceleration distribution; the excerpt does not report that tuning.

The weather layer veto is visibility and contrast. The paper says football is “affected by weather, light, field conditions and other factors” [PAGE 2]. Under rain, lens water, or hard shadow, the likelihood is low and the prior dominates. The adjudication is that the reported indoor/outdoor mix is not stratified by weather in the tables. A weather veto is triggered when visibility falls below the scale needed to resolve a player at the reported resolution: if the player box is only a few pixels, mAP and tracking-success values cannot be transferred from the 720p+ dataset [PAGE 10] to the degraded feed.

The deployment-layer veto is the clock mismatch. Table 2’s Jetson Nano I3-YOLOv5 at 8.47 img/s [PAGE 11] implies about 118 ms/frame, while Table 3’s “system processing speed” is 13.26–16.15 ms [PAGE 12]. The paper does not prove that the full YOLOv5+UKF-DeepSORT pipeline meets <20 ms. The physical/biomechanical/weather veto proof is therefore conditional: the method is admissible for short occlusions, moderate lighting, and offline or low-frame-rate analysis; it is not proven for dense occlusion, high acceleration, severe weather, or strict low-latency embedded operation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
