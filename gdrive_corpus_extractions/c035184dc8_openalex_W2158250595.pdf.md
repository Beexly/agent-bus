<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes the tendency of assessors under temporal or cognitive pressure to revert to the traditional teaching-evaluation approach that isolates technique from tactics [PAGE 2]. When a coach faces a tight session clock—e.g., GPET's two 4-min parts with a 3-min break and no stoppage time [PAGE 7]—the observer may stop coding nuanced tactical principles (penetration, depth mobility, defensive balance) and regress to counting visible technical executions (pass completion, shot success) because those are faster to tally and more salient under load. This regress is epistemically dangerous: it collapses the ecological framework back into technique-centric assessment, precisely what the review argues against [PAGE 2].

The counter-trap is the structured observational grid itself. TSAP's grid forces two marks per ball possession event—how possession was gained (CB or RB) and how it was disposed (NB, LB, OB, SS) [PAGE 5]. FUT–SAT's 76-variable topology [PAGE 8] and GPET's role-specific matrices [PAGE 7] raise the cognitive cost of regression, making it harder to slip into unidimensional technical counting. However, this counter-trap introduces its own failure mode: observer overload. With 76 variables across two macro-categories and seven categories [PAGE 8], an assessor watching live video may under-sample rare events (e.g., defensive unity or offensive coverage) and over-sample frequent events (passes), biasing the Tactical Performance Index.

The clock layer constrains all three. GPAI's 2 × 4-min halves [PAGE 2], KORA's 3-min game [PAGE 5], TSAP's 2 × 7-min matches [PAGE 4], and FUT–SAT's 4-min standard test [PAGE 8] compress tactical behavior into brief episodes. Short clocks amplify sampling variance: a single turnover or successful shot in a 3-min KORA bout [PAGE 5] can swing the numerical-superiority principle disproportionately. Recursive theory of mind predicts that assessors will model opponents' tactical intentions more accurately under longer observation windows, but the instruments deliberately constrain windows to preserve ecological fidelity to small-sided games. Therefore, the assessment design creates a tension between clock-limited reliability and ecological validity, and crisis regression is most likely exactly when the clock is shortest.
