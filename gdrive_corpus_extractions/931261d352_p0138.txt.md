<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman's work sits at a critical juncture in the history of psychology, acting as a retrocausal bridge between strict behaviorism and modern cognitive science.

**Scientific Lineage:**
*   **Pre-Tolman (S-R Dominance):** Watsonian behaviorism and Thorndike's connectionism held that learning was the strengthening of stimulus-response bonds through trial-and-error. Behavior was seen as a direct function of environmental stimuli and past reinforcements. Internal mental states were considered epiphenomenal or irrelevant.
*   **Tolman's Intervention (Purposeful Behavior):** Tolman introduced "intervening variables" (like the cognitive map) to explain phenomena like latent learning and insight. His maze experiments were designed to show that rats formed expectations about goals and organized their behavior purposively to achieve them, not merely through conditioned reflexes. The distinction between *learning* (acquisition of a map) and *performance* (its expression under drive) was revolutionary.
*   **Post-Tolman Legacy:**
    *   **Cognitive Revolution:** Tolman is a key precursor to the cognitive turn of the 1950s-60s. His emphasis on internal representations, expectations, and purpose laid groundwork for concepts like mental models and schema theory.
    *   **Neuroscience of Navigation:** The discovery of place cells (O'Keefe) and grid cells (Moser et al.) in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex provided a neural substrate for Tolman's "cognitive map." These cells fire based on spatial location, forming a metric representation of environment, exactly as Tolman postulated.
    *   **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Modern RL distinguishes between model-free (S-R habit) and model-based (planning using an internal model) agents. Tolman's hungry-reward group likely engages model-based planning (using the map), while the non-reward groups might rely more on model-free mechanisms or weaker model-based signals. His work anticipated this duality decades before it was formalized computationally.

**Multi-Week/Long-Term Traps:**
A major interpretive trap is viewing Tolman through a purely historical lens as "refuting behaviorism." In reality, his work was internally rigorous within a behaviorist framework—he used measurable behaviors (errors, times) to infer unobservable intervening variables. The trap is to dismiss his findings as outdated; instead, they remain foundational for understanding how motivation interacts with spatial cognition. Another trap is assuming the "cognitive map" is unitary. Modern research shows it's multi-scale, involving different brain regions for allocentric (hippocampal) vs. egocentric (parietal) navigation, which Tolman's coarse behavioral measures couldn't dissect.

    1.  Using a complex maze where no single cue predicts success (minimizing simple S-R bonds).

This temporal averaging might obscure rapid fluctuations in strategy. The pronounced bias in the hungry-reward group might reflect a shorter "switching time" to exploitation once the map is acquired. The clock also applies to development: juvenile vs. adult rats might show different baseline biases due to maturation of hippocampal circuits.
