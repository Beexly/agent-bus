Formal operators and units. All coordinates \(x_t,y_t\) are image-plane pixels; optical-flow displacements \(u_t,v_t\) are pixels per frame; frame indices and trajectory length \(L\) are in frames; class posteriors are dimensionless probabilities in \([0,1]\); accuracy and recall are percentages.

1. Shi-Tomasi homogeneous-region rejection. From [PAGE 3], Eq. (1):
\[
T = 0.001 \times \max_{i\in I}\min(\lambda_{i,1},\lambda_{i,2}),
\]
where \((\lambda_{i,1},\lambda_{i,2})\) are eigenvalues of the auto-correlation matrix at point \(i\) in image \(I\). If the image intensity is normalized, eigenvalues and \(T\) are dimensionless; otherwise they carry intensity-squared units. Assumption: homogeneous regions have insufficient trackable texture. Breakdown: if \(T\) is too high, no points survive; if too low, background clutter and noise are tracked. The source criteria are stated as “points are eliminated if the eigenvalues … exhibit smaller values” [PAGE 3].

2. Optical-flow tracking with median smoothing. From [PAGE 3], Eq. (2):
\[
P_{t+1}=(x_{t+1},y_{t+1})=(x_t,y_t)+(M*w_t)_{x_t,y_t},
\]
where \(w_t=(u_t,v_t)\) is dense optical flow in pixels/frame and \(M\) is a \(3\times3\)-pixel median kernel. Assumption: local motion is coherent over the kernel support. Breakdown: occlusion, motion blur, camera cut, or nonrigid player motion makes the median displacement nonrepresentative; then the trajectory drifts.

3. Trajectory shape normalization. From [PAGE 3], Eq. (3) and Eq. (4):
\[
\Delta P_t=P_{t+1}-P_t=(x_{t+1}-x_t,\ y_{t+1}-y_t),
\]
\[
T=\frac{(\Delta P_t,\ldots,\Delta P_{t+L-1})}
{\sum_{j=t}^{t+L-1}\|\Delta P_j\|}.
\]
The normalized descriptor is dimensionless. Exact singularity: if \(\sum_{j=t}^{t+L-1}\|\Delta P_j\|=0\), the denominator vanishes for a stationary trajectory; the paper does not state an \(\epsilon\)-regularizer. Breakdown: near-zero denominator amplifies sensor noise; long \(L\) smooths but blurs event timing; short \(L\) is noisy. The source fixes \(L=15\) frames and \(W=5\) pixels [PAGE 3].

Assumption: \(2\times2\times3\) space-time cell division and eight orientation bins. Breakdown: coarse bins lose orientation resolution; fine bins increase variance and dimensionality.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
VETO 1 — Physical sampling veto. The paper’s own numbers are 225 km/h and 30 fps [PAGE 2, PAGE 7]. The derived per-frame displacement is \(225000/3600/30=2.083\) m, versus a 0.067 m ball. That violates the implicit assumption that optical flow can associate the same small object between adjacent frames. Unless the camera is high-speed or the search is initialized by a physics model, high-speed serve tracking is vetoed as a frame-by-frame optical-flow claim.

VETO 2 — Geometric planar-homography veto. The source says “ball detection and tracking are accomplished through Homograph transformation” [PAGE 2] and defines \(x'=Hx\) [PAGE 5]. A homography is exact only for points on one plane. A tennis ball’s height above the court is not zero during flight, so its image point is not generated by the court-plane homography. This vetoes bounce localization and 3-D trajectory claims unless the method explicitly estimates ball height or uses a calibrated camera model.

VETO 3 — Biomechanical/semantic veto. “Serve” is a player action, not merely a ball-trajectory discontinuity; [PAGE 2] lists “serve, bounce, hit, and net” as four key events, but the method detects events from “discontinuities in the tennis ball’s motion” [PAGE 6]. Racket-head, arm, and body pose are absent. Thus a serve-versus-hit distinction can be vetoed when ball motion alone is ambiguous.

VETO 4 — Weather/illumination veto. The paper mentions “variable lighting” [PAGE 1] and “varying illumination” [PAGE 2], but reports no radiometric normalization, shadow model, or weather-stratified evaluation. HOG and MBH are gradient-based descriptors; hard shadows and exposure changes alter gradients. Any all-weather outdoor deployment claim is therefore outside the evidenced validity domain.

VETO 5 — Evaluation-construct veto. The confusion matrix on [PAGE 8] lacks Serve, while [PAGE 9] claims “all four types of event detection.” The architecture table labels softmax with “3 classes” and “7 classes” alongside a four-label sigmoid statement [PAGE 6]. This is not a physical veto but a construct-validity veto: the reported 84.34% cannot certify a four-class event detector without the missing class row and a consistent output layer.
