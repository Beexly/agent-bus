The abstract asserts OBB superiority, yet the paper's own Table III [PAGE 6] undercuts the detection-performance argument: "YOLOv11-Medium (HBB)" scores mAP@0.5 = 0.865 versus OBB's 0.859, with HBB also higher on recall (0.88 vs 0.87). The authors concede this honestly: "All differences remain within ±1%, well inside typical experimental variance." So the real thesis is not "OBB detects better" but "OBB localizes geometry better," carried entirely by the Tightness Ratio (TR) analysis. This is a pivot under pressure, and I must trace whether it holds.

Multi-hop chain: (1) The metric pipeline defines coverage as c = min(1, A/A_f), exposure as E = Δt·Σz, both derived "directly from model predictions without requiring manual correction" [PAGE 1]. (2) If OBB and HBB detect equally well, the only channel through which box geometry changes E and c is A_{ℓ,i}, the polygon area. (3) HBB overfills area for rotated logos; TR quantifies the overfill. (4) Therefore OBB's value is real but bounded to the area/coverage metrics, not to detection presence/absence.

Gray-zone audit — three cracks. First, the VFL contribution is never numerically ablated. The six configurations in Table II vary size (nano/small/medium/large) × version (v8/v11) [PAGE 3], not loss function. The prose claims "We also ablated Focal Loss" and "We instead adopt Varifocal Loss" [PAGE 3-4], but no BCE-vs-FL-vs-VFL table exists. The paper's headline methodological novelty is therefore asserted, not demonstrated. Second, A_{ℓ,i} is defined as "the sum of all OBB polygons clipped to the frame" [PAGE 4]. Summation overcounts overlapping detections of the same logo; the min(1,·) cap only fires when the inflated sum crosses A_f, so moderate double-counting silently inflates c. A union area was needed, not a sum. Third, E = Δt·Σz with Δt = 1/r and N = T_v·r assumes constant frame rate, yet the ingestion layer accepts "HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) playlists" [PAGE 3], which are frequently variable-frame-rate; under VFR, r is ill-defined and E accumulates bias. Finally, the reported inference of 19.98 FPS [PAGE 7, Table IV] is below PAL (25) and NTSC (30) broadcast cadence, so "real-time broadcast applications" is optimistic — it is sub-real-time.

I am confident the TR-based thesis is sound in principle but that three of its supporting pillars (VFL ablation, non-overlapping area, CFR assumption) are load-bearing and unproven.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All operators below are the paper's own, transcribed with SI/information units and breakdown regimes.

Coverage and support (Page 4). Frame area $A_f = W \times H$ (pixels²). Visible logo area $A_{\ell,i} = \sum \text{OBB polygons clipped to frame}$ (pixels²).
$$c_{\ell,i} = \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{A_{\ell,i}}{A_f}\right)\in[0,1],\qquad z_{\ell,i} = \begin{cases}1 & c_{\ell,i} > 0\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

Exposure (seconds). With frame rate $r$ (frames·s⁻¹), $N = T_v\, r$ frames, $\Delta t = 1/r$:
$$E_\ell = \Delta t \sum_{i=1}^{N} z_{\ell,i},\qquad \max E_\ell = \Delta t\cdot N = T_v.$$
Breakdown regime: if $r$ is variable (VFR/HLS), $\Delta t$ is not constant and $E_\ell$ is biased by $\sum_i(\Delta t_i - 1/r)$; the identity $\max E_\ell = T_v$ fails.

Coverage means (dimensionless → %):
$$\bar{C}^{\text{present}}_\ell = 100\cdot\frac{\sum_i z_{\ell,i}\,c_{\ell,i}}{\sum_i z_{\ell,i}},\qquad \bar{C}^{\text{overall}}_\ell = 100\cdot\frac{\sum_i z_{\ell,i}\,c_{\ell,i}}{N}.$$
Singularity: $\bar{C}^{\text{present}}$ is $0/0$ (undefined) when a brand never appears ($\sum_i z_{\ell,i}=0$); it must be guarded.

Classification objective (nats; Page 3-4). With sigmoid probability $p\in(0,1)$, label $y\in\{0,1\}$, quality target $q\in[0,1]$:
$$\text{BCE}(p,q) = -q\log p - (1-q)\log(1-p)$$
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VFL}} = \Big[\underbrace{\alpha\,p^{\gamma}(1-y)}_{\text{negative damping}} + \underbrace{q\,y}_{\text{quality gain}}\Big]\times \text{BCE}(p,q),\quad (\gamma,\alpha)=(2.0,0.75).$$
$$\mathcal{L}^{\text{ours}}_{\text{cls}} = \frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\mathcal{L}_{\text{VFL}}(p_{a,c},y_{a,c},q_{a,c}),\qquad \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{box}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{box}} + \lambda_{\text{cls}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda_{\text{dfl}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dfl}}.$$
Behavior: as $p\to 0$ on negatives, weight $\alpha p^\gamma \to 0$ (gradient vanishes on easy negatives); on positives, weight $=q$, so IoU-quality positives dominate. Breakdown: if the assigner emits $q\to 0$ for a true positive (poor early-training localization), that positive is silenced — VFL can suppress hard-but-correct positives during the first epochs.

Tightness Ratio (dimensionless, Page 6). $\text{TR} = \text{area}(\text{OBB})/\text{area}(\text{HBB})\in(0,1]$, shoelace area [23]. Closed form for an OBB of sides $w,h$ rotated by $\theta$ (its minimal enclosing HBB has area $(w\cos\theta+h\sin\theta)(w\sin\theta+h\cos\theta)$):
$$\text{TR}(\theta) = \frac{wh}{wh + \tfrac{1}{2}(w^2+h^2)\sin 2\theta} = \frac{1}{1 + \kappa\sin 2\theta},\quad \kappa \equiv \frac{\rho + \rho^{-1}}{2}\ge 1,\ \rho=w/h.$$
This is symmetric about $\theta=45^\circ$ with minimum $\text{TR}_{\min}=1/(1+\kappa)$ at exactly $45^\circ$ for every aspect ratio. The paper reports the empirical minimum "near 55°–60° (TR around 0.40)" [PAGE 7] — see adjudication below.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage of operators, traced backward from the paper's citations. The detector sits on the YOLO spine (one-stage, speed-favoring [9]) forked into oriented heads that add angle regression [17]; the OOD framing is imported from remote sensing (DOTA [18], HRSC2016 [19], surveys [15],[16]) where arbitrary-angle objects are native, and from scene-text detection. Varifocal Loss is a direct transplant of VarifocalNet (Zhang et al., CVPR 2021) [22]; the classification term is "localized" onto the modern YOLO composite $\mathcal{L}=\lambda_{\text{box}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{box}}+\lambda_{\text{cls}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}+\lambda_{\text{dfl}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dfl}}$, leaving oriented-box IoU regression and distribution focal loss "intact" [PAGE 4]. The geometry audit rests on the shoelace/surveyor's formula (Braden, 1986 [23]). Commercially, the work descends from Relo Metrics [7], Blinkfire [8], Hive [3], against which the authors state "direct benchmarking... is not feasible" [PAGE 5] because those pipelines are proprietary.

Multi-week traps latent in this genealogy. (1) The "detection parity" trap: because OBB and HBB tie on mAP [PAGE 5-6], a naive reader concludes OBB is pointless — the paper must re-anchor value on TR, and any downstream team that only tracks mAP will silently revert to HBB. (2) The transfer-domain trap: OOD gains were proven on aerial/satellite imagery with dense small objects; soccer logos are few, large, motion-blurred, and fabric-warped, so the remote-sensing priors (that justified OOD) only partially transfer. (3) The loss-ablation debt: importing VFL from VarifocalNet presumes the negative-dominance pathology it was designed for; with 670 long-tailed classes [PAGE 2-3] that presumption is plausible, but the paper never pays the empirical debt with a BCE/FL/VFL table, so the lineage is asserted rather than closed. (4) The metric-drift trap: the exposure algebra predates the tracking layer, and the authors themselves warn "Frame-wise aggregation... is susceptible to drift and flicker" [PAGE 7], with polygon-IoU tracking deferred to future work — a known unmaintained seam.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Modeling the actors around the evidence. The authors anticipated the "OBB doesn't raise mAP" reviewer objection and pre-empted it: Table III is published, not hidden, and the narrative reroutes to geometric tightness [PAGE 5-6]. That is a competent counter-trap — they concede the null result to defend the real claim. A sponsor-ROI reader, however, reasons one level deeper: "the number is auditable, but is it stable?" The paper's own admission of flicker/drift [PAGE 7] means a sophisticated buyer will discount raw detection counts and demand track-level exposure, which is not yet implemented — so the trust the dashboard seeks is not yet earned.

Crisis-habit regression under distribution shift: the detector was tuned on 2024 Swedish elite soccer, 32 matches, 16 teams [PAGE 2, Table I]. Confronted with a new league, night floodlight glare, or rain-smeared lenses, the model regresses to its dominant-class habits — it keeps firing on the sponsors with "over 500 instances" [PAGE 3] and misses the long-tail, inflating apparent exposure concentration. The 0.96 precision / 0.87 recall [PAGE 1] were measured in-distribution; the recall gap widens out-of-distribution, so exposure E_ℓ becomes systematically low for rare brands exactly when a sponsor most wants proof.

Counter-trap vs clock: the event-weighting and 9:16 ROI valuation the Discussion proposes [PAGE 7] would let an agent "retrieve all clips of the primary sponsor during goal events" — but that automation "inherits detector errors" [PAGE 8]. The temporal clock cuts two ways: post-hoc analytics tolerate 19.98 FPS comfortably, whereas the "real-time" pitch does not clear 25/30 fps cadence. A rational adopter picks the post-hoc use case and treats the real-time framing as aspirational.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Claim under test: coverage $c_{\ell,i}$ and exposure $E_\ell$ faithfully measure on-screen logo prominence. I prove a cross-layer veto — geometric precision cannot rescue accuracy lost in other layers.

Layer separation. Let $A^{\text{vis}}_{\ell,i}$ be the true human-marked visible logo area. The estimator uses $\hat{A}_{\ell,i}=\sum\text{OBB polygons}$. Error decomposes as
$$\hat{A}_{\ell,i} - A^{\text{vis}}_{\ell,i} = \underbrace{(\hat{A}_{\ell,i}-A^{\text{full}}_{\ell,i})}_{\text{geometry layer (TR-governed)}} + \underbrace{(A^{\text{full}}_{\ell,i}-A^{\text{vis}}_{\ell,i})}_{\text{physical layer (occlusion/blur)}}.$$
The TR analysis only shrinks the first term. It is structurally blind to the second.

Biomechanical veto. A player body occludes fraction $\varphi_{\text{occ}}$ of the logo. The visible region becomes an L-shape or fragment, but the OBB is a single rotated rectangle and cannot represent a concave visible mask; it overfills the occluded notch. Thus $\hat{A}>A^{\text{vis}}$ whenever $\varphi_{\text{occ}}>0$, and the min(1,·) cap does not correct it unless the overfill crosses $A_f$. The paper's own motivation lists "jersey folds" and occlusion [PAGE 1-2], conceding the term is nonzero.

Weather/optics veto. Rain, snow, floodlight glare, and motion blur reduce effective recall. With recall 0.87 [PAGE 1], roughly 13% of true presences yield $z_{\ell,i}=0$, so $\mathbb{E}[\hat{E}_\ell]\approx \text{recall}\cdot E_\ell$ under missed-detection — a systematic downward bias that TR cannot touch, because a missed detection contributes nothing to any polygon. Formally, $\partial \hat{E}_\ell/\partial(\text{TR}) = 0$ for frames where $z_{\ell,i}=0$.

TR-curve discrepancy adjudication. My closed form $\text{TR}(\theta)=1/(1+\kappa\sin2\theta)$ forces the minimum to exactly $45^\circ$ for every $\rho$, and averaging symmetric curves preserves that. The reported minimum "near 55°–60° (TR around 0.40)" [PAGE 7] therefore cannot arise from single-logo geometry; it must be a sampling/95%-CI artifact, an angle-folding convention in the "Orientation Necessity" binning, or an aspect-ratio mixture correlated with angle. This is a genuine gray-zone flag: the empirical curve's minimum location is not explained by the stated model, and the paper offers "95% confidence intervals" [PAGE 7] without widths, so the shift cannot be adjudicated as signal vs noise. Verdict: TR-ordering (lower TR at higher rotation) is proven; the precise argmin is not.

Net adjudication: OBB improves the geometry term only. Presence (recall) and occlusion (representation) veto overall metric accuracy independently. Claim downgraded from "accurate visibility metrics" to "less background-biased area estimates, conditional on detection and occlusion."
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable scenarios (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards style: each has an objective pass/fail measurable against ground truth).

1. VFL ablation veto. Re-train YOLOv11-Medium on the released dataset [PAGE 2] three times each with BCE, Focal Loss, and VFL, identical seed budget (200 epochs, 3×A100 [PAGE 4]). Prediction: mAP@0.5 differences fall inside ±0.6% (the same "experimental variance" band the authors invoke for OBB/HBB [PAGE 6]), i.e., VFL yields no statistically separable gain. This is verifiable and, if confirmed, falsifies the paper's implicit claim that VFL is the beneficial modification. Pass criterion for the authors: VFL beats BCE by > 1% mAP with non-overlapping bootstrap CIs.

2. Exposure-bias-vs-orientation veto. On a held-out set stratified by Orientation Necessity bins, compare OBB-derived $\bar{C}^{\text{overall}}_\ell$ to human annotation. Prediction from the TR model: bias is negligible below ~20° ON, grows monotonically with ON, and peaks in the 45–60° band; time-weighted, OBB lowers absolute coverage error versus HBB by a margin proportional to $(1-\text{TR})$. Verifiable against manual masks. Pass: OBB mean absolute coverage error < HBB by a margin that correlates with $(1-\text{TR})$ at $R^2>0.5$.

3. Real-time cadence veto. Run the full pipeline on 1920×1080 (not 1280×720 [PAGE 3]) at 25 and 30 fps targets on the G4dn.xlarge GPU. Prediction: throughput stays near the reported 19.98 FPS at 720p but falls below 25 fps at 1080p (pixel-count scaling ≈ 2.25×), so "real-time broadcast applications" [PAGE 7] is falsified for standard broadcast cadence and resolution. Pass: sustained ≥ target fps with frame-time variance under one frame budget.

Each veto is cheap to run against the public dataset and model card, converting the paper's soft claims into auditable pass/fail tests — which is precisely the "auditable and interpretable sponsor measurement" [PAGE 1] the system promises but has not yet fully self-applied.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Bottom line: the paper's honest core is that OBB does not detect better (Table III proves parity) but localizes geometry tighter (TR), and that geometry only feeds the area/coverage metrics. The three load-bearing weak seams are the un-ablated VFL claim, the sum-not-union area term that can inflate coverage under overlapping detections, and the constant-frame-rate assumption behind the exposure integral. I'd trust the dataset and the TR-ordering; I'd withhold trust from the real-time framing and from any VFL-superiority claim until the vetoes above are run.
