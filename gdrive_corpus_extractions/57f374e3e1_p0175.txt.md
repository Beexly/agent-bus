Week over week, an opponent that expects pure defense deep in one’s own territory can be caught by a defense that retains attack optionality (blitzes, quick kicks, disguise), converting the opponent’s assumption into a turnover and an attack → defense transition against them.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under pressure—close score, deep own territory, late clock—coaches tend to regress to low-order habits. One habit is position-only doctrine (“we are backed up, therefore purely defensive”); the other is possession absolutism (“we have the ball, therefore force the attack”). Roper’s middle ground is a higher-order habit that explicitly resists both regressions [PAGE 136].

Level structure:
- Level 0: React to current (x,p). Assign mode by position or possession alone.
- Level 1: Model the opponent’s mode assignment. “Do they think we are attacking or defending on this drive?”
- Level 2: Model the opponent’s model of our model. “Do they think we believe they think we are purely defending?”

Counter-trap: The counter-trap operates at Level 2. If a defense lines up in a “purely defensive” posture deep in its own territory (deep safeties, prevent alignment), the offense may commit to long-developing plays assuming no counter-pressure. Roper’s defense, however, retains attacking developments [PAGE 136]; it can blitz, disguise, or quick-kick, converting the offense’s Level-0 assumption into an attack → defense transition (turnover or failure on downs). Conversely, an offense that appears to be playing conservatively to “minimize risk of transformation into defense” [PAGE 136] can audibling into attack, punishing a defense that has relaxed into pure prevention mode.

Clock boundary layer: Game time T_remaining imposes a finite horizon. As T → 0, the possession flag’s weight can diverge relative to x: a possession at one’s own 10-yard line with seconds left and no opponent timeouts is more valuable than position alone suggests, because the opponent may not get the ball back. This is a boundary layer in which Roper’s inclination (“attack always inclines toward the team which holds the ball” [PAGE 136]) dominates the position term, and the attack policy’s mandate to minimize transformation into defense becomes urgent. The singularity at possession change is therefore time-modulated: late-game turnovers have higher terminal cost than early-game turnovers.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: The field geometry is fixed (L = 109.728 m). The 1920-era prolate spheroid on dirt/grass introduces high-variance bounces on fumbles and kicks, making possession security stochastic. Weather (rain, mud, wind) degrades ball security, kicking distance, and passing viability. A strategic rule that ignores these physical bounds is vetoed.

Biomechanical layer: Tackle probability per down, ball-carrying security, and blocking effectiveness bound the translation of possession into position advance. Roper’s claim that attack inclines to the ball-holder “wherever it is” [PAGE 136] is bounded above by the probability of retaining possession and advancing; if the tackle-for-loss rate exceeds the gain rate, possession alone cannot sustain attack viability.

Weather veto: High wind or heavy mud suppresses the passing game and increases incompleteness/interception risk. An attack plan built on deep passing to “minimize risk of being transformed into a defense” [PAGE 136] may be vetoed because wind raises the probability of attack → defense transition via turnover; a ground-possession plan becomes the non-vetoed alternative.

Veto rules for proposed strategic rules:
A rule R is vetoed if any of the following hold:
(i) R reduces mode to position alone (e.g., “inside own 20, purely defensive”), contradicting Roper’s middle ground [PAGE 136].
(ii) R implies λₛ(x,0) > 0 (scoring without possession), violating the necessary condition [PAGE 136].
(iii) The physical layer renders R infeasible (e.g., muddy field makes the proposed ball-security threshold unattainable).
(iv) R eliminates attacking developments from defense, violating the defense policy prescription [PAGE 136].

Example adjudication: Rule “Always punt from inside the own 20 because deep territory is purely defensive.” Vetoed under (i) and (iv): possession is retained, attack inclines to the holder wherever it is [PAGE 136], and punting forfeits defensive counter-attack optionality.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios and predictions:

Scenario 1 — Fourth down at the own 15-yard line, possession.
Position-only doctrine says punt (treat as purely defensive deep in territory) [PAGE 136]. Roper vetoes this: possession inclines to attack wherever it is, and the defense should retain attacking developments [PAGE 136].
Counterfactual: Run an offensive play from scrimmage rather than punt.
Verification/prediction: On matched game states (same down, distance, field position, clock, and weather), teams that attempt a conversion instead of punting should show higher expected scoring differential over the ensuing two drives when the conversion probability exceeds the break-even set by the field-position exchange and the opponent’s post-punt counter-attack value. Falsifiable by drive-chart conditioning: if go-for-it teams do not outperform punting teams in subsequent scoring differential across matched states, the veto is falsified.

Scenario 2 — Defense, opponent has the ball at midfield (your 40-yard line).
Standard prevent defense treats this as pure prevention, surrendering counter-attack optionality. Roper vetoes pure prevention: defense must leave “as much chance for attacking developments as possible” [PAGE 136].
Counterfactual: Use aggressive, turnover-seeking pressure rather than soft prevent coverage.
Verification/prediction: Defenses that pressure for turnovers at midfield should generate more defensive scores and short-field offensive possessions than prevent defenses, at the cost of higher variance in yards allowed. The net prediction is positive scoring differential when the offense can capitalize on short fields; verifiable by conditioning on drive-start position after defensive stops and turnovers.

Scenario 3 — Offense in opponent territory protecting a lead late in the game.
Excessive conservatism (run to clock, avoid all risk) treats attack as only about minimizing transformation into defense, but risks forfeiting scoring while retaining possession [PAGE 136]. Roper’s attack plan minimizes transformation risk without abandoning attack.
Counterfactual: Continue calling scoring-oriented plays rather than purely clock-running plays.
Verification/prediction: Teams that sacrifice scoring attempts entirely to preserve possession late should allow more opponent possessions with time to score after punts or turnovers on downs. The veto triggers when the run-play success rate falls below the probability that the opponent scores after regaining possession. Falsifiable by comparing end-game scoring differential between aggressive and ultra-conservative play-callers matched for lead size and time remaining.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
