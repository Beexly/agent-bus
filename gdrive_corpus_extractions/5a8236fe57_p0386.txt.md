Under acute sensory-emotional crisis, subjects regress to prepotent autonomic habits rather than deploying flexible regulation: sudden sound produces breath catching, strong attention produces breath holding, fear produces panting with constrained inspiration, and anger produces expiratory constraint [PAGE 376]. These are regressive because they recruit early-evolved brainstem and hypothalamic patterning instead of cortical voluntary breath control. The recursive theory-of-mind problem is that an observer reading page 376 may attribute a single respiratory signature to a mental state. That is the counter-trap: a sigh may be scored as “tension relieved,” but it could be a CO2 reset after prior hyperventilation, a voluntary deep breath, or a sigh-like arousal unrelated to subjective relief; breath-holding may be scored as “attention engaged,” but it could be startle-induced apnea, speech preparation, or passive orientation. State attribution requires convergent measures, not a one-to-one sign-to-emotion map.

Clock dependence enters through respiratory phase. Let theta(t)=2 pi t/T_R mod 2 pi be phase in radians, with T_R seconds the breath period. The probability of a catch-breath after a sudden sound should be phase-gated: P_cb(theta_0)=sigma(alpha+beta cos theta_0), where theta_0 is phase at sound onset, sigma is the logistic map to [0,1], and alpha,beta are fitted parameters. Sounds at different lung volumes recruit different Hering-Breuer and startle interactions, so identical acoustic stimuli can produce apnea, inspiration, or no visible change depending on theta_0. Breakdown occurs if T_R is nonstationary or if stimulus rise time is too slow to phase-lock the central pattern generator; then beta is unidentifiable and the clock model must be vetoed. Crisis habit regression predicts that under threat the subject cannot voluntarily choose phase at stimulus onset; the counter-trap predicts that an observer may over-read phase-locked apnea as evidence of fear even when the sound alone, independent of content, explains the response.

Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof. A physiological response model is valid only if the stimulus layer actually transduces. Define sound pressure level L_s in dB re 20 micro pascal and corneal red-light irradiance Phi_red in W/m^2. Let L_thr and Phi_thr be subject- and modality-specific thresholds. Let air temperature T_air in degrees Celsius and relative humidity RH in percent. The veto predicate is V=1 if L_s<L_thr, or Phi_red<Phi_thr, or |T_air-21|>3 °C, or the subject is not in a rested, supine or seated baseline state; otherwise V=0. If V=1, all predictions for p(t), D_i(t), D_e(t), f_R(t), and V_T(t) are vetoed. Page 376 does not report L_s, Phi_thr, T_air, RH, or posture [PAGE 376], so the equations are conditional on demonstrating stimulus adequacy.

Biomechanical layer: breath-holding under attention is limited by laryngeal adduction and respiratory-muscle fatigue. Maximal voluntary apnea in ordinary subjects lasts seconds to roughly a minute depending on training, after which chemosensory override forces inspiration. The model D_i(t)=0 during high A(t) therefore has a hard upper duration T_hold,max; beyond it, the veto predicate adds 1{t_hold>T_hold,max}. Anger-related expiratory hardness is modeled as increased R_aw and reduced peak expiratory flow in L/s; if the subject cannot sustain expiratory effort because of glottic closure or bronchoconstriction, the expiration equation reaches a flow ceiling and f_R cannot compensate indefinitely. Weather and ambient conditions are not in the source, but if uncontrolled they veto generalization: cold dry air raises airway resistance and alters V_T; warm humid air changes evaporative and thermoregulatory breathing. All vetoes are physical, not statistical: no amount of repeated observation can validate a model whose input never crossed the sensory threshold or whose output violated muscle and gas-exchange limits.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Red-light threshold veto. Manipulation: in condition A, present the page-376 red light from t_a to t_b at suprathreshold corneal irradiance Phi_red>Phi_thr [PAGE 376]. In counterfactual condition B, present an otherwise identical light with Phi_red<Phi_thr verified by photodiode, or have the subject wear verified opaque goggles. Measure ECG-derived pulse p(t) at 1 Hz for t in [t_a-10, t_b+60] seconds. Model prediction: in condition A, p(t)-p_0 exits the ±2 sigma_p noise band within tau_s after t_a or returns within tau_p after t_b; in condition B, p(t)-p_0 remains within ±2 sigma_p for the entire interval. Verifiable veto condition: if condition B shows a deflection exceeding the 95% band, or condition A shows no deflection across n≥20 paired trials, veto the red-light pulse model. Probability statement: the veto decision uses alpha=0.05; the null of no threshold effect is rejected only if the posterior probability of a condition difference exceeds 0.95.

Scenario 2: Respiratory-phase veto for sudden sound. Manipulation: deliver a sudden sound at L_s above auditory threshold, randomized across respiratory phase theta_0 estimated by a respiratory belt; measure apnea duration T_ap in seconds and inspiratory-onset latency in seconds. Model prediction: catch-breath probability follows P_cb(theta_0)=sigma(alpha+beta cos theta_0), with beta credibly nonzero; end-expiratory sounds produce longer T_ap than end-inspiratory sounds because of lung-volume history. Verifiable veto condition: across n≥30 trials, if the 95% credible interval for beta includes zero, veto the phase-gated clock model. Units: theta_0 radians, T_ap seconds, L_s dB SPL.

Scenario 3: Fear-pant hypocapnia veto. Manipulation: expose the subject to the feared object described on page 376 [PAGE 376] while measuring f_R in breaths/min, V_T in liters, and end-tidal CO2 P_ETCO2 in mmHg. Model prediction: fear raises f_R and constrains V_T below V_T,max such that dot V_E=f_R V_T increases minute ventilation enough to lower P_ETCO2 below 35 mmHg within 60 seconds, with possible paresthesia if P_ETCO2 approaches 30 mmHg; these values are model thresholds, not source values. The anger clause can be tested analogously by measuring peak expiratory flow in L/s; if anger does not reduce expiratory flow relative to neutral expectancy, veto the anger-expiration-constraint model. All three scenarios are falsifiable because they specify counterfactual input manipulations, measured SI quantities, and explicit rejection regions.
