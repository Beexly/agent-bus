Hop two: the reaction is conditioned not only to a tone S but to a whole zone of tones of other pitches and intensities spreading in both directions along each dimension from the point conditioned. [PAGE 197] calls this primary stimulus generalization, and the functional equivalence of these stimuli primary stimulus equivalence. Hop three: experiments show that habit strength generalized to stimuli other than S diminishes progressively as the difference between S and S' increases. When this difference is measured in units of the discrimination threshold (j.n.d.), [PAGE 197] asserts that "the gradient of generalization closely approximates a simple negative growth or decay function." Hop four: knowing habit strength at reinforcement points is sufficient to predict reaction potentiality provided motivation (drive) remains constant. Hop five: the actual or effective habit strength mobilizable by a given evoking stimulus S is a joint function of habit strength at reinforced points and the difference between S and those points.

Gray-zone audit. First, the source text does not specify the exact mathematical law of the negative growth or decay function; "closely approximates" is insufficient to close the summation operator numerically. Is the kernel exponential, hyperbolic, or Gaussian? Second, ssHR is not defined operationally: summation may be linear, thresholded, or subject to divisive normalization, and the text offers no equation. Third, the ceteris paribus clause "drive remaining constant" hides a multiplicative singularity: if drive D varies across trials or subjects, the prediction of reaction potentiality cannot be isolated from habit strength alone. Fourth, j.n.d. units presume commensurability across dimensions (pitch and intensity) that is not established; cross-modal j.n.d. equating is an open psychometric problem. Fifth, breakdown regimes include D → 0 (zero response regardless of ssHR), Δ → ∞ (asymptotic habit strength may not reach zero if higher-order equivalence exists), and sensory transduction limits imposing a minimum resolvable Δ below which the gradient is physically indistinguishable from a step function. These gray zones mean that [PAGE 197] provides a qualitative topology of generalization but not a closed-form predictive calculus without auxiliary assumptions.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be a psychophysical stimulus space. A conditioned stimulus is \(S \in \mathcal{S}\); a test or generalizing stimulus is \(S' \in \mathcal{S}\). Let \(H(S_i)\) denote the habit strength at reinforced point \(S_i\), measured in dimensionless Hull habit units. Let \(\Delta_i = \| S' - S_i \|_{\text{j.n.d.}}\) be the separation between test and reinforced points expressed in just-noticeable-difference units (dimensionless ratio of j.n.d.s). Let \(G(\Delta)\) be the generalization-gradient kernel. Hull's assertion on [PAGE 197] that the gradient "closely approximates a simple negative growth or decay function" is formalized as:

\[ G(0) = 1, \qquad \frac{dG}{d\Delta} < 0, \qquad \lim_{\Delta \to \infty} G(\Delta) = 0. \]

A specific admissible choice consistent with the text is the negative exponential

\[ G(\Delta) = e^{-\lambda \Delta}, \qquad \lambda > 0, \]

where \(\lambda\) is the discrimination-sharpness parameter in units of j.n.d.\(^{-1}\). Because [PAGE 197] does not fix the functional form, \(G\) must be treated as an unknown decay kernel until fit to data.

The summed effective habit strength mobilizable by evoking stimulus \(S'\) is

\[ ssHR(S') = \sum_{i=1}^{n} H(S_i) \, G\!\left( \| S' - S_i \|_{\text{j.n.d.}} \right), \]

with units of Hull habit strength (dimensionless). Reaction potentiality \(\mathcal{P}\), measured as a probability in \([0,1]\) or a response rate in \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), is then

\[ \mathcal{P}(R \mid S') = \Phi\bigl( ssHR(S'), \, D \bigr), \]

where \(D\) is drive/motivation, assumed constant on [PAGE 197]. \(\Phi\) is monotonically increasing in both arguments; a breakdown singularity occurs at \(D = 0\), where \(\mathcal{P} = 0\) for all \(ssHR\).

Breakdown regimes:
1. \(D \to 0\): motivational collapse; no reaction potentiality regardless of habit summation.
2. \(\Delta_i \to 0\) for all \(i\): perfect primary stimulus equivalence; gradient collapses to identity.
3. \(\Delta_i \gg 1\) for all \(i\): asymptotic regime where \(ssHR \to 0\); prediction fails if baseline responding exceeds this asymptote.
4. Cross-dimensional j.n.d. incommensurability: if pitch and intensity j.n.d. are not on a common scale, \(\| \cdot \|_{\text{j.n.d.}}\) is undefined.
5. Non-stationarity: \(H(S_i)\) decays with extinction or recovers with spontaneous recovery, violating the fixed-reinforcement-point assumption.

[PAGE 197] verbatim: "the gradient of generalization closely approximates a simple negative growth or decay function"; "motivation (drive) remaining constant"; "primary stimulus generalization"; "primary stimulus equivalence."
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage: Hull's 1943 Principles of Behavior [PAGE 197] synthesizes Pavlovian conditioned-reflex physiology with a quantitative drive-habit formalism. The stimulus-generalization gradient anticipates Spence's (1936, 1956) differentiation theory of excitation and inhibition gradients, Rescorla and Wagner's (1972) error-correction learning over stimulus elements, Pearce's (1987) configural theory of stimulus compounds, and modern kernel-based machine learning (radial-basis-function networks and Gaussian processes), where the generalization kernel \(G(\Delta)\) is mathematically identical to a covariance function over stimulus space. The lineage runs from physiological reflexology to computational learning theory, but each link assumes a fixed metric over stimulus dimensions.

Multi-week traps: In longitudinal experiments spanning weeks, the j.n.d. is not a fixed constant. Sensory adaptation, threshold drift, and criterion shifts alter the effective j.n.d. between sessions, causing the same physical \(\|S - S'\|\) to occupy different j.n.d. coordinates week to week. If the experimenter fails to re-estimate the j.n.d. weekly, apparent changes in the generalization gradient will be misattributed to learning or extinction when they are actually sensory re-calibration. A second trap is overnight consolidation: habit strength \(H(S_i)\) may increase during post-training consolidation, so a Monday gradient does not predict a Tuesday gradient without re-measuring \(H\). A third trap is renewal after extinction: extinguishing \(S\) on week one can produce spontaneous recovery at \(S'\) in week three, violating the static summation assumption of [PAGE 197].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under acute stress or crisis, an organism's sensory noise increases, effectively raising the j.n.d. and flattening the generalization gradient \(G(\Delta)\). Behavior regresses from narrow primary stimulus equivalence to broad equivalence: stimuli that would normally be discriminated now evoke the same reaction \(R\), a panic-generalization mode. The drive variable \(D\) also rises under crisis, multiplicatively amplifying \(ssHR\) and increasing reaction potentiality even at remote \(S'\).

Counter-trap: A differential contingency can narrow the gradient even during crisis. If only the exact \(S\) is reinforced and nearby \(S'\) are placed on extinction or punishment, the effective kernel sharpens (\(\lambda\) increases), producing counter-generalization discrimination despite elevated drive.

Clock: Hull's summation operates over a temporal integration window \(\tau\). If inter-trial intervals are shorter than \(\tau\), effective habit strength sums temporally across trials as well as across stimulus points, producing temporal generalization. If intervals exceed \(\tau\), the summation resets and each trial is evaluated independently. The clock therefore couples the spatial gradient to the trial schedule.

Recursive theory-of-mind nesting: The experimenter models the subject's internal \(ssHR(S')\) and \(D\). The subject, in turn, may model the experimenter's reinforcement schedule and modulate \(D\) strategically (e.g., frustration-induced drive changes). This second-order modeling means that observed generalization gradients can reflect the subject's belief about contingency volatility, not merely the stimulus geometry described on [PAGE 197].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Three veto layers must be cleared before any observed primary stimulus equivalence is accepted as genuine Hullian generalization.

1. Physical/psychophysical layer. For auditory tones, the cochlea imposes critical bands and frequency-resolution limits. If \(\|S - S'\|\) falls within a critical band, the afferent discharge \(s\) cannot physically discriminate the two stimuli, so behavioral equivalence is forced by the periphery, not learned habit. Veto condition: if \(\Delta < \Delta_{\text{phys,min}}\), the observed equivalence is a transduction artifact.

2. Biomechanical/motor layer. Multiple distinct efferent discharges \(r\) may converge on the same observable reaction \(R\) because the effectors lack fine topographic control. Output-layer equivalence masks underlying gradient structure. Veto condition: if two stimuli produce identical \(R\) but different latent \(r\) patterns (measured electromyographically or kinematically), behavioral equivalence is a measurement floor effect.

3. Environmental/weather-analog layer. Background noise, temperature, and barometric conditions alter signal detectability, effectively raising the j.n.d. and flattening \(G(\Delta)\). A low signal-to-noise ratio \(\rho\) produces pseudo-generalization. Veto condition: if \(\rho < 1\) (or the experimentally determined detection threshold), gradient estimates are vetoed as environmental confounds.

Adjudication rule: An observed \(ssHR(S') > 0\) at an unreinforced point is admitted as genuine stimulus generalization only if all three layers simultaneously fail to veto; that is, the stimuli are physically discriminable, the motor output can dissociate, and environmental SNR is sufficient. [PAGE 197] provides no such layer-crossing controls, so its claims hold strictly under "favorable experimental conditions" where these confounds are held constant.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each is falsifiable and generates a precise prediction.

Veto Scenario 1 — Drive equating. Hold reinforcement points and \(ssHR\) constant across two groups, but manipulate drive \(D\) through differential food deprivation. Prediction: raw response rates scale with \(D\), but when normalized by \(D\), the generalization-gradient slope \(\lambda\) is invariant across groups. Failure condition: if normalized slopes diverge, the gradient is not purely habit-based; veto any claim that drive-independent learning changed the gradient.

Veto Scenario 2 — j.n.d. equating across modalities. Pre-measure psychophysical j.n.d. for tone pitch and tone intensity in the same subjects. Construct test stimuli \(S'\) that are equidistant from \(S\) in j.n.d. units but different in physical units. Prediction: j.n.d.-equated \(S'\) values collapse onto the same negative decay curve \(G(\Delta)\); physical-equidistant values do not. Failure condition: if physical-equidistant values match but j.n.d.-equated values do not, the j.n.d. metric is invalid for this stimulus space; veto cross-dimensional gradient comparisons.

Veto Scenario 3 — Extinction probe at an unreinforced point. Extinguish \(S\) to zero habit strength, then probe a nearby unreinforced \(S'\). Prediction under pure Hullian summation: response at \(S'\) decays proportionally to \(G(\Delta)\) and is not immediately zero. Failure condition: if response at \(S'\) persists at asymptote despite extinction of \(S\), or if response at \(S'\) extinguishes faster than \(G(\Delta)\) predicts, the representation is configural rather than elemental; veto the simple summation equation on [PAGE 197] for that subject population.

These scenarios are verifiable with standard psychophysical and operant equipment and require no betting lines, odds, or wagering constructs.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
