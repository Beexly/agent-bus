The lineage of James’s argument runs from 19th-century psychophysics through associationist psychology into modern predictive coding. Fechner’s elementarpsychophysik and Helmholtz’s “unconscious inference” established that perception is not a direct copy of the proximal stimulus but is constructed by the nervous system; James’s claim that the form of the psychophysical process depends on “the constitution of the entire nervous apparatus … and on the state in which it finds itself” ([PAGE 1]) is a direct descendant of Helmholtzian inference. The associationist tradition (Hartley, Bain, Spencer) held that repeated co-occurrence strengthens mental connections; James operationalizes this as the nerve-substance’s “reproductive power, or memory,” whereby “every particular activity of the organ makes it more suited to a repetition of the same” ([PAGE 1]). This anticipates Hebb’s 1949 cell-assembly rule (“cells that fire together wire together”) and long-term potentiation, though James supplies no molecular mechanism, only the phenomenological law of ever-smaller thresholds for recurrence.

The “elective reproduction” doctrine—that common portions across similar images awaken a stronger echo ([PAGE 1])—prefigures Bartlett’s constructive memory (1932), Neisser’s perceptual cycle, and contemporary predictive-processing accounts in which priors (habit traces) weight sensory evidence (Friston, 2010; Rao & Ballard, 1999). James’s refusal to interpolate an unobservable “pure sensation” between image and percept ([PAGE 1]) is an operationalist or phenomenalist move echoing Mach and the early positivists, yet it leaves the transformation \mathcal R unnamed, creating a black box that modern computational neuroscience attempts to open.

Multi-week traps. In a longitudinal design spanning weeks, the central trap is to treat \mathbf h_t as stationary when it is explicitly cumulative: each week of repeated exposure adds to the habit trace, so the “same stimulus” yields a percept that drifts systematically (\mathcal P_t \neq \mathcal P_{t-7}) even under optical constancy. A second trap is to attribute this drift to a changing “pure sensation,” which James correctly brackets as unknowable ([PAGE 1]); the drift is instead in \mathbf h. A third trap is attentional crystallization: if the same portion is attended week after week, the inequality among constituents becomes chronic, effectively producing a functional attentional scotoma for the unattended portions. A fourth trap is to assume that re-testing after a long inter-session interval resets \mathbf h; if T_{\text{ISI}} exceeds \tau_h, the trace decays and the percept may regress toward the initial state, mimicking “learning from scratch” when in fact the system has passed through a decay-and-reacquisition cycle.
