<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Coach–opponent interaction is a recursive theory-of-mind loop: Coach A models Coach B’s model of Coach A’s formation. AI changes the fixed point of this loop by exposing collective dynamics (graph metrics, pitch control, EPV) that were previously opaque [PAGE 1].

Crisis habit regression: under pressure, teams regress to previously drilled formations. Narizuka & Yamazaki identified average formations “1-4-4-2,” “1-4-1-4-1,” and “1-4-3-3” from Delaunay clustering [PAGE 12, 18]. If the opponent’s classifier recognizes a habitual low-block, the regressed habit is predictable and exploitable.

Counter-trap: Forcher et al. identified defensive principles—pressing the ball-leading player and creating numerical superiority near the ball—as predictors of successful ball gains [PAGE 11]. A counter-trap consists of deliberately inviting the press in one zone to create a numerical superiority in an adjacent zone, then switching play. AI-based pass-effectiveness models (Goes et al.) quantify which passes disrupt opposing defenses, enabling the counter-trap to be pre-computed [PAGE 16–17].

Clock: Stival et al. showed that the first five seconds after ball possession predict whether the ball reaches the final quarter and creates a goal-scoring chance [PAGE 13]. At 10 Hz sampling, that window is 500 frames; at 5 Hz it is 250 frames. The control clock is the latency budget between ball acquisition and tactical instruction. If model inference plus communication latency exceeds the possession dwell time, the real-time decision-support loop is open and the veto is triggered. Explainability is the missing recursive layer: “black box” CNN/LSTM outputs erode coach trust because the coach cannot verify why the model assigned high pitch control to a specific sector [PAGE 20–21]. Without XAI, the coach cannot model the model, collapsing the recursion into blind automation.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: tracking derives from GPS, LPM, or VBMA raw data (x, y, z) [PAGE 2]. Sampling rates are 5, 10, 25, or 30 Hz where reported [PAGE 10, 14], but 16 of 32 studies do not describe sampling frequency [PAGE 14]. Veto: any real-time tactical model certified at 30 Hz cannot be safely deployed on a 5 Hz stream without a proven anti-aliasing filter; player-motion extrapolation \(g(x,t_{pj},s_i)=t\) assumes continuous trajectory observability that sub-Nyquist sampling violates [PAGE 5].

Biomechanical layer: 3D ball trajectories and player trajectories are modeled as sequences indexed by global time [PAGE 8]. The motion model infers direction and velocity from trajectory at time step \(s_i\) [PAGE 5]. Veto: fatigue and injury are outside the review’s exclusion criteria [PAGE 3]; thus no biomechanical fatigue state (e.g., reduced sprint capacity altering pressing radius) is represented in the tactical graph. A model that ignores accumulated load may press with a fatigued player whose actual velocity envelope has contracted.

Weather layer: no meteorological covariates (temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation) appear in Table 2 formulas, Table 3 platforms, or Table 4 outcomes [PAGE 4–13]. Veto: any claim that pitch-control efficiency or pass probability is invariant to weather is unverified; wind alters 3D ball trajectory \(p\) correlation metrics [PAGE 8], and rain alters surface friction and hence player-motion model validity. The source text does not adjudicate weather; this absence is itself a veto condition for weather-conditioned tactical deployment.

Platform/provenance layer: data come from SPADL, STATS LLC, Prozone, Opta, StatsBomb, TRACAB, InStat, Whyscout, Metrica, and others [PAGE 14]. Veto: cross-platform transfer learning is blocked by proprietary data structures and non-standard sampling; a pitch-control classifier trained on TRACAB Bundesliga data cannot be assumed valid on SPADL EPL data without re-derivation.

Ethical layer: data privacy, algorithmic bias, and responsible use of player tracking data are flagged as unresolved [PAGE 20–21]. Veto: deployment without player consent frameworks and bias audits fails the professional-profile standard the review itself demands.

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-from-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios:

Veto Scenario 1 — Underspecified sampling frequency. Counterfactual: if all 32 studies had reported \(f_s \in \{5,10,25,30\text{ Hz}\}\), a temporal alignment layer could resample streams to a common clock. Actual state: 16 studies have \(f_s = \text{ND}\) [PAGE 14]. Veto: block real-time substitution and pressing-schedule models. Prediction: deployed on mismatched \(f_s\), out-of-distribution error in player-motion extrapolation exceeds 15% because sprint acceleration events (duration < 0.4 s) are aliased below Nyquist.

Veto Scenario 2 — Proprietary platform lock-in. Counterfactual: if tracking data were published in an open benchmark schema (SPADL/StatsBomb unified), cross-league transfer could be validated. Actual state: platforms are fragmented (STATS, Opta, Prozone, TRACAB, etc.) [PAGE 14]. Veto: block transfer of a pitch-control classifier from EPL to Campeonato Brasileiro Série A. Prediction: zero-shot transfer accuracy drops by >20 percentage points because pitch dimensions, camera calibration, and sampling jitter differ across leagues; fine-tuning requires local labeled data not present in the corpus.

Actual state: ethical/XAI consensus is “dubious” and model transparency is not standardized [PAGE 20–21]. Veto: block coach adoption of automated defensive-shift recommendations. Prediction: coach acceptance remains below 30% and false-positive press triggers increase, because the recursive theory-of-mind loop cannot close when the coach cannot model why the AI designated a zone as controlled.
