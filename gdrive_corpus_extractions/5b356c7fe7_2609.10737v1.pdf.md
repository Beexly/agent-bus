<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual lineage of TCC begins with distribution-free conformal prediction under exchangeability: Vovk et al. (2005), Shafer & Vovk (2008), Lei et al. (2018), and Angelopoulos & Bates (2023) establish that split-conformal calibration yields finite-sample marginal coverage when calibration and test data are exchangeable ([PAGE 1–2]). The paper inherits the split-conformal order-statistic convention bq_{1−α} = S_{(⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉)} ([PAGE 4], footnote 1).

The shift-aware branch draws on importance-weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Shimodaira, 2000; Sugiyama et al., 2007), which recovers target coverage via density ratios when source and target share a feature space ([PAGE 2]). TCC departs from this by explicitly rejecting the common-space assumption: X_s ≠ X_t, so direct reweighting is inapplicable and unlabeled pairing becomes the indispensable resource ([PAGE 2]). Online and adaptive conformal procedures (Gibbs & Candès, 2021, 2024) address drifting distributions over time, but TCC's setting is a one-shot sensor or modality transition where labeled target calibration is unavailable ([PAGE 2]).

Domain adaptation supplies the transport machinery: Ganin et al. (2016) adversarial domain alignment, Isola et al. (2017) pix2pix paired image translation, Zhu et al. (2017) CycleGAN unpaired translation, and Courty et al. (2017) optimal-transport alignment ([PAGE 3]). Source-free domain adaptation (Liang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022) adapts predictors using only unlabeled target data, but targets accuracy rather than distribution-free coverage ([PAGE 3]). TCC repurposes transport not to improve point predictions, but to relocate labeled calibration information into X_t and then certify residual mismatch.

"Multi-week traps" emerge from the temporal dimension of deployment. In real sensor upgrades or modality changes, the target distribution is not static across weeks; the transport map f learned at installation may stale as hardware drifts, seasonal lighting changes, or processing pipelines are patched. The paper's finite-sample guarantee in Theorem 3.2 is conditional on a fixed δ+ estimated at deployment time ([PAGE 5]). If δ+ is estimated from an early transport checkpoint (e.g., SAR→RGB epoch 5: δ+ = 0.7405, [PAGE 24], Table 16) but the system runs for weeks on an improved map (epoch 150: δ+ = 0.3200), the certificate is stale and over-conservative. Conversely, if δ+ is estimated late and the target domain subsequently degrades (severe shot noise or motion blur at severity 4–5, [PAGE 6], Table 2), the certificate understates mismatch and under-coverage can occur (transport-only 0.875 vs. oracle 0.899 at α=0.1). The trap is therefore a recursive one: each week of operation without re-estimating δ+ and re-checking ESS% invalidates the assumption that the unlabeled diagnostic still tracks the true-label score gap δS under A2 ([PAGE 5]).

Crisis habit regression describes the operator's instinctive fallback when coverage is observed to drop: revert to the most familiar, label-intensive procedure or to the simplest uncorrected transport. In the paper's stress test, Transported CP and weighted-TCC exhibit mild under-coverage at α = 0.1 under shot noise and motion blur severities 4–5 ([PAGE 6], Table 2). This regression is cognitively cheap but statistically wasteful.

The counter-trap is TCC-KS's guardrail logic. Rather than regressing to naive transport, TCC-KS uses the label-free certificate δ+ to detect mismatch before coverage failure and intentionally tightens α⋆ = max(0, α − δ+) ([PAGE 4], Eq. 11). In all 11 under-coverage cases, TCC-KS restores valid coverage (100% recovery success), raising mean coverage from 87.9% to 97.9% with mean set-size inflation 1.82× ([PAGE 28], Appendix G.1). Weighted-TCC acts as a counter-trap to conservatism: when residual weights are stable (ESS% > 70%), it approaches target-calibrated efficiency without the KS penalty ([PAGE 28], Appendix G.4). On Tiny-ImageNet-C +KL epoch 150, weighted-TCC achieves 0.900 coverage with size 69.71 while TCC-KS remains conservative at 0.935 with size 86.33 ([PAGE 24], Table 15). The counter-trap is conditional: it is only safe to invoke when ESS% is high and δ+ ≤ α; otherwise it reintroduces under-coverage risk.

The clock is the sample-splitting and training-epoch schedule mandated by Assumption A3 ([PAGE 5]). Transport learning, conformal threshold estimation, and δ+ estimation must occupy disjoint unlabeled splits or cross-fitting folds, creating temporal isolation that prevents leakage. On SAR→RGB, δ+ decays from 0.7405 at epoch 5 to 0.3200 at epoch 150 under +KL ([PAGE 24], Table 16), and ESS% rises from 35.3 to 39.5 ([PAGE 24], Table 16). Using a δ+ computed at epoch 5 to calibrate a model operating at epoch 150 over-covers (excessive conservatism); using a δ+ from epoch 150 after a sudden sensor degradation under-covers. The clock also governs deployment operations: Appendix G.5 prescribes monitoring δ+, α⋆, ESS%, and set-size inflation on a dashboard, with alerts if δ+ > α (review shift) or δ+ > 2α (prioritize investigation) ([PAGE 28]). The recursive theory of mind is therefore: anticipate that operators will regress to Transported CP under crisis, pre-empt with TCC-KS's automatic guardrail, weight-gate weighted-TCC with ESS%, and re-synchronize the clock whenever transport checkpoints or target conditions change.

Physical layer veto: The paper's motivating scenarios are sensor upgrades and modality changes where labeled calibration exists only in a legacy space X_s while predictions are required in X_t ([PAGE 1]). SAR→RGB is a physical cross-modal shift: SAR (x_s) and RGB (x_t) are co-registered tiles with four land-cover classes ([PAGE 18], Appendix B.1). Imperfect or approximate pairing primarily degrades f, increasing residual mismatch between ˜X_t = f(X_s) and X_t ([PAGE 9], Scope and Limitations). When pairing is weak, TCC-KS's δ+ grows and forces α⋆ → 0, physically vetoing tight prediction sets. On SAR→RGB no-KL epoch 5, δ+ = 0.7405 ⇒ α⋆ = 0, coverage 1.000, size 4.00 ([PAGE 24], Table 16).

Biomechanical/operational layer veto: Expert annotation requirements, regulatory constraints, and rapid deployment needs make labeled target calibration prohibitive ([PAGE 1]). This is an operational veto on standard split-conformal calibration, which requires labeled target data ([PAGE 2]). TCC's entire design is a workaround for this veto, using unlabeled paired data U instead. The finite-sample analysis explicitly assumes disjoint splits for transport learning, calibration, and mismatch estimation (Assumption A3, [PAGE 5]), which reduces effective sample size when unlabeled budgets are small ([PAGE 9]).

Weather/environmental layer veto: CIFAR-100-C and Tiny-ImageNet-C corruptions (contrast, brightness, shot noise, motion blur) model environmental degradation ([PAGE 7], Section 4.1). At severities 4–5, these approach support-mismatch regimes where overlap and importance weighting degrade ([PAGE 7]). Transport-only CP under-covers at α = 0.1 (0.875 vs. 0.899 oracle), but TCC-KS vetoes the under-coverage by inflating sets (0.984 coverage, 1.76× inflation on Tiny-ImageNet-C motion blur severity 2, [PAGE 6], Table 4). Shot noise and motion blur are the weather analogues: high-severity atmospheric or sensor noise that breaks the surrogate–score coupling assumed in A2.

Veto adjudication hierarchy:
1. Primary signal: δ+ > α triggers TCC-KS conservative mode (α⋆ = max(0, α − δ+), [PAGE 4]). If δ+ > 2α, Appendix G.5 recommends prioritizing investigation as a high-severity mismatch regime ([PAGE 28]).
2. Secondary signal: ESS% < 50% vetoes weighted-TCC because residual weights concentrate; defer to TCC-KS ([PAGE 28], Appendix G.5). On Tiny-ImageNet-C no-KL epoch 5, ESS% = 13.0 and weighted-TCC coverage (0.976) falls below TCC-KS (1.000) at α = 0.1 ([PAGE 24], Table 15).
3. Tertiary signal: surrogate–score decoupling (low ρ(T,S)) vetoes all label-free methods. On SAR→RGB no-KL epoch 5, ρ(T,S) = 0.120 versus 0.966 for +KL epoch 150 ([PAGE 7], Table 5). In such regimes, TCC-KS's δ+ no longer bounds δS under A2, and a small labeled target audit (50–100 examples) is recommended ([PAGE 28], Appendix G.5).

This layered veto is weather-proof in the sense that TCC-KS remains valid even when physical, operational, and environmental conditions jointly degrade: the worst-case cost is conservative set growth, not silent under-coverage, provided δ+ is recomputed and A2 is not catastrophically violated.
