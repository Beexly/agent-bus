Instead, Tolman argues this supports his "Cognitive Map" hypothesis: the animal has formed a latent representation of the spatial layout. If the recent forced path was a "wrong" turn in the context of the eventual goal, the cognitive map might incorporate this as a dead-end or a complex route, creating interference when a simpler path (the test habit) is required. This suggests that learning efficiency is governed by the statistical structure of the input stream, not just the summation of discrete S-R bonds. High alternation prevents the formation of stable, chunked expectancies, leading to a fragmented cognitive map.

**Hop 3: Gray-Zone Audit — From Cognitive Maps to Predictive Coding.**
Where does Tolman's framework hold, and where does it fracture? The primary gray zone is the nature of the "map." Tolman posits a static, topological representation. Modern neuroscience, however, frames this through the lens of predictive coding and Free Energy Principles [FEP]. The brain minimizes surprisal (variational free energy) by updating generative models. In this light, the "mean number of trials to form habit" can be reinterpreted as the time required for the prediction error associated with the forced history to decay below a decision threshold under a new reward contingency. The "recency effect" anomaly can be modeled as a conflict between a strong short-term prior (induced by the recent forced turns) and a newly introduced reward signal. If the recent turns were frequent but unrewarded, the model assigns them low value; however, their motor execution creates a strong kinematic trace. When reward is finally introduced on that same side, there is a conflict between the kinematic prior (which says "this path is familiar but empty") and the new reward likelihood. This conflict increases the "surprisal" of the correct answer, delaying learning—a phenomenon Tolman noted but could not quantify probabilistically.

The "Trial and Error" doctrine is thereby reduced to a special case: it only appears valid when the generative model is so simplistic that it cannot compress the history into anything other than a noisy sequence of errors. Tolman’s refutation stands because he proved that *structure* in the error stream dictates the speed of subsequent success, proving the agent is doing more than blind trial and error—it is implicitly parsing the statistical grammar of its environment.

Let the agent's knowledge at time $t$ be represented by a posterior distribution $q_t(Z)$ over a set of latent maps $Z$. The environment emits a sequence of sensory-motor transitions $D = \{(s_t, a_t)\}_{t=1}^T$, where $a_t \in \{L, R\}$ are forced actions.

**Operator Definition:**
The change in the cognitive map is governed by a Variational Gradient Descent step minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the approximate posterior and the true posterior implied by the data:
$$ \Delta q_t(Z) = -\eta \nabla_{q} \mathbb{E}_{p(s,a|Z)} [ -\log p(a|s,Z) + \beta \text{KL}[q(Z) || p(Z)] ] $$
where $\eta$ is the learning rate and $\beta$ controls the regularization towards the prior $p(Z)$.

**Unit of Analysis: Surprisal Decay.**
The "Mean no. of trials to form habit" corresponds to the number of updates $N$ required for the probability mass of the optimal policy $\pi^*$ to exceed a confidence threshold $\theta$ (e.g., $p(\pi^*) > 0.95$).

**Formalizing the "Interlarding" Effect:**
We can quantify the "solid blocks" vs "interlarded" conditions using **Mutual Information** between adjacent steps in the training sequence. Let $X_t$ be the binary variable for the turn at step $t$.
The Serial Correlation Coefficient $\rho$ for the sequence determines the effective dimensionality of the learned map:
- For Solid Blocks ($LLLLLL...RRRRRR$): $\rho \approx 1$. The transition matrix is nearly deterministic, allowing the model to compress the sequence into two distinct states (Left Zone, Right Zone).
- For Interlarded ($LRLRLR...$): $\rho \approx -1$. The transition matrix represents a perfect oscillator.

The learning difficulty $D$ (measured in trials) can be approximated by the inverse of the mutual information $I(X_{t}; X_{t-1})$ available to reduce uncertainty about the future:
$$ D \propto \frac{1}{I(X_t; X_{t+1} | Z_{learned})} $$
When $X_t$ alternates perfectly, knowing $X_t$ gives maximum information about $X_{t+1}$ (it must be the opposite), but zero information about the *goal-directed* affordances of the space if the reward location is invariant. The model learns a rigid temporal loop rather than a spatial map, causing severe interference when the task switches from "follow the forced pattern" to "find the goal". This explains why Pattern $LRLRLR...$ yields $D = 9.67$ (maximum difficulty).

**Formalizing the "Recency" Anomaly:**
Standard S-R theories posit an additive accumulation of associative strength $V$: $\Delta V = \alpha (\lambda - V)$. Under this model, the most recent stimulus-response pair should have the highest $V$, facilitating learning. Tolman’s observation that high recency *impedes* learning requires a non-linear interaction term. We introduce a **Predictive Conflict Term** $C_t$:
$$ C_t = \| \hat{r}_{model}(s_t) - r_{observed} \|^2 $$
If the most recent forced turns consistently led to no reward (during pre-training), the model's predicted reward $\hat{r}$ for those states approaches zero. When the test phase introduces a reward on that exact side, the sudden spike in prediction error generates a large $C_t$. The system interprets this not as immediate confirmation, but as a violation of its well-established zero-reward map, necessitating deeper model revision (higher $N$). This mathematically formalizes Tolman’s statement: "the greater the recency, the more difficult thereafter to learn to go to that side."

**Breakdown Regimes:**
These equations break down if the agent possesses no internal model (purely reflexive). In such a limit, $q(Z)$ collapses to a delta function on the immediate stimulus, reducing the operator to simple Hebbian plasticity, thereby artificially resurrecting the "Trial and Error" doctrine. Tolman’s experiment is specifically designed to push the agent out of this breakdown regime into the cognitive domain.
