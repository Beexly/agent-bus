The derivation chain I follow here is a five-hop deduction from manifold geometry to a real-time engineering constraint, and each hop introduces a distinct failure surface.

**Hop 1 — Why pose is not a vector.** The paper defines the pose space on [PAGE 5] as the set of 4×4 homogeneous matrices with a right-multiplied symmetry group: `C = {P | P = [R·G t; 0^T 1], t ∈ R³, R ∈ SO(3), G ∈ SO(3)}`. This is a *quotient-space* statement disguised as a matrix equation. `R·G` with `G` free over a continuous symmetry subgroup means the map from parameters to rendered appearance is many-to-one; therefore no loss defined on raw parameters can be identifiable for the "Cookie Jar" model. The authors state this consequence explicitly: for spherical symmetry, "the description of the pose of an object with spherical symmetry requires just 3 numbers: (tx,y,z), as G can be any instance of SO(3) with the imprinted shape of the object remaining the same" [PAGE 5]. Hop 1 is therefore: *identifiability* fails before *accuracy* fails.

**Hop 2 — Why the loss must be Riemannian.** Because SO(3) is a compact Lie group with a bi-invariant metric, the correct residual is the geodesic length on the manifold, `arccos((Tr(ΔR̂ᵀ·ΔR_GT) − 1)/2)` [PAGE 6, Eq. 2], in radians. But this residual has a *non-Lipschitz gradient* at both `ΔR = I` and `ΔR` = rotation by π: the derivative of arccos diverges as `∓1/√(1−x²)` → ∓∞ at `x = ±1`. The authors do not state this singularity, but they empirically route around it: "Since the Geodesic distance suﬀers from multiple local minima [6], following [27], we ﬁrst warm-up the weights, aiming ﬁrst to minimize the LogCosh[1] loss function for 25 epochs" [PAGE 9]. LogCosh is the smooth surrogate; the 25-epoch warm-up is a numerical-stability protocol masquerading as an optimization-schedule choice.

**Hop 3 — Why parameterization, not the metric, was the dominant error term.** The ablation on [PAGE 10, Table 3] is the single most informative number set in the paper: baseline 36.38°±36.31°; rotational MSE → 46.55°±40.88° (*worse* than baseline); geodesic only → 37.69°±35.39° (*also marginally worse*); geodesic + 6D continuous representation [40] → 14.90°±21.76°; geodesic + 6D + inertial-tensor weighting → 9.99°±13.76°. The counterintuitive middle of that table is the audit's center of mass: swapping MSE for a geodesic metric *alone* buys nothing (−1.31° is within the reported standard deviation of 36.31°). The entire 26° improvement arrives when the Euler/quaternion parameterization is replaced by the 6D continuous map [PAGE 6, Eq. 3]. Hop 3 therefore inverts the paper's own rhetorical ordering: the continuity of the representation, not the geometry of the metric, is the causal operator.

**Hop 4 — Why the auxiliary heads work without hard segmentation.** The attention modules are trained by two BCE losses against binary masks that are *free byproducts* of synthetic compositing: "both the foreground and unoccluded object binary masks are kept during both of these augmentation procedures. Hence, we can use them as ground truth segmentation signals" [PAGE 8]. This is a supervision-extraction trick, not a perceptual one. It explains the striking qualitative result that "although our supervising signals are uniform, both attentional modules learn to highlight speciﬁc keypoints of interest during the tracker's inference" [PAGE 10] — the masks are uniform, so the keypoint selectivity is emergent from the pose gradient, not from the mask gradient. And because the masks come from the synthetic renderer, they are noise-free, which is exactly the regime in which BCE soft attention outperforms a Mask R-CNN hard-segmentation branch (the iPose-style baseline the authors position against on [PAGE 3]–[PAGE 4]).

**Hop 5 — Why the clock binds.** The reflective-symmetry repair on [PAGE 7] is a *test-time heuristic with an optional second forward pass*: "we may choose to perform a second (or more) iterative forward CNN pass(es), if the time constraints of our broader application allow so" [PAGE 7]. A discrete-symmetry branch that can be iterated is therefore the only component in the system whose latency is *state-dependent*, and it is precisely the component guarding against the worst error mode (a 180° flip that "is not limited to a single frame, but is accumulated as we proceed to the following frames, due to the temporal nature of the tracker, until it is reset" [PAGE 7]).

Units: `t` in mm (dataset frame), `R, G` dimensionless orthogonal with det = +1. Assumption: rigid body, calibrated camera intrinsics, fixed world frame. Breakdown regime: for asymmetric objects `G = I₃` and the set collapses to `SE(3)` (6 DOF); for continuous axial symmetry the stabilizer subgroup is `SO(2)` and the effective parameter count drops by 1; for spherical symmetry it drops by 3. Near a spherical object the Jacobian of the rendering map has rank 3, and any loss on the 6 rotational parameters is structurally rank-deficient.

**Operator O2 — Geodesic rotation residual.** [PAGE 6, Eq. 2]

```
L_Rot(ΔR̂, ΔR_GT) = arccos( ( Tr(ΔR̂ᵀ · ΔR_GT) − 1 ) / 2 )     [rad]
```

Units: radians; converted to degrees only for reporting (all tables report degrees). Assumption: `ΔR̂, ΔR_GT ∈ SO(3)`. Breakdown regimes: (a) `ΔR̂ = ΔR_GT` ⟹ argument = 1 ⟹ `d/dx arccos(x) = −(1−x²)^{-1/2} → −∞`, infinite gradient at zero error; (b) `ΔR` = π-rotation ⟹ argument = −1 ⟹ same divergence with opposite sign; (c) floating-point: `Tr(·)` can drift to `3 + ε` and produce NaN without an explicit `clamp(·, −1, 1)` — the paper does not document a clamp. The LogCosh warm-up on [PAGE 9] is the mitigation for (a).

**Operator O3 — 6D continuous rotation chart.** [PAGE 6, Eq. 3]

```
ΔR_x = N(Δr_x)
ΔR_y = N[ Δr_y − (ΔR_xᵀ · r_y) · ΔR_x ]
ΔR_z = ΔR_x × ΔR_y
N(·) = (·) / ‖(·)‖
```

with `Δr = (Δr_xᵀ, Δr_yᵀ)`, `Δr_{x,y} ∈ R³`, `ΔR_{x,y,z} ∈ R³` as rows, `ΔR = (ΔR_x, ΔR_y, ΔR_z)ᵀ`. Units: dimensionless. Assumption: Gram–Schmidt construction of an orthonormal frame. Breakdown regimes: (a) `‖Δr_x‖ → 0` ⟹ `N(·)` undefined (division by zero); (b) `Δr_y ∥ ΔR_x` ⟹ the subtracted projection removes the entire component ⟹ `ΔR_y → 0` ⟹ degenerate frame (gimbal singularity relocated, not eliminated); (c) this chart is a double cover patch of `SO(3)` and remains continuous but *not* injective — which is why the discrete reflective case still needs a heuristic (Operator O6).

**Operator O4 — Inertial-tensor reweighting.** [PAGE 6]

```
Λ = ∫_S ρ(r) ( ‖r‖² I₃ − r rᵀ ) dS        [kg·m²]
Λ_orth = GramSchmidt(Λ)  ∈ SO(3)
```

The paper states: "we multiply both rotation matrices with an approximately diagonal Inertial Tensor Λ, calculated on the object model's weighted surface and with respect to its center mass … since we want that matrix product to still lie in SO(3), we perform a Gramm-Schmidt orthonormalization on the Inertial Tensor Λ before right-multiplying it with each rotation matrix" [PAGE 6]. Units: kg·m² pre-normalization, dimensionless post-orthonormalization. Breakdown regime: if the principal moments are near-degenerate (a symmetric object), `Λ` is ill-conditioned and Gram–Schmidt amplifies noise in the two nearly-equal axes — precisely the "Cookie Jar" regime. Empirical cost of getting this right: `37.69° → 9.99°` on [PAGE 10, Table 3].

**Operator O5 — Uncertainty-weighted multi-task objective.** [PAGE 8, Eqs. 4–5]

```
L_Track = e^{−v₁}·MSE[(Δt̂, Δt)] + v₁ + v₂
        + e^{−v₂}·arccos( ( Tr[(ΔR̂·Ĝ*·Λ)^{T}·(ΔR·Λ)] − 1 ) / 2 )

Loss = e^{−s₁}·L_Track + e^{−s₂}·L_Unoccl + e^{−s₃}·L_Foreg + s₁ + s₂ + s₃
```

Units: `v, s` are dimensionless log-variance scalars; `MSE` in mm²; the geodesic term in rad. Assumptions: homoscedastic per-task uncertainty (Kendall et al. [20]); both `s` and `v` initialized to zero [PAGE 8]. Breakdown regimes: (a) if a task is intrinsically high-variance, `e^{−s_i} → 0` and the task is silently vetoed by the optimizer — the BCE attention heads can be switched off by the loss itself; (b) parameterization is `e^{−s}L + s`, i.e. `s = log σ²`, not `log σ`; the gradient `∂/∂s = −e^{−s}L + 1` has a fixed point at `e^{s} = L`, so a task whose residual is below 1.0 in its native units is *rewarded* for being ignored; (c) dimensional inhomogeneity across the summands (flagged in the audit).

**Operator O6 — Reflective-symmetry discrete veto.** [PAGE 7]

```
if  d_A^(o)( r̂_i(t), r̂_i(t−1) ) > 100°  then  r̂_i(t) ← r̂_i(t−1)
```

Units: degrees on the Euler chart. Assumption: no physically plausible single-frame rotation exceeds 100° on any Euler component. Breakdown regimes: (a) Euler charts are singular at gimbal lock, so a smooth physical motion can produce an arbitrarily large *chart* derivative; the test is chart-dependent, not metric; (b) genuine fast manipulation is frozen — the veto is one-sided (it can only suppress motion, never inject it), so it trades variance for bias and can lock a permanently wrong branch; (c) it is applied per-component, so partial freezes create non-orthonormal recombinations before the 6D chart re-orthogonalizes them.

**Operator O7 — Continuous-symmetry uniformization barrier.** [PAGE 8, Eqs. 6–7]

```
Loss(Symm) = Loss + e^{−s₄}( (1/B) Σ_{b=1}^{B} 1/ξ_b ) + s₄

ξ_b = 1/(B2(B2−1)) Σ_{j=1}^{B2} Σ_{k≠j} d_Rot^(Geod)( Ĝ_k, Ĝ_j )     [rad]
```

with "we train B2=64 such parameters for each continuous rotational component" [PAGE 8]. Units: `ξ` in rad, `1/ξ` in rad⁻¹. Breakdown regime: `ξ → 0` (all symmetry hypotheses collapse to one) ⟹ `1/ξ → ∞` with gradient `−1/ξ²`, an unbounded, non-Lipschitz barrier; numerically this is the dominant explosion risk in the whole architecture, and it is the only term whose magnitude is unbounded *below*.

**Operator O8 — Kinect noise generative model.** [PAGE 9]

```
n_A ~ N(0, σ_A(z, θ_y)),   n_Lx ~ N(0, σ_Lx(z, θ_y)),   n_Ly ~ N(0, σ_Ly(z, θ_y))
```

"a product of an z-Axial: nA ∼ N(0, σA) and two z-Lateral: nLx ∼ N(0, σLx), nLy ∼ N(0, σLy) 1D distributions that vary with the object depth z and its angle around the y-axis, θy" [PAGE 9]. Units: mm, mm/rad respectively. Breakdown regime: lateral standard deviation grows with `z` and with `1/cos θ_y`-like grazing geometry; at large `z` and high `θ_y` the synthetic training distribution has no support, and the tracker's depth-dominant policy ([PAGE 6]) becomes uncalibrated.

**Reported metrics and their units.** Translation error in mm (mean ± std, plus failure count), rotation error in degrees, Euler-z component error `d_A^(o)(r̂_z, r_z^GT)` in degrees [PAGE 11, Table 5]. Best ablation row: `10.43 ± 6.63` mm / `9.57 ± 10.01`° / `2.07 ± 24.52`°.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage of this paper runs backward along three independent strands that only meet in 2018–2020, and each strand carries its own inherited debt.

**Strand A — the tracking-by-regression lineage.** Garon & Lalonde, *Deep 6-DOF tracking* [7], established the two-stream synthetic-pair formulation: render an "Observed" and a "Predicted" frame from independent viewpoints and regress the delta. Garon, Laurendeau & Lalonde [8] then contributed the benchmark and the baseline that this paper measures against on every table. The retrovirus here is that the *baseline is also the data generator*: the augmentation recipe, the 15-frame re-initialization schedule, the scenario taxonomy ("75% Vertical Occlusion", "Rotation Only", "Hard Interaction"), and the metrics all descend from [8] — so the reported `34.03%` translation and `40.01%` rotation gains [PAGE 1] are improvements *on the lineage's own instrument*. DeepIM [23] (FlowNet2 backbone, subtractive fusion) and PoseRBPF [4] (Rao-Blackwellized particle filter over [33]'s autoencoder) are sibling branches that the paper positions but does not re-implement.

**Strand B — the pose-representation lineage.** Brégier et al. [2] supplies the symmetry-augmented pose definition that becomes Eq. 1 [PAGE 5]. Huynh [15] and Hartley et al. [10] supply the geodesic metric that becomes Eq. 2. Zhou et al. [40] supplies the 6D continuous chart that becomes Eq. 3 — and, critically, [40]'s contribution is *the* load-bearing import: the ablation on [PAGE 10, Table 3] shows `37.69° → 14.90°` from this single substitution. Fletcher [6] is cited for the multiple-local-minima pathology of Riemannian distances, which motivates the LogCosh warm-up. Mahendran et al. [27] supplies the warm-up protocol itself.

**Strand C — the supervision-and-weighting lineage.** The attention heads descend from the mask-based occlusion handling of iPose [18] — "Our attention modules have the same eﬀect, but are computationally cheaper than MaskRCNN, as they relax the requirement for hard segmentation" [PAGE 4] — which is an explicit cost-driven substitution, not a conceptual novelty claim. The loss weighting descends from Kendall, Gal & Cipolla [20], and the paper's own ablation [PAGE 10, Table 4] validates the import: learnable weights `11.63 ± 8.79` mm / `8.31 ± 6.76`° versus steady weights `11.83 ± 8.94` / `10.98 ± 16.74` versus recursive batch standardization via Welford [34] `13.97 ± 10.23` / `14.76 ± 19.24`. Backbone: ResNet18 [13] ImageNet [3] initialization, SqueezeNet Fire modules [16], SUN3D [37] backgrounds, golden-spiral sphere sampling [21], Kinect noise physics [28].

**Multi-week trap 1 — the metric-swap illusion.** A team reading only the abstract will swap MSE for geodesic loss and expect the paper's gains. Table 3 shows this *loses* 1.31° on rotation (36.38° → 37.69°) before the 6D chart is applied. Three weeks can be burned validating a metric change that is, in isolation, null.

**Multi-week trap 2 — the symmetry identifiability trap.** Anyone deploying this on a rotationally symmetric part will find that the `1/ξ` barrier (Eqs. 6–7) is unbounded below and the `100°` Euler veto (Operator O6) is chart-dependent. The paper's own numbers show the residual: even with optimal batch selection, the Cookie Jar rotation std is `10.01°` and the z-Euler std is `24.52°` [PAGE 11, Table 5] — ten times the mean. A team that assumes symmetry handling is "solved" will under-budget the std column.

**Multi-week trap 3 — the synthetic-to-real scope trap.** The claim "overﬁtting to that object's features helps the tracker" [PAGE 5] is a *single-known-object* design decision, defended explicitly. Any reuse on unseen instances inverts the paper's stated intent. Compounding this: 20,000 training samples [PAGE 9] and a 12-hour single-GPU budget mean the claimed generalization across five dissimilar objects [PAGE 9, Table 1] is demonstrated in a low-data regime whose variance is not reported.

**Multi-week trap 4 — the 40 fps claim as a systems contract.** "Although more computationally intense than [8], it runs in 40 fps" [PAGE 14] is a best-case latency; the symmetry branch may require "a second (or more) iterative forward CNN pass(es), if the time constraints of our broader application allow so" [PAGE 7]. A deployment that assumes a hard 25.0 ms deadline inherits an unbounded tail.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

The architecture contains a literal recursion that the paper never names as such: the network's input `Î(t)` is the "Predicted" stream, i.e. a rendering conditioned on the tracker's own prior belief. Three internal agents compete over that loop.

**Agent 1 — Habit regression.** The prior-pose stream is a self-confirming hypothesis channel. The paper's explicit anti-generalization choice accelerates it: "we find beneﬁcial not to freeze those two layers during training. The reason is that we aim to track the pose of the single objects we train on and not to generalize to unseen ones. So, overﬁtting to that object's features helps the tracker to focus only on distinguishing the pose change" [PAGE 5]. Habit regression's signature failure is exactly the one documented on [PAGE 7]: a flip that "is not limited to a single frame, but is accumulated as we proceed to the following frames, due to the temporal nature of the tracker, until it is reset." The recursive Ô(t) is the transport mechanism for the error.

**Agent 2 — Counter-trap (the second-order doubt).** Two mechanisms exist solely to let the system disbelieve itself. The reflective-symmetry handler (Operator O6) is a first-order veto: a hard threshold at 100° that replaces the current Euler component with the previous one. The continuous-symmetry classifier is a second-order veto: it selects `Ĝ*` from a batch of 64 hypotheses and then forces those hypotheses apart via the `1/ξ` barrier, i.e. it *manufactures* doubt and then resolves it. Note the epistemic honesty of the authors' own remediation — "we may choose to perform a second (or more) iterative forward CNN pass(es)" [PAGE 7] — which concedes that the first pass may be wrong.

**Agent 3 — Clock.** The 25.0 ms budget at 40 fps [PAGE 14] is the arbiter between Agents 1 and 2. Iterative repair is only affordable when occlusion is high — which is precisely when the attention trade-off on [PAGE 2] fires: "as the occlusion increases, foreground attention, which focuses on the moving parts of the scene (i.e. the hand and the object), gets blurrier, while occlusion attention gets sharper and shifts focus from the object center to its body parts." So the system's own confidence signal (attention sharpness) is anti-correlated with the availability of the clock budget needed to act on it.

**Theory-of-mind of the occluder.** The system models the *human*, not just the object. The paper renders "a 3D hand model-occluder on the object frame with probability 60%" and, crucially, "preparing our network for cases of 100% occlusion, by completely covering the object by the occluder for 15% of the occluded subset" [PAGE 8]. The resulting behavior is diagnostic: for the Dragon, "it learns to ignore the one wing when it is in front and focuses on the other one, at the back, if its appearance is more distinctive of the pose. The same can be said for the legs of the 'Dog' model … This is a clue that this module has, indeed, learned the concept of occlusions and has not overﬁtted to the shape of the user's hand" [PAGE 10–11]. That is a claim about generalization of an *agent model* rather than an object model — and it is falsifiable: swap the hand model for a glove or a tool and the occlusion head's keypoint selectivity should either transfer or collapse.

**The regression risk.** The three agents can converge on a shared delusion. If habit regression leads, the counter-trap fires only when a chart-space jump exceeds 100°; a slow drift of 5°/frame accumulates 300° over 60 frames without ever tripping the veto. The clock cannot help because the drift is smooth. This is the paper's uncovered failure mode: it defends against *discrete* symmetry flips and not against *continuous* bias accumulation — a gap visible in the reported standard deviations, which remain 5–10× the means on the symmetric object ([PAGE 11, Table 5]: mean `2.07°`, std `24.52°`).

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Every cross-layer veto below is stated as a predicate on the tracker's output `Δp ∈ R⁹` (three translation parameters in `[−1, 1]`, six rotation parameters [PAGE 5]) and is evaluated against constants taken from the paper, not from outside it.

**Veto V1 — Rigid-body / gravitational admissibility.** The object is rigid by construction (Eq. 1). Predicate: admissible iff `|v_z(t)| ≤ v_max` and the signed distance to the dominant support surface satisfies `δ ≥ −ε_pen`. Physical derivation: `v_max = g·Δt = 9.81 m/s² × 0.025 s = 0.245 m/s = 245 mm/s` — the free-fall velocity gain per frame at the paper's 40 fps [PAGE 14]. Any z-translation between frames exceeding 245 mm/s while the object is supported is physically impossible and therefore a tracker artifact. *No such filter exists in the paper*, which is why the "Translation Interaction" failures on [PAGE 14, Table 6] remain non-zero (Dragon: 15 → 1; Cookie Jar: 16 → 5; Lego: 29 → 12). A gravity veto would delete a subset of those.

**Veto V2 — Occluder-kinematics admissibility (biomechanical layer).** The occluder is a "3D hand model" [PAGE 8]. Human hand-joint angular velocities are bounded; at 40 fps a finger cannot traverse 100° between frames. Therefore Operator O6's own threshold is *loose by roughly an order of magnitude* relative to hand biomechanics: `100° / 0.025 s = 4000 °/s`, whereas volitional hand-manipulation angular rates sit in the 10² °/s band. Consequence: the veto cannot distinguish "physically impossible tracker flip" from "fast but physiological hand motion." Adjudication: the threshold is conservative in the correct direction (it suppresses false flips) but will also suppress genuine fast reorientations — visible as the residual `24.52°` std on the Cookie Jar z-axis [PAGE 11].

**Veto V3 — Self-occlusion misattribution.** If the occlusion-attention mass on a region whose depth is consistent with the object's own surface exceeds a threshold `τ`, the region must be reclassified as self-occlusion, not occluder. The paper's own evidence that this happens: the Dragon's wing and the Dog's legs are learned as self-occluders [PAGE 10–11]. Veto predicate: `M_occ(Ω_self) > τ ⟹ suppress L_Unoccl on Ω_self`. Without it, the BCE occlusion head is trained against a mask that contradicts the geometry.

**Veto V4 — Sensor-physics / weather-illumination admissibility.** The depth modality is modeled with the Kinect noise product `n_A · n_Lx · n_Ly` with `σ(z, θ_y)` [PAGE 9]. Two hard vetoes follow. (a) *Range/grazing veto*: lateral standard deviation scales with depth and incidence angle; at high `z` and grazing `θ_y` the pose estimate must be down-weighted or rejected, since the training distribution provides no support there. (b) *Illumination veto*: structured-light or time-of-flight depth is corrupted by ambient near-infrared; direct sunlight or specular outdoor reflections invalidate the depth channel outright. This matters disproportionately here because "the tracker places more emphasis to the Depth cue, in general, in its eﬀort to estimate the object's pose" [PAGE 6]. A depth-invalid frame therefore does not degrade gracefully — it removes the dominant cue. All training backgrounds are from SUN3D [37], an indoor database; the paper offers no outdoor evidence.

**Veto V5 — Motion-blur / clock admissibility.** Blur length `b ≈ ω · T_exp · f` in pixels. At the paper's 150×150 input [PAGE 5], a blur exceeding ~10 px destroys the high-frequency cues the attention heads key on. Since exposure time is not reported, this veto must be calibrated empirically — but the paper already trains for it ("blurring (to simulate rapid object movement)" [PAGE 8]), which means the *training* distribution contains blur while the *deployment* admissibility criterion is unstated. Adjudication: blur is a soft veto (down-weight), not a hard one.

**Adjudication summary.** V1 and V4 are *hard* physical/sensor vetoes the paper does not implement. V2 and V3 are *soft* vetoes the paper implements implicitly (via the 100° heuristic and via the learned attention trade-off). V5 is a soft veto the paper trains for but does not adjudicate. None of these vetoes can improve the reported means; all of them can only trim the tails — which is exactly where the residual `24.52°` standard deviation lives.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three verifiable counterfactuals, each with a pre-registered prediction tied to numbers that already exist in the paper.

**RLVR-1 — Reflective-symmetry heuristic ablation (Operator O6).**
*Intervention:* disable the `100°` Euler freeze on [PAGE 7] and leave everything else identical; re-run the Cookie Jar and Lego through the "Hard Interaction" and "Full Interaction" scenarios of [PAGE 14, Table 6].
*Verifiable signal:* the "Fails" column and the rotational standard deviation.
*Prediction:* (a) failure counts *increase* for Cookie Jar Hard Interaction from 8 to ≥12 and for Lego Hard Interaction from 11 to ≥15; (b) mean rotational error increases by less than one standard deviation (i.e. < 13.20° for Cookie Jar), because the heuristic trades mean for variance; (c) the rotational std for Cookie Jar z-Euler rises strictly above `24.52°` [PAGE 11, Table 5]. Veto verdict: **retain** the heuristic if and only if (a) and (c) both hold. If only (c) holds, the heuristic is a variance-reduction device with no accuracy benefit and should be replaced by a geodesic-space (chart-free) test.

**RLVR-2 — Attention-head topology permutation (Operator O5 / [PAGE 10, Table 2]).**
*Intervention:* re-run the three configurations the authors already report — single occlusion head (`17.60 ± 10.74` mm / `37.10 ± 35.08`°), hierarchical clutter-then-occlusion (`14.99 ± 9.89` / `39.07 ± 33.22`), parallel (`14.35 ± 10.21` / `34.28 ± 29.81`) — but with the auxiliary BCE weights `s₂, s₃` *fixed* rather than learned, isolating the topology from the weighting scheme.
*Verifiable signal:* whether the parallel advantage survives when [20]'s uncertainty weighting is removed.
*Prediction:* translation advantage of parallel over hierarchical persists (14.35 vs 14.99 mm, a 0.64 mm gap) but *shrinks or reverses* on rotation (34.28 vs 39.07), because the rotation gain in the learned-weight run is partly attributable to the `L_Track` dominance that the learnable `s₁, s₂, s₃` establish (Table 4: `11.63/8.31` with learnable vs `13.97/14.76` with Welford [34]). Veto verdict: the "parallel is optimal" claim [PAGE 10] is **conditionally verifiable** — it holds under learnable weighting and is untested outside it.

**RLVR-3 — Depth-cue dependence test (Operator O8 / [PAGE 6]).**
*Intervention:* at inference only, apply the paper's own "probabilistic dropout of one of the modalities" augmentation [PAGE 8] deterministically to the depth channel; measure rotation and translation error separately on all five objects.
*Verifiable signal:* the ratio Δrotation/Δtranslation against the paper's stated cue ordering — "the tracker places more emphasis to the Depth cue, in general, in its eﬀort to estimate the object's pose" [PAGE 6].
*Prediction:* (a) depth dropout increases rotation error proportionally more than translation error, because translation is recoverable from the RGB silhouette scale while rotation is not; (b) the degradation is largest for the "Dog" and "Watering Can," the two least-textured objects per [PAGE 9, Table 1] ("Almost None" and "Poor"), since their appearance prior cannot substitute for the lost shape cue; (c) the degradation is smallest for the "Dragon" ("Rich" texture). Veto verdict: if (a) fails — i.e. translation degrades more — then the [PAGE 6] claim is falsified and the entire attention design rationale (which assumes shape-dominant inference) must be re-argued.

**Meta-veto (applies to all three).** Any of these interventions that leaves the reported `40 fps` [PAGE 14] contract intact is admissible; any that adds a second forward pass must be latency-budgeted against the `25.0 ms` frame period. That single inequality — `t_forward · n_passes ≤ 25.0 ms` — is the only constraint that can veto all three counterfactuals simultaneously, and it is the one the paper leaves as an exercise for "the time constraints of our broader application" [PAGE 7].

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
