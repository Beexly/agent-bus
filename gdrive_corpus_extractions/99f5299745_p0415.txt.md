Variables and units:
- t [s]: postnatal age.
- t_c [s]: critical maturation time at which the standing/walking impulse becomes expressible.
- σ_t [s]: inter-infant standard deviation of t_c, capturing “Individual infants vary enormously” [PAGE 405].
- G(t; t_c, σ_t) = 1 / (1 + exp(−(t − t_c)/σ_t)): smooth maturational gate, dimensionless ∈ [0,1]. Assumption: biological transition is sigmoidal, not a mathematical discontinuity. Breakdown: σ_t → 0 yields a Heaviside step, which is biologically invalid because observation noise and individual variation preclude true discontinuity.
- S(t): plantar contact sensation, either dimensionless afferent firing rate or contact force [N]. M(t): motor output, muscle activation a ∈ [0,1] or joint torque τ [N·m].
- Before t_c: I(S; M | t < t_c) ≈ 0 bits despite repeated S, consistent with [PAGE 405]. After t_c: contact elicits extensor stiffening [PAGE 405].
- Muscle activation dynamics (first-order Hill-type): τ_a ȧ(t) = u(t) − a(t), where τ_a [s] is activation time constant, u(t) neural drive. Assumption: quasi-static or slow length changes; isometric conditions. Breakdown: rapid fascicle shortening/lengthening (|ḋ| large), fatigue, or developmental changes in τ_a violate the fixed-τ_a assumption.
- Quasi-static standing torque requirement: τ_req ≈ m g d [N·m], where m [kg] is infant mass, g [m/s²] gravitational acceleration, d [m] horizontal moment arm from ankle to center of mass. Veto: if maximum available torque τ_max(t) < τ_req, the legs remain limp [PAGE 405] regardless of impulse.
- Inverted-pendulum quiet-standing model: natural timescale T_p = 2π√(l/g) [s], where l [m] is center-of-mass height. Closed-loop stability requires total sensorimotor delay τ_total = τ_neural + τ_MTU + τ_viscous [s] to satisfy τ_total < α T_p, with α ≈ 0.1–0.2 set by phase margin. This is a linearized stability bound, not a toy ratio. Breakdown regime: when τ_total approaches or exceeds α T_p, sway becomes oscillatory—consistent with James’s “grogginess” [PAGE 405]—and multi-segment compliance, stepping, and soft tissue invalidate the single-link bound.
- CPG interlimb alternation: left/right leg phase difference φ; anti-phase φ = π requires sufficient interlimb coupling k and supraspinal drive. Alternation while suspended [PAGE 405] implies a CPG exists without load; weight-bearing introduces load feedback that entrains or disrupts depending on gain.
All equations are interpretive models laid over [PAGE 405]; James supplies no numerical constants.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage backward: James explicitly opposes the “older English associationist school” [PAGE 405], traceable to Hartley’s associationism, James Mill, John Stuart Mill, Alexander Bain, and Spencerian accounts in which complex conduct is “altogether” the product of gradual association among perceptions, movements, and pleasures [PAGE 405]. James substitutes an endogenous maturational trigger: “natural ripening of the nerve-centres” [PAGE 405].

Lineage forward: Gesell’s maturational theory (1920s–1940s) echoes James’s nerve-centres ripening; McGraw’s neuromuscular maturation work (1930s–1940s) similarly emphasized readiness before training. Thelen & Smith’s dynamic-systems account (1994) replaces “instinct” with task-specific attractors emerging from brain-body-environment coupling, which can absorb James’s “sight of the floor” as an affordance [PAGE 405]. Grillner’s CPG research identifies spinal oscillators for locomotion; Clark’s reflex-as-building-blocks literature treats primitive responses as substrates later under cortical control.

Retrocausal reversal: later researchers project critical-period language backward onto James’s “suddenness” [PAGE 405], producing multi-week developmental traps. Trap 1: the claim that walking education completes “within a week’s compass” [PAGE 405] hardens into an implicit deadline; clinicians or parents who expect walking by a particular week pathologize normal variance, despite James’s warning that “Individual infants vary enormously” [PAGE 405]. Epidemiologically, independent walking onset spans roughly 9–18 months, not a universal week; James’s one-week window describes the final refinement phase after prehistory (suspended alternation, creeping) and ignores posthistory (months of gait refinement). Trap 2: because James notes some babies stand first while others creep first [PAGE 405], creeping is not obligatory; nevertheless later normative charts often mandate crawling before walking, misclassifying bottom-shufflers or early standers as delayed. Trap 3: Holmes’s “reminiscence of prehuman ancestral habits” [PAGE 405] fed Haeckelian recapitulationism and then bifurcated into two errors—literalizing primitive reflexes as ancestral memories, or dismissing them as irrelevant vestigial noise—missing their real role as subcortical primitives that are suppressed or repurposed during corticospinal maturation. The multi-week trap operates by taking a compressed phenomenological description (“within a week” [PAGE 405]) and retroactively imposing it as a population-level scheduling constraint.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The caregiver and infant maintain recursive models of each other. Before t_c, repeated foot placement yields no response [PAGE 405], so the caregiver infers “no impulse.” After t_c, identical plantar contact produces stiffening and downward press [PAGE 405], and the caregiver infers that the impulse has “been born.” Because observation is sparse, the transition is attributed to an internal awakening rather than to a threshold crossing in a coupled caregiver-infant system.

Crisis habit regression: when the new upright pattern is still shallow in state space—during fatigue, novelty, or cognitive load—the infant regresses to an older, more stable attractor (sitting, creeping, or limp legs). James’s “grogginess” [PAGE 405] and the creeping infant who “falls on his nose” [PAGE 405] are instances of attractor switching under instability, not evidence that the instinct is absent. Regression is predictable when the new pattern’s basin of attraction is narrower than the perturbation magnitude.

Counter-trap: assuming that because the impulse is endogenous (“natural ripening” [PAGE 405]), environmental opportunity is irrelevant. James himself notes that creeping is “aroused by the sight of the floor” [PAGE 405]; the affordance gates expression. The caregiver’s belief that the infant should now creep or walk changes handling, floor time, and encouragement, thereby altering the statistics of afferent input. The endogenous clock and the social clock are recursively coupled; attributing the transition solely to “nerve-centres” [PAGE 405] underestimates the caregiver’s role in changing the input regime once the behavioral threshold is crossed.

Clock dualism: an endogenous maturational clock (t_c with σ_t) runs against an exogenous social-clock (caregiver scheduling, sitting practice, floor exposure). A recursive theory-of-mind failure occurs when the observer models the infant’s mind as passively waiting for an internal signal while ignoring that the observer’s own changed behavior—placing the child on the floor more often once sitting becomes unstable—modifies the very afferent events that James describes as triggering the impulse [PAGE 405].
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto (musculoskeletal strength): absolute and upstream. Before sufficient muscle cross-sectional area, tendon stiffness, and bone ossification, τ_max < m g d [N·m], so gravitational torque about the ankle cannot be countered; the legs remain limp [PAGE 405]. No amount of “impulse” or floor contact overrides this layer.

Biomechanical veto (stability and delay): conditional on passing the physical layer. Quiet standing is an inverted pendulum of length l [m]; stable control requires τ_total < α T_p with T_p = 2π√(l/g) [s]. If neural conduction, electromechanical delay, and viscous damping sum to τ_total [s] exceeding α T_p, corrective torques arrive too late and sway diverges—phenomenologically matching “grogginess” [PAGE 405]. Assumptions: rigid segments, linearized single-link pendulum, point-mass center of mass. Breakdown: multi-segment dynamics, stepping (not static balance), and soft-tissue compliance invalidate the simple bound.

Weather/surface veto (environmental modulation): surface coefficient of static friction μ_s [dimensionless], floor temperature T_floor [°C], and surface compliance modulate afferent quality and dynamics. Required friction f_req = |F_horizontal| / F_vertical [dimensionless]; if μ_s < f_req, slipping vetoes locomotion. Cold T_floor reduces nerve conduction velocity v [m/s] (Q10 temperature dependence) and slows muscle contraction, increasing τ_neural and τ_MTU and potentially pushing τ_total beyond α T_p. These variables are absent from James [PAGE 405] but are necessary to prove that “feels the floor” is not sufficient under all conditions.

Adjudication order: (1) Physical strength veto—binary, upstream; (2) biomechanical delay/stability veto—conditional on strength; (3) weather/surface veto—modulatory, can veto expression even when the first two pass. James’s “standing impulse” [PAGE 405] is permissive, not sufficient; it cannot override any layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions:

Veto Scenario 1 — Afferent reafference nullification. Counterfactual: during the pre-impulse period [PAGE 405], the infant’s feet contact a force-sensing treadmill belt moving backward at belt speed equal to foot speed, so plantar contact occurs with near-zero ground reaction force (GRF ≈ 0 N), eliminating the reafferent match between motor command and resistive feedback. Prediction: despite intact cutaneous receptors, no leg stiffening or downward press occurs [PAGE 405]; soleus/gastrocnemius EMG remains below 5% of maximal voluntary contraction. Verifiable via synchronized instrumented treadmill and surface EMG; onset of pressing is recorded only when GRF becomes nonzero. Veto confirmed if contact alone fails to evoke response before t_c; breakdown if optic flow or vestibular stimulation from belt motion independently triggers extension.

Veto Scenario 2 — Neuromuscular delay violation. Counterfactual: after standing is established [PAGE 405], the lower limbs are locally cooled to reduce nerve conduction velocity and muscle contraction speed, increasing τ_total [ms] beyond the inverted-pendulum stability bound α T_p. Prediction: quiet standing collapses within seconds; center-of-mass sway exceeds 2 cm peak-to-peak; gait reverts to a wide-based, “groggy” pattern [PAGE 405]; effects reverse within ~10 minutes of rewarming to baseline EMG latency and sway. Verifiable via motion capture and EMG latency; breakdown if systemic hypothermia (not local limb cooling) confounds arousal and attention.

Veto Scenario 3 — Associationist contingency saturation. Counterfactual: for four weeks during the pre-impulse period, an experimenter delivers contingent auditory/visual “pleasure” [PAGE 405] for random haphazard leg movements in suspension that are uncorrelated with floor contact or downward pressure. Prediction consistent with James [PAGE 405]: no earlier emergence of standing; t_c unchanged relative to matched controls; the gradual association of perceptions, movements, and pleasures does not produce the locomotor instinct before maturation. Verifiable via longitudinal onset age; the associationist null is rejected if there is no statistically detectable shift in t_c (e.g., p > 0.05, Cohen’s d < 0.2). Breakdown: reward may raise general arousal or produce non-specific practice effects unrelated to standing.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
