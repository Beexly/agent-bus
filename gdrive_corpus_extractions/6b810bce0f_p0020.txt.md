\[
\hat{\mathbf x}
=
\arg\min_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal B}
\sum_{i} w_i\,r_i^2(\mathbf x),

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical and scientific lineage plus multi-week deployment traps.

The technical genealogy runs from differential carrier-phase GPS through real-time kinematic and on-the-fly ambiguity resolution to embedded sports telemetry. Double differencing removes receiver and satellite clock offsets, a foundational GNSS operator; the search-box integer-least-squares approach descends from ambiguity-resolution methods such as least-squares ambiguity decorrelation adjustment, which the source lists alongside curve fitting, redundant satellites, and raw Doppler cycle-slip detection [PAGE 1]. The wavelengths and widelane values cited—19 cm L1, 24 cm L2, approximately 86 cm widelane as rendered [PAGE 1]—place the architecture in the L1/L2 GPS-era RTK lineage, where fixing the integer vector converts carrier phase from decimetre-level noise to millimetre-level relative positioning under ideal conditions. The embedded side draws on early-2000s mixed-signal design: Hitachi H8/300H-family microcontroller (HD64F3672FP, 2 kbyte RAM, 10-bit ADC), ADXL202 MEMS accelerometer, Melexis MLX90215 Hall sensor, PWM timer capture, and a Compaq iPAQ host with IrDA and data logging [PAGE 1]. Rowing-specific outputs—stroke rate, drive-to-recovery ratio, intra-stroke velocity, distance per stroke, and velocity per stroke—are derived from accelerometer, GPS, and clock data [PAGE 1], placing the invention in the lineage of instrumented rowing that moved from impellers/paddlewheels toward GNSS-aided biomechanics.

Multi-week traps arise from the gap between the patent’s preferred competition architecture and its realizable embedded implementation. Because temperature-drift compensation for the accelerometer is explicitly deferred “to a later date” [PAGE 1], drift accumulates across sessions and seasons unless externally calibrated; there is no field update mechanism described in the excerpt. Cycle slips are not cumulatively self-healing: loss of lock, low SNR, and multipath require re-detection and possibly re-initialization, and over weeks of changing river geometry, tide, weather, and satellite constellations the probability of an uncorrected integer bias increases. The impeller is exposed to biofouling, debris, and cavitation; the alternative microfluidic flow sensor measures temperature change past a heater and is sensitive to boundary-layer thickness, hull orientation, weeds, and flow asymmetry. GPS multipath varies with bank structures, bridges, boat motion, and atmospheric conditions, exactly the “unfavourable observation environments” the source warns about [PAGE 1]. The five-second heart-rate rolling average smooths acute events, so a week-long trend analysis may miss transient tachycardia or arrhythmia while displaying a stable mean. Mechanical factors—splash-proof cover but glove-accessible buttons, 25 mm×30 mm×9 mm accelerometer envelope, 9 V battery regulated to 5 V [PAGE 1]—create operational failure modes in cold, wet, or high-usage conditions. The retrocausal point is that later claims of real-time race-profile monitoring inherit the earliest unresolved assumptions: integer ambiguity correctness, stochastic error bounds, deferred thermal compensation, and a 1 Hz heart-rate telemetry backbone.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto proof.

Define a monotonic veto predicate
\[
V_t = V^{\mathrm{GNSS}}_t \lor V^{\mathrm{sensor}}_t \lor V^{\mathrm{biomech}}_t \lor V^{\mathrm{weather}}_t,
\]
such that if \(V_t=1\), the system shall suppress or flag position, boat speed, stroke metrics, and heart-rate outputs; no averaging, search-box smoothing, or rolling-window filter may override a veto. Re-initialization is required after a cleared veto, particularly after loss of lock or an unresolved cycle slip [PAGE 1].

Physical-layer vetoes: undetected or miscorrected cycle slip; loss of lock; low signal-to-noise ratio; severe multipath; reference-to-rover baseline beyond the regime for which differential atmospheric cancellation is valid; unsatisfactory satellite geometry or \(\operatorname{rank}(G)<3\); search-box computation exceeding the real-time latency budget; and integer ambiguity not fixed within the required time [PAGE 1]. The source explicitly limits all cycle-slip techniques by their shared stochastic assumptions and notes that misdetection or miscorrection can occur [PAGE 1].

Biomechanical-layer vetoes: accelerometer mounting change or axis misalignment on the oarsman or boat; uncompensated temperature drift because the detailed thermal algorithm is deferred [PAGE 1]; splash-proof but not necessarily immersion-proof housing; button accessibility failure when rowers wear gloves [PAGE 1]; impeller fouling, debris ingestion, or cavitation; microfluidic flow-sensor boundary-layer error, heater drift, or hull-location bias; and heart-rate beat-detection failure at high rates. Because maximum heart rate is capped at 250 bpm in the source, the minimum RR interval is 240 ms, while the telemetry backbone updates heart rate once per second and smooths over five seconds [PAGE 1]; acute physiological events are therefore vetoed from beat-to-beat claims.

Weather-layer vetoes: rain or splash exceeding the housing seal; temperature excursions outside the calibrated range, interacting with the deferred accelerometer temperature compensation; wind and waves that change multipath, antenna occlusion, boat attitude, and differential atmospheric conditions [PAGE 1]; and satellite visibility loss from terrain, bridges, or foliage.

Adjudication rule: the veto predicate is evaluated before any output is presented to the coach or rower. A GNSS veto invalidates GPS-derived intra-stroke velocity, distance per stroke, and velocity per stroke even if the accelerometer remains healthy [PAGE 1], because the source states those metrics are derived from accelerometer, GPS, and clock data jointly. A biomechanical veto invalidates impeller-derived speed and accelerometer-derived stroke rate unless an independent cross-check (for example, GPS position differencing over a valid window) bounds the error. A weather veto that disables GPS does not automatically validate the impeller; the source prefers GPS and accelerometers for competition [PAGE 1], so the fallback must be explicitly bounded or suppressed.
