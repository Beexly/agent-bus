Domain: vision-based force estimation from monocular RGB via MediaPipe pose + SSD MobileNet object detection + BiLSTM temporal regression. Audit stance: treat every quantitative claim as a hypothesis with an identifiable failure mode, not a fact.

---

<THINK_TRACE>
**Multi-hop derivation of the paper's central inferential chain.**

*Hop 1 — the observability substitution.* Force is not a pixel-observable quantity. The paper's implicit claim is that a kinematic sufficient statistic plus an object-class prior can substitute for a transducer. The chain is: RGB frame → 33 landmarks with $(x,y,z,c)$ [PAGE 2] → derived joint angles, velocities, accelerations → integration with bounding boxes and object class [PAGE 3] → 16-frame sequence → TCN + BiLSTM → two heads, one scalar magnitude in newtons and one 3-vector direction [PAGE 3, Fig. 2]. Each arrow is a lossy projection. The $z$ coordinate is explicitly "relative depth" [PAGE 2], i.e., scale-ambiguous under monocular projection, so any metric distance computed between a hand landmark and an object centroid is identifiable only up to an unknown affine scale unless calibrated by torso normalization ("Normalization relative to torso dimensions to account for different body sizes" [PAGE 3]). That normalization fixes *body* scale, not *scene* scale.

*Hop 2 — the physics baseline is a diagnostic, not a straw man.* The paper's own physics baseline ("Using mass estimation and acceleration to calculate force (F = ma)" [PAGE 4]) scores MAE 15.6 N — worse than pose-only at 9.3 N. Read carefully, this is informative: the physical relation is exact, so a 15.6 N error means the *inputs* (mass, acceleration) are the error source. The learned model therefore succeeds partly by learning an object-class mass prior from SSD MobileNet classifications rather than by learning dynamics. That is a legitimate engineering result, but it relocates the claim: the model is a learned mass-and-kinematics prior, not a dynamics solver. The paper never states this.

*Hop 3 — temporal identifiability ceiling.* The window is "16 frames (approximately 0.5 seconds at 30 fps)" [PAGE 3]. Exact value: $16/30 = 0.533$ s. The Nyquist frequency of a 30 Hz capture is 15 Hz; impact transients in hammering and striking — one of the four trained categories — contain spectral content far above that. The paper concedes "Force plates often operate at 1000+ Hz vs. video at 30-60 Hz" [PAGE 6]. Therefore peak force is structurally unobservable, not merely under-trained. Any downstream use requiring peak or impulse (injury thresholds, tool-impact characterization) is outside the identifiable set.

*Hop 4 — arithmetic audit of the headline number.* Table I gives ForcePose 5.83 N versus Pham et al. 8.1 N. Direct computation: $(8.1-5.83)/8.1 = 0.28025$, i.e., **28.0%**, not the "27.5%" asserted on [PAGE 1] and [PAGE 4]. Direction: $(10.2-7.4)/10.2 = 0.2745$, i.e., 27.4%, which matches. The conclusion on [PAGE 6] states "outperforming existing computer vision methods by 27.5" — truncated, no unit, no comparator named. This is a reproducible discrepancy of ~0.5 percentage points in the primary claim and must be resolved before the number is cited downstream.

*Hop 5 — comparator fairness.* Pham et al. [9] is described on [PAGE 2] as requiring "depth information" (RGB-D), while ForcePose is RGB-only. Beating an RGB-D method with RGB is a stronger claim, not a weaker one — but the two methods were not evaluated on a shared protocol here, and no indication is given that Pham et al. was retrained on the 850-video corpus. Cross-paper table comparisons without a common test set are not verifiable.

*Hop 6 — split validity.* "680 videos for training, 85 for validation, and 85 for testing, ensuring that each split contained a balanced representation of interaction types" [PAGE 4]. Balanced by *interaction type* is stated; **subject-disjoint** is not. Frames within a video are strongly correlated, so frame-level metrics with $n$ inflated by frames-per-video produce confidence intervals roughly $\sqrt{k}$ too narrow for $k$ frames per clip. No CI, no clustered bootstrap, no LOSO protocol is reported — while [PAGE 4] simultaneously admits "the model showed poor generalization to new subjects and objects."

*Hop 7 — label provenance.* Ground truth is "Force transducers embedded in interaction objects" [PAGE 3]. For a hammer or screwdriver, a transducer in the handle measures the force transmitted at the sensor plane, which differs from the hand–tool contact resultant by lever-arm and moment effects. Label validity is therefore conditional on sensor placement at the contact interface, which is not specified.

*Gray-zone register (ranked by downstream blast radius):* (1) the 27.5% vs 28.0% inconsistency; (2) absent subject-disjoint evaluation; (3) direction head as "FC 3" with no stated unit-norm constraint, making angular error ill-posed unless the vector is normalized post hoc; (4) "real-time" claimed at 18 fps against 30 fps capture [PAGE 4] — throughput is sub-capture-rate, requiring the frame skipping that the paper itself lists; (5) rehabilitation survey figures "83%... 91%... 75%" [PAGE 5] measure perceived usefulness, not accuracy — they are satisfaction data presented adjacent to validity claims; (6) no reported force range, so extrapolation behavior is unknown; (7) the ablation deltas sum to 7.47 N, far exceeding any single effect, confirming strong non-additivity that the paper does not model.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**E1 — Pose operator** [PAGE 2]: $\mathcal{P}_t=\{(\hat x_i,\hat y_i,\hat z_i,\hat c_i)\}_{i=1}^{33}$, with $\hat x_i,\hat y_i\in[0,1]$ (dimensionless image-normalized), $\hat z_i$ relative depth (dimensionless, scale-ambiguous), $\hat c_i\in[0,1]$ (probability). Gating: retain only $\hat c_i\ge 0.5$ — verbatim: "Filtering low-confidence detections (threshold = 0.5)" [PAGE 3].

**E2 — Detection operator** [PAGE 3]: $\mathcal{D}_t=\{(b_j,\kappa_j,s_j)\}_{j=1}^{M_t}$, $b_j\in\mathbb{R}^4$ in pixels, $\kappa_j$ class label, $s_j\in[0,1]$ score. Gating: $s_j\ge 0.65$, NMS at $\mathrm{IoU}=0.45$ — verbatim: "Implemented a confidence threshold of 0.65" and "Applied non-maximum suppression with IoU = 0.45" [PAGE 3].

**E3 — Contact feature** [PAGE 3]: $d_{j}=\lVert \pi(\hat p_{\text{hand}})-\pi(b_j)\rVert_2$, in normalized image units; converts to metres only under a calibration factor $\lambda$ (m·unit⁻¹), so $d^{\text{m}}= \lambda d$. Assumption: $\lambda$ constant within a clip. Breakdown: $\lambda$ is unidentifiable from monocular RGB when subject–camera distance varies.

**E4 — Temporal window** [PAGE 3]: $\tau = N_f/f_s = 16/30\ \text{s}=0.533\ \text{s}$. Nyquist $f_N=15$ Hz.

**E5 — Composite loss**, verbatim [PAGE 3, Eq. 1]:
$$\mathcal{L}=\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\text{mag}}+\beta\mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}+\delta\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}},\quad (\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=(1.0,\,0.8,\,0.5,\,0.1).$$
Dimensional audit: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mag}}$ is MSE in $\mathrm{N}^2$; $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}}$ mixes MSE on unit-vector components (dimensionless) with cross-entropy (nats); $\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}$ is a plausibility penalty; $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$ is a norm penalty. The sum is dimensionally inhomogeneous — the weights $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$ absorb units and are not physically interpretable. This is standard practice in multi-task learning but must not be read as an energy functional.

**E6–E10 — Evaluation operators**, verbatim metric list [PAGE 4]:
$$\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lvert \hat F_i-F_i\rvert \quad [\mathrm{N}],\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\hat F_i-F_i)^2}\quad[\mathrm{N}],$$
$$\varepsilon_{\text{rel},i}=\frac{\lvert\hat F_i-F_i\rvert}{\lvert F_i\rvert}\ \ (\text{dimensionless}),\qquad
\theta_i=\arccos\!\left(\frac{\langle \hat{\mathbf v}_i,\mathbf v_i\rangle}{\lVert\hat{\mathbf v}_i\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf v_i\rVert}\right)\ [\mathrm{deg}],$$
$$r=\frac{\sum_i(\hat F_i-\bar{\hat F})(F_i-\bar F)}{\sqrt{\sum_i(\hat F_i-\bar{\hat F})^2\sum_i(F_i-\bar F)^2}}\ \ (\text{dimensionless}).$$
**Exact singularities:** $\varepsilon_{\text{rel}}\to\infty$ as $F_i\to 0$ — the paper lists relative error as a metric but reports only MAE/RMSE/$\theta$/$r$, implicitly avoiding this pole. $\theta_i$ is undefined when $\lVert\mathbf v_i\rVert\to 0$ (zero-force contacts have no direction); the "FC 3" direction head [PAGE 3, Fig. 2] must be unit-normalized or $\theta$ is not computable — the paper does not state the normalization.

**E11 — Physics baseline** [PAGE 4]: $\mathbf F = m\mathbf a$, $\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{kg\cdot m\cdot s^{-2}}$; reported MAE 15.6 N.

**E12 — Reported improvement:** $\Delta\%=100\,(\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{base}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{model}})/\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{base}}$. Substituting Table I [PAGE 4]: magnitude $=100(8.1-5.83)/8.1=\mathbf{28.0\%}$; direction $=100(10.2-7.4)/10.2=27.4\%$. Claimed: "27.5% and 27.4% respectively" [PAGE 4]. The direction figure verifies; the magnitude figure does not.

**E13 — Impulse-consistency veto** (proposed falsifier): with known object mass $m$ and tracked velocity change $\Delta\mathbf v$ over $\tau$,
$$\Big\lVert \int_{t}^{t+\tau}\hat{\mathbf F}(t')\,\mathrm{d}t' - m\,\Delta\mathbf v\Big\rVert \le \epsilon_I\ [\mathrm{N\cdot s}].$$
This is testable only for instrumented or known-mass objects; it is the only physics-layer check that does not require the model's own output to be trusted.

**E14 — Sampling veto:** valid reconstruction requires $f_s>2f_{\max}$. With $f_s=30$ Hz, $f_{\max}=15$ Hz; consequence: impulse peaks in hammering/striking are not reconstructible, since force plates run "1000+ Hz" [PAGE 6].

**Documented breakdown regimes:** occlusion — verbatim "Performance degrades when key body parts or objects are occluded" [PAGE 5]; novel objects — "Accuracy is lower for object categories not well-represented in the training data" [PAGE 5]; multi-contact — "The current model primarily handles single-point force application" [PAGE 5], where superposition $\mathbf F_{\text{tot}}=\sum_k \mathbf F_k$ has $\lVert\mathbf F_{\text{tot}}\rVert \ne \sum_k\lVert\mathbf F_k\rVert$, so a single resultant is non-identifiable; illumination — "Very bright or dark environments can affect pose estimation and object detection" [PAGE 5].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The paper is a 2025 recombination of components whose lineage terminates almost entirely between 2014 and 2019. Direct-measurement ancestry: force plates and dynamometry [12], hand-held dynamometry versus isokinetic "gold standard" [15], force-sensing technologies for human–object interaction [16], and the biomechanics methods canon [1]. Markerless-pose ancestry runs from pictorial structures [3] through OpenPose's part-affinity real-time multi-person estimation [2], DeepCut's joint subset partitioning [10], DensePose's dense correspondence [5], to MediaPipe/BlazePose's 33-landmark efficiency claim [8]. Detection ancestry runs R-CNN [4] → YOLO [11] and SSD [7] → MobileNet backbones [6]. The vision-to-force sublineage is the shortest and most honest about its constraints: Pham et al. [9] required RGB-D; Rogez et al. [13] recovered contact points, not forces; Zhu et al. [17] fused vision with tactile sensing; Schneider et al. [14] named the gap between action recognition and physical understanding. ForcePose's genuine novelty is therefore compositional (integration + a new 850-video corpus) and not operator-level — a distinction the abstract's "novel deep learning framework" [PAGE 1] glosses.

Retro-causal reading: the 2019–2025 gap is explained by the *labeling bottleneck*, not by architecture. Instrumented-object corpora are expensive; the paper's contribution is fundamentally a dataset contribution dressed as an architecture contribution.

**Multi-week traps** (failure modes that incubate over deployment weeks rather than appearing at validation):

1. **Distribution drift by object turnover.** SSD MobileNet V2 is COCO-pretrained and "fine-tuned on our custom dataset" [PAGE 3]; new tool geometries entering a workplace after week 3 are out-of-distribution, and the paper already concedes novel-object degradation [PAGE 5].
2. **Transducer/dataset staleness.** Ground truth came from a bespoke rig with a "Synchronization system to align video frames with force readings" [PAGE 3]; residual sync latency biases acceleration–force pairing by $a\cdot\Delta t$ N, systematically and invisibly.
3. **Camera recalibration creep.** Torso-normalized features [PAGE 3] absorb body-size variation but not lens/framing changes; a mounting shift changes effective $\lambda$ in E3 and silently rescales all distance features.
4. **Operator calibration drift (human).** Once staff trust the number, spot-checks stop; the 83%/91%/75% usefulness figures [PAGE 5] are exactly the kind of early satisfaction signal that suppresses skepticism.
5. **Lighting seasonality.** Indoor deployments (rehabilitation center, three manufacturing sites, tennis academy [PAGE 5]) do not exercise the "extreme lighting" regime; a summer skylight change can move a site into the documented failure band without any code change.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**Crisis habit regression.** Under acute stakes — a therapist observing a patient's post-operative lift, an ergonomist watching a high-load manual task — attention collapses to the most legible number on screen. The system reports an MAE of 5.83 N [PAGE 1], a *mean* absolute error; humans reliably re-encode a mean error as a symmetric bound, which it is not. The regression is not stupidity; it is the standard response to a point estimate rendered without an interval. The recursive loop: designers model users as needing "force feedback" and surface a scalar; users model the scalar as a measurement; the model's own confidence information (landmark $\hat c_i$, detection $s_j$ [PAGES 2–3]) never reaches the human, so the human cannot form an accurate model of the model. The paper's rehabilitation evaluation reinforces this — "83% found the force feedback useful for guiding patients" [PAGE 5] is a usability measure, and usability is not calibration.

**Counter-trap.** The symmetric failure is a single salient error — an occluded hand, a novel tool — producing total abandonment or overcorrection back to unaided judgment. Both directions are habit regressions around the same missing signal. The designed counter-trap is forced disconfirmation: scheduled instrumented spot-checks, mandatory display of predictive intervals rather than MAE, and an explicit abstention channel when E1/E2 gates fail (a channel the paper does not describe — the pipeline gates *features* at $\hat c_i\ge0.5$ and $s_j\ge0.65$ but has no documented policy for the interaction where both gates fail simultaneously).

**Clock.** Three clocks run concurrently. (i) Capture clock: 30 fps, $33.3$ ms per frame. (ii) Inference clock: "18 fps on a laptop with an NVIDIA GTX 1660 Ti GPU, and 7 fps on a Jetson Nano" [PAGE 4] — 55.6 ms and 142.9 ms per frame respectively, i.e., *slower than capture* at both tiers; a 16-frame decision therefore costs $16/18=0.89$ s or $16/7=2.29$ s of wall-clock latency. (iii) Human clock: the reaction window in which a coach or therapist can still intervene. The paper's own optimization — "Frame skipping for non-critical frames" [PAGE 4] — collides with its own feature-importance finding that "certain 'key frames'... typically moments of initial contact or maximum acceleration" carried "disproportionate importance" [PAGE 6]. The frames most likely to be skipped under clock pressure are precisely the frames carrying the signal. That is a recursive conflict between the latency budget and the information budget, and it is invisible in the reported MAE because the reported MAE was computed without frame skipping.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Physical layer.** The strongest available falsifier is impulse–momentum consistency (E13). It does not validate the model, but it can veto it: if $\lVert\int\hat{\mathbf F}\,\mathrm{d}t - m\Delta\mathbf v\rVert$ exceeds $\epsilon_I$, the prediction is physically inadmissible regardless of its MAE. Because the 850-video corpus contains instrumented objects [PAGE 3], this test is executable retroactively on the existing test split — and its absence from the paper is a notable omission.

**Biomechanical layer.** The model outputs an external contact resultant in newtons. It does **not** output joint reaction forces, moments, or internal tissue loading; those require inverse dynamics with segment inertial parameters [12], which the pipeline never estimates. Veto: any claim of injury-risk quantification, tissue-load estimation, or clinical force prescription derived from this output. The single-point assumption ("primarily handles single-point force application" [PAGE 5]) additionally breaks for two-handed tool use, asymmetric loading, and collaborative lifting — the paper itself flags "Disambiguating individual contributions to total force" and "Modeling force transfer between participants" as unsolved [PAGE 6].

**Weather / environmental layer.** All reported deployments were indoor or semi-controlled: a rehabilitation center, "three manufacturing environments," and a tennis academy [PAGE 5]. The only stated environmental failure mode is illumination: "Very bright or dark environments can affect pose estimation and object detection" [PAGE 5]. Rain, fog, dust, and specular glare are untested and are not covered by any reported ablation. Posture-occluding loose clothing and PPE (common in the exact manufacturing settings the paper targets) are likewise unablated.

**Composite veto rule (certification gate).** An output may be treated as a measurement only if all hold simultaneously:
$$\big[\hat c_i\ge0.5\ \forall i\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{crit}}\big]\wedge\big[s_j\ge0.65\big]\wedge\big[\text{single contact}\big]\wedge\big[\hat F\in[F_{\min},F_{\max}]_{\text{train}}\big]\wedge\big[\text{illumination}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\big].$$
Failure of any conjunct forces abstention rather than a degraded point estimate. The paper defines four of the five conditions in prose but publishes no force range $[F_{\min},F_{\max}]_{\text{train}}$, which makes the fourth conjunct unenforceable by any downstream user.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario below is executable on the published 85-video test split with a machine-checkable reward and a pre-registered veto threshold. Predictions are falsifiable in the strict sense: a miss invalidates the accompanying mechanism claim.

**RLVR-1 — Occlusion stress counterfactual.**
Intervention: synthetically occlude the dominant wrist/hand bounding region (and, separately, the manipulated object) for 30% of test frames.
Verifiable metric: $\rho_{\text{occ}}=\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{occ}}/\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{clean}}$.
Veto threshold: $\rho_{\text{occ}}\ge 1.4$ ⇒ **deny field certification**; the model is not deployment-safe in cluttered manufacturing environments.
Prediction: $\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{occ}}\ge 8.2$ N (from 5.83 N) and $\theta_{\text{occ}}\ge 10.4°$ (from 7.4°).
Grounding: verbatim "Occlusion: Performance degrades when key body parts or objects are occluded" [PAGE 5]; the $\hat c_i\ge0.5$ gate [PAGE 3] converts occlusion into feature dropout rather than into a flagged abstention.

**RLVR-2 — Sampling-rate counterfactual.**
Intervention: decimate test video 30 fps → 10 fps while holding the 16-frame window fixed, stretching $\tau$ from 0.533 s to 1.6 s.
Verifiable metric: per-category MAE, specifically on the sports category.
Veto threshold: sports $\mathrm{MAE}\ge 10$ N or $\theta\ge12°$ ⇒ **declare the temporal reasoning non-robust to capture-rate variation**, which invalidates any claim of platform portability (phone cameras, embedded sensors).
Prediction: sports MAE rises from 7.1 N [PAGE 5] to $\ge10$ N; lifting, being quasi-static, rises least (4.2 N → $\le$5.5 N).
Grounding: 15 Hz Nyquist at full rate; "Force plates often operate at 1000+ Hz vs. video at 30-60 Hz" [PAGE 6]; and the claim that "Temporal patterns over 0.3-0.5 seconds are more informative than single frames" [PAGE 6] — a claim that presupposes the 30 fps window specifically.

**RLVR-3 — Leave-one-subject-out generalization counterfactual.**
Intervention: re-split the 850 videos so that no subject appears in both train and test (LOSO), holding interaction-type balance constant.
Verifiable metric: $\Delta_{\text{LOSO}}=\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{LOSO}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{in-dist}}$.
Veto threshold: $\Delta_{\text{LOSO}}\ge1.5$ N ⇒ **the reported 5.83 N is an in-sample-subject figure and must not be quoted as expected field accuracy.**
Prediction: $\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{LOSO}}\ge7.3$ N and $r$ falls from 0.89 to $\le0.82$; the degradation concentrates in tool and sports categories (6.8 N, 7.1 N → $\ge$8.5 N, $\ge$9.0 N) because those are most body-geometry-sensitive.
Grounding: verbatim "Initially, the model showed poor generalization to new subjects and objects" and the mitigations listed — "domain adaptation techniques to improve cross-subject performance" [PAGE 4] — plus the fact that the published split guarantees only "a balanced representation of interaction types" [PAGE 4], with no stated subject-disjointness.

**Aggregate decision rule.** Any single veto firing blocks citation of the 27.5% headline; two or more firing reclassifies the contribution from "validated force measurement" to "relative-motion force proxy requiring instrumented spot-checks," which is still useful — and is, on the paper's own evidence (MAE 5.83 N versus a 15.6 N physics baseline, 18 fps on commodity hardware, non-invasive capture), the more defensible claim.
