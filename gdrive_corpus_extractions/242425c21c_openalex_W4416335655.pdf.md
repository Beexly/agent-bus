Deep multi-hop derivation and gray-zone audit of SW2S.

The paper's core failure mode is hard horizontal partition discontinuity in PCB-style Re-ID [PAGE 2]. When a feature map F ∈ ℝ^{c×h×w} is sliced into P non-overlapping horizontal bands, semantic structures crossing band boundaries—most critically a basketball jersey number straddling two strips—are bifurcated and lose spatial continuity [PAGE 3]. SW2S replaces the hard cut with a sliding-window partition parameterized by band height h′ and stride s, requiring s < h′ so that every band retains a margin of its neighbor [PAGE 4]. This is a local-topology preservation constraint: the overlap guarantees that the union of adjacent bands covers the full jersey-number support, preventing the aliasing of identity-discriminative textural features across disjoint index sets. However, the derivation assumes the feature map's spatial resolution is fixed at h = H/16, w = W/16 because stage-4 downsampling is set to stride 1 [PAGE 3]. If the input resolution changes or the camera zoom varies across games, the absolute pixel extent of a jersey number in feature space changes, invalidating the fixed h′ = 8, s = 4 setting selected by the P-sweep on the self-built basketball set [PAGE 5]. That sweep (P ∈ {2,3,4,7}) is underpowered: it never tests P = 5 or P = 6, yet the authors claim P = 7 is worst and P = 3 optimal based on a volatile mAP/Rank-1 trend [PAGE 5].

The pose branch introduces a second, more brittle dependency. HRNet produces 17 heatmaps, but the head's five joints are fused into one, forcing K = 13 [PAGE 5]. Each joint heatmap M_j gates the backbone feature map by element-wise multiplication, producing F′_j = F ⊙ M_j [PAGE 4–5]. When the pose estimator misfires under occlusion, M_j assigns high activation to invisible joints. After global average pooling (GAP), the global vector is f_global ∈ ℝ^c and each band yields f_i ∈ ℝ^c.

Triplet loss. For anchor a, positive p, and negative n with margin α > 0 [PAGE 4]:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tri}} = \max\bigl(d(a,p) - d(a,n) + \alpha,\, 0\bigr),
\]
where d(·,·) is feature distance; the loss vanishes when the positive–negative separation exceeds α, and such triplets are ignored during optimization [PAGE 4].

Cross-entropy loss. For input feature f, true label y, and predicted class probabilities \(\hat{y}_i\) [PAGE 4]:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} y_i \log \hat{y}_i.
\]

Sliding-branch joint loss. With β = 0.4 [PAGE 4]:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sliding}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tri}}(\mathrm{BN}_{\text{global}} f) +
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\mathrm{BN}_{\text{global}} f) +
\beta \sum_{i=1}^{P} \Bigl[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tri}}(\mathrm{BN}_i f_i) +
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\mathrm{BN}_i f_i)
\Bigr].
\]

Pose and weighting branch. HRNet extracts K = 17 heatmaps, fused to K = 13 (five head joints merged) [PAGE 5]. Joint heatmap M_j gates F to give F′_j = F ⊙ M_j; GAP produces v_j ∈ ℝ^{2048} [PAGE 4–5]. \mathcal{L}_{\text{RE}} =
\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(f'_{\text{global}}) +

\]

Historical and scientific lineage of SW2S, with multi-week operational traps.

The algorithm descends directly from the part-based convolutional baseline PCB (Sun et al., ECCV 2018), which partitions the feature map into hard horizontal strips [PAGE 2]. SW2S inherits PCB's local-feature philosophy but rejects its hard partition, citing the information loss and discontinuity between adjacent strips [PAGE 2–3]. The multiple-granularities network MGN (Wang et al., ACM MM 2018) attempted to fuse global and local features but still suffered from hard block boundaries [PAGE 2]. SW2S's sliding-window overlap is explicitly inspired by the soft-splitting idea in Tokens-to-Token ViT (Yuan et al., ICCV 2021) [PAGE 2], translating a vision-transformer token-overlap concept into CNN band convolution.

The pose-assist lineage runs from PGFA (Miao et al., ICCV 2019), which uses pose labels to gate similarity computation on unoccluded parts, through HOReid (Li et al., TPAMI 2022) and PVPM (Gao et al., CVPR 2020), which fuse joint topology with Re-ID features [PAGE 2]. SW2S diverges by not merely gating on pose but by learning a data-driven importance weight per joint via Sigmoid and by reweighting the per-joint cross-entropy loss with a learned confidence score c′_j [PAGE 5]. The backbone augmentation uses RGA-S (Zhang et al., CVPR 2020) on ResNet-50 stages 1–3 to reduce attention to irrelevant regions [PAGE 3]. Pose estimation itself relies on HRNet (Sun et al., CVPR 2019) [PAGE 4], and data augmentation draws on random erasing, random patch, and Cutout [PAGE 5].

Multi-week trap: If SW2S is deployed across a sports season rather than on a static dataset, the fixed sliding geometry (P = 3, h′ = 8, s = 4 at 1/16 resolution) will misalign as camera zoom, player distance, and jersey-sponsor patch layouts vary week to week. The pose estimator's prior bias—visible in [PAGE 9] where occluded joints receive inflated scores—compounds under kit changes (new sponsor logos, compression sleeves, tape) because HRNet was not retrained on the target domain. Cross-season identity drift (players traded, numbers reassigned) further invalidates the gallery, and the small self-built identity pool (56 players from 5 games) offers no temporal holdout to measure week-over-week Rank-1 decay. Ice-hockey multi-camera work (Um et al.) [PAGE 2] already showed that single-view appearance Re-ID degrades under severe cross-camera appearance shift; SW2S has no multi-camera geometry module, so its generalization beyond the basketball arena is unproven.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto conditions.

Physical-layer veto. The paper explicitly states that basketball players wear similar jersey colors and occlude one another severely, making global features difficult to extract [PAGE 2]. A hard physical veto applies whenever two players wear visually identical kits and their bounding boxes overlap such that the sliding bands cannot isolate a unique jersey-number support. Because SW2S is appearance-only (no jersey-number OCR, no temporal trajectory model), no amount of sliding-window overlap can disambiguate two fully occluded, identically dressed players; the system must defer to a higher-layer tracker or reject the match.

Biomechanical veto. HRNet extracts 17 joints, reduced to K = 13 by fusing the head [PAGE 5]. A biomechanical veto triggers when limbs leave the frame (right hand, feet out of view) or are occluded by teammates [PAGE 9]. In those cases M_j ≈ 0, v_j carries no signal, and the Sigmoid gate may either suppress a valid but low-activation joint or pass a noisy one. If more than a critical fraction of K joints are invalid, the part-based embedding collapses to the global vector alone, reducing SW2S to a baseline ResNet-50 Re-ID with no local discriminability. The ablation (method b, Rank-1 71.32%) quantifies this degraded mode [PAGE 7].

Weather/environmental veto. Although basketball is typically indoor, arena lighting, motion blur, and camera exposure changes violate the spatial-continuity assumption of the sliding window. Motion blur smears the high-frequency jersey-texture features that the 1/16-resolution feature map depends on [PAGE 3]. If blur reduces effective spatial resolution below the stride scale s = 4, adjacent bands become correlated copies of blurred structure rather than distinct local observations, and the overlap benefit vanishes. A veto is warranted when the point-spread function of the imaging system exceeds the band overlap margin (h′ − s pixels), because then the continuity guarantee of s < h′ is physically nullified.
