<THINK_TRACE>
The paper's central move is to collapse a multi-stage heterogeneous (CNN→Transformer) fusion pipeline into a homogeneous (Transformer→Transformer) one-stage fusion, and to justify that collapse via a contrastive alignment (CoA) pre-step. The multi-hop reasoning chain I audit is: (1) vision and language live in divergent feature distributions — [PAGE 1] states "vision is spatial redundancy and semantic sparse, while language is highly semantic and information-dense"; (2) direct fusion of divergent distributions is "intractable" [PAGE 8], so alignment is needed first; (3) alignment is operationalized as InfoNCE mutual-information maximization; (4) aligned features are then fused by a plain stack of 6 encoder layers; (5) this yields SOTA on five datasets plus the new VL-SOT500.

Gray-zone audit #1 — the MI claim versus the batch-size ceiling. The InfoNCE loss is a variational lower bound on mutual information: I(F^v;F^l) ≥ log N − L_InfoNCE. The right-hand side is capped by log N (batch size). The authors are pinned to N ≤ 14 by a 24 GB RTX 3090 ([PAGE 16]: "we can only use a relatively small batch size (i.e., N ≤ 14)"). So the *theoretical ceiling* on the mutual information they can maximize is log(14) ≈ 2.64 nats; even the A6000 experiment at N=20 only lifts the ceiling to log(20) ≈ 3.00 nats. The paper frames larger batches as merely a memory tradeoff and even claims small batches "may help mitigate the impact of false negative samples" [PAGE 16], but the information-theoretic reading is sharper: the alignment objective is *structurally* starved, not just practically constrained. This is the deepest gray zone — the CoA's headline mechanism is bounded by a hardware wall they cannot cross.

Gray-zone audit #2 — is "one-stage" really one-stage? CoA "occurs exclusively during the training phase" [PAGE 7] and is discarded at inference. At test time the fusion is literally Concat(e_Fv, e_Fl, O) → 6 MHSA encoders over 441 tokens. So the "alignment" is not a runtime operation; it is a weight-shaping regularizer. The honest description is "contrastive-pretrained two-tower, jointly fine-tuned with a 6-layer fusion transformer," not a runtime one-stage aligner. The distinction matters for their efficiency claims.

Gray-zone audit #3 — where language actually helps versus where it is discarded. The paper is candid that on OTB99-L/TNL2K/WebUAV-3M they "discard the linguistic branch before testing" [PAGE 11] because first-frame descriptions are ambiguous ([PAGE 9] shows the "Bird1"/"INF_crowd2" counterexamples). So the linguistic branch is only *used* on LaSOT, LaSOT-Ext, and VL-SOT*. The generalization gains on OTB99-L (77.3% AUC) are therefore attributed to "implicit linguistic information in the visual branch" [PAGE 12] — i.e., language injected via alignment then read out through the vision tower. This is a subtle but defensible claim, yet it means the "language-enhanced" story is really "language-as-augmentation-baked-into-vision," not online language reasoning.

Gray-zone audit #4 — the SOTA framing. On LaSOT, COST is 69.2% AUC vs OSTrack's 69.1% ([PAGE 14], Table 5) — a 0.1-point tie, with the paper conceding OSTrack uses MAE pretraining while COST trains "from scratch." The dominant gains are on their *own* dataset, where they control the annotations. This is legitimate but should be weighted: self-curated benchmarks inflate relative deltas.

Gray-zone audit #5 — the failure regressions. On full occlusion and deformation, COST *loses* to the visual baseline TransT (0.065 vs 0.066 and 0.038 vs 0.041, [PAGE 11]). The mechanism is stated: COST "tracking incorrect targets that are semantically similar to the real target after full occlusion or severe deformation" [PAGE 12]. This is a genuine, physically-motivated failure mode: language can actively mislead when the target's class-level semantics are shared by a distractor. That is the core tension of the whole method and it is under-emphasized relative to the 13/17 attribute wins.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All operators are the paper's own; units and breakdown follow.

Scaled dot-product / multi-head self-attention ([PAGE 6], Eqs. 2–3), dimensionless after softmax:
$$\text{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V,\qquad \text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V)=\text{Concat}(H_1,\dots,H_h)W^{O}$$
$$H_i=\text{Attn}(QW_i^{Q},KW_i^{K},VW_i^{V}),\qquad W^{Q/K/V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_m},\ W^{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_m}.$$
For BERT_BASE ([PAGE 7]): 12 layers, hidden 768, 12 heads ⇒ per-head $d_k=768/12=64$, scale $\sqrt{d_k}=8$.

Transformer encoder block ([PAGE 7], Eqs. 4–5), pre-normalization residual:
$$X'_l=\text{LN}\!\big(X_l+\text{MultiHead}(X_l)\big),\qquad X_{l+1}=\text{LN}\!\big(X'_l+\text{FFN}(X'_l)\big).$$

Small-object size metric ([PAGE 4]): $s=\sqrt{wh}$ with $w,h$ in pixels; selection rule (verbatim) "an average relative size smaller than a threshold s (i.e., 1%) and an average absolute size smaller than √k × k (i.e., √22 × 22) pixels."

Normalized relative speed ([PAGE 4], Eq. 1) — units [body-lengths · s⁻¹]:
$$\Delta_t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{t-1}s_t}}\cdot\frac{\lVert p_t-p_{t-1}\rVert_2}{T_t-T_{t-1}},\quad s_t=\sqrt{w_th_t}\ [\text{px}],\ p_t=(x_t,y_t)\ [\text{px}],\ T_t\ [\text{s}].$$
Dimensional check: numerator $\lVert\Delta p\rVert/\Delta T$ is px·s⁻¹; dividing by $\sqrt{s_{t-1}s_t}$ (px) gives s⁻¹ scaled by object size — i.e., body-lengths traversed per second. VL-SOT230 mean $\overline{\Delta}=0.755$; VL-SOT270 mean $=3.930$; VL-SOT500 overall $=2.469$ ([PAGE 3], Table 1).

Contrastive alignment (InfoNCE), cosine similarity $\text{sim}(a,b)=\dfrac{a\cdot b}{\lVert a\rVert\,\lVert b\rVert}\in[-1,1]$ ([PAGE 8], Eqs. 6–8), temperature $\tau=0.5$:
$$L_{v2l}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\exp(\text{sim}(F^v_i,F^l_i)/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}_{[j\ne i]}\exp(\text{sim}(F^v_i,F^l_j)/\tau)},\quad
L_{l2v}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\frac{\exp(\text{sim}(F^l_i,F^v_i)/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{1}_{[j\ne i]}\exp(\text{sim}(F^l_i,F^v_j)/\tau)},$$
$$L_{CoA}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{(F^v_i,F^l_i)\sim(F^v,F^l)}\big[L_{v2l}+L_{l2v}\big].$$
Notation flag: [PAGE 8] renders the indicator as "$\mathbb{1}_{[j,i]}$…evaluating to 1 iff $j\ne i$" — the intended condition is $j\neq i$; the subscript is a typographical artifact. The MI link is $I(F^v;F^l)\ge \log N - L_{\text{InfoNCE}}$, so with $N=14$ the bound saturates at $\log 14\approx2.64$ nats.

Regression + classification + total loss ([PAGE 9], Eq. 10; $\lambda_1=5,\ \lambda_G=2,\ \alpha=1$):
$$L_{reg}=\sum_i \mathbb{1}_{[y_i=1]}\big[\lambda_1 L_1(B_i,\hat B_i)+\lambda_G L_{GIoU}(B_i,\hat B_i)\big],\qquad L=L_{CoA}+L_{reg}+\alpha L_{ce}.$$
As printed, $L_{ce}=\sum_i y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)$ omits the leading negative sign and uses a sum rather than a mean; the standard binary cross-entropy carries the minus sign.

Complexity ([PAGE 20]): visual-branch self-attention is $\mathcal{O}(N_v^2 d)$ with $N_v=1024$ (verbatim: "quadratic with respect to the token length"); VLT self-attention over $N'_v+N_l+1=400+40+1=441$ tokens is $\mathcal{O}(441^2 d)$, i.e., cheap. This asymmetry explains the measured 0.0231 s visual vs 0.0071 s fusion cost (Table 14).
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Lineage has three converging trunks. (a) Attention: Vaswani et al. 2017 [PAGE 22, ref 15] → BERT [ref 21] → ViT [ref 54] → DETR's set prediction [ref 58] → TransT [ref 17, CVPR-2021], the direct baseline. COST reuses TransT's ResNet50-modified visual branch verbatim (stage-4 stride 2→1, dilated conv stride 2, [PAGE 7]) and BERT_BASE verbatim, so most parameters are inherited, not novel — the CVLF module is only 7.2M of 146.5M total ([PAGE 20], "5% of the total parameters").

(b) Contrastive learning: CPC/InfoNCE (van den Oord 2018 [ref 29]) → MoCo [ref 70] / SimCLR [ref 71] → CLIP [ref 114]. The "align before fusion" doctrine traces to ALBEF (Li et al. 2021 [ref 51]) and single-stream alignment [ref 53]. COST ports this recipe from pretraining into a tracker's training loop.

(c) VL tracking datasets & models: SNLT [ref 3], VLT_TT/ModaMixer [ref 16/28], JointNLT [ref 41], MMTrack [ref 43], UVLTrack [ref 110]. On the dataset side: LaSOT [ref 4/31], TNL2K [ref 2], WebUAV-3M [ref 5], and small-object precedents Small90 [ref 37], TSFMO [ref 33], LaTOT [ref 38] (from which 165 test videos are absorbed and re-annotated, [PAGE 5]).

Multi-week traps for anyone reproducing this: (1) The pseudo-language trap — GOT-10k/TrackingNet lack descriptions, so the authors inject "major class and motion class" strings; CLIP scores (Table 3, GOT-10k: 0.635293 vs "initial detailed" 0.639789) look near-identical, but [PAGE 19] warns verbose annotations inject "redundant or erroneous phrases (e.g., 'towards the left side of the image')" that "mislead the tracker." A naive replication using detailed captions will regress. (2) The train/test annotation-consistency trap — [PAGE 19] shows best results occur when training and testing annotation *styles* match (except for the motion-class variant), so silently swapping caption generators between splits corrupts the alignment. (3) The training-regime trap — two regimes exist (aligned four-set vs complete three-set fine-tune, [PAGE 11]); conflating them produces non-comparable LaSOT vs OTB99-L numbers. (4) The "discard language at inference" trap — the linguistic branch is dropped for OTB99-L/TNL2K/WebUAV-3M; a reproducer who keeps it online will see degradation on ambiguous captions.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the field's belief state and its regression under pressure. The community's prior (habit): "more fusion stages + more modalities = better." COST's counter-thesis: alignment first, then a *dumb* 6-layer stack suffices. The paper's own ablation adjudicates this — Table 6 shows CoA contributes −1.8% (LaSOT) / −2.2% (LaSOT-Ext) if removed, and the [OBJ] token +1.0%/+1.1%, while merely swapping GloVe→BERT_BASE adds only +0.7% ([PAGE 15], Table 7). The larger structural lever is CNN→Transformer at the *tracking* level: Table 10 shows SiamRPN++ 49.9%→54.0% and TransT-GloVe 56.1% vs SiamRPN++-BERT 54.0%. So the recursive read is: the field over-attributes gains to the fusion module; the data say the *visual backbone* and *alignment regularizer* carry most of the lift.

Crisis-habit regression under distribution shift: when the tracker meets a *semantically ambiguous* caption (Fig. 9's "the person on the right bottom corner"), the habit that worked on clean LaSOT captions regresses catastrophically — the language prior now actively competes with weak visual evidence and the model locks onto the wrong same-class instance (the full-occlusion/deformation regressions, [PAGE 12]). The authors' counter-trap is regime 2: discard language, fine-tune vision only ([PAGE 11]). This is a defensive retreat, not a solution — it concedes the online-reasoning thesis precisely where it is most needed.

The "clock" is the real-time budget: 36 FPS on one RTX 3090 (Table 14), with language features computed *once* ("only extract language features once in the first frame," [PAGE 20]) at a fixed 0.0204 s. This single-frame amortization is what makes a 110M-parameter BERT affordable; it also means the language channel is *static* across the sequence — the "clock" freezes language at t=0, so any target that changes behavior mid-sequence (Golf-1's ball landing, [PAGE 21]) receives no corrective semantic signal. The recursive trap: teams chasing higher FPS will reach for linear-attention/Mamba ([PAGE 20–21] explicitly names Mamba [ref 126]), but that attacks the visual 0.0231 s term, not the frozen-language problem — optimizing the wrong term of the latency budget.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto 1 — feature-starvation floor. A 14-px target (VL-SOT500 mean, Table 1) cropped at 4× → ~56-px region, resized to 256² (upscale ×4.57) → target ≈64 px; backbone stride 8 → an ~8×8 ≈ 64-token footprint, later reshaped to a 20×20 = 400 grid ([PAGE 7]: $N'_v=400$). So the object activates on the order of ~60 of 441 VLT tokens. Raw information is bounded by ≈$14^2=196$ pixels; after 8× downsampling the discriminative signal is a few dozen activations. No fusion architecture can manufacture localization precision below this sensing floor — this is a hard physical veto on AUC, and it is why *all* methods collapse from LaSOT (~69% AUC) to VL-SOT230 (~33%, Table 4): the modality itself is starved.

Physical veto 2 — motion-blur vs frame budget. Blur is exposure-driven, an independent physical channel from the relative-speed metric. $\Delta=3.930$ body-lengths·s⁻¹ at 30 fps ⇒ ≈0.13 body-lengths·frame⁻¹ ≈1.8 px·frame⁻¹ for a 14-px object — which does *not* by itself exceed the 4× search window. Therefore the VL-SOT270 failure is *not* pure search-window escape; it is blur + appearance ambiguity compounding under a tiny footprint. This is a precise, testable claim: raising frame rate (shorter exposure) should help more than enlarging the search window. The verbatim failure datum supports the veto — COST's tracking failure rate is 34.9% (TNL2K) → 70.1% (VL-SOT270), and only COST/MMTrack/KYS exceed 10% mACC (12.1/10.4/10.1, [PAGE 13]).

Physical veto 3 — VRAM/batch wall on the MI objective. As derived, $I\ge\log N - L$; the 24 GB card caps $N\le14$ ⇒ MI ceiling ≈2.64 nats. This is a hardware-physics veto on the *mechanism*, orthogonal to model quality. It is confirmed empirically: $N{=}20$ on a 48 GB A6000 adds only +0.5%/+1.4% (LaSOT/LaSOT-Ext), consistent with a saturating log-N bound rather than a linear one.

Adjudication: the physical layer wins the argument. The method's ceiling is set by (i) the ~60-token target footprint and (ii) the frozen-language clock, not by fusion depth. The paper implicitly concedes (i)–(ii) in Limitations ([PAGE 20–21]: "struggles to handle fast-moving targets," "relies on accurate language annotations"). No architectural claim can veto these constraints; only more sensing (resolution/frame-rate) or a temporal memory mechanism ([PAGE 21], SAM2/motion-dynamic memory [ref 128/129]) can lift them.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable, pre-registerable counterfactuals, each with a falsifiable numeric prediction drawn from the paper.

VETO 1 — CoA necessity. Counterfactual: remove contrastive alignment (the "w/o CoA" row). Prediction: LaSOT AUC falls from 58.6% to 56.8% and LaSOT-Ext from 41.8% to 39.6% ([PAGE 15], Table 6). Verifiable in-code; if a reproduction shows CoA neutral or positive-to-remove, the MI-alignment thesis is falsified. Confidence: high (author-measured).

VETO 2 — language as a liability under class ambiguity. Counterfactual: subject the tracker to full occlusion / severe deformation with a same-class distractor present. Prediction: COST underperforms the *language-free* TransT baseline — measured 0.065 vs 0.066 (full occlusion) and 0.038 vs 0.041 (deformation) on VL-SOT230 ([PAGE 11]). This is the sharpest veto: adding language *hurts* here. Verifiable per-attribute; a positive language gain in these two attributes would refute the failure-mode claim on [PAGE 12].

VETO 3 — high-speed small-object cliff. Counterfactual: raise average relative speed from the VL-SOT230 regime (≈0.755) to the VL-SOT270 regime (≈3.930) at fixed ~14-px size. Prediction: COST mACC collapses from 33.6% to 12.1% (≈21.5-point absolute drop, matching the "≈20%" claim, [PAGE 13]), and on the extreme Golf-1 sequence ($\Delta=3.238$) the failure rate hits 70.1% ([PAGE 21]). Falsifiable: a tracker maintaining >30% mACC at $\Delta>3$ on VL-SOT270 would break the "high-speed is unsolved" conclusion. As of this paper, only COST/MMTrack/KYS clear 10% mACC, so the cliff is empirically real.

Cross-veto consistency check: VETO 1 (CoA helps on clean data) and VETO 2 (CoA hurts on ambiguous data) are not contradictory — they jointly predict that the benefit of alignment is *sign-gated* by caption reliability and class-distractor geometry. Any holistic evaluation must therefore stratify by annotation quality (the paper's own CLIP-score analysis, Table 3, and the "discard language" regime 2, [PAGE 11], are the correct stratification axes). The three vetoes are jointly satisfiable only by a system that gates language influence per-frame — which COST does *not* implement, and which its single-frame language clock structurally precludes.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Bottom line: COST is a well-engineered repackaging — TransT visual branch + BERT + CLIP-style alignment + a 6-layer fusion stack — whose genuine novelty is (a) the first language-annotated small-object benchmark (VL-SOT500) and (b) evidence that "align-then-shallow-fuse" beats multi-stage fusion on clean data. Its ceiling is physically bounded by a ~60-token target footprint, an exposure-driven blur channel, and a 24 GB-card MI bound of ≈2.64 nats; its most instructive result is negative — language can *degrade* tracking under class ambiguity and extreme speed, which the single-frame language clock cannot repair.
