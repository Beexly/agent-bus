Hull’s move is to replace unobservable mental entities with an intervening variable—habit strength—whose presence is inferred from reinforcement after-effects. The scientific lineage proceeds forward through several multi-week traps that Hull’s static excerpt does not resolve. Rescorla and Wagner (1972) replaced Hull’s simple reinforcement-after-effect accumulation with a prediction-error rule, making habit strength sensitive to contingency rather than contiguity. Multi-week traps specific to this lineage: (1) spontaneous recovery, where an apparently extinguished habit re-emerges after weeks without reinforcement, violating the monotonic decay assumption of simple after-effect models; (2) renewal and context-shift effects, where a habit strong in Context A fails to evoke in Context B despite unchanged H_t, because competing stimuli 𝒞_t changed [PACE with Hull’s “other stimuli” clause on PAGE 1]; (3) latent inhibition, where pre-exposure to a stimulus without reinforcement slows later habit acquisition over weeks, showing that H_t is not solely a function of experienced reinforcement after-effects. A counter-trap embedded in this genealogy is that an adversary who knows an agent’s week-scale habit profile can cue the original acquisition context after a period of apparent stability, precipitating relapse to a high-H_t response that the agent had ostensibly abandoned.
