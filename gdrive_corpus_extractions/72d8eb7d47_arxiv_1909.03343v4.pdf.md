Formal objects and operators. Let \(V\) be the vocabulary of words and noun phrases, and let \(J\) be the number of observed player covariates. \[
p(w\mid e)\propto \exp\!\left(\beta_w+(\gamma y_e)_w\right)
=\exp\!\left(\beta_w+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,w}y_{e,j}\right),
\]
with the normalized form
\[
p(w\mid e)=
\frac{\exp\!\left(\beta_w+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,w}y_{e,j}\right)}
{\sum_{w'\in V}\exp\!\left(\beta_{w'}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\gamma_{j,w'}y_{e,j}\right)}.
\]
Here \(\gamma_{j,w}\) is “the difference in log-probability of \(w\) for the \(j\)th player feature being on versus off” [PAGE 3]. A generic sparse MAP objective consistent with the paper’s description is
\[
\hat{\gamma}
=
\arg\max_{\gamma}
\left[
\sum_{e,w} c_{e,w}\log p(w\mid e)
-\lambda\sum_{j,w}|\gamma_{j,w}|
\right],
\]
where \(c_{e,w}\) is the observed count of term \(w\) in windows for entity \(e\), and the penalty corresponds to “a uniform Laplace prior on all elements of \(\gamma\) to induce sparsity” [PAGE 3]. The authors learn this with “the LibLBFGS implementation of OWL-QN, an L1-capable quasi-Newtonian convex optimizer” [PAGE 3], from “a sample of one million noun phrases and noun tokens from the corpus” [PAGE 3].

The paper does not state the log base, so the safe statement is “log-probability units under the chosen base.” \(y_{e,j}\) are dimensionless binary indicators. Counts and mention totals are dimensionless. The regularization rate \(\lambda\) has inverse units to \(\gamma\) under the chosen log base. The corpus contains 267,778 mentions of 4,668 unique players, 65.7% nonwhite [PAGE 2]. Counter-trap: the analyst can overcorrect by treating every race-correlated word as proof of bias. The counter-trap is visible in the paper’s own caveats: “big hole” is an action, “strong safety” is a position term, and “beast mode” is a nickname [PAGE 4, PAGE 5]. A recursive analyst must model the commentator modeling the audience, and then model their own modeling of the commentator. If the analyst ignores position, action, and lexicon noise, the analyst’s “bias detector” becomes a stereotype detector.

Clock: the temporal dimension changes the mental-state inference. Figure 2 shows that “The percentage of nonwhite quarterbacks mentions has drastically increased over time” [PAGE 3]. A commentator in 1970 and a commentator in 2010 face different roster compositions and different public norms. Without a clock control, a pooled race coefficient can mix changing position demographics with changing commentary habits. The safest recursive statement is therefore conditional: within position and decade, and with action terms separated from attribute terms, the paper finds naming and sentiment patterns consistent with prior work; outside those conditions, the mental-state attribution is underdetermined. The paper’s limitation that non-QB per-position sentiment is underpowered [PAGE 5] is the clock-and-support veto on strong recursive claims.
