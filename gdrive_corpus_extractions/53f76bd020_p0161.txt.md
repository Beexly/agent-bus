For non-renewable natural agents or degrading infrastructure, \delta exceeds replacement, so K declines. Any supply analysis invoking Condition 5 without a positive \delta is physically invalid; the veto is absolute for all non-renewable or wear-out subject factors.

Biomechanical layer veto: Condition 1's labor-force composition \boldsymbol{\lambda} [PAGE 146] is bounded by physiological and demographic limits. Skill acquisition, working-hour endurance, health status, and turnover rates bound how rapidly \boldsymbol{\lambda} can shift. A static model assuming instantaneous recomposition of the labor force violates biomechanical and demographic constraints; the veto applies to any scenario requiring \boldsymbol{\lambda}(t+\Delta t)\neq\boldsymbol{\lambda}(t) on a timescale shorter than human training or replacement cycles.

Weather and geographical layer veto: Condition 6 (geographical distribution of productive agencies) [PAGE 146] and Condition 5 (natural agents) [PAGE 146] are exposed to weather. Let g\in\mathcal{G} denote a geographic cell. A weather shock W(g,t) (temperature extreme, precipitation anomaly, wind event) alters the productivity of natural agents in that cell:
\[
K_{\text{natural}}(g,t+\Delta t) = K_{\text{natural}}(g,t)\cdot[1 - \phi(W)] , \quad \phi(W)\in[0,1].
\]
If \phi(W)>0, the geographic supply map G changes discontinuously. Any pre-shock supply parameterization is vetoed; the weather shock invalidates Condition 5's natural-agent assumption and Condition 6's fixed spatial distribution simultaneously. The adjudication rule: whenever W(g,t) induces non-negligible \phi(W) over the decision horizon, the static supply list [PAGE 146] cannot serve as a basis for physical supply projections.
