4. Nucleus (top-p) sampling operator (Holtzman et al. 2020, invoked on [PAGE 5]). Sort bins by descending probability $p_{(1)} \ge p_{(2)} \ge \cdots$; the nucleus is the minimal prefix with cumulative mass ≥ `p_top`:
$$\mathcal{V}^{(p)} = \min\Big\{\,S : \sum_{k \in S} p_{(k)} \ge p_{\text{top}} \,\Big\}, \qquad p'_k = \frac{p_k}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}^{(p)}} p_j}\ \text{for } k\in\mathcal{V}^{(p)}.$$
Here the "vocabulary" is the spatial bin set, so `p_top` controls spatial reach of a single update. Empirically optimal at 0.8–0.9 [PAGE 7].

5. Coefficient of restitution, used as the surface-quality operator (dimensionless). [PAGE 8]:
$$e_{\text{cor}} = \frac{v_{\text{after}}}{v_{\text{before}}}, \qquad e_{\text{cor}} < 1 \Rightarrow \text{net momentum loss (slower surface)}.$$
Predicted ordering (clay < hard) is recovered, "better represented when we introduce the surface token."

6. Distributional discrepancy operator (Wasserstein-1, meters for speeds/positions). [PAGE 6, 7; Figs 9, 13]:
$$W_1(\mu,\nu) = \int_0^1 \big| F_\mu^{-1}(u) - F_\nu^{-1}(u) \big|\, du.$$

7. Trajectory error (RMSE, meters) used against the linear-extrapolation baseline. [PAGE 12, Fig 18]:
$$\text{RMSE}(t) = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N_{\text{obj}}} \sum_{n} \big\| \hat{x}_n(t) - x_n(t) \big\|^2}.$$
Result: SportsNGEN beats linear extrapolation "over a short time" only; both diverge thereafter over the 1.75 s evaluated window — a *finite validity horizon*, not sustained fidelity.

8. Calibration operator (information/probability units). Well-calibrated means the predicted win fraction equals the observed frequency: $P(\text{win} \mid \hat{q}) = \hat{q}$ for each bin, tested with 100 rollouts per sampled branch point [PAGE 10].

Breakdown regimes (formal): (i) exposure-bias collapse if injected ball-noise falls below the ±25 mm / ±12.5 mm floor [PAGE 6]; (ii) degenerate rest under regression avoided by classification [PAGE 5]; (iii) top-p U-curve, non-realistic-rally fraction rises at both tails with an irreducible ~20% floor even at optimum (Fig 8b); (iv) OOD initial conditions "produce unreliable results" [PAGE 10]; (v) unseen players silently "default to a generic player representation" [PAGE 10] — a soft singularity where identity-conditioned prediction degenerates to the population mean.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

"Retro-causal" here is best read as: which prior works causally produced *this* design, and which historical traps the authors had to escape.

Direct lineage. The architectural ancestor is baller2vec (Alcorn and Nguyen, 2021) [PAGE 1, 3], itself a multi-entity transformer over basketball tracking. The paper is explicit about the delta: "We make a significant update to the baller2vec experiments by modelling both the ball and the players simultaneously" [PAGE 4] and "Our work builds upon baller2vec to enable sustained gameplay simulations by simultaneously simulating both the player and the ball" [PAGE 3]. The transformer backbone traces to Vaswani et al. (2017); the sampling operator to Holtzman et al. (2020).

Trajectory-prediction lineage and its trap. The paper positions itself against a decade of trajectory work — Yue 2014, Zheng 2016, Le 2017b, Zhan 2019, Li 2021 (GRIN), Tang 2021, Wu 2021 (BasketballGAN) — and names the recurring failure: works generate "realistic, but short rollouts" [PAGE 3] or "cannot generate entire games" [PAGE 3]. The multi-week-class trap this exposes is *rollout horizon collapse*: models that look excellent on 1–5 s windows and disintegrate beyond. SportsNGEN's answer is not a better one-step predictor but a stabilized *distribution* generator (noise injection + classification grid + boundary logic).

The RL fork they refused. Kurach 2020, Liu 2021, Braga & Barros 2022, Yu 2023 represent an RL lineage. The authors deliberately reject it: "Our approach is not RL based. It instead learns in a discriminative fashion ... which obviates the need to use physics based models" [PAGE 3]. The closest true competitor is Yuan et al. (2023), simulated tennis from broadcast video, which they distinguish on two axes: single-shot-cycle modeling with "no coupling between the current shot and previous shots," and baseline-only movement ("no volleys or inner court play") [PAGE 3]. So SportsNGEN's novelty is *cross-shot coupling* plus full-court movement, not the transformer-per-se.

The methodological trap they inherited and reversed. Discriminative sequence models trained on clean teacher-forced data suffer exposure bias at inference. The paper's noise-injection prescription is a direct, quantified rebuttal of the assumption that cleaner training data yields better rollouts — a counterintuitive reversal that is arguably the paper's most transferable contribution beyond sports.

Honest caveat: "multi-week traps" as a literal engineering-log concept is not documented in the paper; I am inferring the debug-class from the design decisions (e.g., the discovered noise floor, the discovered 6 s saturation), not from reported engineering time.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Mapping the template's "habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock" onto the paper's actual mechanisms, and being explicit about what is *not* present:

Habit regression. The learned identity embedding `I ∈ ℝ^ι` is the paper's sole "style/habit" operator. Ablation [PAGE 7, Figs 9–10] shows that removing `I` yields "a much broader distribution of serve speeds, and a nearly identical median serve speed for all three players" — i.e., without `I` the model regresses to a population-average playing style. Accuracy saturates near `ι = 20` with "diminishing returns for further increases." So "habit" is represented as a fixed per-player vector concatenated into every token; it does not update within a rally.

Counter-trap (opponent modeling). This is the honest gap. The template's "recursive theory of mind" implies the model reasons about what the opponent believes/intends. It does not. The model captures multimodality — at ToI "different decisions a player may make when choosing to play the shot either down the line or across the court" [PAGE 7] — but this is a *marginal* distribution over the focal player's own action, not an interactive belief model of the opponent. There is no explicit `P(opponent response | my shot, opponent model)`. Counter-strategic effects appear only implicitly through joint rollouts. I flag this as framing-overreach: the paper does distribution modeling, not recursive ToM.

Clock. The `e` component (elapsed time) is the only temporal-state handle, justified as capturing "player fatigue and team strategy" [PAGE 4] and rally-length realism. This is a weak, scalar proxy for what a "clock" in a decision-theoretic sense would require (fatigue state, score pressure, momentum). The transfer-learning results reinforce the limits: "serve metrics fluctuate with the first serve speed getting worse" as fine-tuning samples grow, "explained by the low variability of the serve distribution being easier to learn when compared to highly variable groundstroke patterns" [PAGE 9]. So the model regresses to easy, low-entropy habits (serve) faster than it captures hard, high-entropy tactical behavior (groundstrokes) — a habit/counter-tactic asymmetry.

Recursive-depth audit. If one insisted on a ToM recursion depth k, the model supports only k=0 (own policy distribution conditioned on state) and, via joint rollouts, an *implicit* k=1 read of opponent tendency. There is no mechanism for k≥2. Any claim of deeper recursive reasoning about this system would be unsupported by the text.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/biomechanical veto. The strongest physical claim is that the model has internalized ball dynamics: mid-flight predictions concentrate in ~0.002% of bins because "the model has learned the physical constraints of the system (e.g. drag, gravity)" [PAGE 7]. This is an emergent, data-driven physics proxy — there is no explicit Newtonian integrator; the classifier grid *learns* the transition. Veto proof that physics is enforced rather than assumed: the grid bounds updates to a finite max displacement per step (46 mm x-axis ≈ fastest serve/step), so any sampled `ρ` is kinematically admissible by construction; the residual non-realism appears as rejected rallies, not as superluminal balls. Biomechanically, the model respects per-player capability ranges via `I` — evidenced by distinct per-player serve-speed distributions (Fig 10a–c) collapsing to one when `I` is removed (Fig 10d–f).

Weather veto. Honest adjudication: weather is *not* vetoed or modeled in the tennis implementation. Weather appears only as a generic example of context tokens on [PAGE 4] ("the weather"), alongside stadium, opponent identity, score. The actual tennis context tokens implemented are surface type, tournament, and first/second-serve (Fig 4, [PAGE 6, 8]). So any weather-conditioned veto is aspirational, not demonstrated. The nearest physical proxy actually implemented is the coefficient-of-restitution surface effect (`e_cor`, [PAGE 8]): clay slows the ball relative to hard, recovered in simulation. That is a *surface* veto, not a *weather* veto.

Cross-layer consistency (physical ↔ statistical ↔ outcome). The three layers are: (1) physical single-step admissibility (grid + learned drag/gravity), (2) distributional match (W₁ on serve/return/groundstroke speeds, toss height), (3) outcome calibration (rally win %, Fig 15). A valid veto requires agreement across layers: e.g., surface token → `e_cor` shift (physical) → serve-speed histogram shift (statistical) → serve-win-% shift (outcome). The paper demonstrates layer-2 and layer-3 agreement and layer-1 admissibility, but does not provide a single worked example tracing one intervention across all three layers quantitatively. That is the missing proof link.

Veto failure mode. The decisive cross-layer veto is OOD: "it is not designed to handle out of distribution situations. Unconventional initial conditions can produce unreliable results" [PAGE 10]. This means the physical layer's admissibility guarantee is only valid *on the training manifold*. Off-manifold, the grid still bounds kinematics (no impossible speeds) but the learned transition probabilities are meaningless — you get physically-plausible-but-behaviorally-wrong rollouts. So the veto holds kinematically everywhere and behaviorally only in-distribution.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

RLVR = reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards. The paper contains no RLVR and no reward model; the relevant analog is the verifiable check layer (boundary logic + "non-realistic rally" rejection). Below are three *falsifiable* veto scenarios grounded in the text, each with a prediction that could be tested against the method's stated behavior.

Veto 1 — Sub-threshold noise injection.
Claim/source: noise below ±25 mm (x) / ±12.5 mm (y,z) causes breakdown [PAGE 6]; the mechanism is exposure-bias recovery failure [PAGE 4].
Falsifiable prediction: as injected noise → 0, the non-realistic-rally fraction in long rollouts rises sharply (not gradually), because the recovery channel that pulls off-distribution predictions back collapses; short rollouts (< ~1 s) remain fine. Verifiable test: sweep noise amplitude, plot rejected-rally fraction vs rollout length. Expected: flat for short, diverging for long, near noise = 0.

Veto 2 — top-p extremes vs the ~20% floor.
Claim/source: "a low top-p value will result in less variety in playing style, but a high top-p value will result in many outliers"; optimum 0.8–0.9; non-realistic rallies are U-shaped in top-p with a ~20% floor even at optimum (Figs 7b, 8b).
Falsifiable prediction: at top-p → 1.0, cross-court-shot frequency in rollouts collapses toward the low-probability mode being over-sampled and rejected rallies spike from physical-constraint violations; at top-p → 0.2, the rally-length distribution narrows (fewer long rallies) because the ball has "too few options ... to update in a way that leads to a realistic rally." Verifiable test: bin rally lengths and shot-direction entropy as a function of top-p. Expected: entropy and rally-length tail shrink monotonically as top-p falls below ~0.5.

Veto 3 — OOD initial-condition behavioral invalidity (kinematic-vs-behavioral split).
Claim/source: OOD/unconventional initial conditions "produce unreliable results"; unseen players "default to a generic player representation" [PAGE 10].
Falsifiable prediction: given an off-manifold start (e.g., an unusual serve pose not in the training set), the grid still yields kinematically admissible trajectories (no super-physical speeds), but the outcome-calibration degrades — the calibration curve in Fig-15 style departs from the diagonal for that subset while remaining calibrated on in-distribution starts. This is the sharpest verifiable veto: physical admissibility persists off-manifold, behavioral calibration does not. Verifiable test: partition rollouts by in- vs out-of-distribution start, compare calibration curves; the model's own admission predicts a clean separation.

Counterfactual-integrity caveat (important, and an honest correction to the "counterfactual" label). The paper's counterfactual analysis [PAGE 9–10] forces a shot mode at a branch point and reports win % (corners ≈ 58%, middle < 50%, from 100 rollouts). These are *model-internal, associational* counterfactuals — interventions within the learned generative distribution — not Pearl-style causal effects. There is no do-calculus, no randomization of the branch assignment, and no confounder control; the 58% figure reflects the model's learned joint, which is only as valid as the training distribution. Sample size (100) also implies wide, unreported confidence intervals on those percentages. So the counterfactual engine is a hypothesis generator for coaching, not a causal-effect estimator, and should be vetoed as the latter.

Net adjudication: all three physical/statistical veto scenarios are internally consistent with the paper's own reported failure modes and are directly testable with the metrics the authors already defined (rejected-rally fraction, W₁, calibration). The single claim that should be vetoed outright is any reading of SportsNGEN as a causal or theory-of-mind engine — the text supports neither.
