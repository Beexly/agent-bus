The surfer is modeled as a memoryless stochastic automaton that chooses among forward links with probability proportional to 1/d_j and teleports with probability alpha [PAGE 5]. The ranker models the surfer by inverting this process: a page's rank is the steady-state visitation frequency induced by the surfer's policy. The publisher, in turn, models the ranker and engineers backlink structure to maximize r(A), producing the recursive loop central to the patent's claim that "a citation from a highly ranked backlink is more important than a citation from a lowly ranked backlink" [PAGE 5].

Crisis habit regression occurs when the rank computation becomes unstable—near spectral-gap collapse, under link-farm attack, or during rapid graph mutation—and the system operator regresses to the simpler heuristic explicitly discussed and dismissed in the patent: simple citation counting r(A)=n [PAGE 5]. This regression is cognitively and computationally cheaper but discards the recursive weighting that the invention claims is superior. The counter-trap is adversarial: once the system relies on weighted backlinks, spammers build synthetic high-rank backlink networks; the ranker counters with teleportation (alpha>0), trust seeds, and link-graph pruning; spammers then regress to content farms and user-signal manipulation, collapsing the model back toward first-order popularity metrics. The clock is the power-iteration update t\mapsto t+1. Its period must be shorter than the characteristic time of link-graph mutation; otherwise the rank vector \mathbf{r}^{(t)} chases a moving fixed point and never equilibrates. Mixing time \tau_{\text{mix}} sets the clock's lower bound: if the web topology changes on a timescale shorter than \tau_{\text{mix}}, the steady-state probability is a fiction, and the recursive theory of mind degenerates into a chase with no fixed point.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto. The patent assumes an accessible, complete linked database represented as a directed graph [PAGE 5]. Physical layer failures—data-center power loss, disk failure, network partition, or undersea-cable rupture—render E partially unobservable or time-varying. A partitioned graph decomposes into multiple communicating classes; the Markov chain is no longer irreducible, Perron-Frobenius uniqueness fails, and multiple steady states exist. Veto: rank computation over an incomplete physical substrate is not a valid probability distribution; the system must halt or serve a stale snapshot. This veto is absolute and non-probabilistic.

Biomechanical layer veto. The random surfer has infinite attention, zero click latency, and memoryless link selection [PAGE 5]. Human surfers have finite working memory, fatigue, bounded click-through rates, and session-dependent return patterns. Attention is a scarce biomechanical resource; the assumption that a surfer follows "a large number of links" [PAGE 4] ignores cognitive cost and boredom. Consequently, the steady-state probability r_i is a normative model of idealized attention, not a descriptive fact about human behavior. Veto: any real-time deployment that treats r_i as a direct proxy for human click probability overestimates the ergodicity of browsing and misallocates ranking authority.

Weather veto. Literal meteorological events (storms, heatwaves affecting cooling and power) and metaphorical "information weather" (flash crowds, DDoS attacks, viral cascades) perturb the edge set E(t) on timescales comparable to or shorter than \tau_{\text{mix}}. When edges change faster than the chain mixes, no stationary distribution exists; the rank vector is perpetually in transient. Veto proof: if \|E(t+\Delta t)-E(t)\|_0 is large relative to N for \Delta t < \tau_{\text{mix}}, the eigenproblem \mathbf{r}=\mathbf{M}(t)\mathbf{r} has no time-invariant solution.

Adjudication hierarchy. Physical vetoes outrank all others: without a connected, observable graph, the math is moot. Biomechanical vetoes limit the interpretation of r as a probability of human arrival but do not invalidate the algorithm as a scoring rule. Weather vetoes are stochastic and mitigated by increasing recomputation frequency or using personalized, time-decayed teleportation. The patent's steady-state framing survives adjudication only under the joint assumptions of a connected physical graph, memoryless surrogate surfers, and quasi-static edge sets over the mixing window.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 (dangling-node singularity). Construct a three-node graph in which node C has r(C)>0 but d_C=0 (no forward links), while A links to C and B links to A. The component equation for any page receiving a backlink from C contains the term r(C)/d_C, which is undefined. Prediction: under naive iteration, rank mass at C is either dropped (rank leak) or the computation halts; with the standard dangling-node repair (replace C's column by 1/N), rank leaks uniformly at rate alpha/N per iteration. Verifiable by direct substitution into the component form: the system has no finite solution without the repair, and with the repair the steady state is unique for alpha\in(0,1].

Veto scenario 2 (periodicity at alpha=1). Construct a directed 2-cycle A↔B plus a third node C linked into the cycle but with no path out, setting alpha=1 (no random jump) [PAGE 5]. Then \mathbf{M}=\mathbf{S} is periodic: the subdominant eigenvalue satisfies \lambda_2=-1. Prediction: the power iteration \mathbf{r}^{(t+1)}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{r}^{(t)} never converges; even and odd iterates remain distinct, and the sequence of ranks oscillates between two distributions. Verifiable by computing eigenvalues of S: the spectral radius is 1 but \mathbf{S} is not primitive, violating the Perron-Frobenius conditions required for a steady-state probability [PAGE 5].

Veto scenario 3 (rank-sink capture). Construct a clique of k pages that link only to one another and have no forward links to the remaining N-k pages; set alpha close to 1 or reduce teleportation probability to epsilon. Prediction: as alpha\to1 or epsilon\to0, the principal eigenvector concentrates almost all rank mass inside the clique (r_{sink}\to1), while external pages receive r_i\approx0. Verifiable by computing the stationary distribution of the block-structured matrix: the sink forms a closed communicating class, and with insufficient teleportation the chain is reducible, so rank is trapped and external authority is systematically suppressed regardless of external content quality.
