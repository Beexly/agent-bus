Perin's Figure 49 is offered as confirmation, but if the true inflection occurs at 18 h or 30 h rather than exactly 24 h, the drive-stimulus mapping is mis-calibrated and the recursion between the experimenter's model of D and the animal's experienced drive state is off by several hours.

Recursive theory of mind closes the loop: the experimenter models the animal's drive state D; the animal's behavior is a function of its model of the reinforcement contingency; under caffeine, the animal's internal model of arousal-extinction contingency shifts, altering perseveration and extinction resistance. The researcher must therefore model the animal modeling the researcher's reinforcement schedule, while simultaneously modeling how caffeine changes the animal's estimate of response-outcome contingency. Failure to nest these models produces the counter-trap: attributing to drive-stimulus generalization what is actually a state-dependent drug-arousal interaction.

Caffeine's reported increase in A and decrease in L [PAGE 236] could reflect peripheral vasoconstriction or central arousal rather than altered extinction learning. Veto condition: if skin temperature varies by more than 1 °C across conditions, or if electrode conductance drifts, the A and L changes are confounded and R_e cannot be attributed to extinction-specific pharmacology.

Biomechanical layer: Salivation volume V_s depends on salivary-gland stimulation and baseline hydration after food privation. The tactile stimulus generalization from forepaw to back in the Nikiforovsky report [PAGE 236] assumes comparable receptive-field density and innervation across skin loci; the forepaw and dorsal surface have distinct somatotopic representations. Veto condition: if the extinguished "back point" lies outside the generalized receptive field of the forepaw representation, persistence of 5 drops/min at the paw [PAGE 236] after back-point extinction indicates response independence, not stimulus-intensity generalization across the body surface.

Weather/environmental layer: Although not mentioned on [PAGE 236], ambient temperature and humidity directly affect skin conductance (GSR) and evaporative water loss, thereby altering both baseline salivation and galvanic skin measures. Veto condition: if chamber temperature deviates from thermoneutrality, food privation interacts with thermoregulatory drive, so D is no longer a single primary drive and the claimed specificity of extinction effects to "the primary drive or need intensity" [PAGE 236] fails.
