A decrease in ambient temperature slows conduction, increasing effective \(L(E)\) and central delays [PAGE 1]. A 5–10 °C drop can shift \(\Delta_{\mathrm{neural}}\) by milliseconds—comparable to the entire “fraction of a second” optimum [PAGE 1]. If room temperature is not held constant, the measured “decay with delay” is actually a thermal confound, vetoing the claim that delay per se causes the decline.

Adjudication rule: The behavioral prediction \(H(\Delta)\) is valid only if all layers pass simultaneously: (1) physical rise time \(< \Delta^{*}\); (2) biomechanical response latency within the plasticity window; (3) ambient temperature stable such that \(L(E)\) does not shift \(\Delta_{\mathrm{neural}}\) by more than the temporal resolution of the design. Any single layer failure voids the inference, making the model veto-proof only under strict environmental and physiological control.
