The lineage is unusually well documented and runs in five distinct strata. **1928–1949:** Martland's *"punch drunk"* description of a progressive dementing condition in boxers [PAGE 2]; Critchley's 1949 relabelling as chronic traumatic encephalopathy of boxers [PAGE 2]. **1937–1968:** nomenclature churn — dementia pugilistica [PAGE 2], traumatic progressive encephalopathy, with case reports from Brandenburg and Hallervorden (1954), Grahmann and Ule (1957), Neubuerger (1959), Courville (1962), Mawdsley and Ferguson (1963), Payne (1968) [PAGE 7 references]. **1973:** Corsellis's 15-boxer series establishes the macroscopic syndrome — cerebral atrophy, ventricular enlargement, corpus callosum thinning, cavum septum pellucidum with fenestrations, cerebellar scarring, substantia nigra neurofibrillary degeneration [PAGE 2]. **1990–1999:** Roberts re-examines Corsellis's material with Aβ immunohistochemistry and finds diffuse deposits in 95% of cases [PAGE 2]; Hof establishes the laminar inversion — CTE NFTs in layers II/III versus AD layers V/VI [PAGE 2]; Geddes identifies the perivascular cluster and sulcal-depth predilection and names it *"distinctive to CTE"* and absent from other tauopathies [PAGE 2]. **2005–2015:** Omalu's NFL case reports, McKee's 2009 tauopathy series, and the 2013 Brain paper defining four stages [PAGE 2, PAGE 7].

**Retrocausal inversion to flag.** The 95% Aβ figure from re-examined 1973 boxer brains [PAGE 2] was for decades read as evidence that CTE *is* an amyloidopathy. The modern re-analysis of a larger, younger series reverses the arrow: *"Aβ plaques were not present in early or mild stages of CTE, but were significantly associated with age at death"* [PAGE 2]. So the historical finding was an age effect misread as a disease mechanism — the older boxer cohort was selected for longevity. The same inversion applies to the punch-drunk stereotype: Critchley's 69 boxers generated the motor-predominant, late-onset picture because that cohort was old; Stern's behavioural/mood-onset-at-35 subtype [PAGE 5] was invisible until younger brains were examined.

**Multi-week traps.** (1) The recovery-interval trap: inter-event spacing is a free parameter the source acknowledges but never fixes, so a "repetition count" model and a "repetition density" model are empirically indistinguishable within the published data. (2) The gyrencephaly trap: rodent models cannot instantiate sulcal-depth stress concentration, so a null rodent result does not falsify the human mechanism and a positive rodent result does not confirm it. (3) The blast-attribution trap: [PAGE 6] notes blast injury *"is often complicated by concussive mTBI,"* so any veteran cohort attributing pathology to blast alone inherits an unremovable confound; the five graded veteran cases were *"mild and ranged from stage I to II"* at a mean age of 31.0 years — consistent with early disease but also with an unrepresentative sample of six.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
**Crisis habit regression.** Under diagnostic uncertainty the clinician or investigator reverts to the historical heuristic: count the fights, count the concussions, and infer the brain. The source explicitly denies the sufficiency of this move — the kinematic primitive is strain [PAGE 2], and the two clinical subtypes arise at ages 35 and 59 from cohorts with overlapping exposure histories [PAGE 5]. The regression is seductive precisely because it is auditable (a count is a number) while strain is not observable in a living patient.

**Counter-trap.** The symmetric failure is diagnostic overshadowing: attributing every mood, cognitive, or motor symptom in a contact-sport athlete to CTE. The source pre-empts this with a hard constraint — all three proposed criteria sets require that *"the symptoms cannot be explained by some other condition"* [PAGE 5]. The base rate makes the trap quantitatively severe: 84 of 133 confirmed cases (63.16%) had CTE only, meaning 36.84% carried a comorbid MND, AD, LBD, or FTLD diagnosis [PAGE 4] capable of generating the identical phenotype. A clinician who assigns all symptoms to CTE is wrong roughly a third of the time even in a *neuropathologically confirmed* series — and confirmation is impossible in life.

**The clock.** Two distinct clocks operate and are routinely conflated. Clock A is the latent interval — symptoms *"typically present years or decades after exposure"* [PAGE 4]. Clock B is the competing risk of death, and it is phenotype-dependent: mean age at death 51 in the behavioural-onset group versus 69 in the cognitive-onset group [PAGE 5]. A naive survival analysis pooling the two subtypes will produce a length-biased estimate of progression, because the fast-converting group is censored earlier. A third clock — the post-injury recovery window — is the one the source says determines whether a single event resolves in *"a few weeks"* or converts to PCS in *"10–15%"* of cases [PAGE 2].

**Recursive correction.** The correct mental model is nested: the patient's own model of their symptoms, the clinician's model of the patient, and the pathologist's model of the clinician's referral — all three are biased in the same direction (toward CTE) inside a brain bank, because families who donate are families who believe the diagnosis. The paper's own architecture acknowledges this by insisting that any diagnosis without tissue or biomarker be called *"traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (TES)"* rather than CTE [PAGE 5] — a deliberate lowering of epistemic commitment, not a synonym.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
**Layer L1 — Kinematic/biomechanical.** Constraint: deformation, not impact count, is causal [PAGE 2]; the lesion localizes to *"focal stress points of the brain"* and to sulcal depths [PAGE 3]. *Veto:* any claim that a lissencephalic rodent preparation establishes or refutes the human sulcal-depth mechanism is vetoed at L1, because the geometry that generates the stress concentration does not exist in that model. The paper itself supplies the admissible substitute — *"gyrencephalic miniature pigs"* [PAGE 3] and primates [PAGE 2].

**Layer L2 — Vascular/perivascular.** Constraint: the lesion is perivascular and *"distinctive to CTE"*, absent from other tauopathies [PAGE 2]; the BBB is breached at injury [PAGE 2]. *Veto:* any purely neuronal-somatic mechanism claim is vetoed at L2, since the anatomical epicentre is the vessel, not the soma.

**Layer L3 — Metabolic.** Constraint: supply falls while demand rises [PAGE 2]; by Eq. 3 the deficit diverges as supply vanishes. *Veto:* a claim that supplemental energy substrate alone prevents chronification is vetoed unless it also addresses the demand side, because the divergence is two-sided.

**Layer L4 — Glial/inflammatory.** Constraint: *"Repetitive mTBI resulted in astrocytic and microglial activation that was not found in the single mTBI group"* [PAGE 2]. *Veto:* any model in which inflammation is a linear function of total injury burden is vetoed here, because the single-versus-repetitive dissociation shows a qualitative, not quantitative, difference.

**Layer L5 — Proteostatic.** Constraint: CTE is *"both a 3R and 4R tauopathy"* with astrocytic tau *"predominantly 4R"* and more widely distributed than in AD [PAGE 3]; TDP-43 is present in *"most cases"* [PAGE 3]. *Veto:* an isoform-agnostic "tauopathy" claim is vetoed, and so is any claim that TDP-43 is incidental — it is staged-linked and appears in roughly half of stage I cases in the fornix and subcortical frontal white matter [PAGE 3].

**Layer L6 — Environmental/chemical.** Constraint: alcohol, opiates, and performance-enhancing drugs *"could also play a role"*; opiate-user brains show significantly higher PHF-tau immunoreactivity; glucocorticoids increase tau phosphorylation and cognitive deficits in rodents [PAGE 3]; stress alone increases tau phosphorylation [PAGE 3]. *Veto:* attributing a subject's tau burden wholly to mechanical exposure is vetoed where any of these exposures is present, because each independently moves the same readout.

**Layer L7 — Exposure-source heterogeneity.** Constraint: military mTBI *"is acquired in widely heterogeneous ways"* and blast *"is often complicated by concussive mTBI"* [PAGE 6]. *Veto:* blast-only attribution in the six veteran cases is vetoed by the paper's own text, since five were blast-exposed but the exposures were not isolated.

**Adjudication rule.** A claim survives only if it is consistent with every layer above it. The perivascular-sulcal-depth-tau conjunction survives L1–L5 as a single coherent object: a geometric stress concentrator acting on a vulnerable vascular-axonal interface, producing a focal proteostatic seed that propagates. Nearly every looser claim — "concussions cause CTE," "blast causes CTE," "Aβ proves CTE" — fails at least one layer.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
**Veto 1 — Single-injury sufficiency (the repetition axiom).** Claim under test: repetitive mTBI is necessary for CTE pathology; a single injury is insufficient. *Falsifying observation:* in h-tau mice, PHF-tau immunoreactivity after a single injury statistically indistinguishable from repetitive injury, with no group difference in astrocytic or microglial activation. *Source anchor:* the paper asserts the opposite — repetitive exposure produced *"significantly greater PHF-tau immunoreactivity compared with mice exposed to a single injury"* and glial activation *"not found in the single mTBI group"* [PAGE 2]. *Complication the veto must survive:* [PAGE 2] also reports single-blast wild-type mice developing *"PHF-tau immunoreactivity, axonopathy, and widespread astrocytosis"* — so the repetition axiom is already partially contradicted at the species/model level. *Prediction if the axiom holds:* the single-versus-repetitive effect size on PHF-tau burden should be positive and reproducible across independent laboratories using the same injury parameters; if it fails to replicate, the axiom is vetoed and Eq. 2's cumulative-dose functional loses its empirical base.

**Veto 2 — Sampling artifact in the perivascular/sulcal-depth signature.** Claim: the perivascular, sulcal-depth, layers-II/III pattern is disease-specific. *Falsifying observation:* age- and exposure-matched prospectively sampled controls showing equal densities of perivascular p-tau foci at sulcal depths. *Source anchor:* the pattern is called *"in stark contrast to the tau pathology in AD"* and *"distinctive to CTE"* [PAGE 2, PAGE 3], with the comparison drawn against *"over 150 prospectively studied controls from the Framingham Heart Study"* and the BU ADC [PAGE 3]. *Prediction if the claim holds:* the perivascular-focus count should separate cases from controls with specificity high enough that Eq. 5's PPV remains usable at plausible prevalence — if it does not, the signature is a sampling artifact and the Table 1 criteria [PAGE 3] are vetoed as diagnostic.

**Veto 3 — The Aβ age confound.** Claim: Aβ plaque burden is a stage marker of CTE progression. *Falsifying observation:* in a series balanced on stage but stratified by age at death, plaque burden tracking age with a partial association with stage near zero after conditioning. *Source anchor:* *"Aβ plaques are not present in early or mild stages of CTE, but were significantly associated with age at death"* [PAGE 2]; *"Approximately 13% of stage III CTE cases show sparse diffuse and neuritic Aβ plaques"* [PAGE 3]; *"Aβ plaques are not found"* in stages I and II [PAGE 3]. *Prediction if the age-confound account holds:* the stage-III plaque prevalence (13%) should be fully explained by the elevated mean age of stage-III versus stage-I/II decedents, and should vanish in an age-matched comparison. If instead plaque prevalence exceeds the age-matched expectation, age alone is insufficient and a genuine stage-linked amyloid process must be admitted — which would partially rehabilitate the 95% figure from the re-examined Corsellis series [PAGE 2].

**Cross-cutting veto on Eq. 5.** Any claim that an in vivo tau-PET or CSF criterion is "diagnostic" is vetoed at unknown prevalence: with $c<1$ and $p\to 0$, $\mathrm{PPV}\to 0$ regardless of sensitivity, and [PAGE 1] states prevalence *"remain[s] unknown."* *Prediction:* the first adequately powered prevalence estimate will move PPV by more than any plausible assay improvement, and until then no in vivo criterion can be validated — which is exactly the gap the source flags as *"critically needed"* research [PAGE 1].
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
