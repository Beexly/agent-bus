We formalize the transition from measure-theoretic convergence to biomechanical viability using the following operators and equations, strictly adhering to SI and Information theoretic units.

**1. The Domination Condition (Theorem A):**
Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space representing the spatiotemporal domain of movement. Let $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ be a measurable function representing the instantaneous force/torque applied to a joint. Let $g: X \to [0, \infty]$ be an integrable function representing the physiological stress tolerance (the "safety envelope").
The theorem states:
$$ \text{If } |f(x)| \le g(x) \quad \mu\text{-almost everywhere (a.e.), and } \int_X g \, d\mu < \infty, \quad \text{then } f \in L^1(\mu). $$
*Units:* Force $[N]$, Stress Tolerance $[N]$. Measure $\mu$ is typically time $[s]$ or spatial volume $[m^3]$. Integral yields Impulse $[N\cdot s]$ or Work $[J]$.
*Breakdown Regime:* The inequality fails if a compensatory movement creates a force spike $f(x_0)$ such that $|f(x_0)| > g(x_0)$ at some point $x_0$ within a set of non-zero measure. This represents a localized overload exceeding tissue capacity.

**2. Monotone Convergence (Theorem B):**
Let $\{f_n\}$ be an increasing sequence of non-negative measurable functions (e.g., cumulative fatigue damage per session) such that $f_n(x) \uparrow f(x)$ a.e. Then:
$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n \, d\mu = \int_X f \, d\mu. $$
*Assumptions:* Non-negativity and monotonicity. 
*Singularity:* If $\int f \, d\mu = \infty$, the accumulated damage diverges, implying catastrophic structural failure regardless of how slowly the increase occurs, provided the limit $f$ is infinite on a set of positive measure.

**3. Mean Convergence and Conservation of Work (Exercise 7):**
Consider a sequence of force profiles $f_n \ge 0$ converging a.e. to $f$. Define the error function $g_n = f_n - f$. The exercise posits that if:
$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n \, d\mu = \int_X f \, d\mu, $$
then $f_n \to f$ in the mean ($L^1$), i.e., $\int_X |f_n - f| \, d\mu \to 0$.
*Derivation Insight:* Using the decomposition $g_n = g_n^+ - g_n^-$, we have $\int g_n^+ \, d\mu - \int g_n^- \, d\mu = 0$. Since $g_n^- \le f$ and $f$ is integrable, the Bounded Convergence Theorem applies to $g_n^-$, forcing $\int g_n^- \, d\mu \to 0$ and consequently $\int g_n^+ \, d\mu \to 0$. Thus, $\int |g_n| \, d\mu = \int g_n^+ + \int g_n^- \to 0$.
*Information Unit Mapping:* Interpret $f_n$ as a probability density of kinematic states. The condition $\int f_n = \int f$ is the preservation of total probability mass (normalization). The result implies that conservation of the first moment (mean work) combined with pointwise alignment forces the entire distribution to collapse onto the target in $L^1$.
*Failure Prediction:* If the work integral fluctuates or increases slightly above the target while $f_n \to f$ a.e., it indicates the presence of "hidden mass" (unmeasured high-stress regions) that does not vanish, violating mean convergence.

**4. Failure Mode Operator (Based on Exercise 5):**
Let $E_n = (0, 1/n)$ with Lebesgue measure. Consider characteristic functions scaled by $n$: $h_n = n \cdot \chi_{E_n}$.
Here, $\int h_n \, d\mu = 1$ for all $n$, but $h_n(x) \to 0$ for all $x \neq 0$. The limit function is $h=0$ a.e., yet $\int h_n \not\to \int h$.
In biomechanics, this represents a "force spike": A very short duration ($1/n$) of extreme force ($n$) that maintains constant impulse but causes pointwise divergence (infinite pressure) at the limit. This demonstrates that without a dominating function $g$ (boundedness), mean convergence cannot be inferred from a.e. convergence and mass conservation alone if the sequence is not uniformly integrable.
