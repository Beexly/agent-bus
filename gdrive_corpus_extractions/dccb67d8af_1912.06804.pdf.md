Csató (2019) previously showed UEFA Champions League entry violated strategy-proofness across three seasons [PAGE 2], and Dagaev and Sonin (2018) documented the same defect in UEFA Europa League entry before 2016 [PAGE 2]; both were corrected, leaving the group-stage seeding as the residual defect [PAGE 2].

The multi-week / multi-season trap is structural: the Champions League operates in three-year cycles [PAGE 6], so the 2015/16 rule persisted through 2017/18. The 2018/19 reform—automatic group-stage Pot 1 placement for the Europa League titleholder and backfilling vacancies via the next-ranked associations' champions under UEFA (2019, Article 13.06) [PAGE 6]—expanded the vulnerability to two national leagues rather than one, although no triggering vacancy materialized in 2018/19 or 2019/20 [PAGE 6]. The historical record thus shows a repeating pattern: governing bodies patch qualification incentives (entry) while leaving seeding incentives misaligned, and media attention recurs each cycle (Guyon's regular coverage cited on [PAGE 2]) without closing the logical gap until a rule change is imposed.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
First-order: clubs maximize domestic results because league position determines Champions League access and seeding. Second-order: UEFA designs Pot 1 to reward league champions and titleholders, expecting this to strengthen the competition's brand. Third-order: a club such as Sevilla, anticipating that a better La Liga finish could trigger a titleholder-domestic-champion overlap, faces an inverted belief—its own improved result, or a rival's improved result that changes the titleholder's domestic status, may relegate it to a weaker pot. This is a crisis-habit regression: administrators repeatedly revert to the habit of filling Pot 1 vacancies through European-title identity or association-rank mechanics rather than round-robin outcomes, recreating the perverse incentive each cycle.

The counter-trap is the minimal amendment on [PAGE 7]: Pot 1 comprises the titleholders and the champions of the six highest-ranked associations; if either titleholder is already a domestic champion of a top-six association, the pot is completed with runner(s)-up (and third-placed) from the same association(s); all other pots follow club coefficients. Because no champion can gain a Pot 1 slot by virtue of titleholder identity, \(\partial \pi_t / \partial r_t \ge 0\) and the counter-trap removes the team's incentive to temper domestic performance. The clock is the 2021–24 cycle recommendation [PAGE 7]: UEFA is urged to adopt the amendment before controversy materializes. The recursive danger is that if administrators misjudge the probability of the triggering conjunction as negligible (as the non-occurrence in 2018/19–2019/20 might suggest [PAGE 6]), the habit persists until a high-stakes season realizes it, at which point the integrity cost—teams punished for scoring points—becomes publicly salient and retroactively delegitimates prior cycles.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: player fatigue and match congestion are invariant to pot assignment; they scale with total fixtures, not with whether a team draws from Pot 2 or Pot 3. Biomechanical layer: injury risk per match is a function of opponent intensity and match state, not of seeding pot. Weather layer: local conditions affect individual match outcomes but are ex ante symmetric across potential group opponents and independent of the draw algorithm. None of these layers can veto the logical finding, because the preference reversal is established at the rule-execution layer before any match is played: pot assignment changes the expected opponent Elo distribution \((1689.625 \text{ vs. } 1830.9)\) and hence the expected advancement probability \((0.7402 \text{ vs. } 0.5582 \text{ per matchup expectation; } 0.633 \text{ vs. } 0.483 \text{ knockout qualification})\) [PAGE 5-6] while holding physical, biomechanical, and weather conditions fixed. Formally, \(\partial \mathbb{E}[U_t] / \partial r_t < 0\) under the 2015/16 regime is a structural derivative of the seeding operator, not a mediated effect of \(\text{weather}\) or \(\text{injury}\) variables; therefore those layers are causally downstream and cannot restore incentive compatibility. The veto of the rule change is overruled: the defect is separable from match-day physicality and would persist even in a controlled environment with identical player loads, biomechanics, and weather.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Veto scenario 1 (realized-trigger falsification): If a season occurs in which the Champions League titleholder becomes domestic champion of a top-seven association, the Europa League titleholder is from that same association and is the lowest-coefficient team in Pot 2, then under the 2015/16 rule that Europa League titleholder must be displaced to Pot 3 and its expected knockout probability must fall from the Pot-2 benchmark (0.633 [PAGE 5]) toward the Pot-3 benchmark (0.483 [PAGE 5]). Prediction: in any such realized draw, the displaced team's group-stage points and knockout qualification rate will be statistically lower than a coefficient-matched control team remaining in Pot 2, ceteris paribus. This is verifiable from historical draw and outcome data; absence of the effect would falsify the claimed payoff relevance of pot placement.

Veto scenario 2 (amendment sufficiency): If UEFA adopts the minimal amendment—Pot 1 filled by titleholders plus top-six association champions, with runner(s)-up backfilling any titleholder-domestic-champion overlap, all vacancies awarded via national-league results [PAGE 7]—then for every team \(t\) and every pair of feasible scenarios \(s,s'\), \(r_t(s) > r_t(s')\) implies \(\pi_t(s) \succeq \pi_t(s')\). Prediction: a simulation of historical 2015–2021 seasons under the amended rule will show zero instances of a team moving to a strictly weaker pot solely because its domestic league result improved. Verifiable by recomputing pot composition from published final standings; any counterexample would falsify the sufficiency claim.

Veto scenario 3 (pre-2015 restoration): If the pre-2015 regime is restored—pots formed by club coefficients with the titleholder automatically in Pot 1 [PAGE 7]—then strategy-proofness holds but the champion-reward principle is weakened, and Pot-1 composition becomes more coefficient-homogeneous (fewer association champions seeded purely by domestic rank). Prediction: comparing 2015–2018 groups to pre-2015 groups, group strength variance (by mean Elo of constituents) will decrease under coefficient seeding, and no team will exhibit a negative \(\partial P(\text{advance}) / \partial r_t\). Verifiable from archived seeding pots and Elo ratings; detection of a team punished for a better domestic result under coefficient-only seeding would falsify the strategy-proofness claim for that regime.
