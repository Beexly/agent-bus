Multi-week traps arise because the “final adjustment” [PAGE 1] is presented as a static limit of a dynamic re-sorting process. Trap 1 (capital lock-in): in weeks 1–2, groups acquire indivisible capital; by week 3, the marginal product of labor is conditioned on that sunk capital, so the competitive wage no longer equals the stand-alone counterfactual “without him.” Trap 2 (team-production contamination): as membership changes week to week, the counterfactual output without individual \(i\) is recomputed over a different complement set, making the marginal contribution path-dependent and non-retroactively attributable. Trap 3 (collusive convergence): repeated interaction among the same “large number of groups” [PAGE 1] generates tacit collusion through the folk theorem, violating the “entire absence of collusive action” [PAGE 1] exactly when the model needs it most. The exclusion of the entrepreneur as intermediary [PAGE 1] is itself a genealogical trap: Knight's central innovation is that profit is the return to judgment under uncertainty, yet this page brackets the entrepreneur away, creating a theoretical gap that the later chapters of the same volume must fill by re-introducing uncertainty-bearing as the source of non-competitive residual income.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression operates as follows: under stress, groups abandon the competitive re-sorting ideal and revert to status-preserving, collusive, or seniority-based retention of members. This is a regression from equilibrium play—where each individual “is under pressure to make [his contribution] as large as possible by placing himself in the position where he is really most effective” [PAGE 1]—to low-level heuristic behavior in which agents protect existing positions rather than migrate to their socially most effective post.

The counter-trap is the belief that, once the crisis passes, the “final adjustment” [PAGE 1] automatically restores \(w_i = \operatorname{VMP}_{g,i}\). This is false when hysteresis is present: specific capital, reputation, and group-specific human capital mean that the exit and re-entry margins are asymmetric. The counter-trap predicts persistent wage-productivity gaps after the shock ends, because the competitive path to Eq. 1 is not time-reversible.

Clock structure: let \(\tau_{\text{adj}}\) be the average calendar time required for group membership and capital to re-sort to a new competitive configuration, and let \(\tau_{\text{shock}}\) be the mean inter-arrival time of demand, technology, or weather shocks. If \(\tau_{\text{adj}} / \tau_{\text{shock}} > 1\), the economy is perpetually in disequilibrium; realized output contributions never converge to the ex ante competitively bargained values. If \(\tau_{\text{adj}} / \tau_{\text{shock}} \ll 1\), the static Eq. 1 is a reasonable approximation. The “final adjustment” is therefore a long-run limit that is empirically relevant only under timescale separation.

Recursive theory of mind: perfect knowledge [PAGE 1] implies common knowledge of rationality, corresponding to the \(k \to \infty\) limit of level-\(k\) cognition, in which each agent models every other agent's model of his own marginal contribution ad infinitum. Bounded rationality collapses this regress at finite \(k\); at \(k=0\), an agent does not best-respond to others' models of his contribution at all. Singularity: with heterogeneous priors and \(k\to\infty\), Aumann's agreement theorem implies that common knowledge of rationality forces posterior agreement; if priors differ and cannot be common-knowledged, no fixed point of the recursive belief system exists, and competitive price-taking on VMP is not implementable.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: Capital and many productive factors are indivisible. Let \(K_{\min} > 0\) be the minimum batch size of a machine or plant. If the labor adjustment \(\Delta x_i\) required to realize a positive MPP is smaller than the capital indivisibility margin, then \(\partial f_g/\partial x_i = 0\) almost everywhere and is undefined at the jump. The physical layer therefore vetoes Eq. 1 whenever \(K\) is lumpy relative to labor divisibility, which Knight identifies as the condition under which “competition depends on a degree of divisibility in productive factors” [PAGE 1].

Biomechanical layer veto: Human labor input \(L\) is bounded by physiological limits \(L_{\max}\) [hours per day] and by skill-acquisition time \(\tau_{\text{skill}}\) [seconds]. Labor cannot be treated as an infinitely divisible continuous fluid; discrete shifts and training lags introduce non-convexities in the input space. When \(\Delta L < 1\) shift or when \(\tau_{\text{skill}}\) exceeds the contract horizon, the marginal condition \(w_i = \mathbf{p}^{\top}\partial f_g/\partial L\) fails because the input cannot be varied infinitesimally.

Weather layer veto: For weather-exposed production, output is stochastic: \(\mathbf{y}_g = f_g(\mathbf{x}_g,\omega)\), \(\omega \in \Omega_{\text{weather}}\). Perfect knowledge [PAGE 1] is impossible because agents know the distribution \(\mathbb{P}(\omega)\) but not the realization. The relevant bidding object is \(\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{p}^{\top}\partial f_g/\partial x_i \mid I_i]\), a conditional expectation, whereas the realized contribution is \(\mathbf{p}^{\top}\partial f_g/\partial x_i(\omega)\). When \(\operatorname{Var}[\mathbf{y}_g \mid \mathbf{x}_g]\) is large, risk-averse groups will not pay the full expected VMP; the weather layer vetoes risk-neutral marginal pricing.

Adjudication hierarchy: A competitive equilibrium satisfying Eq. 1 exists only if all layers satisfy convexity and complete information simultaneously. The veto order is sector-dependent: physical indivisibility binds first in capital-intensive manufacturing; the weather layer binds first in agriculture and transport; the biomechanical layer binds first in manual-labor-intensive tasks. The formal veto proof is contraposition: if Eq. 1 holds, then all layers must be convex and informationally complete; therefore the first layer to introduce non-convexity or incomplete information invalidates the marginalist wage rule as a description of that sector's equilibrium.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Indivisible-capital veto). Setup: A group requires a machine with minimum efficient scale \(K_{\min}\). Counterfactual intervention: set \(K=0\) (remove the machine) while holding labor constant. Veto condition: \(K < K_{\min} \implies f_g(\mathbf{x}_g) = f_g(\mathbf{x}_{-K}, 0)\) is invariant to small changes in labor inputs that require the machine, so \(\operatorname{MPP}_{g,i} = 0\) almost everywhere. Observable implication: wage offers should exhibit a discontinuity at \(K = K_{\min}\), and employment should be zero or rationed below the threshold. Prediction: In industries with high minimum scale, measured wages will systematically deviate from computed VMP for workers below the scale threshold, with the gap closing only after \(K \ge K_{\min}\).

Scenario 2 (Information-asymmetry veto). Setup: Group members possess private ability types \(\theta_i\) drawn from distribution \(F(\theta)\). Counterfactual intervention: the organizer observes \(\theta_i\) perfectly before contracting. Veto condition: under private information, \(I_i \neq I_{\text{organizer}}\), so competitive bidding for members operates on adverse-selection contaminated expectations. Observable implication: as entry barriers fall and information becomes more private, the average quality of accepted members declines (market-for-lemons unraveling). Prediction: The membership market will either collapse to a pooling contract at a wage below high-ability VMP or fragment into segmented groups; high-ability types will be underpaid relative to Eq. 1, and low-ability types overpaid, until exit restores a separating equilibrium or market failure.

Scenario 3 (Temporal-clock veto). Setup: Wage contracts are negotiated monthly (\(T_{\text{contract}} = 30\) days), but demand shocks arrive weekly (\(T_{\text{shock}} = 7\) days). Counterfactual intervention: reset contracts weekly. Veto condition: \(\tau_{\text{adj}} > \tau_{\text{shock}}\), so the “final adjustment” [PAGE 1] cannot track shocks. Observable implication: wage changes should lag demand-shock residuals, and realized VMP should deviate from contracted wages by a serially correlated error term. Prediction: Regressing wage growth on contemporaneous and lagged shock residuals will yield significant negative coefficients on lagged shocks (wage rigidity), and the variance of wage-VMP gaps will increase with shock frequency; weekly reset contracts reduce but do not eliminate the gap because adjustment costs remain positive.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
