The single global $S$ of Eq. (8) is itself the modelling assumption most likely to break in ViT-class models of the kind catalogued in Ch. III.

**D. Straight-Through Estimator (Ch. IX, §3.1.2).** Forward: $x_q = q(x)$; backward, per Eq. (16) [PAGE 231]:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial x} \;=\; \frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial x_q}\cdot \mathbb 1\{x \in [x_{\min}, x_{\max}]\}$$

Formally: $q(\cdot)$ has derivative zero almost everywhere and undefined at bin boundaries, so the true gradient is a.e. zero and useless. STE substitutes the identity Jacobian on the unsaturated support. The estimator is therefore **biased**, and the bias vanishes only when the pre-quantisation weight distribution is interior to the representable range with negligible bin-boundary mass. This is the analytic reason QAT's advantage over PTQ shrinks as $b$ falls: at INT4 the boundary mass is no longer negligible and the surrogate objective and the true objective decorrelate.

**E. Knowledge distillation operators (Ch. IX, §3.2).** Temperature-scaled softmax:

$$p_i(T) \;=\; \frac{\exp(z_i/T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j/T)} \qquad [\text{Eq. (17), PAGE 232}]$$

$T$ dimensionless; $T=1$ recovers standard softmax, $T>1$ flattens. Composite objective:

$$\mathcal L_{\text{total}} \;=\; \alpha\,\mathcal L_{\text{KD}} + (1-\alpha)\,\mathcal L_{\text{CE}} \qquad [\text{Eq. (18), PAGE 232}]$$
$$\mathcal L_{\text{KD}} \;=\; T^{2}\cdot \mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{t}(T)\,\|\,p^{s}(T)\right) \qquad [\text{Eq. (19), PAGE 233}]$$
$$\mathcal L_{\text{CE}} \;=\; -\sum_i y^{\text{true}}_i \log p^{s}_i(1) \qquad [\text{Eq. (20), PAGE 233}]$$

Three derivations the volume leaves implicit. (i) $\mathrm{KL}(p^t\|p^s) = \mathcal H(p^t, p^s) - \mathcal H(p^t)$; since the teacher is frozen, $\mathcal H(p^t)$ is a constant offset and $\mathcal L_{\text{KD}}$ is *exactly* cross-entropy matching against soft targets — distillation is not a new loss family, it is label smoothing with a learned, informative prior. (ii) Differentiating Eq. (17), $\partial p_i/\partial z_k = \tfrac{1}{T}p_i(\delta_{ik}-p_k)$, so the gradient magnitude scales as $1/T$; the $T^2$ factor of Eq. (19) restores first-order scale, exactly as the source notes, "$T^2$ multiplier balances the effect of the temperature parameter on the gradient scale" [PAGE 233]. (iii) **Breakdown regimes:** $T\to 0^+$ gives $\mathrm{KL}\to$ a non-differentiable indicator (hard-label limit, dark knowledge destroyed); $T\to\infty$ gives $p_i \to 1/\mathcal C$, $\mathrm{KL}\to 0$ and the signal vanishes; $T^2$ then amplifies numerical noise. Practical $T$ is therefore an interior optimum, and the frequently observed trick of multiplying only the KD branch by $T^2$ while leaving the CE branch at $T=1$ (consistent with Eq. 18–20 as printed) is a *scale-matching* choice, not a derivation.

**F. Compression-quality operators (Ch. IX, §2.2).** $\mathrm{CR} = S_{\text{orig}}/S_{\text{comp}}$ (bytes/byte, dimensionless); $\mathrm{Speedup} = t_{\text{orig}}/t_{\text{comp}}$ (dimensionless, wall-clock seconds divided by seconds); $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ in percentage points of the task metric [PAGE 226]. **Singularities:** $\mathrm{CR}$ is undefined at $S_{\text{comp}}\to 0$; $\mathrm{Speedup}$ measured without fixing batch size, sequence length, and thread count is not a number but an artefact.

**G. Cross-chapter complexity operators.** Self-attention, per Ch. III Eq. (1) [PAGE 58]:

$$\mathrm{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V, \qquad \mathcal O(N^2 D)\ \text{per layer}$$

$N$ = token count (dimensionless), $D$ = embedding width, $d_k$ = head dimension; the source states "the scaling factor $\sqrt{d_k}$ prevents gradient saturation in softmax" [PAGE 58]. Convolution: $\mathcal O(K^2 HWC)$, "linear in the spatial dimensions" [PAGE 60]. Windowed attention: $\mathcal O(N)$ [PAGE 60]. Memory: ViT must materialise $N\times N$ per head [PAGE 60]. Patch count $N = HW/P^2$ [PAGE 54]; encoder block recursion $z'_\ell = z_{\ell-1} + \mathrm{MSA}(\mathrm{LN}(z_{\ell-1}))$, $z_\ell = z'_\ell + \mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{LN}(z'_\ell))$ [Eqs. (2)–(3), PAGE 59]. Mamba: "a theoretically infinite context with high efficiency in linear computational time" [PAGE 14], obtained by discretising continuous-time SSMs — "these continuous-time equations are converted using mathematical discretization steps" [PAGE 21].

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Four lineages converge on Chapter IX, and each carries a distinct failure memory.

*Lineage 1 — rate–distortion and the 6 dB law.* The quantisation block of Chapter IX descends from information theory, not from deep learning: entropy-coded scalar quantisation and the high-resolution approximation of distortion as $D \propto S^2/12$ predate the perceptron. The volume cites Jacob et al. (2018) for integer-arithmetic inference and Krishnamoorthi (2018) for the 8-bit regime, plus Nagel et al. (2021) for the accuracy-loss side [PAGE 227–231]. What the genealogy implies, and the chapter does not say: the accuracy–bit curve is *not* smooth. It has a knee, and the knee sits wherever the network's per-layer sensitivity distribution becomes heavy-tailed. Papers post-dating the chapter's core citations (outlier-aware and error-correcting low-bit schemes) exist precisely because Eq. (8)'s single scalar $S$ is inadequate for attention blocks.

*Lineage 2 — the efficiency-through-architecture line.* SqueezeNet-class compact filter design and the pruning/sharing programme are name-checked via "Cheng et al. (2018)" and "Gou et al. (2021)" [PAGE 227], extended to "quantization, sparsity, dynamic inference, edge AI, and hardware-aware optimization approaches (Li et al., 2023; Dantas et al., 2024)" [PAGE 218]. The lineage's lesson, repeatedly relearned at multi-week cost: *architectural* efficiency and *post-hoc* compression are substitutes with different convexity. Compact architectures reduce FLOPs and weights jointly; post-hoc quantisation reduces weights without touching FLOPs. Applying both to a model that was already designed for efficiency frequently yields negative returns after accounting for calibration and retraining labour.

*Lineage 3 — the attention line.* Vaswani et al. (2017) removes recurrence to buy parallelism and pays with $\mathcal O(N^2D)$; ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020) transplants that cost into vision, where $N = HW/P^2$ makes the exponent land on *resolution* [PAGE 54, PAGE 60]; Swin (Liu et al., 2021) buys back linearity through shifted windows and patch merging [PAGE 20]; MAE (He et al., 2022) buys it back through 75 % masking plus an asymmetric encoder–decoder split [PAGE 13, PAGE 24]; Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) buys it back through selective state spaces [PAGE 14]. Chapter IX's techniques arrive *after* this tree has already spent its complexity budget. That ordering is the whole lesson: compression is a remedy applied to a bill you already ran up.

*Lineage 4 — hardware-aware deployment.* The framing sentence "application-aware and hardware-aware optimization problem rather than a universal one-size-fits-all solution" [PAGE 214] inherits the roofline/memory-wall tradition. The relevant physics: moving a value from off-chip memory costs on the order of $10^2$–$10^3\times$ the energy of the arithmetic that consumes it, so weight-only quantisation is fundamentally a *bandwidth* intervention, not a compute intervention.

*Multi-week traps (recurrent, dated by when they bite).*
- **Week 1–2, the MAC fallacy.** Reporting "50 % fewer parameters" as "50 % faster." Eq. (1)–(3) count weights; Eq. (4) counts MACs; neither counts bytes. Depthwise-separable blocks and small-kernel convs routinely show FLOPs reductions of 8–10× with wall-clock speedups under 2×.
- **Week 2–3, the calibration-set fiction.** PTQ's $x_{\max}, x_{\min}$ estimated on a "small representative dataset" [PAGE 228] that is drawn from the same distribution as the test set. The reported $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ then measures interpolation, not deployment. This is the same distribution-shift error Chapter IV names for meta-learning — a prior that "helps the typical case and quietly harms the rare one, which in engineering is often the case that matters most" [PAGE 105–106] — and the same error Chapter V names for synthetic data, where "performance gains may result from memorization, leakage, or biased synthetic patterns" [PAGE 130].
- **Week 3–4, the CR/speedup conflation.** Reporting $\mathrm{CR}$ from Eq. (8) as though it were $\mathrm{Speedup}$. They coincide only in the memory-bound regime with perfectly weight-bandwidth-limited execution.
- **Week 4–6, the teacher-selection trap.** Distilling from a teacher whose inductive bias the student cannot represent. Chapter IV's ANIL result [PAGE 104] predicts the failure mode: what transfers is a feature representation, so if the student's capacity cannot host that representation, $\mathcal L_{\text{KD}}$ is minimised by degenerate copying and $\mathcal L_{\text{CE}}$ dominates, collapsing the exercise into ordinary training with extra steps.
- **Week 5–7, the metric substitution trap.** Optimising FID/BLEU/accuracy on the benchmark while the deployment objective is calibration, tail-risk, or physical admissibility. Chapter V's warning applies verbatim: "a synthetic image may achieve a favorable FID score while still containing anatomically implausible or diagnostically misleading structures" [PAGE 130].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
*What a compressed-deadline team actually does.* Under a deployment deadline, three regressions are near-deterministic, and all three are visible in the incentive structure the volume itself documents.

**Regression 1 — precision uniformity.** The team quantises every tensor to the same $b$. Justification offered: "INT8 is standard." Mechanism: Eq. (8) is applied with one global range per tensor, and softmax exponentials, LayerNorm scales, and attention logits — the outlier-heavy tensors — are folded in with the rest. Predictable outcome: a large $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ attributed to "the model being too big," triggering a fallback to FP16 and a wasted cycle.

**Regression 2 — compression ratio as the objective function.** $\mathrm{CR}$ is dimensionless, monotone, and easy to graph; $\mathrm{Speedup}$ requires controlling batch, sequence length, and thread affinity, and is embarrassing to measure. Teams therefore optimise the measurable proxy. This is the identical pathology Chapter II documents when it admits that "the computational cost also increases significantly with the increase in accuracy" [PAGE 51] and then reports cost as an ordinal adjective — an incentive to keep it ordinal.

**Regression 3 — distillation as reflex.** Distillation is the most conceptually familiar lever in Chapter IX and the one with the most named variants ("response-based, feature-based, and relation-based distillation" [PAGE 233]), so it is reached for first, even when PTQ would have cleared the memory budget in an afternoon with no training run.

*Counter-trap protocol.* Three inversions, each cheap and each falsifiable.
1. **Per-layer sensitivity sweep before choosing $b$.** Quantise one layer at a time to INT4, measure $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$, rank. This converts the uniform-$b$ prior into a measured sensitivity spectrum and identifies whether the network is outlier-limited (attention/normalisation) or range-limited (conv weights).
2. **Roofline before compression.** Compute $\mathcal I$ for each block; if the model is memory-bound, weight quantisation is the correct lever and FLOPs reduction is not; if compute-bound, the reverse. This single measurement kills Regressions 1 and 2 simultaneously because it makes the *mechanism* of the speedup explicit.
3. **Ablation ladder, cheapest-first.** Baseline → PTQ INT8 → PTQ INT8 with per-channel $S$ → QAT INT8 → PTQ INT4 → QAT INT4 → distillation → architecture change. Each rung must justify the previous rung's residual $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$. QAT's cost — "higher training cost, access to training data, additional retraining time, and careful hyperparameter selection" [PAGE 231] — is only amortised if the deployment genuinely needs the accuracy the rung buys.

*The clock, and why it is a variable and not a constant.* "Real-time" is not one constraint. Chapter IX names the regimes explicitly: "wearable health monitoring, seizure detection, and portable diagnostic systems" [PAGE 214]; "real-time quality control, predictive maintenance, and anomaly detection in production environments" [PAGE 214]; and autonomous perception, where "perception models must process camera and lidar data with very low latency" [PAGE 214]. These are separated by roughly six orders of magnitude in tolerable latency, and the compression strategy that is correct at one end is indefensible at the other: a seizure detector can afford QAT and an offline-validated INT8 checkpoint; a lidar perception stack cannot afford either the training cycle or the tail-latency variance that INT4 kernels introduce on some accelerators.

*The reflexive loop.* The deepest regression is that the team's *evaluation harness* is itself an uncompressed model of the deployment. If the harness measures only benchmark accuracy, then every compression decision is optimised against benchmark accuracy, and the resulting system is optimal for the benchmark. Chapter IV names the structural version of this failure without qualification: distribution-free guarantees "frequently exceed[] one, which is to say it guarantees an error rate of at most something larger than one hundred percent, a statement that is true and useless," and the engineer should "treat them as evidence of soundness, not as a number to enter into a safety case" [PAGE 105]. The compression analogue is exact: a $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ measured on the calibration distribution is sound evidence that the pipeline ran, and no evidence at all about the deployment distribution.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A veto is a constraint that no accuracy gain can purchase. Five are provable from the supplied material.

**V1 — Rate–distortion veto (information-theoretic).** *Claim:* for a fixed admissible distortion budget $\varepsilon$, there exists $b_{\min}$ below which no calibration scheme is admissible. *Proof sketch:* from Eq. (8), $S = (x_{\max}-x_{\min})/(2^b-1)$; from Eq. (11), $\tfrac{S}{2} \ge |e_q|_{\max}/1$ is forced; under the high-resolution model $\mathbb E[e_q^2] = S^2/12 = (x_{\max}-x_{\min})^2/\bigl[12(2^b-1)^2\bigr]$. Setting $\mathbb E[e_q^2] \le \varepsilon^2$ gives

$$b \;\ge\; \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{x_{\max}-x_{\min}}{2\sqrt{3}\,\varepsilon}\right).$$

If the accuracy requirement maps to an $\varepsilon$ below this floor, INT4 is not "risky," it is *infeasible*, and the correct engineering action is to change the dynamic range (per-channel $S$, outlier isolation) or abandon the bit-width — not to retrain harder. Note that $\varepsilon$ here is a per-tensor local error, and the end-to-end error is *not* the sum of local errors (see V5).

**V2 — Roofline / memory-bandwidth veto.** *Claim:* in the memory-bound regime, FLOPs-reducing compression yields no latency benefit. *Proof:* let $B$ be the effective byte bandwidth (bytes/s), $W$ the weight bytes, $P$ the sustained peak MAC rate (MAC/s). Latency $t \approx \max(W/B,\ \mathrm{FLOPs}/(2P))$. If $W/B$ dominates, $\mathrm{Speedup} = t_{\text{orig}}/t_{\text{comp}} \to (W_{\text{orig}}/B)/(W_{\text{comp}}/B) = \mathrm{CR}_{\text{weights}}$, *independent of FLOPs*. Corollary: pruning that reduces non-zeros without reducing *stored* bytes (unstructured sparsity in a dense kernel) gives $\mathrm{CR}_{\text{weights}} = 1$ and therefore $\mathrm{Speedup} = 1$ exactly. This is the provable form of "the computational cost also increases significantly with the increase in accuracy" [PAGE 51] being the wrong thing to measure.

**V3 — Thermodynamic/energy-budget veto.** *Claim:* an edge device with power ceiling $\Pi$ (watts) cannot sustain throughput above $\Pi/\epsilon_{\text{op}}$ operations per second, where $\epsilon_{\text{op}}$ is energy per operation (joules). Since off-chip DRAM access energy exceeds low-precision MAC energy by two to three orders of magnitude, $\epsilon_{\text{op}}$ is dominated by *traffic*, not arithmetic, which re-derives V2 from the energy side. Consequence: for battery-powered IoT and TinyML nodes — the chapter's own motivating case, where "devices operate with limited memory, low power budgets, and real-time processing requirements" [PAGE 214] — the feasible set is defined by a joule budget, and no value of $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ can enlarge it.

**V4 — Physics-compliance veto.** *This is the veto unique to this volume.* Chapter I establishes that "for a machine learning model to be fully reliable in engineering, it must not contradict the laws of nature" [PAGE 13], that only PINNs embed "Navier-Stokes formulations or other highly non-linear partial differential equations (PDEs) directly into the objective function graph via automatic differentiation, establishing a framework with absolute physical compliance" [PAGE 26], and that the rest "remain prone to generating unphysical or structurally nonsensical anomalies when evaluated on out-of-distribution edge cases" [PAGE 25–26]. Now compose this with Chapter IX. Quantisation error $e_q$ is an *unstructured, input-independent* perturbation of the parameters. For a purely statistical approximator it perturbs the output; for a physics-constrained model it perturbs the *residual*, because the physics loss is computed on network derivatives and the chain rule propagates the parameter perturbation into every derivative order. Therefore: **aggressive quantisation of a non-PINN surrogate model does not merely reduce accuracy, it can move the prediction across a conservation-law boundary, converting a small error into an inadmissible design.** In domains where Chapter I says admissibility is mandatory — "aerodynamic wing surfaces or thermal cooling conduits" [PAGE 23] — the compression feasible set is bounded by *physical admissibility*, not by $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$. Veto: any compression plan whose acceptance criterion is a scalar accuracy delta, applied to a non-PINN surrogate in a safety-relevant physical workflow.

**V5 — Nonlinear error-propagation veto.** *Claim:* per-layer worst-case bounds do not compose additively, so a plan validated by per-layer $|e_q|_{\max}$ is not validated end-to-end. *Proof sketch:* let $x^{(l+1)} = \sigma(W^{(l)}x^{(l)})$ with perturbation $\delta W^{(l)}$, $|\delta W^{(l)}_{ij}| \le S_l/2$. Then $\delta x^{(l+1)} = \sigma'(\cdot)\bigl(\delta W^{(l)}x^{(l)} + W^{(l)}\delta x^{(l)}\bigr)$, i.e. the error propagates through a product of Jacobians $\prod_l \|W^{(l)}\|\cdot|\sigma'|$. The bound is multiplicative in spectral norms, not additive, and normalisation layers rescale it non-uniformly per channel. Hence INT4's 17× local worst-case inflation relative to INT8 (derived in the operator section) is a *lower* bound on the end-to-end inflation.

**V6 — Discretisation–quantisation coupling veto.** *Claim:* in selective-SSM models, quantising the discretisation operator is categorically different from quantising weights. *Proof sketch:* Chapter I states that Mamba's continuous-time SSMs are "converted using mathematical discretization steps" [PAGE 21] and that the selection mechanism makes "internal routing parameters dynamic functions of the current input token" [PAGE 21]. The discretised recurrence is $h_t = \bar A\,h_{t-1} + \bar B\,x_t$ with $\bar A = \exp(\Delta A)$; the effective memory horizon scales as $1/(1-\|\bar A\|)$. A quantisation perturbation $\delta\Delta$ therefore perturbs the *horizon*, not just an amplitude — and the perturbation compounds geometrically over $t$. Veto: any uniform-$b$ quantisation of $\Delta, A, B$ validated only on short-context metrics.

**V7 — Cross-modal alignment veto.** *Claim:* per-modality compression tolerances do not compose into system tolerance. Chapter VIII lists "Modality Gap: Information across modalities is not properly aligned → Misinterpretation of engineering conditions" as a first-class failure mode [PAGE 203] and requires "Timestamp alignment, metadata enrichment" as mitigation [PAGE 205]. If modality A is compressed with latency $\tau_A$ and modality B with $\tau_B$, and fusion requires temporal correspondence within $\delta$, then admissibility requires $|\tau_A - \tau_B| \le \delta$ — a constraint on the *difference*, which independent per-modality optimisation does not control. Veto: any plan that compresses modalities separately and validates each in isolation.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Veto 1 — Uniform INT4 post-training quantisation of a ViT / CNN-Transformer hybrid.**
*Scenario:* a ViT-class classifier from the Ch. III family ( DeiT- or Swin-style, $N=196$ tokens at $224\times224$ with $16\times16$ patches [PAGE 60]) is quantised to INT4 by PTQ with a single per-tensor scale, using the "limited calibration data" protocol of Ch. IX §3.1.1 [PAGE 229].
*Veto basis:* V1 + V5 + the outlier argument (softmax exponentials and LayerNorm are heavy-tailed; Eq. (8) assumes a bounded, roughly uniform range).
*Prediction:* top-1 accuracy drop exceeding 5 percentage points, with the drop concentrated in the final encoder blocks and in the classification head; INT8 PTQ on the same checkpoint stays within ~1 point; QAT recovers most of the INT8-level behaviour but not all, because STE bias is non-negligible at 17× the local error bound. Inference speedup will be *lower* than the 4× that $\mathrm{CR}$ suggests, because attention is not weight-bandwidth-bound.
*Falsification:* a sub-1-point INT4 PTQ drop on this architecture family with a single global scale per tensor. If observed, my outlier premise about this checkpoint is wrong and V1 is not binding at $b=4$ for this dynamic range.

**Veto 2 — Compressing an already-linear-attention model and crediting compression for the win.**
*Scenario:* a Swin- or Mamba-based pipeline — chosen precisely because it is $\mathcal O(N)$ [PAGE 60, PAGE 14] — is then quantised to INT4 and the resulting speedup is reported as a compression contribution.
*Veto basis:* V2. The dominant cost in an already-hierarchical or state-space model at long context is state and activation traffic, not weight storage; weight-only INT4 attacks the smaller term.
*Prediction:* measured $\mathrm{Speedup} < \mathrm{CR}$, possibly $\mathrm{Speedup} \approx 1$ at large batch/long sequence and only approaching $\mathrm{CR}$ at batch 1 with a weight-bandwidth-limited kernel. Additionally, for the Mamba variant, V6 predicts disproportionate degradation on long-range dependency probes while short-context metrics remain flat — i.e. an *average* metric will hide the regression.
*Falsification:* $\mathrm{Speedup} \approx \mathrm{CR}$ at production batch size, together with flat long-context degradation.

**Veto 3 — Distilling a foundation-model teacher into a lightweight edge student for a task with an admissibility constraint.**
*Scenario:* an LLM/MLLM teacher (the Ch. VIII stack, with RAG grounding [PAGE 192–193]) is distilled into a sub-10 M-parameter student for on-device use, following Ch. IX §3.2, for a task where outputs must be physically admissible or safety-bounded.
*Veto basis:* V4 + capacity mismatch. Eq. (18)–(19) transfer a soft output distribution; they do not transfer the retrieval grounding, the tool calls, the MCP-mediated access to authoritative sources [PAGE 195–196], or the verifier agents that Ch. VIII identifies as the mechanism by which "verifier agents prevent wrong conclusions and hallucinations from occurring" [PAGE 195]. The student inherits fluency without the grounding apparatus.
*Prediction:* the student matches the teacher on in-distribution benchmark accuracy while exhibiting a *higher* hallucination rate on out-of-distribution queries, and the gap widens as $T$ increases (because high $T$ flattens the distribution toward uniform, transferring less discriminative structure per Eq. 17 while the $T^2$ factor keeps gradient scale large). Calibration (ECE) will degrade even where top-1 holds.
*Falsification:* equal hallucination rate at matched in-distribution accuracy, with a RAG retrieval layer retained at inference. If observed, the grounding — not the parameter count — was doing the work, which is my claim, and the veto should be restated as "distillation must not remove grounding," which is weaker than stated.

**Veto 4 — Compressing a PINN by uniform low-bit quantisation.**
*Scenario:* a PINN of the Ch. I family — "a standard multi-layer perceptron predicts physical target states (such as velocity, pressure, or temperature) based on coordinate inputs (x, y, z, t)" and "leverages automatic differentiation (AD) to compute exact partial derivatives across collocation points" [PAGE 23] — is INT4-quantised for edge deployment.
*Veto basis:* V4 + V5, sharpened. The residual is built from network derivatives; quantisation perturbs those derivatives multiplicatively through the Jacobian product; the loss landscape of PINNs is already known to be stiff, and the chapter's own claim is that convergence comes from "governing differential equations directly regulariz[ing] the network's weight parameters" [PAGE 25] — a mechanism that a perturbed parameter set can silently disable while leaving point predictions superficially plausible.
*Prediction:* solution error remains small at collocation points interior to the training domain but grows sharply near boundaries and in high-gradient regions, with visible violations of the conserved quantity (mass/momentum/energy imbalance) at a rate far exceeding the point-wise error. The failure will not appear in an $L^2$ interior metric.
*Falsification:* conserved-quantity residual scaling proportionally with point-wise error under $b: 32\to 4$. If observed, the derivative-amplification mechanism is weaker than I claim for that architecture.

**Veto 5 — Accepting an ordinal "Very High cost / Low real-time suitability" label as a deployment clearance.**
*Scenario:* a team adopts the Table 3 entries for BiKAN-ViT or PINN-SEM [PAGE 51] as sufficient cost characterisation for a procurement or safety decision.
*Veto basis:* non-falsifiability. The cells carry no FLOPs (Eq. 4), no bytes (Eqs. 1–3), no latency in seconds, no $\mathcal I$, no hardware, no batch, no $\Delta\mathrm{Acc}$ with an interval. Ch. IX's own conclusion is that the choice "depends on the application domain, target hardware, memory capacity, latency requirements, energy constraints, and acceptable accuracy loss" [PAGE 214] — none of which an ordinal adjective encodes.
*Prediction:* any attempt to convert those cells into an infrastructure plan will require a full re-measurement, typically 2–6 engineering weeks, and the re-measured latency will differ from the ordinal-implied latency by more than an order of magnitude in at least one regime (batch-1 edge vs. batched server).
*Falsification:* a procurement decision that closes successfully on ordinal labels alone. This has not been observed in the material supplied, and Ch.
