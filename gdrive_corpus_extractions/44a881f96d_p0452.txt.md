To analyze Tolman's assertion that "all gestalts are for us sign-gestalts—and all relations, in the last analysis, means-end-relations," we must execute a deep multi-hop derivation bridging 1932 behaviorism with modern predictive information theory and control landscapes.

**Hop 1: Deconstructing the Sign-Gestalt.**
Tolman is arguing against a mechanistic or purely reflexive interpretation of perception. The "blemish" he addresses from a pure Gestalt perspective is the introduction of *purpose*. While Köhler or Koffka might focus on static perceptual organization (figure-ground), Tolman insists this organization is functional. A perceptual field is only organized because it guides action toward a specific outcome. Therefore, spatial relations ("up and down") are not primitive geometric facts but "means-end-strands." They are cues (signs) pointing to rewards or punishments (goals).

**Hop 2: Information-Theoretic Translation.**
How do we formalize a "means-end-strand"? In modern terms, an organism maintains a latent variable representing its goal state, $G$. The environment provides sensory inputs, $S$, which are processed into features $F$ (the "gestalt"). The mapping from $F$ to $G$ constitutes the "sign-value." Mathematically, this is a conditional probability distribution $P(G|F)$ or, more dynamically, an influence on a policy $\pi(A|F)$. Tolman’s "purposive structure" is essentially a learned reward function $R(s,a)$ embedded within the topology of the state space.

**Hop 3: The Retention Problem and Zeigarnik Connection.**
The source text explicitly footnotes Bluma Zeigarnik’s work on "erledigten und unerledigten Handlungen" (completed and uncompleted actions) [PAGE 421]. This is crucial. Why does an "uncompleted" gestalt persist as a "means-end" structure? Because the goal vector has not been zeroed out. In cognitive science, this is the "intentional arc"—a motor plan held in working memory that biases future perception. Tolman uses this to prove his system is a "thorough-going purposivism": the cognitive map is not a passive map but an active script.

**Hop 4: Gray-Zone Audit and the Breakdown Regime.**
Where does Tolman's model fail? It relies on the assumption that *all* relations can be reduced to instrumental value. The "gray zone" arises when an agent encounters novel stimuli that have no immediate or even distal connection to reinforcement—pure aesthetic appreciation or curiosity without a clear teleological end. If every "left and right" is a "means-end affair," how does the system handle exploratory noise or stochasticity that serves no obvious purpose? We will define the breakdown regime via an equation where the signal-to-noise ratio of the "means-end" cue drops below a critical threshold, causing the intentional arc to collapse into random exploration rather than directed purposive behavior.

**Conclusion of Trace:**
Tolman effectively anticipated Reinforcement Learning (RL) by decades. His "cognitive map" is a world model optimized to minimize prediction error regarding reward locations. The "means-end-strand" is the gradient of value flow through the cognitive graph.

\Psi_{ME}(\phi(s)) = \nabla_{\phi} \mathbb{E}_{\pi} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t R(s_t, a_t) \mid s_0 = s \right]
\]
where $R$ is the reward function (the "end"), $\gamma$ is a discount factor, and the expectation is taken over the policy $\pi$ derived from the cognitive map.

**Units & Dimensions:**
- $V$: Utility / Expected Return (arbitrary behavioral units, e.g., probability of reinforcement).
- $\nabla_{\phi}$: Gradient with respect to the feature space (units: Utility per unit change in sensory grouping).

\[
I_{t+1} = \alpha \cdot I_t + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\{G \notin \text{Observed}\}}
\]
Here, $\alpha \in (0,1)$ is the decay constant of working memory, and $\mathbb{1}$ is an indicator function equal to 1 if the goal $G$ has not yet been achieved. Tolman argues this residual $I_t$ warps the "contours" of the next gestalt, making certain cues more salient.

**Assumptions:**
- **Markovian Purposiveness:** The current sensory gestalt $\phi(s_t)$ contains all necessary history required to determine the optimal means-end strand.
- **Global Optimization:** The organism acts to maximize the cumulative means-end value, implying a globally consistent cognitive map.

This is the singularity of *extinction*: the cognitive map fails to guide purposive behavior, reverting the organism to stimulus-bound, non-purposive trial-and-error.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 thesis sits at a precise junction of scientific lineage, acting as a retrocausal node that redefines earlier psychological paradigms while prefiguring 21st-century artificial intelligence.

**The Immediate Predecessors:**
1. **Edward Thorndike (Connectionism):** Tolman directly opposes Thorndike's law of effect, which suggested learning was a blind strengthening of stimulus-response bonds. Tolman introduced the "intervening variables" (the cognitive map), arguing that animals learn the layout of the maze (the *space*), not just the turns.
2. **Wolfgang Köhler & Max Wertheimer (Gestalt Psychology):** As noted in the source [PAGE 421], Tolman tries to reconcile his purposivism with Gestalt principles. Gestalt psychology provided the mechanism for how disparate stimuli group together (proximity, similarity). Tolman co-opted this, arguing these groupings are not just perceptual illusions but semantic networks of "signs."

**The Multigenerational Trap:**
For nearly 50 years, Tolman’s cognitive revolution was eclipsed by B.F. Skinner’s radical behaviorism, which aggressively rejected any internal mental states ("intervening variables"). Skinner argued that "purpose" was merely a retroactive explanation of conditioned responses. This created a massive "multigenerational trap" in cognitive science: researchers spent decades artificially separating *perception* (Gestalt) from *action/value* (Operant conditioning), treating them as distinct modules.

**Modern Retrocausality:**
Today, the rise of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Model-Based RL has validated Tolman almost exactly. Modern AI agents (like those navigating Minecraft or complex 3D environments) do not rely on simple S-R mappings; they build internal "world models" (cognitive maps) and use them to simulate futures. Tolman’s "means-end-strands" are mathematically identical to the value gradients traversed by modern RL algorithms (e.g., Q-learning or Policy Gradients). The "Zeigarnik effect" cited by Tolman is recognized today in neural correlates of prospective coding—the brain holding motor plans open until a target is reached.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
When analyzing Tolman’s claim that "Types of organization of the environmental field are... threaded upon, means-end-strands," we can construct a recursive Theory of Mind (ToM) regarding the cognitive load during crisis versus strategic counter-trapping.

**Level 0: The Mechanistic Baseline.**
An organism operating without a "sign-gestalt" sees raw pixels or isolated sensory events. There is no ToM here, only reactive physics.

**Level 1: Tolman’s Basic Agent (Self-Model).**
The agent perceives itself as an entity moving through a "means-end" landscape. "Up" means "closer to food." This is a first-order ToM: "I want X, and Y leads to X." The cognitive map is a static repository of spatial affordances.

**Level 2: Crisis Habit Regression.**
When an animal (or human) is placed under extreme stress or a sudden environmental shock (e.g., the maze walls suddenly move or the reward is removed), the high-level "sign-gestalt" processing collapses. The sophisticated means-end reasoning is too computationally expensive and fragile under high uncertainty. The organism regresses to Level 0 or early habit-based routines (stereotypies, wall-following). Tolman observed this: rats with rich cognitive maps, when faced with completely novel contradictory cues, often defaulted to rigid spatial habits. The "purpose" is lost in the panic of re-mapping.

**Level 3: Counter-Trap and Clock Awareness.**
A higher-order agent (an experimenter observing the rat, or a sophisticated predator) possesses a second-order ToM: "The rat expects the cheese at location A because of its means-end strand; therefore, I will place a trap at A."
However, Tolman introduces a crucial temporal element implied by his citation of Zeigarnik [PAGE 421]: the "clock" of intention. An intelligent agent doesn't just know the spatial map; it knows the *duration* of the trap's effectiveness.
If the rat realizes the cheese is gone (the sign-gestalt is violated), it must enter a deliberative state. The "within-variables" (internal anxiety, recalculating paths) take over. The counter-trap requires predicting the trap-setter's mind: "They know I know about the cheese, so they will hide the new reward at B." This recursive loop creates a dynamic tension where the "environmental field" is no longer just physical geometry, but a constantly shifting topological representation of other agents' intentions.

**The Tension:**
The core tension lies in the fragility of the "means-end" thread. In a crisis, the thread snaps (regression). In a strategic game, the thread is actively manipulated by multiple minds (counter-trap). Tolman’s framework brilliantly highlights that *purpose* is the glue, but also the single point of failure, in adaptive cognition.

The "sign-gestalt" cannot be instantiated. Without the visual cues to thread the "means-end-strand," purposive navigation halts. The bird circles aimlessly. The external physics layer issues a hard veto on the execution of the cognitive plan.

**Proof of Cross-Layer Dominance:**
If Tolman’s thesis were absolute—that "all relations... are means-end affairs"—then the cognitive map should always dictate behavior. But we observe that under extreme biomechanical strain or environmental occlusion, the map becomes useless. The hierarchy of veto is strict:
`Biomechanical Viability > Sensory Availability > Cognitive Mapping`
