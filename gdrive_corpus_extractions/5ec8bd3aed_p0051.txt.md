1. Finite-additivity-only on P: if \mu(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i) \neq \sum_i \mu(A_i) for some countable disjoint P-family, then \bar{\mu} is not countably additive on R; continuity from below fails.
2. Non-ring P: if P is only a semi-ring and lacks the finite disjoint-refinement property, \bar{\mu}(E) is undefined by the sum formula; one must use \mu^*(E) = \inf\{\sum_i \mu(A_i) : E \subset \bigcup_i A_i, A_i \in P\}.
3. Non-\sigma-finite \mu: uniqueness of extension from R to \sigma(R) may fail; distinct countably additive extensions can exist.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage traces from Jordan content (finitely additive, not countably additive on \mathbb{R}) through Borel and Lebesgue's countably additive measures on \mathbb{R}, to Carathéodory's 1918 outer-measure extension theorem, which abstracts precisely the [PAGE 36] move from P to R via decomposition into P-pieces. Hahn and Banach later showed that finitely additive measures (Banach limits, ultrafilter measures) exist on richer algebras where countable additivity fails, creating the fork between Jordan-style content and Lebesgue-style measure. Kolmogorov's 1933 axiomatization made countable additivity a foundational probability axiom, and Halmos's 1950 *Measure Theory* systematizes the extension-from-algebra theorem that [PAGE 36] proves. Theorem D and Theorem E cited on [PAGE 36] are earlier results in Halmos's text establishing the finite-additive extension and its notation; exercises (1)-(3) on [PAGE 36] request an alternative proof of Theorem D avoiding Theorems A-C via transfinite induction ordered by left endpoints, and introduce a finite increasing continuous function g as a setup for integration by substitution.

Multi-week traps for learners and formalizers: (a) regressing to finite-additivity intuition and assuming countable sums behave like finite sums at limit steps; (b) accepting uniqueness of extension without checking \sigma-finiteness, which fails for non-\sigma-finite base measures; (c) accepting Halmos's notation abuse "without any possibility of confusion" as a domain identity rather than a coercion, which breaks type-checking in Lean/Coq/Isabelle when E \in R \setminus P; (d) substituting transfinite induction (exercise (2) on [PAGE 36]) for the elementary refinement argument without recognizing that transfinite induction is overkill here and obscures the finite-additivity core.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: when a reader stalls on the countable-additivity step on [PAGE 36], the mind habitually collapses the countable sequence \{E_i\} into a finite union and re-applies finite additivity, effectively pretending \mathbb{N} is bounded. This regress treats R as if it were closed under countable unions of P-sets rather than merely finite unions, skipping the verification that \sum_{i,j} \mu(A_{ij}) converges and equals \bar{\mu}(E). Counter-trap: enforce the epsilon-2^{-n} or monotone-class discipline; explicitly separate E \in P (direct countable additivity of \mu) from E \in R \setminus P (finite P-decomposition plus piecewise additivity); never let the symbol \mu on [PAGE 36] silently change domain from P to R without inserting the coercion \bar{\mu}. Clock: under deadline pressure (problem set due, formalization sprint, exam), the reader tends to quote Halmos's phrase "as we may without any possibility of confusion" and skip proving \bar{\mu} is well-defined, thereby embedding a latent domain error that surfaces only when the proof assistant rejects the coercion or when a non-\sigma-finite counterexample invalidates uniqueness.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: the extension theorem assumes a mathematical sigma-algebra of measurable sets. Physical sensors have finite resolution \delta x and noise floor \eta, so only sets measurable at scale \delta x are operationally accessible; subsets of null sets (Vitali-type constructions requiring the axiom of choice) are physically unrealizable and veto the application of \bar{\mu} to arbitrary subsets of R. Biomechanical/weather layer: atmospheric and biomechanical observables often admit invariant measures (e.g., SRB measures for chaotic flows) that are singular with respect to Lebesgue measure on the ambient phase space; if P consists of Lebesgue-boxes and \mu is Lebesgue measure, the physical attractor may have \mu(A)=0 yet carry all observable probability, vetoing any extension that assigns zero to the physically relevant set. Cross-layer veto proof: \bar{\mu} is valid only if (i) the sigma-algebra is complete (null-set subsets included), (ii) \mu is \sigma-finite on P (for uniqueness to \sigma(R)), and (iii) the physical observable is absolutely continuous with respect to \bar{\mu}; violation of any layer voids the measure-theoretic prediction for that observable.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Construct a finitely additive extension \nu on R via a Banach limit assigning \nu([n,\infty)) = c > 0 for every n while \nu(\varnothing)=0. Veto condition: countable additivity requires \lim_{n\to\infty} \nu([n,\infty)) = \nu(\cap_n [n,\infty)) = \nu(\varnothing)=0. Prediction: verification fails because \lim c = c \neq 0; the RLVR reward signal is negative and the policy must switch from Banach-limit-style finite additivity to countably additive Lebesgue extension.
