Hull then takes up Thorndike's "law of effect" and argues that it breaks down in the Pavlovian conditioned-reflex experiment. He states that the dog's salivary reaction "can by no stretch of the imagination be regarded as the cause of the receipt of food." Instead, food is a common cause: it produces salivation first and, later, the reduction of hunger that is commonly considered the reinforcing agent. The reinforcing state is therefore temporally related to the response but not produced by it. A second exception is the conditioned kneejerk, in which termination of the receptor discharge from the blow on the patellar tendon occurs because of the brief duration of the hammer impact, "rather than because of the occurrence of the kneejerk."

Despite these exceptions, Hull treats Thorndike's generalization as "a very penetrating bit of scientific insight into the dynamics of adaptive situations in general" but prefers the expression "law of reinforcement." He then identifies the central unresolved problem of the page: in Figures 13, 14, and 16, both need reduction and receptor-response reduction follow as consequences of the act involved in the reinforcement process. The question is which is the critical reinforcing agent. Hull's epistemic status is explicit and verbatim: "The writer is aware of no critical evidence of this kind. Until such becomes available the issue must remain uncertain." He provisionally elects "reduction in drivereceptor response" as the more probable alternative, adding that an error in either direction would not have far-reaching systematic implications at this stage of ignorance.

Gray-zone audit: The page is explicitly underdetermined. Hull acknowledges the lack of a radical experiment that eliminates one candidate while holding the other constant. The shock-circuit example demonstrates that reinforcement can be delivered by a physical interruption independent of the subject's response, which empties a purely response-contingent reading of the law of effect. The Pavlovian case displays a common-cause structure: food causes both salivation and hunger reduction, so \(P(\text{reinforcement} \mid R)\) may be high even though \(R\) has no causal efficacy over reinforcement. The kneejerk case shows that receptor discharge can end because of stimulus physics (impact duration), not because of the motor response. Taken together, these observations imply that the reinforcement variable may be indexed to environmental and receptor physics rather than to the subject's behavior. The unresolved choice between need-reduction and receptor-reduction constitutes a systematic trap: an applied protocol that targets need reduction while the operative signal is actually receptor-impulse extinction will mis-specify the contingency and will show drift, relapse, or extinction over repeated trials.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let \(S(t)\) denote the stimulus situation, \(R(t)\) the emitted response, \(D(t)\) drive intensity in Hull's drive units (D), \(N(t)\) need level, \(s_u(t)\) receptor-impulse frequency in impulses per second (imp/s, Hz), and \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}}(t)\) the reinforcing state.

Contingency operator:
\[
\Delta P_{\text{cont}} = P\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}} \mid R, S\bigr) - P\bigl(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}} \mid S\bigr)
\]
If \(\Delta P_{\text{cont}} \simeq 0\), the reinforcing state is not response-contingent.

Common-cause decomposition (Pavlovian paradox, [PAGE 90]):
\[
F \longrightarrow \text{Sal}(t), \qquad F \longrightarrow \Delta N(t+\Delta t), \qquad \text{Sal} \nrightarrow \Delta N
\]
where \(F\) is food presentation. Salivation is not a cause of hunger reduction; food is the common cause.

Temporal contiguity kernel:
\[
\kappa(\Delta t) = \mathbb{I}\!\left(|\Delta t| \le \tau_c\right), \quad \tau_c \sim \mathcal{O}(1\text{--}10\,\text{s})
\]
Breakdown/singularity regime: if \(|\Delta t| > \tau_c\), the contiguity requirement fails and the association does not form.

Hull's provisional drive-receptor signal ([PAGE 90]):
\[
\delta_{\text{dr}} = s_u(t_{\text{pre}}) - s_u(t_{\text{post}}) \quad [\text{imp/s}]
\]

Information measure between response and reinforcement:
\[
I(R; \mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}} \mid S) \quad [\text{bits}]
\]

Singularity condition: When the reinforcing state is generated by circuit interruption independent of \(R\) (shock experiment) or by common cause \(F\) (salivation), \(P(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}} \mid R)\) is non-identifiable from \(P(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}} \mid S)\); the response-contingency parameters are singular and cannot be estimated from observational contingency alone.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The theoretical lineage on [PAGE 90] runs from Thorndike's law of effect (1911) through Pavlovian conditioned-reflex physiology to Hull's 1943 Principles of Behavior. Hull explicitly cites the conditioned kneejerk as reference (10) and invokes Finan (4) regarding the intensity of the need involved in reinforcement. The page marks a historical pivot: Hull retains Thorndike's inductive insight but renames it "law of reinforcement" because the strict causal reading of the law of effect fails under common-cause and non-contingent termination structures. Multi-week traps emerge from this pivot. Because Hull admits he lacks critical evidence to decide between need-reduction and receptor-reduction as the operative agent, a multi-week behavior modification program cannot know whether to schedule reinforcement according to homeostatic drive cycles (need, \(N(t)\), fluctuating across feeding and circadian states) or according to stimulus-locked receptor patterns (\(s_u(t)\)). If a trainer reinforces according to need reduction while the subject's nervous system tracks receptor-impulse extinction, behavior will appear stable for days or weeks and then undergo sudden relapse when the internal drive state drifts outside the actual reinforcement window. This is a multi-week drift trap rooted in the page's admitted epistemic gap: the wrong layer of causation is being targeted, and the error only compounds across repeated trials.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: Under acute stress, hierarchical behavioral control collapses to lower, over-learned habits. If the subject's nervous system has been reinforced by receptor-impulse extinction rather than need reduction, crisis regression will re-instate behaviors historically paired with \(s_u\) termination, not behaviors paired with need alleviation. Counter-trap: The experimenter who believes need is the critical factor will design crisis interventions that manipulate food deprivation or drive state \(D(t)\); these will fail if the actual contingency was receptor-impulse termination. Clock: The temporal contiguity parameter \(\tau_c\) operates as a biological clock; reinforcement arriving outside \(|\Delta t| \le \tau_c\) fails to enter the habit hierarchy. Recursive modeling: The subject forms a model of the experimenter's contingency schedule; the experimenter forms a model of the subject's drive state \(D(t)\). When both models disagree about the critical reinforcing variable—need versus receptor-response—the dyad enters a recursive misalignment loop in which each party's prediction errors are attributed to the wrong layer (motivation versus contingency), producing persistent cycle of failed intervention and misread regression.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: The grid circuit interruption is a physical event that ends current and \(s_u\) independently of the animal's response. If the response does not open the circuit but shock still ends, response-necessity is vetoed; the reinforcement event is exogenous to the subject's behavior.

Biomechanical veto: The patellar hammer impact has a fixed mechanical duration; the kneejerk cannot alter the impact duration. Receptor discharge ends because the mechanical stimulus ends, not because of the motor response. Response-efficacy is vetoed by the physics of the impact.

Weather/environmental veto: Ambient temperature and caloric availability impose boundary conditions on \(D(t)\) and \(N(t)\). A cold environment can increase thermal drive independent of behavioral history, overriding learned reinforcement schedules. Internal-drive primacy is vetoed by external physical layer constraints.

Formal veto condition: \(V = 1\) iff there exists a physical layer \(L\) such that the termination time \(t_{\text{term}}\) of \(s_u\) or \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{inf}}\) is independent of \(R(t)\) for all \(t < t_{\text{term}}\). Under this condition, any model asserting that \(R\) causes reinforcement is structurally invalid.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 (Shock-circuit counterfactual): A rat on the shock grid receives shock termination via an automatic circuit breaker at fixed intervals, independent of bar pressing. Verifiable condition: record bar-press rate before, during, and after non-contingent shock termination. Prediction: The law of effect is vetoed; bar-press rate will not systematically increase relative to baseline because the response does not control the reinforcing event. Any observed variation will be superstitious and non-stationary across sessions.

Scenario 2 (Salivation-blockade counterfactual): A dog undergoes CS-food pairing, but salivation is pharmacologically blocked or the salivary duct is diverted while food still arrives after the CS. Verifiable condition: compare conditioned response magnitude to the CS with intact versus blocked salivation. Prediction: Conditioning persists because food is the common cause that produces both salivation and hunger reduction; salivation is epiphenomenal. The claim that salivation causes food receipt is vetoed.

Scenario 3 (Kneejerk-blockade counterfactual): A subject receives a patellar tendon hammer strike under spinal anesthesia that prevents the kneejerk extension while afferent receptor discharge from the muscle spindle is recorded. Verifiable condition: compare afferent discharge latency and duration with and without the motor response. Prediction: Receptor discharge terminates at the same post-impact latency regardless of whether the kneejerk occurs. Reinforcement tied to receptor termination is independent of the motor response; response-contingent reinforcement is vetoed.
