<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive-theory-of-mind layer has three competing modes. First, crisis habit regression: when the target is missed, a naive observer falls back to a low-order heuristic such as checking each player’s marginal frequency of actions. That heuristic is exactly the opening example on [PAGE 2], where separate long-run frequency checks identify the deviator. But the paper’s second example is a coupled one-dimensional random walk, and [PAGE 2] presents it as a genuinely different test. Second, counter-trap reasoning: the deviator knows the blame function and chooses a strategy to create ambiguity. The minimax proof on [PAGE 15] models exactly this: an adversary selects a player and a deviation strategy, nature generates a realization, and the statistician must guess the deviator after seeing only the cylinder. The statistician’s strategy is a blame function, and the adversary’s strategy is a unilateral deviation. The paper notes that a random blame function removes the constant \(2\) in Theorem 2.6 [PAGE 6]. Recursive theory of mind therefore says: the observer must reason about the deviator reasoning about the observer; the minimax value is the fixed point of that recursion.

Third, the clock: the proof is not a single-shot rule but a limiting construction over horizons. The truncated games \(\Gamma(D_n)\) are finite, the statistician has an optimal mixed strategy \(\xi_n\), and a diagonal argument extracts \(f\) [PAGE 17-18]. In the finite-horizon version, the failure probability goes to zero as \(N\to\infty\) [PAGE 14], and Proposition 3.8 says that for sufficiently large \(N\) the algorithm identifies the deviator with failure probability at most \(\varepsilon\) [PAGE 14]. The clock matters because delaying a deviation changes the test statistics: the random-walk tests use scales such as \(\sqrt{n\log\log n}\), \(\sqrt{n}(\log n)^{3/4}\), and \(n\log n\) [PAGE 9]. The observer’s recursive model must therefore include not only “who benefits from ambiguity?” but also “at what horizon does the ambiguity become statistically resolvable?” That is the counter-trap versus clock tension.
