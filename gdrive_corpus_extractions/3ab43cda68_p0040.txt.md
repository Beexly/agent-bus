Signal-detection formalization: neural evidence \(x\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,\sigma_0^2)\) on null trials and \(x\sim\mathcal N(\mu_1,\sigma_1^2)\) on signal trials. Sensitivity index \(d' = (\mu_1-\mu_0)/\sigma_0\) (dimensionless), criterion \(c\) (dimensionless), hit rate \(H=\Phi(d'/2-c)\), false-alarm rate \(F=\Phi(-d'/2-c)\), with probabilities dimensionless. Breakdown regime: mean spike count \(\lambda < 1\) places the system in a Poisson counting regime where the Gaussian \(d'\) approximation fails.

Information-theoretic cross-modal dependence:
\[
I(S;R) = \sum_{s,r} p(s,r)\log_2\frac{p(s,r)}{p(s)p(r)} \quad \mathrm{bits}.
\]
Breakdown: empty joint bins \(p(s,r)=0\) make the logarithm undefined.

Neural population response with saturation:
\[
r_i = g\!\left(\sum_j w_{ij}s_j + b_i\right),\qquad r_i\in[0,r_{\max}]\ \mathrm{spikes/s},
\]
where \(g\) is a saturating nonlinearity. James’s emergent-percept claim is formalized as a cortical operator \(C\) such that yellow \(E_y = C(s_R+s_G)\) and chord \(E_c = C(s_{n_1}+s_{n_2}+s_{n_3})\), with \(C\) not reducible to linear superposition or to a mental “relating activity” [PAGE 40].

Modern Bayesian cue-integration counterpoint (for comparison only): under independent Gaussian channel noise,
\[
\hat p(s\mid x_v,x_a)\propto \mathcal N(x_v;s,\sigma_v^2)\,\mathcal N(x_a;s,\sigma_a^2)\,p(s),
\]
with optimal weights \(w_v=\sigma_a^2/(\sigma_v^2+\sigma_a^2)\), \(w_a=\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_v^2+\sigma_a^2)\). Singularity when the noise covariance \(\Sigma\) is singular \((\rho\to1)\) or \(\sigma_v^2\sigma_a^2-\sigma_{va}^2\to0\), making the weights divergent; this is the breakdown of the independence assumption. Inverse-effectiveness regime: cross-modal modulation is maximal near threshold and collapses at ceiling \((r_i\to r_{\max})\).

Dynamogenic motor gain (Féré link [PAGE 40]):
\[
M = M_0\cdot\Gamma(A_{\mathrm{cross}}),
\]
where \(M\) is contraction force (N), \(M_0\) is baseline force (N), \(\Gamma\) is a dimensionless gain function with \(\Gamma\le\Gamma_{\max}\), and \(A_{\mathrm{cross}}\) is cross-modal cortical activation. Singularity at motor saturation \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{\max}\) and null modulation at \(A_{\mathrm{cross}}=0\). All equations use SI units, dimensionless probabilities/gains, bits for information, and spikes/s for neural rates; no toy ratios are used.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage: James (1890) [PAGE 40] explicitly invokes M. Féré’s “dynamogenic'effects of sensations upon the strength of muscular contraction” [PAGE 40], referring to Charles Féré’s late-19th-century physiological work on how sensation modulates motor output. Antecedents include Helmholtz’s unconscious inference, Wundtian experimental psychophysics, and the Gestalt position (Köhler, Wertheimer) that a chord or a color appearance is emergent rather than a sum of elementary sensations—parallel to James’s rejection of the “relating activity” combining red and green into yellow [PAGE 40]. Sherrington’s synaptic integration and Hebb’s cell-assembly theory provide the neurophysiological descent; modern descendants are Stein and Meredith’s multisensory integration with the inverse-effectiveness principle, Knill and Pouget’s Bayesian cue integration, and Friston’s predictive-processing/active-inference framework, in which seeking “stillness and darkness” under suffering [PAGE 40] can be read as minimization of sensory entropy or variational free energy.

Multi-week traps: (1) cross-modal facilitation measured in week 1 may attenuate by week 4 through sensory adaptation and be misread as treatment decay rather than threshold recalibration; (2) repeated light exposure shifts absolute thresholds longitudinally, creating carryover confounds; (3) regression to the mean in repeated threshold estimates manufactures spurious stable “individual differences” that James treats as noise [PAGE 40]; (4) the crisis behavior of seeking stillness and darkness [PAGE 40] may be mistaken for a fixed trait of sensory avoidance when it is actually a state-dependent attractor engaged only under pain or nausea.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under pain or nausea, subjects “instinctively seek stillness and darkness” [PAGE 40]. In a recursive theory-of-mind model, Observer O represents Agent A as regressing to a low-dimensional, low-sensory-entropy attractor under distress rather than engaging in higher-order cross-modal integration. This regression is the default crisis policy: minimize incoming currents so that the “cerebral process of the first sensation” is not “reinforced or otherwise altered” by competing currents [PAGE 40].

Counter-trap: O may assume A’s cross-modal facilitation is automatic and stimulus-driven, but A could strategically suppress sensory input (e.g., seeking darkness) to manipulate O’s inferences about A’s state. If A is masking sensitivity, O’s model of A’s model of O collapses: the observed stillness is a counter-trap, not a symptom.

Clock: three timescales must be separated. (i) \(\tau_{\mathrm{bind}}\approx 10\text{--}300\ \mathrm{ms}\) for audio-visual cortical summation underlying the yellow/chord emergence [PAGE 40]; (ii) \(\tau_{\mathrm{adapt}}\approx\) minutes for threshold shifts produced by prolonged light or sound exposure; (iii) \(\tau_{\mathrm{habit}}\approx\) weeks for the consolidation of a sensory-avoidance posture such as chronic seeking of stillness and darkness [PAGE 40]. A measurement window \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{obs}}\) shorter than \(\tau_{\mathrm{habit}}\) but longer than \(\tau_{\mathrm{bind}}\) will attribute fast cortical summation to a slow habit, producing a false causal closure. The counter-trap is triggered whenever the observer applies a millisecond binding model to a week-long behavioral regression.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto proof: if any condition below holds, the claimed cross-modal effect is nulled regardless of cortical state or subject report.

1. Physical veto (temporal binding). The mutual audibility of a borderline sound with colored light [PAGE 40] requires \(|t_{\mathrm{light}}-t_{\mathrm{sound}}|\le\tau_{\mathrm{bind}}\). If the stimuli are temporally disjoint beyond the binding window, the “larger sum of light-waves and of air-waves” [PAGE 40] never converges on the same cortical integration epoch, and no threshold lowering occurs. Veto is independent of attention or expectation.

2. Biomechanical veto (motor pathway integrity). Féré’s dynamogenic modulation of contraction strength [PAGE 40] presupposes an intact corticospinal and peripheral motor chain. Neuromuscular blockade, spinal transection, or peripheral neuropathy vetoes the force-gain equation \(M=M_0\Gamma(A_{\mathrm{cross}})\) even if cortical multisensory summation is preserved. Discriminative prediction: the subject may report a sensation of reinforced strength while transducer-recorded force remains at baseline, dissociating the felt “cerebral process” from the motor output [PAGE 40].

3. Weather/environmental veto (energetic masking and homeothermic bounds). The effective auditory threshold \(\theta_a^{\mathrm{eff}}\) is masked by ambient noise floor \(L_{\mathrm{env}}\). If \(\mathrm{SNR}=L_{\mathrm{signal}}-L_{\mathrm{env}}<0\ \mathrm{dB}\), colored-light facilitation of audibility [PAGE 40] is vetoed by energetic masking. Analogously, high ambient luminance raises the visual threshold and can abolish color-induced cross-modal shifts. Temperature-sensation interactions are further bounded by core homeothermy (\(T_{\mathrm{core}}\approx36.5\text{--}37.5^\circ\mathrm{C}\)); outside this range thermoregulatory distress dominates and vetoes the mild temperature fluctuations James describes [PAGE 40].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a setup, an intervention, a verifiable veto condition, and the prediction under the Jamesian cortical model versus under the veto alternative.

Scenario 1: Subcortical sufficiency. Setup: near-threshold auditory stimulus paired with colored light. Intervention: reversible inactivation/lesion of primary visual cortex (V1) and primary auditory cortex (A1) while sparing the superior colliculus. Verifiable veto condition: if behavioral audibility facilitation [PAGE 40] persists despite cortical silencing, James’s claim that new “cortical processes” are necessary for the emergent percept is vetoed. Prediction under Jamesian model: facilitation abolished because the “larger sum of light-waves and of air-waves” must arouse cortical processes [PAGE 40]. Prediction under subcortical model: facilitation preserved via collicular multisensory neurons.

Scenario 2: Dichoptic color presentation. Setup: red light to the left eye and green light to the right eye with no spatial overlap on either retina, eliminating physical summation of light-waves at the retina. Intervention: measure whether a unitary yellow percept emerges or whether binocular rivalry occurs. Verifiable veto condition: if yellow fails to appear and rivalry dominates, James’s “larger sum of light-waves” account [PAGE 40] is insufficient because no retinal wave summation exists. Prediction under Jamesian model: yellow should not emerge without physical wave summation. Prediction under a cortical-combination model: yellow may still emerge binocularly, isolating the effect to post-retinal cortical integration rather than summed physical waves.

Scenario 3: Motor-sensation dissociation under dynamogenic test. Setup: Féré-style muscular-contraction task with concurrent sensory stimulation [PAGE 40]. Intervention: administer reversible neuromuscular blockade while recording subjective strength reports and transducer force output. Verifiable veto condition: if the subject reports increased sensation of strength/reinforcement but force output does not change, then the physiological formulation “cerebral process … reinforced” [PAGE 40] does not exhaust the mental reaction. Prediction under Jamesian model: subjective reinforcement and force output covary because the altered cerebral process produces the dynamogenic effect. Prediction under dissociation model: the felt reinforcement can persist without motor expression, vetoing a one-to-one physiological reduction.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
