The word "impact" is a misnomer; the operator is a sign test on the difference of two durations.

Gray-zone audit summary of breakdown regimes: (i) exact singularity at $\sum_k\text{Magnitude}_k=0$ — static frames, freeze-frames, black frames, fades — where Eq. (2) is $0/0$; (ii) $p_k=0$ requires the convention $0\log 0=0$, unstated; (iii) log base unstated, so EMV's unit (bits vs nats, ratio $1/\ln 2=1.4427$) is undefined and every threshold in Table 2 inherits a 44 % scale ambiguity; (iv) homoscedasticity fails at camera cuts and crowd-noise bursts; (v) SlopeChange-only search is structurally blind to pure level shifts with no slope sign change; (vi) Ival ties at zero are arbitrarily resolved as "not significant."

Table 3 does not reconcile with Eq. (9). Row "Original [13]": 28 detected, 20 correct, 1 missing [PAGE 7] gives $P=20/28=71.4\%$, $R=20/21=95.2\%$, $F_1=81.6\%$ — matching the printed 81.6 %. This fixes the reference set at 21 events. Row "Triangle model": 28 correct, 0 missing, printed $F_1=76.3\%$; but with $P=28/39=71.8\%$ and $R=1$, $F_1=83.6\%$, and with 28 correct the reference set must be $\ge 28$, contradicting 21. Row "Modified": 21 correct of 26 with 0 missing implies $F_1=89.4\%$, not the printed 88.7 %. **The three rows cannot share a denominator under Eq. (9) as printed.** Either "no. missing segments" is not the recall complement, or the FScore column used a different reference. This is a verifiable, replication-blocking inconsistency.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**O1 — Directional quantization.** $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ mapped to $k\in\{1,\dots,N_a\}$, $N_a=16$ [PAGE 2], bin width $\Delta\theta=2\pi/N_a=0.3927$ rad. Units: dimensionless (radians). Assumption: orientation distribution is adequately represented at 22.5° resolution. Breakdown: $\Delta\theta$ aliasing for spin, ball rotation, or limb trajectories whose angular signature is finer than one bin.

**O2 — Bin probability.** $p_k(t)=\dfrac{|\{\mathbf{x}:\theta(\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x},t))\in\mathcal{B}_k\}|}{|\{\mathbf{x}\}|}$, dimensionless, $\sum_k p_k=1$.

**O3 — Directivity surprisal.** [PAGE 2, Eq. 1]
$$E_k=-p_k\log p_k \quad\text{[bits if base 2; nats if base } e\text{]}$$
Units: bits or nats. **Log base is not specified in the source** [PAGE 2]. Assumptions: $0\log 0\equiv 0$. Breakdown: $p_k=0$ is undefined without the convention; the paper never states it.

**O4 — Magnitude weight.** [PAGE 2, Eq. 2]
$$M_k=\frac{\text{Magnitude}_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{N_a}\text{Magnitude}_j},\qquad \text{Magnitude}_k=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{B}_k}\|\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x},t)\|$$
Units of numerator/denominator: pel·frame⁻¹ (summed over blocks); $M_k$ dimensionless, $\sum_k M_k=1$. **Exact singularity**: $\sum_j\text{Magnitude}_j=0\Rightarrow M_k=0/0$, undefined. Regimes: static scene, freeze-frame, black frame, fade-to-black, dead-camera segment. The paper provides no guard [PAGES 2–3].

**O5 — Entropy motion value.** [PAGE 2, Eq. 3]
$$\text{EMV}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{16}M_k(t)\,E_k(t)=-\sum_{k=1}^{16}M_k p_k\log p_k$$
Units: bits or nats. Derived bound (mine, from O3–O4): $\text{EMV}\le\max_k(-p_k\log_2 p_k)=e^{-1}/\ln 2=0.5307$ bits. Uniform histogram gives 0.25 bits (47.1 % of ceiling). **Breakdown: EMV is non-monotone in entropy** — it peaks at $p_k=e^{-1}=0.3679$, so a 36.8 %-concentrated histogram can outscore an isotropic one. The stated design intent, "regular motion vectors would yield lower entropy value while irregular motion vectors would yield higher entropy value" [PAGE 2], holds only for $p_k<e^{-1}$.

**O6 — Temporal sampling.** $t\mapsto t_s$, $\Delta t=1$ s [PAGE 2], source 30 fps [PAGE 5]. Decimation 30:1; Nyquist 0.5 Hz. Reduction rule (mean/instantaneous/max) unspecified — replication ambiguity. Breakdown: any event with dominant motion-content above 0.5 Hz aliases into the baseband.

**O7 — Piecewise-linear model.** [PAGE 3, Eq. 4]
$$y(t)=w_{i1}t+w_{i0}+e_i(t),\quad t_{i-1}\le t<t_i,\quad e_i\sim\text{iid}(0,\sigma_i^2)$$
Units: $w_{i1}$ bits·s⁻¹ (or nats·s⁻¹), $w_{i0}$ bits, $t$ s.

**O8 — Homoscedastic criterion.** [PAGE 3, Eq. 5 and text]
$$\sigma_1=\sigma_2=\cdots=\sigma_m;\qquad L(1,n)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}m_i\sigma_i^2$$
**Gray zone:** the concentrated Gaussian likelihood scales as $\prod_i\sigma_i^{-m_i}$, not $\prod_i m_i\sigma_i^2$; the printed form is increasing in $m_i$ and $\sigma_i$, so its minimization is a segment-count penalty rather than a maximum-likelihood estimator. The source calls it both "the maximum-likelihood function" [PAGE 3] and a "homoscedastic error value" [PAGE 6]. Breakdown: heteroscedastic regimes — camera cuts, crowd-noise bursts, lighting change — violate the equality by construction.

**O9 — Constrained change point.** [PAGE 4, Eqs. 6–7]
$$CP=\arg\min_j\big[L(1,j)+L(j+1,n)\big],\quad p\le j\le n-p,\quad j\subseteq\text{SlopeChange}$$
with $p=25$ [PAGE 3]. At $\Delta t=1$ s this is a **25 s minimum segment duration**. Breakdown: a pure level shift (slope zero on both sides, no sign change) never enters the SlopeChange set, so a genuine step is undetectable by construction; the original method in [13] scanned all $j$ and would find it.

**O10 — Impact value.** [PAGE 5, Eq. 8]
$$IVal=PIVal-NIVal,\qquad PIVal=m_{i1},\qquad NIVal=m_{i2}$$
Units: **frames** (equivalently seconds at 1 Hz), *not* EMV units. Decision rule: "$IVal>0$" significant, else not [PAGE 5]. Breakdown: amplitude-blind and scale-blind; a $10^{-3}$-bit rise over 30 frames and a 1-bit rise over 30 frames are identical inputs.

**O11 — Evaluation metrics.** [PAGE 5, Eq. 9] Precision, Recall, $F_1=2PR/(P+R)$; dimensionless on $[0,1]$. Internal inconsistency: Table 3's three rows are not jointly satisfiable under Eq. (9) with a shared reference set [PAGE 7].

**Cascade.** $\mathbf{v}\xrightarrow{\text{O1}}(p_k)\xrightarrow{\text{O3}}E_k$, $\mathbf{v}\xrightarrow{\text{O4}}M_k$, $\xrightarrow{\text{O5}}\text{EMV}\xrightarrow{\text{O6}}y(t)\xrightarrow{\text{O7–O9}}\{t_i\}\xrightarrow{\text{O10}}\{\text{kept, dropped}\}$.
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The operator has a legible four-generation lineage, all of it cited in the source's own bibliography [PAGES 7–8]. **Generation 1 — optimal partitioning of sequences.** Hawkins & Merriam, "Optimal zonation of digitized sequential data," *Mathematical Geology* 5(4):389–395, 1973 [PAGE 8, ref. 16]. The idea that a one-dimensional series admits an optimal set of breakpoints under a penalized criterion is the direct ancestor of Eq. (6). **Generation 2 — partition regression.** Guthery, *JASA* 69(348):945–947, 1974 [ref. 17]; Hawkins, *JRSS-C* 25(1):51–57, 1976 [ref. 18]. These supply the piecewise-regression likelihood machinery that Eq. (5) attempts to invoke. **Generation 3 — the time-series event detector.** Guralnik & Srivastava, KDD '99, pp. 33–42 [ref. 13, PAGE 8]. This is the paper's immediate algorithmic parent; the source explicitly inherits its iterative search and then modifies it: "a heuristically selected threshold $S$ used in [13] is also eliminated" [PAGE 4]. **Generation 4 — motion salience in video.** Ma & Zhang, ICIP '01 [ref. 15] and the watermarking use of "motion entropy masking" [ref. 14, PAGE 8] precede the entropy formulation here; Liu, Zhang & Qi's perceived motion energy (PME) model, *IEEE TCSVT* 13(10):1006–1013, 2003 [ref. 11] is the direct foil, benchmarked in every table [PAGES 6–7]. The 2008 paper's own novelty is narrow and precisely locatable: replacing a magnitude-only salience with a magnitude-*weighted directional surprisal*, plus a slope-change-restricted change-point search.

**Multi-week trap 1 — the AngNum overfit.** "we choose AngNum = 16 based on extensive experimentation" [PAGE 2]. Sixteen is tuned on three sports (Table 1: World Cup 2006 GER–CRC, 101 min; NBA 1998 IND–CHI, 135 min; Wimbledon 2005 final, 137 min [PAGE 7]) — roughly six hours total. A team that spends weeks sweeping $N_a$ on this corpus will land on 16 and inherit a 22.5° blind spot that no amount of re-tuning on the same corpus can reveal, because the test set and the tuning set are the same six hours.

**Multi-week trap 2 — the 1 Hz / 25-frame prior.** $\Delta t=1$ s and $p=25$ [PAGES 2–3] jointly encode an assumption that a "semantically relevant event" lasts $\ge 25$ s. Any deployment at 25 fps or 60 fps silently rescales both constants unless re-derived. A quarter spent re-tuning will converge on $p=25$ again, because $p=25$ was chosen against the same 30 fps, 1 Hz sampling.

**Multi-week trap 3 — the Table 3 denominator.** Because the three rows cannot share a reference set under Eq. (9) [PAGE 7], any team attempting to reproduce "88.7 %" will spend weeks on a number that is not the $F_1$ of the reported counts. The reconstruction is $F_1=89.4\%$ for 21/26 with zero misses. This is a *retrocausal* trap: the published figure becomes the target, and the target is not the quantity the formula defines.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The clock.** Two clocks, not one. The *sensor* clock is 30 fps [PAGE 5]; the *decision* clock is 1 Hz [PAGE 2]; the *segment* clock enforces 25 s [PAGE 3]. Every claim the paper makes is indexed to the decision clock, but every datum arrives on the sensor clock, and the reduction between them is unspecified. An agent reasoning about this system must model three nested clocks and cannot verify the mapping between the outer two.

**Crisis habit regression.** Under distribution shift, the pipeline regresses to its lowest-complexity component. The classifier's entire semantic content is Eq. (8) plus the rule "$IVal>0$" [PAGE 5] — a sign test on the difference of two *frame counts*. Strip the entropy layer, the change-point layer, the regression layer, and what remains is: "an increasing EMV trend over a segment means something happened." That is precisely the behavior the paper criticizes in its own related work, where "the apparent camera motion may cause excessive false alarms" [PAGE 2] and PME's triangle model "requires the determination of an additional variable $T$ [11], and may lead to higher false-alarm rate" [PAGE 3]. The regression is not hypothetical; it is visible in the architecture. The entropy layer's only job is to suppress camera-motion false alarms between the front end and the sign test — and the sign test itself never sees magnitude.

**Counter-trap.** The paper's own counter-example pair is instructive. Near time index 400, "the EMV value ($\sim$60) is markedly larger than the PME value ($\sim$40)" for an "attacking-goal event"; near 1400, "the EMV value ($\sim$40) is much less than the corresponding PME value ($\sim$80)" for "a closeup shot of players walking around aimlessly" [PAGE 2, Figures 2(b)–2(c)]. EMV wins both. But the counter-trap is that EMV's advantage comes from *regularity rejection*, and regularity is exactly what a slow camera zoom-out produces. A monotone zoom-out with growing parallax yields a rising, structured EMV over tens of seconds — increasing $m_{i1}$, small $m_{i2}$ — hence $IVal>0$, hence "semantically significant." The adversary need not be adversarial; a director's establishing shot suffices. The paper never runs this control.

**Recursive layer — modeling the annotator.** The ground truth is manually labelled: each candidate segment "is manually labelled as being significant or not depending on whether it contains one of the corresponding significant sport events specified above" [PAGES 5–6], with nine event classes fixed in advance (Fault/Ace/Volley; Scoring/Miss/Free throw; Goal/Free kick/Corner) [PAGE 6, Table 2]. The annotator's label function is therefore *segment-scoped* — a label attaches to a candidate produced by the very segmentation under test. This makes the evaluation a closed loop: the algorithm decides what the annotator is allowed to see. A perfect $F_1$ under this protocol is compatible with a segmented world that omits events entirely, so long as every *offered* segment is labelled correctly. Table 3's "no. missing segments = 0" for the Modified row [PAGE 7] is not evidence of recall; it is the protocol's own definition.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

**Claim under adjudication:** that O1–O11 license any inference about physical, biomechanical, or meteorological state. **Verdict: vetoed, with proof.**

*Proof by data-processing inequality.* Let $X$ be the image sequence, $V$ the motion-vector field produced by "full-search motion estimation" [PAGE 3, Fig. 1 caption], and $Y$ any target variable. The chain is deterministic at every step: $X\to V\to(p_k,M_k)\to\text{EMV}\to\{t_i\}\to\{\text{kept/dropped}\}$. By DPI, $I(X;Y)\ge I(V;Y)\ge I(\text{EMV};Y)\ge I(\{t_i\};Y)$. Hence any $Y$ with negligible mutual information with $X$ has *exactly zero* mutual information with EMV. The pipeline cannot create information; it can only destroy it. This is not a heuristic — it is a theorem, and it closes the door on every variable that is not a function of the pixel stream.

*Biomechanical veto — sampling theorem.* Limb-impact transients, tendon loading rates, and ground-reaction spikes carry dominant spectral content well above 15 Hz. The sensor clock is 30 fps [PAGE 5], Nyquist 15 Hz. The decision clock is 1 Hz [PAGE 2], Nyquist 0.5 Hz. Any biomechanical quantity is decimated by 30:1 before the entropy layer is even reached. Beyond that, the spatial operator is a block-lattice vector field quantized to 16 angular bins of 22.5° — a representation in which a knee angle and a camera pan are the same object. The paper itself concedes the representational collapse: "motion vectors due to global camera motion are quite regular, as oppose to motion vectors due to complex object motion" [PAGE 2] — the only distinction the operator draws is *regular vs. irregular*, not *which limb*.

*Weather veto — absent operator.* Table 1 lists exactly three clips with columns "Video type / Description / Length (minutes)" [PAGE 7]. No temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation, altitude, or surface variable is recorded, modelled, or referenced anywhere in eight pages. There is no channel. A variable that is never measured has, by construction, no estimator here. This veto is trivially decisive and requires no empirical work.

*Physical-state veto — non-identifiability.* Suppose two physically distinct scenes, $S_1$ and $S_2$, produce the same $(p_k,M_k)$ at the 16-bin, 1 Hz resolution. Then O5 returns identical EMV and O7–O10 return identical segments. Such collisions are generic, not exceptional: the map from $\mathbf{v}$ to $(p_k,M_k)$ is many-to-one, and the map from the underlying physics to $\mathbf{v}$ is many-to-one again. Non-identifiability is a *proof of impossibility*, not a data limitation.

*Residual legitimate use.* The operator is sound for exactly one job: detecting temporal boundaries in the image stream itself, which is what Table 2's nine event classes are defined over [PAGE 6]. Any extension must add a new measurement channel with its own sampling theorem and its own identifiability argument. None exists in this source.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Each scenario is a runnable experiment on the published pipeline, with a falsifiable prediction. None involves an outcome variable; all are tests of the operator.

**Veto 1 — Zero-motion singularity in Eq. (2).**
*Intervention.* Splice 60 s of frozen frame (or black) into the soccer clip of Table 1 [PAGE 7]. *Mechanism.* $\text{Magnitude}_k=0\ \forall k\Rightarrow \sum_j \text{Magnitude}_j=0\Rightarrow M_k=0/0$ [PAGE 2, Eq. 2]. *Prediction.* EMV at those samples is either NaN or $0$ depending on implementation, and the change-point detector treats the run as a single segment because the SlopeChange set on a constant run is empty (no zero crossing) [PAGE 4, Eq. 7]. *Falsifier.* A finite, nonzero, reproducible EMV trace across the frozen block, or any change point detected at its entry/exit. *Why it matters.* The paper gives no guard, so any deployment into broadcast footage containing replays, freeze-frames, or transitions carries an unhandled $0/0$.

**Veto 2 — SlopeChange blindness to pure level shifts.**
*Intervention.* Construct a synthetic series $y(t)=10+\varepsilon_t$ for $t<100$ and $y(t)=60+\varepsilon_t$ for $t\ge 100$, $\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, at 1 Hz. *Mechanism.* Eq. (7) restricts $j$ to $j\subseteq\text{SlopeChange}$, the zero crossings of the EMV slope [PAGE 4]. On both sides the slope is statistically zero; there is no sign change at $t=100$. *Prediction.* The modified algorithm returns **no** change point at $t=100$ (or a spurious one far from it); the original Guralnik scan over all $j$ [ref. 13, PAGE 8] returns $t\approx 100$ with $L(1,j)+L(j+1,n)$ sharply minimized there. *Falsifier.* A detected change point within $\pm5$ samples of 100, reproducibly, by the SlopeChange-constrained variant. *Why it matters.* Camera cuts and hard lighting changes are *exactly* level shifts, and they are the dominant false-alarm source the paper is trying to suppress.

**Veto 3 — Amplitude blindness of IVal.**
*Intervention.* Two segments, both with $m_{i1}=30$ frames rising and $m_{i2}=20$ frames falling; segment A rises from 0.00 to 0.01 bits, segment B from 0.00 to 0.50 bits. *Mechanism.* Eq. (8) sets $IVal=m_{i1}-m_{i2}$ in *frames* [PAGE 5], so amplitude never enters. *Prediction.* $IVal=+10$ for both; both are classified "semantically significant" by the rule "$IVal>0$" [PAGE 5]. Converse arm: a segment rising for 10 frames and falling for 11 — containing the global maximum of the whole clip — yields $IVal=-1$ and is **dropped**. *Falsifier.* Any dependence of the kept/dropped decision on peak EMV amplitude at fixed $(m_{i1},m_{i2})$. *Why it matters.* The words "accumulated impact value" [PAGE 5] imply an integral; the formula is a duration difference. The four-segment illustration in Figure 4(b) — "1st dropped, 2nd dropped, 3rd reserved, and 4th dropped" [PAGE 6, caption] — is fully explained by segment durations alone.

**Veto 4 (supplementary) — AngNum indistinguishability.**
*Intervention.* Two synthetic vector fields with different continuous orientation densities but identical 22.5°-bin counts and identical per-bin magnitude sums. *Mechanism.* O1 collapses $\theta\mapsto k$ before O3 [PAGE 2]. *Prediction.* EMV identical to machine precision; no downstream stage separates them. *Falsifier.* Any $N_a=16$ pipeline output that differs between the two fields. *Why it matters.* It localizes the information loss to a single, testable line of code.

**Aggregate prediction.** All four vetoes are structural, not statistical — they do not shrink with more data, more sports, or more tuning. The published $F_1$ values in Table 2 (67.0 %–98.8 % across nine classes) [PAGE 6] are therefore not evidence of robustness; they are measurements made inside a protocol whose segmentation defines the annotator's input [PAGES 5–6]. The correct reading of this paper is narrow and honest: a computable, cheap, camera-motion-suppressing scalar for temporal boundary detection in sport broadcast video, with a documented set of exact singularities, an unstated log base, a likelihood formula that is not a likelihood, and a Table 3 that does not reconcile. Nothing more may be inferred from it, and no inference about physical performance, athlete state, or environment is admissible.
