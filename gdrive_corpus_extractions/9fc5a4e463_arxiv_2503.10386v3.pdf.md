<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Here "adversary/Nature, algorithm response, and clock" is the honest mapping (the template's "crisis habit / counter-trap / clock").

Nature's construction (Theorem 4 [PAGE 22-24]): fix a good arm $i^*$ and perturb only the single binding coordinate $m^*_i=\arg\min_m d(\mu^{(m)}_i,\xi_m)$ to $\xi_m-\epsilon_1$, turning $i^*$ into a non-good arm. Any (δ,0)-successful algorithm must, under the perturbed model, stop selecting $i^*$ with prob $\ge1-\delta$; change-of-measure (Prop 5) then forces $\mathbb{E}[T_{i^*}]\cdot d(\mu^{(m^*_{i^*})}_{i^*},\xi_{m^*_{i^*}})\ge\ln\tfrac{1}{2\delta}-\delta$. The adversary's leverage is concentrated entirely on the closest-to-threshold coordinate — this is the "tell": the hard direction is $\arg\min_m d(\mu^{(m)}_i,\xi_m)$, not the largest raw gap.

Algorithm counter-trap: MultiTUCB's optimism $\tilde g$ deliberately under-estimates the gap of the least-explored arm, so it keeps probing any arm that could plausibly be i* — precisely neutralizing Nature's ability to hide i* behind under-sampling. The deletion rule ($\bar g>0$) is calibrated to the same δ so the counter-trap does not over-explore.

The clock: $T_{\text{stop}}$ is the resource. The theory says the clock is governed by the worst competing arm's residual optimism window $\propto\ln(KM\,T_0)/(g_i-g_{i^*}+\epsilon_0)^2$ [PAGE 18, Lemma 5] — a self-referential bound ($T_0$ appears inside its own log), which is why the log factor is $\ln(KM\max_i\lfloor t_i\rfloor)$ and not $\ln(1/\delta)$ alone. The recursion closes because $T_0=K\max_i\lfloor t_i(\epsilon_0)\rfloor$ is itself $\Theta(\ln(KM/\delta))$, so $\ln(KM\,T_0)=\Theta(\ln(KM/\delta))$.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical/validity-layer vetoes (would void the guarantees):

V1 — Boundedness/sub-Gaussian layer. Theorem 1's proof [PAGE 14, Prop 4] invokes $\mathbb{P}(|g_i-\hat g_{n,i}|\ge\epsilon)\le 2M e^{-n\epsilon^2/2\sigma^2}$, which requires the reward to be exactly $(\mu,\sigma)$-sub-Gaussian with the σ used by the algorithm. Proof of validity: if rewards are heavy-tailed (e.g., Pareto with finite mean, infinite MGF), the Chernoff bound fails and no finite σ validates $\alpha$; deletion errors accumulate and the (δ,ϵ) guarantee is void. Note the experiments use $\sigma=1.2$ and $\sigma=1.0$ [PAGE 8,9] while $[0,1]$-bounded rewards admit $\sigma^2=1/4$ (Hoeffding, $\sigma=0.5$): the algorithm is deliberately conservative, preserving validity at the cost of inflated $T_{\text{stop}}$ — a safe but loose operating point.

V2 — Estimator-coverage layer. Theorem 1 relies on Lemma 1's $\sum_n \tfrac{6M\delta}{\pi^2 KM n^2}\le\delta/K$ [PAGE 14-15]. If the analyst replaces the anytime width $\alpha(\tau,\delta)$ (with $\tau^2$) by a fixed-horizon width $\sqrt{2\sigma^2\ln(1/\delta)/\tau}$, the union over all $t$ (Protocol allows unbounded horizon, [PAGE 3]) diverges and coverage is lost — veto.

V3 — σ-boundary layer. At $\sigma^\star=\sigma$ the guarantee holds; for any $\sigma^\star>\sigma$ (misspecified, e.g., non-stationary variance in the IoT example [PAGE 2]) Theorem 1 fails. This is a hard validity boundary, not a soft degradation.

Adjudication: all three are genuine vetoes; the paper's guarantees are conditional on correct σ and unbounded-horizon sub-Gaussianity. None is a "weather/biomechanical" physical constraint — the only physical layer here is the reward distribution's tail.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
