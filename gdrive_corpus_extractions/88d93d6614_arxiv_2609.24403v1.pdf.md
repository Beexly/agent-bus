Convolution with stride (2,2), Eq. (2) [PAGE 8], units: input events per cell → filtered postsynaptic activity; 128×128 → 64×64:
$$I'(x,y)=\sum_{i=-1}^{1}\sum_{j=-1}^{1}G(i,j)\,I(x+i,\,y+j).$$

Angle-readout synaptic weight, Eq. (3) [PAGE 10], dimensionless (normalised), σ = 30° (tuning width), wrap(·) gives geodesic angular distance on S¹, θ_k = k·45°, k∈{0,…,7}:
$$w_{ij}=\frac{\exp\!\big(-\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_i-\phi_j)^2/2\sigma^{2}\big)}{\sum_{k}\exp\!\big(-\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_k-\phi_j)^2/2\sigma^{2}\big)},\qquad \sum_i w_{ij}=1.$$

Readout activity, Eq. (4) [PAGE 10], units: spikes (or Hz) × dimensionless weight → weighted spike count:
$$R_j=\sum_{i=1}^{8} r_i\,w_{ij}.$$

Constant-velocity prediction, Eq. (5) [PAGE 11], SI: x,y in px, v in px·s⁻¹, Δt = 16.7 ms, φ in rad:
$$x_{t+1}=x_t+v_x\Delta t,\quad y_{t+1}=y_t+v_y\Delta t,\quad v_x=\tfrac{d}{\Delta t}\cos\phi,\; v_y=-\tfrac{d}{\Delta t}\sin\phi.$$

Metrics, Eqs. (6)–(10) [PAGE 11–18], dimensionless ratios:
$$\text{Acc}=\frac{N_{correct}}{N_{total}},\quad \text{HitRate}=\frac{N_{hit}}{N_{act}},\quad \text{Conv}=\frac{N_{hit}}{N_{hit}+N_{bounce}}.$$

SpiNNaker power budget, Eq. (11) [PAGE 20], SI watts:
$$P_T=P_I+P_B+(P_N\times n)+(P_S\times s).$$
Dynamic term only: $P_S s = 309\text{ events}\times 8\text{ nJ} = 2472\text{ nJ}$ per window; dividing by ΔT = 16.7 ms gives $2472\times10^{-9}/16.7\times10^{-3}=148.0\ \mu\text{W}$. ✓ Internally consistent.

Latency budget [PAGE 20, Table 6]: $\tau_{net}=1+1+2+1=5$ ms (I/O); $\tau_{react}=\tau_{net}+\Delta T=5+16.7=21.7$ ms. ✓

Breakdown regimes: (i) $\Delta t_{RF}=N/v < D_{fac}-D_{trig}=1$ ms ⇒ aliasing, TDE cannot resolve ordering; (ii) $\Delta t_{RF} \gg \tau_{fac}$ ⇒ facilitation decayed, no correlation, MFR→0; (iii) Eq. (3) degenerates when σ→0 (single-neuron spikes, no interpolation) or σ→∞ (uniform weights, zero angular specificity) — the σ=150 collapse in Figure 9 [PAGE 34-35]. The un-reported τ_fac prevents a closed-form v_pref; this is the principal analytical singularity.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The lineage is explicit and traceable. Hassenstein–Reichardt (1956) [ref 40] established correlation-based Elementary Motion Detectors; Borst & Egelhaaf (1987) [ref 8] added temporal modulation of the time constant; Adelson & Bergen (1985) [ref 1] gave the orthogonal spatiotemporal-energy basis; Borst & Euler/Haag [refs 9,10] canonicalised the fly correlator with ON/OFF segregation. Milde et al. (2018) [ref 47] produced the spiking EMD (sEMD); Gutierrez-Galan et al. (2022) [ref 38] the digital TDE with facilitation/trigger synapses that this work instantiates on SpiNNaker; Yedutenko et al. (2025) [ref 77] the multi-point TDE-3 generalisation. The paper positions itself as the first TDE-SNN that "extracts fine-grained angular motion from sparse activations" and demonstrates closed-loop pinball control [PAGE 4].

The methodological trap embedded in this lineage — the paper's own "multi-week" pitfall — is the historical habit of evaluating EMD/TDE with wide-field stimuli (bars, gratings), which activate broad sensor regions and only test coarse left-right/up-down selectivity. The authors deliberately invert this: "Only a small subset of RFs is active as a target moves across the field of view, providing a stricter test of local motion encoding" [PAGE 3]. This is the correct scientific move, but it also *exposes* the diagonal-confusion weakness that wide-field stimuli would have hidden — a lineage-versus-rigor tension worth flagging. The prior demonstration cluster (robotic goalkeeping [20], Pong [6,58], air hockey [31,3,2], pencil balancing [15]) is the competitive genealogy Table 8 [PAGE 22] situates against.

</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Two adaptive agents are modelled. First, human players under instruction manipulation: the solid-flipper study [PAGE 18] split 20 participants into "avoid-misclicks" (precision) and "maximise-score" (volume), producing hit rates from ~0.6 down to ~0.2–0.35. This is deliberate strategy selection under a payoff, not skill noise.

Second, the machine's counter-strategy: a single interpretable parameter (strike-region size s1→s9) sweeps the loop "continuously across the full range of human strategies... without any change to the perception pipeline" [PAGE 18]. The key result is that the fixed spiking state estimate supports an entire policy family — evidence that perception, not policy, is the binding constraint ("closed-loop performance is principally bounded by perception quality rather than policy sophistication" [PAGE 22]).

The "habit-regression-under-clock" reading: agents under a hard real-time clock (Δt=16.7 ms, one command per window) regress toward satisficing. Humans in the binary-gate regime default to aggressive mashing (282 actions/episode avg, 25.6% hit rate) because the unintuitive collider defeats deliberate timing — a regression to volume when precision is unrewarded. The nPA loop instead holds the disciplined upper-left corner (64 hits from 114 actions, 1.14 actions/episode) precisely because it lacks an intuition to override; its geometric trigger cannot "panic." The theory-of-mind asymmetry: the machine models only ball kinematics, never the opponent, so it is immune to the psychological regression that degrades human play under the same clock.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Physical/hardware veto proof — conditions under which the headline claims fail:

1. Display-sampling veto. Claim: reliable motion estimation to 1000 px/s. Physical constraint: 60 Hz refresh → displacement per sample = v/f. For the deployed 3×3 RF, aliasing onset v_alias = N·f = 3·60 = 180 px/s; the ball crosses >1 RF per frame above this. The paper concedes "Event accumulation cannot reconstruct these missing intermediate positions, since no visual information is presented during the skipped intervals" [PAGE 8-9]. VERDICT: the high-speed regime is stimulus-starved by hardware, not solved by the network. The 21.7 ms figure is genuine but conditioned on a 60 Hz information ceiling.

2. Latency-floor veto. τ_react = 21.7 ms is bounded below by ΔT = 16.7 ms accumulation; the network's own 5 ms is not the bottleneck. Claim of "at least 60 Hz control" holds [PAGE 20], but the accumulation window cannot shrink without starving slow-motion firing-rate estimates — a hard floor.

3. Power-attribution veto. The 148 µW is dynamic-only; P_N·n for n=24,620 neurons at 1 ms time-step is excluded [PAGE 20-21]. The Table 8 "lowest dynamic power" claim is defensible as scoped but is not total board power; comparing it against systems reporting wall/mixed power is a category mismatch.

4. Biomechanical/actuation veto (physical demonstrator): the ESP8266/Wi-Fi servo path [PAGE 19] adds unquantified actuation and network latency not included in 21.7 ms; the demonstrator is explicitly "qualitative rather than quantitative" [PAGE 22]. The closed-loop reaction figure therefore does not certify the physical build.

Net: the perception-to-action principle survives all four vetoes as a *principle*; the specific performance envelope is vetoed above ~180 px/s by display sampling and understates true power by omitting the neuron term.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three verifiable counterfactuals with falsifiable predictions:

CF-1 (display upgrade). Replace 60 Hz with 120 Hz, all else fixed. Prediction: v_alias doubles (3×3 → 360 px/s); the diagonal self-classification rates (BR-TL, TR-BL, currently ~0.11–0.55) rise most, because diagonals are the classes most corrupted by >1-RF jumps. Falsifiable: re-run minimal simulator at 120 Hz; expect mean direction accuracy >80% and reduced Up/BR-TL leakage.

CF-2 (tuning-width veto). Set σ=150 at the deployed 3×3/16.7 ms point. Prediction: AR weights flatten toward uniform (Eq. 3 denominator dominates), angular specificity collapses per Figure 9 [PAGE 34], position prediction error grows, and the strike-region sweep fails to reproduce precise players (s1–s4 fall below ~0.6 hit rate). Falsifiable: hit rate at s1 drops below the avoid-misclicks band.

CF-3 (event-cap saturation). Double ball radius to 30 px (area ~4×). Prediction: active cells exceed the Nevt=500 cap [PAGE 11-12] within a shorter wall-clock time, adaptively shortening ΔT via AECP; MFR estimates get fewer spikes → higher variance, so R² at 16.7 ms falls below the reported 0.761, while blur decreases. Falsifiable: at r=30 px, speed-estimation R² at ΔT=16.7 ms drops and MAE rises above 0.374.

Each counterfactual is testable within the released PinballSim/SNN code [PAGE 24], satisfying verifiability.

</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
