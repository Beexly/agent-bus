Crisis habit regression: under stress or after a delay, an organism may regress to an apparently extinguished habit because the inhibitory potential \(I_R\) that suppressed it has disintegrated per Proposition C. The “crisis” is not a loss of new learning but a time-dependent unmasking of the old reaction potential as \(sE_R^{\text{eff}}\) rises.

Counter-trap: an observer or model may infer that extinction has permanently deleted the habit, predicting zero responding at all post-extinction delays. The counter-trap is therefore to mistake temporary inhibition for permanent erasure and to fail to schedule test sessions at sufficiently long delays to observe recovery.

Because recovery is a function of this clock, the organism’s future behavior is recursively coupled to its own response history: emitting a response terminates one inhibitory series and may start another, resetting \(t\) and altering \(I_R(0)\) for the next cycle. If the agent monitors its own recovered strength and chooses to act when \(sE_R^{\text{eff}}\) crosses a threshold, the act itself replenishes inhibition, producing a limit-cycle or oscillatory pattern of regression and re-suppression governed by the same negative-growth operator.
