If the information arrival rate over Ω exceeds the contractual clock speed—if new states arrive faster than the wage or policy can be renegotiated—the plane of indifference collapses and the contract is revealed as incomplete. The recursion terminates only when the principal can commit to a residual claimancy rule that aligns control, uncertainty-bearing, and payoff across the full interval [t₀, t₁].

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: information processing is bounded by thermodynamic and temporal constraints; neither the entrepreneur nor the electorate can compute Ω or identify 𝒫 in real time. Biomechanical layer: cognitive limits constrain the voter's capacity to evaluate agent competence—[PAGE 302] explicitly describes the populace as "admittedly ignorant in regard to political issues and the technique of government"—and constrain the entrepreneur's capacity to monitor effort and venture choice continuously. Weather layer: non-stationary environmental stochasticity shifts the data-generating process over time, so a probability P estimated at t₀ is invalid at t₁; the venture environment is not ergodic.

Veto proof: a complete contract is vetoed when all three layers simultaneously bind. (1) The weather layer induces non-identifiability of 𝒫, so no unique premium can be priced. (2) The biomechanical layer prevents verification of the agent's action a, so the fixed wage w̄ cannot implement first-best effort. (3) The physical layer imposes a decision deadline shorter than the time required to resolve ambiguity, so the principal cannot wait for learning before contracting. Under this triple veto, no wage contract can sustain the "plane of indifference at the wages fixed" described on [PAGE 302], and profit must be understood as the return to holding an uncontractible, real-time control option under non-ergodic uncertainty rather than as the return to measurable risk alone.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario A (insurable risk): A venture outcome is fully insurable because P is measurable and markets are complete. Veto condition: if profit were identical to interest as a pure risk premium, full insurance should drive the residual Π to zero. Prediction: persistent positive Π after full insurance falsifies the classical profit–interest conflation and verifies that profit is a return to Knightian uncertainty and effective control rather than to measurable risk.

Scenario B (labor retains control): A wage contract fixes w̄, but labor retains de facto venture-choice control (e.g., a professional partnership). Veto condition: the plane of indifference on [PAGE 302] requires absence of control; if control is present, wages should diverge across employment lines according to the risk of the chosen ventures. Prediction: wage dispersion correlates with autonomy over venture selection even after controlling for skill, hours, and measurable risk, verifying that contracting out control—not merely risk—is what flattens the wage plane.

Scenario C (political delegation under crisis): An electorate delegates policy formation to a manager-plan government during a multi-week crisis. Veto condition: if selection on competence works, policy outcomes should track pre-crisis expert benchmarks throughout the crisis window. Prediction: during crisis weeks, observed policy drifts toward agents selected on charismatic or personal-superiority cues rather than issue competence, and voter approval tracks those cues rather than policy outcomes, verifying the delegation trap described in the [PAGE 302] footnote.
