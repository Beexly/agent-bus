Verifiable condition: ∑ⱼ Mₖⱼ = 0. Veto: skip row-normalization for row k, assign dₖ its initial relevance score only, and exclude its column from affinity-based suppression. Prediction: dₖ is ranked by relevance without diversity penalty; if it is topically unique it appears in the first page, restoring diversity for isolated topics rather than suppressing them due to a division-by-zero failure.

Scenario 2 (Graph fragmentation under high threshold). Counterfactual: for a long-tail query, all pairwise affinities fall below τ = 0.2 because documents share few of the 25 keywords. Verifiable condition: M is the zero matrix (or block-diagonal with singleton components). Veto: automatically lower τ adaptively until at least one edge per component exists, or bypass diversity entirely and return initial scores. Prediction: the system avoids a false “all documents are unrelated” conclusion; the first page returns a relevance-ordered list rather than an arbitrary subset selected from an empty graph, preserving user-facing relevance when topical signal is too weak for affinity-based diversity.

Verifiable condition: in-degree anomaly where ∑ᵢ Mᵢₜ exceeds the 99th percentile of in-degree across D, or m duplicates from the same domain point to t. Veto: cap per-domain contribution to rₜ or apply a duplication-discount factor to r before the greedy selection. Prediction: t’s richness score no longer scales linearly with the number of synthetic inbound pages; the greedy loop selects t once and suppresses its true near-duplicates, while rival pages with independently high relevance escape artificial suppression, restoring competitive diversity.
