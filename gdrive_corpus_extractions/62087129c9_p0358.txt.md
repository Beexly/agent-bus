<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The theoretical lineage begins with Pavlov’s distinction between excitatory and inhibitory conditioning, in which a compound can acquire inhibitory properties while its elements remain excitatory under specific reinforcement arrangements [PAGE 358]. Hull’s 1943 *Principles of Behavior* formalizes this within a deductive, postulate-driven behaviorism in which stimulus compounds are summed or configured according to habit strength and reaction potential [PAGE 358]. The excerpt explicitly cites an earlier report, “(8, p. 144),” for the reverse-direction phenomenon in which a formerly stimulatory compound becomes negative while its components stay positive [PAGE 358]. It then introduces Woodbury’s (10) unpublished canine data as a detailed, contemporaneous example of simultaneous patterning [PAGE 358].

Tracing forward, Hull’s elemental account struggles to generate positive patterning in which a compound evokes far more strongly than the mean of its parts without an explicit configural cue; this gap was later addressed by Rescorla and Wagner’s (1972) error-correction model, by Pearce’s (1987) configural theory assigning a unique stimulus node to the compound, and by occasion-setting accounts in which the compound modulates the salience of its elements. The “retro-causal” feature is that the behavioral value assigned to Q is not fixed at acquisition but is retrospectively reassigned by later contingencies: if the experimenter reverses the reinforcement schedule, the compound’s meaning is revised after the fact, and earlier training is re-interpreted by the organism in light of subsequent outcomes. In a multi-week protocol this produces a trap: an animal may show Π ≈ +100 in week one, yet exhibit spontaneous recovery of component excitation, renewal in a changed context, or relapse to inhibitory compound control in week three if the differential contingency is inverted. Hull’s system contains no formal parameter for such retrospective revaluation; the patterning coefficient is a snapshot on the experimental clock, not a storage of value immune to later editorial revision by the animal’s subsequent experience. The multi-week trap is therefore that early patterning coefficients may look stable while concealing a latent contingency structure that only later test sessions expose.

Recursive theory of mind here means the dog is tracking the experimenter’s schedule, and the experimenter is tracking the dog’s learning curve, both indexed by the experimental clock.
