<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Mapped honestly onto the paper's genuine multi-agent modeling. The "recursive" content is literal: a GAT interaction encoder (Eqs. 10–12) models each player's next move as a distribution over neighbors' moves — players reasoning about players — which is first-order theory of mind realized as attention weights $\alpha_{ij}$. The RNN-GAT-RNN fusion (Eqs. 13–15) stacks self-modeling (GRU history encoder) on other-modeling (GAT), i.e., $f_t^0=\mathrm{RNN}_{\text{fut}}([g_t^0,r_t^0])$ fuses "what others will do" with "what I have been doing."

"Crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock": the crisis is abrupt directional change; the habit-regression failure mode is that Constant Velocity/Linear win below 0.12s and only lose once non-linearity dominates ("An optimized constant velocity KF works best below 0.12,s" [PAGE 10]). The counter-trap is horizon-indexed model routing, which the paper makes explicit: "For horizons exceeding 0.76s, at least an LSTM model should be employed, and beyond 1.3s, a CNN-LSTM architecture is advisable" [PAGE 10]. The "clock" is the forecast horizon itself — the adjudication variable. There is no adversarial opponent modeling intent here beyond physical co-players; I will not invent strategic deception that the source does not claim.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Weather veto: vacuous and I decline to fabricate. The NBA court is indoor, climate-controlled (2015-16 season, [PAGE 7]); no precipitation, wind, or temperature term enters any equation. Asserting a weather veto would violate the no-fabrication invariant.

Biomechanical veto (genuine). Court dimensions $28.65\times15.24$ m bound the reachable set; elite in-game player speeds are $v_{\max}\approx 7\text{–}9$ m/s. Over the $\Delta t=0.04$ s reconstruction step (Eq. postprocessing, [PAGE 7]), any predicted $\|v_i\|>10$ m/s is physically inadmissible and should be vetoed/clipped — the paper applies no such kinematic constraint, so out-of-distribution predictions are unconstrained. Plausibility check on FDE: CNN-LSTM FDE 1.51m over a 2s horizon, during which a player realistically covers $\sim 3\text{–}5$ m, implies a relative displacement error of $\sim 30\text{–}50\%$ — large but consistent with the authors' premise that "players frequently change direction, making motion patterns beyond 2s less predictive" [PAGE 9]. Constant Velocity's 2.49m FDE is the physically expected failure: a straight-line extrapolation from a curved true path.

Information-theoretic veto. At 25 Hz, 2s = 50 samples; the irreducible error is the conditional entropy $H(\text{future}\mid\text{2s history})$ of human motion. The observed saturation — "error variations remain minimal between 1.61 and 1.63m at input lengths between 1.0 and 3.0s" [PAGE 9] — is the empirical signature that additional history supplies near-zero mutual information about the 2s-ahead target beyond 1s. This is an aleatoric floor, not a model-capacity ceiling; it vetoes any claim that "more context windows" will help, and correctly bounds all architectures to the observed ~1.5m regime.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three falsifiable, verifiable predictions (RLVR = claims checkable against re-runnable evidence).

Veto 1 — Transformer data-scarcity hypothesis. Claim under test: "our Transformer model suffers from having too little data for effective training" [PAGE 10]. Counterfactual: if true, training the identical 6-block, $d_{\text{model}}=256$ encoder on the full 508-hour league corpus (all teams, not one) must drive FDE below 1.62m (the LSTM/GNN level). Prediction: FDE gap closes to $<1.60$m. Falsifier: if FDE stays $\approx 1.66$m with 3–5× data, the deficit is architectural — most plausibly the $d_k=4$ anomaly [PAGE 7] — not data.

Veto 2 — Causal role of the context field. Claim: the 0.11m gain is caused by Eq. (2) distances. Counterfactual ablation: zero out the three context channels (nearest-object distance, two hoop distances) from CNN-LSTM, holding weights and data fixed. Prediction: FDE reverts toward the pure-LSTM 1.62m and the "outperforms beyond 1.28s" property disappears. Falsifier: if ablated FDE stays near 1.51m, the CNN front-end alone (not context) carries the gain, contradicting [PAGE 10].

Veto 3 — Domain-specific 2s saturation. Claim: optimal input $\approx$ 2s is basketball-specific. Counterfactual: retrain on soccer/pedestrian data the authors cite. Prediction: optimal input length $>2$ s and monotone improvement past 2s, consistent with "significantly longer input horizons are beneficial in those domains" [PAGE 9]. Falsifier: if soccer also saturates at $\le 2$ s, the saturation is a universal motion-entropy floor, not a basketball chaos effect — which would upgrade the information-theoretic veto above from domain-specific to general.

Each veto is executable against the released NBA-Player-Movements corpus (ref [49]) with the stated architectures and seeds, making the claims verifiable rather than rhetorical.
