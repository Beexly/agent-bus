Theorem 1 assumptions ([PAGE 7 of 50]): (i) well-posed, bounded/continuous distributions of $(x_{m,B}, z_m)$; (ii) injectivity of operators $L_{x_{m,C}|z_m}$ and $L_{x_{m,A}|x_{m,C}}$; (iii) measurement change — $p(x_{m,B}|z_t^{(1)}) \ne p(x_{m,B}|z_t^{(2)}, s)$ for $z_t^{(1)} \ne z_t^{(2)}$ ("significantly weaker than monotonicity"); (iv) differentiability of a functional $M$ with $M[p_{x_{m,B}|z_m,s}] = h(z_m,s)$. Conclusion: $[\hat z_m, \hat s] = h(z_m, s)$, $h$ invertible and differentiable.

Proof mechanism ([PAGE 40–42 of 50]): conditional independence gives $p(x_{m,C},x_{m,B}|x_{m,A}) = \int p(x_{m,C}|w_m)p(x_{m,B}|w_m)p(w_m|x_{m,A})\,dw_m$ with $w_m=[z_m,s]$; marginalizing yields the operator identity $L_{x_{m,B};x_{m,C}|x_{m,A}} = L_{x_{m,C}|w_m} D_{x_{m,B}|w_m} L_{w_m|x_{m,A}}$, and after marginalization and injective inversion $L^{-1}_{x_{m,C}|w_m} L_{x_{m,C}|x_{m,A}} = L_{w_m|x_{m,A}}$. A canonical conjugation $L_{x_{m,C}|w_m} D_{x_{m,B}|w_m} L^{-1}_{x_{m,C}|w_m}$ invokes "uniqueness of spectral decomposition (see (Conway, 1994), Chapter VII, and (Dunford & Schwartz, 1971), Theorem XV.4.5)," leaving only permutation $P$ and scalar $C$; normalization $\int p(x_{m,C}|\hat w_m)\,dx_{m,C}=1$ forces $C=1$.

Theorem 2 (shared subspace): minimizes $\sum_{k\in[M]} H\big(\hat g_{x_k}^{-1}(x_k)\big)$; via block-identifiability of each $[z_m,s]$ and Lemma 1 (Yao et al., 2023), $\hat s = h_s(s)$.

Theorem 3 (component-wise): sufficient-variability vector (Eq. 33) plus sparsity $\|\hat G\|_0 \le \|G\|_0$, and the Lin (1997) identity (verbatim, [PAGE 8 of 50]): if $z_{m,i}\perp z_{m,j}\mid\{s, z\setminus\{z_{m,i},z_{m,j}\}\}$ then $\frac{\partial^2 \log p(z_m)}{\partial z_{m,i}\partial z_{m,j}} = 0$. Conclusion: $\hat z_{m,i} = h_i(z_{m,\pi(j)})$.

Estimation objective ([PAGE 8 of 50]): $L = \alpha_{\mathrm{Recon}} L_{\mathrm{Recon}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{Ind}} L_{\mathrm{Ind}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{Sp}} L_{\mathrm{Sp}}$, with $L_{\mathrm{Recon}}=\sum_m\sum_k \|x_{m,k}-\hat x_{m,k}\|_2^2$, $L_{\mathrm{Ind}}=\mathrm{KL}(p(\gamma)\,\|\,N(0,I))$, $L_{\mathrm{Sp}}=\|\hat A\|_1$.

Units. These are dimensionless/information-theoretic, not physical. $R^2$ and MCC are dimensionless coefficients; embedding dimensionalities are $d=1024$ (ImageBind) and $d=3584$ (gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct) per [PAGE 4 of 50]. Entropy terms in Theorem 2 carry nats/bits if a base is fixed; the KL term in $L_{\mathrm{Ind}}$ is in nats.

Breakdown/singularity regimes: (a) additive-homoskedastic noise voids Assumption iii; (b) $<3$ measurements voids Theorem 1's premise (AthlePersona images, [PAGE 27 of 50]); (c) non-injective operators void Assumption ii; (d) insufficient variability in $s$ voids Theorem 3.i; (e) $\|\hat G\|_0 > \|G\|_0$ voids Theorem 3.ii.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
(Reinterpreted as intellectual lineage.) The work sits at the confluence of three streams. First, causal representation learning: Schölkopf et al. (2021) "Toward Causal Representation Learning" frames the problem; nonlinear ICA (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020, iVAE) supplies the auxiliary-variable identifiability that Assumption iii/iv operationalize. Second, multimodal/multi-view CRL: Von Kügelgen et al. (2021), Daunhawer et al. (2023), Sturma et al. (2023), and especially Yao et al. (2023) — from which Lemma 1 is lifted verbatim — and Sun et al. (2025, MMCRL) used as the primary baseline. The paper explicitly "unifies and extends" Yao et al. (2023) and Sun et al. (2025). Third, the nonparametric identification toolbox: Hu & Schennach (2008) on measurement-error models furnishes the linear/diagonal operator formalism (Definitions 1–2), continued by Fu et al. (2025) and Li et al. (2025b, hierarchical temporal CRL). Lin (1997) is the linchpin connecting conditional independence to vanishing cross-partial derivatives. On the psychology side the lineage runs Cattell 16PF → Eysenck EPQ → MBTI → Goldberg (1993) Big Five, then LLM-personality (Serapio-García et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2023).

Reinterpreted "multi-week traps" (methodological pitfalls the lineage exposes): (1) the self-report fallacy — equating measured personality with outward behavior traits, which the authors deliberately separate ([PAGE 1 of 50]); (2) the invertibility trap — Yao et al. (2023) assume invertible measurement processes, and this paper's contribution is precisely to relax that; (3) the correlation-causation trap — the five independence tests (KCI, RCIT, HSIC, Chi-square, G-square, [PAGE 5 of 50]) establish dependence, not direction, so the causal graph must come from the CRL machinery, not the heatmaps.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
(Reinterpreted as machine theory-of-mind: the LLMs inferring human behavioral traits.) The dataset is literally an exercise in machine ToM — inferring Big Five "behavior traits" from public text. The reliability regime is quantified: the "Number-L3-Inc" prompt minimizes variance; [PAGE 33–34 of 50] report Llama-4-Maverick with "standard deviations consistently below 0.2," whereas Qwen2.5-Max is least reliable. Aggregation discards score-0 (insufficient information) then takes the median across three LLMs, rounding up ([PAGE 35 of 50]) — a robust estimator against single-model outlier drift.

The "habit regression vs counter-trap" dynamic maps onto trait- and domain-dependent reliability. [PAGE 34 of 50] finds Openness most stable (std < 0.3) while Neuroticism is dataset-dependent, "considerably higher variability in AthlePersona." This is a regression-toward-noise pattern: thinner public text for less prominent athletes ([PAGE 31 of 50], higher missing rate) pushes the inferred trait toward the neutral 2. The recursive loop appears in evaluation — context consistency and factual accuracy are scored by four *evaluator LLMs* judging the generator LLMs (Prompt 2, [PAGE 31 of 50]), a self-referential LLM-judges-LLM protocol the authors justify by cost and by LLMs having broader factual coverage than typical human raters. The counter-trap here is circularity: generator and evaluator pools overlap (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Llama-4, ChatGPT-4o appear on both sides).

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
(Reinterpreted as cross-layer consistency: where the physical/data substrate vetoes the theoretical guarantee.)
Veto 1 (data substrate vs Theorem 1): AthlePersona images are single-measurement ([PAGE 27 of 50]); Theorem 1 requires three. The physical layer vetoes direct application to AthlePersona's $Z_{1,k}$ image latents; the reported Figure 6 AthlePersona graph therefore rests on cross-modal borrowing, not the theorem.
Veto 2 (noise structure vs Assumption iii): the theorem needs heteroskedastic, $z$-dependent conditional noise. ImageBind/gte embeddings passed through invertible obfuscation are plausibly near-additive in the latent decoder; if homoskedastic, the identification claim weakens to permutation/scaling. This is not tested empirically in the paper.
Veto 3 (oracle assumption vs verifiability): $\|\hat G\|_0 \le \|G\|_0$ references the unknown true graph. No procedure verifies it on real data; it can only be assumed, so Theorem 3's guarantee on PersonaX is conditional and unfalsifiable in practice.
These three constitute the "physical veto proof": the empirical substrate (one photo, unknown noise class, unobservable true sparsity) constrains which theorem conclusions actually hold for the released datasets.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable scenarios (verifiable against the paper's own artifacts):

1. Single-measurement veto. Prediction: if AthlePersona image latents were re-estimated using ≥3 genuinely distinct photos per athlete, the density of the learned image-latent subgraph (nonzeros in $\hat A$ restricted to $Z_1$) should drop or stabilize relative to Figure 6. Falsified if edge density is unchanged — implying the single-image limitation was not the binding constraint.

2. Homoskedastic-noise veto. Prediction: regress residuals of $x_{m,B}$ on $z$ (heteroskedasticity test); if variance is constant in $z$, Assumption iii fails and per Theorem 1 the block estimate is non-unique — concretely, permuting latent components $\{ \hat z_{m,i} \}$ should leave $L_{\mathrm{Recon}}$ invariant. Falsified if reconstruction loss strictly increases under nontrivial relabeling, which would indicate the heteroskedastic signal is present.

3. Sparsity-oracle veto. Prediction: on the synthetic colored/fashion-MNIST task with a known ground-truth graph (Figure 5b), forcing a denser estimator ($\|\hat G\|_0 > \|G\|_0$) should degrade $R^2$ and MCC below the reported 0.96 / 0.92 ([PAGE 9 of 50]). Falsified if $R^2$/MCC hold at 0.96/0.92 despite denser-than-truth graphs, which would show component-wise identifiability (Theorem 3) is robust to the sparsity assumption as stated.

Each is decidable with the released code (github.com/lokali/PersonaX) and embeddings, making the theory's central conditions empirically testable rather than merely asserted.
