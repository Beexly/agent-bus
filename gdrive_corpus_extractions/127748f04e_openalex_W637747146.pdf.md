<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The system's intellectual lineage traces through several prior computer-football projects. Purdy (1977) identifies William Witzel as the developer of an initial computerized play-analysis system for the Washington Redskins in 1968, later extended to the Chicago Bears, Dallas Cowboys, San Francisco 49ers, and Atlanta Falcons ([PAGE 19]). Joe Guardino's enterprise, originally APEX and later Sports Data, Incorporated, began in the mid-1960s when Guardino interested USC's John McKay and the Los Angeles Rams' George Allen in computerized game analysis; by 1979 Sports Data offered over 300 subscriber programs running on a Datapoint Corporation 1500 minicomputer ([PAGE 19]–[PAGE 20]). Frank Ryan, a former Cleveland Browns quarterback and mathematician, developed the PROBE generalized report-generating system with Francia and Strawser, described as "one of the most sophisticated play-analysis systems developed to date" ([PAGE 19]–[PAGE 20]). Earlier dissertations by Nelson (1968) and Kerns (1970) are criticized by Covault for excessive attention to rudimentary variable selection and neglect of operable system design ([PAGE 25]–[PAGE 26]).

The decision-theoretic foundation draws on game-film epistemology: Kerns (1970) quotes Rote and Winter's The Language of Football (1966) that scouting-report conclusions "only support the images recorded on motion picture film" ([PAGE 32]). Covault adopts film as the sole provenience of data, rejecting live scouting as insufficiently objective ([PAGE 32]). Related work in optimal strategies includes Ladany and Machol's Optimal Strategies in Sports (1977), which contains chapters on the value of field position, Ivy League scoring, extra-point strategy, and computerized football play analysis ([PAGE 159]–[PAGE 161]).

Multi-week traps are embedded in the data-source architecture. The program is required to maintain analytic capability for at least twelve contests ([PAGE 32]), but the standard exchange is only the three most recent games ([PAGE 32]). Because no time-decay or recency weighting is specified in the core algorithm, a Week-3 performance is weighted identically to a Week-11 performance, creating a temporal stationarity assumption that is likely false in intercollegiate football. Score-differential weighting, suggested by Blackman (1970) and advocated by Ryan, Francia, and Strawser (1977), is listed only as a future recommendation, not as an implemented operator ([PAGE 25], [PAGE 56]–[PAGE 57]).

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The dissertation's theory of mind is recursive across three layers: crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock.

Crisis habit regression occurs when, under in-game clock pressure, the play-caller reverts to the opponent's prevailing tendencies as displayed in the composite evaluation. The overall offensive and defensive reports organize data into broad conceptual categories—eight offensive formations and twelve defensive categories ([PAGE 60]–[PAGE 61]). Because these composites reveal "an opponent's prevailing offensive and defensive systems" ([PAGE 61]), a stressed coach is likely to call the base play associated with the highest-frequency conceptual cell rather than a situation-specific adjustment.

Counter-trap is the second-order layer: the defensive staff studies the offensive strong-right report and knows whether an opponent runs a particular play toward or away from formation strength ([PAGE 76]). Because offensive plays bear the characteristic of mirror imaging—any right-side formation-play combination has a sister play to the left ([PAGE 76])—the defensive coordinator can anticipate alignment. However, the offense knows the defense is using this report. The recursive move is therefore to run the mirror-image play away from the expected strength or to insert a play adjustment (columns 26–27) that converts a run formation into a pass action, invalidating the defense's first-order reading. The dissertation notes that four adjustments change run to pass ([PAGE 39]), which is precisely the counter-trap mechanism.

Clock operates on two timescales. The preparation clock is the week-long cycle: reports are generated early in the week to allocate practice time and develop game strategy ([PAGE 22]–[PAGE 23], [PAGE 54]–[PAGE 55]). The game clock is the in-game constraint that forces rapid down-distance-field-position lookup. The system is designed to accelerate the preparation phase so that the game-clock decisions rest on a "sound aggregate statistical foundation" ([PAGE 15]). The recursion closes when the coach, under game-clock pressure, regresses to the habit formed during the preparation week, which was itself shaped by the composite report.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
