Three veto layers determine whether a sign-gestalt expectation is behaviorally expressed.

Physical layer [PAGE 1]: the stimulus must physically impinge on the receptor. If wind, rain, or acoustic masking removes the automobile horn from the auditory stream, s_horn ∉ 𝒮_presented and the sign map σ cannot be invoked. No amount of prior expectancy can compensate for absent stimulus energy.

Biomechanical layer: motor execution requires that the expected support features be physically accessible. If the rat’s expected maze turn is blocked by a barrier, the mnemonization generates an expectancy violation but the efferent complex cannot be realized. The body vetoes the gestalt.

Weather/environmental layer: occlusion, fog, or darkness removes visual components of the gestalt (the automobile itself), leaving only the auditory sign. If σ is cross-modal and one modality is vetoed, the expectation degrades from the full complex “automobile plus impatient driver” [PAGE 1] to a partial, lower-G gestalt.

Formal veto predicate: V = ∨_{l ∈ {physical, biomechanical, weather}} ¬Feasible_l(s, E). If V = true, then G = 0 and the system collapses to either null perception or uninterpreted sensation. This is a veto proof because a single layer failure is sufficient to suppress the sign-gestalt expectation, regardless of the strength of prior “enjoyed” patterns [PAGE 1].
