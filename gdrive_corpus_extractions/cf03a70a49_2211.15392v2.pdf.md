\[
\operatorname{vol}\!\left(I_1^{(n_1)}\times\cdots\times I_r^{(n_r)}\right)=(-1)^{n_1+\cdots+n_r},
\]
with [PAGE 13] stating, “Since µ(R(n)) = (−1)n, it follows that the measure µ is regular.” Integration is Euler-characteristic integration: for a Schwartz function \(\phi\) with nonzero values \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\),
\[
\int_{R(n)}\phi(x)\,dx=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i\,\chi_c(\phi^{-1}(a_i)),
\]
and examples include \(\int_{\mathbf R}\mathbf 1_{(p,q)}(x)\,dx=-1\) [PAGE 13]. Units: all counts \(D(n,m)\), dimensions, multiplicities \(\binom{n}{\ell(\lambda)}\), and Euler characteristics are dimensionless; \(t\) in Hilbert series is a formal dimensionless variable; categorical dimensions are algebraic elements of \(k\), equal to signs \(\pm1\). There are no SI units or probability units in this source.

The simple objects are \(L_\lambda\), where \(\lambda\) is a word in \(\{\bullet,\circ\}\). [PAGE 17, Definition 4.2] says: “For a weight λ of length n, we define Lλ to be the G-submodule of C(R(n)) generated by the ϕI, where I is an ordered tuple of half-open intervals of type λ.” Theorem 4.3 [PAGE 17] proves that \(L_\lambda\) is simple and \(\operatorname{End}_G(L_\lambda)=k\). The Schwartz-space decomposition [PAGE 19, Theorem 4.7] is
\[
C(\mathbf R(n))\cong\bigoplus_{\ell(\lambda)\le n}L_\lambda^{\oplus m_\lambda},
\qquad
m_\lambda=\binom{n}{\ell(\lambda)}.
\]
Consequently [PAGE 20, Corollary 4.10] the length is \(3^n\), since
\[
\sum_{s\ge0}\binom{n}{s}2^s=3^n.
\]
The categorical dimension is [PAGE 25, Corollary 5.7]
\[
\dim_{\mathrm{cat}}(L_\lambda)=(-1)^{\ell(\lambda)}.
\]
Induction from \(G(0)\cong G\times G\) satisfies [PAGE 26, Theorem 6.1]
\[
\operatorname{ind}(x,y)=x\odot[C(\mathbf R)]\odot y,
\qquad
[C(\mathbf R)]=a_\bullet+a_\circ+1,
\]
so for weights [PAGE 26, Corollary 6.2]
\[
\operatorname{ind}(a_\lambda,a_\mu)
=a_{\lambda\bullet\mu}+a_{\lambda\circ\mu}+a_{\lambda\mu}.
\]

\[
\operatorname{res}(a_\lambda)
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{\lambda[1,i]}\otimes a_{\lambda(i,n]}
+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{\lambda[1,i)}\otimes a_{\lambda(i,n]}.
\]

\[
L_\lambda\otimes L_\mu\cong
\bigoplus_{r}\bigoplus_{\nu\in P_r(\lambda,\mu)}L_\nu,
\]
where \(r\) runs over \((n,m)\)-ruffles and each neutral collision `?` is replaced by \(\bullet\), \(\circ\), or the empty string. The Adams operations are trivial [PAGE 41, Theorem 8.2]:
\[
\psi^i=\operatorname{id}_K\quad\text{for all }i\ge1.
\]

\[
[p_1]\circ[p_2]=\sum_{p_3\in\Gamma(n,\ell)}\epsilon(p_1,p_2,p_3)[p_3],
\]
where \(\epsilon(p_1,p_2,p_3)=(-1)^{\ell(q)+\ell(p_3)}\) if there is a unique three-dimensional Delannoy path \(q\) projecting to \(p_1,p_2,p_3\), and \(0\) otherwise. Theorem 9.7 [PAGE 47] identifies \(D\) with the full subcategory spanned by the \(C(\mathbf R(n))\)’s.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The reader-model audit has three failure modes. “Crisis habit regression” is what happens when a proof becomes non-obvious and the reader falls back to finite-group character theory: one mentally replaces \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf R,\lt)\) by a finite symmetric group, assumes ordinary Maschke and ordinary tensor products, and then misreads \(C(X)\otimes C(Y)\) as \(C(X)\otimes_k C(Y)\). [PAGE 10, Remark 2.8] directly vetoes this: “the forgetful functor from Rep(G) to k-modules is not monoidal.” The counter-trap is over-correction: having learned that finite intuition fails, the reader refuses all finite-group analogies and misses that [PAGE 25, Corollary 5.7] still gives categorical dimension \(\pm1\), and that [PAGE 41] makes \(K\) binomial. The clock is theorem-dependency order: first the principal measure [PAGE 13], then orbit classification [PAGE 14], then simple construction and decomposition [PAGE 17–20], then dimension [PAGE 25], then induction/restriction [PAGE 26–30], then tensor/Adams [PAGE 32–43], then path equivalence [PAGE 45–47]. A reader who jumps to Adams triviality without the primitive-element classification [PAGE 31, Proposition 6.15] will not see why \(\psi^i(a_\lambda)-a_\lambda\) must vanish. A reader who jumps to tensor products without the induction/restriction formulas will not see why the projection formula pins down the product uniquely [PAGE 35, Proposition 7.9]. The social-theory-of-mind layer is that the authors repeatedly flag “remarkable” or “special” features—\(\pm1\) dimensions, trivial Adams operations, semisimplicity in all characteristics—precisely to warn against default expectations. [PAGE 4] says “It is a remarkable feature ... that every simple object has dimension ±1,” and [PAGE 41] says “this is an extremely special property.” The correct recursive model is therefore: trust the stated hypotheses, track characteristic and measure regularity, and do not import unstated compactness, finiteness, or moderate-growth assumptions.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
There is no physical, biomechanical, or weather layer in the source; the admissible layers are algebraic, combinatorial, representation-theoretic, and characteristic-theoretic. Any claim that imports physical forces, biological mechanics, or meteorological variables is vetoed as source-external. The internal veto proof is as follows. Layer A, measure: the principal measure is regular [PAGE 13], so the Schwartz-space and semisimplicity machinery is licensed. Layer B, orbit combinatorics: [PAGE 14, Proposition 3.5] gives a bijection between \(G\)-orbits on \(R(n)\times R(m)\) and Delannoy paths, so the Hom-space dimension is exactly \(D(n,m)\). Layer C, simple decomposition: [PAGE 19, Theorem 4.7] forces the multiplicity \(m_\lambda=\binom{n}{\ell(\lambda)}\); the counting identity \(\sum_s 2^s\binom{n}{s}^2=D(n)\) is not an extra assumption but a consistency check. Layer D, categorical dimension: [PAGE 25, Corollary 5.7] gives \((-1)^{\ell(\lambda)}\), and [PAGE 29, Example 6.11] independently recovers the same sign from restriction, so the dimension layer does not conflict with the restriction layer. Layer E, tensor: [PAGE 32, Theorem 7.2] and [PAGE 36–39, Proposition 7.14] prove the ruffle product satisfies the projection formula, and [PAGE 35, Proposition 7.9] proves uniqueness under commutativity, unit, projection, and the \(\pi(n)\)-multiplication condition. Layer F, Adams: [PAGE 43] proves triviality by induction on filtration, using the primitive-element classification [PAGE 31, Proposition 6.15] and the \(S_{a,b}\)-closure lemmas [PAGE 43, Lemmas 8.8–8.9]. The only “singular” adjudications are formal: \(t=1\) in Hilbert series denominators [PAGE 27]; non-quasi-regular measures [PAGE 13, Remark 3.4]; and positive-characteristic \(p\)-adic dimension [PAGE 25, Remark 5.8]. None is a physical veto; they are algebraic parameter regimes. The cross-layer verdict is therefore: no external empirical veto is admissible, and the internal algebraic vetoes are mutually consistent.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
RLVR veto 1: Counterfactual claim \( \operatorname{length} C(\mathbf R(n))=2^n \). Verifiable prediction: the claimed length contradicts [PAGE 20, Corollary 4.10], which proves length \(3^n\). The verification is arithmetic: there are \(2^s\) weights of length \(s\), each appears with multiplicity \(\binom{n}{s}\) in [PAGE 19, Theorem 4.7], so the total is \(\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}2^s=3^n\). A model that outputs \(2^n\) should be vetoed; a model that outputs \(3^n\) and cites Corollary 4.10 passes.

RLVR veto 2: Counterfactual claim “the tensor product is an ordinary shuffle product with no collision terms,” i.e. \(a_\bullet\cdot a_{\bullet\circ}=a_{\bullet\bullet\circ}+a_{\bullet\circ\bullet}+a_{\bullet\circ\bullet?}\) or only shuffle terms. Verifiable prediction: [PAGE 32, Example 7.1] gives
\[
m'(a_\bullet,a_{\bullet\circ})
=2a_{\bullet\bullet\circ}+a_{\bullet\circ\bullet}+2a_{\bullet\circ}+a_{\bullet\bullet}+a_\bullet,
\]
and [PAGE 32, Example 7.4] gives
\[
L_\bullet\otimes L_{\bullet\circ}
\cong L_{\bullet\bullet\circ}^{\oplus2}\oplus L_{\bullet\circ\bullet}
\oplus L_{\bullet\circ}^{\oplus2}\oplus L_{\bullet\bullet}\oplus L_\bullet.
\]
A no-collision claim predicts fewer summands and is vetoed by the neutral-collision replacement rule: each `?` is replaced by \(\bullet\), \(\circ\), or the empty string.

RLVR veto 3: Counterfactual claim “Adams operations are nontrivial on \(K\)” or “\(\psi^2(a_\bullet)\neq a_\bullet\).” Verifiable prediction: [PAGE 41, Theorem 8.2] states “The Adams operations are trivial, that is, ψi is the identity for all i,” and [PAGE 42, Corollary 8.6] gives \(\psi^i(a_\bullet)=a_\bullet\) and \(\psi^i(a_\circ)=a_\circ\) for all \(i\ge1\). A check can compute \(\psi^2(a_\bullet)-a_\bullet\) and must obtain \(0\). If a model instead invokes Czenky’s positive-characteristic symmetric-fusion result from [PAGE 5], that is a category-mismatch veto: Czenky’s theorem concerns nontrivial symmetric fusion categories in characteristic not \(2\), not the Delannoy category, whose Adams operations are explicitly trivial [PAGE 41]. A fourth auxiliary check would be the restriction formula: any claim that omits the second sum in [PAGE 29, Theorem 6.9] predicts a wrong co-unit and fails the Proposition 6.12 computation [PAGE 30].
