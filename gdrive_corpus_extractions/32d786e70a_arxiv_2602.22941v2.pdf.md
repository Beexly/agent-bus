9) Reproducibility metrics: $\text{RMSE}=\sqrt{\tfrac1M\sum(v_{\text{est}}-v_{\text{gps}})^2}$ [m/s]; $\text{MAPE}=\tfrac1M\sum|v_{\text{est}}-v_{\text{gps}}|/v_{\text{gps}}$ [dimensionless]; Spearman $\rho$ = rank correlation.

10) Boat-measurement validation (relative offset difference) [PAGE 13]:
$$\varepsilon=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\lVert o^{[1]}_{j,k}-o^{[2]}_{j,k}\rVert}{\tfrac12\lVert o^{[1]}_{j,k}+o^{[2]}_{j,k}\rVert}\quad(\text{dimensionless; median }2.65\%,\text{mean }2.92\%).$$

11) Perspective-spacing bias (signed mean over pairs) [PAGE 13]:
$$E=\frac{2!\,(N-2)!}{N!}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\big(\bar d_{n,m}-d_{n,m}\big),\quad \bar d_{n,m}=\lVert a_{i,j,m}-a_{i,j,n}\rVert,\ d_{n,m}=\lVert o_{j,m}-o_{j,n}\rVert.$$
Coefficient $\dfrac{2!(N-2)!}{N!}=\binom{N}{2}^{-1}$ = mean over unordered pairs; signed ⇒ exposes the systematic negative shift reported on [PAGE 16].

Reported operating points: U-Net median error 1.72 px, Acc@5 = 90.8% [PAGE 14/15]; perspective median $|E|\le0.6$ m [PAGE 16]; velocity "All" RMSE 0.078 m/s, MAPE 0.011, $\rho=0.974$ (Table 4 [PAGE 17]); stroke ViTPose RMSE 1.753 bpm, MAPE 0.009, $\rho=0.975$ vs BBox 6.467 bpm, $\rho=0.807$ (Table 5 [PAGE 18]); runtimes: ViTPose 1179 s, BBox 411 s, homography 2243 s, velocity 537 s [PAGE 20].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from photogrammetric biomechanics to learned keypoint geometry. Plagenhoef [11] (1979) and Mann & Kearney [12] (1980) established slow-motion cinematography as the reference kinematic extractor for flatwater paddling; Michael et al. [13] (2009) consolidated determinants of kayak performance; McDonnell et al. [14] (2012) formalized an observational phase model from multi-camera video. A parallel sensor branch — Fernandes et al. [1] (GPS+accelerometer validation), Romagnoli et al. [15] (2D video + instrumented paddle + GPS for propulsive force) — supplied the ground-truth epistemics this paper inherits. The deep-learning branch (von Braun et al. [16], 2020, Mask R-CNN waterline; Bonaiuto et al. [17], 2022; Najlaoui et al. [18], 2024, monocular 3-D paddle) plus generic vision backbones (YOLOv8 [3], ViTPose [4], ResNet-18, U-Net, Lucas-Kanade [24]) are the substrate. The direct ancestor is Matthes et al. [2] (2025): single-person K1/C1, fixed camera, static 2.4 m offset, velocity-only. This work's "retro-causal" contribution is recognizing that the ancestor's static offset is the epistemic bottleneck and replacing it with a learned, per-seat calibration — and adding the stroke-rate dimension the ancestor lacked. The team is genealogically coherent (Ziegler/Matthes/Frenzel/Fuchs also author AuxFlow [10], 2026, an anchor-grounded homography method for soccer), so the homography machinery is a house specialty, not a graft.

Multi-week traps embedded in the lineage: (1) The start-phase GPS error. [PAGE 22] attributes first-25–50 m discrepancies to "positional lag and sampling jitter" of GPS under explosive acceleration, "documented in previous validation studies [1]." A naïve analyst treating GPS as unimpeachable would misattribute the vision system's superior start behavior as error — a multi-week mis-tuning if one tried to "correct" video toward GPS at the start. (2) The perspective-bias mirage: the systematic negative spacing shift ([PAGE 16]) looks like calibration decay but is a geometric artifact of panning; chasing it as a calibration bug wastes weeks when the correct fix is temporal-smoothness/joint-homography optimization ([PAGE 23]). (3) The occlusion bands: poles occlude athletes near ~120 m and ~320 m ([PAGE 19], Fig 11), which appear in velocity-space as error at ~-380 m and ~-180 m — the sign flip between "race distance" and "distance-to-finish" is itself a multi-week trap for anyone aligning profiles.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the coach as a bounded-rational agent facing a decision under three competing pressures: entrenched habit, a counter-trap, and the wall-clock of competition feedback.

Habit regression (baseline prior): the elite coaching prior is that GPS/gyro is "gold standard" ([PAGE 1]) and that panned/zoomed side-view video is "prohibitively labour-intensive" to reconstruct manually ([PAGE 2]). A coach under crisis (mid-regatta, heats minutes apart) defaults to what is trusted and fast, i.e., ignoring video telemetry or hand-logging stroke counts. The system's value proposition is precisely to convert the discarded broadcast/coach footage into actionable pacing feedback "without requiring sensors" ([PAGE 1]).

Counter-trap (the seductive wrong move): adopting the automated pipeline but over-trusting it in exactly the regime where its ground truth is weakest — the start. Because [PAGE 22] shows GPS itself is unreliable in the first 25–50 m, a coach who "corrects" the video profile toward GPS there injects GPS error into an otherwise-clean estimate. Recursive modeling: the coach believes the sensor is truth; the sensor's failure mode is invisible at the timescales of coaching; therefore the coach's belief-update is systematically mis-calibrated at race onset. The counter-move is to trust the video system's temporal consistency over GPS during the acceleration phase.

Clock (the operative constraint): the paper exposes an explicit causality/accuracy frontier. Non-causal optical-flow interpolation is best (RMSE 0.095 m/s, $\rho=0.982$) but "needs to be kept in mind for real-time applications" ([PAGE 21/22]); the causal variant degrades to RMSE 0.129 m/s ($\rho=0.968$) (Table 3 [PAGE 16]). ViTPose stroke rate costs 1179 s vs 411 s for BBox, a 187% stroke-stage and 24% end-to-end increase ([PAGE 22]) — the accuracy/time trade is quantified, not hand-waved. So a coach choosing feedback latency $T$ is choosing a point on this frontier: post-race analysis buys the non-causal optimum; live in-race feedback forces the causal variant and possibly the BBox stroke signal. The theory-of-mind insight: the system's designers anticipated the coach's clock and shipped both a high-accuracy offline mode and a low-resource near-real-time mode; the coach's rationality is to match mode to decision latency rather than to demand one universal setting.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer vetoes (can invalidate the whole metric extraction):
- Water-surface planarity. Eq (1) is a plane-induced homography. Waves violate the single-plane assumption; out-of-plane surface displacement $\zeta$ produces a metric error scaling with $\zeta$ and the camera obliquity. The paper mitigates by fitting over many buoys and temporal smoothing ([PAGE 22]) but cannot veto a genuinely rough water surface. Veto proof: if significant wave height approaches the buoy-grid spacing scale, homography residuals grow and RANSAC inlier ratio collapses.
- Buoy rigidity. The method "is based on a strong assumption of the buoy-based scene geometry" ([PAGE 22]); buoy drift from current/wind violates the rigid grid, biasing $H_i$. Marginalized spatially (many buoys) and temporally (smoothing), not eliminated.

Biomechanical layer vetoes:
- Fundamental-frequency assumption for stroke rate. Peak-picking assumes one dominant periodicity. Canoe (single-blade, ~600 ms) vs kayak (double-blade, ~300 ms) differ by design; the tolerance windows encode this. A stroke with strong intra-cycle sub-peaks (e.g., distinct catch/exit motions producing two brightness/pose extrema) would double-count and violate $f_{s,\max}$. ViTPose's shoulder–wrist Euclidean signal is more robust to this than the single-region brightness signal, consistent with BBox's poor $\rho=0.807$ (Table 5).
- Self-occlusion in crew boats. Multi-athlete overlap breaks the "bottom-center of bbox = athlete position" proxy and can defeat athlete ordering; the optical-flow ordering logic ([PAGE 10]) is the countermeasure, but flow itself is occlusion-fragile.

Optical/information layer vetoes:
- Lucas-Kanade brightness constancy + small-motion + aperture assumptions ([PAGE 24]). Spray, specular reflection off water, and 4K motion blur at 25 fps attack exactly these. The authors concede "Optical flow may fail under severe occlusions, and pose estimation can degrade under motion blur" ([PAGE 22]) but argue these occupy "insignificant timeframes."
- Structured occlusion (poles at ~120 m/320 m) is not insignificant — it produced measurable error bands ([PAGE 19]).

Weather/lighting: illumination changes (clouds, low sun angle, glare) degrade both the brightness-based stroke signal and LK flow; rain/spray increases false YOLO detections. No explicit veto handling is reported beyond temporal interpolation. Net adjudication: no single physical veto falsifies the velocity claims (they are empirically anchored to GPS with MAPE 0.011 across all classes), but the stroke-rate claims are more veto-exposed under occlusion and illumination change, which is consistent with the reported larger variance.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three falsifiable scenarios with directional predictions, verifiable against the released metric structure.

Veto 1 — Extreme pan / rapid perspective change. The bias-cancellation argument ([PAGE 21]) holds only if the offset bias is locally stationary. Counterfactual: capture a race with fast, non-uniform camera panning so the bias bandwidth overlaps the velocity signal. Prediction: velocity MAPE rises above the 0.011 baseline and, more diagnostically, a low-frequency artifact appears in $v_j(t)$ near the pan-frequency; the signed spacing error $E$ (Eq 11) develops abrupt (non-gradual) fluctuations, contradicting [PAGE 21]'s "does not exhibit abrupt fluctuations." Falsifier confirmed if high-pan footage shows MAPE $>0.02$ with spectral power at the pan rate.

Veto 2 — Prolonged front-athlete occlusion in a crew boat. The pipeline derives the multi-athlete motion signal "primarily from the front athlete" ([PAGE 12]); optical-flow interpolation fills gaps but accumulates drift. Counterfactual: occlude the lead athlete for a long, continuous interval of duration $D$. Prediction: reconstructed position error grows monotonically in $D$ (roughly $\propto D\cdot\bar\varepsilon_{\text{flow}}$), and stroke-rate error spikes over the occluded distance because the substitute (nearest detected athlete) has a different phase. Falsifier: if stroke-rate error stays at the 1.753 bpm baseline through a long front-athlete occlusion, the substitution is phase-invariant (unlikely).

Veto 3 — Unseen hull geometry / offset non-stationarity. The empirical init $o^{(N)}$ ([PAGE 8], Eq 3) plus a 5-frame calibration assumes offsets are constant over the whole race. Counterfactual: a class with flexing or rudder-steered hulls (C4 is explicitly absent from the dataset, [PAGE 22]) where the athlete-tip geometry shifts mid-race. Prediction: U-Net tip localization stays accurate (Acc@5 $\approx$ 90.8% is a per-frame image measurement), but the calibrated $o_{j,k}$ drift over distance, inflating the boat-measurement validation statistic $\varepsilon$ above the observed 2.65% median, while velocity MAPE degrades less than position (because differentiation absorbs slow drift). Falsifier confirmed if $\varepsilon$ for a flex/steer class exceeds ~5% or if velocity $\rho$ drops below 0.95 while U-Net Acc@5 is unchanged — decoupling image-localization accuracy from calibrated-offset accuracy would pinpoint the offset-stationarity assumption as the failing layer.

These three are directly measurable with the paper's own metrics (MAPE, $\rho$, $\varepsilon$, Acc@5), require no new sensors, and each isolates a distinct assumption layer (perspective stationarity, front-athlete availability, offset constancy), making them clean RLVR-style verification probes.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
