<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The page belongs to Hull’s 1943 Principles of Behavior, a neobehaviorist system built on intervening variables such as reaction potential, habit strength, and reactive inhibition. Its intellectual lineage runs through Pavlovian conditioned reflexes, Watsonian behaviorism, Thorndikian connectionism, and Tolmanian purposive behavior, but Hull’s distinctive move is to make stimulus patterning a deductive consequence of generalization gradients plus perseverative traces rather than a primitive associative principle. The page’s claim that “the process of genuine stimulus patterning thus turns out to be at bottom the learning to discriminate afferent interaction effects” [PAGE 1] anticipates later configural and occasion-setting accounts: Rescorla-Wagner elemental error correction, Pearce’s configural cueing, and SOP representations of stimulus compounds all formalize the idea that a compound is not merely the sum of its independently conditioned elements. Hull’s “perseverative stimulus trace” [PAGE 1] is a direct ancestor of trace-decay models in temporal conditioning, short-term habituation/dishabituation, and memory consolidation literatures.

Multi-week traps arise because the model assumes stationarity of Γ, τ_p, r_i, and θ across sessions. Over weeks, extinction of components can generate inhibitory gradients that generalize back to the compound; spontaneous recovery can reinstate r_i after apparent extinction; context renewal can shift d_{iC} and effectively change λ; and reinforcer devaluation or motivational shifts change the mapping from reaction potential to overt response. Quasi or spontaneous patterning [PAGE 1] is especially dangerous across multi-week designs: if afferent interaction effects are strong, the model predicts patterning even without explicit differential reinforcement, so a control group exposed to unpaired compounds may show apparent discrimination that is actually artifactual overlap. Positive-over-negative asymmetry and simultaneous-over-temporal asymmetry [PAGE 1] create another trap: an experimenter who masters simultaneous positive patterning in week one may fail with temporal negative patterning in week four not because the subject forgot, but because τ approached or exceeded τ_p and the perseverative overlap O(τ) fell below the interaction threshold. These are failure modes of the Hullian deductive system, not probabilistic guarantees about future sessions.

The recursive theory-of-mind problem here involves two agents modeling each other’s contingency structure: the subject models the experimenter’s reinforcement program, and the experimenter models the subject’s learning curve. Crisis habit regression occurs when Δ=R_C^+−θ shrinks because inhibition generalizes back too strongly or because the perseverative trace decays between compound presentations. Under that pressure, the subject regresses to the most recently reinforced component habit rather than maintaining the compound discrimination. In Hullian terms, ρ_i^+ may remain closer to θ than R_C^+, so the isolated component recaptures control of behavior exactly when the compound should be dominant.

The counter-trap is experimental: differential reinforcement intended to produce patterning can accidentally reinforce the very inhibition that destroys it. If compound AB is reinforced while A and B are each extinguished, I_i=κr_i builds inhibition proportional to component reaction strength [PAGE 1]. When J_{i→C}=κr_iΓ(d_{iC}) is summed across many trials, repeated back-generalization can erode R_C^+ toward θ. The experimenter then observes declining compound responding and may mistakenly conclude that the subject has “lost” the discrimination, when the contingency itself has manufactured excessive inhibitory generalization. The recursive fix is to vary the reinforcement schedule so that component extinction does not produce monotonic back-generalization; for example, occasionally reinforce components in a way that resets r_i without abolishing compound control, while preserving the gradient asymmetry ρ_i<R_C [PAGE 1].

The clock is the temporal layer. The trace p_i(t)=a_i e^{−(t−t_i)/τ_p} imposes a critical window: patterning is tractable only while O(τ)=(a_1a_2τ_p/2)e^{−τ/τ_p} remains above the interaction threshold needed for Φ_res(C). Because “the longer the time interval separating the components in a temporal stimulus compound, the more difficult will be the patterning” [PAGE 1], the clock sets τ_max approximately on the order of a few τ_p. Beyond τ_max, the subject cannot bind the components into a single afferent compound, and recursion collapses to independent component models. A counter-trap against the clock is to insert a bridging stimulus or conditioned reinforcer at the interval midpoint; in Hullian terms this artificially extends effective perseveration and preserves O(τ), but it changes the compound from a two-element temporal pattern to a three-element chain, so the original patterning law no longer applies without modification.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer veto: afferent interaction requires two component volleys to arrive at a common integrator within the synaptic integration window T_int and within the perseverative trace window τ_p. Let component i travel path length L_i at conduction velocity v_i, producing arrival delay Δ_i=L_i/v_i in seconds. Effective afferent overlap is nonzero only if, for some pair (i,j), |(t_j+Δ_j)−(t_i+Δ_i)|≤T_int+τ_p and the peripheral receptors are not refractory. If this physical condition fails, the premise “the mutual interaction of afferent impulses” [PAGE 1] is false for that paradigm, and Φ=0 regardless of λ, κ, or θ. This is a hard veto: no psychological generalization gradient can create interaction effects that the nervous system never registers.

Biomechanical layer veto: Hullian reaction potential is dimensionless [0,1], but overt expression requires an effector to cross a motor threshold θ_m and produce force or movement F≥F_min within a twitch/refractory cycle. Even when R_C^+≫θ psychologically, fatigue, muscle spindle inhibition, or motor-unit refractory periods can make F<F_min. In that case the measured response rate is vetoed at the biomechanical layer, and a decline in responding cannot be attributed to loss of stimulus discrimination. The model must therefore be paired with an independent force/kinematic criterion before R_C^+−θ is interpreted as behavioral output.

Weather/environmental context layer: ambient temperature, humidity, illumination, and background odor shift receptor gain and the effective psychological distance d_{iC} between compound and components across sessions. If |d_{iC}^{(w)}−d_{iC}^{(ref)}| exceeds the gradient width 1/λ, then Γ(d_{iC}^{(w)}) differs materially from Γ(d_{iC}^{(ref)}), violating stationarity of ρ_i=Γ(d_{iC})R_C across weeks. A compound that discriminates in a cool, quiet session may fail in a warm, odorous session not because learning changed but because the gradient itself moved. The veto rule is: re-estimate d_{iC} and λ under each ambient condition; do not transfer patterning parameters across environmental shifts without a gradient re-measurement.

Adjudication: the Hullian patterning claim survives only if (1) physical overlap O_phys>0, (2) biomechanical expression F≥F_min, and (3) environmental stationarity or explicit re-estimation of Γ holds. If any one layer fails, the psychological equation R_C^+=R_C−Σ_iκr_iΓ(d_{iC})+Φ_res(C) is formally intact but empirically vacuous for that condition.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1 — Flat-gradient veto. Counterfactual: independently measure the excitation/inhibition generalization gradient from compound C to probes near its components. If the measured gradient is flat over the tested range (λ≈0 within experimental error), then Γ(d_{iC})≈Γ_0 and ρ_i≈R_C, violating the source premise that the component’s potential “is less when it is acting separately than when in the compound” [PAGE 1]. Verifiable prediction: no discrimination margin Δ will emerge; response rates to C and to its isolated components will be statistically indistinguishable after equating overall reinforcement. RLVR veto condition: if P(response|C)−P(response|component)≈0 while the model requires Δ>0, veto the gradient-based patterning account for that subject.

Scenario 2 — Temporal-trace veto. Counterfactual: train a temporal compound A→B with interval τ, then test at τ′≫5τ_p where τ_p is estimated from separate trace-decay or spontaneous-recovery probes. The model gives O(τ′)=(a_Aa_Bτ_p/2)e^{−τ′/τ_p}<0.007(a_Aa_Bτ_p/2), so afferent interaction is functionally null and M(τ′)≈baseline. Verifiable prediction: significant above-baseline patterning at τ′≫τ_p falsifies the perseverative-trace postulate as stated unless a non-Hullian bridge stimulus is present. RLVR veto condition: if patterning persists at τ′>5τ_p with no marker stimulus and no change in sensory modality, the “longer interval = more difficult” law [PAGE 1] is rejected for that paradigm; response should equal the sum of independent component responses.

Scenario 3 — Negative-patterning counter-trap / inhibitory-gradient veto. Counterfactual: run differential reinforcement with compound AB+ and components A−, B−, but measure separate excitation and inhibition gradients. The source assumes the same downward gradient for excitation and inhibition [PAGE 1] and predicts positive patterning easier than negative. If inhibition generalizes more steeply or more broadly than excitation (λ_inh≠λ_exc), then J_{i→C}=κr_iΓ_inh(d_{iC}) may not be “much less” than the original compound potential; the net loss can exceed R_C−θ and drive R_C^+ below threshold while isolated components are also suppressed. Verifiable prediction: under steep inhibitory back-generalization, negative patterning becomes easier than positive patterning or compound responding collapses entirely, contradicting the source ordering unless the boundary condition λ_inh≥λ_exc and κ small holds. RLVR veto condition: if measured Γ_inh(d)>Γ_exc(d) at the relevant d_{iC} and compound response falls below component response after differential reinforcement, veto the positive-easier-than-negative deduction.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
