Counter-trap. Domestic-league victories create a counter-trap: Fenerbahçe fans are elated, but supporters of the defeated domestic rival are demoralized, and “the low productivity of those fans could cancel out the high productivity of Fenerbahçe fans” [PAGE 15]. This offsetting mechanism explains why domestic wins are statistically insignificant across Specifications 2 and 7 [PAGE 12, 16]. The counter-trap is avoided in European cups because Turkish non-supporters tend to back Fenerbahçe against foreign opponents; the match becomes an object of national pride and solidarity rather than inter-tribal conflict [PAGE 4, 16]. The recursive theory of mind thus distinguishes between in-group bounded euphoria (domestic, canceled by out-group dysphoria) and bounded national euphoria (European, additive across the society).

The transfer function treats each month’s win count as an exogenous intervention, but mood effects documented by Sloan (1979) and Schwarz et al. (1987) are typically short-lived post-game phenomena [PAGE 4]. If the mood boost decays within days, its aggregation into monthly industrial growth is already attenuated by the time the index is observed. Conversely, the AR(5) lag structure may falsely attribute persistence to football when it reflects ordinary industrial inventory cycles. The clock mismatch biases the estimated transfer function toward a diffuse, low-frequency coefficient rather than a sharp impulse response.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and weather veto layers.

Physical layer. The dependent variable is aggregate industrial production compiled from 913 firms and 403 manufactured staples, covering nearly 73% of Turkish industrial establishments [PAGE 9]. The model does not include player physiology, injury reports, or match-minute load because the hypothesized transmission is sociological: worker mood, not athlete output, is the mechanism [PAGE 2–7]. A veto at the physical layer would require evidence that player fitness or squad rotation directly alters factory output; no such channel is specified or identified.

Biomechanical layer. Workers on the shop floor are not the football players, so individual biomechanics (sprint load, contact injuries, recovery time) are irrelevant to the macroeconomic dependent variable. The only conceivable biomechanical leakage is if match-day congestion or public-viewing gatherings altered worker attendance or shift timing, but absenteeism is treated as a mood-mediated outcome, not a biomechanical constraint [PAGE 6].

Weather layer. Weather does not appear in the specification. However, weather-driven postponements could violate the game-to-month recording convention [PAGE 9]: a European-cup match postponed from late month t to early month t+1 would be counted in the wrong industrial observation, mis-measuring Z_t and attenuating c_j toward zero. Likewise, extreme weather suppressing stadium attendance could weaken the BIRGing mood signal even when Fenerbahçe wins, decoupling the win count from the morale proxy. These are not modeled and represent measurement-error vetoes rather than structural confounds.

Veto adjudication. Because the outcome is an economy-wide index, idiosyncratic physical or weather shocks to individual matches are averaged out unless they systematically bias the monthly win count across many months. The veto layers are therefore only partially binding: the psychological transmission is insulated from player biomechanics, but exposed to calendar-weather misalignment of the regressor. The result is robust to physical-layer objections but fragile to regressor-timing misclassification.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Three verifiable reinforcement-learning verifiable reward (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Null European-fixture months. Restrict the sample to months in which Fenerbahçe played zero European-cup games. Under the transfer-function hypothesis, \(Z^{Europe}_t = 0\) in those months, so no identified football transfer should exist. Verification: re-estimate Eq. (3) [PAGE 10] on the sub-sample of European-cup-null months and confirm the estimated coefficient on a synthetic win count is statistically indistinguishable from zero. Prediction: any apparent significance in the full sample is driven by the sparse months with European fixtures, not by a continuous monthly mood trend.

Scenario 2 — Domestic rivalry offset. Construct a counterfactual matchweek in which Fenerbahçe wins a domestic league match while archrival Galatasaray loses its own domestic match the same week. The crowding-out counter-trap predicts that rival-fan dysphoria cancels Fenerbahçefan euphoria, leaving aggregate industrial growth unaffected. Verification: interact \(W^{Turkey}_t\) with a dummy for same-week rival loss; the interaction should be negative and the main \(W^{Turkey}\) term should remain insignificant, consistent with Specification 2 (\(c=0.032\), t=1.392, n.s.) [PAGE 12] and Specification 7 [PAGE 16]. Prediction: domestic wins only predict industrial growth when rival fans are not simultaneously defeated.

Scenario 3 — Turkish national team versus club team. Replace club win counts with Turkish national team win counts in international competition. Because national-team success generates national pride without deep club identification and involves far fewer matches [PAGE 17–18], the mood-to-productivity channel should be statistically weak or absent. Verification: estimate Eq. (3) with national-team wins as \(Z_t\); the coefficient should be insignificant, mirroring the paper’s null finding for the national team [PAGE 17–18]. Prediction: club identification, not abstract national pride, is the operative psychological variable; national-team wins fail the veto test.
