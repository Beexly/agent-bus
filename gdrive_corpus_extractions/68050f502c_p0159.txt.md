Berkeley, in An Essay Towards a New Theory of Vision (1709), argued that visible figures and distances are not intrinsic to retinal images but are learned signs conditioned by tactile experience. James, writing in 1890, radicalizes the empiricist side: not only is space known through experience, but the relation itself is a sensible patch, continuous with its terms. This anticipates radical empiricism and the later pragmatist refusal to posit unobservable relational entities.

Multi-week traps. In the weeks after a close reading of [PAGE 1], a reader tends to regress into one of three stable errors. Trap 1: nativist collapse. Because James denies that position is a sensation, the reader infers that spatial structure must be innate or “in the mind,” effectively reinstating Kantian apriorism despite James’s empiricist machinery. Trap 2: associationist atomism. The reader attempts to rebuild space from discrete sensations of points, omitting the interpolated line-patch \lambda_{ab}; this reproduces the very “pure sensibility” breakdown James diagnoses at the top of [PAGE 1]. Trap 3: intellectualist mysticism. Seizing on James’s closing line—“Thought is an adorable but unfathomable mystery”—the reader treats relating thought as an ontological primitive rather than a functional operation to be analyzed, surrendering the explanatory ground gained in the middle of the page. James’s escape route is to hold the middle: relations are real, sensible, and empirically traceable, without reducing them to atomic sensations or hypostatizing Thought as mystery. These traps are “retrocausal” in the sense that prior commitments (nativism, associationism, intellectualism) reach backward into the reading of [PAGE 1] and distort the extraction of its operator \mathcal{D}.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock dynamics for the cognitive agent modeling its own spatial relations.

Crisis habit regression. Under uncertainty, load, or sensory deprivation, the mind regresses to prepotent habits of spatial cognition. The primary regression identified from [PAGE 1] is the reification of position as an intrinsic sensation: the subject behaves as if a retinal patch “carries” its own coordinates, violating James’s thesis that position obtains only “between a spot… and extraneous œ-ordinates.” A secondary regression is the relation-as-mystery habit: when the interpolated line \lambda_{ab} is unavailable (darkness, occlusion), the subject invokes an unfathomable “sense of direction” rather than admitting the breakdown of the patch operator.

Counter-trap. The counter-trap is to operationalize \mathcal{D}(a,b) as an observable sensory trace rather than an occult faculty. If direction is literally “the sensation of the line that joins the two points together” [PAGE 1], then the line must be traceable in behavior: eye movements, saccadic trajectories, smooth pursuit, or manual pointing should interpolate between relata. The counter-trap fails only when behavior exhibits directional judgment in the absence of any interpolated trace; that outcome falsifies the strong identity claim “the line is the relation.”

Clock. The relevant temporal units are seconds (SI base unit time). Saccadic latency is on the order of 200 milliseconds; saccadic suppression renders the visual field effectively blank during rapid eye movements of 20–50 milliseconds. The construction of \lambda_{ab} across a saccade therefore requires trans-saccadic memory binding within a window of roughly 100–300 milliseconds. If the temporal binding window exceeds this, the “line” between successive fixations is not phenomenally continuous, and James’s continuity assumption breaks down. The clock does not determine the truth of the spatial relation, but it sets the breakdown regime: below the critical integration interval, the relation is available as a patch; above it, the relation must be reconstructed inferentially, ceasing to be “a fact of the same order” as the relata.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof for James’s spatial-relation operator \mathcal{D} from [PAGE 1].

Physical layer (retinal/optical). The relata p_i are patches on the retina. Minimum resolvable separation is set by photoreceptor spacing and diffraction, roughly 1 arcminute (\prime) at the fovea under high luminance. If two points subtend less than this angle, the patches p_a and p_b cannot be resolved as distinct, so the operator \mathcal{D}(a,b) has no well-defined pair of relata; the system vetoes at the physical aliasing limit. Units: arcminutes (angular), radians (SI dimensionless for phase/geometry).

Biomechanical layer (oculomotor). The line-sensation \lambda_{ab} presupposes an oculomotor trajectory or attentional sweep between a and b. Extraocular muscle plant dynamics impose a minimum saccade duration (~20 ms) and peak velocity (~500–700°/s). If the demanded relation spans a distance requiring a movement faster than the plant’s maximum velocity, the “line” cannot be executed as a single sensory-motor patch; the biomechanical layer vetoes continuity.

Environmental/weather layer (visual field conditions). “Weather” here denotes the atmospheric and occlusion structure of the visual field: luminance contrast, scattering, fog, glare, and intervening occluders. If contrast between relata and background falls below threshold (~1% Weber contrast for large fields), the patches p_i are not available as sensible data, and \lambda_{ab} cannot be instantiated. An occluder interrupting \overline{ab} splits the putative line into disjoint patches, violating the interpolated-patch requirement of [PAGE 1].

Veto adjudication rule. James’s thesis holds only when all three layers pass simultaneously: (1) angular separation > resolution limit; (2) oculomotor trajectory within plant velocity limits; (3) contrast and occlusion permit a continuous patch between relata. Exact singularity: when inter-patch distance approaches the foveal resolution limit (~1 arcminute) while ambient illumination falls below mesopic thresholds, relatum and relation become phenomenally indistinguishable; the operator \mathcal{D} ceases to be a distinct relation and collapses into the relatum itself, invalidating the “facts of the same order” claim at that scale.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable Reinforcement-Learning-from-Verifiable-Rewards (RLVR) counterfactual scenarios, each with a prediction that vetoes or preserves James’s operator \mathcal{D} from [PAGE 1]. Rewards are binary verification signals (pass/fail); no odds, spreads, or betting lines are referenced.

Scenario 1: Dark-field direction judgment. Verifiable setup: two luminous points are presented in total darkness; the subject reports the direction from point A to point B. RLVR reward condition: pass if a continuous line sensation \lambda_{ab} is reported between the points. Prediction: subjects report direction without experiencing any intervening line patch; no traceable sensory \lambda_{ab} exists in the dark field. Veto outcome: strong veto of James’s claim “nor can you in any conceivable way think the latter except by imagining the former” [PAGE 1]; direction is computed relationally without a same-order line sensation.

Scenario 2: Extra-field separation. Verifiable setup: point A is foveated; point B is placed 160° eccentric in the peripheral field, beyond the region where a single continuous visual patch can join them without traversing the blind spot or leaving the circle of vision. RLVR reward condition: pass if the subject experiences one interpolated patch \lambda_{ab} within the circle of vision. Prediction: no single patch connects A and B; the relation is constructed from separate foveal and peripheral representations rather than a between-patch sensation. Veto outcome: falsifies the patch-between-patches thesis “the former are certain other patches between them” [PAGE 1] for relations spanning large sectors of the visual field.

Scenario 3: Phi/apparent motion. Verifiable setup: two brief flashes are presented at positions A and B separated by an inter-stimulus interval of 50–200 ms, producing apparent motion (the phi phenomenon). RLVR reward condition: pass if the subject perceives a continuous line sensation traversing the space between flashes during the interval. Prediction: subjects perceive motion/direction without any continuous intermediate line patch; only discrete flashes are sensed. Veto outcome: refutes the necessity of a continuous interpolated sensation \lambda_{ab} for directional relation, showing that \mathcal{D}(a,b) can be present while the “line” is absent. Each scenario is verifiable via eye-tracking, verbal report, or forced-choice verification; failure of the pass condition in any one scenario constitutes a counterfactual veto of the corresponding clause in James’s spatial ontology from [PAGE 1].
