Multi-week traps. In systems that track volatility (Glicko-2, TopCoder), a player can “farm” volatility by alternating strong and weak performances over weeks, then exert honest maximum performance to achieve a rating exceeding what honest play would yield [PAGE 1, refs. 3, 14]. This is a temporal arbitrage: short-term rating loss is converted into long-term amplification. Elo-MMR closes this loop because Theorem 5.5 [PAGE 7] proves monotonicity in every historical ranking, so no sequence of deliberate underperformances can increase the eventual rating. A second multi-week trap is momentum exploitation: if uncertainty decays too slowly, a player can “ride” a stale high rating; Elo-MMR’s \(\rho\)-bounded pseudodiffusion caps momentum by making \(\rho\) inversely proportional to desired momentum strength [PAGE 8]. A third trap is rating inflation through asymmetric new-player initialization; the paper initializes newcomers with fixed \((\mu_{\mathrm{newcomer}},\sigma_{\mathrm{newcomer}})\) in Algorithm 1 [PAGE 6], but does not prove inflation stability over open-ended time horizons.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression: under rating pressure, players in exploitable systems regress to a habit of strategic underperformance—tanking early to inflate volatility, then dominating later to net-gain rating points. The first-order belief is “my current loss buys a multiplier on future wins.” The second-order belief is “opponents will not punish me because the system rewards variance.” This recursive habit is documented in TopCoder and Pokémon Go [PAGE 1].

Counter-trap: Elo-MMR’s aligned-incentives theorem removes the volatility multiplier entirely. Because the rating is monotonic in each historical ranking [PAGE 7, Theorem 5.5], the first-order belief collapses: tanking never increases future rating potential. The counter-trap is stable only if the \(Q_i\) root remains unique (log-concavity) and if performances are estimated without opponent-belief contamination (large \(|P_t|\)).

Clock: computational latency bounds the depth of recursive reasoning. The algorithm is embarrassingly parallel; each player solves independent equations with \(O(1)\) memory overhead per thread [PAGE 9]. Runtime per round is \(O\!\left(\sum_{i\in P}(|P|+|H_i|)\log\log\epsilon^{-1}\right)\) [PAGE 9]. When \(|P|\) or \(|H_i|\) reaches millions, exact computation becomes cost-prohibitive, forcing adaptive subsampling and history compression [PAGE 9]. Subsampling trades exact incentive alignment for speed: the paper notes that if aligned incentives must hold exactly, one must avoid choosing different opponents for each player [PAGE 9]. Thus the clock imposes an approximation boundary—under severe latency constraints, the system may sacrifice exact monotonicity for feasible runtime, reopening a narrow strategic window.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, weather, and model-layer veto proof.

Physical layer. In online multiplayer games (CounterStrike, Halo, League of Legends), observed performance \(P_{i,t}\) is mediated by network latency, server tick rate, and packet loss [PAGE 1, refs. 20, 24, 28]. If latency introduces non-i.i.d. deviations \(P_{i,t}-S_{i,t}\), the i.i.d. assumption in Equation (1) [PAGE 2] is violated, and the performance posterior no longer concentrates cleanly. Veto condition: if jitter variance is comparable to \(\beta^2\), the logistic scale parameter becomes mis-specified and Theorem 3.2’s zero may shift.

Biomechanical layer. Obstacle-course races and rock climbing involve physical fatigue and route novelty [PAGE 1]. If fatigue accumulates across rounds, skill evolution is not a zero-mean Gaussian diffusion independent of history; the pseudodiffusion’s zero-uncertainty property fails because \(\sigma_{t-1}\to0\) no longer implies a clean Gaussian reset [PAGE 6]. Veto condition: when performance deviations correlate with recent competition load, the independence of \(\{P_{i,t}-S_{i,t}\}\) collapses.

Weather layer. Outdoor Spartan Races and similar events place obstacles on hiking trails around the world [PAGE 1, ref. 8]. Weather (temperature, precipitation, wind) shifts the performance distribution non-stationarily. The model assumes fixed \(\beta_k\) per contest [PAGE 4]; unmodeled weather effects make \(\beta_k\) a latent time-varying parameter, invalidating the fixed-scale logistic posterior in Equation (5) [PAGE 5].

Model-layer veto proof. The system is valid only if: (a) all \(f_j\) are continuously differentiable and log-concave [PAGE 3, Lemma 3.1]; (b) ties are measure-zero, handled by \(\epsilon\to0\) buckets [PAGE 2]; (c) \(|P_t|\) is sufficiently large for Doob concentration [PAGE 2]; (d) \(\rho\in(0,\infty)\) [PAGE 6]. If any condition fails, \(Q_i\) may lack a unique zero, the MAP rating becomes non-unique, and the incentive-alignment proof in Theorem 5.5 [PAGE 7] is unsound. There is no graceful degradation stated in the paper for these boundary regimes; they are hard vetoes.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
