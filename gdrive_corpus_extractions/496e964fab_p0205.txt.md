θ ≥ max_x H_joint(x): no superthreshold zone exists; all threshold-dependent claims are vacuous.
Non-stationary Weber fraction: j.n.d. size w(x) varies with intensity, so Δx in j.n.d. must be replaced by ∫ dx'/w(x'), invalidating uniform 5 j.n.d. sampling.
Saturation: if response R = tanh(α·H_joint), linear summation overestimates joint strength where gradients overlap densely; Postulate 4 breaks down at high reinforcement density.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Historical lineage. The j.n.d. unit descends from Weber (1834) and was formalized by Fechner (1860) in psychophysics; Hull imports it as the spatial unit of the behavioral continuum. The "negative growth curve" gradient echoes Pavlov's (1902/1927) excitation and inhibition gradients around a conditioned stimulus, generalized here to multiple reinforcement points. Spence (1936, 1952) later operationalized gradient steepness as an index of discrimination learning, directly descending from Hull's Figure 46 logic. Rescorla and Wagner (1972) re-interpreted habit strength as associative strength updated by prediction error, transforming Hull's static H_i^0 into a dynamic, experience-dependent parameter. Modern kernel methods (radial-basis networks, Gaussian-process regression, inducing-point sparse approximations) are the direct computational descendants: H_joint(x) = Σ_i H_i^0 K(|x − x_i|) is exactly kernel regression with X_c as inducing points.

Retrocausal reinterpretation. Later theories project backward onto Hull's page: what Hull calls "habit strength" is re-read as expected value; the one-third-per-j.n.d. decrement becomes a kernel bandwidth tuned by reinforcement history rather than an immutable sensory constant. The "superthreshold zone" (claim I) anticipates modern notions of function approximation between training samples, where generalization emerges from overlap rather than from contiguous reinforcement.

Multi-week traps. (1) Assuming j.n.d. constancy across weeks despite sensory adaptation and peripheral noise changes. (2) Treating H_i^0 as static across weeks despite extinction, spontaneous recovery, and reconsolidation. (3) Assuming the distribution X_c remains fixed in perceptual space when the experimental context (room, time of day, experimenter) drifts, which would shift the animal's effective x-coordinate and invalidate claim II. (4) Interpolating habit strength between weekly test sessions as if the field were stationary, producing false confidence in a stable central maximum when the underlying reinforcement distribution has drifted.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Level 0 (organism): behavior is governed by H_joint(x) > θ, with the organism responding most strongly near the distribution center (claim II) and showing residual responding between and beyond reinforced points (claims III–IV).

Level 1 (observer/experimenter): the experimenter infers X_c and H_i^0 from the reinforcement schedule and predicts a smooth superthreshold arch peaking at the middle, with generalization into unreinforced zones.

Level 2 (organism models observer): the animal can detect which points are reinforced and which are not. Claim III creates a counter-trap: unreinforced points between reinforced loci carry "only a little less" strength than the adjacent reinforced means, so the organism will approach or respond at non-reinforced locations. An experimenter who expects responding only at reinforced points will be systematically surprised by strong responding at intermediate loci—a prediction error generated by the observer's failure to model summation.

Crisis habit regression vs counter-trap vs clock. Under crisis (high drive, though D is omitted on [PAGE 196]), behavior regresses to the dominant habit at the central tendency (claim II), abandoning exploratory sampling of the superthreshold periphery (claims I and IV). The counter-trap is precisely that periphery: in non-crisis conditions, the unbroken superthreshold zone beyond the trained range draws responses to remote stimuli, producing false positives if the experimenter assumes responding is confined to X_c. The clock is twofold: spatially, the "intervals of 5 j.n.d.'s" [PAGE 196] form a discrete sampling grid that clocks the resolution of the habit field; temporally, Hull's broader system requires inter-reinforcement intervals and drive D(t) to determine when H_i^0 rises above θ. Without a temporal clock, the theory cannot predict the onset or extinction of the superthreshold zone. The recursive loop closes when the experimenter's beliefs about where the animal will respond alter where reinforcements are delivered, thereby changing X_c and relocating the maximum—an observer-expectancy loop embedded in the habit field itself.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Veto model. Overt behavior B(x,t) occurs iff
B = 1 ⇔ H_joint(x) > θ ∧ ¬V_phys ∧ ¬V_bio ∧ ¬V_env,
where each V is an independent veto predicate. Hullian habit strength is necessary but not sufficient; the three layers form a serial bottleneck.

Layer 1 — Physical / psychophysical veto. Reliable localization on the j.n.d. continuum requires receptor signal-to-noise above the Weber threshold. If ambient illumination falls or background noise rises, the Weber fraction w(x) increases, expanding the physical size of one j.n.d. The effective kernel bandwidth λ_eff shrinks, flattening H_joint and potentially pushing the maximum below θ. Veto condition: if sensory SNR < SNR_min, Δx cannot be encoded, K(Δx) is undefined, and no generalization prediction from [PAGE 196] holds.

Layer 2 — Biomechanical veto. Habit strength is motivational, not mechanical. Let F_req(x) be the force required for the response at locus x and F_max(t) the animal's available force under fatigue. If F_req > F_max, V_bio = 1 and behavior is suppressed even when H_joint(x) ≫ θ. A dissociation is verifiable: a low-force response (nose poke) shows superthreshold habit expression at center while a high-force lever press remains at zero.

Layer 3 — Weather / environmental veto. Ambient context (temperature, humidity, background sound) adds extraneous dimensions to the stimulus continuum or shifts its origin. A 10 °C drop, for example, shifts the animal's thermal frame of reference, so the test point x maps to an effective coordinate x' = x + δx with δx ≠ 0. Claim II ("maximum at the middle of the distribution of conditioned stimuli") then fails because the "middle" is computed in trained coordinates while the animal responds in shifted coordinates. Veto is reversible: returning context restores the original peak, proving a context-layer veto rather than habit erasure.

Adjudication proof. Construct truth table over (V_phys, V_bio, V_env). Case (0,1,0): intact sensation and context, paralyzed response limb → H_joint > θ but B = 0. Case (1,0,0): receptor noise high, biomechanics intact → no reliable Δx, gradient collapses. Case (0,0,1): context shift moves x outside trained range → H_joint(x') < θ despite strong trained-center habit. Each layer can independently null behavior, so no single layer suffices and the Hullian equation on [PAGE 196] describes only the pre-veto motivational potential.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with falsifiable predictions. RLVR verifiability requires that each condition be manipulable and each outcome measurable without reference to betting lines.

Scenario A — Sensory-adaptation veto (V_phys).
Counterfactual: pre-expose the subject to the full continuum for 60 minutes before testing, inducing adaptation that doubles the Weber fraction w(x).
Veto trigger: effective j.n.d. unit doubles, so λ_eff = λ/2 and sensory noise raises effective θ.
Verifiable prediction: the psychometric slope of responding vs. x halves; the superthreshold zone predicted by claim I [PAGE 196] narrows or vanishes; responding at distal points beyond the trained range falls to baseline even though the unadapted model predicts above-threshold generalization. Measurement: response rate at x = max(X_c) + 10 j.n.d. drops below pre-adaptation level by >50%.

Scenario B — Biomechanical veto (V_bio).
Counterfactual: after a fatigue protocol, maximum sustainable lever-press force F_max = 10 N while the required force F_req = 15 N; simultaneously verify that H_joint at the distribution center remains superthreshold via a low-force alternative response.
Veto trigger: F_req > F_max.
Verifiable prediction: nose-poke response rate at center remains high (confirming H_joint > θ), but lever-press rate is exactly zero across all x. This dissociation confirms that habit-strength summation on [PAGE 196] does not guarantee performance when the biomechanical layer vetoes.

Scenario C — Environmental/context shift veto (V_env).
Counterfactual: test in a novel room 10 °C cooler with continuous white noise, shifting the effective thermal/auditory continuum origin by δx = +8 j.n.d. relative to training.
Veto trigger: the animal's effective test coordinate x' = x + δx lies outside or at the edge of X_c.
Verifiable prediction: the peak of responding shifts by approximately +8 j.n.d. or disappears entirely; claim II's "middle of the distribution" [PAGE 196] no longer predicts the behavioral maximum in the novel context. Reversibility check: returning to the training room restores the central peak, verifying a context-layer veto rather than extinction of H_i^0.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
