<THINK_TRACE>
The source is not a single “Fusion-Net50” network in the strict neural-architecture sense; it is a composed pipeline: YOLOv8n detection, deduplication, ByteTrack association, OSNet-AIN plus ResNet-50 color embedding for Re-ID, Feature–Position Fusion for Track ID refinement, and MiniBatchKMeans for team clustering. The abstract names the integration “Fusion-Net50-Based” and reports “a total of 25 Track IDs (Players 21 Track IDs, Goalkeeper 1 Track ID, Referee 2 Track IDs, Ball 1 Track ID)” [PAGE 1]. The methods section then decomposes the pipeline, so the first audit point is terminological: the paper’s contribution is integration and identity refinement, not a monolithic fused backbone.

Multi-hop derivation: (1) YOLOv8n emits class-conditioned boxes for Player, Goalkeeper, Referee, Ball at 25 fps, 1920×1080 [PAGE 3]. (2) Deduplication uses IoU and center distance to remove double boxes; the paper reports reduction from “38.922 detections” to “14.630 detections” with `iou_threshold = 0.3`, `min_center_dist = 10`, and `shrink_ratio = 0.6` [PAGE 6–7]. (3) ByteTrack consumes deduplicated and low-confidence detections, with class-specific buffers; Table 1 lists Player `lost_track_buffer` 300, GK/Referee 100, and `minimum_matching_threshold` 0.9/1.0/1.0 [PAGE 7]. (4) Re-ID embeddings are concatenated as OSNet 512-D plus ResNet-50 color 128-D, normalized to 640-D [PAGE 7]. (5) Feature–Position Fusion merges Track IDs when cosine similarity is at least 0.90 and center distance is at most 60 pixels [PAGE 9]. (6) K-Means clusters 128-D color embeddings into two teams; anchors are applied only after clustering [PAGE 5, PAGE 9]. (7) Evaluation reports HOTA 0.919, MOTA 0.898, IDF1 0.949, IDSW 17, Frag 196 [PAGE 10]. (8) Speed uses center-circle scale: “calculated scale factor is 0.0132 m/pixel” and “approximately 1.1891 km/h per pixel” [PAGE 11].

Gray-zone audit. First, the final Track ID count is internally tense: final reporting gives 21 Player Track IDs plus 1 Goalkeeper [PAGE 1], while the paper also states “each team consists of only 10 players and 1 goalkeeper” [PAGE 8]. For two teams that implies 20 outfield plus 2 goalkeepers; the final count indicates one goalkeeper or one outfield identity is missing, merged, or filtered. Second, the temporal recovery clock is inconsistent: text says tracks lost within “the `max_time_lost` tolerance (30 frames)” can be recovered [PAGE 4], but Table 1 lists Player `lost_track_buffer` as 300 [PAGE 7]. At 25 fps, 30 frames is 1.2 s, while 300 frames is 12 s. That is not a cosmetic discrepancy; it changes the recovery horizon. Third, Table 2 point HOTA for Match 3 is 0.598 [PAGE 10], while Table 3 bootstrap HOTA mean is 0.650 with CI 0.602–0.685 [PAGE 10]. The paper does not fully explain whether these are different aggregations, segments, or evaluation subsets. Fourth, the ablation is cumulative, not factorial: “The ablation study is conducted in a cumulative manner” [PAGE 9], so the final row’s gain cannot be attributed cleanly to Feature Fusion alone. Fifth, the paper admits no sensitivity analysis or ground-truth speed validation: “this study has not conducted a sensitivity analysis, error quantification, or validation against ground-truth player motion” [PAGE 13]. These are the main gray zones that limit strong causal claims.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Deduplication geometry is formalized on [PAGE 4]. For a box \(b=(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)\) in pixels,
\[
c_x=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\qquad c_y=\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}
\]
\[
w=x_2-x_1,\qquad h=y_2-y_1
\]
\[
w'=\frac{w\alpha}{2},\qquad h'=\frac{h\alpha}{2}
\]
\[
x_1'=c_x-w',\quad x_2'=c_x+w',\quad y_1'=c_y-h',\quad y_2'=c_y+h'
\]
with \(b'=(x_1',y_1',x_2',y_2')\). Units are pixels; \(\alpha\) is dimensionless. The paper uses `shrink_ratio = 0.6` [PAGE 6]. Breakdown regimes: \(\alpha=0\) collapses the box to zero area; \(\alpha>1\) expands rather than shrinks; zero-area boxes make IoU undefined or zero depending on implementation.

IoU is defined as
\[
\mathrm{IoU}(A,B)=\frac{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{intersection}}}{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{union}}}
\]
\[
w_{\mathrm{intersection}}=\max\!\left(0,\min(x_2^A,x_2^B)-\max(x_1^A,x_1^B)\right)
\]
\[
h_{\mathrm{intersection}}=\max\!\left(0,\min(y_2^A,y_2^B)-\max(y_1^A,y_1^B)\right)
\]
\[
\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{intersection}}=w_{\mathrm{intersection}}h_{\mathrm{intersection}}
\]
\[
\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{union}}=\mathrm{Area}_A+\mathrm{Area}_B-\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{intersection}}
\]
[PAGE 4]. Units are square pixels. Singularity: if both boxes have zero area, the denominator is zero. The center distance is
\[
d(i,j)=\sqrt{(c_x^i-c_x^j)^2+(c_y^i-c_y^j)^2}
\]
in pixels [PAGE 4]. A fixed 60-pixel merge threshold [PAGE 9] is not scale-invariant across camera zoom or pitch region.

K-Means is
\[
\arg\min_{S}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{x\in S_i}\|x-\mu_i\|^2
\]
where \(S=\{S_1,\dots,S_k\}\), \(x\) is a 128-D color embedding, and \(\mu_i\) is the centroid [PAGE 5]. Units are squared embedding units; the objective is nonconvex and label IDs are arbitrary: “the numerical labels generated by K-Means are arbitrary and may vary across executions” [PAGE 5]. Breakdown: \(k>n\), empty clusters, identical embeddings, or cluster centroids colliding make assignments unstable. Also, anchors do not affect clustering: “The anchor Track IDs are not involved in the clustering process; they are applied only after clustering is completed” [PAGE 5].

Speed and distance equations are on [PAGE 11]:
\[
S=\frac{D_{\mathrm{field}}}{D_{\mathrm{field,px}}}
\]
with \(S\) in meters per pixel. The paper reports \(S=0.0132\ \mathrm{m\,pixel^{-1}}\), using the 18.3 m center-circle diameter and a pixel reference [PAGE 11]. Then
\[
v_{\mathrm{km/h}}=d_{\mathrm{px}}\,S\,F\,3.6
\]
where \(d_{\mathrm{px}}\) is pixel displacement per frame, \(F=25\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), and 3.6 converts m/s to km/h. The paper states this yields “approximately 1.1891 km/h per pixel” [PAGE 11]. Distance and aggregation are
\[
d_m^{(i)}=d_{\mathrm{px}}^{(i)}S,\qquad
D_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_m^{(i)},\qquad
\bar v=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{\mathrm{km/h}}^{(i)}
\]
[PAGE 11]. Assumptions: constant frame rate, one-frame displacement, planar pitch scale, and negligible perspective distortion. Breakdown regimes: \(D_{\mathrm{field,px}}=0\) makes \(S\) singular; \(F=0\) or \(N=0\) makes speed or average speed undefined; camera motion and box jitter add to \(d_{\mathrm{px}}\). The paper itself warns that perspective distortion may introduce errors “particularly in regions of the pitch located farther from the reference point” [PAGE 13].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is visible in the paper’s own citations. Detection moves from R-CNN/Fast R-CNN/Faster R-CNN and SSD/YOLO to DETR-style transformers, with YOLO retained for real-time behavior; the paper says YOLO “still lags behind transformer-based methods in terms of accuracy” but has “a primary advantage in its ability to perform real-time detection” [PAGE 2]. Tracking lineage runs through SORT/Kalman/ByteTrack, with the paper citing OC-SORT and BoostTrack++ for low-confidence and tracklet handling [PAGE 2, PAGE 13]. Re-ID lineage runs through jersey-color tracking, HSV masking, EIoU-distance loss, OSFL/OSNet-AIN, and CNN-DBN hybrids [PAGE 2]. Team clustering lineage runs through YOLOv5 team detection, HSV masking, and K-Means region extraction [PAGE 2–3].

The “retrocausal” trap is that later choices retroactively define earlier identities. ByteTrack + Re-ID first yields “40 unique Track IDs, comprising 35 Player Track IDs” [PAGE 8]; Feature–Position Fusion then merges to 21 Player Track IDs by comparing first and last crops with similarity ≥0.90 and distance ≤60 px [PAGE 8–9]. Thus the final identity set is not the raw tracker identity set; it is a post hoc merge product. The same pattern applies to team semantics: K-Means forms clusters, then manual anchors assign Team_0/Team_1 labels. The authors state anchors “do not affect cluster formation, centroid computation, or membership assignment” [PAGE 5], but semantic interpretation is still anchor-dependent. They admit: “semantic team labeling relies on a predefined anchor selection during post-labeling. While it does not affect the clustering process, it limits full automation” [PAGE 13].

Multi-week traps: (1) single-match detector training—YOLOv8n was trained on one match and cross-match HOTA drops to 0.682 and 0.598 [PAGE 10]; (2) manual anchor selection must be redone for new jersey/team combinations; (3) thresholds such as 60 px, 0.90 cosine similarity, 20-crop minimum, and field Y bounds [PAGE 9] are fixed rather than calibrated per camera; (4) no sensitivity or ground-truth speed validation [PAGE 13]; (5) 9 FPS operating speed limits live use [PAGE 13]. These are not one-off implementation details; they are recurring multi-week maintenance debts for any new match, stadium, camera angle, or lighting regime.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The system’s “theory of mind” about players is a layered belief state. In a crisis—missed detection, occlusion, motion blur—the habit regression is to trust motion and stored history: “(1) utilizing lost_tracks with Kalman prediction, (2) motion-history-based fallback from the last two frames, and (3) fallback from previously stored history frames in `_saved_tracks` when the number of predictions was fewer than 5” [PAGE 4]. Low-confidence boxes are also retained as a fallback. This is a sensible crisis habit, but it can regress to spatial continuity when appearance is ambiguous; the paper itself notes that Kalman prediction becomes “less informative” for slow-moving or temporarily stationary objects [PAGE 2].

The counter-trap is explicit: the framework “avoids using Re-identification (Re-ID) embeddings to directly override tracking identities. Instead, the embeddings serve solely as an auxiliary reference to support identity consistency during recovery stages” [PAGE 4]. That is a counter-trap against appearance-only identity hijack: a similar jersey or lighting shift cannot by itself rewrite a track. But the counter-trap has a cost: if motion and low-confidence spatial validation are wrong, the system can preserve a false identity. The paper’s manual anchor scheme adds another recursive layer: the evaluator’s semantic team belief is anchored to selected Track IDs, not to an automatic rule [PAGE 9].

The clock discipline is central. The video is 25 fps [PAGE 3]. Text says lost tracks are recoverable within “the `max_time_lost` tolerance (30 frames)” [PAGE 4], i.e. about 1.2 s, while Table 1 lists a 300-frame Player buffer [PAGE 7], i.e. about 12 s. Stability is checked at frame 15 with an average of 20.1 detections against a threshold of 15 [PAGE 7], then minimum consecutive frames requires 2 frames for players and 1 for goalkeeper/referee [PAGE 8]. Snapshots are updated “every 3 frames” [PAGE 8]. These clocks determine whether a temporarily lost player is re-associated or assigned a new ID. The mismatch between 30 and 300 frames is therefore a first-order behavioral ambiguity, not a minor parameter note.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: the speed layer is vetoed as a global physical measurement unless a homography or full camera calibration is applied. The paper derives \(S\) from a single center-circle reference and admits perspective distortion “particularly in regions of the pitch located farther from the reference point” [PAGE 13]. A fixed \(S=0.0132\ \mathrm{m\,pixel^{-1}}\) cannot be both correct at the center circle and correct at the far touchline under strong perspective. The reported top speeds—11.31 to 23.12 km/h across Tables 5–6 [PAGE 11]—are therefore internally derived estimates, not externally validated physical measurements. Veto proof: without a per-region scale or homography, no claim about absolute sprint speed, acceleration, or distance covered at the far side is physically identified.

Biomechanical veto: the source computes motion from bounding-box centroid displacement and optical-flow camera compensation [PAGE 5, PAGE 11]. It does not measure foot contact, limb kinematics, ground reaction forces, or player orientation. The authors state they have not performed “validation against ground-truth player motion” [PAGE 13]. Veto proof: any biomechanical claim about fatigue, force, load, or change-of-direction mechanics is unsupported by centroid speed alone. The correct interpretation is “tracked centroid displacement rate under the estimated scale,” not “true player locomotion.”

Weather veto: the source’s dataset description lists SoccerNet-Tracking, 550 matches, 764 hours, 25 fps, and 1920×1080 resolution [PAGE 3], but no weather variables, pitch moisture, wind, temperature, or visibility measurements. Veto proof: the source cannot adjudicate weather effects. Any proposed weather layer would require external, time-stamped, location-matched data; absent that, weather is an unidentifiable confound.

Data-layer veto: generalization is already falsified by the paper’s own cross-match test. YOLOv8n was “trained only on data from the first match without exposure to Match 2 and Match 3 sequences” [PAGE 10], and HOTA falls to 0.682 and 0.598 [PAGE 10]. IDSW rises to 320 and 723, and Frag to 412 and 469 [PAGE 10]. Veto proof: a claim of match-independent identity consistency is vetoed unless the detector and thresholds are retrained or adapted per match context. The paper’s own conclusion says “generalization remains a challenge” [PAGE 12].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
