The state representations are PVS with dimension 92 and EDMS with dimension 74 [PAGE 6, Table 1]. The action space has 15 feasible discrete classes: five on-ball attacking actions, one aggregated defensive action, and nine off-ball actions, with role-based masking [PAGE 6–7, Tables 2–3]. The Markov strategy and finite-horizon value operators are
\[
\pi_{i,t}(\cdot\mid s_t)\in\Delta(\mathcal A_i(s_t)),
\qquad
V_i^{\pi}(s_t)=\mathbb E_{\pi}\left[\sum_{k=t}^{T}\gamma^{k-t}r_i(s_k,a_k)\mid s_t\right],
\]
with \(\gamma=1\) in the reported experiments [PAGE 7–8]. The MPE motivation is the inequality
\[
V_i^{\pi^\ast}(s_t)\ge V_i^{(\tilde\pi_i,\pi^\ast_{-i})}(s_t),
\quad \forall i,\ \forall t,\ \forall s_t,\ \forall \tilde\pi_i,
\]
but the empirical estimator does not estimate \(\pi_{i,t}\) or verify this inequality [PAGE 8]. The paper states: “The empirical implementation does not explicitly estimate the Markov policies \(\pi_{i,t}\) or verify an equilibrium strategy profile” [PAGE 8]. Continuation targets use the next observed action: “continuation values are bootstrapped using the next actions observed in each possession rather than actions obtained from an explicit best-response or greedy-policy update” [PAGE 8].

\[

\]
with \(d=3\) features [PAGE 8–9]. Let \(V(x_t,y_t)\) be the EPV probability at ball location \((x_t,y_t)\), \(S_{i,t}=\mathbf 1\{a_{i,t}=\text{shot}\}\), \(E_t\) the final time step of a possession segment, \(L=105\ \mathrm m\), and the attacking-third boundary \(x=L/6=17.5\ \mathrm m\). Define
\[

\[
z_i(s_t,a_t)=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\mathbf 1\left\{\sum_{j\in I_{\mathrm{att}}}v_t S_{j,t}\gt 0\right\}\\

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive theory-of-mind content is present mathematically but not empirically identified. The MPE condition is a mutual best-response recursion: each player’s value depends on others’ strategies, and the inequality must hold for every unilateral deviation [PAGE 8]. The reward and value decomposition then makes each player’s Q depend on the joint state and on future feature accumulation under the strategy profile [PAGE 10]. But the estimator replaces the policy recursion with observed next actions, so the model learns state-conditioned continuation correlations under observed behavior, not explicit beliefs about opponents’ beliefs [PAGE 8, PAGE 12–13]. In that sense, the “theory of mind” is shallow: other players enter through the state and the realized transition, not through an estimated recursive belief hierarchy.

Crisis habit regression appears in the independent baseline: with EDMS held fixed, it assigns the highest value to forward movement in 99.21% of evaluated off-ball states [PAGE 1, PAGE 15]. That is a habit-like collapse to a single directional prior. The proposed model’s most frequent direction is 17.63%, which shows the ranking is less concentrated, but the paper itself warns that diversity “does not by itself prove tactical optimality” [PAGE 15]. The counter-trap is therefore: a less concentrated ranking can look context-sensitive while still being an artifact of architecture, reward representation, training procedure, or noise. The paper’s own formulation-level caveat blocks a clean “MPE caused tactical intelligence” inference [PAGE 16, PAGE 22]. The clock is the finite horizon: \(\gamma=1\), terminal time \(T\), and possession segments retained at 30–300 steps at 10 fps [PAGE 8, PAGE 14]. The recursion is truncated by the possession clock, and terminal rewards—shot value or possession termination—enter directly at \(T\) [PAGE 12]. There is no infinite-horizon equilibrium recursion and no game-clock or score-state variable in the current EDMS; score difference is listed only as a future extension [PAGE 32]. Thus the defensible reading is: finite-horizon, observed-behavior, state-conditioned valuation, with habit collapse reduced but not proven tactically optimal.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The cross-layer veto is physical and biomechanical before it is tactical. The paper’s feasible action set is role-based: attacking ball carrier, attacking off-ball player, and defending player masks [PAGE 7, Table 3]. It is not a biomechanical feasibility set. PVS includes positions and velocities, and the preprocessing computes velocity and acceleration [PAGE 14, PAGE 28], but the action mask does not enforce acceleration limits, body orientation, turning radius, fatigue, injury status, surface friction, or aerial ability. EDMS uses one-metre directional displacements for space-score and dribble-score changes [PAGE 30–31], not sprint or joint kinematics. Therefore, if a high-Q action \(a^\ast\) lies outside a physically admissible set \(\mathcal A_i^{\mathrm{phys}}(s_t)\subseteq\mathcal A_i(s_t)\), then \(Q_i(s_t,a^\ast)\) is an extrapolated valuation, not an actionable proof. The transition kernel itself depends on all players’ actions, \(P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)\) [PAGE 6], so a physically impossible joint action changes the transition and invalidates the continuation.

Weather is a second veto layer. Neither PVS nor EDMS includes wind, precipitation, temperature, pitch moisture, altitude, or ball-surface interaction; Table 1 lists only positions/velocities for PVS and tactical variables for EDMS [PAGE 6, PAGE 32, Table 5]. The learned \(h_i\) is estimated under the observed J1 League data distribution, so it cannot certify action values under a different weather-conditioned transition kernel. A high-Q pass or off-ball run that depends on dry-surface friction or still-air ball flight is not validated by the model. The same veto applies to defensive organization: EDMS lacks defensive-line structure, coverage, compactness, and passing-lane control from the defender’s perspective [PAGE 19–20]. Thus defender Q-values can be descriptive of the fitted basis but cannot adjudicate defensive positioning as tactically correct. The cross-layer proof obligation is: before a Q-ranking is used as a tactical recommendation, show that the action is in \(\mathcal A_i^{\mathrm{phys}}\), that the weather/surface conditions are in the training support, and that the relevant defensive variables are represented or the claim is explicitly limited. The paper does not establish those conditions.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
