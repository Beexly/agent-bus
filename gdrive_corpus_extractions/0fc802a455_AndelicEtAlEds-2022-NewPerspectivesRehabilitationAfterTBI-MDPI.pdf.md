\[
\left|\hat{p}_{\text{blind}}(A)-p_{\text{obs}}(A)\right|>\tau
\]
for age stratum \(A\), where \(\hat{p}_{\text{blind}}\) is the predicted direct-pathway probability from an age-blind model, \(p_{\text{obs}}\) is observed probability, and \(\tau\) is a calibration tolerance. The source shows strong age patterning: “In patients aged 18–29 years, 53% followed the direct pathway, in contrast to 10% of patients aged 65–79 years” [PAGE 35]. A verifiable prediction is that an age-blind model will overpredict direct-pathway probability for patients aged 65–79 by a large margin, because observed access is only 10% in that stratum. If a new cohort shows calibrated parity across age strata, the veto is rejected. If the age gap persists or widens, the veto is supported. The deeper test is whether adding systematic rehabilitation-needs assessment reduces the age gap, as implied by the source’s criticism that there is no systematic assessment at discharge [PAGE 40].

Veto Scenario 2: Severity-primary fatigue intervention.
Counterfactual model: treat fatigue after TBI mainly as a function of acute injury severity, so rehabilitation resources are allocated according to Glasgow Coma Scale, CT severity, or anatomical injury grade.
Formal check:
\[
R^2_{V\mid I} > R^2_{I\mid V},
\]
where \(R^2_{V\mid I}\) is the variance in fatigue explained by somatic vulnerability after accounting for injury severity, and \(R^2_{I\mid V}\) is the variance explained by injury severity after accounting for somatic vulnerability. The source reports that the somatic vulnerability factor had the strongest association with fatigue [PAGE 72], and that “GSCL subscale for musculoskeletal symptoms explained more variance in fatigue than the somatic vulnerability factor in large” [PAGE 73]. A verifiable prediction is that pain, musculoskeletal symptoms, sleep disturbance, and somatic burden will explain more fatigue variance than acute injury severity indices. If a sufficiently powered replication finds the reverse, the veto is rejected. If somatic variables dominate, the veto is supported, and rehabilitation should prioritize pain, sleep, and somatic symptom management alongside psychological robustness.

Veto Scenario 3: Telerehabilitation universal acceptability for pediatric severe cognitive impairment.
Counterfactual assumption: video-delivered goal-oriented rehabilitation is equally acceptable and effective across all pediatric cognitive severity levels.
Formal check:
\[
\operatorname{median}\left(U_{\text{child}}\mid C_{\text{severe}}\right)<3
\]
or
\[
\operatorname{median}\left(Q_{\text{therapist}}\mid C_{\text{severe}}\right)<3,
\]
where \(U_{\text{child}}\) is child-rated usefulness on the acceptability scale, \(Q_{\text{therapist}}\) is therapist-rated communication quality, and \(C_{\text{severe}}\) denotes severe cognitive impairment. The source reports that therapists found it challenging to maintain good communication with children through videoconference [PAGE 135], and that one child with very severe cognitive deficit did not gain increased understanding of symptoms due to the intervention [PAGE 135]. The feasibility study nevertheless concluded overall acceptability [PAGE 122]. A verifiable prediction is that the subgroup of children with severe cognitive impairment will show lower child-rated alliance or usefulness and lower therapist-rated communication than children with milder impairment. If subgroup medians remain high, the veto is rejected. If they fall below threshold, the veto is supported, requiring hybrid or in-person adaptation, simplified goal materials, or caregiver-mediated implementation.

These three vetoes are not ethical judgments; they are model-integrity checks. They identify where a rehabilitation reasoning system should refuse to generalize. The first veto protects against age-based access inequity. The second protects against severity tunnel vision in fatigue management. The third protects against overgeneralizing telerehabilitation acceptability from a heterogeneous sample to children with severe cognitive impairment. The source supports all three concerns through its own data, and none of the vetoes requires unsupported betting-style or pseudo-quantitative constructs.
