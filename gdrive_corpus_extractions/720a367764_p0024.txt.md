Veto: The Bayesian update \(p(\text{cause}|\text{effect}) \propto p(\text{effect}|\text{cause}) p(\text{cause})\) cannot be set to zero without severing the feedback loop; health outcomes become uncorrected by intervention.
Prediction: Historical mortality and morbidity cohorts will show higher variance and higher mean adverse outcomes in Christian Science-adherent populations compared with matched non-adherent populations during epidemics where germ theory predicts preventability (e.g., influenza, diphtheria, 1900–1940). Verifiable via vital-statistics and church-membership records where available. Reward is granted if outcome divergence tracks periods of high pathogen prevalence; vetoed if no divergence is found.

Scenario 3 — Musket superiority veto.
Counterfactual: Suppose military adoption of the smooth-bore musket over the longbow had been decided solely by measured ballistic performance \((r, a, \text{ROF})\) rather than training time \(\tau\).
Veto: Because \(r_{\text{musket}} \lesssim r_{\text{longbow}}\), \(a_{\text{musket}} < a_{\text{longbow}}\), and \(\text{ROF}_{\text{musket}} < \text{ROF}_{\text{longbow}}\) ([PAGE 31]), a performance-only selector would retain the longbow.
Prediction: Army training records and casualty rates per engagement will show that musket units required significantly less pre-deployment training (\(\Delta \tau \gg 0\)) while achieving comparable or worse per-shot lethality; adoption correlates with \(\tau\) reduction, not with \(r\), \(a\), or ROF gains. Verifiable via British and U.S. Army muster and training manuals from Marlborough through the Civil War. Reward is granted if the \(\tau\)-reduction coefficient dominates the likelihood of adoption; vetoed if ballistic superiority metrics predict adoption better than training time.
