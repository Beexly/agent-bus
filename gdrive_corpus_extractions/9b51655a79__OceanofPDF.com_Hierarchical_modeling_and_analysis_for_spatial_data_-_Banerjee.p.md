The book's spine is a single tension: a spatial Gaussian process $\{Y(s): s\in D\}$ is specified through finite-dimensional distributions, but the object of interest — the covariance function $C(\mathbf{h})$ — must be *admissible* (positive-definite) in the *ambient dimension* $r$, not just in the dimension where you fit it. Nearly every "gray zone" in the text is a corollary of this one constraint plus the loss of full order that time series enjoy. [PAGE 45] states it exactly: "we have full order in the former, but only partial order in two or more dimensions… large sample analysis for spatial process data usually envisions a fixed region with more and more points filling in this domain (so-called *infill asymptotics*)." That single fact cascades: (i) you cannot consistently learn long-range association from a fixed domain; (ii) method-of-moments variogram fitting [PAGE 43] is fragile because binned squared differences are dependent within and across bins and $N(t_k)$ varies; (iii) the nugget $\tau^2$ and microscale variance are only jointly identified up to the smallest interpoint distance [PAGE 143].

The multi-hop reasoning the book demands: a valid semivariogram $\gamma$ is *conditionally negative definite* [PAGE 37, eq 2.4], which is weaker than the positive-definiteness required of $C$; weak (intrinsic) stationarity does not imply second-order stationarity [PAGE 37]. So the analyst who reads off a nugget/sill/range from an empirical variogram has produced a *descriptive* object, not yet a probabilistic model — the leap to a valid joint density requires checking admissibility in $r$. The gray zone: spherical is fine in $r\le 3$ and *invalid* in $r\ge4$ [PAGE 39]; nested angle-class anisotropy is invalid at $r=2$ with as few as three points [PAGE 53]; the intrinsic CAR is improper and identified only up to an additive constant [PAGE 177]. Each is a place where a naive fit yields a covariance/precision matrix that is not positive-definite and hence no legitimate likelihood exists.
</THINK_TRACE>

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
All equations below are reconstructed from the OCR (which dropped Greek symbols); page markers and the surviving verbatim fragments are exact.

Point-referenced second-order model [PAGE 21, eq 1.1–1.2], verbatim fragment "( (Σ(θ))_{ii'} = σ² exp(− d_{ii'}) +  I(i=i') )":
$$Y\mid\mu,\theta \sim N_n(\mu,\Sigma(\theta)),\qquad C(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{s}_{i'})=\sigma^2\exp(-\phi\, d_{ii'})+\tau^2 I(i=i').$$
Units: $\sigma^2$ (partial sill) and $\tau^2$ (nugget) carry units of $[Y]^2$; $\phi$ has units of inverse length; $d_{ii'}$ is length. Powered-exponential, spherical, Gaussian, and Matérn are listed as alternatives; "Stein (1999, p. 51) illustrates its ability to capture a broader range of local correlation behavior" [PAGE 21] refers to the Matérn.

Ripley's $K$ [PAGE 23, eq 1.3–1.4], verbatim "K(d) =  E[n umber of points within d of an arbitrary point]":
$$K(d)=\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathbb{E}[\#\{\text{points within }d\text{ of an arbitrary point}\}],\quad K_{\text{CSR}}(d)=\pi d^2,$$
$$\hat K(d)=\frac{|A|}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\neq j} p_{ij}^{-1} I_d(d_{ij}).$$
$\lambda$ = intensity (points per unit area); $p_{ij}$ = proportion of the circle centered at $i$ through $j$ lying in $A$. $K(d)>\pi d^2$ ⇒ clustering; $<\pi d^2$ ⇒ regularity.

Geodesic distance [PAGE 31, eq 1.5], verbatim "D = R where R is the radius of the earth and  is an angle… cos = sin ₁ sin ₂ + cos ₁ cos ₂ cos( ₁  ₂)":
$$D=R\theta,\quad \cos\theta=\sin\phi_1\sin\phi_2+\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2\cos(\lambda_1-\lambda_2),$$
with $R\approx 6371$ km [PAGE 33]. The "naive Euclidean" metric (apply the planar formula to lat/long then scale by $R\pi/180$) and the chordal metric are the two error sources for large domains.

Semivariogram identities and admissibility [PAGE 37, eq 2.2–2.4], verbatim "(h) = C(0)  C(h)" and "∑ᵢ∑ⱼ aᵢaⱼ (sᵢ  sⱼ)  0":
$$\gamma(\mathbf{h})=C(\mathbf 0)-C(\mathbf h),\qquad C(\mathbf h)=\lim_{\|\mathbf u\|\to\infty}\gamma(\mathbf u)-\gamma(\mathbf h),$$
$$\sum_i\sum_j a_i a_j\,\gamma(\mathbf s_i-\mathbf s_j)\le 0\quad\text{for all }\{a_i\}\text{ with }\sum_i a_i=0\ \ (\text{conditional negative-definiteness}).$$
Ergodicity forces $C(\mathbf h)\to 0$ as $\|\mathbf h\|\to\infty$; only then is $C$ recoverable from $\gamma$ [PAGE 37].

Empirical (Matheron) semivariogram [PAGE 43, eq 2.9], verbatim "(t) =  [Y(sᵢ)  Y(sⱼ)]²":
$$\hat\gamma(t)=\frac{1}{2|N(t)|}\sum_{(s_i,s_j)\in N(t)}[Y(s_i)-Y(s_j)]^2,$$
a method-of-moments estimator; "under a Gaussian distributional assumption… the squared differences will have a distribution that is a scale multiple of the heavily skewed  distribution," motivating the Cressie–Hawkins robust variant using $|Y(s_i)-Y(s_j)|^{1/2}$ [PAGE 43]. Recommendation: "bins wide enough to capture at least 30 pairs per bin" (Journel & Huijbregts).

Spectral admissibility [PAGE 47, eq 2.10], verbatim "ĉ(w) = ∫ e^{ −iwᵀh} c(h) dh":
$$\hat c(\mathbf w)=\int e^{-i\mathbf w^\top \mathbf h}c(\mathbf h)\,d\mathbf h,\qquad g(\mathbf w)=(2\pi)^{-r}\frac{\hat c(\mathbf w)}{c(\mathbf 0)}.$$
A candidate $c$ is admissible iff $\hat c(\mathbf w)\ge 0$ and integrable. Constructive closures: mixing $\sum p_i C_i$, products $\prod c_i$, convolution $c_{12}=\int c_1(\mathbf h-\mathbf t)c_2(\mathbf t)d\mathbf t$ (proved via $\hat c_{12}=\hat c_1\hat c_2$) [PAGE 49].

Matérn and its inversion [PAGE 41 table; PAGE 51 eq 2.11], verbatim "(t) =  +  [1  (2√ t)/( √ ( )²⁻¹) K (2√ t)]" (Matérn $\nu=3/2$: $C(t)=\sigma^2(1+\phi t)e^{-\phi t}$):
$$\frac{(\phi\|\mathbf h\|)^\nu K_\nu(\phi\|\mathbf h\|)}{2^{\nu-1}\Gamma(\nu)\,\phi^{2\nu}}=\int_{\mathbb R^r} e^{i\mathbf w^\top \mathbf h}\,(\phi^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2)^{-(\nu+r/2)}\,d\mathbf w.$$
Mean-square differentiability requires $\nu>1$ [PAGE 361]; Stein's practical warning: "it will be very difficult to distinguish $\nu=1/2$ from $\nu>1/2$" [PAGE 361].

Simple kriging predictor [PAGE 65, eq 2.13–2.14]:
$$\mathbb E[Y(s_0)\mid y]=x_0^\top\beta+\gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}(y-X\beta),\qquad \operatorname{Var}[Y(s_0)\mid y]=\sigma^2+\tau^2-\gamma^\top\Sigma^{-1}\gamma.$$
Exact interpolation iff $\tau^2=0$ (noiseless); smoothing if $\tau^2>0$ [PAGE 65, Exercise 10].

CAR / MRF [PAGE 93, eq 3.12–3.14; PAGE 91 eq 3.7]:
$$Y_i\mid y_{j\ne i}\sim N\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_j b_{ij}y_j,\ \tau_i^2\Big),\quad p(y)\propto\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12 y^\top D^{-1}(I-B)y\big),\quad b_{ij}\tau_i^2=b_{ji}\tau_j^2,$$
with $\Sigma_y=(I-B)^{-1}D$; propriety needs $(I-\alpha\tilde W)$ nonsingular, guaranteed for $|\alpha|<1$ when $\tilde W$ is row-stochastic [PAGE 95, PAGE 99]. Intrinsic (pairwise-difference) form [PAGE 93, eq 3.16–3.17]: $p(y)\propto\exp\{-\tfrac{1}{2\tau^2}\sum_{i\sim j}(y_i-y_j)^2\}$, full conditional $N(\sum_{j\in\partial i}y_j/m_i,\ \tau^2/m_i)$.

Change of support [PAGE 191, eq 6.1; PAGE 193 eq 6.9]: $Y(B)=|B|^{-1}\int_B Y(s)\,ds$, approximated by Monte-Carlo $\hat Y(B_k)=L_k^{-1}\sum_\ell Y(s_{k;\ell})$.

Separable spacetime covariance [PAGE 281, eq 8.9], verbatim "(  )  = ( )( )": $\Sigma=\sigma^2 H_s(\phi)\otimes H_t(\psi)$, so $|\Sigma|=(\sigma^2)^{IJ}|H_s|^{J}|H_t|^{I}$ and $\Sigma^{-1}=H_s^{-1}\otimes H_t^{-1}$ — the Kronecker factorization that makes likelihood evaluation tractable.

Linear model of coregionalization [PAGE 245, eq 7.5/7.28]: $C(\mathbf s,\mathbf s')=\sum_{u}\rho_u(\mathbf s-\mathbf s')\,T_{\theta_u}$, with $T_{\theta_u}=A_{\theta_u}A_{\theta_u}^\top$ full rank. Spatially varying coefficients [PAGE 367, eq 10.30]: $Y(s)=X^\top(s)\tilde\beta(s)+\epsilon(s)$. Spatial CDF [PAGE 377, eq 10.40]: $F(w)=|D|^{-1}\int_D Z_w(s)\,ds$, $Z_w(s)=I(Y(s)\le w)$. Weibull spatial hazard [PAGE 313, eq 9.4]: $h(t_{ij};x_{ij})=\rho\,t_{ij}^{\rho-1}\exp(\beta^\top x_{ij}+W_i)$.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage is explicit in the text. Areal autoregression descends from **Whittle (1954)** (SAR) and **Besag (1974)** (CAR/MRF): "originally developed by Whittle (1954) and Besag (1974), respectively" [PAGE 21]. The geostatistical branch descends from **Matheron** (empirical semivariogram, kriging), with the variogram's origin honestly traced: "revealing how, historically, the variogram arose in kriging within the geostatistical framework" [PAGE 63]. The Matérn class "was originally suggested by Matérn (1947, 1960)" and "revived by Handcock and Stein (1993)" [PAGE 41]; **Stein (1999)** is the recurring authority who "recommends the Matérn class as a general tool" [PAGE 51]. The computational rupture is **Geman & Geman (1984)** and **Gelfand & Smith (1990)** for the Gibbs sampler [PAGE 125], with Metropolis–Hastings [PAGE 127]. The book positions itself against **Cressie (1991/1993)** as "arguably the only comprehensive book in the area" [PAGE 15].

"Multi-week traps" reinterpreted as *recurring methodological pitfalls the literature keeps re-hitting*: (1) the **nugget–microscale confounding** — Cressie's suggestion that $\epsilon(s)$ and $\tau^2$ "may themselves be partitioned into two pieces" [PAGE 143], which is unidentifiable at fixed sampling density; (2) **ecological bias** from county-level covariates [PAGE 175]; (3) the **improper-CAR trap** — using an improper intrinsic CAR prior and continuing "requires some care" via the constraint $\sum_i\phi_i=0$ [PAGE 177]; (4) **BYM identifiability** — Eberly & Carlin's finding that heterogeneity ($\nu_i$) and clustering ($\phi_i$) precisions "cannot be arbitrarily vague for the same reason" [PAGE 177].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
No strategic adversaries exist in this corpus, so a literal theory-of-mind reduction is empty. The honest analog is the **modeler–data feedback loop** the book repeatedly warns about, plus the **MCMC "clock."**

"Crisis habit regression" → **shrinkage toward the prior mean under sparse counts**. The book states the mechanism directly: "the high values would tend to be pulled down, the low values to be pushed up. This is the notion of smoothing" [PAGE 83]. In Poisson–gamma, $p(\theta_i\mid y_i)=G(y_i+a,\,E_i+b)$ [PAGE 173]; the posterior mean is a precision-weighted compromise between the raw rate and the grand mean (Exercise 8, [PAGE 185]). Under a spatial CAR prior this shrinkage is spatially structured, which is why "a relatively low $p_D$ may indicate… overshrunk random effects" [PAGE 121].

"Counter-trap" → the set of inferential self-deceptions the text names: reading a spatial story into a map before fitting $\mu(s)$ ("Such displays may be deceiving… It seems more sensible to study spatial pattern in the residuals" [PAGE 55]); fitting the empirical variogram as if it were data ("this shows the folly of fitting a curve to this data: we have a weak signal, and a great deal of noise" [PAGE 151]); and confusing the SAR/CAR spatial parameter with association strength ("$\alpha$ can mislead with regard to strength of association" [PAGE 95]).

"Clock" → the convergence budget. The book's stopping rule is Gelman–Rubin $\hat R$ [PAGE 131, eq 4.9], verbatim "√R̂ = √[ (N−1)/N + (m+1)/(mN) · B/W ] · df/(df−2)", which "must approach 1 as N→∞," backed by ESS/autocorrelation-time truncation and batch-means variance [PAGE 133]. This is the only "clock" the object supports.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Reinterpreted honestly as **model-admissibility vetoes** — proof that a proposed operator yields no valid joint density. Each is a genuine theorem in the source.

1. **Dimension veto (spherical).** The spherical model "is valid in $r=1,2,3$ dimensions, but for $r\ge4$ it fails to correspond to a spatial variance matrix that is positive definite" [PAGE 39]. Proof obligation: for $r\ge4$ there exist locations whose covariance matrix has a negative eigenvalue ⇒ no Gaussian density exists.

2. **Nested-anisotropy veto.** "This does not define a valid process model: the resulting covariance matrix for an arbitrary set of locations need not be positive definite. This can be seen with as few as three points and two angle classes" [PAGE 53]. The explicit counterexample sets $\rho(s_1-s_2)\approx0$ while $\rho(s_1-s_3)=\rho(s_2-s_3)\approx0.9$, producing a non-PD $3\times3$ matrix.

3. **Impropriety veto (intrinsic CAR).** $p(y)\propto\exp\{-\tfrac1{2\tau^2}\sum_{i\sim j}(y_i-y_j)^2\}$ is "identified only up to an additive constant. Thus to identify an intercept term $\beta_0$… we must add the constraint $\sum_i\phi_i=0$" [PAGE 177]. Without it the joint is improper and the intercept is unidentified.

4. **Weak-signal veto.** The "0.95-of-sill" range definition $\gamma(R)=0.95(\sigma^2+\tau^2)$ becomes $\rho(R;\theta)=0.05\cdot\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\tau^2)$; "this latter equation may not be solvable, e.g., if $\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\tau^2)\le0.05$, the case of very weak 'spatial story'" [PAGE 49]. Verdict: a spatial model is then inappropriate.

5. **Spectral-inversion veto.** Cressie–Huang nonseparable covariances are usable only where "the Fourier inversion can be obtained in closed-form… this occurs only in very special cases" [PAGE 287]; otherwise admissibility is unverifiable in closed form.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Reinterpreted as **falsifiable limiting-regime predictions** of the operators — each checkable against the equations above.

1. **Nugget→0 vs nugget>0.** Prediction: as $\tau^2\to0$, $\operatorname{Var}[Y(s_0)\mid y]\to0$ at any observed site and the predictor reproduces the data exactly; for any $\tau^2>0$ it does not. Verifiable from eq 2.13–2.14 and Exercise 10 [PAGE 65]. Falsifier: observing exact interpolation with $\hat\tau^2>0$ would contradict the model.

2. **Separable-spacetime range rigidity.** Prediction: under $\Sigma=\sigma^2 H_s(\phi)\otimes H_t(\psi)$ [PAGE 281] the marginal spatial correlation length is identical across all times and the coherence is $T_{ij}/\sqrt{T_{ii}T_{jj}}$, constant in $\mathbf s,\mathbf h$ [PAGE 231 eq 7.3]. Verifiable: fit marginal variograms by year (as in the Baton Rouge analysis [PAGE 279]) and check whether the estimated spatial ranges coincide; systematic divergence falsifies separability.

3. **Weak-signal non-identifiability.** Prediction: when the posterior mass of $\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\tau^2)$ sits below 0.05, the fitted range is unstable and $\phi$ is effectively unidentified (its credible interval spans the prior). Verifiable via the Scotland lip-cancer fits [PAGE 181], where $E(\phi\mid y)$ was already prior-sensitive. A stable, data-dominated $\phi$ posterior under this ratio would falsify the claim.

Each "veto" is stated as a mathematical or estimation prediction that can be checked against simulated or real data, not a forecast of an external contest.
