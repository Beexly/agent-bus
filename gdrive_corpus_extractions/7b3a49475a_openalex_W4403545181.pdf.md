Spearman’s rank correlation is used for feature selection. With no ties,
\[
\rho=1-\frac{6\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_i^2}{n(n^2-1)},
\]
where \(d_i\) is the rank difference for subject \(i\), \(n\) is sample size, and \(\rho\) is dimensionless on \([-1,1]\). With ties, the correct implementation is Pearson correlation on midranks. The paper reports a 5% significance level: “The statistical significance level for this test was taken as 5%” [PAGE 7]. In standard inference, statistical significance means rejection of a no-association null, not acceptance of an effect null.

SMOTE is described as a method that “generates synthetic data points for the minority class using K-Nearest Neighbours” [PAGE 3]. The standard interpolation is
\[
x_{\text{new}}=x_i+\lambda\left(x_{zi}-x_i\right),\qquad \lambda\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1),
\]
where \(x_i\) is a minority-class observation and \(x_{zi}\) is one of its \(k\) nearest minority neighbours in standardized feature space. The synthetic point is a vector in the same units as the original features. Breakdown regimes: if \(k\) is greater than or equal to the minority count, the neighbour search is ill-posed; with only 18 goalkeepers [PAGE 2], a large \(k\) can create implausible goalkeeper profiles; naive SMOTE on categorical or integer-coded features can create invalid hybrid records; and applying SMOTE before the train-test split leaks synthetic information into evaluation. The paper does not specify \(k\), split timing, or per-fold resampling, so the SMOTE result is not independently verifiable from the source text.

s_j=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{ij}-\bar{x}_j\right)^2},
\]
where \(x_{ij}\) is feature \(j\) for subject \(i\), \(\bar{x}_j\) is the sample mean, \(s_j\) is the sample standard deviation, and \(z_{ij}\) is dimensionless. The source says standardization “rescaled the values to have a mean of 0 and variance of 1” [PAGE 3]. Breakdown regimes: \(s_j=0\) gives division by zero; near-zero variance amplifies noise; and the 120 m maximum of 1298 in Table 2 [PAGE 4] is an extreme outlier relative to the median 12.96.
