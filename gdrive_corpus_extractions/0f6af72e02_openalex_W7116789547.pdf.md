$$A=\frac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN}\in[0,1],\qquad \mathrm{SE}(A)=\sqrt{\frac{A(1-A)}{N}}$$
with $A$ dimensionless (a probability), $N$ the evaluation-set cardinality. For $A=0.7422$ and $N=100$, $\mathrm{SE}\approx0.044$; for $N=30$, $\mathrm{SE}\approx0.080$. Assumption: exchangeable, prevalence-matched test set. Breakdown regimes: (i) prevalence $\pi\to0$ or $1$ — $A\to$ majority-class rate and the metric loses discriminative content; (ii) $N<p$ — variance dominates; (iii) temporal leakage — $A$ inflates without bound toward 1. The correct companion operators, absent from the review, are $\mathrm{Precision}=TP/(TP+FP)$, $\mathrm{Recall}=TP/(TP+FN)$, and $\mathrm{AUROC}$.

**2. Support-vector machine primal.** The review names SVM, SVR and kernel variants [PAGE 3] without the objective:
$$\min_{w,b,\xi}\ \tfrac12\|w\|^2+C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_i\quad\text{s.t.}\quad y_i\big(w^\top\varphi(x_i)+b\big)\ge1-\xi_i,\ \xi_i\ge0$$
$w$ has units of inverse feature units; $C$ has units of [loss]·[feature]$^{-2}$ and is only dimensionless when features are standardised. Breakdown: kernel bandwidth $\sigma\to0$ (memorisation, $A\to1$ on train, $\to$ chance on test); $C\to\infty$ (hard margin, non-separable data, no feasible solution); class imbalance without cost weighting.

**3. Support-vector regression "97% prediction accuracy."** "SVR achieved 97% prediction accuracy" [PAGE 3]. SVR is a regressor; "accuracy" must be a normalised error, e.g.
$$\text{Acc}_{R}=1-\frac{\frac1N\sum_i|y_i-\hat y_i|}{\bar y},\qquad \bar y=\frac1N\sum_i y_i .$$
Assumption: $\bar y>0$ and bounded relative error. Breakdown: $\bar y\to0$ makes $\text{Acc}_R$ unbounded below; a single outlier can drive it negative; the metric is not comparable to a classification rate despite sharing the word "accuracy."

**4. Coefficient of determination.** "R² value approached 1" [PAGE 3].
$$R^2=1-\frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}=1-\frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2}$$
$R^2$ is dimensionless and can be negative. Breakdown: $SS_{\text{tot}}\to0$ (low-variance target, e.g. a dominant team's points) makes $R^2$ unstable and trivially large; in-sample fitting with $N\lesssim p$ pushes $R^2\to1$ mechanically. Adjusted $\bar R^2=1-(1-R^2)\frac{N-1}{N-p-1}$ is required and is not reported.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Three agents reason about each other here: the analyst who builds the model, the coach who consumes it, and the opponent who adapts to it.

*Crisis habit regression.* The analyst's habit is to report the metric that maximises apparent performance: 74.22% accuracy, 97% accuracy, $R^2$ approaching 1 [PAGE 3]. Under time pressure — match-week deadlines — the analyst regresses to the single-number report because it terminates discussion. The coach, receiving one number, treats it as a probability of success rather than a conditional estimate with a confidence interval, and the feedback loop closes: the coach's decisions become the training distribution for the next model, so the model learns to predict the coach, not the game. This is a self-fulfilling recursion. The review names the symptom — "enhancing model interpretability for practical applications" remains a "persistent challenge" [PAGE 2] — but not the mechanism.

*Counter-trap.* The opponent's staff observes the published model class (the review is public; the algorithms are named in Table 1 [PAGE 3–4]) and adapts. Any tactical model whose outputs influence selection becomes a target for adversarial perturbation: pressing triggers tuned to the model's feature weights, pass-lane choices that shift the receiver distribution away from the trained prior. The pass-receiver models — "Top-1: 84.1%, Top-3: 91.5%" [PAGE 4] — are especially exposed, because their candidate set is bounded at eleven and adaptation narrows the effective support. Goodhart's law is the formal statement: when the metric becomes the target, it ceases to be a good metric. Reinforcement-learning tactical agents [PAGE 6] are the most exposed of all, since they explicitly optimise against a modelled opponent that is, in reality, also learning.

*The clock.* Model value decays. Write $P(t)=P_\infty+(P_0-P_\infty)e^{-\lambda t}$ for the model's out-of-sample performance, with $P_0$ the deployment performance, $P_\infty$ the floor set by irreducible outcome noise, and $\lambda$ the drift rate in $\text{season}^{-1}$. The review's recommendation to use "multi-season datasets" [PAGE 4] raises $P_0$ but does nothing to $\lambda$; in fact pooling seasons with different $\lambda$ biases the estimate. The correct governance operator is a retraining trigger, e.g. retrain when the monitored $P(t)$ falls below $P_0-\kappa\,\mathrm{SE}$, with $\kappa$ chosen to bound false-alarm rate. The review supplies no such rule, so the analyst's habit fills the vacuum — retrain annually, or after a bad result, which is a superstition schedule rather than a control law.

The recursive equilibrium: the analyst trusts the number because the coach trusts the number because the analyst produced it. Breaking the loop requires an external anchor — a held-out, temporally future, prevalence-matched test set — which is precisely the element the review's thirty studies most often omit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A model can be vetoed by a layer that never appears in its feature matrix. Four layers adjudicate.

*Physical layer.* Sprint mechanics cap locomotion: elite maximal speed is bounded near 10–12 m·s$^{-1}$, acceleration near 5–8 m·s$^{-2}$, and GPS-derived "high-speed distance" is a proxy with its own error floor. Any injury-prediction operator must use load in SI units (m, m·s$^{-2}$) and must be benchmarked against an acute:chronic workload ratio; an SVM at "74,22 %" [PAGE 3] without a load specification cannot be distinguished from a model of fixture count. Veto condition: if the positive class is non-contact injury and the model's top features are calendar variables, the model is a schedule detector, not a tissue-risk model.

*Biomechanical layer.* Shot accuracy is governed by foot–ball impact angle and ball speed — the review records these as significant [PAGE 3]. An xG operator omitting $\theta$ and $v_{\text{ball}}$ is misspecified; the omitted-variable bias $\operatorname{plim}\hat\beta=\beta+\Gamma\delta$ is not hypothetical but measured by the review's own cited study. Veto: any xG-based "shooting efficiency" comparison [PAGE 4] across leagues is inadmissible unless the shot-parameter distribution is matched or reweighted.

*Weather and surface layer.* Wind vector alters ball trajectory and thus both shot outcomes and pass completion; precipitation alters pitch traction and thus acceleration profiles and GPS-derived speed metrics; heat load (WBGT above ~28–32 °C) alters pacing and total distance. Because leagues differ systematically in these variables, cross-league coefficient transfer is vetoed a priori. *Calendrical and competition layer.* Fixture congestion, continental travel, and squad-rotation policy differ across the leagues in Table 1 (EPL, CSL, Malaysian Super League, Bundesliga) [PAGE 4]. These are confounders for both injury and match-outcome targets.

*Veto proof sketch.* Let $U$ be an unmodelled layer variable correlated with both a retained feature $X$ and the target $Y$. Then $\hat\beta_X\xrightarrow{p}\beta_X+\gamma\,\delta$, where $\gamma$ is the projection of $U$ on $X$ and $\delta$ the true effect of $U$ on $Y$. A non-zero $\gamma\delta$ vetoes causal interpretation of $\hat\beta_X$ regardless of predictive accuracy. Predictive accuracy can survive this bias; the review's recommendations — "feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, and multi-season data" [PAGE 4] — address variance but not bias, and therefore cannot detect the veto.

**Veto 2 — temporal-shuffle leakage test on the passing models.** Counterfactual: retrain the pass-receiver Random Forest that reports "Top-1: 84.1%, Top-3: 91.5%" [PAGE 4] and the LightGBM model at "Top-5 accuracy reached 94%" [PAGE 4], but independently permute the time index of the feature tensor. All three vetoes share one structure: they hold the *reported metric* fixed and vary only the *evaluation protocol*. If the metric is robust to protocol variation, the finding stands; if it collapses, the finding was a property of the protocol and not of football. The review's own conclusion — that "data imbalance, limited standardized multimodal datasets, and low model transparency" persist [PAGE 6] — is precisely the admission that the protocols have not yet been standardised, which is why these vetoes are the necessary next experiment rather than a criticism of any single tabulated study.
