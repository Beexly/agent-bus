The trap is assuming that because the behavior appears volitional on the surface, it must be consciously controlled. In reality, repeated suggestion creates a conditioned reflex loop that bypasses executive oversight. This trap persists in forensic settings today, where assessors must distinguish between simulated symptoms and genuinely experienced negative hallucinations.

Furthermore, the historical context includes the rise of psychoanalysis. Freud initially used hypnosis but abandoned it for free association, partly due to such "resistance" phenomena. James’s detailed description of the "impotent spectator" prefigures Freud’s concept of the observing ego and the dynamics of repression, where part of the mind acknowledges what another part denies.
