Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Orthogonalize spatial and temporal cost. Construct two start-to-goal paths with equal centerline length \(\mathcal{C}_s(p_A) = \mathcal{C}_s(p_B)\) but different traversal times by covering path B with a high-viscosity strip that reduces \(v_i\) without changing \(\ell_i\). Veto condition: if rats continue to prefer path A because it was spatially shortest during pre-training, despite B now being temporally shorter, the pure temporal-optimization hypothesis is falsified. Prediction: model-based rats switch to B within a small number of trials; habit rats perseverate on A. Verification metric: path-choice frequency \(\pi(B)\) crossing 0.5 within N sessions.

Scenario 2: Hold temporal cost constant, vary spatial length. Install speed-matched moving walkways or adjust running surfaces so that \(\mathcal{C}_t(p_A) = \mathcal{C}_t(p_B)\) while \(\mathcal{C}_s(p_A) < \mathcal{C}_s(p_B)\). Veto condition: if rats prefer B (the longer spatial path) because it offers more salient visual landmarks, the spatial-distance minimization hypothesis is falsified. Prediction: under strict spatial optimization, \(\pi(A) \to 1\); under landmark-chaining, choice tracks landmark salience, not \(\mathcal{C}_s\).

Scenario 3: Sensory-deprivation replication of Higginson vs Valentine. After pre-training on a shortcut, test rats in darkness with uniform tactile walls and airflow that eliminates odor gradients. Veto condition: if immediate short-circuiting (Higginson effect) disappears and path length converges only gradually over many trials, or converges to a safety-compromised path, the claim of immediate cognitive-map shortcut is falsified. Prediction: gradual reduction in \(\mathcal{C}_s\) across trials; in the blind or dark condition, rats adopt a compromise trajectory consistent with \(J(p) = \mathcal{C}_s(p) + \lambda \mathcal{C}_{\text{risk}}(p)\) [PAGE 106], supporting Valentine’s null replication over Higginson’s immediate effect.
