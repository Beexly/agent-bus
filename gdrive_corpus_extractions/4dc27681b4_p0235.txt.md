Any field application must therefore stratify by ambient conditions or treat weather as a multiplicative suppressor on the motor-output layer only.

Adjudication rule: Consciousness/ideation is inferred only when (a) blockage is independently verified, (b) expectancy conflict is measured at or above threshold, and (c) no physical, biomechanical, or environmental veto is active on the motor-output layer. If any veto is active, the absence of running-back-and-forth cannot be used to reject the hypothesis that sign-gestalt-expectations are jammed [PAGE 235].

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

Three verifiable Reinforcement-Learning-from-Verifiable-Rewards (RLVR) veto scenarios with formal veto conditions and predictions.

Scenario 1 — Blockage without expectancy jam (dominance veto).
Veto condition: B(t)=1 but one sign is already strongly predictive, e.g., P(g_+|s_w)=0.90 and P(g_-|s_b)=0.90, so C(s) \approx 0.14 bits < C^*.
Prediction: Despite sustained blockage, the animal exhibits no significant running-back-and-forth; it makes a single corrected choice toward the high-expectancy side and latency to choice remains low.
Verification: Measure choice latency and number of side transitions during blocked trials; predict transition count near baseline (≈1) and latency significantly below jammed-state trials. If frequent alternation is observed under high expectancy dominance, Tolman's blockage-plus-jam hypothesis is vetoed.

Scenario 2 — Expectancy jam without blockage (free-choice veto).
Veto condition: B(t)=0 (no obstruction; both sides freely accessible) but the contingency is ambiguous, so C(s_w) \approx C(s_b) \approx 1 bit and P(g_+|s_w) \approx P(g_+|s_b) \approx 0.5.
Prediction: High entropy persists, but because there is no practical blockage, the animal does not pause to run back and forth; it chooses one side and proceeds. I(t) remains 0 despite maximal C(s).
Verification: Compare blocked vs. unblocked ambiguous-cue sessions. Predict ideational running occurs only in the blocked condition. If running-back-and-forth appears at equal frequency in unblocked ambiguous sessions, the blockage trigger is falsified.

Scenario 3 — Expectancy-circuitry interruption (neural veto).
Veto condition: A reversible lesion or chemogenetic inhibition prevents formation or maintenance of differential sign-gestalt-expectations, so P(g|s) remains near the initial uniform distribution (0.5, 0.5) for both signs but the animal cannot represent "good" vs. "bad" significates as distinct prospective outcomes; effectively C(s) is high but not structured as a conflict over significates.
Prediction: Under blockage, the animal may show undirected locomotor agitation but not the specific organized back-and-forth scanning between the two sign locations; the ideational signature is absent because the expectancies are not "jammed relative to one another" in a meaningful sign-significate geometry [PAGE 235].
Verification: Use place-cell or expectation-related neural recordings to confirm loss of differential coding; compare behavioral topography during blockage before and after inactivation. If organized vicarious trial-and-error disappears while general activity remains, the expectancy-jam mechanism is supported; if organized scanning persists without differential expectancy coding, the theory is vetoed.
