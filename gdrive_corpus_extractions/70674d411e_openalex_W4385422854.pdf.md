<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

Model three recursive agents inside the decision loop and separate their signatures.

**Agent 1 — the crisis-habit regressor.** Under a 3-match losing run, a coaching analyst reverts to the habit distribution: the simplest, most legible model. Formally the reversion is a softmax over model complexity with an inverse-temperature that rises under stress:
$$P(\text{model}=m\mid \text{crisis})=\frac{\exp\!\left(-c(\tau)\,C_m\right)}{\sum_{m'}\exp\!\left(-c(\tau)\,C_{m'}\right)},\qquad \frac{\partial c}{\partial\tau}<0$$
where C_m is model complexity (or parameter count) and τ is time-to-next-fixture. Units: C_m dimensionless (or parameter count, nats of description length); c(τ) in nats⁻¹. **Signature to detect:** the analyst switches from the fused ensemble to a single decision tree *and* reports the training accuracy rather than the cross-validated column — exactly the Γ inflation catalogued in O1. The paper's own data predicts this: Decision Tree has Γ = 0.1100, near the top.

**Agent 2 — the counter-trap.** The opponent (or the market) anticipates reversion-to-habit and shifts its own behavior to exploit the predictable, low-entropy policy. Formalized as a mutual-information maximization by the counter-party:
$$\max_{\pi_{\text{opp}}}\ \mathbb{I}\!\left(\pi_{\text{habit}};\ \pi_{\text{opp}}\right)\ \text{[bits]},\qquad \mathbb{I}=H(\pi_{\text{habit}})-H(\pi_{\text{habit}}\mid\pi_{\text{opp}})$$
The habit policy is *low-entropy and public* (it is the default tree, the league-standard evaluation), so I is large and the counter-trap is cheap. **Signature:** the reverted model's out-of-sample accuracy degrades *faster* than its in-sample accuracy — the gap widening is diagnostic of exploitation, not of drift.

**Agent 3 — the clock.** The fixture calendar imposes a hard decision interval Δt that is shorter than the label latency L of every model in this review. Injury labels lag by weeks [PAGE 4]; transfer-value labels lag by windows [PAGE 3]; form labels lag by the head-to-head window [PAGE 2]. The veto condition is
$$\Delta t < L\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{information set is stale by construction},\quad \text{CE} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[-\log_2 \hat{p}(y_{\text{obs}})\right]\ \text{[bits]}$$
with cross-entropy CE as the realized penalty. **The recursion:** the crisis-habit agent *knows* the clock is short, so it prefers the fast model; the counter-trap agent *knows* the habit is fast, so it prices the habit; the clock *knows* neither and simply closes. The stable equilibrium is not a better model — it is a **shorter latency label pipeline** (injury ascertainment at session end rather than at diagnosis; exposure tracking per session rather than per match). This is the only move that exits the recursion, and it is absent from the source document, which offers no latency discussion at all.

**Discriminating the three.** Habit-regression → Γ widens, model complexity drops, accuracy metric substituted for proper score. Counter-trap → Γ widens *asymmetrically* toward the opponent's known preferences; entropy of the played policy falls. Clock → Γ is stable but calibration drifts monotonically with Δt; CE rises while accuracy is flat. Only the third is curable by more data.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**V1 — Physical layer: ball–surface interaction and pass-success drift.** The pass success rate is an explicit input to the scoring systems at [PAGE 2] (*"goals, assists, pass success rates, and defensive contribution"*). Under a water film of thickness δ, the traction coefficient falls, μ(δ) < μ_dry, and ball roll decelerates differently; the observable consequence is a **shift in the conditional distribution** P(pass success | W). **V2 — Biomechanical layer: anthropometrics cannot carry acute load.** [PAGE 4] asserts anthropometric sufficiency: *"models based on anthropometrics that effectively predict a player's risk of injury."* Biomechanically, hamstring strain risk is governed by the interaction of eccentric knee-flexor torque (N·m), torque asymmetry (%), and *acute* neuromuscular fatigue accumulating within and across sessions — a state variable, not a trait variable. Anthropometrics are **trait** covariates (m, kg, limb length). A trait-only hazard (O7) has a null space: two players with identical anthropometrics but different cumulative session load have identical predicted h_i(t), which is falsified whenever load is the dominant driver. **Proof obligation:** add a time-varying load covariate and test the proportional-hazards assumption (Schoenfeld residuals); a significant time trend in β for the anthropometric block vetoes the "effectively predict" wording.

**V3 — Weather/environment layer plus competition-structure layer.** Weather is named at [PAGE 2] as a result-determining factor (*"including home and away games, weather, player fatigue, past head-to-head records of both sides, recent team form"*) yet is absent from every model reported. Additionally, at the robot layer [PAGE 4] the physics is different but the pattern is identical — the detector's "Point" class (AP 0.912, recall 85.76 %) fails under field-lighting and marker-occlusion variation that the table does not stratify. **Proof obligation:** stratify Table 2 by illumination/occlusion bin; a monotone AP decay across bins confirms an environment-vetoed claim rather than a class-intrinsic limit.
