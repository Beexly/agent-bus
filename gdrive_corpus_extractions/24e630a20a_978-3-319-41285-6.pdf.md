The genealogy here is not a genealogy of literal backward-in-time signalling. The source repeatedly denies that space-like correlations require superluminal or backward signals; it instead treats them as correlations fixed by common past and by an “ontology conservation law” [PAGE 57–58, 79–80]. Dirac’s positron trick and the Jordan–Wigner transformation are the technical ancestors of the fermionic part [PAGE 103–104, 147–150]; BCH and the locality/positivity problem are the technical descendants [PAGE 73–74, 141–144].

The multi-week traps are predictable. First trap: treating the wave function as ontic in the ordinary template basis. The source says the ontological wave function contains only a single 1 and zeros; templates are superpositions [PAGE 34, 67–68]. Second trap: treating the energy basis as the ontological basis. The source warns that this gives a static classical world and is “not useful” [PAGE 75]. Third trap: using decoherence as a collapse mechanism. The CAI says collapse is automatic because classical states are beables, not because off-diagonal terms are erased [PAGE 37, 52–53, 67–68]. Fourth trap: assuming local counterfactual definiteness or unrestricted free will. The source explicitly rejects counterfactual definiteness [PAGE 78, 112] and redefines free will as freedom to choose an initial state, not as an uncaused reset [PAGE 45–47]. Fifth trap: forgetting that the Bell reconstruction needs three-point correlations and thermodynamic suppression of counterfactual histories. Seventh trap: ignoring the unfinished Planck-scale and interaction programme. A correlation can look as if the future setting reached back to the source, but the source’s proposed explanation is that the source, the detector, the observer, and the setting all share a common deterministic ontology. The quasars “seem to conspire” only because ontological nature is conserved in time [PAGE 79–80]. The trap is to convert that conservation law into a new causal channel. The source explicitly says the laws are local but the correlation functions are not [PAGE 17]. That distinction is the historical and conceptual spine.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
The recursive observer problem is sharpest in the mouse/quasar version of Bell. Alice and Bob are not external spectators; they are physical systems whose decisions have roots in the past [PAGE 18, 41–45]. The source’s “free will” replacement is not a denial that observers choose settings; it is a denial that the choice can be treated as an uncaused local reset while Bob, the photons, and the distant past are held fixed [PAGE 45–47]. The mathematical surrogate is freedom to choose an initial state, but the price is that a counterfactual setting change back-evolves to a different and generally less probable past state [PAGE 59–60]. This is the recursive loop: the observer’s model of the experiment is itself part of the ontology being modeled.

“Crisis habit regression” names the trained habit of reifying the template description. After a century of successful quantum calculations, the mind instinctively says: if \(|\psi_1\rangle\) and \(|\psi_2\rangle\) are possible, then \(\alpha|\psi_1\rangle+\beta|\psi_2\rangle\) is a possible real state. The CAI counters that this is a habit of the template basis, not a law of ontology. The dead cat and live cat are ontological states; the superposition is a template [PAGE 67–68]. The regression is to ask “when exactly did the collapse happen?” The source answers that the collapse is not a timed physical process inside the ontological evolution; it is the mismatch between template bookkeeping and beable reality [PAGE 52–53, 67–68]. The habit returns whenever one forgets that inner products of two templates are not generally ontic probabilities; only \(\langle A|\psi\rangle\) with \(A\) ontological has that status [PAGE 70–71].

“Counter-trap” names the attempt to defeat superdeterminism by imagining a single-setting change while holding everything else fixed. The source’s reply is that the back-evolved photon state acquires a spin-2 admixture and the initial state becomes thermodynamically suppressed [PAGE 59–60]. The trap is not that the mathematics forbids the counterfactual; the trap is that the counterfactual is not a physically neutral operation. In a deterministic ontology, changing Alice’s apparatus is a change in the ontological state, and the conservation law forbids a non-ontological template from being freely selected [PAGE 80, 112–115]. The mind that thinks it can “just rotate the polarizer” is implicitly assuming a local counterfactual definiteness that the CAI rejects [PAGE 78].

“Clock” names the positive alternative: the deterministic tick \(\delta t\), the periodic orbit, and the phase \(e^{-iHt}\). In the cogwheel, the phase records where the ontological state sits in its cycle [PAGE 36–38]. In interference, the source explains phase dependence by multiple ontological paths reaching an out-state with different phase alignments; if two paths are in phase, probability increases, and if out of phase, it decreases [PAGE 86]. This is a clock-like, non-mystical account of phase, but it is not a complete derivation of optical interference from a known Planck-scale automaton. The recursive mind must therefore hold two levels simultaneously: at the template level it uses Schrödinger amplitudes and Born probabilities; at the ontological level it treats the universe as a single beable state with a deterministic clock. The source’s own warning is that no intelligent being can compute faster than Nature itself [PAGE 68], so the observer’s inability to predict the outcome is not evidence for ontic randomness.

The strongest recursive claim is the “ontology conservation law”: ontological states evolve only into ontological states, and this conserved information prevents Alice and Bob from choosing settings that would require a non-ontological template [PAGE 80, 115]. This is a theory of mind only insofar as minds are physical: their decisions are correlated with the source, not magically free of it. The source puts it bluntly: “mice guts also obey energy-, momentum- and angular momentum conservation because these are general laws” [PAGE 57]. The recursive theory of mind therefore has three regimes: crisis-habit regression, where collapse is reified; counter-trap, where a local counterfactual is assumed without its thermodynamic past-cost; and clock, where deterministic phase and ontology conservation replace the mystery. The first two are cognitive failures relative to the CAI; the third is the proposed disciplined habit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A cross-layer veto is a rule that rejects an interpretation move when it violates a constraint at a more basic layer. The source supplies four such constraints. First, the automaton update is local:
\[

\]
with inverse \(Q(\vec x,t-1)=Q(\vec x,t+1)-F(\vec x,\{Q(t)\})\) [PAGE 57–58]. Second, no superluminal signal is allowed: space-like separated observables commute in ordinary quantum field theory [PAGE 32]. Third, a physical Hamiltonian must be bounded below and, if possible, extensive/local [PAGE 101–102]; the source admits the conflict between positivity and locality [PAGE 143–144]. Fourth, Planck/holographic counting bounds information density [PAGE 90, 107–108]. Any claimed mechanism that violates these is vetoed or demoted to a template-level correlation.

Physical-layer veto: Bell/CHSH cannot be explained by a signal from Alice’s detector to Bob’s photon. The source’s own mechanism is a three-point correlation \(W(a,b,c)\) [PAGE 56] plus ontology conservation [PAGE 57–58, 80]. The CAI passes only conditionally, because the source itself admits the microscopic realization is incomplete [PAGE 103–105, 116–117].
