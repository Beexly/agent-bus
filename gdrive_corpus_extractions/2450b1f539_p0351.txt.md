<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
These postulates represent the apex of Edward Tolman E. Hull's neo-behaviorist synthesis, aiming to provide a calculus for psychology akin to Newtonian mechanics. Historically, they build upon Thorndike's law of effect but reject purposive explanations in favor of quantitative drive-reduction mechanisms. The genealogy traces back to Weber-Fechner psychophysics (linear/logarithmic scaling in Postulates 14/15) and Gauss's error theory (the ogival function in Postulate 12). 

In terms of multi-week traps, relying solely on Hull's linear and monotonic assumptions creates a severe blind spot in modern predictive modeling. Hull assumes a static environment where "other things equal" holds true. However, in dynamic real-world scenarios (e.g., financial markets, prolonged stress environments), the "drive" ($D$) component feeding into $sER$ undergoes recursive feedback. A trap arises when one uses Postulate 14 (linear increase in extinction resistance) to predict human quitting behavior over weeks; in reality, the *perceived value* of reinforcement decays exponentially, causing $sER$ to drop faster than the linear model anticipates. Another trap involves Postulate 16's deterministic winner-take-all logic. Over a multi-week period, biological systems adapt via neuroplasticity. A reaction that was previously "incompatible" might develop parallel pathways, allowing concurrent execution (e.g., learning to type while talking), thereby shattering the rigid exclusivity assumed in Postulate 16. Retrocausal analysis reveals that Hull's formalisms, while elegant for micro-temporal slices (milliseconds to minutes), fail to account for the macro-temporal evolution of the reaction space itself.
