The deliberate-feint prohibition acknowledges that dead-ball restarts are vulnerable to temporal deception: a punter can fake the kick to draw opponents across the line, then snap or punt before they recover.

The multi-week trap embedded in this rule structure is exploitable across a season. Because the penalty for a successful feint is only a re-punt plus a 5-yard spatial regression—not loss of possession or down—the expected cost of repetition is low. A team can use deliberate feints in early weeks to condition opponents to jump at the whistle. Once opponents are conditioned to flinch, the offense can withhold the feint in a critical later week, execute a hard count or sudden snap, and draw a genuine offside penalty under Section 3, moving the opponent restraining line 5 yards back of their goal line [PAGE 121]. The referee’s mandatory waiting period after a feint (“until the oppoñents have time to return”) becomes a rhythmic weapon: the punter controls the delay duration, disrupting the opponent’s pre-snap cadence. Over multiple weeks, this creates a trap where disciplined opponents are punished for restraint and undisciplined opponents are punished for aggression, with the referee’s subjective timing gate as the decisive variable [PAGE 121].
