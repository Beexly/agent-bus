A counter-prediction is that if the keeper’s low-dive frequency does not shift with pressure, the counter-trap channel is weak and the habit channel is favored.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The behavioral claim is vulnerable to a cross-layer veto: physical, biomechanical, and weather variables can generate the same negative volume-conversion correlation without any optics utility. Let the true shot-location response be
\[
y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1\pi_i+\beta_2\tau_i+\beta_3c_i+\epsilon_i,
\]
where \(c_i\) is unobserved controllability (plant-foot stability, hip rotation, ankle inversion, surface traction, wind-induced lift). If the estimated model omits \(c_i\), the probability limit of the off-target coefficient is
\[
\hat\beta_2\to\beta_2+\beta_3\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(\tau,c)}{\operatorname{Var}(\tau)}.
\]
If \(\beta_3>0\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\tau,c)>0\), then \(\hat\beta_2\) is biased downward, potentially becoming negative even when \(\beta_2=0\). The paper’s own physical description supplies the mechanism: top corner partitions have “10 feet of post-or-crossbar borders versus 4 feet for bottom-corner partitions” [PAGE 8]. Weather enters explicitly through “different weather conditions and different goalkeepers” [PAGE 5]. Wind affects lift and curl; rain and turf affect plant-foot traction; cold affects ball stiffness and proprioception. These are physical vetoes, not reputational ones.

The adjudication proof is a stratification test. If the negative correlation and the negative off-target coefficient persist within zero-wind, dry-surface, same-keeper, same-fatigue strata, then the physical veto fails and the behavioral interpretation survives. If the negative coefficient attenuates toward zero when controllability is held constant, the physical veto succeeds. The breakdown regime is non-identification: when \(\operatorname{Var}(c)\) is large relative to \(\operatorname{Var}(\tau)\), the behavioral parameter \(\beta_2\) is not separately identified. A second breakdown is mechanical complementarity: if a partition has a structural zero save rate, as the paper says for top-right [PAGE 5], then \(\pi_i=\tau_i\) in that partition and \(\tau_i=1-x_{2i}\), so conversion and off-target are exact complements. A third breakdown is keeper heterogeneity: if elite keepers are better at low saves but not high saves, then top shots have both lower save rates and higher miss rates, which can produce the observed negative correlation without optics. The cross-layer veto is therefore not speculative; it is a formal omitted-variable and stratification problem. The paper’s behavioral conclusion is admissible only if the physical and weather layers are controlled or shown to be uncorrelated with the top-versus-bottom partition contrast.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

Scenario 1: weather-stratified reanalysis. Verifiable test: reconstruct the 536-penalty sample with match-level wind speed, precipitation, temperature, and surface state; estimate the linear model within dry/no-wind and wet/windy strata. Prediction under the behavioral hypothesis: \(\beta_2<0\) with a confidence interval excluding zero in both strata, and the top-minus-bottom miss gap remains near the reported \(12.88\%\) versus \(2.48\%\) [PAGE 8]. Prediction under the physical veto: \(\beta_2\) attenuates by at least \(50\%\) or its confidence interval includes zero in the dry/no-wind stratum, and the top-bottom miss gap shrinks substantially. If the dry/no-wind stratum shows no negative off-target coefficient while the full sample does, the behavioral claim is vetoed as weather-confounded.

Scenario 2: biomechanical controllability perturbation. Verifiable test: use instrumented plant-foot and hip-rotation data, or a randomized surface-traction intervention, to improve top-shot controllability without changing the public visibility of misses. Prediction under the physical hypothesis: when controllability improves, top-partition volume rises and the negative top-volume/top-conversion correlation weakens by at least \(30\%\); the off-target coefficient moves toward zero. Prediction under the behavioral hypothesis: top-partition volume and the negative correlation are unchanged because the shooter’s optics utility has not changed. If the controllability intervention changes top volume but not the off-target coefficient, the effect is physical execution, not optics. If it changes neither, the physical veto fails.

Scenario 3: optics anonymity randomization. Verifiable test: randomized feedback conditions in training or closed-door matches, where one condition makes missed shots publicly visible and another makes misses private or non-displayed. Prediction under the behavioral hypothesis: the private-miss condition increases top-partition shot volume by at least \(10\%\) and reduces \(|\beta_2|\) relative to the public-miss condition. Prediction under the non-behavioral counterfactual: top volume and the off-target coefficient are invariant to miss visibility. If anonymity has no effect on top-partition allocation, then “looking credible” is not the operative channel, and the paper’s behavioral interpretation is vetoed even if the negative correlation remains.
