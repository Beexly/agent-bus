Hop 1 — Residency precedes speed. A network must first *fit* in on-chip/DRAM capacity before any throughput claim is meaningful. [PAGE 10] states low-end memory "ranging from a few hundred kilobytes (kB) to a few megabytes (MB)"; [PAGE 14, Table 2] pins the SparkFun Apollo3 at 384 KB. A parameter tensor of $P$ weights at bit-width $b$ occupies $M=Pb/8$ bytes. ResNet-50 ($\approx 2.56\times10^{7}$ params, [PAGE 22]) at FP32 needs $102.4\ \text{MB}$ — 267× the Apollo3 budget; INT8 needs $25.6\ \text{MB}$ (67× over); even 1-bit needs $3.2\ \text{MB}$ (8.3× over). So a hard *residency veto* fires before speed is ever discussed. This is the first-order filter the paper implies but never states numerically.

Hop 2 — Once resident, the roofline governs throughput. Attainable rate is $P_{\text{attain}}=\min(P_{\text{peak}},\,I\beta)$ with arithmetic intensity $I$ in $\text{FLOP·Byte}^{-1}$ and bandwidth $\beta$ in $\text{Byte·s}^{-1}$. Edge accelerators are almost always left of the ridge point $I^\star=P_{\text{peak}}/\beta$ (memory-bound), which is precisely why the survey pushes structured sparsity ([PAGE 22]) and shift-add ([PAGE 60]) — techniques that lower the numerator of transferred bytes, not just FLOPs.

Hop 3 — Quantization moves both budgets simultaneously. The bit-width scaling $M_b=M_{32}\cdot b/32$ maps FP32→INT8 as 4× footprint and (on integer MAC hardware) roughly comparable compute reduction. [PAGE 26] notes "weights originally represented as 32-bit floating-point numbers can be quantised to 8-bit integers," and [PAGE 27] reports a ResNet-50 mixed-precision point at "top–1 accuracy of 75. 3% on ImageNet while reducing model size by 4× and inference latency by 3×." Note the asymmetry: 4× size but only 3× latency — bandwidth and compute do not scale in lockstep, consistent with a partially memory-bound regime.

Hop 4 — Energy is where the paper's own Table 3 becomes decisive. [PAGE 18] lists YOLOv3 on three boards with power (W) and "Inference time (s)." $$\text{depth}=\alpha^{\phi},\ \text{width}=\beta^{\phi},\ \text{res}=\gamma^{\phi},\quad \alpha\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}\approx 2,\ \alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge 1.$$
