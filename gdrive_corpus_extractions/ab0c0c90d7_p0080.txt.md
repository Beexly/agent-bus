Veto condition: measurable efferent modulation of sensory receptors during central imagery. Example: the olivocochlear efferent system suppresses cochlear amplifier gain during attention and imagined listening. If outer-hair-cell motility changes correlate with imagery vividness, then \vec{J}_{\text{imag} \to \text{organ}} \neq 0 at the physical layer even without conscious sound. Breakdown regime: otoacoustic-emission suppression during auditory imagery tasks; James’s Reason 1 is vetoed as a universal claim.

Biomechanical layer veto. James supposes the sense-organ is “left out” in imagination [PAGE 70]. Veto condition: visual imagery accompanied by ocular microsaccades and scanpaths that drive retinal stimulation. If eye-tracking shows reproducible retinal slip during imagined scene inspection, the peripheral organ is not excluded. Breakdown regime: vivid visual imagery with fixation; the biomechanical loop (motor command → extraocular muscle → retinal image) re-enters the sensory stream, falsifying the pure-cerebral seat claim.

Weather / environmental layer veto. James denies any borderline where weak sensation and strong imagination become confusable [PAGE 70]. Veto condition: manipulation of the external noise floor. In an anechoic chamber, signal-to-noise ratio SNR → 0, so a weak real tone and a strong imagined tone may occupy the same decision region. The signal-detection expression d′ = (μ_real − μ_imag)/σ collapses as σ (internal + external noise) dominates the mean separation. Breakdown regime: SNR below the discrimination threshold; the “almost absolute” quality difference [PAGE 70] is vetoed by physics, not phenomenology. Adjudication rule: a veto at any layer invalidates the corresponding universal clause, but not necessarily the whole theory; James’s claims hold only in the intact, unmodulated, moderate-noise regime he implicitly describes.

Scenario 1 — Continuum veto. Counterfactual: an RL agent assumes imagination and sensation lie on one intensity continuum (a “stronger/weaker” ratio model). Veto test: two-alternative forced choice with subjective intensity equated via magnitude matching; present weak real tones and strong imagined tones. Verifiable metric: proportion correct P(C) and signal-detection d′. Prediction: P(C) > 0.55 (e.g., 0.65–0.75) and d′ > 0 at matched intensity, falsifying the continuum; James’s borderline denial is upheld only if discrimination remains above chance at all intensities.

Scenario 2 — Peripheral silence veto. Counterfactual: an agent assumes sense-organs are entirely quiescent during pure imagination (James’s common-case assumption on [PAGE 70]). Veto test: simultaneous central EEG/fMRI and peripheral recording (cochlear microphonics or ocular EMG / eye-tracking) during auditory and visual imagery. Verifiable metric: peripheral gain modulation amplitude correlated with imagery vividness (Pearson r, p < 0.05). Prediction: nonzero efferent modulation detected during vivid imagery, vetoing the “organ left out” claim as a blanket statement while leaving the phenomenological distinction intact.

Scenario 3 — Source-attribution clock veto. Counterfactual: an agent assumes the imagination/sensation source boundary is stable across days or weeks. Veto test: longitudinal source-monitoring task under 24–36 h sleep restriction, with daily confidence and reality judgments. Verifiable metric: false-alarm rate for imagined items and criterion β (log likelihood ratio at the decision boundary) while holding d′ constant. Prediction: after extended wakefulness, β shifts toward “external” (increased false alarms) while d′ remains unchanged—a criterion-drift regression, not a sensitivity collapse. This veto shows that the “almost absolute” boundary [PAGE 70] is state-dependent and can regress under crisis conditions without the two distributions merging.
