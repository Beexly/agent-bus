<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
A recursive game-theoretic reading requires three layers: the team’s own habit under stress, the opponent’s counter-model of that habit, and the clock/rest constraint that limits adaptation. Crisis habit regression is the tendency for a team to fall back on its lowest-entropy offensive identity when the game state becomes adverse. France is the clearest case: the paper reports “a higher percentage of rushing plays (80.7% ± 9.7%) compared to Italy (33.0% ± 14.7%) and other EU teams (46.0% ± 0.8%) (p &lt; 0.05)” [PAGE 4]. The BAL teams are closer to 1 bit. The paper does not measure opponent adjustments, but the mechanism is straightforward: a defense that models an 80% run tendency can allocate more defenders to run fits, creating a counter-trap.

Counter-trap logic is recursive because the offense can anticipate the defense’s anticipation. Austria is the paper’s example of adaptation: “the victorious Austrian team’s offensive strategy was close to BAL, with 58.3% of their total offensive plays being rushing plays” [PAGE 4], and the conclusion credits “the ability to adapt one’s game style during the tournament” [PAGE 7]. France, by contrast, is RUN in all three event rows [PAGE 4], and the paper reports the highest CV for drives, plays, and plays per drive [PAGE 4]. A high-CV RUN team may be simultaneously predictable in play type and volatile in drive length; those are different dimensions and should not be conflated.

The clock layer constrains this recursion. If rest intervals are only 40–50 s and are “not enough time for complete ATP-PCR repletion” [PAGE 6], then repeated high-intensity plays impose a physiological tax on both the habit and the counter-trap. The paper reports that “the mean playing time for RUN and PASS teams was longer than the average duration of plays for BAL teams (p &lt; 0.05)” [PAGE 1] and that BAL rest was shorter than RUN and PASS rest [PAGE 1]. Thus the clock can veto a purely cognitive account: even a well-timed counter-trap may fail if the team cannot recover between plays. In recursive theory-of-mind terms, the optimal policy is not simply “be balanced”; it is to preserve enough conditional entropy \(H(C \mid S)\) over game state \(S\) to avoid exploitation while respecting the metabolic recovery clock. The source does not estimate \(H(C \mid S)\), because it lacks play-by-play state variables in the reported tables; that is a breakdown regime, not a confirmation.
