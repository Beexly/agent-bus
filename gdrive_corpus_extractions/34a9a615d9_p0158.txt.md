Crisis habit regression describes the tendency of decision-makers, under pressure, to revert to cognitively cheap heuristics: fixed supply, static distribution, and the assumption that current prices embody all relevant information. Knight's chapter title—"CHANGE WITH UNCERTAINTY ABSENT"—is precisely the condition under which such habits are formally licensed; remove uncertainty, and the fixed-supply short-run model becomes coherent. The counter-trap is the inverse error: assuming that an equilibrium distribution exists when uncertainty is present. In a crisis, the "uncertainty absent" premise is false, so the short-run fixed-supply operator \( \mathcal{F}_{SR} \) no longer describes the environment, yet agents continue to behave as if it does. The clock is the adjustment interval \( \Delta t_{adj} \) between short-run fixity and long-run supply responsiveness. If the policy or bargaining horizon is shorter than \( \Delta t_{adj} \), the fixed-supply model applies; if longer, supply-as-function-of-price governs. Misreading the clock produces recursive misalignment: managers negotiate contracts as if supply were fixed when it is already adjusting, or unions strike as if supply were perfectly elastic when it is biologically and institutionally fixed for the season. The theory-of-mind recursion is that each side models the other as having already adapted to the long-run price signal, while the physical reality of fixed factor supply has not yet moved; the result is a distribution of income that neither short-run nor long-run theory predicts, because the clock was never explicit.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The economic model on [PAGE 143] is layered over physical, biomechanical, and environmental constraints that can veto its equilibrium predictions. 

Physical layer: Productive agencies are not abstract homogeneous units; they are energy- and matter-embodied stocks. If the energy input \( E \) is thermodynamically bounded and the model assumes \( \partial S_i / \partial p_j > 0 \) without an energy-resource constraint, the long-run supply operator is physically unrealizable. The veto condition is \( \dot{E} < \text{required } \dot{E} \) for the postulated output expansion.

Biomechanical layer: Labor supply is bounded by human physiological limits—hours per day, days per week, skill-acquisition time, and fatigue recovery. The short-run assumption \( \mathbf{S}_{SR} = \bar{\mathbf{S}} \) holds only if \( \bar{L} \) lies below the biomechanical ceiling. If wage-induced labor supply attempts to exceed sustainable work duration, the fixed-supply assumption is vetoed by the human body; the supply curve bends backward not by choice but by physiological necessity.

Weather/environmental layer: Agricultural and extractive supplies are functions of weather state \( \omega \). The long-run operator must be \( \mathbf{S}_{LR} = \mathbf{S}(\mathbf{p}, \boldsymbol{\tau}, \omega) \). Knight's "uncertainty absent" assumption excludes \( \omega \) shocks by fiat; in reality, a drought or flood makes supply stochastic, so the deterministic equilibrium \( \mathbf{D} = \mathbf{S} \) is vetoed unless weather is either held constant or modeled as a state variable with its own distribution.

Adjudication rule: The Knightian/Marshallian/Clarkian distribution model is admissible only when physical, biomechanical, and weather layers are explicitly either (a) held invariant over the horizon of analysis, or (b) entered as state variables in \( \mathbf{g} \) or \( \boldsymbol{\tau} \). Since [PAGE 143] abstracts away uncertainty and secular change simultaneously, it satisfies condition (a) only within a closed laboratory of thought; it fails as soon as any layer is allowed to vary.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: A short-run wage negotiation is modeled with fixed labor supply \( \bar{L} \) [PAGE 143], but within the bargaining window an immigration shock or visa-policy change increases the labor force. Verifiable veto: observed \( \partial L / \partial w > 0 \) inside the alleged short-run period. Prediction: The wage share of income will fall relative to the fixed-supply marginal-productivity prediction, and profit rates will not adjust as the short-run distribution model implies.

Scenario 2: Clark's static state is imposed (\( d(\cdot)/dt = 0 \)) over a multi-month forecast horizon, but measured total factor productivity grows at \( \text{TFP} > 0 \). Verifiable veto: positive TFP growth in national accounts during the period. Prediction: Long-run normal price will diverge from the short-run equilibrium price, and entrepreneurial profit will remain persistently positive despite the static-state model's implication of zero excess return.

Scenario 3: Knight's chapter assumes uncertainty absent, yet an uninsurable novelty shock arrives—a new production process with no objective probability distribution. Verifiable veto: realization of an event for which no ex ante insurable premium can be priced. Prediction: Positive economic profit emerges and is not competed away within the model's time frame, falsifying any distribution theory that treats profit as a transitory deviation from long-run equilibrium under certainty.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
