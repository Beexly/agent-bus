Wiener's examples of dots and dashes and telephone conversation [PAGE 4–5] are bounded by human perceptual and motor bandwidth. Veto condition: if demodulation shifts speech outside the 300–3400 Hz intelligibility band, or if the symbol rate exceeds the operator's copy speed, information is transmitted but not received by the human terminal. This veto overrides spectral efficiency: one cannot simply modulate voice to 20 kHz and expect human listeners to understand it after demodulation.

Weather/environmental layer veto: Temperature variation changes copper-line resistance and capacitance, producing carrier-frequency drift Δf_drift (Hz). Humidity alters insulation leakage and noise floor. Veto condition: if |Δf_drift| > G (guard band), adjacent channels collapse into crosstalk even when nominal spacing satisfies the separability inequality [PAGE 5]. This veto is time-varying and probabilistic, requiring adaptive guard bands or automatic frequency control.

Adjudication hierarchy: Physical veto is first and absolute (no signal through a dead medium). Biomechanical veto is second (the channel must terminate in a human-perceptible form). Weather veto is third and dynamic (mitigated by feedback control, not by static design). Economic optimization of carrier packing [PAGE 5] is admissible only after all three veto layers are satisfied.

Scenario 2: Unary-alphabet veto. Counterfactual: A telegraph circuit is constrained to a single symbol (only the letter "e" may be sent; the alternative "o" is removed from the alphabet) [PAGE 4]. Prediction: Mutual information I(X;Y) = 0 bits per transmission regardless of line quality or transmission duration; the receiver cannot distinguish mark from space without an external clock or a second symbol. Verification: Compute source entropy H(X) = log₂(1) = 0 bits and confirm received-symbol entropy H(Y|X) = H(Y), yielding zero information transfer.

Prediction: Intermodulation products appear at 2f₁ − f₂ and 2f₂ − f₁, corrupting a third nominally separated channel even when carrier spacing satisfies the linear separability inequality. Verification: Two-tone test with a spectrum analyzer; observe distortion products at the predicted sum/difference frequencies and measure degradation in the third channel's signal-to-distortion ratio.
