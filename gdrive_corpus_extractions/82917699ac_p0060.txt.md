<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ be a probability space where $\Omega$ is the state space, $\mathcal{F}$ a $\sigma$-algebra of events, and $P$ a probability measure. Units: probability is dimensionless, $P(E) \in [0,1]$.

Measurable risk (actuarial). A random payoff $X \in L^1(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ has known law $P$. Expected utility under a concave, strictly increasing utility $u: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is
\[
EU(X) = \int_{\Omega} u(X(\omega)) \, dP(\omega).
\]
The certainty equivalent is $CE = u^{-1}(EU(X))$. For a loss variable $L \ge 0$, the actuarial premium with zero loading is
\[
\pi_{ins} = E_P[L] = \int_{\Omega} L(\omega) \, dP(\omega).
\]
Because $\pi_{ins}$ fully compensates expected loss, bearing measurable risk yields no economic profit in equilibrium.

Knightian uncertainty (ambiguity). The decision-maker does not know $P$; instead she faces a set of priors $\mathcal{P} \subset \Delta(\Omega)$, where $\Delta(\Omega)$ is the simplex of probability measures on $(\Omega,\mathcal{F})$. Maxmin expected utility (Gilboa–Schmeidler) gives the value of action $a \in \mathcal{A}$:
\[
V(a) = \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \int_{\Omega} u\big(R(a,\omega)\big) \, dP(\omega),
\]
where $R(a,\omega)$ is the reward in currency units. The entrepreneurial profit operator is
\[
\pi^{*} = \sup_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \left[ \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} E_P[R(a,\omega)] - C(a) \right],
\]
with $C(a)$ the cost in currency units.

Information-theoretic measures. Shannon entropy of a discrete prior $p$:
\[
H(P) = -\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p(\omega) \log_2 p(\omega) \quad \text{bits},
\]
maximal at $H(P) = \log_2 |\Omega|$ for the uniform prior. Kullback–Leibler divergence between two priors:
\[
D_{KL}(P\|Q) = \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} p(\omega) \log_2 \frac{p(\omega)}{q(\omega)} \quad \text{bits},
\]
which is singular ($+\infty$) when $P$ is not absolutely continuous with respect to $Q$ (disjoint supports).

Assumptions: (i) $\mathcal{P}$ is convex and weak-* compact; (ii) $u$ is twice continuously differentiable, $u' > 0$, $u'' < 0$; (iii) rewards are integrable for every $P \in \mathcal{P}$.

Breakdown regimes. (1) Misspecification: the true data-generating process $P^* \notin \overline{\mathcal{P}}$, so the inner minimization optimizes over an incorrect set. (2) Non-ergodicity: the time average $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \delta_{\omega_t}$ does not converge weakly to any $P \in \mathcal{P}$, invalidating calibration from history. (3) Singularity at $\mathcal{P} = \{P\}$: ambiguity collapses to measurable risk, and competitive arbitrage drives $\pi^{*} \to 0$. (4) Singularity at $\mathcal{P} = \Delta(\Omega)$: total ignorance; no action dominates and the maxmin problem may admit no solution.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage begins on [PAGE 1] with Hawley’s “The Risk Theory of Profit,” Quarterly Journal of Economics, vol. VII, p. 468, and Carver’s “Risk Theory of Profits,” QJE vol. XV, pp. 456 f., plus Carver’s The Distribution of Wealth, chap. VI, and A. A. Young in Ely’s Outlines of Economics, 3d ed., chap. xxv. Knight (1921), page 45 [PAGE 1], intervenes in this same lineage and publishes in the same year as Keynes’s Treatise on Probability (1921), which likewise separates measurable risk from unmeasurable uncertainty. The scientific descendants are direct: Ellsberg’s ambiguity paradox (1961) formalizes the violation of subjective expected utility when agents face unknown urn compositions; Gilboa and Schmeidler (1989) axiomatize maxmin expected utility over a set of priors; Hansen and Sargent develop robust control for model misspecification in macroeconomics; and the 2008 financial crisis is widely interpreted through Knightian uncertainty—liquidity vanished precisely because market participants could not assign probabilities to tail events.

Multi-week traps emerge from this lineage when decision horizons extend over weeks rather than milliseconds. Trap A (calibration trap): after a quiescent multi-week window, an agent estimates $\hat{P}$ from recent returns and treats it as an objective law; when a regime shift occurs, losses exceed the model’s confidence bounds. Trap B (insurance trap): insuring every measurable risk creates the illusion that all exposure has been transferred, while residual uninsurable Knightian uncertainty remains correlated across agents and produces systemic failure not captured by actuarial tables. Trap C (commitment trap): sunk costs in a multi-week venture are mistaken for sunk uncertainty, causing escalation of commitment because the agent cannot distinguish bad luck from a misspecified model.

Knight’s retro-causal insight reverses the causal arrow: profit is not paid because risk was borne, but because judgment was exercised under conditions where no actuarial market exists. Historically this shifts profit theory from a reward for abstinence (capital) or land ownership to a reward for decision-making under non-insurable uncertainty. The footnote on [PAGE 1] calling for an examination of “the conditions under which [risk-taking] will be successful” is the foundational program for modern ambiguity-averse decision theory.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the entrepreneur’s beliefs recursively over levels of intentionality. Level 0: nature generates states $\omega$ according to an unknown law $\theta$. Level 1: agent $i$ holds a belief $\mu_i(\theta)$ over probability laws. Level 2: agent $i$ holds beliefs about other agents’ beliefs, $\mu_i(\theta, \{\mu_j\}_{j \neq i})$. Given history $\mathcal{H}_t = \{\omega_1,\dots,\omega_t\}$, the recursive Bayesian update is
\[
\mu_i^{(t)}(\theta) \propto \mu_i^{(t-1)}(\theta) \prod_{s=1}^t p(\omega_s \mid \theta).
\]

Crisis habit regression occurs when stress collapses the agent from level-2 recursive reasoning to a level-1 plug-in estimate. The agent computes a maximum-likelihood law $\hat{\theta}_t = \arg\max_\theta \prod_{s=1}^t p(\omega_s \mid \theta)$ and then behaves as if $\hat{\theta}_t$ were the true, known probability law. This is the ergodicity trap: it assumes that a finite time series identifies the governing law, i.e., that $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \delta_{\omega_t} \Rightarrow P^*$ weakly. Formal regression condition: when cognitive load $L(t)$ exceeds a critical threshold $L_{crit}$, or neuroendocrine stress (cortisol $c(t) > c_{crit}$) impairs prefrontal computation, the agent cannot sustain the minimization $\min_{P \in \mathcal{P}_t}$ and reverts to empirical frequencies.

The counter-trap is to maintain recursive ambiguity and preserve optionality. Define an ambiguity set updated by set-valued Bayes:
\[
\mathcal{P}_t = \{ P : D_{KL}(P \| \hat{P}_t) \le \epsilon_t \}.
\]
The value of waiting is
\[
O(t) = \sup_{\tau \ge t} \left\{ \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}_{\tau}} E_P[e^{-r(\tau-t)} \pi(\omega_\tau)] \mid \mathcal{H}_t \right\} - \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}_t} E_P[\pi(a_{now})].
\]
The clock is an exogenous deadline $T$. As $t \to T$, the option value decays and the agent is forced to convert ambiguity into sunk commitment. A clock singularity occurs when information arrival stops before $T$ but ambiguity remains high: the decision must be made under maximal $\mathcal{P}_T$, and the maxmin action may be severely suboptimal ex post.

Breakdown: if the learning rate $\epsilon_t$ decays slower than the environmental non-stationarity, $\mathcal{P}_t$ never shrinks and the agent is paralyzed; if $\epsilon_t$ decays too fast, the agent overfits noise and mislabels randomness as structure.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The proposition “profit compensates for Knightian uncertainty” is a layered construct. It holds only if three independent layers pass veto inspection.

1. Physical layer. Capital stock $K$ depreciates at rate $\delta$, and thermodynamic irreversibility imposes entropy production $\dot{S} \ge 0$. Actuarial tables assume stationarity of physical decay. Veto condition: if realized depreciation $\delta_{real}$ exceeds the modeled $\delta$ such that no $P \in \mathcal{P}$ satisfies $E_P[\delta] = \delta_{real}$, the physical layer voids the model. Units: $\delta$ in $time^{-1}$, $\dot{S}$ in $J \cdot K^{-1} \cdot s^{-1}$.

2. Biomechanical layer. Bounded cognition and stress response constrain the computation of $\min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} E_P[u(X)]$. Let $L(t)$ denote cognitive load and $c(t)$ cortisol concentration. When $L(t) > L_{crit}$ or $c(t) > c_{crit}$, expected-utility maximization over an ambiguity set is computationally infeasible; the agent reverts to heuristics. Veto condition: the neurobiological constraint invalidates the assumption of a well-defined preference functional over $\mathcal{P}$.

3. Weather/exogenous layer. Macro shocks are modeled with tail index $\alpha$, where $P(|X| > x) \sim x^{-\alpha}$. If $\alpha \le 2$, variance is undefined and the law of large numbers / central limit theorem fail. Veto condition: Gaussian or light-tailed actuarial assumptions break when $\alpha \le 2$; fat tails generate correlated extremes that idiosyncratic risk pricing cannot capture.

Adjudication operator: let $\mathcal{V}_{\ell} \in \{0,1\}$ denote the veto indicator for layer $\ell \in \{physical, biomechanical, weather\}$. The aggregate veto is
\[
\mathcal{V} = \bigvee_{\ell} \mathcal{V}_{\ell}.
\]
If $\mathcal{V} = 1$, the profit-theory model is void for that trajectory. Singularity/breakdown regime: simultaneous veto across all three layers—e.g., a financial crisis in which physical capital is destroyed ($\delta_{real}$ spike), panic impairs biomechanical computation ($L > L_{crit}$), and macro shocks are fat-tailed ($\alpha \le 2$)—collapses $\mathcal{P}$ either to a single panic measure or to total ignorance $\Delta(\Omega)$, eliminating any calculable entrepreneurial profit rule.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with formal veto conditions and predictions.

Scenario 1: Complete insurance versus residual Knightian ambiguity.
Setup: A firm purchases full insurance for every measurable risk (property, liability, etc.). Verifiable data: policy contracts, premiums paid, and realized demand $D^*$. Veto condition: if $D^*$ falls outside the support of the insurer’s probability model $q$ such that $q(D^*) = 0$ while the true law satisfies $P^*(D^*) > 0$, then the firm was exposed to uninsurable uncertainty despite full actuarial coverage. RLVR prediction: profit will display excess kurtosis and negative skewness uncorrelated with insured loss events; the firm cannot be restored to its pre-shock state by insurance contracts alone.

Scenario 2: Crisis habit regression under regime shift.
Setup: A trader sets position size using a 52-week rolling historical 99% VaR. Verifiable data: daily profit-and-loss series and the corresponding VaR series. Veto condition: if a regime shift produces $D_{KL}(P_{new} \| P_{hist}) > \kappa$ and a VaR breach occurs within $W$ weeks, the habit of treating recent history as the objective law is falsified. RLVR prediction: historical VaR will underestimate the maximum drawdown by a factor strictly greater than 1, and breaches will cluster in time, violating the model’s independence assumption.

Scenario 3: Real-options deadline singularity.
Setup: An investor may invest immediately or wait one week to resolve ambiguity about a technology’s adoption. Verifiable data: expert-derived ambiguity set $\mathcal{P}_t$ and the actual exercise date $T$. Veto condition: if waiting yields negative option value $O(t) < 0$ at deadline $T$ because $\mathcal{P}_T$ expands rather than contracts, while immediate investment at $t$ had positive maxmin NPV, then the option to wait was a trap. RLVR prediction: in high-ambiguity, short-horizon environments, immediate commitment dominates waiting; the value of waiting is negative once the clock is binding.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
