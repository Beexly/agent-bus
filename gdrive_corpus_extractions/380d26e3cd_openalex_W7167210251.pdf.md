The derivation proceeds by treating the MMViT architecture as a constrained optimization over three competing operators—multi-head self-attention (MHSA), Multi-scale Pooling Attention (MPA), and Mamba state-space modules—allocated across a spatial-temporal pyramid. The core mathematical tension, stated explicitly on [PAGE 19], is that vanilla self-attention carries O(N²D) complexity for QKᵀ and another O(N²D) for the value-weighted sum, where N is sequence length and D is per-token dimension. MPA reduces this to O(N_q N_kv D) by independently pooling Q, K, and V with strided 3D convolutions (Equation 1, [PAGE 18–19]), such that N_q, N_kv ≪ N. The output spatial resolution collapses according to H′ = H/s_q and W′ = W/s_q (Equation 3, [PAGE 19]), yielding N_q = T × H × W / s_q² tokens (Equation 4, [PAGE 19]). This establishes the first gray-zone boundary: pooling Q determines output resolution hierarchy, while pooling K and V governs compute savings. If s_q is too aggressive, local inductive bias is lost; if too conservative, the quadratic wall re-emerges.

The second derivation hop concerns the Mamba middle stage. Mamba provides linear time complexity for long-range temporal modeling, but the authors identify a critical failure mode on [PAGE 8]: SSMs use sequential recursion, so information from frame 5 to frame 150 must pass step-by-step through hidden states, and any decay or interference loses the distant dependency. The A-M-A layout answers this by confining Mamba to intermediate stages (Scale 3 and Scale 4 in Table 5, [PAGE 23]) where sequence length has already been reduced by downsampling, yet features retain sufficient semantic maturity. The deep stage (Scale 5) then returns to MPA (Equation 1–2) because, at 8D × (T/2) × (H/32) × (W/32), the token count is small enough that quadratic attention is computationally negligible while providing stronger all-to-all semantic fusion than Mamba’s state-space recursion ([PAGE 22–23]).

The third hop is the Computation-Downsampling Decoupling (CDD) mechanism. Equation 5–7 on [PAGE 20] formalize the veto: X_Mamba = Mamba(X_in) at full resolution, then X_out = D(X_Mamba). The coupled alternative X_out = Mamba(D(X_in)) discards any fine-grained spatial information in the null space of D before sequence modeling, an irreversible information-theoretic loss. Empirically, removing CDD collapses SpaceJam accuracy from 82.74% to 63.65% (Δ19.09 points, [PAGE 13, Table 3]), which is not a marginal tuning effect but a structural breakdown of feature coverage.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The scientific lineage of MMViT traces backward through selective state-space modeling to classical control theory. The paper cites Kalman’s 1960 linear filtering and prediction formulation ([PAGE 32, Ref. 49]) as the conceptual root of state-space models, extended by structured state spaces (S4) in 2021 ([PAGE 32, Ref. 47–48]), then enriched by bilateral gating (H3, [PAGE 8, Ref. 50]) and global convolution parameterization (Hyena, [PAGE 8, Ref. 51]). Mamba (Selective SSM, Gu & Dao 2023, [PAGE 7, Ref. 21]) introduces memory masking and linear-time sequence modeling, which VideoMamba ([PAGE 8, Ref. 23]) and MambaVision ([PAGE 8, Ref. 24]) adapt to video and vision backbones. On the attention side, the genealogy passes through Vaswani et al. 2017 ([PAGE 30, Ref. 18]), ViViT ([PAGE 7, Ref. 19]), TimeSformer ([PAGE 7, Ref. 20]), and Video Swin Transformer ([PAGE 8, Ref. 40]), which introduced shifted window attention for hierarchical spatio-temporal relations.

A multi-week trap is embedded in the temporal axis itself. The authors note that SSMs model frame 5 to frame 150 through sequential hidden-state recursion, where decay or interference can destroy distant dependency ([PAGE 3, lines 93–97]). Over a multi-week or long-horizon video window, this recursion depth grows, and the flat architecture of conventional SSMs cannot separate foreground athletes from background clutter ([PAGE 3–4]). MMViT’s countermeasure is not a single operator but a staged relay: MPA at shallow resolution preserves local spatial structure (edges, textures, player boundaries); Mamba at intermediate scales absorbs the long-range temporal chain with linear cost; and a final MPA stage aggregates global semantics when the token count is small enough that O(N²D) is affordable again. The historical trap being avoided is the “one-size-fits-all” operator strategy of VideoMamba and MambaVision, which apply uniform Mamba or hybrid blocks across all stages ([PAGE 6, lines 98–100; PAGE 11, lines 363–366]). The retro-causal insight is that the deep attention stage only works because earlier downsampling has already compressed N; placing attention at the input resolution would re-trigger the quadratic OOM wall observed at 64 frames for ViViT ([PAGE 16]).

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression describes the tendency of a system under computational or representational stress to collapse to a single, familiar operator. In the MMViT ablation space ([PAGE 12–13, Table 3]), two regressive attractors appear. First, pure attention (A-A-A) reaches 80.54% accuracy but incurs the highest FLOPs (48.65G) and redundancy; under latency pressure, a designer might regress to this because attention is the “default” global dependency tool, yet it cannot process long temporal contexts without excessive overhead ([PAGE 13, lines 452–454]). Second, pure Mamba (M-M-M) drops to 71.06% accuracy with the lowest FLOPs (32.17G); under efficiency pressure, a designer might regress to Mamba everywhere, but the flat architecture lacks hierarchical feature separation and fails on fine-grained spatial tasks such as distinguishing a pass from a dribble under occlusion ([PAGE 13, lines 446–451]).

The counter-trap is the A-M-A heterogeneous allocation itself: shallow attention (MPA) for local texture, middle Mamba for linear long-range recursion, deep attention for semantic fusion. This is not a compromise but a stage-specific veto of each operator’s failure mode. Mamba is vetoed from Stage 1 because one-dimensional SSMs cannot encode full non-local 2D spatial receptive fields at high resolution ([PAGE 21, lines 683–688]). Pure attention is vetoed from the middle stage because quadratic complexity makes long temporal contexts prohibitive ([PAGE 13, lines 452–454]). The architecture thus recursively defends against its own component weaknesses.

The clock variable is the input sequence length and its latency mapping. Supplementary Table S3 ([PAGE 15–16]) shows ViViT suffers OOM at 64 frames due to quadratic complexity, while MMViT exhibits linear-like scaling: at 64 frames it maintains 158.8 ms/clip latency and 83.24% accuracy ([PAGE 16]). This defines a hard temporal wall: beyond the clock boundary where standard Transformers exceed GPU memory, MMViT remains feasible, but only because the Mamba middle stage has already absorbed the recursion depth that would otherwise explode attention cost. The regression-versus-counter-trap dynamic is therefore clocked—under short sequences, pure attention may be tolerable; under long sequences, only the staged A-M-A relay avoids both the accuracy collapse of flat Mamba and the latency/OOM wall of pure attention.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

A flat Mamba processing the full-resolution sequence cannot separate foreground athletes from background clutter ([PAGE 3–4]), so MPA with strided 3D convolution pooling is vetoed-in at Stage 1 and Stage 3. The 3×7×7 Cube1 embedding stride 2×4×4 ([PAGE 23]) establishes the spatio-temporal tubelet resolution before backbone processing; skipping this local preprocessing would violate the pose discrimination required for pass/dribble/block actions, which reach 93.54%–94.43% category accuracy in the full model ([PAGE 9]).

(2) Weather/illumination veto: Basketball-51’s dynamic lighting and complex backgrounds act as noise perturbations. A veto condition is: if D is applied before Mamba (coupled paradigm, Equation 7, [PAGE 20]), high-frequency information in the null space of D is irreversibly discarded, and accuracy collapses from 82.74% to 63.65% ([PAGE 13]). (3) Hardware veto: Edge devices with ultra-low power budgets may prefer X3D despite its lower representational capacity ([PAGE 12, 17]). MMViT’s 45.32M parameters and 35.49G FLOPs are not ultra-lightweight; the veto here is that MMViT should not be deployed where memory and power constraints dominate accuracy, e.g., on-device wearables. The cross-layer adjudication is that CDD’s “compute first, reduce later” order ([PAGE 20, Equation 5–6]) is mandatory whenever the downstream task is fine-grained pose recognition under clutter, because downsampling before state-space modeling destroys the spatial resolution that Mamba needs for dependency tracing.
