The epistemic bottleneck—knowing the accuracy of other men's knowledge of nature [PAGE 293]—exceeds cognitive bandwidth.

Biomechanical layer: Circadian cortisol rhythms and sleep deprivation impair executive function during earnings calls and proxy contests. Intuitive personality judgments [PAGE 293] are rendered under physiological load, increasing false-positive readings of confidence as competence and fatigue as caution.

Weather layer: Macroeconomic shocks—interest-rate spikes, commodity shifts, demand collapses—are exogenous "weather" that confound attribution of firm performance to the manager. A CEO may appear incompetent during a recession and brilliant during a boom while competence remains constant, because the signal $S = g(C,\epsilon,\omega)$ is dominated by $\omega$.

Veto proof: Any model of profit as a measurable risk premium that assumes cleanly separable decision and risk functions is vetoed by these layers. Because the final decision comes as near to intuition as possible [PAGE 293], and because previous observation enters little [PAGE 293], no financial model can bypass the physically constrained, biomechanically noisy, and environmentally confounded evaluation of human judgment. The veto is absolute: without a valid probability measure over managerial quality, expected-value compensation and risk-based performance evaluation are undefined.

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Scenario 1: A board terminates the CEO after two consecutive quarters of negative earnings surprises. Veto condition: Knightian uncertainty implies the decline may be an unforecastable state $\omega$, not low effort $e$; no probability $P$ can be calculated from tabulations [PAGE 293]. Verifiable test: Compare three-year ROA of firms that terminated the CEO after a shock versus matched control firms that retained the CEO. Prediction: Retention firms exhibit equal or higher ROA; termination causes transition-cost destruction without skill improvement.

Scenario 2: A board hires a new division head based on an unstructured "gut feel" personality assessment with no structured performance record. Veto condition: Direct evidence of actual performance is usually not very far, and the final decision rests on intuitive perception of relations [PAGE 293]. Verifiable test: Correlate pre-hire interview intuition scores with 36-month subsequent ROA or stock-adjusted ROA. Prediction: Correlation is statistically indistinguishable from zero; outcome variance across hires is high, confirming no valid estimator of judgment quality exists.

Scenario 3: A compensation committee sets executive bonuses using expected-value formulas that assume a measurable risk distribution $P$ over profit targets. Veto condition: The result can never be calculated from a priori data or tabulations [PAGE 293], so $\mathbb{E}_P[\cdot]$ is undefined under true uncertainty. Verifiable test: Compare talent retention and risk-taking in firms using probabilistic bonus targets versus firms using residual-claimant (profit-share) contracts. Prediction: Probabilistic-target firms overpay for luck in boom years and underpay for uncertainty bearing in bust years, causing talent to flow toward low-variance divisions and away from genuine uncertainty-bearing roles.
