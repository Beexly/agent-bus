The lineage on [PAGE 1] runs from Helmholtz’s Physiological Optik (1860s; cited p. 817) through Hering’s opponent-color theory (1878, later formalized in 1920) to James’s Principles of Psychology, Vol. 2 (1890; source text p. 244, OCR page [PAGE 1]). Helmholtz institutionalized perception as unconscious inference: the mind infers distal reality from proximal sensations, so conquered feelings ought to survive as recognized illusions. Hering countered with a physiology of the light sense in which color arises from antagonistic nerve-processes—red/green, blue/yellow, black/white—rather than from intellectual synthesis. James, on [PAGE 1], places simultaneous color-contrast in Hering’s camp: one color beside another excites a new nerve-process, and the modified feeling corresponds immediately, making the explanation physiological, not psychological.

Later nodes in the genealogy: Mach bands (late 19th c.) demonstrated lateral enhancement at luminance edges; Hartline’s recordings from Limulus retina (1930s) measured lateral inhibition directly; Hubel and Wiesel (1950s–60s) showed cortical orientation and contrast tuning; Land’s Retinex theory (1970s–80s) explained color constancy through local ratios; Carandini and Heeger’s normalization model (2012) gave a canonical gain-control form for contrast; predictive-coding accounts (Rao & Ballard, 1999; Friston’s free-energy principle) revived Helmholtzian inference at the cortical level; Gibsonian direct perception rejects inference altogether. The modern synthesis treats early contrast as retinal/LGN gain control and later illusions as inferential—precisely the division James gestures toward on [PAGE 1] without the circuitry to implement it.

Multi-week traps in research programs descending from this page: (1) the simultaneity trap—treating simultaneous contrast and successive contrast as one mechanism, when the former depends on spatial kernel L_c and the latter on adaptation time constant τ_adapt relative to interstimulus interval; a multi-week adaptation study that ignores τ_adapt will misattribute temporal effects to spatial nerve-processes. (2) the inference universalism trap—assuming every illusion, including color contrast, is Bayesian inference; local gain control can generate contrast with zero prior, so a weeks-long “natural statistics” manipulation may produce null results that are wrongly interpreted as failed learning. (3) the authority trap [PAGE 1]—accepting Helmholtz’s “great weight” without the close-quarters particularism James demands, leading to decades of “unconscious inference” used as an unfalsifiable homunculus. (4) the metamerism trap—using spectrally mismatched stimuli across weeks so that apparent contrast changes are actually illuminant chromaticity changes, not neural opponent-process effects.
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression, counter-trap, and clock structure.

Crisis habit regression: when perception becomes ambiguous—as when a judged “same gray” appears shifted by a colored surround—the observer regresses to a folk theory of mind: “I am judging, therefore my perception is inferential.” This regress attributes simultaneous color contrast to unconscious inference or to learned spatial sensations, exactly the Helmholtzian position James challenges on [PAGE 1]. The habit is reinforced by introspection: we experience better judgment overcoming sensation, so we project that recursive model onto all optical cognition.

Counter-trap: James’s counter-trap is methodological particularism. Rather than granting that all conquered feelings persist as illusions, he isolates group (a)—simultaneous color-contrast—and demands a mechanism: Hering’s nerve-process [PAGE 1]. The counter-trap blocks the infinite regress of “inference all the way down” by positing a non-psychological layer that operates before judgment can intervene.

Clock structure: a recursive theory of mind must nest layers by timescale. Phototransduction: ~10–100 ms. Retinal lateral inhibition / horizontal-cell feedback: ~10–50 ms. V1 contrast normalization: ~40–80 ms. Covert attention shift: ~100–300 ms. Conscious judgment / “better judgment based on experience” [PAGE 1]: >300 ms, often seconds. The crisis occurs when the slow judgment clock attempts to veto the fast sensation clock: an observer reports “the centers are identical” while the V1 center–surround response remains shifted. If the physiological response precedes the report by more than ~50 ms and survives dual-task load, the inference model is falsified for that instance; the mind is not recursively judging color contrast at the moment it occurs, even though it can later describe it in judgmental terms.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical/neural, and environmental veto proof.

Physical layer (optics and radiometry): the retinal irradiance at x is E(x) = ∫ T(λ)L(x;λ) dλ, where T is lens/macular pigment transmission and L is scene radiance. If two samples are metameric under the test illuminant but not under daylight, any apparent contrast is an illuminant artifact, not a nerve-process. Veto condition: measure spectra; if ΔE_test = 0 but ΔE_daylight ≠ 0, the psychological inference claim is vetoed for that pair. Units: E in W·m⁻²·sr⁻¹ or photons·s⁻¹·deg⁻²; ΔE in CIELAB.

Biomechanical/neural layer: horizontal cells in the outer plexiform layer and amacrine cells in the inner retina implement L_c; LGN and V1 implement contrast normalization. Veto operation: record V1 BOLD or single-unit center–surround responses while attention is withdrawn or under high cognitive load. If the antagonism persists (ΔBOLD > 2 SD above baseline, p < 0.01, Cohen’s d > 0.8) while behavioral report drops to chance, top-down inference is not necessary for simultaneous contrast; the “physiological, not psychological” claim on [PAGE 1] is confirmed at this layer.

“Weather” / environmental noise layer: luminance flicker, chromatic aberration, and atmospheric scattering inject noise into S(x,t;λ). Photon shot noise gives σ_Q = √Q; when Q < Q_crit such that √Q / Q exceeds the contrast threshold, no layer—neural or inferential—can recover the signal. Veto condition: if the measured contrast ΔC falls below the noise-equivalent contrast ΔC_noise = k·σ_Q/Q (k set by criterion), the instance is below breakdown regime 6 and must be excluded from any claim about perception.

Adjudication rule: any inference-based explanation of simultaneous color-contrast is vetoed iff (i) the center–surround physiological response survives removal of attention and judgment, (ii) the sign and magnitude of contrast are invariant to weeks of natural-statistics rearing, and (iii) the contrast magnitude follows the physical center–surround convolution rather than a learned prior. All three conditions must hold simultaneously; failure of any one reinstates an inferential or environmental account for that instance.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1 — Attention-necessity claim. Counterfactual model: simultaneous color-contrast requires attention / unconscious inference, so withdrawing attention should abolish the neural contrast signal. Veto operation: cue covert attention away from the target–surround pair while recording V1 via fMRI or VEP; also collect behavioral detection. Verifiable ground truth: concurrent retinotopic V1 BOLD (or VEP center–surround component) and forced-choice detection report. Prediction if veto holds: V1 antagonism remains significantly above baseline (ΔBOLD > 0, p < 0.01, d > 0.8) while detection drops to chance (P_detect ≈ 0.5). An attention-dependent inference model is falsified; a Heringian nerve-process survives.

Scenario 2 — Learned-statistics reversal claim. Counterfactual model: the sign of color contrast is learned from natural scene statistics as a Bayesian prior, so weeks of exposure to an anti-statistical chromatic environment should reverse the Hering effect (e.g., a reddish surround should come to make the center look reddish rather than greenish). Veto operation: rearing or goggle-based exposure for ≥4 weeks under inverted surround–center statistics, then test contrast sign under neutral conditions. Verifiable ground truth: direction of perceived hue shift (greenish vs. reddish) and magnitude in ΔE. Prediction if veto holds: contrast sign remains fixed by opponent cone circuitry after 4 weeks (no reversal; |ΔE_shift| within 5% of baseline). A prior-only model is falsified; the physiological nerve-process is confirmed as statistically encapsulated.

Scenario 3 — Uniform-inference claim. Counterfactual model: Helmholtzian unconscious inference applies equally to space and color [PAGE 1], predicting equal susceptibility of spatial and color illusions to cognitive load. Veto operation: dual-task n-back load during (a) Müller–Lyer spatial size illusion and (b) simultaneous color contrast. Verifiable ground truth: percent size overestimation for space; ΔE color shift for color; measured under load vs. no load. Prediction if veto holds: spatial illusion degrades by >20% under load; color contrast changes by <5% (within test–retest reliability). A uniform inference model is falsified; the domain-specific physiological account on [PAGE 1] is supported, with spatial inference load-dependent and color contrast load-resistant.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
