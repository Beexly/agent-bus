APT is performed in water, where effective weight is reduced by buoyancy (\(F_{\mathrm{eff}}=mg-\rho Vg\)) and ground reaction forces are attenuated (impact force 1.31 vs 2.38 body mass; 44.8% reduction) [PAGE 7]. Veto: full replacement of LPT by APT is physically vetoed because the competition surface imposes a higher GRF and a different force-time integral than water; specificity of transfer dictates that jump and sprint performance on land require land-based loading.

Biomechanical-layer veto: The discussion notes increased ankle dorsiflexion torque in APT but no difference in time to reach maximal central force compared to land, despite greater water resistance [PAGE 7][PAGE 8]. This indicates altered kinematic strategy (joint kinematics under drag) without a corresponding temporal advantage. Veto: APT cannot be assumed to improve sprint acceleration mechanics because ankle joint kinematics and ground contact time under buoyancy do not map isomorphically to dry-land sprinting.

Weather/environmental-layer veto: The trial occurred December–February in Erbil [PAGE 4]. If pool water temperature was below thermoneutral (~27–30°C), cold-induced reductions in muscle contractility and RFD would blunt explosive output; if air temperature was low, land-group warm-up requirements differ from water-group immersion effects. Veto: without recorded water and air temperature, the environmental layer is unidentified; any claim of APT superiority under winter conditions is vetoed until thermoregulatory confounds are excluded.

Physiological-layer veto: Lower muscle-damage markers and lower impact forces are protective but may reduce mechanotransductive stimulus for tendon and bone adaptation [PAGE 8, refs 6, 8, 24]. Veto: reduced soreness does not equal superior structural adaptation; longitudinal tendon stiffness requires ground-reaction loading magnitude that water attenuates.

Adjudication: APT is conditionally admissible as an adjunct or recovery-phase modality, especially during periods requiring low impact (post-injury, congested fixture blocks). It is vetoed as a stand-alone substitute for LPT in youth football players whose performance surface is land. The decision rule: prescribe APT only when (a) water depth and temperature are standardized and reported, (b) land-based plyometric volume is maintained above the specificity threshold, and (c) the outcome of interest is not exclusively ground-contact sprint mechanics.

Verification: measure displaced volume V or use a standardized depth marker; compute \(F_{\mathrm{eff}}/mg\). Prediction: vertical jump improvement falls below 10% rather than 21.05%, and between-group t for vertical jump drops below the critical value (t<2.05), nullifying the claimed APT superiority.

Scenario 2 (baseline covariate veto): If the pre-test baseline imbalance (APT vertical jump 39.00 cm vs LPT 34.90 cm [PAGE 5]) is modeled via ANCOVA rather than raw change scores, the LPT group has more headroom for improvement. Verification: re-run analysis with post-test as dependent variable, group as factor, and pre-test as covariate; inspect adjusted means and group effect. Prediction: the adjusted between-group effect size for vertical jump shrinks, and the group × time interaction may become nonsignificant (p>0.05) because regression to the mean partially accounts for the raw +21.05% gain.

Scenario 3 (thermal veto): If ambient air temperature in Erbil during Dec–Feb falls below 10°C and pool water is not maintained at thermoneutral temperature, cold-induced suppression of RFD and muscle contraction velocity disproportionately affects the land group's warm-up but also blunts water-group power output if immersion is cold. Verification: record water temperature and air temperature at each session; stratify analysis by thermal condition. Prediction: under cold-water conditions, the APT speed gain falls below 12.50% and the RFD improvement is less than 30.4%, removing the mechanistic basis for the claimed aquatic advantage. Under thermoneutral conditions, the original effects are reproducible; under cold conditions, the null hypothesis of no medium difference cannot be rejected.
