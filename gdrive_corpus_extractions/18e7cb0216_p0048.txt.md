Scenario 1: Population freeze with capital accumulation. Intervention: impose Δ_P = 0 (closed borders, zero natural increase) for T = 4 quarters while permitting Δ_K > 0. Prediction: capital deepening raises the capital–labor ratio, increasing w relative to r; because factor mobility is assumed perfect but supplies are fixed, p = AC no longer holds uniformly. Capital-intensive sectors exhibit positive pure profit π_pure > 0, labor-intensive sectors π_pure < 0. Verification: measure sectoral profit-rate dispersion and the wage–rental ratio over 4 quarters. Veto falsified if profit-rate dispersion remains within one standard deviation of the static baseline and no sector shows persistent π_pure ≠ 0.

Scenario 2: Method freeze with multiplying wants. Intervention: hold production methods fixed (Δ_M = 0, no new technology adoption) for T = 6 months while allowing consumer wants to multiply (Δ_W > 0 via new product categories). Prediction: relative prices of new want-satisfying goods rise above static average cost; entrants in those categories earn temporary pure profit until imitation. Verification: track price indices and firm entry counts in newly defined product categories. Veto falsified if fewer than 30% of new categories show price exceeding cost by a margin greater than measurement error, or if entry does not occur within the window.

Intervention: maintain perfect factor mobility but expose two matched venture cohorts to different Knightian regimes: Cohort R faces insurable risk with known frequency Pr(loss) = ρ; Cohort U faces novel regulatory uncertainty with no objective frequency distribution. Prediction: Cohort R earns zero expected excess return after premiums equal ρ·loss; Cohort U exhibits non-zero mean excess return (positive or negative) because no actuarial premium can be set, consistent with Knight’s residual profit/loss under uncertainty.
