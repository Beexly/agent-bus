<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The lineage runs from Pavlov's physiological digestion research circa 1900, through his 1927 *Conditioned Reflexes* and 1928 *Lectures on Conditioned Reflexes*, to Woodworth's 1929 *Psychology* (pp. 152–153), and finally to Tolman's 1932 *Purposive Behavior in Animals and Men*, page 320 [PAGE 320]. Pavlov began with a minor operation on dogs to measure salivary gland output while studying digestion; he noticed that saliva flowed to distal cues—the sight of food, the dish, the feeder's approach, footsteps in the next room—because these stimuli had become associated with food through the animal's past experience [PAGE 320]. He labeled these artificial, experience-dependent responses "conditioned reflexes" and then sought to manufacture them with arbitrary stimuli: a bell, a light, skin brushing [PAGE 320].

Tolman retells this history not to endorse a pure stimulus–response psychology but to mark its boundary. The conditioned reflex chapter sets up the mechanism that Tolman will later challenge with purposivism: if a dog salivates to footsteps, is that an automatic reflex or an expectancy of the feeder? The historical trap embedded in this genealogy is the assumption that temporal contiguity alone—bell ringing for "a certain time (often for a minute, but a few seconds were sufficient)"—is sufficient to explain learning [PAGE 320]. A multi-week trap follows: extinction is not instantaneous. After pairings cease, the CR weakens across sessions distributed over days or weeks; spontaneous recovery can resurrect it after a rest period. Another trap is sensory preconditioning: the dish and the feeder's footsteps were already associated with food before the formal bell experiment began, so the animal enters the laboratory with a pre-existing expectancy structure that the "neutral" CS must compete with [PAGE 320]. Tolman's retrospective move is to treat the conditioned response as evidence of a cognitive map—an expectancy of food—rather than a mere elastic reflex. The multi-week hazard for modern readers is to import Tolman's critique back onto Pavlov's original data as if Pavlov had measured expectancy, when the page itself only reports salivation flow and procedural description [PAGE 320].
