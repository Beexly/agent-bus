**1. Neuro-Metabolic Reserve Capacity ($E_{res}$)**
Let $P(t)$ be the instantaneous power consumption of the neural ensemble (SI unit: Watts). The total available energy reserve over time $\tau$ is bounded by metabolic supply $S_{meta}$.
$$ E_{res}(t) = \int_0^t (S_{meta} - P(\tau)) d\tau $$
Breakdown Regime: When $E_{res}(t) \rightarrow 0$, the capacity for top-down modulation vanishes.

**2. The Inhibitory Control Operator ($\mathcal{I}$)**
Inhibition is modeled as a linear-nonlinear-Poisson (LNP) filter applied to excitatory inputs $x(t)$. Let $w_I$ be the synaptic weight matrix of inhibitory interneurons.
$$ y_{out}(t) = \mathcal{F} \left( W_E x(t) - \mathcal{I} * (W_I y_{out}(t)) \right) $$
where $\mathcal{I}$ represents the convolution kernel of GABAergic transmission. 
Assumption: $\mathcal{I}$ requires significant $E_{res}$ to maintain high-frequency synchronization.
Singularity: If $\|\mathcal{I}\| < \theta_{crit}$ (a critical threshold dependent on $E_{res}$), the system exhibits unbounded growth in output variance, manifesting as "uncontrollable impulses."

**3. Information-Theoretic Entropy of Impulse ($H_{imp}$)**
James notes "impulsive tendencies... are innumerable." We quantify the loss of self-control as an increase in the Shannon entropy of behavioral output distributions.
$$ H(Y) = - \sum p(y) \log p(y) $$
Under normal inhibition, $p(y)$ is sharply peaked around socially acceptable actions. Under "Inhibitory Insanity," $p(y)$ flattens, increasing $H(Y)$, representing the "loss of the ego" or will.
