Football's physiology imposes a hard ceiling on that: 90 minutes plus stoppage, fixed roster, limited substitution, and thermoregulatory collapse in late-match play. The source contains the signature without drawing the inference: Yugoslavia vs Iraq, *"hitting Iraq with four goals in the final 35 minutes"* [PAGE 69]. Four goals in the final 39% of the match is a late-phase opponent-degradation profile, not a moral verdict on Yugoslav unity. **Verdict: the "temporary unification" window is bounded above by the match clock (~5,400 s) and by the tournament cycle (~4 yr). Any cohesion produced inside that window decays on the timescale of the window.** The Toronto Star's *"football will save Yugoslavia"* [PAGE 91] is a claim about a 90-minute phenomenon extrapolated to a decade.

**V4 — Kinematic veto on iconicity.** Boban's kick [PAGE 82] is presented as an epochal image. Energetically it is tens of joules over ~0.1 s. Its causal weight comes entirely from reproduction and circulation, not from the act. **Verdict: iconic acts are vetoed as evidence of structural magnitude.** They are evidence of *camera placement and editorial choice* — which, note, the thesis's own source base (wire photography, YouTube documentaries such as Podgorelec's *bbb*) is a product of.

**V5 — Ecological veto (Slovenia).** The thesis's own account has Slovenia decoupling from football *before* the crisis — football became *"synonymous with feelings that stimulate the lowest human instincts"* [PAGE 11], and popularity returned only after 1992 independence. Slovenia was the most economically advanced republic and exited first with the least violence. **Verdict: the football-mediation mechanism is not necessary for disintegration.** It may be sufficient in the Serbian-Croatian dyad and is at best epiphenomenal in the Alpine republic. This is the strongest cross-layer veto in the file.
