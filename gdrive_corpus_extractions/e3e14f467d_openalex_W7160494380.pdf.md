1) Binary classification AUC (area under the receiver-operating characteristic curve). Let S(x) be the continuous score assigned by a classifier to instance x, with positive class y = 1 (injury / protracted recovery / high risk) and negative class y = 0. Then
\[
\mathrm{AUC} = \Pr\bigl(S(X_{+}) > S(X_{-})\bigr) = \int_{0}^{1} \mathrm{TPR}(t)\,d\bigl(1-\mathrm{FPR}(t)\bigr),
\]
where the probability is over independently drawn positive and negative instances. Units: dimensionless probability in [0, 1]. Source values: XGBoost AUC 0.948 (NHL skaters) and 0.956 (goalies) versus logistic regression 0.937 and 0.947 [PAGE 9]; gradient-boosted trees AUC 0.84 males / 0.78 females for adolescent concussion protracted recovery [PAGE 8, 10]; youth soccer best-fit AUC 0.700 [PAGE 7]. 2) Root-mean-square error for continuous targets. For N observations with targets y_i and predictions \hat{y}_i,
\[
\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}.
\]
Units: identical to the target (m for pitch-location error; mm for pose-joint error; ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ for VO₂). Source values: mean Euclidean error 0.154 m for the multi-output deep network on baseball pitch location versus 0.215 m linear regression; 95th percentile 0.362 m; MAE 0.105/0.093 m horizontal/vertical [PAGE 7]; VO₂ RMSE 4.976 for LSTM in the n=6 pilot [PAGE 7]; pitch-velocity RMSE 0.014 with R² 0.975 in Bayesian multilevel regression [PAGE 8]. Breakdown regime: scale-dependent, sensitive to outliers, and invalid under heteroscedastic sensor noise or radar-gun calibration drift [PAGE 7, 8].

3) Trajectory forecasting errors (average/final displacement error). For predicted positions \hat{p}_{t+h} and ground truth p_{t+h} over horizon H,
\[
\mathrm{ADE} = \frac{1}{HM}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\| \hat{p}^{(m)}_{t+h} - p^{(m)}_{t+h} \|_2,\qquad
\mathrm{FDE} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\| \hat{p}^{(m)}_{t+H} - p^{(m)}_{t+H} \|_2.
\]
Units: feet (as reported in the NBA tracking study) or meters after unit conversion [PAGE 6]. Source value: MBT1 at H = 10 gives ADE 1.43 / FDE 2.98 [PAGE 9]. Breakdown regime: multi-modal ambiguity at long horizons (one ground-truth future vs many plausible futures), normalization sensitivity, and covariate shift when possession structure changes across seasons [PAGE 6].

4) Higher-order tracking accuracy for multi-object tracking. HOTA factorizes as
\[
\mathrm{HOTA} = \sqrt{\mathrm{DetA}\times \mathrm{AssA}},
\]
where DetA measures detection-alignment quality and AssA measures association-ID consistency. Units: dimensionless in [0, 1]. Source values: FairMOT fine-tuned HOTA 57.882 (MOTA 83.565) on SoccerNet-Tracking; off-the-shelf DeepSORT HOTA 36.663 and ByteTrack HOTA 47.225 without ground-truth detections [PAGE 7]. Breakdown regime: severe under player occlusion, fast motion, and broadcast-view ID switches; domain fine-tuning is required because off-the-shelf models are substantially lower [PAGE 6, 7].

5) Mean per-joint position error for 3D pose estimation. For J joints and N frames,
\[
\mathrm{MPJPE} = \frac{1}{NJ}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\| \hat{x}^{(n)}_{j} - x^{(n)}_{j} \|_2,\qquad
\mathrm{P\text{-}MPJPE} = \text{MPJPE after rigid Procrustes alignment}.
\]
Units: millimeters. Source values: TCPFormer MPJPE 65.80 mm trained on AthletePose3D versus ~213.64 mm trained on Human3.6M; combined training MPJPE 65.07 and P-MPJPE 7.68 [PAGE 7]; SportsPose mean error 34.5 mm versus marker-based motion capture [PAGE 7]. Breakdown regime: domain shift from laboratory pose datasets to elite athletic motion; monocular depth ambiguity without multi-camera triangulation [PAGE 7].

6) Classification accuracy, sensitivity, specificity for imbalanced injury risk. With TP, TN, FP, FN,
\[
\mathrm{Accuracy}=\frac{TP+TN}{TP+TN+FP+FN},\quad
\mathrm{Sensitivity}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\quad
\mathrm{Specificity}=\frac{TN}{TN+FP}.
\]
Units: dimensionless probabilities [0, 1]. Source values: soccer injury SVM-RBF accuracy 0.78 (p < .01), sensitivity 0.73, specificity 0.85 with 7 features; DEV-only accuracy 0.62; ACWR-only 0.76 [PAGE 7]. Breakdown regime: accuracy is misleading under class imbalance; the youth soccer screening battery reports AUC 0.700 and F-score 0.380 precisely because injury prevalence is low and TPR/TNR trade off [PAGE 7, 8].

7) Pearson correlation for ecological team-level validation. For variables X (model aggregate) and Y (team performance measure),
\[
r = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}.
\]
Units: dimensionless in [-1, 1]. Source values: Q-Ball versus points-per-game coefficient 0.622; offensive efficiency versus average points coefficient 0.85 [PAGE 6]. Breakdown regime: ecological fallacy—team-level correlation does not license player-level causal intervention; non-stationarity across seasons invalidates extrapolation [PAGE 6].

8) Formal veto operator for cross-layer adjudication. Let layers \ell \in \{ \mathrm{physical}, \mathrm{biomechanical}, \mathrm{environmental} \} each emit a confidence C_\ell \in [0,1] with threshold \tau_\ell. A model output \hat{y} is admitted iff
\[
V(\hat{y}) = \bigwedge_{\ell} \bigl[ C_\ell \ge \tau_\ell \bigr],
\]
otherwise the prediction is vetoed and a fallback (physics-only, linear regression, or human coach rule) is engaged. Thresholds are sensor- and context-specific; e.g., IMU sampling at 50 Hz for tennis stroke classification [PAGE 8], 500 Hz for baseball pelvis/sternum IMUs [PAGE 8], 90 Hz synchronized cameras for SportsPose [PAGE 7], and 0.04 s NBA tracking sampling [PAGE 6].
</SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The methodological lineage in the review can be traced backward through four intersecting traditions, each creating distinct multi-week traps when projected forward.

First, the systematic-review genealogy begins with PRISMA 2020 (Page et al., 2021), which the authors cite for transparent reporting [PAGE 3, 15]. The PRISMA flow itself is partially reconstructed because source platforms do not support unified export of stable counts [PAGE 3, 4], introducing a provenance trap: any downstream citation of “21 studies” inherits an unverifiable identification denominator.

Second, the computer-vision benchmark genealogy runs from SoccerNet to SoccerNet-v2 to SoccerNet-Tracking. SoccerNet-v2 extended SoccerNet with action spotting, camera-shot segmentation, and replay grounding, reporting ~300k annotations across 500 untrimmed broadcast videos [PAGE 1, 2]; SoccerNet-Tracking added 200 sequences of 30 seconds plus a fully annotated 45-minute half with players, referees, and ball [PAGE 6]. FineGym supplies the gymnastics/fine-grained branch: 303 competition records (~708 hours), 530 element categories, 4,883 events, 32,697 sub-actions [PAGE 1, 2]. The multi-week trap here is benchmark ossification: models fine-tuned on SoccerNet or FineGym inherit broadcast-view bias and temporal annotation granularity that do not transfer to non-broadcast training footage or lower-resource leagues [PAGE 6, 7, 13, 14].

Third, the reinforcement-learning and inverse-reinforcement-learning genealogy traces Q-Ball (deep RL, extension of DDPG handling discrete and continuous actions) to IRL-DK (inverse reinforcement learning with domain knowledge) applied to 4.5M NHL play-by-play events [PAGE 6, 8, 10, 16]. The multi-week trap is sparse-reward non-stationarity: goals and injuries are rare events, so dense reward surrogates inferred from past play-by-play may misprice tactical alternatives under new rule sets, roster turnover, or coaching philosophy shifts. Q-Ball itself notes cold-start and the need for prospective evaluation [PAGE 6]; Luo’s IRL-DK shows improved NLL/HMD but still relies on historical action sequences [PAGE 10].

Fourth, the sports-medicine genealogy runs from clinical tabular modeling to tree-based boosting for injury and concussion prognosis: Luu’s NHL next-season injury XGBoost (AUC 0.948/0.956) [PAGE 9], Chu’s adolescent concussion gradient-boosted trees (AUC 0.84 males / 0.78 females) [PAGE 8], Tsilimigkras’s soccer training-load SVM-RBF (accuracy 0.78) [PAGE 7], and Robles-Palazón’s youth soccer screening battery (AUC 0.700, F-score 0.380) [PAGE 7]. The multi-week trap is label-latency and workload-window misalignment: “next-season injury” aggregates an entire season of exposure into a binary label, while ACWR and DEV features use rolling acute/chronic windows that assume stationarity of training-load response across micro-cycles and meso-cycles [PAGE 7, 10, 14]. When competition calendars compress or congest, the acute:chronic reference baseline shifts, invalidating the feature distribution on which the model was trained [PAGE 14].
</RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Model the coach or clinician as an agent maintaining a recursive theory of mind about the ML system: they form beliefs about what the model believes about player state, and they update those beliefs under crisis conditions. Three dynamical regimes structure this recursion.

Crisis habit regression. Under high-stakes uncertainty (imminent injury decision, return-to-play clearance, tactical substitution), decision-makers regress to parsimonious, familiar models rather than maximal-complexity deep networks. The review documents this as a preference for tree-based ensembles and SVMs on structured tabular data—training load, screening batteries, injury risk, clinical outcomes [PAGE 11, 13, 14]—and specifically notes the desire for “deployable screening rules rather than maximal model complexity” [PAGE 13]. Recursively, the coach expects the model to be interpretable because the coach must justify the decision to athletes, parents, and management; thus model complexity is socially constrained, not merely statistically constrained.

Counter-trap. The interpretability layer can become a counter-trap: SHAP values in Q-Ball [PAGE 6] and permutation importance in pitch-location modeling [PAGE 7] are presented as bridges to coach-actionable insight, yet post-hoc attribution assumes feature independence and can rationalize correlation as causation [PAGE 13]. If the coach treats a SHAP bar chart as a causal license, the recursion collapses into confirmatory justification. The counter-trap is triggered whenever explanation is used to legitimize a pre-existing tactical intuition rather than to falsify it.

Clock. Latency and temporal horizons bound the recursion. Real-time coaching feedback is explicit in the swimming analytics framework (Python 3.8 implementation, real-time goal) [PAGE 8], broadcast analytics target low-resolution real-time constraints [PAGE 7], and trajectory forecasting evaluates horizons H = 10/20/40 [PAGE 6]. The clock has two timescales: (i) inference latency within a possession or match, and (ii) retraining cadence across weeks of a season. If the model’s inference clock exceeds the possession clock, the output is useless; if the retraining clock is slower than the domain-shift clock (new opponent, new venue, congested fixture list), the feature distribution drifts and the model’s recursive beliefs become stale [PAGE 14].

The recursive equilibrium is therefore: the coach delegates only when (a) the model’s complexity matches the decision’s justifiability budget, (b) interpretability survives counterfactual scrutiny, and (c) both inference and retraining clocks are faster than the relevant physical and tactical clocks.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
A prediction is admitted to the decision layer only if every physical, biomechanical, and environmental sub-layer passes its veto threshold. Formalize with
\[
V(\hat{y}) = \bigwedge_{\ell \in \{P,B,E\}} \mathbb{I}\{ C_\ell \ge \tau_\ell \},
\]
where \mathbb{I} is the indicator, C_\ell is layer confidence, and \tau_\ell is the layer-specific acceptance threshold. If any conjunct is false, the ML output is vetoed and a fallback (physics-only predictor, linear regression, or coach heuristic) is engaged.

Physical layer (sensor and sampling). Tennis stroke classification uses a single wrist IMU (6-axis, ±16 g accelerometer, ±2000°/s gyroscope) at 50 Hz [PAGE 8]; baseball pitching biomechanics uses pelvis/sternum IMUs at 500 Hz with radar-gun velocity labels [PAGE 8]; SportsPose uses 7 synchronized cameras at 90 Hz with marker-based mocap validation [PAGE 7]; NBA tracking samples at 0.04 s [PAGE 6]. A veto fires if sampling falls below the Nyquist rate for the fastest biomechanical event (e.g., racket-stroke contact, ball release), if IMU Bluetooth link drops below real-time throughput, or if camera synchronization desynchronizes multi-view triangulation.

Biomechanical layer (physics priors and kinematic validity). The baseball pitch-location study explicitly shows that excluding projectile-motion physics features degrades deep-model performance [PAGE 7]; Gomaz et al. model ball velocity from peak angular velocity of pelvis/trunk plus pitcher height, achieving R² 0.975 [PAGE 8]. A biomechanical veto fires when release metrics are missing, when radar-gun calibration is suspect, or when kinematic sequences violate known segmental coordination (e.g., pelvis-trunk sequencing in the kinetic chain). In badminton MonoTrack, upstream hit-detection error dominates end-to-end trajectory reconstruction [PAGE 6], so a hit-detection confidence below threshold vetoes the 3D shuttle trajectory output.

Environmental / broadcast layer. SoccerNet-Tracking shows tracking remains challenging under occlusion and fast motion; off-the-shelf models are substantially lower than domain fine-tuned FairMOT-ft (HOTA 36.663 vs 57.882) [PAGE 6, 7]. A veto fires under broadcast occlusion, poor lighting, wide-angle distortion, or when the pose estimator’s MPJPE shifts from in-domain (65.80 mm on AthletePose3D) to out-of-domain (~213.64 mm under H3.6M training) [PAGE 7]. Weather is not explicitly quantified in the source, but outdoor broadcast conditions (rain, snow, stadium lighting variation) implicitly degrade video-based pose and tracking confidence; the absence of explicit weather modeling is itself a veto criterion for deployment in uncontrolled outdoor venues.

Adjudication order: physical veto is evaluated first (hard sensor failure), then biomechanical (implausible kinematics), then environmental (occlusion/broadcast degradation). Only after all three pass does the model output reach the coach-clinician decision layer, where interpretability (SHAP/permutation) acts as a final human-in-the-loop veto [PAGE 13, 14].
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable reinforcement-learning-via-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) counterfactual veto scenarios. Each specifies a verifiable trigger condition and a falsifiable prediction.

Scenario 1: NBA Q-Ball tactical override by acute physiological load.
Counterfactual setup: Q-Ball recommends a high-value offensive action (Q-value above threshold) based on SportVU trajectories and play-by-play [PAGE 6]. The verifiable veto condition is that the same player’s acute deviation (DEF) or ACWR load feature exceeds the injury-risk decision boundary established by Tsilimigkras et al. (accuracy 0.78, sensitivity 0.73, specificity 0.85 with 7 features) [PAGE 7]. Prediction: when the physiological veto fires, expected points added from the Q-Ball recommendation decreases relative to the fallback conservative possession, and team offensive efficiency correlation (r = 0.85 with average points) weakens in high-load game states. Verifiable by stratifying Q-Ball recommendations by player load tercile and testing the interaction between Q-value and DEV/ACWR on possession outcomes.

Scenario 2: Youth soccer injury-screening veto under field missingness.
Counterfactual setup: Robles-Palazón’s screening battery uses six field-based measures (drop jump, tuck jump, ROM, etc.) in 5–10 minutes per player, yielding best-fit AUC 0.700, F-score 0.380 [PAGE 7]. The verifiable veto condition is that any required field measure is missing or administered outside the feasibility window (e.g., >10 minutes, no equipment, non-elite setting without trained staff). Prediction: in field deployment, the model’s operating point shifts from the controlled AUC 0.700 toward the no-information rate (AUC 0.50), and the false-negative rate for injury exceeds the controlled TPR 53.7%. Verifiable by prospective deployment with deliberate missingness injection and comparison of observed TPR/TNR against the tabled values.

Scenario 3: Baseball pitch-location veto under physics-feature or sensor failure.
Counterfactual setup: Moore et al.’s multi-output deep network predicts final pitch location with mean Euclidean error 0.154 m using release metrics plus physics-derived projectile-motion context [PAGE 7]; linear regression alone achieves 0.215 m. The verifiable veto condition is loss of the ball-tracking release metrics (spin rate, lateral release position) or failure of the projectile-motion prior. Prediction: upon veto, fallback to linear regression/physics-only increases mean Euclidean error from 0.154 m to at least 0.215 m (and likely toward the 95th percentile 0.362 m), and MAE for horizontal/vertical degrades from 0.105/0.093 m. Verifiable by ablating the physics-context features in a holdout set of NCAA Division I pitches and measuring error inflation against the tabled baseline.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
