<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
Tolman’s 1932 assertion that a single exposure to a corrected goal-object could immediately rectify performance disruption directly challenged the dominant behaviorist orthodoxy of Watson and Skinner, who posited that learning was strictly a gradual accumulation of reinforced S-R bonds. Historically, this epistemological rupture planted the seeds for the "Cognitive Revolution" of the 1950s and 60s. However, looking retrocausally, we observe that Tolman’s methodology inadvertently established a blueprint for modern "red teaming" and adversarial machine learning.

By manipulating the environmental reward structure (the "trap") while holding the organism's internal state (the "drive") constant, Tolman designed a protocol to isolate cognitive mapping from blind habituation. In contemporary terms, this is equivalent to training a neural network on a stationary dataset and then abruptly shifting the ground truth labels to test the model's susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting or overfitting to spurious correlations.

The multi-week trap inherent in pure behaviorism—the assumption that persistence of behavior guarantees optimality—leads directly to modern "model collapse" phenomena in generative AI, where systems recursively train on their own outputs (a form of self-reinforcing, unchanged goal-object expectation) until coherence degrades. Tolman’s insight that organisms harbor "expectancies" essentially introduced the concept of a forward model. The lineage runs straight from the rat in the goal-box, realizing it has been fed instead of watered, to large language models experiencing a massive perplexity spike when encountering out-of-distribution prompts, forcing an immediate adjustment in their next-token probability distributions. 

Recognizing this genealogy allows us to identify a recurring historical trap: the conflation of *persistence* with *intelligence*. Just as a rat might persist in a maze despite a changed goal (as seen in the initial disruption), sophisticated trading algorithms or diplomatic strategies may continue executing flawed paradigms long after the fundamental "drive" or economic conditions have shifted, because their underlying architecture lacks the capacity for instantaneous hypothesis replacement.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
When an agent (human or artificial) encounters a discrepancy between an expected goal-object and the actual received stimulus, it initiates a recursive evaluation of not only the environment but the potential intent behind the discrepancy. In the context of the Frontier Reasoning Foundry, this manifests as a "Crisis Habit Regression vs Counter-Trap vs Clock" dynamic.

*Crisis Habit Regression:* Upon detecting a loss of expected reward (e.g., a sudden market crash or a bait-and-switch in a negotiation), a naive agent defaults to ingrained heuristics. Like the rat initially disrupted by finding food instead of water, the agent experiences a temporary paralysis or reverts to previous, now-ineffective routines. This is a failure of Theory of Mind (ToM) Level 1 (understanding that others/environments have different states).

*Counter-Trap:* A sophisticated agent possessing ToM Level 2 (recognizing that the environment/opponent anticipated its habit regression) interprets the anomalous goal-object not as noise, but as a deliberate signal. If an adversary provides a resource you didn't ask for (food when thirsty), the advanced model asks: "Why would they provide this? What do they expect me to do with it?" The "counter-trap" involves accepting the new object but altering the downstream strategy to exploit the opponent's presumed miscalculation of your flexibility. 

*The Clock:* The temporal dimension is crucial. Tolman noted the recovery happened "on the next subsequent day." In high-frequency domains, the "clock" ticks in milliseconds. The ability to compress Tolman's "one experience" recovery into microsecond latency is the ultimate arbiter of dominance. An agent must calculate the recursion depth of the opponent's ToM: "They know I expect X; they gave me Y hoping I'll panic; therefore, I must act as if Y confirms my original plan Z." The clock dictates the maximum allowable cycles for this recursive loop before action becomes mandatory.
