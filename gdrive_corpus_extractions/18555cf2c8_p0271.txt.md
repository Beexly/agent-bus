The methodological stance presented on [PAGE 1] (p. 249 of Tolman, 1932) represents a critical juncture in the "Behavioral Revolution" and its retroduction to earlier sensory psychophysics. 

**Lineage:**
Tolman's "Martian experiment" is a sophisticated thought experiment that bridges Gustav Fechner’s *Elementarakt* (the fundamental elements of sensation) with Edward Titchener’s strict introspectionism. Fechner had sought to establish universal laws of sensation, but his reliance on human subjects inherently tied his metrics to the specific "dimensions" of human perception (hue, saturation, brightness). Titchener later formalized the study of these "sense-qualities" through rigorous introspective training, insisting that the only way to know the nature of a "red" sensation was through a correctly trained observer describing its immediate "raw feel."

Tolman’s genealogy is a deliberate usurpation of this lineage. He argues that the "true introspection" of the orthodoxist is not a privileged window into an ineffable private realm, but merely a specific form of reporting that could be achieved by any organism capable of making discriminations. By reducing "sensation" to a set of relative choices, Tolman retrojects the concept of "quality" back into the domain of objective, measurable behavior.

**Multi-Week/Multi-Year Traps:**
For the modern analyst or experimentalist applying this logic today, several temporal traps emerge:
1. **The Training Trap:** The assumption that the Martian can be rapidly trained to distinguish stimuli might ignore biological imperatives. If the Martian possesses entirely different photoreceptors, training him on our dimensions (red-yellow vs. green-blue) could take months or years, potentially leading to subject dropout or failure unrelated to cognitive capacity. 
2. **The Dimensionality Trap:** We might spend weeks attempting to map the "pyramid," only to realize we are imposing Euclidean space on a non-Euclidean perceptual reality. A long-term trap is concluding the Martian lacks color vision when it actually operates on a completely different dimensional manifold (e.g., polarization or magnetic fields) that our discrimination-box methodology cannot capture without radical modification.
3. **The Interpretive Lag:** Just as Titchener required years to train observers, interpreting the Martian's behavioral output requires time. An immediate "success" in one week might be reinterpreted as "chance performance" after three months of data collection, leading to a complete reversal of the initial hypothesis regarding the Martian's cognitive architecture. 

Tolman’s argument serves as a warning against the static snapshot of introspection; the true understanding of a cognitive system often emerges from the longitudinal analysis of how it navigates complex, multi-dimensional environmental spaces.

Let $D_M: \mathbf{S} \times \mathbf{S} \rightarrow \{0, 1\}$ be the observable **Discrimination Function** of the Martian, where $D_M(s_i, s_j) = 1$ if the Martian consistently chooses $s_i$ over $s_j$ under the established reward contingencies of the "discrimination-box," and $0$ otherwise.

**Operator: Dimensional Carry-Over (Transfer)**
Tolman asks whether training on one pair carries over to another. We define a **Carry-Over Operator** $\mathcal{T}$ acting on subsets of stimuli representing different "dimensions" (e.g., hue vs. brightness). Let $H_{RY}$ be the set of stimuli varying along the Red-Yellow axis, and $H_{GY}$ along the Green-Yellow axis.
$$ \text{CarryOver}(H_{RY}, H_{GY}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } \exists \text{ training on } D_M|_{H_{RY}} \text{ such that } \forall s_i, s_j \in H_{GY}, \text{ performance on } D_M(s_i, s_j) \text{ improves significantly above baseline} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
If $\mathcal{T}$ yields 0, Tolman posits the dimensions lie in separate psychological spaces.

**Units:** The resulting "Color-Pyramid" is a topological embedding of $\mathbf{S}$ into a latent space $\mathcal{L}$. The distance $d_{\mathcal{L}}(s_i, s_j)$ corresponds to the difficulty of discrimination, inversely related to $D_M$.
