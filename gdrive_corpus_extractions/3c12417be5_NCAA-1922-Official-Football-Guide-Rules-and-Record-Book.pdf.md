$$H(A\mid X) \;=\; -\!\!\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{x\in X} p(a,x)\,\log_2 p(a\mid x)\quad[\text{bits}],\qquad 0\le H(A\mid X)\le \log_2|A|.$$
A shift is a pre-snap relabeling that decorrelates $X$ from $A$, pushing $H(A\mid X)\!\uparrow\!\log_2|A|$ (maximum confusion). Proof sketch from the operator: $q'(m)<0$ and $\lambda'(m)>0$ imply $E_{\text{open}}(m)\downarrow$; line play's short, low-handling structure makes $E_{\text{line}}(m)$ comparatively insensitive to $m$; hence a unique crossing $m^\*$ exists with dominance reversed above it. The veto is "cross-layer" because it overrides the chosen layer rather than merely reducing its payoff uniformly.

**Textual proof of invariance.** The strongest evidence is that the veto struck the *best* open-game team, not a weak one. California's machine — "312 points… 822 to 47" over two seasons [PAGE 149 of 424] — was held scoreless by W. & J. on "a muddy trip and wet field," with conditions that "stripped Smith's team of their most effective means of attack, namely, the open game" ([PAGE 151 of 424]). Intent (California wanted to pass) could not defeat the surface; the outcome $E_{\text{open}}\approx 0$ is the signature of $m>m^\*$. Tulane–LSU is the same theorem at the line-plunge margin: mud "wrecked Louisiana's… open game… and favored the heavy line plunging Legendre" ([PAGE 143 of 424]).

**Biomechanical sub-veto (drop-kick).** A distinct physical constraint: the drop-kicked field goal requires a firm, dry bounce. On mud the same surface that vetoes the pass vetoes the three-point drop-kick, collapsing both the open game *and* a scoring path — this jointly explains the scoreless ties (Penn State–Pitt, [PAGE 17 of 424]; California–W. & J.) as the intersection of an offensive veto and a scoring-mode veto. Note that the perfectly executed plays still fail on surface: Brenkert "went 35 yards and crossed the goal line, but a foul had been called and he was brought back" ([PAGE 55 of 424]); W. & J. lost a score to a teammate "being off-side on the play" ([PAGE 151 of 424]) — the rules layer independently vetoes execution.

**Adjudication.** The physical/weather veto is upheld and is *not* overridable at the strategy layer within a single game; the only escape is structural (reschedule, drain the field, or re-weight toward line play in preparation). Every scoreline the Guide attributes to weather is consistent with $m>m^\*$; none contradicts it.

Verifiable anchor: the source attributes the 0–0 result specifically to conditions stripping "the open game" ([PAGE 151 of 424]), while the same California side scored 312 on the season ([PAGE 149 of 424]). **Prediction:** $\Pr(\text{California scores}\mid m<m^\*) \gg \Pr(\text{California scores}\mid m>m^\*)\approx$ the observed 0. Falsifier: a documented California–W. & J. rematch on firm ground won by California with multiple touchdowns would strengthen, not refute, the model; a repeat 0–0 on a dry field would refute it.
