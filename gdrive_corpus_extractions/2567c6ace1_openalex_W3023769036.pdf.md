**Multi-hop derivation.** The document is self-identified as a "Review Article" [PAGE 1], received 25 March 2020 and published 21 April 2020. That single metadata fact governs everything downstream: there is no primary dataset in these eight pages, so every number is a citation of a citation. The derivation must therefore be run on the *inferential scaffolding*, not on the data.

*Hop 1 — the modal claim.* The Labby pyramid is introduced with a load-bearing necessity operator: "each level must be built on a solid preceding foundational level" and "Should one lower level be weak, or of less width in the case of a geometric pyramid, the pyramid will not be stable and will not function properly" [PAGE 1]. "Must" and "will not" are conjunctive claims, not additive ones. The paper does not cite an empirical test of the necessity; it cites Kirschen & Laby [ref 2] and Laby & Kirschen [ref 1], i.e., the model's own authors.

*Hop 2 — composition law.* If the levels are necessary conditions, the composition of level competences into performance probability is a product, and the product is bounded above by its smallest factor (Fréchet–Hoeffding). This is forced by the model, not chosen by the modeller. The paper never writes the composition law, but the pyramid figure is meaningless without one.

*Hop 3 — the marginal-return corollary.* Under a conjunctive composition, the partial derivative of performance with respect to any *non-binding* level is zero. A training intervention aimed at a level that is not the current minimum has zero first-order return. This corollary is not stated anywhere in the review, yet it is the only way to reconcile the review's enthusiasm with the persistent null results in the field — including the paper's own reference [12], Abernethy & Wood, "Do generalized visual training programmes for sport really work? An experimental investigation" [PAGE 7], a negative-result paper cited at [PAGE 2] inside a list intended to support athlete superiority.

*Hop 4 — heterogeneous effects.* Because the binding level differs across athletes, a sample-averaged training effect must be a mixture over regimes: large where the intervention happens to hit the binding level, ~0 otherwise. This predicts exactly the pattern seen in the strobe literature the paper summarises — small pilots with strong effects.

*Hop 5 — the individual-prescription gap.* The conclusion states that athletes' skills "are amenable to training and improvement through visual sports training" [PAGE 7]. That is a population-level statement. It cannot license an individual prescription without knowing that individual's binding level, which requires the full battery that the paper itself says "can take anywhere from ninety minutes to two hours to complete" [PAGE 5]. The review's own logistics undercut its own conclusion.

**Gray-zone audit of the numerals.**

(a) "information from the dominant eye is processed approximately 14 ms faster than information from the non-dominant eye" [PAGE 4], attributed to Coren & Porac 1982 [ref 16]. This is a *monocular* visual-latency asymmetry under a sighting-dominance paradigm. Transporting it into a binocular, in-competition reaction-time advantage requires the unstated assumption that the asymmetry survives binocular integration — where summation could either preserve or cancel it. The paper then leaps from a latency measurement to a training prescription ("use visual training to create symmetries in the dominances"), which inverts the causal direction: latency asymmetry would have to be shown to be plastic, and the MEG/fMRI evidence cited [refs 17, 18] is about cortical activation *area*, not plasticity. Note also the transcription "magnetoencephatolography (MGE)" [PAGE 4] — MEG.

(b) "Buys and Ferrira attribute acquisition of 80% of sportive environment information through sight" [PAGE 5]. This is a folk number with a long optometric lineage and no primary measurement in this document. More importantly it is a claim about *modality share* of information, not *modality leverage* over outcome. Only leverage is actionable, and the two are not the same quantity: a modality can carry 80% of the input and 0% of the variance in results if the remaining 20% is the discriminative part.

(c) "Mitroff report an18% improvement in on-ice skill performance from pre-training to pos-training, whereas the control group´s performance did not improve" [PAGE 6]. Reference [43] is titled "… - A pilot Study". Percentage improvement with an unspecified denominator, a small control arm whose flat mean is equally consistent with noise, and no blinding described. This is the weakest link in the empirical chain and it is the one the review leans on hardest.

(d) "only one out of four athletes who needed to use correction for playing football actually used it" [PAGE 3]. This is the single most actionable number in the paper — and it is a *compliance* number, not a performance number. It implies a large, cheap, unglamorous upstream fix that dominates any downstream training effect, which is precisely the kind of finding a training-focused review tends to bury in a bullet.

(e) "focus ability is limited to 3 degrees" and "the full range of vision extending to 180 degrees" [PAGE 5]. These are the only two clean geometric constants in the document, and they are the ones that yield hard quantitative structure (below).

**Internal tensions.** The paper asserts both "The peripheral field of vision is genetically determined and unchangeable" and "peripheral awareness may be trained and enhanced" [PAGE 5] in adjacent sentences. The distinction is coherent only if the boundary between "field extent" and "awareness" is drawn operationally; the paper gestures at it ("without moving your eyes") but does not measure it, and the supporting citations [refs 25, 26] are 1968 and 1995 secondary reports of "Russian studies of vision" [PAGE 8] — a thin lineage for a strong plasticity claim. Likewise, "These approaches are limited because their isolate component visual performance factors" [PAGE 6] is an admission that the entire component-isolation paradigm may be mis-specified, yet the paper's own battery is exactly a component-isolation battery.

**The causal inversion.** Every athlete-superiority finding cited [PAGE 2, refs 4–12] is cross-sectional. Cross-sectional superiority is compatible with *selection* (better visual systems choose into sport) and with *training* (sport trains the visual system) and with *reverse causation*. The review never resolves this, and the resolution is the hinge on which the entire "train vision → improve sport" inference turns. A conjunctive model rescues part of it: if a base level is a hard gate, improving it must help; if it is not, improvement is decorative.

<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>

**E1. Conjunctive pyramid gating.** Let $c_i\in[0,1]$ be the competence (probability of adequate function under task demand) at pyramid level $i$, ordered $i=1$ monocular (acuity, contrast), $i=2$ binocular (stereopsis), $i=3$ go/no-go decision, $i=4$ motor effector. Under independence,

$$P_{\text{succ}}=\prod_{i=1}^{4}c_i,\qquad P_{\text{succ}}\le\min_i c_i \quad\text{(Fréchet–Hoeffding)}.$$

Dimensionless. Source: "Without optimal function of each monocular ability, binocular function cannot be optimal" and "must be coordinated into a rapid, efficient, and precise motor action" [PAGE 1–2]. **Breakdown/singularity:** $c_i\to 0$ at any level forces $P_{\text{succ}}\to 0$ exactly — a hard gate, e.g. uncorrected refractive error rendering the target undetectable. Under perfect comonotonicity the bound is attained: $P_{\text{succ}}=\min_i c_i$. The independence assumption is the model's weakest point and is not testable from this source. **Marginal-return corollary:** for $j\neq\arg\min_i c_i$, $\partial P_{\text{succ}}/\partial c_j = \prod_{i\neq j}c_i \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{non-binding}]\to 0$ when the minimum is near zero.

**E2. Visual-motor reaction time decomposition.** From "including the period required for the retinal cell information to the visual cortex, and time required for the neuromuscular system to send the information to the muscles" [PAGE 4]:

$$T_{\text{VMRT}}=t_{\text{photo}}+t_{\text{ret}}+t_{\text{aff}}+t_{\text{V1}}+t_{\text{dec}}+t_{\text{eff}}+t_{\text{NMJ}}+t_{\text{MT}},\quad [\mathrm{ms}].$$

**Breakdown:** the sum has a physiological floor set by $t_{\text{photo}}+t_{\text{ret}}+t_{\text{aff}}+t_{\text{NMJ}}$; no training can drive $T_{\text{VMRT}}$ below it. Any claimed improvement must be located in $t_{\text{dec}}$ (decision) — the paper's "millisecond-level decision making" [PAGE 1] — not in the sensory or neuromuscular terms.

**E3. Speed–accuracy law for inspection time.** Modelling the "go/no-go" level as a symmetric drift-diffusion with boundary separation $a$ (in units of the noise $\sigma$, dimensionless), drift $v$ $[\sigma\cdot\mathrm{ms}^{-1}]$, non-decision time $T_{er}$ $[\mathrm{ms}]$:

$$\mathbb{E}[T]=T_{er}+\frac{a}{2v}\tanh\!\left(\frac{av}{2}\right),\qquad P_{\text{corr}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-2va}}.$$

Source: "Shorter inspection time allow accurate decisions to be made from shorter stimulus duration than from longer inspection times" and tachistoscopic exposure "at on-hundredth of a second" ($10\ \mathrm{ms}$) [PAGE 5]. **Regimes:** $v\to 0$ gives $\mathbb{E}[T]\to T_{er}+a^{2}/4$ (pure diffusion limit, error-dominated); $va\gg 1$ gives $\mathbb{E}[T]\to T_{er}+a/(2v)$ (drift-limited, the only regime where training can buy speed). **Singularity:** $v=0$ is an absorbing failure — no finite boundary yields reliable decisions.

**E4. Interocular latency asymmetry.** $T^{(\text{dom})}_{\text{VMRT}}-T^{(\text{non})}_{\text{VMRT}}=-\Delta t$, with $\Delta t\approx 14\ \mathrm{ms}$ [PAGE 4, ref 16]. Normalised: $\Delta t\,/\,T_{\text{VMRT}}\approx 0.06$–$0.10$ for typical values. This term is *additive and bounded*; it cannot explain large group differences and must not be multiplied into E1 as if it were a competence.

**E5. Foveal solid angle.** With foveal half-angle $\theta=1.5°$ ("focus ability is limited to 3 degrees" [PAGE 5]):

$$\Omega_f=2\pi(1-\cos\theta)=2.153\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{sr},\qquad \frac{\Omega_f}{2\pi}=3.43\times10^{-4}.$$

So the fovea samples $\approx 0.034\%$ of a hemisphere; against a $180°$ field [PAGE 5] the peripheral channel is the only wide-area detector. **Breakdown:** this ratio is why peripheral *detection* latency, not peripheral *acuity*, is the operative variable — acuity outside the fovea is below any useful threshold.

**E6. Stereoscopic depth resolution and its quadratic collapse.** With disparity threshold $\delta$ [rad] and interpupillary baseline $b$ [m],

$$\Delta d\approx\frac{\delta\,d^{2}}{b}.$$

Using a normative $\delta=40\ \mathrm{arcsec}=1.939\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{rad}$ and $b=0.065\ \mathrm{m}$ (assumed parameters, not from source): $\Delta d=0.30\ \mathrm{m}$ at $d=10\ \mathrm{m}$, $1.19\ \mathrm{m}$ at $d=20\ \mathrm{m}$. **Breakdown regime:** beyond the distance where $\Delta d$ exceeds the task tolerance, stereopsis is vetoed and the athlete is effectively monocular — matching the paper's note that "binocular vision can improve performance on certain tasks compared with performance by individuals using only one eye" [PAGE 3].

**E7. Defocus, Strehl ratio, and the validity boundary.** For defocus $D$ [$\mathrm{D}$] over pupil radius $r_p$ [m],

$$W_{rms}=\frac{D\,r_p^{2}}{4\sqrt3},\qquad S\approx e^{-(2\pi W_{rms}/\lambda)^{2}}.$$

With $D=0.50\ \mathrm{D}$, $r_p=3\ \mathrm{mm}$: $W_{rms}=6.50\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m}=0.65\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, versus $\lambda/14=39.6\ \mathrm{nm}$ at $\lambda=555\ \mathrm{nm}$. **Breakdown:** the Maréchal approximation is valid only for $W_{rms}\lesssim\lambda/14$; at $0.65\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ it is far outside its domain and returns $S\to0$ spuriously. The correct statement is qualitative and is exactly the paper's point: "possible low astigmatisms induce a feeling of good vision to athletes, but which can limit the constant and fluid sharpness during a competition" [PAGE 2–3]. Low-order defocus is not negligible; the approximation that says it is, is the thing that breaks.

**E8. Stroboscopic duty cycle and the Nyquist constraint.** Lenses "alternate between transparent and opaque states" with control of "transition frequency and ratio of image" [PAGE 6]. Define

$$D=\frac{t_{on}}{t_{on}+t_{off}},\qquad f_s=\frac{1}{t_{on}+t_{off}},\qquad \Phi=D\,\Phi_0,$$

with $D$ dimensionless, $f_s$ in Hz, $\Phi$ the luminous flux delivered [lm]. **Breakdown:** if $f_s\le 2f_{kin}$, where $f_{kin}$ is the characteristic frequency of the tracked kinematics, the strobe stops intermittently sampling a continuous percept and starts aliasing it — the task becomes a guessing task with no perceptual-learning signal. This is the unstated constraint that separates a training effect from a novelty effect, and the review does not report $f_s$ for any cited study.

**E9. Fatigue as drift decay.** Phenomenological, with fatigue time constant $\tau$ [min] and $v_0$ the rested drift rate:

$$v(t)=v_0e^{-t/\tau}\ \Rightarrow\ t^{*}=\tau\ln\!\left(\frac{v_0}{v_{crit}}\right).$$

Source: "reaction times have been shown to be impaired by factors such as reduced IQ, cold, fatigue, and restriction of peripheral visual fields with protective eyewear" [PAGE 4–5]. **Assumption:** drift, not boundary, is what fatigue degrades. **Breakdown:** past $t^{*}$ the athlete crosses from latency-limited to error-limited performance; further speed is bought only with accuracy, and E3's $P_{\text{corr}}$ collapses.

**E10. Contrast cascade.** $C_{ret}=C_{obj}\cdot M_{opt}\cdot M_{tear}\cdot M_{neural}$, all dimensionless modulation factors. Source: tear film changes "will cause fluctuations in the sharpness of the image throughout a competition" [PAGE 3–4], and indoor sport occurs "where the contrast is not the maximum contrast that is measured with a Snellen table" [PAGE 3]. **Singularity:** when the product falls below the detection threshold, the athlete is functionally blind to the object regardless of attention, effort, or training — a multiplicative hard veto.

<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>

The pyramid is not original to this review: it is attributed to "Labby et all" [PAGE 1] and to Laby & Kirschen, "A new model for Sports and Performance Vision," *Vision Development and Rehabilitation* 4.2 (2018): 91–97 [ref 1], restated in Kirschen & Laby, "The role of sports vision in eye care today," *Eye Contact Lens* 37.3 (2011): 127–130 [ref 2]. The 2011 paper predates the 2018 one in print but the 2018 paper carries the model — a publication-order inversion that matters when dating the idea. The decision layer traces to Miller & Clapp, "From Vision to Decision" [ref 3, PAGE 1], and the millisecond framing to the same source.

The quantitative spine of the dominance argument is older still: Coren & Porac, "Monocular asymmetries in visual latency as a function of sighting dominance," *Am J Optom Physiol Opt* 59.12 (1982): 987–990 [ref 16], which supplies the 14 ms figure; Shima, "Ocular dominance affects magnitude of dipole moment: an MEG study," *NeuroReport* 21.12 (2010) [ref 17]; and Mendola & Conner, "Eye dominance predicts fMRI signals in human retinotopic cortex," *Neurosci Lett* 414.1 (2007) [ref 18]. Three different instruments — psychophysics, magnetoencephalography, fMRI — measuring three different quantities, concatenated into one causal story. The MEG measure is a dipole moment; the fMRI measure is a retinotopic activation area; neither is a reaction time. The chain is genealogically respectable and inferentially broken.

The training wing descends from Erickson's textbook [ref 14, PAGE 2], Winton's *VisionPro* [ref 19], and Teig's *High Performance Vision* [ref 20] — a practitioner literature, not a trial literature. The stroboscopic branch descends from Appelbaum et al. 2011 (*Frontiers in Psychology* 2:276) and 2012 (*Atten Percept Psychophys* 74:1681–1691) [refs 38, 40], Smith & Mitroff 2012 (*Int J Exerc Sci* 5.4:344–353) [refs 28, 37, 39 — cited three times], and Mitroff et al. 2013, "Enhancing Ice Hockey Skills Through Stroboscopic Visual Training – A pilot Study" [ref 43]. The same anticipatory-timing paper appears as references 28, 37, and 39, which inflates the apparent evidential mass of the strobe claim by a factor of three in a casual read of the bibliography. The multiple-object-tracking branch (NeuroTracker) descends from Faubert's group [refs 32–36] and is the only branch with an explicit transfer-to-game-performance claim, in NBA and soccer players [refs 35, 36].

**Multi-week traps.** (i) *The transfer trap*: four to eight weeks of training reliably improves the trained task; the question is always the untrained one, and the review's own ref [12] is the canonical negative test. (ii) *The novelty trap*: any new, effortful, attention-demanding regimen produces a short-term arousal effect that decays over weeks — a stroboscopic effect measured at post-test week 1 cannot be separated from this without a placebo-strobe arm (powered but transparent), which no cited study reports. (iii) *The compliance trap*: the one-in-four correction figure [PAGE 3] implies that a substantial fraction of any "untrained" control group is optically compromised at baseline, inflating apparent training effects and confounding the literature for as long as it goes unmeasured. (iv) *The regression trap*: athletes are recruited at a performance peak; pre/post pilot designs without a control regress toward the mean and manufacture improvement.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

**The clock.** The athlete's objective is deadline-bounded: "elite athletics falls into a small class of human behaviors in which the perception-action cycle is required to function under intense temporal demands" [PAGE 2]. Formalise slack time $\tau_{slack}$ [s] and a deadline-discounted objective

$$J=\sum_{t}\gamma^{t}r_{t},\qquad \gamma=e^{-1/(\lambda\,\tau_{slack})},\ \lambda\ [\mathrm{s}].$$

As $\tau_{slack}\to 0$, $\gamma\to 0$: the horizon collapses to the immediate reward, and the optimal policy becomes myopic. A myopic visuo-motor policy is the overlearned one — the earliest-trained, lowest-level, dominant-eye/dominant-limb pattern. This is the formal content of **crisis habit regression**: under time pressure the athlete does not choose the trained skill, they *default* to the habit, because the trained skill's advantage is realised only over a horizon the collapsing $\gamma$ has already discounted away.

**The counter-trap.** The trained override is executive and resource-hungry; it is the first thing to fail under load. Adding a second, attentionally expensive cue (a new fixation pattern, a new head position, a "symmetrise the dominance" drill [PAGE 4]) raises the trained policy's cost precisely when resources are scarcest. The counter-trap is therefore: *the correction that helps in the quiet laboratory is the correction that collapses in the crisis*, and it collapses into the very habit it was meant to replace. Prediction: a drill that improves a component measure at rest while degrading it under dual-task load is net-negative and will look positive in every pre/post design the review cites.

**The recursive layer.** The athlete models the opponent's visuo-motor clock and tries to force the opponent into the regressed regime — the paper's "read the play" and anticipation [PAGE 2] are exactly this: "decisions must typically be made in advance of an opponent`s." Anticipation is a second-order move: instead of resolving the stimulus, the athlete resolves the *distribution over* stimuli, which short-circuits E3's drift-diffusion by replacing evidence accumulation with a prior. That is the only lever that beats the E2 latency floor, and it is a cognitive/perceptual lever, not an optical one — which is why the strobe branch (intermittent vision forcing predictive control) is theoretically the most defensible training idea in the paper, and why its empirical support being a single pilot [ref 43] is the paper's central weakness.

**Diagnostic ordering implied.** Because of E1, the coach's decision problem is a bottleneck search, not an enrichment problem. Habit regression and counter-traps are second-order only once $c_1,c_2>0$. Any protocol that trains level 4 while level 1 is dark is not merely inefficient; under E1 it is exactly zero.

</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

A veto is a constraint that binds, in the KKT sense: maximise performance $J(\mathbf{x})$ subject to $g_k(\mathbf{x})\le0$; if $g_k$ is active at the optimum, the shadow price of investment in every blocked direction is zero. Complementary slackness is the proof obligation — the veto must be *shown* active, not asserted.

**Physical veto — optics.** E7: uncorrected low astigmatism produces "a feeling of good vision" while limiting "the constant and fluid sharpness during a competition" [PAGE 2–3]. Combined with "only one out of four athletes who needed to use correction for playing football actually used it" [PAGE 3], this is an active constraint in ~75% of the indicated subpopulation. Proof route: measure uncorrected VA and contrast sensitivity, correct, re-measure dynamic VA. If dynamic VA rises on correction alone by more than the test–retest SD, the optical constraint was binding and any concurrent training effect is confounded.

**Physical veto — tear film.** E10: "changes in the defective structure or composition of some of the layers of tear film, will cause fluctuations in the sharpness of the image throughout a competition" [PAGE 3–4]. Proof route: tear break-up time plus serial contrast sensitivity across a simulated competition block; a monotone decline in $C_{ret}$ that tracks TBUT confirms the veto is time-varying and therefore un-trainable by any SVT protocol.

**Biomechanical veto — the oculomotor plant.** Saccadic and pursuit execution is bounded by muscle and neural dynamics, and the paper is explicit that binocular alignment "is basically based on muscles and their strength and endurance" and that under effort the system "may not allow a clear and stable view with the course of the competition" [PAGE 3]. Proof route: fixation stability and heterophoria measured pre- and post-fatigue protocol; if decompensation appears before the fatigue time constant $\tau$ of E9, the binocular level is the binding gate and all higher-level training is shadow-priced at zero.

**Biomechanical veto — protective equipment.** "restriction of peripheral visual fields with protective eyewear" degrades reaction time [PAGE 4–5]. Because the fovea samples only $3.43\times10^{-4}$ of the field (E5), field restriction is not a minor loss: it removes the detection channel that redirects the fovea. Proof route: measure reaction time and field extent with and without the mandated equipment; a field loss that pushes detection latency past the task's $\tau_{slack}$ is a hard veto, not a training target.

**Environmental veto — contrast regime.** "there are many sports that are played in sports hall, where the contrast is not the maximum contrast that is measured with a Snellen table" [PAGE 3]. A Snellen-normal athlete can be functionally deficient in the actual competition contrast regime. Proof route: Pelli-Robson or Peek CS under the competition's measured luminance and contrast; if CS falls below the threshold for the object's size and speed, no amount of eye-movement training recovers detection.

**Adjudication rule.** Rank the candidate vetoes by the *measured* gap between the athlete's value and the task requirement, normalised by that requirement; the largest normalised gap is the presumed bottleneck, and all interventions not addressing it are held in abeyance until it is cleared. This converts the review's flat list of eleven primary and six secondary skills into a decision procedure it never provides.

</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>

**V1 — The duty-cycle identity test (kills the novelty explanation for stroboscopic training).**
*Design.* Three arms, eight weeks, matched total luminous exposure $\Phi=D\Phi_0$ and matched total training time: (a) strobe at $D=0.5$, $f_s$ set above $2f_{kin}$ for the trained task; (b) placebo strobe — lenses powered, held transparent, identical hardware and identical schedule; (c) continuous-light training. Primary outcome: anticipatory timing error [ms] on an untrained transfer task.
*Prediction.* Arm (a) − arm (b) exceeds the within-arm test–retest SD on the transfer task; arm (b) − arm (c) is within noise.
*Veto.* If (a) ≈ (b), the effect is arousal/hardware, and E8's mechanism is refuted. Cite [PAGE 6] for the device class and [ref 43] for the pilot that this design supersedes.

**V2 — The base-level gate test (tests E1's conjunctive claim directly).**
*Design.* Recruit athletes with uncorrected astigmatism $\ge0.50\ \mathrm{D}$ (the paper's own "low astigmatisms" population [PAGE 2–3]). Four arms: correction only; SVT only without correction; correction + SVT; neither. Eight weeks. Primary outcome: dynamic visual acuity and eye–hand reaction time.
*Prediction.* Correction-only recovers at least as much dynamic VA as correction + SVT; SVT-without-correction produces a gain indistinguishable from zero on E1's gate logic.
*Veto.* If SVT-without-correction produces a gain comparable to correction-only, the conjunctive model is false and the levels compose additively — a decisive, publishable refutation.

**V3 — The dominance-latency dissociation test (tests whether the 14 ms is plastic).**
*Design.* Athletes with crossed eye–hand dominance [PAGE 4]. Twelve weeks of the "create symmetries in the dominances" protocol the paper proposes. Two outcomes measured independently: (i) behavioural sensory-dominance index (sighting/binocular-rivalry balance); (ii) electrophysiological interocular latency difference $\Delta t$.
*Prediction.* (i) shifts significantly; (ii) stays within $\pm2\ \mathrm{ms}$ of its baseline 14 ms value.
*Veto.* If $\Delta t$ shifts materially, the latency asymmetry is trainable and E4 must be promoted from a fixed offset to a state variable — which would be a genuinely novel finding and would contradict the reading of Coren & Porac [ref 16] as a stable trait.

**V4 (supplementary) — The cue-specificity transfer test.** Mobile eye tracking ("one to monitor eye position and another to monitor motor performance characteristics" [PAGE 4]) to test whether trained fixation patterns transfer to untrained opponents' kinematics. Prediction: fixation entropy decreases on trained stimuli only; no transfer to novel kinematics within eight weeks. Veto: transfer observed implies a general perceptual-learning mechanism and would justify the review's optimism; its absence confines SVT to stimulus-specific gains.
