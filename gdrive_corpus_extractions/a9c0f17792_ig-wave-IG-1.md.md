Equation 1 — Expected Points Added (EPA), state-value difference [IG1-01, IG1-02]:
Let \(s_t = (\text{down}, \text{distance}, \text{yardline}, \text{score differential}, \text{time remaining})\). Then
\[
\text{EPA}(\text{play}_t) \;=\; \mathbb{E}[P_{t+1} \mid s_{t+1}] \;-\; \mathbb{E}[P_t \mid s_t],
\]
where \(P\) is expected points. Unit: points. Assumption: sufficient historical density in each state cell for stable conditional expectations. Breakdown regime: sparse cells (rare down-distance-yardline combinations) produce high-variance EPA; requires smoothing or Bayesian shrinkage before use as a play-value label.

Equation 2 — NET EPA/play by phase [IG1-02]:
\[
\overline{\text{EPA}}_{\text{team}} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \bigl(\text{EPA}^{\text{off}}_i + \text{EPA}^{\text{def}}_i + \text{EPA}^{\text{st}}_i\bigr).
\]
Unit: points per play. Breakdown: special-teams EPA is noisier than offense/defense; rate statistics require min-snap floors (cf. PFF min-150-pass-snap threshold [IG1-16]) to avoid small-sample leaderboard noise.

Unit: expected points or win-probability equivalent. Discount \(\gamma \approx 1\) for single-play decisions. Breakdown: misspecification of \(P(s' \mid s,a)\) in rare situations (e.g., 4th-and-long backed up) invalidates the policy comparison.
