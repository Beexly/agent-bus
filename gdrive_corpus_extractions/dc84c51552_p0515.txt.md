Crisis habit regression versus counter-trap versus clock.

Crisis habit regression: Under acute stress or crisis, subjects regress to the naive innervation habit: they report “feeling the will” drive the muscle because high-intensity sympathetic contractions—frowning, teeth clinching, scalp tension—dominate salience [PAGE 1]. This is a regression to pre-scientific folk psychology in which effort is experienced as an efferent, antecedent force rather than as a consequent afferent registration. James’s universal claim that every case of effort is explicable by muscular contraction [PAGE 1] directly targets this habit; under crisis, however, the peripheral signal is noisy and the sympathetic overflow is vivid, making the central illusion cognitively sticky.

Counter-trap: The decisive experimental counter-trap is to generate efferent command \(\mathbf{E}(t)\neq\mathbf{0}\) while suppressing afferent reafference \(\mathbf{A}(t)\approx\mathbf{0}\) (selective dorsal root block, ischemic sensory anesthesia, or curare with preserved motor drive). James predicts \(C_e(t)=0\) because consciousness of effort is “conditioned by the actual fact of muscular contraction” and its centripetal impressions [PAGE 1]. If subjects nevertheless report effort in the absence of afference, the afferent theory is vetoed and an efferent or central-innervation account regains viability.

Clock: The temporal structure of conscious effort is recursively shifted. James states that the idea of required exertion is “an anticipatory image of the movement’s sensible effects” [PAGE 1]. Operationally, efferent command \(E(t)\) precedes movement and afferent feedback \(A(t+\Delta)\); the subject timestamps effort at \(t_e\approx t\), not at \(t+\Delta\). The “clock” of felt effort is therefore advanced by the forward-model prediction \(\mathcal{M}\{E(t)\}\), creating the phenomenological illusion that effort precedes movement. Verbatim: consciousness of effort “is conditioned by the actual fact of muscular contraction” [PAGE 1], yet the *idea* of exertion is anticipatory [PAGE 1]. The crisis habit regresses because subjects read the forward-model timestamp as evidence of volitional causation; the counter-trap removes afference to test whether the clock can run without the peripheral signal.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical, biomechanical, and environmental veto proof.

Physical layer: Neuromuscular transmission is the necessary transduction boundary. If motor command reaches the muscle but excitation-contraction coupling is pharmacologically blocked (e.g., curare at the motor endplate), then \(\mathbf{F}_{m}(t)\equiv\mathbf{0}\) despite \(\mathbf{E}(t)\neq\mathbf{0}\). James’s rule yields \(C_e(t)=0\): no contraction, no centripetal impression, no effort [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: if effort is reported under complete peripheral paralysis with intact volition, the afferent necessity claim is falsified.

Biomechanical layer: Respiratory fixation \(R(t)\) is not an epiphenomenon but a load-bearing variable [PAGE 1]. Fixation of intercostal and diaphragmatic muscles alters intra-abdominal pressure, trunk stiffness, and the global afferent baseline. For matched limb force \(\mathbf{F}_{m}\), variation in \(R(t)\) should shift \(C_e\) because “the fixation of the respiratory muscles… is the basis of the general sense of effort in all its varying degrees” [PAGE 1]. Veto condition: if effort ratings remain invariant across postures that drastically change respiratory fixation while limb force is held constant by a dynamometer, James’s rebuttal to Wundt fails and the afferent model requires a dominant limb-force term with negligible \(R(t)\).

Weather/environmental layer: Ambient temperature \(T_a\) affects muscle-spindle firing gain and nerve-conduction velocity \(v_c(T)\), and cold stress induces shivering and respiratory co-contraction, elevating \(R(t)\) independent of voluntary limb force. At low \(T_a\), James’s model predicts an increased global effort sense even for constant \(\mathbf{F}_{m}\) because \(R(t)\) rises and afferent gain shifts [PAGE 1]. Conversely, local cooling of a limb reduces spindle afferent gain; if reported effort drops despite unchanged motor command and force, the afferent veto is confirmed. Veto condition: if temperature manipulation changes effort reports without any corresponding change in either \(\mathbf{F}_{m}\) or measured afferent firing, then an unmodeled central or thermal sensorimotor term is required.

Singularity/breakdown: Complete spinal transection above the lesion severs ascending centripetal paths [PAGE 1]; the model predicts total loss of conscious muscular sense below the lesion even if supraspinal motor planning persists. This is the exact regime in which James’s afferent theory is most exposed: if any vestige of effort is reported below a complete sensory block, the “no vestige of a muscular sense” claim [PAGE 1] collapses.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

<RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
Three verifiable RLVR (reward-verifiable counterfactual) veto scenarios with predictions.

Scenario 1: Selective sensory deafferentation with intact motor output.
Manipulation: Apply an ischemic or dorsal-root sensory block to the right upper limb while leaving motor efferents intact; instruct the subject to attempt a maximal voluntary contraction. Measure visible force with a load cell and collect effort reports.
Afferent prediction (James/Münsterberg [PAGE 1]): \(\mathbf{A}_{\text{right}}\approx\mathbf{0}\Rightarrow C_e\approx0\) for the blocked limb, despite \(\mathbf{E}_{\text{right}}\neq\mathbf{0}\) and visible muscle contraction; the unblocked left limb reports normal effort.
Efferent/innervation prediction: Motor command is intact, so effort should be felt in the blocked limb.
Verifiable veto: If the subject reports no conscious effort in the deafferented limb while force is objectively produced, the efferent account is vetoed; if effort is reported, James’s afferent necessity claim [PAGE 1] is falsified.

Scenario 2: Tendon-vibration illusory afference without voluntary effort.
Manipulation: Apply 80-Hz vibration to the biceps tendon to evoke spindle afference and an illusory sense of arm elongation while the subject remains relaxed or produces minimal force. Record force-plate output and effort ratings.
Afferent prediction: Spindle-driven \(\mathbf{A}\) increases without corresponding \(\mathbf{E}\) or \(\mathbf{F}_{m}\); James’s afferent dependency permits effort to rise with afference even absent strong motor command, because consciousness is “conditioned by the actual fact of muscular contraction” and its impressions [PAGE 1].
Efferent prediction: Little or no motor command means little or no effort.
Verifiable veto: A strong illusory effort report with zero measured force confirms afferent sufficiency; absence of effort despite robust spindle afference vetoes the claim that centripetal impressions alone generate the feeling of exertion.

Scenario 3: Respiratory-fixation manipulation at matched limb force.
Manipulation: Have subjects produce an identical submaximal elbow-flexion force (verified by dynamometer) in two conditions: upright seated with normal respiratory fixation (\(R\) high) and supine with relaxed breathing (\(R\) low). Collect effort ratings.
Afferent prediction (James’s Wundt rebuttal [PAGE 1]): The general sense of effort scales with respiratory fixation; therefore upright/high-\(R\) yields significantly higher effort ratings than supine/low-\(R\) for the same limb force.
Efferent prediction: Same motor command and same force imply same effort.
Verifiable veto: A significant rating difference in the direction of \(R\) supports James; equivalent ratings across conditions veto respiratory fixation as “the basis of the general sense of effort” [PAGE 1] and force the model toward a pure limb-force afferent readout.
</RLVR_COUNTERFACTUAL_VETOES>
