Let $R$ be a set of $N$ spatially arranged receptors (e.g., retinal ganglion cells), indexed by $i=1...N$. When a stimulus is presented at physical location $L_p$, the activation vector $A$ across the population is:
$$ A_i = \rho_i(L_p) + \eta_i $$
where $\eta_i$ is stochastic neural noise. 

**The Single Quale Incompleteness:**
Consider a hypothetical isolation where only one receptor $k$ fires significantly, while others are near-zero. The perceived sensation $S$ is a scalar intensity. The probability of the physical location given this isolated state is uniform over the support of $\rho_k$:
$$ P(L_p | A_k \gg 0, A_{j\neq k} \approx 0) \propto \rho_k(L_p) $$
This distribution has high entropy; it does not pinpoint $L_p$. Thus, a single isolated sensation carries negligible mutual information about exact position:
$$ I(L_p; A_k) = H(L_p) - H(L_p | A_k) \approx 0 $$
Units: Bits (Shannon entropy). Assumption: Uniform prior over the receptive field. Breakdown regime: As $\sigma \to 0$ (infinitely dense sampling), the single field approaches a Dirac delta, restoring intrinsic position, but biological constraints enforce $\sigma > 0$.

**The Relational Differential Operator:**
James posits that a "second point" is required for determination. In operator terms, the brain computes a gradient or a ratio. Let two stimuli occur at $L_1$ and $L_2$. The perceived separation is derived from the cross-correlation or the difference in peak activations. A simple measure of "unlikeness" (qualitative contrast) between two sensations induced by proximal points is the Mahalanobis distance between their activation vectors $A^{(1)}$ and $A^{(2)}$ in the neural space:
$$ D^2(A^{(1)}, A^{(2)}) = (A^{(1)} - A^{(2)})^T \Sigma^{-1} (A^{(1)} - A^{(2)}) $$
where $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix of neural noise. 
Assumptions: Linear summation of inputs, Gaussian noise. 
Breakdown Regime: If $L_1$ and $L_2$ are closer than the critical spacing (e.g., the Rayleigh criterion equivalent for the neural population, approx $1.2 \sigma$), $D^2$ falls below the detection threshold, leading to the "fusion" James describes on the back.
