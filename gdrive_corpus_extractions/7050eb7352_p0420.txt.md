A modeler who fits a static curve to multi-week acquisition data and then interprets a dip as "unlearning" commits a retro causal error: the dip is a phase of the oscillation operator, not a reversal of the habit-strength accumulation operator $\Gamma$. Conversely, attributing long-term behavioral change to "evolutionary adaptation" when it is actually operant learning (or vice versa) violates the separation Hull marks at [PAGE 385–386].

A second trap is the "amount of reinforcement" confusion across [PAGE 128] and [PAGE 206]. If a multi-week intervention varies reinforcement amount without holding the law of primary reinforcement constant, the model cannot identify whether changes in $H$ derive from amount, contingency, or temporal asynchronism [PAGE 167 f., 171 f.]. The index's separation of these entries by 78 pages is a warning that the formalism may shift across the text; applying one page's equation to another page's phenomenon is a category error.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Under crisis or high-drive conditions, the organism does not recompute an optimal reaction from current contingencies. The index entry "motivated behavior, 60 f." (cross-referenced from "Behavior") and "maladaptive behavior, 25" imply that the dominant response is the one with the highest accumulated habit strength $H(s,r)$, regardless of whether it is currently adaptive. This is crisis habit regression: the behavioral system regresses to the historically strongest trajectory.

The counter-trap is the modeler's recursive inference that crisis choice reveals current preference. Because $P(\text{evoke } r \mid s,t)$ depends on $sE(s,r,t)$, which integrates $H$ over prior trials, a crisis response reflects the habit-strength spectrum over a trailing window $[t-\tau, t]$, not a revealed preference at instant $t$. The "reaction-evocation paradox [PAGE 314f.]" reinforces this: high $sE$ does not guarantee evocation if $\theta$ is elevated or $\varepsilon_t$ is adverse. Therefore, observing a maladaptive response under crisis does not imply the organism "prefers" it; it implies that the threshold-crossing operator selected the historically dominant habit.

The clock layer is the interaction of habit decay and spontaneous recovery. "Behavioral oscillation [PAGE 304 f.]" with "spontaneous neural oscillation [PAGE 309 f.]" creates a phase lag: behavior at time $t$ is a function of the reinforcement history filtered through the oscillation operator, not merely the current contingency. If a crisis occurs during a trough of the oscillation cycle, even a strong habit may fail to evoke; if it occurs during a peak, a weak habit may evoke spuriously. Any theory of mind that ignores this clock and treats behavior as a static function of current incentives is recursively mis-specified.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
The index on [PAGE 420] supplies three veto layers that can falsify any behavior-theory prediction.

\textbf{Neural/biomechanical layer.} "All-or-none law: of muscle fiber, 51 f." imposes a hard threshold on effector output. "Action potentials, 40 f." and "Afferent neural impulse, 41 f." establish that neural signaling is discrete and all-or-none. "After-discharge: of heart, 42; of optic nerve, 40" establishes refractory periods. Veto condition: if a muscle fiber exhibits graded sub-threshold contraction, or if the optic nerve shows linearly graded after-discharge amplitude rather than all-or-none events, the Hullian effector model is falsified at its physiological base.

\textbf{Temporal/antecedent layer.} "Antecedent conditions: example of, 4, 22, 26, 57, 102" and "stimulus-response asynchronism, 167 f.; asynchronism gradients, 171 f., 180; point of optimal stimulus, 171, 180" define a temporal window for effective conditioning. Veto condition: if conditioning occurs when the stimulus-response interval falls outside the asynchronism gradient, or if behavior changes without any recorded antecedent condition, the antecedent-condition operator is mis-specified.

\textbf{Environmental/weather-analog layer.} While [PAGE 420] does not index "weather" directly, it indexes "organic basis of behavior, 18f.;" "activity of organism, 17 f.;" and "adaptation... receptors and effectors in, 384 f." These imply that the organism is embedded in an environmental field that can modulate receptor input and effector output. Extreme environmental states that alter afferent neural impulse conduction [PAGE 41 f.] or muscle-fiber threshold [PAGE 51 f.] act as vetoes: no central habit-strength computation can overcome a peripheral conduction block or an all-or-none effector refractory period.

Veto proof: A prediction is adjudicated false if (i) $sE \ge \theta$ yet no evocation occurs due to peripheral all-or-none blockade; (ii) evocation occurs when $sE < \theta$ (threshold violation); (iii) response persists under $A=0$ beyond the extinction boundary predicted by [PAGE 128 f.]; or (iv) generalization occurs across the full afferent generalization continuum [PAGE 188 f.] contrary to the gradient-width limit.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>

\textbf{Scenario 1 (Extinction amount veto).} Set reinforcement amount $A=0$ for $M$ trials after asymptotic responding. Based on "Amount of reinforcement hypothesis, 128; implications of, 129 f." and "Behavioral oscillation... experimental demonstration of, 304f.," the prediction is $\Delta H = 0$ and $P(\text{evocation})$ decays toward the baseline oscillation floor. Verifiable veto: if response rate remains statistically stationary above baseline for the full $M$ trials, the amount-of-reinforcement hypothesis is falsified; the habit-strength operator $\Gamma$ cannot be amount-dependent as indexed.

\textbf{Scenario 2 (Threshold singularity veto).} Hold effective reaction potential $sE$ constant while artificially elevating reaction threshold $\theta$ (e.g., via satiation, fatigue, or stimulus degradation). Based on "effective reaction potential, 313 f., 393f." and "reaction threshold, 325," the prediction is $P(\text{evocation}) \to 0$ once $\theta > sE$. Verifiable veto: if an evocation is recorded when independently measured $sE < \theta$, the threshold-crossing model is falsified; either $sE$ is mis-measured or evocation is not threshold-governed.

\textbf{Scenario 3 (Generalization-gradient veto).} Present a test stimulus at maximal distance on the afferent generalization continuum [PAGE 188 f.] from the conditioned stimulus, using compound conditioned stimuli with distributed habit strength [PAGE 206 f.]. The prediction is near-zero transfer at maximal dimensional distance. Verifiable veto: if robust responding occurs to the distant stimulus, the generalization-continuum operator is mis-specified (e.g., the system uses categorical perception rather than a graded continuum), and any model assuming Gaussian or monotonic generalization is falsified.
