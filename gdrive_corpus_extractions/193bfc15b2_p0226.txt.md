Then some \(T^{-1}(B)\notin\mathcal S\), so \(\mu T^{-1}(B)\) is undefined and no conditional kernel \(p(E,y)\) can be constructed. Physical analogue: a sensor whose output is not a measurable function of the system state cannot generate a pushforward probability law; the measurement layer is vetoed before any probability computation.

Layer 2 — Integrability (energetic/biomechanical): Veto if \(h(x)=f(x)g(T(x))\notin L^1(\mu)\) even though \(f\in L^1(\mu)\) and \(g\in L^1(\mu T^{-1})\). The product of integrable observables under a deterministic transformation need not be integrable. Biomechanical analogue: two individually bounded-force muscle actuators can produce an unbounded joint torque when composed through a nonlinear lever; the system-level energy integral diverges, so equation (7) of [PAGE 1] has no finite interpretation.

Layer 3 — Boundedness / compactness (material/thermodynamic): Halmos restricts §49 to uniformly bounded real-valued functions and unit-interval components [PAGE 1]. Veto if components are unbounded real lines without tightness. Weather analogue: prescribing marginal temperature distributions on an unbounded domain does not guarantee a tight sequence of finite-dimensional weather laws; mass can escape to extreme events, so no infinite-horizon climate measure exists absent a tightness/uniform-integrability condition.

Layer 4 — Consistency (causal/meteorological): Veto if finite-dimensional distributions are inconsistent under projection, i.e., for some \(m<n\), the \(m\)-dimensional marginal of the prescribed \(n\)-dimensional law differs from the independently prescribed \(m\)-dimensional law. Weather analogue: the joint distribution of (temperature today, temperature tomorrow) must project to the marginal distribution of temperature today; if it does not, the stochastic weather model is causally incoherent and no probability space \((X,\mathcal S,\mu)\) realizes the sequence.

Layer 5 — Almost-everywhere qualification (observational/null): Veto if one treats \(p(E,y)\) as pointwise exact for all \(y\) rather than for \(\mu T^{-1}\)-a.e. \(y\). On [PAGE 1], the a.e. qualification is explicit; altering the kernel on a null set changes nothing integrally but destroys pointwise claims. This is the null-observation veto: instruments with null-set resolution cannot distinguish versions.

Three verifiable counterfactual veto scenarios with checkable conditions and predictions, using only the operators of [PAGE 1]:

Scenario 1 — Non-measurable transformation veto.
Condition (verifiable): Let \(X=Y=[0,1]\) with Borel \(\sigma\)-algebras and \(\mu\) Lebesgue measure. Define \(T(x)=x\) if \(x\in A\) and \(T(x)=1-x\) if \(x\notin A\), where \(A\subset[0,1]\) is a non-measurable set (Vitali-type). Then \(T^{-1}(B)\notin\mathcal S\) for some Borel \(B\).
Prediction: \(\mu T^{-1}\) is not a measure on \([0,1]\); the conditional kernel \(p(E,y)\) cannot satisfy the disintegration equation on [PAGE 1]; equation (6) is undefined because the outer integral over \(Y\) with respect to \(\mu T^{-1}\) does not exist. The veto is triggered at the measurability layer, before any integral identity is evaluated.

Scenario 2 — Integrable factors, non-integrable composition veto.
Condition (verifiable): \(X=Y=(0,1)\), \(\mu=\) Lebesgue, \(T(x)=x\), \(f(x)=x^{-1/2}\), \(g(y)=y^{-1/2}\). Direct integration gives \(\int_0^1 f\,d\mu=2<\infty\) and \(\int_0^1 g\,d(\mu T^{-1})=2<\infty\), but \(h(x)=x^{-1}\) satisfies \(\int_0^1 h\,d\mu=\infty\), so \(h\notin L^1(\mu)\).
Prediction: The premise of equation (7) on [PAGE 1] fails. The left-hand side \(\int_X f(x)g(T(x))\,d\mu(x)\) diverges; the identity is not an equality of finite quantities. The veto is triggered at the integrability layer even though both factors are individually \(L^1\).

Scenario 3 — Inconsistent finite-dimensional distributions veto.
Condition (verifiable): \(X_1=X_2=\{0,1\}\). Let \(\mu_1\) be Bernoulli(1/2) on \(X_1\). Let \(\mu_2\) on \(X_1\times X_2\) be the point mass at \((0,0)\). The first-coordinate marginal of \(\mu_2\) is \(\delta_0\neq\mu_1\).
Prediction: No probability space \((X,\mathcal S,\mu)\) and measurable \(T_2:X\to X_1\times X_2\) can satisfy \(\mu T_2^{-1}=\mu_2\) while simultaneously having the first coordinate distributed as \(\mu_1\). The Kolmogorov consistency condition fails at \(n=2\); the dependent extension theorem of §49 [PAGE 1] cannot be invoked, and the construction halts. The veto is triggered at the consistency layer.
