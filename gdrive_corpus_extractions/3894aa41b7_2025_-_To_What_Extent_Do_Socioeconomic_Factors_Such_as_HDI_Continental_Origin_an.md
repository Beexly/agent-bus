The paper’s operators are mean medal count, population standard deviation, coefficient of variation, Theil entropy index, Lorenz curve, Pearson correlation, and one-sample t-test. The HDI scale is presented as a 0–1000-style “HDI points” scale in several places, e.g. “above 800” [PAGE 9] and “Low (Below 0.55)”-style tier language [PAGE 11]; the paper’s own tier wording is not fully consistent because page 10 also says “Low constituting all scores below 500” [PAGE 10]. A clean formulation is to define \(h_i\in[0,1]\) as the UNDP HDI and \(H_i=1000h_i\in[0,1000]\) as the paper’s HDI-point convention.

For \(N\) countries with medal counts \(m_i\ge 0\):
\[
\bar m=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_i,\qquad [\bar m]=\text{medals per country}.
\]
The paper reports \(\bar m=19.69\) medals per country [PAGE 14]. This is consistent with \(1634/83=19.69\) if \(N=83\) [PAGE 4, PAGE 9].

Population standard deviation:
\[
\sigma_m=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(m_i-\bar m)^2},\qquad [\sigma_m]=\text{medals}.
\]
The source presents “Formula for standard deviation” [PAGE 7] but the extracted rendering is ambiguous and appears to omit or obscure the square root; Table 6 reports SD=5.764 for medals [PAGE 14]. As shown above, that value is numerically incompatible with China’s 220 medals and N=83.

Coefficient of variation:
\[
\mathrm{CV}_m=\frac{\sigma_m}{\bar m},\qquad [\mathrm{CV}_m]=1.
\]
The paper reports “medals have a CV (coefficient of variation) score of 1.698” [PAGE 27]. With \(\bar m=19.69\), this implies \(\sigma_m=33.44\) medals, not 5.764; the paper’s SD/CV pair is internally inconsistent.

Theil index:
\[
T=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{m_i}{\bar m}\ln\!\left(\frac{m_i}{\bar m}\right),\qquad [T]=1,
\]
with the convention \(0\ln 0=0\). The source states “Formula for Theil Index” [PAGE 7] and reports \(T=0.8469\) for the dataset [PAGE 12]. Breakdown regimes: \(T\) is undefined if \(\bar m=0\); negative medal counts are inadmissible; log base must be stated because base-10 and natural-log values differ by a constant factor; and \(T\) is not bounded by 1. For one country holding all medals, \(T=\ln N\). Thus the claim that values above 1 are “extremely rare” [PAGE 12] is not a mathematical invariant.

Lorenz curve:
\[
L(p)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor pN\rfloor}m_{(i)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{(i)}},\qquad p\in[0,1],
\]
where \(m_{(i)}\) are ordered by HDI, is dimensionless. The paper says the curve “strays away from the 45 degree line” [PAGE 8] and that this indicates concentration [PAGE 18]. Breakdown: Lorenz requires nonnegative quantities and a defined ordering; changing the ordering variable changes the visual interpretation.

Pearson correlation:
\[
r_{XY}=\frac{\sum_i (X_i-\bar X)(Y_i-\bar Y)}
{\sqrt{\sum_i (X_i-\bar X)^2\sum_i (Y_i-\bar Y)^2}},\qquad [r_{XY}]=1.
\]
The paper reports \(r=0.914\), \(r=0.585\), \(r=0.096\), \(r=0.145\), and \(r=0.612\) [PAGE 15-16]. Breakdown: \(r\) is undefined if either variable has zero variance; it assumes a linear relationship and independent observations; and it is not valid for arbitrary nominal labels. The coding “1 - Africa ... 6 - Europe” [PAGE 6] makes “continent” nominal, so the reported continent correlations are not invariant to relabeling.

One-sample t-test:
\[
t=\frac{\bar m_{\text{low}}-\mu_0}{s_{\text{low}}/\sqrt{n_{\text{low}}}},\qquad [t]=1,\qquad df=n_{\text{low}}-1.
\]
The source reports \(t=-9.804\), \(df=2\), and \(p=0.012\) for low-HDI medal mean versus the dataset mean [PAGE 17]. Breakdown: \(n_{\text{low}}=3\) makes the test highly sensitive to normality and outliers; \(s_{\text{low}}=0\) makes the statistic undefined; and the p-value does not establish a causal HDI effect. Exact source wording: “The extremely negative T-value of -9.804 with 2 degrees of freedom” [PAGE 17]. The paper also states “The total Theil value for the data set is 0.8469” [PAGE 12] and “The most immediate outlying value is that of Quintile 2 with a value greater than one” [PAGE 12].
