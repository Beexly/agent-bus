<RETROCAUSAL_GENEALOGY>
The intellectual genealogy runs from Weber's 1834 just-noticeable-difference principle and Fechner's 1860 psychophysics into Hull's 1943 drive-reduction system. Hull treats the delay of reinforcement as an intensity-like dimension on which the organism performs a Weberian discrimination: the just-noticeable difference in delay scales with the absolute magnitude of the delay. The cited Anderson study and Grice's alley experiments provide the empirical bridge between spatial extent and temporal delay. In the modern lineage, this anticipates intertemporal-choice formalisms in which delay discounting depends on the ratio of delays rather than their arithmetic difference.

The multi-week trap is longitudinal: if an experimenter holds ΔD constant at 30 seconds but shifts the pair from (30 s, 60 s) to (60 s, 90 s) over successive weeks, the subject's preference for the shorter act will decline from roughly 81.2 percent toward 71.0 percent purely because the Weber fraction halves from 1.0 to 0.5. A naive analyst may attribute this decline to satiation, fatigue, or "loss of the habit," when it is actually the predicted consequence of ratio coarsening. Running such a protocol across weeks creates a retrospective illusion that the subject has regressed, when the schedule itself has moved the discrimination into a coarser-ratio regime. The historical lesson is that any multi-week manipulation of absolute delay must control the Weber fraction, not merely the absolute difference, or the observed preference trajectory will be misattributed to changes in motivation rather than to the geometry of the delay ratio.

<RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: under prolonged training, food restriction, or aversive conditions, the organism may regress from ratio coding (the Weber fraction) to absolute-difference coding. If the contingency switches from fine-ratio pairs to coarse-ratio pairs mid-crisis, a regressed subject will over-respond to absolute ΔD and show preference reversals that violate Corollary VII.

Counter-trap: the experimenter expects a linear effect of absolute delay difference and therefore interprets the drop from 81.2 percent to 71.0 percent as a failure of acquisition rather than a Weber-compliant shift. Treating the subject as "impaired" when it is actually encoding ΔD / D_s is the counter-trap: the external model misattributes ratio-consistent behavior to an internal deficit.

Clock layer: the biological timing clock exhibits scalar variability, σ_t ∝ D, the same Weber property. Discrimination succeeds only when ΔD exceeds the clock's standard deviation; when ΔD < k·D_s, the two delay representations overlap and choice approaches indifference. This creates a recursive loop: the subject's internal model of the reinforcement schedule sets the clock's gain; clock noise determines choice; choice determines reinforcement history; history updates the internal model. Across multiple sessions, the subject can entrain to the experimenter's delay pairs, making the apparent preference trajectory a function of both the external schedule and the subject's recursively updated temporal model. The clock is therefore both a measurement instrument and a participant in the contingency, and its noise floor defines the exact regime where Corollary VII ceases to predict behavior.

Physical layer veto: The alley-length data confound physical distance with post-choice delay. A 276-inch alley requires more running time than a 210-inch alley even if reinforcement is delivered immediately at the goal box; thus preference for the shorter path may reflect transit-time minimization rather than delay-of-reinforcement discounting. Unless running speed is held constant and arrival times are equated, the spatial comparison cannot be admitted as evidence for the temporal corollary.

Biomechanical layer: stride frequency, limb loading, and metabolic cost scale with distance. A 66-inch difference imposes a nonzero effort differential that a 23-inch difference does not; effort discounting is a separate dimension from temporal discounting and can veto the alley-to-delay analogy unless equated.

Weather layer: The 1943 laboratory setting is environmentally controlled, so ambient temperature and humidity do not enter the reported data. However, any generalization to field conditions must veto the temporal inference unless temperature is controlled, because thermal load alters running speed and therefore effective delay.

Adjudication: Accept Corollary VII for pure temporal delays (where reinforcement is deferred after the choice act). Reject the spatial alley inference for temporal delay unless transit times are equated. The textual move from Grice's alleys to Hull's delay corollary is therefore an analogy, not a deduction, and it fails the cross-layer veto unless an explicit equating assumption is added. The temporal data (81.2% vs 71.0%; 76% vs 66%) survive the veto because they manipulate delay directly rather than path length.
