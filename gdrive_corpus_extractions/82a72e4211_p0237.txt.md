**A Priori Probability (Type 1):**  
Defined via symmetry groups \(G\) acting transitively on \(\Omega\). For finite sets, \(P(A) = \frac{|A|}{|\Omega|}\) under uniform measure. Assumption: perfect homogeneity such that all elementary outcomes are interchangeable. Breakdown regime: when no natural group action exists or when underlying symmetries are broken by external constraints.

**Statistical Probability (Type 2):**  
Derived from relative frequencies in long-run sequences. Formally, \(P(A) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{N_n(A)}{n}\), where \(N_n(A)\) counts occurrences of event \(A\) in first \(n\) trials. Requires ergodicity assumption—that time averages equal ensemble averages. Units: dimensionless ratio, but grounded in empirical observation. Breakdown regime: non-stationary environments, small samples, or when structural breaks invalidate past-future linkage.

**Estimative/Subjective Probability (Type 3 — Knight’s innovation):**  
No closed-form expression. Represented as a belief function \(b: 2^\Omega \rightarrow [0,1]\) satisfying certain coherence axioms (e.g., Dempster-Shafer theory) or simply as a set-valued mapping reflecting ambiguity aversion. May incorporate confidence levels or plausibility measures instead of precise probabilities. No standard units—qualitative ordering suffices. Breakdown regime: when beliefs become internally inconsistent or fail to update rationally with new evidence.

Knight’s key operator is the **Classification Homogeneity Test**: Given two instances \(x_i, x_j\), define similarity metric \(d(x_i,x_j)\). But \(\epsilon\) itself becomes a source of judgment—hence the third type emerges precisely when choosing \(\epsilon\) is problematic.

Formally, let \(C: X \rightarrow K\) be a classifier into classes \(K\). Knight argues that only when \(C\) yields truly homogeneous bins can we apply Types 1 or 2 safely. - G.L.S. Shackle’s theory of imagination in economic choice
- Herbert Simon’s bounded rationality and satisficing behavior
- Modern behavioral finance critiques of efficient market hypothesis

Crucially, Knight anticipated the limitations of neoclassical equilibrium models by showing that much of economic life operates outside calculable risk zones. His genealogy thus spans from classical philosophy of probability to contemporary complexity economics and resilience engineering.
