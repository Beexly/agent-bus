<SOURCE_OPERATORS_AND_EQUATIONS>
To rigorously formalize Knight's qualitative descriptions of risk, moral hazard, and organizational scaling, we introduce a mathematical framework mapping economic organization to information-theoretic and probabilistic systems.

**1. The Moral Hazard Risk Operator ($\mathcal{M}$)**
Let $A$ denote the principal (the enterprise/corporation) and $B$ denote the agent (the individual enterpriser/employee/sub-model). Let $\theta \in \Theta$ represent the state of the world (uncertainty), and $d \in D$ represent the decision space. The consequence $C(d, \theta)$ is a random variable. 
Moral hazard arises because $B$ chooses $d$ based on their utility $U_B(d, \theta)$, while $A$ bears the consequence based on $U_A(C(d, \theta))$. We define the Moral Hazard Risk as the expected divergence between the optimal decision for $A$ and the actual decision made by $B$:
$$
\mathcal{M}(A, B) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta} \left[ \left| U_A \left( C \left( \arg\max_{d} U_B(d, \theta), \theta \right) \right) - \max_{d} U_A(C(d, \theta)) \right| \right]
$$
*Units:* Dimensionless expected utility loss (or expressed in monetary SI units like Joules of wasted energy/compute). 
*Breakdown Regime:* $\mathcal{M}$ approaches singularity (systemic collapse) when the gradient of $U_B$ with respect to $d$ points entirely opposite to the gradient of $U_A$, particularly in high-dimensional $D$ (e.g., complex strategic environments).

**2. Organizational Scaling Limit ($S_{max}$)**
Knight states that the "special 'risk'... limits the scope of operations." Let $V$ be the total capital/resources mobilized. The effective capacity of a business unit form $F$ (partnership vs. corporation) is constrained by its ability to manage $\mathcal{M}$.
Let $K(F)$ be the coordination constant (related to communication overhead and legal rigidity, see [PAGE 253]: "inefficiency of organization").
The maximum viable scope $S_{max}$ is given by:
$$
S_{max}(F) = \frac{V}{K(F) \cdot \mathcal{M}_{threshold}}
$$
where $\mathcal{M}_{threshold}$ is the maximum tolerable moral hazard before the partnership dissolves or fails. 
*Citation:* This formalizes [PAGE 253]: "It is the special 'risk' to which large amounts of capital... are subject which limits the scope of operations..."

**3. The Information Bottleneck of Unification**
The "effective unification of interests" can be modeled using Variational Information Bottlenecks. If the enterprise has a global objective function $O$, and the agents possess local views $X$, the degree of unification $U$ is the mutual information preserved about $O$ through the organizational channels $Y$:
$$
U = I(X; O | Y)
$$
Knight argues this task is "impossible, in any absolute sense" [PAGE 253]. Mathematically, unless the channel $Y$ (organizational rules, contracts, oversight) has infinite bandwidth, $I(X; O | Y)$ will always be bounded away from $H(O)$ (the total entropy of the objective), leaving a residual, irreducible Knightian uncertainty.

*Assumptions:* Agents act rationally within their bounded rationality; the environment follows stochastic processes where probabilities are not fully known (Knightian Uncertainty).
