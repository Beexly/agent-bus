Conversely, reports made within the first few hundred ms capture the peak contextual modulation. The "clock" thus defines an observation window: θ'(t) = θ_i + e^(−t/τ_adapt) · [context term], meaning the magnitude of the illusion is a decaying function of time since stimulus onset, not a fixed property of the stimulus.
</RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical veto: James explicitly isolates the retinal image as invariant—"yet their retinal images are the same they always were" [PAGE 1]. Any physical theory that locates the Zöllner distortion or the ellipse formation in changes to the retinal image is vetoed. The distortion must arise downstream of the retina, in cortical or interpretive stages. This is a hard physical constraint: identical proximal stimulus, different percept.

Biomechanical veto: Fixational eye movements, microsaccades, and drift continuously shift the after-image across the retinal mosaic. Unless fixation is stabilized (for example, with a retinal image stabilizer), the apparent projection of an after-image onto a wall is confounded by retinal smear. James reports no fixation control [PAGE 1]; therefore the reported divergences and ellipses could partially reflect biomechanical instability rather than pure projective geometry. Any model must either demonstrate stabilization or include a motion-blur term in the projection operator P(t).

Weather veto: The task is indoor and under the observer's control, but if performed with natural illumination through a window, atmospheric scintillation, refractive index gradients, or dirty glass could introduce optical distortions that mimic ellipse formation or line divergence. Controlled photometric conditions—uniform luminance L in cd/m², collimated or diffuse illumination without thermal gradients—are required to veto optical weather confounds. Without such controls, James's observations remain phenomenological anecdotes rather than physically verified measurements.

Veto proof: A valid causal account must pass all three layers simultaneously. Failure of the physical veto (retinal change) falsifies proximal accounts; failure of the biomechanical veto (unstable fixation) falsifies pure projective accounts; failure of the weather veto (uncontrolled optics) falsifies laboratory generality. Only a post-retinal, cortical, context-sensitive model operating under stabilized fixation and controlled illumination survives.
</CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
