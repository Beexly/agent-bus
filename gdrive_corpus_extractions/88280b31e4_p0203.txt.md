Hull’s innovation was to make the system deductive: postulates, intervening variables, and derived empirical consequences. <RECURSIVE_THEORY_OF_MIND>
Crisis habit regression: when the organism encounters an unpredictable stimulus flux—precisely the condition Hull describes where conditioned stimuli are never exactly repeated [PAGE 194]—it regresses to the most available habit system. For humans, that is verbal mediation: the subject begins narrating, categorizing, and applying logical similarity rules (e.g., “90 is forty times more different from 10 than 12 is”) [PAGE 194, footnote]. This regression is cognitively economical but epistemically corrupt: the verbal report reflects the secondary system, not the primary generalization gradient that actually governs naïve behavior.

Counter-trap: the experimenter, facing the same unpredictability, regresses to common-sense intuition and treats introspective reports as data. Hull’s warning is that this is a trap because verbal reports are “open to a certain amount of doubt” unless anchored to objective continua and verified against naïve gradients [PAGE 194]. The recursive layer is that both subject and experimenter are using the same secondary, verbal machinery; the experimenter’s “objective” interpretation is itself mediated by linguistic categories (weapon, number, value) that do not map onto receptor space.

Clock: There are two timescales in conflict. The fast clock is trial-by-trial superthreshold reaction potential \(\Pi(t)>\theta\), which must adapt within milliseconds to seconds as \(s_t\) drifts [PAGE 194; p. 326 ff.]. The slow clock is the accrual of secondary generalization over sessions, as verbal habits consolidate and \(H\) accumulates fortuitous episodes [PAGE 194]. The paradox arises when the experimenter samples behavior on the fast clock but interprets it with slow-clock verbal categories. A subject may show a smooth primary gradient on trial 10, then report a discontinuous “logical” boundary on trial 1000 after speech mediation has reorganized the representational space. The crisis habit regression is fast; the counter-trap correction requires resetting the clock to naïve, pre-verbal baselines—which, once contaminated by speech, cannot be truly recovered within the same organism.

<CROSS_LAYER_VETO_ADJUDICATION>
Physical layer: receptor transduction imposes a just-noticeable difference (JND) governed by Weber’s law, \(\Delta I/I = k\), with \(k\) dimensionless and measured in SI stimulus units (e.g., Michelson contrast, sound pressure level in dB). If \(d_p(s,s_0) < \text{JND}\), no primary generalization gradient can resolve the pair; any claimed similarity difference at this scale is physically unrealizable.

Biomechanical layer: speech mediation requires articulatory and auditory-phonetic resolution. The channel \(V:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\) is bounded by phoneme discrimination thresholds (roughly 10–20 ms voice-onset-time, categorical perception boundaries) and by working-memory limits on label retention. If two stimuli map to the same phonemic category or exceed the subject’s articulatory loop capacity, the verbal mediator collapses them regardless of their primary receptor distance.

Weather/environmental layer: “Weather” here denotes ambient, non-controlled stimulus flux—background luminance drift, acoustic noise, temperature-induced receptor sensitivity shifts. Hull’s infinite-variety flux [PAGE 194] implies \(\|ds/dt\|>0\) continuously. If environmental drift exceeds the adaptation rate of the superthreshold reaction potential \(\Pi(t)\), the system never reaches threshold \(\theta\) stably, and learning becomes an online tracking problem rather than bond formation.

Veto proof (composite): The secondary-generalization claim is vetoed if any of the following holds:
\[
\text{Veto} \iff \bigl[d_p(s,s_0) < \text{JND}\bigr] \;\lor\; \bigl[C_V < \log_2|\mathcal{S}|\bigr] \;\lor\; \bigl[\|ds/dt\| > \dot{\Pi}_{max}\bigr].

Clause 1 removes physical discriminability; clause 2 removes sufficient symbolic bandwidth; clause 3 removes temporal stability of the adaptive potential. When vetoed, the only admissible explanation is non-mediated primary generalization or random response, and verbal reports must be excluded as confabulated secondary similarities [PAGE 194].
