# QUALITY DOCTRINE — the excellence bar (Garrett, 2026-09-11)

Applies to every task on this bus. This is what "9.2" means.

## The rule
No Walmart quality. Nothing cheap, nothing template-slop, nothing "good enough."
We dominate with intelligence and quality. Every public artifact must answer YES to:
**"Would I proudly charge money for this?"**

## What cheap looks like (banned)
- Emoji as iconography (🌱 ✂️ 📞 as design elements)
- Generic card grids that could belong to anyone
- Lorem ipsum, filler copy, "Your Text Here" energy
- System-font-only designs with no typographic point of view
- Stock-looking layouts: hero → 3 cards → footer, no concept
- Workflows that are a Zapier template with our name on it

## What excellent looks like (required)
- **Typography carries the design.** Distinctive display type, deliberate pairing,
  real hierarchy. Type is the cheapest luxury there is — use it.
- **One concept per piece.** Every site/design/video has a visual idea, not just
  a layout. Lawn care should *feel* like outdoors. A workflow should *feel* inevitable.
- **Copy sounds like a human who cares.** No filler words, no corporate fog.
  Every sentence earns its place. Short is fine — *cheap* is not.
- **Craft in the details.** Spacing rhythm, hover states, loading states, error
  states, mobile. The details are where "handmade" is proven or disproven.
- **Range with a bar.** Portfolio pieces should feel like different designers made
  them for different trades — but all at the same quality bar.

## Simple vs cheap
Simple is allowed. Cheap is not. "Less but better" — fewer elements, each one
excellent. Confusion is not sophistication; decoration is not quality.

## Workflows specifically
A $500+ workflow is a *system*, not a template. It must include: the actual
automation built and tested, a plain-English runbook the owner can follow,
failure handling (what happens when X breaks), and a demo the buyer can see
before paying. If it looks like something anyone could download free, it doesn't ship.

## Websites specifically
A $350 site must look like $350 of craft. Mobile-first, fast, real local voice
in the copy, a sticky call path, and a design concept — not a theme.
