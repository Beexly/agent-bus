Abstract
In intermediated retail channels, estimating consumer price elasticity from wholesale list prices is
confounded by retail pass-through, promotions, competitor movements, and local market frictions.
Rather than focusing solely on point estimation, this paper introduces a unified causal governance
framework under which decision abstention (wait) is treated as a primary, diagnostic output rather than
an estimation failure. Grounded in the companion theory of design-based uncertainty (Delgado, 2026 ),
we show that conventional within-panel bootstrap intervals suffer severe empirical subcoverage on this
panel and that a hierarchical variance-component construction using Paule-Mandel estimation closes most
of the gap; that construction is reported as validated rather than adopted, and every figure in this paper
is produced under the bootstrap the implementation currently runs.
We present an act/wait decision system that combines Double Machine Learning, cost-shock
instrumental contrasts, and conformal prediction to decouple weekly panel estimation from monthly
decisions. The system evaluates recommendations through an admissibility gate and eight guards, jointly
partitioning ten abstention causes, with the structural-break test (Guard 5) operating as a passive temporal
drift sensor rather than an active veto, and the asymmetric Dual Beta estimator retired due to physical
noise floor limits. The decision layer partitions all exit reasons exhaustively into three mutually exclusive
verdict types under a fixed precedence: wait-contamination (GCONT ), wait-transient (GTRANS ), and
wait-identification (GID ). The third is the operationally consequential one, as it marks a product
presentation as a candidate for an actively designed pricing experiment rather than a terminal rejection.
This paper makes three main contributions: (1) an operational framework converting selective
prediction into an active pricing diagnostic, leaving no presentation in an unclassified terminal state; (2)
transforming seven tacit econometric assumptions into active system diagnostics; and (3) showing that
aggregating unidentifiable presentation-level items to brand or category levels restores usable elasticity
estimates (reducing RMSE from 0.571 to 0.159 against a true magnitude of 1.1).
The system is tested against synthetic data-generating processes with known ground truth under
thirty pre-registered engineering rules (11 pass, 18 fail, 1 not applicable); it has not been run against a
commercial panel, and no result reported here is an observation of a real product category. Informative
failures show that component discipline matters more than algorithmic complexity. On identifiable panels,
the family-wise false-veto rate of the whole harness is estimated at 0.053, one veto among nineteen
independently seeded replications. Given the thin data typical of retail channels, reporting when evidence
supports—or fails to support—a decision offers a practical and secure alternative to uncritical estimation.
Keywords: Price optimization; causal inference; double machine learning; decision under abstention;
demand systems; intermediated retail channels.

Introduction

A manufacturer selling through decentralized retail sets the list price and does not set the price the consumer
pays. The shopkeeper does, subject to local margin targets, menu costs and rigid psychological price points
[Levy et al., 2011]. Any elasticity estimated from list-price variation in this channel is therefore a composite:
the consumer’s response to the shelf price convolved with the shopkeeper’s pass-through of the list price
to the shelf [Besanko et al., 2005]. The operational problem is nonetheless posed at the list price, since
that is the only lever the manufacturer holds: set monthly list prices, bounded by ±20%, so as to maximize
contribution without sacrificing volume or share.
Naive regressions of volume on historical prices fail here because they confound the causal slope with
promotions, competitor moves, seasonality and nominal inflationary drift [Villas-Boas and Winer, 1999].
Since an optimizer is a causal intervention machine [Pearl, 2009], the binding problem in this channel is
not optimization but the isolation of the slope the optimizer consumes. The revenue-management literature
and the enterprise deployments it documents [Talluri and van Ryzin, 2006, Hormby et al., 2010, Deng et al.,
2023, Llenas et al., 2026] largely take the observational price–demand relation as sufficient for prescription.
Decentralized Retail violates that premise through three structural conditions: scarce list-price variation
confounded with promotion, indirect and heterogeneous pass-through, and shelf prices captured by audit
rather than by transaction. Section 2 situates that gap against six bodies of literature this work draws on.
From estimation to decision. Most of that literature, and most practice built on it, treats the object of
interest as a number: an elasticity to be estimated as precisely as possible and handed to an optimizer. This
paper treats it instead as an input to a decision that is not always safe to make. Three questions organize
what follows, and they replace “what is the elasticity” as the paper’s central question.
1. When can a price change be executed on the estimate, and when should the system
withhold a recommendation instead? This is the ACT/WAIT question. Treating WAIT as a
first-class, reportable output, rather than as evidence of a system that failed to produce a number, is
the paper’s first contribution (Section 4.4); Section 8.3 shows that a WAIT verdict is also diagnostic of
the remedy it calls for, not only a report that one is needed.
2. What conditions, on the data and on the estimator, have to be made explicit and checked
before an ACT verdict is trusted? Answering this converts assumptions such systems ordinarily
leave tacit into diagnostics the system executes rather than presumes. That conversion, together with a
declared account of which diagnostics adapt an existing econometric tool and which are original to this
setting, is the paper’s second contribution (Section 8.1).
3. At what level of commercial aggregation, presentation, brand or category, is a decision
legitimate when the evidence does not support it at the level the business would prefer to
act on? Showing when and how far the unit of decision should move is the paper’s third contribution
(Section 6.4).
This paper reports a system built to answer those three questions in one specific setting and, more consequentially, the validation run that measured whether it does. The system decouples weekly panel estimation
from a monthly decision cycle and chains Double Machine Learning [Chernozhukov et al., 2018], a cost-shock
instrumental contrast, hierarchical pooling, conformal predictive intervals, and a decision layer that abstains
when identification is unviable. It is validated against two synthetic generators with known ground truth
under thirty engineering-verification rules registered before the run. These thirty rules are a quality-assurance
device internal to the validation run, each pairing a claim the manuscript makes with a threshold that would
settle it (Section 5); they are not the paper’s scientific hypotheses, which are the three contributions listed
above. Eleven of the thirty rules pass, eighteen fail, and one does not apply, and several of the failures are
informative findings in their own right rather than simple defects.
Two further results support the third contribution and the abstention rule together. Aggregating the estimate
from the presentation to the brand or the category level substantially improves its reliability, because designspecific bias among independently priced presentations partly averages out: a category-level estimate reaches
a root mean squared error of 0.159 against a true elasticity of magnitude 1.1, where a presentation-level one
2

reaches 0.571 (Section 6.4). And on a panel this thin, a resampled interval computed within one realized
design cannot always be certified as honest, because it cannot see the dispersion that would arise from having
observed a different set of price movements altogether; that limitation, not a deficiency of estimation, is why
some presentations, at some levels of aggregation, are correctly answered with WAIT rather than with a
number (Section 6.5). This paper motivates that limitation only to the extent needed to justify the abstention
rule; its full econometric treatment, the decomposition of the shortfall, how it scales with aggregation, and
the case for closing it experimentally, is developed in companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026] rather
than here.
That evidentiary shape is not peculiar to this system or this sponsor. A hundred and twenty weekly periods, a
handful of presentations, list prices that move a few times a year and a shelf price captured by audit describe
the evidence available to most manufacturers in this channel. Reporting when such evidence does, and does
not, support a decision is more useful than reporting a system that always appears to clear it.
The methodological claim the paper defends is broader than pricing. The failure modes that survive a
competent build are rarely errors of estimation; they are unstated assumptions about the environment, and
each can be converted into a quantity that costs less to measure than its consequence costs to absorb. Four
such assumptions were found by auditing the design and three more by executing the validation. Table 4
states all seven with the contrast each became and, for each, whether the test adapts an existing tool or is
original to this work.
The remainder is organized as follows. Section 2 situates the three contributions against the literature and
states the gap. Section 3 describes the institutional setting and the panel. Section 4 gives a global view of
the system that answers the first two questions above, with formal specifications deferred to Appendix A and
the extended exposition to Appendix B. Section 5 states the validation design. Section 6 reports what the
run measured, with extended contrasts in Appendix C. Section 7 reports robustness checks and ablations.
Section 8 draws the governance and deployment consequences, and Section 9 closes.

Related Work and the Gap

This work sits at the intersection of six literatures, and states plainly, at the outset, what none of them by
itself supplies.
Structural demand estimation in industrial organization [Berry, 1994, Berry et al., 1995, Nevo, 2001]
identifies substitution patterns and market power from aggregate market shares under strong assumptions
about functional form and instrument availability; it targets the estimate itself and is agnostic about when a
firm should act on it. Vertical pass-through [Villas-Boas, 2007, Nakamura and Zerom, 2010] documents
that retailers rarely transmit a wholesale price change to the shelf one-for-one, which motivates this paper’s
treatment of pass-through as a process rather than a constant (Section 4.3), but does not address how
a manufacturer without transaction-level shelf data should decide whether to move a price at all. Nonexchangeable conformal inference [Gibbs and Candès, 2021, Barber et al., 2023] supplies the machinery
this paper uses to bound the volume response under a chronological, non-stationary split (Section 4.2); it is
a tool the framework adopts, not a decision rule in itself. Selective prediction and learning-to-defer
[El-Yaniv and Wiener, 2010, Mozannar and Sontag, 2020] formalize the general principle that a system can
decline to answer rather than answer badly, and the ACT/WAIT gate is an instance of that principle applied
to a pricing decision; the contribution here is the instantiation, the declared diagnostics that trigger abstention
in this setting, not the general principle. Multiway panel clustering [Chiang et al., 2022] and the broader
inference literature it belongs to inform the resampling and clustering choices audited in Section 4.1. And
performative prediction [Perdomo et al., 2020], alongside the general critique that a relation estimated
under one policy regime need not survive a new one [Lucas, 1976, MacKenzie, 2006], names the risk that
once this system moves prices, the observational identification it relies on will erode; Section B.12 treats that
risk as a governance problem this paper flags rather than solves.
A seventh, more practically oriented literature sits alongside these six and is worth separating from them:
enterprise deployments of pricing and revenue-management systems [Talluri and van Ryzin, 2006, Marn and
Rosiello, 1992, Hormby et al., 2010, Deng et al., 2023, Llenas et al., 2026]. These report what a deployed
3

system achieved, typically a revenue or margin gain, and are the closest thing to a track record this literature
has for whether firms use such systems at all and what they get from them. They are also, for the purpose
of this paper, the sharpest illustration of the gap: none of them reports which of the system’s identifying
assumptions were checked, whether the reported gain would survive an audit of confounding, or at what
level of aggregation the underlying estimates were trustworthy. The comparison is scoped to the three cited
here and is not offered as a survey finding, but it is a repeated pattern and not a coincidence: a literature
organized around outcomes achieved has little occasion to report on evidence that was insufficient, because a
system that only ever reports what it recommended has no channel through which to report having declined
to recommend anything. The adjacent literature on algorithm aversion [Dietvorst et al., 2015, 2018] suggests
why that omission is costly rather than harmless: trust in an automated recommendation is fragile once the
system has been seen to err, and is rebuilt by giving the user visible, legible control over an imperfect system,
of which a declared and costed abstention is the clearest form.
The gap. None of the seven literatures above treats the decision to abstain as a first-class object with its
own diagnostics, its own measured cost, and its own conditions for reversal; the closest, selective prediction,
formalizes the principle in a general classification setting and does not address what the relevant diagnostics
are in an observational pricing panel with retail intermediation. None specifies the conditions under which a
decision illegitimate at one level of commercial aggregation becomes legitimate at a coarser one, or measures
how far aggregation actually closes that gap. And none converts the assumptions a causal pricing system
ordinarily leaves tacit, that controls are pre-treatment, that an instrument acts through one channel, that a
comparison group is inert, that no adjacent optimizer touches the same unit, into diagnostics the system
itself executes and reports. Sections 4.4, 8.1 and 6.4 address the three gaps in that order, each corresponding
to one of the questions posed in Section 1.

Institutional Setting and Data

The institutional detail behind the identification problem stated in Section 1 is what makes this channel
harder than the modern-trade setting most of the causal-pricing and revenue-management literature addresses:
the manufacturer’s price is not the consumer’s price, the intermediary between them is a liquidity-constrained
small business rather than a chain with scanner data, and the panel available to measure any of it is short by
the standards of that literature. This section describes the panel this paper’s system runs on, before Section 4
describes what the system does with it.
The valuation dataset is a weekly panel at the presentation × region × week level, integrating sell-in, marketaudit sell-out, distributor point-of-sale, competitor pricing, promotional schedules and variable costs, with
weather and holiday covariates. Gap imputation and publication rules belong to an upstream data contract:
gap imputation by forward fill, capped at two weeks and carrying an indicator flag, together with non-null
constraints, plausible price–volume bounds, price–cost coherence and weekly partitioning. The system neither
generates nor repairs missing imputations, and validates only a diagnostic alert for week-over-week volume
drops above 80% within a cell, as a proxy for unmarked stockouts; that alert is specified here and executable
exclusively against a production panel, so it is not exercised by any result in this paper. The boundary
is methodologically load-bearing rather than administrative: if imputation flags concentrate at month-end
among smaller retailers, forward fill is masking order refusals rather than random missingness, which is
evidence for the extensive-margin mechanism of Appendix B.8 rather than a capture defect.
The atomic unit is the presentation (brand × format family × pack size), not the SKU: list prices are
assigned per presentation, so intra-presentation SKU variation carries no identifying price variance. The
brand grounds the pooling hierarchy, the format family supplies the instrumental contrasts, and the pack
size governs consumption occasions and price stickiness. The framework organizes around the structural
hierarchy Category > Brand > Presentation rather than around any category domain, and assumes a
portfolio architecture with multiple brands, distinct pack sizes, uninstrumented competitor prices and retail
intermediation. The generators of Section 5 instantiate one portfolio exhibiting that topology and the
system is shown to run end to end on it; transfer to other portfolios sharing the structure remains a design
expectation rather than a verified property. This hierarchy is also what Section 6.4 later aggregates over
when presentation-level evidence is not enough to decide.
4

The week is the temporal unit of inference throughout: cross-fitting folds, block bootstrap, conformal splits,
break tests and recency windows all operate on weekly boundaries, while the month governs only the decision
cadence. That division is an identification decision and not a convenience. Under monthly aggregation
the quota-pressure guard loses its contrast identically, the calendar component of the cash-pressure index
disappears, and the break tests fall from a hundred and twenty observations to twenty-eight. The one genuine
argument for the monthly grain, that it would cancel forward-buying bias within the observation, is already
answered by taking sell-out rather than sell-in as the outcome. Appendix B.2 develops the argument in full.
Feasibility and admissibility. A presentation × region cell is modelable when five joint conditions hold
on deflated prices: a coefficient of variation above 0.03; at least four discrete price changes (|∆ log p| > 0.005);
an absolute price–promotion correlation below 0.6; an absolute price–competitor correlation below 0.9; and
non-frozen shelf status, defined conjunctively as CV(P so ) ≥ 0.01 together with at most 5% zero-volume weeks.
A minimum of forty usable observations applies. A presentation is then admissible when the diagnostic
passes in at least half its regions, the treatment partial R2 after residualization exceeds 0.10, and the bootstrap
interval width for the raw elasticity falls below 0.6. Admissibility is evaluated before the guards, so a failing
presentation reaches WAIT without a guard being evaluated, and it conditions conformal calibration and
QA-Gate scoring. Non-modelable units inherit a brand-level pooled elasticity and are marked non-actionable.
Appendix B.3 states the diagnostic in full, including the reduced-form competitor reaction function r̂ that
makes explicit which estimand the competitive bracket contrasts. These two thresholds, jointly with the
eight guards of Section 4.4 and the ten abstention causes they define, are the diagnostics referred to in the
second question of Section 1: Table 4 and Section 8.1 state which of them adapt an existing tool and which
are original to this setting.

Causal identification

Estimation is the partialling-out estimator with cross-fitting from Double/Debiased Machine Learning
[Chernozhukov et al., 2018] applied to a partially linear model [Robinson, 1988] and grounded in Frisch–
Waugh–Lovell [Frisch and Waugh, 1933, Lovell, 1963]. Gradient-boosted learners [Friedman, 2001] model
the joint dependence of treatment T = log P si and outcome Y = log Qso on a confounder set W carrying
promotional flags and intensity, log competitor prices, temperature, holidays, working days, week-of-year and
log inflation, extensible to distribution, out-of-stock, media and trade investment where the panel supplies
them.
The estimand requires one statement that is often left implicit. The coefficient of the residualized outcome on
the residualized treatment is not the structural consumer elasticity: it is the convolution θ = ε · ρ of consumer
response with shopkeeper pass-through. Separating the two is the job of the pass-through layer, not of a
better nuisance model, and comparing θ̂ against an injected consumer elasticity would report transmission as
estimation bias.
Four properties of the construction carry consequences the run measures. (i) Cross-fitting uses K = 5
contiguous temporal blocks [Bergmeir and Benítez, 2012]; this is not forward-chaining and relies on stationarity
and weak dependence [Chiang et al., 2022] rather than on Neyman orthogonality, which concerns moment
conditions and not information sets. An embargoed variant of that partition is implemented and its coverage
measured, and it is not the one this run uses: adopting it would change the residualizer that two of the
ablations compare, so those contrasts would stop testing what the text says they test. The consequence is
declared rather than absorbed, because it is a leak and not a preference: without the embargo the partition

7

can place a single week on both sides of the split through a different region. (ii) Nuisance learners are shallow
boosted trees, which are piecewise constant and represent a monotone trend as a staircase, so low-frequency
components can survive into the residuals. (iii) Inference is a moving-block bootstrap clustered by week
[Künsch, 1989] with block length L = clip(round(1.5 W 1/3 ), 2, ⌊W/3⌋), giving L = 7 at W = 120. (iv) The
point estimate is the median across folds rather than the pooled DML2 aggregate. Each of the four is
contrasted against its alternative in Section 7, and two of them do not survive as general recommendations:
the choice of nuisance learner reverses between the two generators, and the choice of point estimator reverses
between learners.
Temporal status and the mediator bracket. Every column of W carries a declared status, pre or
post, relative to the pricing decision, and estimation aborts on an undeclared control. Conditioning on
post-treatment realizations blocks causal pathways and changes the estimand [Rosenbaum, 1984, VanderWeele,
2015], so elasticities are reported with and without post-treatment commercial controls and the differential is
published as a diagnostic rather than used to prune automatically. Under the sign structure assumed for this
channel, trade activity rising with list price, the indirect path carries the opposite sign to the direct effect, so
over-conditioning inflates |θ̂|; since the optimizer’s interior optimum carries a markup θ/(1 + θ) falling toward
one as |θ| grows, the failure mode is a systematically conservative recommendation that stays plausible. The
direction depends on the assumed sign and not on the estimator, and is declared as conditional for that
reason.
Two structural contrasts. Residualization addresses observed confounders only, so two contrasts probe
what it cannot. The instrumental contrast uses log variable costs as an instrument for supply-side
shocks, and the divergence between observational and instrumental estimates is an operational Hausman test
[Hausman, 1978]. Category-wide cost shocks move competitor prices too, violating the exclusion restriction
and displacing both estimators together, which understates the contrast [Angrist et al., 1996]; the framework
therefore audits the restriction directly through the residual correlation between the residualized instrument
and the residualized competitor price, with an explicit time index added because a boosted tree partials a
monotone drift poorly. The competitive bracket decomposes price response into three declared estimands,
conditional, historical-equilibrium and cross, and admits the internal identity θtotal − θcond ≈ θcross · r̂. The
identity binds only where the competitor moves and reacts, and Section 6.1 establishes on which panel it is
contrastable before reporting it. Both are specified formally in Appendices A.5 and A.4.

4.2

Regularization, uncertainty and the demand system

The causal layer returns one slope per presentation with an interval, and three problems remain that no
single-unit estimator solves: thin panels borrow strength or return noise, a point estimate is not a bound on
the volume a committee will observe, and an own-price elasticity says nothing about what the rest of the
portfolio does.
Partial pooling. The hierarchy Category > Brand > Presentation shrinks each estimate toward its branch
through a conjugate normal–normal update, with three origins for the prior: a leave-one-out brand mean with
between-presentation heterogeneity τ̂ 2 estimated by Paule–Mandel [Paule and Mandel, 1982]; the previous
cycle, through a local-level dynamic linear model at discount δ = 0.90 [West and Harrison, 1997], whose
information weight halves in roughly seven cycles; and a cold-start branch prior. Two pitfalls are avoided by
construction, using the sample variance in place of τ̂ 2 and summing raw precisions rather than incorporating
τ̂ 2 into branch precisions [Efron and Morris, 1975]. The output is a decision-grade elasticity θdec carrying its
posterior variance, own-data weight and declared origin. Its role is bounded deliberately: the ACT/WAIT
gate votes solely with the raw estimate θ̂, so pooling can never unlock a movement, while θdec sets the level of
the published price and elasticity curves. Appendix A.6 gives the update and two declared approximations.
The heterogeneity component and design variance. The between-presentation heterogeneity τ̂ 2 that
this layer estimates in order to set the shrinkage weight also measures something a within-panel bootstrap
cannot: dispersion that comes from the realized design itself, this set of price movements falling in these

weeks, rather than from sampling noise around it (Section
√ 6.3 sketches why, and treats the question at the
level this paper needs). An interval of the form θ̂ ± 1.96 s2 + τ̂ 2 is one way to fold that source back into
the reported uncertainty; whether, and by how much, it improves on the production bootstrap interval is a
question of its own, developed in companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026] rather than here. The
change is identified and not yet adopted in the production configuration, and every figure in this paper is
produced under the interval the implementation currently runs.
One consequence for this layer is unfavourable and is stated with the rest. Where the raw estimate is
mis-centred, shrinkage can move the interval toward a displaced branch mean while narrowing it, so the
pooling posterior’s coverage is not guaranteed to improve on the bootstrap it shrinks. Partial pooling is not
assumed to help the interval merely because it helps the point estimate, and a system that publishes both
should say so.
The conformal layer. Predictive intervals on log volume are built by Conformalized Quantile Regression
[Romano et al., 2019], Mondrian-stratified by region [Vovk et al., 2003]. Where a stratum is too sparse for
local quantile estimation the estimator falls back to global calibration, which relaxes the guarantee from
group-conditional to marginal coverage, and each fallback is recorded by a unit-level indicator. Because
random splits in a weekly panel mix past and future and produce optimistic bands, the partition is strictly
chronological (50/30/20) and evaluated across rolling origins. Non-exchangeability forfeits the finite-sample
guarantee [Vovk et al., 2005, Lei et al., 2018], so the construction rests on the adaptive conformal argument
for non-stationary environments [Gibbs and Candès, 2021, Barber et al., 2023] and on measured out-of-time
coverage that clears the nominal 0.90 level at the rolling origins tested (Section 6.2). The band over-covers,
which is the conservative direction and costs width rather than validity, and it bounds the volume response
only: price bounds are enforced independently by the competitive corridor, and intersecting the two would
mix an interval on a response with a bound on a decision variable.
Cross-elasticities. Portfolio decisions require knowing how much presentations cannibalize one another.
The system estimates an AIDS demand system [Deaton and Muellbauer, 1980] in its linear approximation
over revenue shares deflated by the Stone index [Stone, 1954], and the identification correction is the layer’s
contribution: standard practice regresses shares on raw prices, which confounds the price effect with everything
that moved at the same time and invents spurious substitution. Here the system runs exclusively on the
residuals of the same causal model, reusing the single cross-fitting kernel, so by Frisch–Waugh–Lovell the
coefficients coincide with those of the full system without inheriting the endogeneity of the observational price.
The theoretical restrictions are tested rather than imposed. Section 7 reports the test and it is unfavourable.
Three declared simplifications, the observed-share Stone deflator [Moschini, 1995], date-level collapse of W ,
and the amplification of noise by division through small revenue shares, are stated in Appendix A.9.

4.3

Intermediated-Retail extensions

Two extensions describe a single agent, the shopkeeper who sets the shelf price and who buys under a liquidity
constraint.
Pass-through as a process, not a constant. Treating ρ as an implicit constant is the standard error
in this channel: measured pass-through is neither unitary nor homogeneous [Besanko et al., 2005], and
two presentations with identical observed elasticities under different transmission regimes call for different
decisions. The system estimates ρ per presentation from a residualized regression of the log shelf price on
the log list price and classifies the regime against two cutoffs, sticky below 0.30 where the shelf is anchored
at a round point and the list lever loses traction, and margin-defending at or above 0.80. To that level
it adds a Markov chain over the shopkeeper’s margin state, conditioned on the sign of the list shock, and
the deconvolution ε̂ = θ̂/ρ̂, which is reported as an interpretive diagnostic only: decoupling introduces a
negative structural covariance, so the optimizer and every band operate directly on θ̂, and ε̂ is undefined
below ρ̂ = 0.30.
What the region × week aggregate does not identify is the discrete rigidity of repricing, the (S, s) behaviour
[Sheshinski and Weiss, 1977, Nakamura and Steinsson, 2008] in which the shelf jump depends on untransferred
9

cost accumulated since the last change; aggregation smooths it into a falsely gradual pass-through [Caballero
and Engel, 2007]. A three-layer structural architecture for store-level panels is specified in Appendix A.10
and not estimated here. One asymmetry in the estimation is declared because a reader will otherwise find it:
ρ is residualized against a reduced confounder set. Excluding the competitor’s list price is defensible, since it
is not a confounder of the shopkeeper’s own margin decision; excluding the inflation index is not, since it is a
common factor of both prices, and the run measures what the conflation costs (Appendix B.7).
The budget constraint of the intermediate buyer. A store may decline or truncate an order for
reasons unrelated to how well the product sells. The intuitive measurement, the store’s observed outlay with
the manufacturer, is built from the treatment and the outcome simultaneously: admitted as a control it
converts the elasticity into within-basket substitution at constant expenditure, and recommends a larger
increase precisely where substitution room exists. The construct is therefore decomposed into three objects
with three entry points: a cash-pressure index built only from sources exogenous to the firm’s own sales,
admitted to W with pre status and published with its variance decomposition; total store expenditure,
which belongs in the demand system as the instrumented allocation variable [Blundell and Robin, 1999];
and the binding state of the constraint, which is an effect modifier and not a control. Order refusal is an
extensive-margin event requiring a two-part treatment [Cragg, 1971] under selection on the constraint state
[Heckman, 1979].
The status of this frontier is stated exactly, because it is an instance of the paper’s governing principle rather
than a gap. One component is implemented, a cheap screening test that regresses portfolio breadth on the
cash-pressure index and requires |t| ≥ 2 before any further construct is built. Three are specified and not
estimated, because a region × week panel cannot support them and estimating them badly at this grain
would smooth a lumpy discrete process into a mild and false elasticity. What the screen returns on this
generator, and why, is reported in Section 7.

4.4

Optimization and the ACT/WAIT decision layer

The optimizer maximizes contribution (P − C)Q̂(P ) subject to minimum margin, volume, share and ladder
constraints, the operating limit of ±20% on the base list price, and the competitive corridor [P comp (1 −
κ), P comp (1 + κ)] at κ = 0.10. The feasible set is the intersection of these, the tighter bound governs, and the
artifact records which one bound. For a single presentation that set is one-dimensional and discrete, since
prices are set in whole units of the smallest circulating denomination, so exact enumeration delivers the global
optimum deterministically and the grid is the feasible set rather than a discretization of it; a metaheuristic
would add stochastic dependence and buy nothing. Genuine non-convexity appears only in the joint portfolio
vector, where cross-cannibalization couples decisions, and differential evolution [Storn and Price, 1997] with
L-BFGS-B polishing [Byrd et al., 1995] is reserved for that scale-up. A Score combining normalized margin
and volume ranks scenarios for the committee and never defines the optimum, since maximizing a normalized
score is not maximizing contribution.
Every recommendation travels with three quantities that a committee can adjudicate: the tolerable volume
loss L = ∆p/(m + ∆p), which is algebraically exact and free of functional-form assumptions; the critical
elasticity at which moving and holding are indifferent, exact conditional on the log-log specification the
optimizer itself uses; and a flag stating whether the whole uncertainty interval falls on one side of that
threshold. The motivation is empirical: the canonical meta-analysis reports a mean elasticity of −1.76 with a
dispersion as wide as the mean across 367 estimates [Tellis, 1988], and a later synthesis over 1,851 estimates
does not agree with it in the first digit [Bijmolt et al., 2005]. Publishing an elasticity to two decimals promises
a precision the method cannot deliver; reporting which side of the break-even the whole range falls on delivers
what the decision requires.
The decision layer. The decision layer adds no analysis. It converts what has been estimated into one
verdict per presentation and makes the optimizer act only where identification holds. It is the least common
component in enterprise pricing systems and the one on which organizational trust turns: reluctance to use
a model intensifies once it has been seen to err [Dietvorst et al., 2015], and is recovered by giving the user
visible control over an imperfect system rather than by improving the model [Dietvorst et al., 2018].
10

ACT requires passing every operative guard simultaneously. The qualifier is load-bearing: a guard that cannot
be evaluated is reported as such rather than counted as passed, so a presentation reaching ACT on seven
evaluable guards is distinguishable from one reaching ACT on eight. A verdict of WAIT, however, is not a
single state. The decision layer partitions the abstention causes into three mutually exclusive types, and the
partition is what makes the abstention diagnostic rather than terminal: it states what would have to change
for the presentation to become actionable, and in one of the three cases that change is an experiment this
system can specify.
Definition 1 (Three-way classification of guard-level abstention causes). The admissibility gate and the

regime shift in θ on excluding broken weeks (1a) and an excessive share of weeks so flagged (1b). Let S be

• GID (identification, five causes): failing admissibility; Guard 1a; Guard 1b; Guard 2; Guard 3.

adapt an existing econometric tool to this decision and others have no close equivalent in that literature,
and Section 8.1 states which is which for each of the diagnostics discussed there. Guards 1–5 address

[Cinelli and Hazlett, 2020], the stability of the recommendation across the width of the uncertainty, the
instrumental contrast, and structural-break tests [Ploberger and Krämer, 1992, Chow, 1960, Andrews, 1993]

external audit, address the ways an estimate can be well measured and still be measuring something else:
over-conditioning on mediators, a failed exclusion restriction, and cross-system interference. Two mechanisms
complement them: the elasticity is treated as a snapshot with an expiry date, re-estimated on expanding
windows with a drift alert; and every ACT-class recommendation is re-evaluated under three competitive
scenarios drawn from the bracket, which is a reduced-form one-round device declared as such rather than
structural game theory.
Cross-system interference. No decision model in an enterprise operates alone, and treating adjacent
systems as inert is an unstated identifying assumption. The structural neighbour here is a commercial
order-recommendation system that functions as a quota allocator: it predicts what the firm aims to place in
the channel, not what demand will absorb. Quota pressure is then an unobserved confounder with a temporal
signature, since a salesperson behind target discounts and pushes, and the incentive cycle concentrates
that contamination in the closing weeks where a weekly panel carries most of its price movement [Oyer,
1998, Larkin, 2014]. Forward buying reverses after a month-end push [Hendel and Nevo, 2006], which is an
independent argument for taking sell-out rather than sell-in as the outcome. And over-inventoried stores
are conjectured to discount on the shelf to rotate, which would enter the pass-through layer as consumer
behaviour; that third channel has no source behind it and is declared as an operational conjecture, unlike the
first two. The correction is to admit the incentive cycle into W and test its influence, which is Guard 8, and
to adopt a reporting convention under which every alternative reaching the committee declares its expected
cost to the adjacent system. One risk no econometrics resolves: performativity [MacKenzie, 2006], the
pricing instance of the general point that a relation estimated under one policy regime need not survive a
new one [Lucas, 1976]. Once the engine moves prices, future variation is the child of its own estimates. The
defense is experimental, and it is instrumented rather than recommended (Appendix B.19).

4.5

Architecture, quality gate and artifacts

Five principles are enforced in code rather than recommended in documentation: a single residualization
kernel feeding the causal estimator, the demand system and the pass-through deconvolution; a single temporal resampling policy, because one component that shuffles rows contaminates everything downstream
with optimism; labeled placeholders, never disguised ones; no undeclared control; and one effective
unit of inference, meaning that the count entering any statistic whose distribution depends on a sample
size is the number of independent periods, never the number of panel rows.
The fifth is stated as a principle because its violation was found where the rest of the system had already got
it right. The bootstrap clusters on the week and the break tests aggregate to it, but the robustness value and
the first-stage F statistic each consumed a raw row count, which inflated their degrees of freedom by the
number of regions and rendered the weak-instrument screens effectively non-binding. That both instances
survived a careful build is the concrete form the paper’s general thesis takes inside its own implementation:

13

the failure that survived was a tacit assumption about the unit of analysis, not an error of estimation.
Section 6.8 reports what correcting it does to the veto rate.
A single QA Gate halts the system with an explicit exception, rather than degrading silently, if the out-of-fold
fit of the demand model on the modelable set falls below 0.65 or mean conformal coverage falls below the
nominal level minus 0.03. One correction to that design is proposed and stated as such: the out-of-fold fit
of a log-volume model is dominated by seasonality and level, so it protects the band that predicts volume
but not the slope that requires the treatment to be identified. The quantity that protects the second is
already computed, the treatment partial R2 at 0.10, and the gate is therefore best presented as two thresholds
answering two questions. The deliverable of a cycle is not a table but a versioned repository: a ±20% lookup
table in 1% steps with the percentage applied once on the base price and a per-region support flag, elasticity
curves by tranche, the cross-elasticity matrix, and a schema-validated dashboard feed, all persisted by cycle
and presentation with quality flags and intervals. The feed’s verdict field is closed over exactly four categorical
states, ACTIONABLE, WAIT_CONTAMINATION, WAIT_TRANSIENT and WAIT_IDENTIFICATION, and the schema
rejects any other value, so a downstream consumer cannot silently receive an unclassified verdict; the binding
cause and the operative status of each guard travel alongside it (Appendix B.14).

5

Validation design

Every quantity reported below is measured against a data-generating process whose answers are known
by construction. The design premise is that a methodological claim about identification is worth what its
recovery experiment shows: a system that abstains correctly on a panel where the truth is unknown is
indistinguishable from one that abstains at random.
Two generators. The five-regime generator simulates W = 120 weekly periods across six regions and
six presentations spanning three brands and two categories, with volume generated jointly with prices, costs,
promotion, calendar effects, weather, minimum wage and an inflation index. Five identification regimes vary
the amount and kind of price variation available, from a clean regime with ample list movement to regimes
built deliberately to fail on weak variation, confounding with promotion, or collinearity with the competitor;
four further mechanisms inject pass-through, a cross-price response, forward buying and margin-dependent
delisting. Appendix B.1 states every parameter.
A generator with known answers is also a generator with chosen answers, so the run uses a second. The
extended generator draws eight presentations across a wider set of brands and varies pass-through, regime
assignment and elasticity magnitude in three ways chosen to remove what the first generator makes artificially
clean; Appendix B.1 states the ranges. Results are reported on both generators wherever they disagree, and
the disagreements are among the more informative outputs of the run: a property that holds on one and not
the other is a property of a configuration and not of the method. Two features both generators share bound
what either can establish, and are declared: both are stationary apart from an inflationary drift, and in both
the confounding is injected at magnitudes the designer chose.
Four layers plus an experimental one. Validation runs in four layers: a statistical layer requiring
significance, a negative own-price sign, dominance of own over cross elasticities and the QA-Gate threshold; a
commercial sanity layer confronting the estimated substitution hierarchy with the consumer decision tree
the business knows; a back-testing layer that deliberately decouples prediction from inference, because a
single predictive model conflates the two and answers both poorly; and a stress layer at the extremes. The
sign requirement carries a selection cost that the run measures rather than assumes away (Section 7).
None of the four can establish that an executed price change caused what followed it. The experimental layer
therefore implements a holdout drawn by a deterministic function of the cycle identifier, so the assignment is
auditable months later; a synthetic control with convex unit weights reproducing the pre-treatment trajectory
[Abadie et al., 2010], with its pre-fit RMSE published alongside the effect and the intercept adjustment of
Doudchenko and Imbens [2016], the time weights of Arkhangelsky et al. [2021] being declared as the natural
upgrade rather than claimed; permutation inference over the assignment mechanism itself, since with six
regions no asymptotic standard error would be credible; and a contamination test of the no-interference half
14

of SUTVA [Rubin, 1980] against the panel’s own placebo distribution plus an economic floor of 0.005 in log
price. Appendix B.19 states the protocol and its resolution limits.
Bookkeeping. Three conventions govern how every figure below should be read, and they are what
separates a validation run from a demonstration. Pre-registered verdicts: thirty decision rules were written
before the run, each pairing a claim the manuscript makes with a threshold that would settle it; eleven pass,
eighteen fail, and one does not apply because the failure it was written to adjudicate did not reproduce.
Table D.2 lists all thirty against their declared thresholds. These thirty rules are engineering verification:
a device against post-hoc selection, internal to this validation run and scoped to individual components of
the system. They should not be confused with the paper’s scientific contributions, the ACT/WAIT decision
framework, the audit of tacit assumptions into diagnostics, and the conditions for legitimate aggregation,
stated in Section 1, which the run as a whole is designed to support and which no single row of Table D.2
either makes or breaks. Separated seeds: where a design choice was selected by measurement, the selection
ran on one set of seeds and the published figure on another, so a variant chosen on one panel does not
inherit the selection bias of having been the best of several. No single R: the replication count is set per
experiment, from 300 seeds for the panel-width and randomized-shock experiments down to 5 for the re-run
liquidity-screen and pass-through-heterogeneity scenarios, and the counts are collected in Table D.1 rather
than folded into one number that would overstate the evidential weight of the thinner blocks.

6.1

Recovery of the injected elasticities

The two rightmost columns are unambiguous. Clean reaches admissibility in 0.968 of replications and ACT

fails the estimator does not degrade gracefully: in collinear the median estimate is +0.248, the wrong sign,

small elasticity is a plausible-looking number.
The round-point regime does not produce a large spurious estimate. A dedicated mechanics
experiment isolates the two candidate explanations for what a round shelf price could do to the recovered
elasticity, a collapsing denominator that the partial R2 would catch against a spurious correlation at ordinary
treatment variance that it would not, and finds support for neither: over 19 replications the estimator recovers
θ̂ = −0.069 against a truth of 0, at a residual treatment variance of 0.6385, comparable to the 0.5767 of the
clean reference, with the partial R2 passing in every replication, a coefficient of variation of the shelf price
of 0.068 confirming that the shelf does not transmit, and a residual correlation of −0.031. The variation
diagnostic correctly flags a pinned shelf as such, but an outsized estimate is not what this experiment measures
there: whatever produces one is a property of a specific configuration and not of the round-point regime in
general.

6.2

Why a resampled interval is not enough on its own

The block-bootstrap interval that the causal layer reports is a standard resampling construction [Künsch,
1989], clustered by week to respect the panel’s serial and cross-sectional dependence. Measured against known
truth, it undercovers relative to its nominal level, and testing eight alternative resampling and clustering
schemes, at two nuisance learners, does not close the gap: none reaches the coverage this paper requires
before an interval is allowed to certify a decision. The shortfall turns out to be a matter of where the interval
is centred rather than of how wide it is, which rules out simply widening it as a fix. The conformal band
used elsewhere in the system (Section 4.2) does not share this problem; measured at rolling origins it remains
the one component of the uncertainty machinery whose behaviour matches its claim.
This finding is a statement about estimator behaviour rather than about any single pricing decision, and a
direct decomposition on this same panel is reported here rather than deferred in full, because it is already
computed and it is what turns the diagnosis above from qualitative to quantitative. Over four independently
drawn designs with forty noise draws taken within each, holding the data-generating process fixed and
redrawing which weeks carried which price movements: the dispersion of θ̂ within a realized design, relative
to the bootstrap’s own standard error, is 0.51 under the boosted learner and 0.41 under the sieve, both below
one, so the bootstrap is, if anything, conservative conditional on the design it was computed from – empirical
coverage conditional on the design is 0.881 for the boosted learner and 0.475 for the sieve. The same ratio
computed against total dispersion, within designs and between them together, is 0.62 for the boosted learner
and 2.04 for the sieve: for the sieve, total dispersion is roughly double the bootstrap’s own width, consistent
with a between-design component the resample cannot see; for the boosted learner it is not, and the two
learners are read separately rather than pooled into a single verdict on this point. Table D.8 reports the full
eight-construction comparison this implies: the best of the eight, multiway clustering, reaches 0.68 under the
boosted learner and 0.80 under the sieve, still short of the 0.95 nominal level, and the rest range down to
0.20, so no resampling or clustering choice tested closes the gap. Section 6.3 takes up the remaining question
directly: whether an interval built on the missing between-design component, rather than on resampling
within one design, can. A full theoretical treatment of that construction remains companion-paper material
(Delgado, 2026); the decomposition above is reported here, rather than left entirely to that work, because it
is what licenses the diagnosis of this section rather than merely asserting it. What matters for the argument
here is the consequence: an interval computed from a single realized panel cannot always be certified as
honest, and a decision layer that ignored that possibility would sometimes authorize a move on evidence
weaker than it appears.

6.3

What a single realized panel cannot tell you

Part of why a resampled interval can be too narrow is conceptual rather than computational. Resampling
weeks inside one realized panel can only characterize dispersion within that panel; it cannot characterize the
dispersion that would arise from having observed a different set of price movements altogether, what Section 6.4
16

treats as design variance. A hierarchical variance component, estimated across several presentations each
of which is effectively a different realized design, can represent that second source in a way a within-panel
resample structurally cannot.
This system folds such a component into its uncertainty accounting through the between-presentation heterogeneity τ̂ 2 it already estimates for shrinkage (Section 4.2), built on a standard between-study heterogeneity
estimator [Paule and Mandel, 1982]. A full theoretical account of that construction’s coverage properties
is companion-paper material; the operational point this paper needs is narrower, and a direct comparison
on this panel is reported below since it is already computed: some of the uncertainty this system faces is a
property of which design was realized, not of how well it is estimated, and no amount of resampling the same
panel removes it.
A demonstrated comparison on this panel. What adopting the hierarchical component would change
is not left unquantified, since it is already measured on a real replica of this panel. Table D.9 contrasts the
production block-bootstrap against three constructions built on τ̂ 2 , over 70 replications on eight presentations,
under a scenario with heterogeneous truths and one in which every presentation shares the same truth, which
isolates design variance by construction. The posterior-predictive construction reaches coverage of 0.914 in the
homogeneous scenario and 0.92 in the heterogeneous one, against the bootstrap’s 0.484 in both; the predictive
interval before shrinkage is close behind, at 0.902 and 0.916. Folding the between-presentation component
back in closes most of the gap the earlier decomposition predicted it would, which is direct evidence that
the missing variance is design variance and not an artefact of this panel. The posterior point estimate
alone tells the opposite story: shrinking the estimate toward the pooled mean without also propagating
the predictive uncertainty covers worse than the bootstrap it would replace, at 0.407 in the homogeneous
scenario and 0.42 in the heterogeneous one, with a residual bias of 0.193 in the homogeneous scenario and
0.179 in the heterogeneous one, because shrinkage narrows the interval without correcting the centre it is
built around. The ratio of between- to within-presentation dispersion, τ̂ /ŝ, runs from 2.51 to 2.78 across
scenarios, confirming directly what Section 6.2 inferred indirectly: on this panel the bootstrap is answering
a question several times narrower than the one a decision needs answered. None of this is adopted in the
production configuration reported throughout this paper; it is reported here because it is what the earlier
diagnosis would look like acted on, and a full account of the construction’s own theoretical properties remains
companion-paper material (Delgado, 2026).

6.4

The unit at which this channel identifies

If part of what defeats a per-presentation interval is design variance, it should average out across units whose
designs are independent, at a rate the arithmetic predicts, and this is the paper’s third contribution: showing
when the unit of decision should move from the presentation to the brand or the category. That prediction is
testable, and testing it turns a decomposition into a usable prescription for where a decision is legitimate.
Table D.5 aggregates from the presentation to the brand and to the category over 70 replications. Under
the boosted learner the standard deviation of the estimate across replications falls by a factor of 2.03 at
the brand, where four presentations are pooled, and by 2.94 at the category, where eight are; the factors
predicted by independent design biases averaging at the square root of the unit count are 2.00 and 2.83.
Under the sieve learner the measured factors are 2.02 and 3.04 against the same predictions, and the largest
relative deviation from the square-root law across the four contrasts is 7.4%. The mechanism is therefore
confirmed rather than argued.
Two consequences follow that a decomposition alone would not deliver. The first is a usable estimate: at
the category level under the sieve learner the root mean squared error is 0.159 on an elasticity of magnitude
1.1, against 0.571 for a single presentation under the boosted learner, a factor of 3.59. Note which learner
wins and why: the low-bias learner looked worst when a single presentation was in view because its problem
was variance, and aggregation is precisely what removes variance. The second is a boundary on where
this purchases anything. Widening the panel in regions does not average design bias, because the list
price is common across regions of the same presentation and the design is therefore shared; widening it in
presentations does. This is the effective-unit-of-inference principle applied to identification rather than to

17

degrees of freedom.
Point-estimate accuracy is not the whole of what a decision needs, and aggregation is not a free repair for
the rest of it: whether an interval at the brand or category level also reaches nominal coverage, and inside a
usable width, is a harder and separate question that Section 6.5 takes up briefly and that companion work
treats in full. What this section establishes on its own is enough to act on: where item-level identification is
unavailable, the brand or the category is where this channel’s evidence is strongest, and a committee choosing
where to publish a number should choose accordingly.

6.5

The limit on per-presentation precision in this panel

Aggregation improves the point estimate; it does not, by itself, guarantee an interval narrow enough for
a per-presentation decision. Across the combinations of aggregation level and estimator examined on this
panel, none delivers both nominal interval coverage and a width inside the admissibility threshold defined
in Section 3 at the presentation level, and the picture improves only partially at the brand and category
levels. Concretely, the narrowest interval that also covers at the nominal level, the hierarchical predictive
construction of Section 6.3, is never narrower than 1.47 times the admissibility width threshold even in the
best combination tested, the category level under a homogeneous scenario and the boosted learner, and runs
up to 3.38 times the threshold at the presentation level under the sieve learner in the heterogeneous scenario;
none of the twelve level-learner-scenario combinations tested clears the threshold (Appendix B). That is a
property of how little independent price variation a hundred-and-twenty-week panel of this shape supplies,
not a defect specific to this implementation.
It is also the operational justification for treating WAIT as a first-class output rather than a residual category,
the paper’s first contribution: on some presentations, at some levels of aggregation, the evidence-honest
answer is that the data does not yet support a per-item decision at the confidence a committee should require.
Characterizing exactly where that limit sits, how it scales with the panel, and what would close it, is beyond
the scope of this paper and is developed in companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026]; Section 8.3
discusses what a committee should do while the limit stands.

6.6

Detectable effect, and how far the gate travels

The diagnostic thresholds are cutoffs on observable variation, but the question they exist to answer is about
detectable effect. The generator injects a known magnitude through the same portfolio mechanism used
in Appendix C.9, swept over six points from 0.1 to 1.0 at W = 120 and 35 replications per point, and
Figure 3 reports, for six candidate learners, the injected magnitude at which the block-bootstrap interval
first excludes zero in 80% of replications. The learners split sharply on where that point falls: under the
production-adjacent boosted-tree learner it is 0.9243, close to the top of the range swept, and under the
alternative tree implementation the curve never reaches 80% power inside the range tested at all; the lasso,
ridge and the sieve reach it at 0.5 or below, and a radial basis kernel at 0.8804.
A companion sweep asks the second half of this section’s question directly: holding the injected magnitude fixed
and varying only how many price movements the portfolio contains, over (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 12) movements.
Under both the boosted-tree and the sieve learner the admissibility gate stays below the point where half
of draws are admitted at six movements or fewer, crosses that mark at seven, and settles at an admitted
share of 0.55 to 0.675 for the boosted-tree learner and 0.525 to 0.675 for the sieve by twelve movements;
the mean bias measured only among the admitted draws does not move in one direction as the count of
movements rises, which is itself informative – the gate is filtering on whether estimation is possible at all, not
on which replications happen to be least biased. Beyond how far the gate travels, the frontier itself needs
correcting: comparing the honestly simulated detectable effect against the textbook formula built on each
learner’s own published standard error, the ratio between them is 2.335 for the boosted learner, the largest of
six learners compared, against 1.738 for a radial basis kernel, 1.183 for the lasso, and close to parity, at 1.057
and 1.045, for ridge and the sieve. The published frontier is optimistic by a factor that depends strongly on
which learner computed the standard error it is built on, from essentially none for ridge and the sieve to
more than double for the boosted tree; it is the same standard error this paper shows to be too small relative
to the dispersion that matters. It is recorded as a quantity to be recomputed from realized dispersion, and

6.7

Does the gate generalize beyond the configuration it was calibrated on?

The abstention gate is the system’s contribution, so whether its thresholds encode a property of the problem
or of the generator they were set on is the most consequential question a validation can ask. Freezing the
thresholds at the values calibrated on configuration A and moving the data-generating configuration answers
it directly. Table D.10 reports the calibration configuration A, a low-magnitude variant B, a few-movements
variant C and a mid-pass-through variant D: under the boosted learner precision is 0.5 in A, undefined in B
because the gate admits nothing at all, 0.0 in C and 0.75 in D; under the sieve learner it is 0.5 in A, 0.0 in B,
where the gate still admits an eighth of cells but none of them is usable, 0.5 in C and 0.6 in D. The worst
precision reached outside the calibration configuration, across both learners and every configuration where
the gate admits anything at all, is exactly 0.0, against a rule of 0.80.
Three consequences are drawn rather than deferred. The near-perfect separation on configuration A is an
artefact of calibration and does not travel as a general claim; what remains true is that on the configuration
the thresholds were set for, the gate separates. The recalibration this implies is not a matter of moving a
cutoff but of publishing the frontier: an admissibility rule stated as a single number on a single configuration
cannot travel, and the object that travels is the surface of precision over the configuration space. And the
direction of the failure is not uniform: a gate that admits nothing, as under the boosted learner in B, wastes a
panel without authorizing a bad decision, whereas a gate that admits cells at zero precision, as both learners
do somewhere in this sweep, authorizes moves it should not. Read against the rest of the paper this is not a
reason to discard the gate but to stop describing it as solved and describe it as calibrated.

6.8

The abstention funnel

The most informative quantity about a system whose contribution is abstention is where its portfolio is lost.
Table D.11 reports the funnel over 50 replications and 300 presentation evaluations. Its coverage is declared
rather than implied: guards 5 through 8 vote on the verdict but the aggregator records no per-guard exit
count for them, so four of the ten abstention causes of Definition 1 appear in the table as (pending) and their
contribution sits inside the ACT row. Those cells are left empty on purpose. The extended-taxonomy study
of Section 6.9 does measure per-cause incidence, but on a different generator, a different estimator and a
disjoint draw, so transplanting its figures into this funnel would manufacture a coherence the two runs do not
have.
The funnel ends at an ACT rate of 0.307, and the most informative property of that number is what it
is nearly equal to. Two of six presentations are drawn from the clean regime, so 0.333 of the portfolio
is identifiable by construction; the variation diagnostic passes 0.33, the admissibility gate 0.307, and the
instrumented guards remove no further share at this precision. By regime, the clean presentations reach ACT
in 0.92 of evaluations and every other regime in 0.000. The dominant recorded exit reasons are an unreliable
θ at 0.429, the broken-weeks regime gap at 0.252, the robustness value at 0.21 and the instability of P ∗ at
0.078; reasons are recorded per cell and a cell can carry several, so these are incidences and not a partition.
What the guards cost on a panel that deserves an answer. A funnel measured on a portfolio that is
two-thirds non-identifiable cannot distinguish discipline from paralysis, so the run adds the complementary
measurement. On a panel identifiable by construction throughout, every veto is by construction a false
positive. On a clean-regime panel with ρ = 0.85, ten movements of 3 to 7.5% and an exogenous competitor,
over 19 replications, the family-wise false-veto rate of the whole harness is 0.053 (one veto out of 19). The
admissibility gate is held inert on this panel by construction, so the rate is produced entirely by the eight
guards. The diagnostic classifies the triggering guard: at this sample size a single cause accounts for the
entire rate, the regime-shift guard (Guard 1a, θ shifting when broken weeks are excluded), at 0.053; no other
guard contributes. The median estimate on that panel is −1.024. Abstention therefore has a bounded cost:
the harness does not veto identifiable panels, and the 0.307 of the funnel is the portfolio and not the guards.

6.9

Does the taxonomy close the classification gap?

A taxonomy that partitions ten causes on paper is worth nothing if the population it is applied to never
exercises the cells it adds. This subsection measures the property directly: how much of a blocked portfolio
the classification rule actually reaches, under the rule as it stood before Definition 1 and under the definition
itself.
The measurement is deliberately separate from the funnel above. It runs 50 independent realizations of
the decision mechanism under the gradient-boosting nuisance learner on synthetic panels disjoint from the
funnel’s draws, for 300 decision instances in total. Of those, 250 return a WAIT of some kind, an 83.3% block
rate against an ACT rate of 16.7%. Two populations, two estimators, two block rates: the numbers below are
a measurement of the classification rule and not a recomputation of Table D.11.
Applied to those 250 blocked instances, the informal five-cause assignment the framework carried before this
paper resolves 97.2% of them. Definition 1 resolves 100%. The difference is 7 instances, 2.8% of the blocked
portfolio, whose only binding causes were among the five the earlier rule never named: Guard 1’s coverage
condition, Guard 3, Guard 4 and Guard 5. Under the earlier rule those seven presentations returned a WAIT
with no attached remedy, which is the operational definition of an unclassified terminal state: the committee
is told to hold the price and told nothing about what would change that.
Across the ten causes on this population, admissibility binds most often at 68.7% of the 300 instances, followed
by Guard 6 at 64.3%; Guard 1a and Guard 7 come next, at 43.7% and 43.0%, with Guard 2 close behind at
35.7%. Among the five causes the definition newly assigns, Guard 1a regime sensitivity is the most frequent
at 43.7%. Guard 5 never binds, at 0.0%. Contamination-type causes dominate the blocked set overall, 96.0%
against 4.0% identification-type, which is a property of this population’s guard incidence rather than of the
classification rule; a portfolio with cleaner channels and thinner panels would invert it.
Two readings of that last figure deserve to be separated. The taxonomy closes the classification gap completely
on this population, without altering a single guard’s threshold or binding rate, which is what it was written
to do. It does not follow that most blocked presentations are experimentable here: on this draw only 4.0% of
the blocked set is identification-type and therefore eligible for the experimental route. The classification is
exhaustive; the eligibility it produces is an empirical quantity that varies with the portfolio, and this paper
reports it on one population rather than claiming it as general.

Discussion

8.1

Seven tacit assumptions, seven executable tests

The strongest claim this work supports is not the integration of causal econometrics into a pricing workflow,
which is by now a well-populated genre [Chernozhukov et al., 2018], but a pattern that emerged from auditing
a carefully built system and then executing it. The failure modes that survive a competent build are rarely
errors of estimation. They are unstated assumptions about the environment, and each can be converted into
a quantity that costs less to measure than its consequence costs to absorb. Table 4 states the seven found
here: four by reading the design, and three by running the validation.

The symmetry of the table is the argument. Each row names an assumption that nothing in the estimation
stage can detect, because each is a statement about the environment rather than about the data; each is
answered by a contrast the system computes and publishes; and in each case the test is cheap while the
consequence is not. That the last three were found inside the system’s own uncertainty machinery, its choice
of unit, and its own thresholds is what makes the pattern worth stating as a general one. The sixth row
deserves one further remark against this paper’s own account: that the same work argues at length for
estimating at the week rather than the month, because the grain of a panel is an identification question, and
then publishes per presentation without asking the same question of the cross-section, is the clearest instance
here of a principle applied on one axis and not on the other. It is also the part of this work with no close
equivalent in the enterprise deployments taken here as the comparison set [Hormby et al., 2010, Deng et al.,
2023, Llenas et al., 2026], which report what the deployed system achieved rather than which of its identifying
assumptions were checked; the observation is scoped to those three and is not offered as a survey finding.
Provenance differs across the seven, and stating it plainly matters for judging what this audit adds against
what it borrows. Three operationalize a concern already established in the causal-inference literature: the
exclusion audit (Guard 7) turns the standard instrumental-variable exogeneity assumption into a residualcorrelation contrast; the contamination test adapts permutation-based inference on the no-interference half
of SUTVA [Rubin, 1980] to a donor-pool interference question; and the threshold-generalization check (row
25

seven) adapts ordinary out-of-configuration validation to a decision cutoff rather than to a fitted model. The
coverage diagnostic of row five reuses established conformal machinery [Gibbs and Candès, 2021, Barber et al.,
2023] for the test itself; what that test revealed about the source of the shortfall is taken up in Section 8.3 and
developed formally in companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026]. Two rows have no close equivalent in
the enterprise-pricing or causal-inference literatures this paper compares itself against: the quota-pressure
bracket (Guard 8), because interference between independently operated optimizers acting on the same unit
is not a standard identification concern, and the aggregation test of row six, which this paper develops as its
own contribution (Section 6.4) rather than importing one. The mediator bracket (Guard 6) sits between the
two groups: the underlying bad-control concern is well known, but declaring temporal status per column and
aborting estimation on an undeclared one is this audit’s own enforcement mechanism rather than a borrowed
diagnostic.

8.2

Where the value of a system like this one lies

In the modern-trade deployments this literature documents, where promotional variation is abundant and
shelf prices are controlled, the reported value rests in the optimization layer navigating a combinatorial space
beyond manual planning. In intermediated retail the binding constraint shifts: the decision space for a single
presentation is small and one-dimensional, the input slope is the fragile link, and optimization is the simplest
component of the system.
The run sharpens that ordering and organizes what follows around the paper’s three contributions, each
stated as a question in Section 1. Value does not concentrate in the estimator, since which of two nuisance
learners is better reverses between two generators that differ only in how pass-through is drawn. What
survives is narrower and more durable: the value lies in measuring which of the system’s own claims hold,
and in publishing the measurement. That discipline is what the three contributions below operationalize,
each in a different part of the system.
The ACT/WAIT framework (RQ1, Section 4.4). Causal identification isolates price effects from
promotion, competitor action and co-trending inflation, and the recovery experiments establish where it does
and does not. The conformal layer is the one uncertainty component whose measured behaviour matches its
claim. Neither is this paper’s contribution on its own; DML, the instrumental-variable contrast and conformal
calibration are supporting tools. The contribution is the gate that reads them together and abstains rather
than publishes a number the evidence does not support, at a family-wise false-veto cost estimated at 0.053 on
an identifiable panel (N = 19; §6.8) – the difference between publishing several different wrong answers and
publishing none. Its specific thresholds are calibrated rather than general (Section 6.7), which is stated as a
limitation rather than qualified away. A WAIT verdict is not a dead end, either: which guard produced it
indicates whether the shortfall is one of identification, calling for independently assigned variation, or an
operational threat that any experiment supplying that variation would have to control for first (Section 8.3).
The admissibility audit (RQ2, Section 8.1). An engine correct in isolation yet blind to an adjacent
optimizer acting on the same unit will model its own firm’s commercial policy and mistake it for demand;
conditioning on a mediator inflates the recovered effect; an instrument’s exclusion restriction can fail silently.
Section 8.1 converts seven such assumptions into diagnostics the system computes and publishes before a
recommendation is released, and states which formalize a concern the causal-inference literature already
recognizes and which do not have a close equivalent there.
Legitimate aggregation (RQ3, Section 6.4). A component should report the unit at which its evidence
identifies rather than the unit at which the business happens to decide. Design bias averages at the square-root
rate across replications, so a category estimate reaches an RMSE of 0.159 where a presentation estimate
reaches 0.571; Section 6.4 supplies the procedure – aggregate, then measure the dispersion the aggregation
buys – for finding the level at which a decision is legitimate rather than assuming it is the level at which the
business happens to decide.
The three are complementary rather than substitutable. Causal elasticity without an admissibility gate is
descriptive analytics that cannot say when to trust itself. A gate without the aggregation result enforces a
precision standard at a unit the evidence cannot reach. Either without the tacit-assumption audit is blind
to failure modes that are properties of the environment rather than of the estimator – and, as the previous
26

section notes, some of those failure modes have no close equivalent in the enterprise deployment literature
taken here as the comparison set.

8.3

Governance, adoption and the recalibration this run forces

The artifacts feed a human-in-the-loop process, not an autopilot: a periodic trigger, the data system, the
recommendation layer with its corridor, commercial validation with a documented override, a committee
with minutes linked to the analysis, and execution with ex-post measurement. Governance assigns explicit
roles and typifies valid overrides (strategic, commercial, model-based, always documented) against the single
invalid one, that this is how it has always been done. To these is added the cross-system convention under
which each alternative declares its expected cost on adjacent decision systems, so conflicts between models
are adjudicated on the same sheet as the price.
Three implementation challenges dominated, and the third has changed. The first was the internal coherence
of the resampling policy, since one component that shuffles rows suffices for optimism to leak into everything
downstream. The second was the discipline of declaring estimands, since the difference between a competitorconditional elasticity and an equilibrium response is invisible in a report that shows only “the elasticity”. The
third is organizational, and the conversation a committee must be prepared for is harder than “the system
will sometimes decline to recommend.” It is instead “the system cannot, on the evidence this channel supplies,
publish a per-presentation elasticity with an interval narrow enough to act on, and here is the level at which
it can.” A system that says the first earns trust; a system that says the second earns it and changes the
operating unit, which is a larger organizational ask and the honest one.
One governance risk is created by the system’s own discipline and should be anticipated rather than discovered.
An engine designed to halt rather than degrade returns WAIT for a substantial share of a real portfolio, which
reads to a committee expecting a price list as a malfunction, and the pressure that follows is predictable:
relax a threshold for one category, waive a guard for a strategic presentation, publish the midpoint because a
number is required. The countermeasure is not technical. It is to publish an explicit degraded mode (no
recommendation, reason, date of next review) rather than silence, and to accompany every abstention with
the decision arithmetic, so that holding is legible as discipline rather than as incapacity.
The run adds a second and more consequential governance item. Two calibrations, the admissibility width
threshold of 0.6 and the construction of the interval it reads, were set independently, and Section 6.5
reports that the interval this implementation currently produces does not reliably clear that threshold at the
presentation level. A committee cannot be asked to adjudicate that as two switches. It is one decision with
three defensible resolutions, each of which should be presented costed: publish at a coarser unit, where the
interval is narrowest; publish per presentation with an interval that does not cover, and say so on the artifact;
or supplement the observational panel with experimental variation, a path Section 7 takes up empirically and
companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026] develops further. The first is available now and the second is
what the system does today without declaring it.
A WAIT verdict is itself diagnostic, and which guard produced it indicates what kind of remedy is called for
and not only that one is needed. Two failure modes are structurally different. When the admissibility gate
fails for want of price variation, or Guard 2’s robustness value flags that a plausible unmeasured confounder
could overturn the estimate, the shortfall is one of identification: the observational record does not contain
variation an omitted-variable story cannot also explain, and only variation assigned independently of that
story resolves it. When the trigger is instead Guard 8’s quota-pressure bracket or the contamination test
of Appendix B.19, the threat is operational rather than statistical: a sales team retaining discretion over
incentives during a test window, or a competitor able to observe and react within the assignment’s own
geography, would undermine an experiment as readily as it undermines the observational estimate. A finding
of the second kind is therefore not evidence against experimenting; it is a requirement on how the experiment
must be run – incentive discretion frozen or centralized for the test’s duration, and treatment and control
drawn at a geographic scale wide enough that a competitor’s local reaction cannot cross between them. Sizing
that scale, and the level of aggregation at which such a design becomes organizationally realistic, is beyond
this paper’s scope and is taken up in companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026].
The ex-post loop remains the most valuable asset. The residual uncertainty this validation isolates behaves
27

like dispersion across designs rather than sampling noise within one – a distinction developed formally in
companion methodological work [Delgado, 2026] – and if that description is right, better estimation of the
same panel narrows it only partially, while generating new designs narrows it further. For the pilot that
would follow, success criteria are stated in advance rather than reported as results, staged across process,
decision, model and business; the business target of a 2–5% revenue increase is recorded as the sponsoring
organization’s commercial ambition and not as a forecast the evidence supports, since the classic evidence
establishes only that price carries high leverage on profit [Marn and Rosiello, 1992] and the documented
deployments only that analytics-enabled pricing has delivered material gains at scale [Deng et al., 2023,
Llenas et al., 2026]. One criterion is added by the run: rather than assume a holdout of any size is adequate,
size it against the experimental layer’s own measured resolution, for which Table D.13 gives the committee
a concrete range to plan against, between twelve and thirty independently assigned units in this panel.
Appendix C.10 develops the governance design and the roadmap.

8.4

Declared limitations

The system is deliberately explicit about what it does not identify. The cross elasticity inherits the endogeneity
of the competitor’s price, for which no instrument exists with these data; the competitive scenarios are
reduced-form, one round, with a constant reaction; and the performativity of the engine has no econometric
fix, only an experimental one.
Two limitations attach to the classification of Definition 1 specifically, and both are properties of the evidence
rather than of the rule. First, the transient category is empirically empty in everything this paper measures:
Guard 5 is reported as not operative in the validated configuration (Section C.2) and binds in 0.0% of the
300 instances of the extended-taxonomy study (Section 6.9). The category is defended on the argument that
a recent structural break is neither a precision shortfall nor a validity violation and that assigning it to either
would be a category error; that argument does not depend on the cell being populated, but the cell is not
populated, and a reader is entitled to treat the third branch as untested. Second, the funnel of Section 6.8
reports per-cause incidence for six of the ten causes and marks four as pending, because this run’s aggregator
does not record separate exits for Guards 5 through 8. A complete per-cause funnel on the same population
as the main run is the obvious next measurement and is not in this paper.
Six further limitations were produced by the validation run itself, and each qualifies a claim made elsewhere
in this paper.
1. The interval this implementation currently produces falls short of nominal coverage at
the presentation level, and the shortfall is one of centring rather than width. An alternative
construction was identified that reaches a nominal-appropriate coverage rate; it is not yet adopted, and
every figure in this paper is produced under the construction the implementation currently runs. The
comparison between the two constructions is developed in companion methodological work [Delgado,
2026].
2. No aggregation level tried both covers and clears the admissibility gate at its current

system has not been run on a commercial panel: it is complete and a single configuration switch redirects
it, but the switch has not been thrown, and no result reported here is an observation of a category. Both
generators are stationary apart from an inflationary drift and inject their confounding at magnitudes the
designer chose, so agreement between them is evidence of robustness to configuration and not of external
validity. And promotional incrementality is outside the system’s scope entirely: the engine treats promotion
as a confounder to be removed, not as a lever to be optimized. Layer-specific limitations, including the likely
violation of the exclusion restriction and the inter-area agreement the quota layer awaits, are recorded in
Appendix A.17 together with every governance threshold, all of which must be recalibrated against a real
category before being fixed. None of these is hidden in the artifacts: they are declared, because a decision
system that feigns certainty where it has none is more dangerous than none at all.

9

Conclusion

This paper set out to ask, of an operationally complete causal pricing system, not what the elasticity is but
when there is enough evidence to act on it and when honest conduct is to wait. The system was implemented
end to end and validated against two data-generating processes with known ground truth under thirty
pre-registered rules. Eleven pass, eighteen fail, and one does not apply. That the failures outnumber the
passes is the result rather than a caveat on it: a validation designed to confirm returns confirmations, and
the value of this one lies in the places where the system’s own claims did not survive the test written for
them. These thirty rules are engineering verification internal to this run; the paper’s scientific claims are the
three contributions below, and a high fail count on the former says nothing against any of the latter.
Section 8.2 states the three contributions in full; briefly, each answers one question posed in Section 1. The
ACT/WAIT framework (Section 4.4) answers when one can act: a gate that reads causal identification,
conformal calibration and the tacit-assumption audit together and abstains rather than publish a number
the evidence does not support, at a false-veto cost estimated at 0.053 on an identifiable panel (N = 19;
§6.8), with thresholds this paper reports as calibrated on the configurations tested rather than shown to be
general – and whose abstentions are diagnostic rather than terminal. Definition 1 makes that last property
exact: every one of the ten abstention causes maps to exactly one of three verdict types, so a WAIT always
carries the reason it would stop being one. A wait-contamination calls for control over the offending
channel, a wait-transient for accumulated history under the new regime, and a wait-identification for
independently assigned price variation, which makes that third verdict a formal statement of experimental
eligibility rather than a shrug (Section 8.3). What this paper does not do is design that experiment. It
establishes the epistemic necessity of one, by showing that a resampled interval computed on observational
variation is blind to the variance a deliberate design would control, and it marks the presentations for which
the necessity binds; the optimization problem of acquiring that variation across a portfolio at least operational
cost is a separate contribution and is left to companion work. The admissibility audit (Section 8.1) answers
what has to hold for that gate to be trusted: seven assumptions a competent build otherwise leaves tacit,
converted into diagnostics the system computes and publishes before it recommends, some adapting a concern
the causal-inference literature already recognizes and some original to this audit. And the aggregation
29

result (Section 6.4) answers at what level a decision is legitimate: a category-level estimate reaches a root
mean squared error of 0.159 against 0.571 at the presentation level, and the paper supplies the procedure –
aggregate, then measure what the aggregation buys – for finding that level rather than assuming it is the
level at which the business happens to decide.
The second and third contributions connect through a single observation. A resample inside one realized
panel cannot represent dispersion that comes from the panel being the particular one it is; in this run
that dispersion behaves like variation across designs rather than noise within one, a distinction Section 8.3
motivates conceptually and companion methodological work develops formally. If that description holds, better
estimation of the same panel would narrow it only partially, which is why aggregation is often the difference
between a decision the evidence supports and one it does not, on the kind of thin, infrequently-moving panel
a intermediated retail channel typically supplies (Section 8.4).
This paper does not resolve that limitation, and does not claim to. What it delivers instead are the three
contributions above, and a system that would rather report a coarser unit, an interval that declares what it
does and does not cover, and an abstention whose cost has been measured, than publish a number it cannot
support. The lesson this case adds to the literature on enterprise pricing systems is that, in hard-identification
environments such as the intermediated retail channel of emerging markets, the greatest returns lie not in
more powerful optimizers but in more honest inputs, and that honesty has a measurable price which a system
should be built to report rather than to obscure. A second lesson generalizes further: the failures that survive
a careful build are rarely errors of estimation, but unstated assumptions about the environment, each cheaper
to test than to leave implicit. Ranges rather than points, abstention rather than false precision, and declared
assumptions rather than convenient ones are what let a system of this kind be adopted, audited, and trusted
to say when it does not yet know.

size the returned quantile is the largest observed score and empirical coverage falls strictly below 1 − α. A
minimum stratum size mmin = ⌈1/α⌉ − 1 is therefore enforced before Mondrian stratification is attempted,
and a stratum below it falls back to the global calibration score. This is a distinct failure mode from the
degradation of the guarantee from group-conditional to marginal described above, and it is logged separately,
since one is a loss of conditionality and the other a loss of coverage. Second, the finite-sample guarantee
requires exchangeability, which the chronological partition of Section 4.2 deliberately breaks; the construction
is therefore justified by the adaptive-conformal argument for non-stationary environments Gibbs and Candès
[2021], Barber et al. [2023] and validated by the out-of-time coverage reported therein, not by the finite-sample
result.
Coverage is verified with rolling origins, moving the panel endpoint back in four-week steps for up to ten
origins and requiring at least 70 weeks of history at each, and must remain around 1 − α without drift; that
history requirement is what makes the verification feasible at this grain and is reported here because it binds.
The QA Gate halts the system if the out-of-fold R2 of the demand model on the modelable set falls below
0.65, computed on temporal folds, or if mean Mondrian coverage falls below 1 − α minus a tolerance of 0.03,
i.e. below 0.87; the same control protects, as a decorator, the artifact stage. As argued in Appendix B.15,
the R2 threshold is a test of predictive health that protects the band, and the partial-R2 threshold of the
admissibility gate is the corresponding test of identification that protects the slope.

Structural-Break Tests: Operating Parameters

Guard 5 runs two complementary tests on the residual of the causal model, both on weekly aggregates.
The aggregation is a correction and not a convenience: the Brownian-bridge limit of the CUSUM and the F
distribution of the Chow statistic both assume approximately independent observations, and running either
on rows with six regions per week inflates the effective sample size and renders both tests liberal.
OLS-CUSUM. The cumulative sum of residuals is standardized by a Bartlett long-run variance [Newey
and West, 1987] with bandwidth q = ⌊4(W/100)2/9 ⌋, and compared against the asymptotic critical value
1.358 at the 5% level [Ploberger and Krämer, 1992]. Detection requires no prior knowledge of the break date
[Andrews, 1993].
Chow tests. Candidate dates are restricted a priori to weeks carrying an own list change or a competitor
jump above 2.5%, which keeps the multiplicity finite and economically motivated. The sample is trimmed by
15% at each end so that no candidate date falls where a subsample would be too short to estimate. The
family is corrected by Bonferroni and a break is declared at p < 0.01 [Chow, 1960].
Recency. A break counts as recent, and therefore vetoes, when it falls within the last 12 weeks; an older
break is recorded without vetoing, since re-estimation already lives in the new regime. A recent break
additionally marks the sequential state of the pooling layer as reset (Section A.6).
Size is controlled by three devices (aggregation, a priori candidates, Bonferroni), and none of them speaks to
power. Appendix C.2 reports both, and the operating parameters above should be read together with what it
measures.
The asymptotic critical value is mis-specified for these residuals. The value 1.358 is the asymptotic
5% level for an OLS-CUSUM on independent residuals. On residuals from a boosted nuisance learner it
produces a rejection rate of 0.679 with no break present; on residuals from a detrended or linear learner
— gbr-detrend, sieve, or sieve-detrend alike — it produces a rejection rate of exactly zero. It is therefore
mis-specified in both directions and not merely conservative or liberal. Calibrating it by simulation under the
null returns 1.72, 0.733, 1.123, and 0.832 on the four residual paths tested (Table D.7), which restores the
size to as low as 0 and no higher than 0.107 on any path. Any deployment of this guard should calibrate the
critical value against the residual it will actually be fed rather than adopt the tabulated one, and should do
so per residual path, since the four calibrated values span more than a factor of two.
Calibration is necessary and not sufficient. With size restored, the detection rate at the largest break
the generator injects does not exceed 0.161 on any of the four residual paths, and only one of the four —
the sieve learner on detrended residuals — discriminates break from no-break at all once the critical value

restricted to week 112, the break date inside the twelve-week recency window, the best calibrated detection

Governance Thresholds and Correspondence with the Implementation

Every threshold in the system is a governance switch, modifiable by configuration without touching the
model, and every one of them is reported here with its operating value. A methodological paper that leaves
its cutoffs as symbols asks a reader to evaluate a design whose behaviour it has not disclosed; Table D.14
closes that gap, and every value in it must be recalibrated against a real category before being fixed.
The fourth column reports the sensitivity of the ACT/WAIT rate to moving a threshold, which is what
distinguishes a calibrated cutoff from an arbitrary one, and its coverage is partial by design rather than by
omission. The sweep runs over the six thresholds that constitute the decision gate itself, where a change
propagates directly to the verdict: the minimum robustness value, the broken-weeks regime gap, the maximum
share of flagged weeks, the minimum partial R2 , the maximum interval width, and the P ∗ stability tolerance.
Every other row carries a dash, meaning not that the threshold is insensitive but that it was not swept. The
distinction matters for how the table should be used: a dash is an absence of evidence about that switch, and
the committee that proposes to move one of them is proposing a change whose effect on the ACT rate this
paper has not measured.
The third column requires the same candour, because “Basis” is doing two different jobs in it. A minority
of the entries are derived or conventional in a defensible external sense: the CUSUM critical value is the
asymptotic 5% level, fmin is the conventional weak-instrument cutoff [Staiger and Stock, 1997], the block
length follows the stated formula, and α is a nominal miscoverage the committee chooses. The majority are
declared conventions: round numbers chosen by the authors as starting points, with no derivation and no
source behind the level. This applies to the QA Gate’s R2 ≥ 0.65 and its coverage tolerance of 0.03, to the
four broken-week signatures (0.5% own move, 2.5% competitor jump, the 75th promotional percentile, −4%
with volume at 1.5× the median), to the pass-through regime cutoffs of 0.30 and 0.80, and to RVmin = 0.12,
for which Cinelli and Hazlett [2020] supply the statistic but no threshold on it.
Worth calling out directly, the threshold δ = 0.30 serves as the tolerance for five separate checks: the regime
gap of Guard 1, the IV/DML divergence of Guard 4, the mediator bracket of Guard 6, the exclusion-audit
residual correlation of Guard 7, and the quota bracket of Guard 8. These five bounds coincide not by
structural necessity, but because a single round figure was selected for all of them initially. Unified until
category-level recalibration differentiates them, these thresholds act as governance switches whose initial
origin must be declared separately from their adjustment rule.
Declared open gaps. Three limitations of this calibration are recorded as open rather than resolved.
Conformal validity is verified marginally and by rolling origins, but not conditionally on regime: a band can
be narrow and correct on average while failing in the regime that matters. Every threshold above is calibrated
against the generator and none against a commercial category. And promotional incrementality is outside the
system’s scope: promotion is treated as a confounder to be removed, never as a lever to be optimized.

Extended Methodology

Section 4 states each layer of the system in the space a journal article allows. This appendix carries the
exposition it was condensed from: the generators in full, the argument for the temporal grain, the diagnostic
and its cutoffs, the confounder architecture, the pooling layer, the demand system, the two intermediated

B.1

Data Generating Process

The data generating process (DGP; Cadahia [2026]) simulates a weekly panel (W = 120 weeks, W-MON)
across six regions and six product presentations (spanning three brands and two categories), replicating the
portfolio structure in Section 3. To capture realistic confounding, volume is generated jointly with prices,
costs, promotional activity, calendar effects (holidays, working days), weather, national minimum wage, and
an inflation index.
Five distinct identification regimes are assigned across presentations, each defined by a specific own-price
elasticity (ε) and structural challenge:
• Clean (ε = −1.3, ρ = 0.85): Baseline regime featuring 8–12 list price changes of 3.0–7.5% per series.
• round-point (ε = −1.1, ρ = 0): Shelf prices are pinned to psychological price points, so no list
movement reaches the consumer and the list-price elasticity is zero by construction.
• Weak (ε = −1.0, ρ = 0.85): Minimal variation (two changes of 0.4–0.9%), serving as a diagnostic
boundary case for non-identification.
• Confounded (ε = −1.4, ρ = 0.85): Price changes systematically co-occur with promotions.
• Collinear (ε = −1.2, ρ = 0.85): Own price closely follows competitor pricing, limiting channel
separability.
Four additional empirical mechanisms are injected: (i) Pass-through: shelf prices are generated from list
prices at the regime-specific rate ρ declared above, so the elasticity the causal layer targets is θ = ερ rather
than ε; (ii) Cross-price response: A +0.30 elasticity on log competitor price enters the demand equation;
(iii) Forward buying: Sell-in inflates by 30% during the two weeks preceding list price increases > 2%, while
sell-out remains unaffected (reproducing the target outcome variable’s structural bias); and (iv) Delisting:
Margin-dependent product attrition induces endogenous sample selection.
Methodological Caveats. Two structural features bound the empirical scope of the generator: first, retail
margins are nearly deterministic functions of list price and promotion, exercising the margin-state framework
(Appendix B.7) architecturally rather than validating it empirically. Second, the cash-pressure index relies on
a smooth national income trend, lacking regional identifying variation.
The extended generator. A generator with known answers is also a generator with chosen answers, and
a design decision that happens to suit one configuration will validate on it. The run reported in Section 6
therefore uses a second generator alongside the first, and reports both wherever they disagree. The extended
generator draws eight presentations across a wider set of brands, and differs from the five-regime one in three
ways that were chosen because they remove features the first generator makes artificially clean. Pass-through
is drawn per region around a presentation-level mean rather than fixed by regime, so that ρ ranges from
0.658 to 0.984 with a regional standard deviation between 0.096 and 0.214 and no presentation shares its
transmission with any other. The identification regimes are attached to individual presentations rather than
to pairs, so that a regime is not confounded with a brand. And the injected elasticities are drawn over a
wider range, from −0.9 to −1.4, so that recovery is not measured at a single magnitude.
Two structural features of the first generator, both of which the second retains by design, are declared here
because they bound what either can establish: both are stationary apart from an inflationary drift, and in
both the confounding mechanisms are injected at magnitudes the designer chose. A result that holds on both
46

is more credible than one that holds on either; neither is a commercial panel, and Section 8.4 states what
that costs.
The two agree on the estimation results and disagree on the gating results, and the disagreement is among
the more informative outputs of the run: a property that holds on one generator and not on the other is
a property of a configuration and not of the method (Sections 6.1 and 6.7). The extended generator also
supplies the units the hierarchical experiments of Sections 6.3 and 6.4 require, since a between-unit variance
component cannot be estimated from a portfolio in which only two presentations are identifiable; those
experiments run at eight presentations, and the level-against-the-gate experiment of Section 6.5 widens the
portfolio further, to twenty-four, so that the brand and category levels have enough units to estimate a
heterogeneity component of their own.

B.2

Why estimation is weekly and decision is monthly

The business runs a monthly pricing cycle, and it would be natural to build the panel on that cycle. The
system does not, and the reason is not convenience: three components lose identification or power under
monthly aggregation, and none of them is recovered by any downstream sophistication. Setting the argument
out explicitly is worth a subsection, because “model at the grain at which you decide” is a common and
costly default.
What aggregation destroys. The quota-pressure guard (Appendix B.11, Guard 8) identifies the contamination of the commercial incentive cycle from variables defined on the day of the month: the distance
to period close and an indicator for the closing window. On a weekly panel these take four or five distinct
values within each month; on a monthly panel they are constant, the guard’s contrast is identically zero, and
a real confounder becomes undetectable rather than absent. The cash-pressure index of Appendix B.8 loses
its calendar component for the same reason, retaining only a national real-income term with no regional
variation. And the structural-break tests of Guard 5 lose most of their power: with roughly twenty-eight
monthly observations in place of a hundred and twenty weekly ones, a CUSUM has little left to detect with,
so an expired elasticity would keep authorizing decisions.
What aggregation would buy, and why it is not needed. The one genuine argument for the monthly
grain is that it would cancel the forward-buying bias within the observation, since a purchase pulled forward
two weeks stays inside the same month. The system already neutralizes that bias by a different route, taking
sell-out rather than sell-in as the outcome: the consumer does not stockpile to meet a salesperson’s target.
Appendix C.1 measures both the bias and the remedy on the generator, and the measurement returns a null:
the injected contamination does not displace the estimate materially in this DGP, so the choice of outcome
variable rests on the argument and on the independent one of Appendix B.12, not on a magnitude this panel
supplies. Reporting it that way is the point; the alternative would be to keep a quantitative justification the
experiment declined to provide.
The division adopted. Estimation, resampling, calibration, and break detection run on the week. The
decision layer aggregates to the monthly cycle at which the committee meets and the operational feed
publishes. Nothing in the commercial process changes: the committee still decides monthly, on evidence that
was assembled at the grain where it is identifiable. The one component whose identification depends on the
panel’s temporal resolution is Guard 8. Rather than a defect, that is a result worth reporting, since it locates
precisely where the grain of a panel becomes an identification question rather than a storage decision.

B.3

Variation Diagnostic and Feasibility Assessment

Price elasticity estimation requires not only sufficient price variation but also unconfounded variation.
Collinearity between prices and simultaneous interventions (such as promotional campaigns or competitor
price changes) prevents statistical identification regardless of downstream estimation complexity. To address
these identification constraints, every presentation × region cell undergoes a diagnostic evaluation comprising
five quantitative criteria. Key failure modes include competitor collinearity (where own-price changes
47

mirror competitor movements) and price rigidity or "frozen shelves" (where consumer prices remain fixed at
psychological price points while volume fluctuates due to unobserved levers, generating spurious elasticity
estimates).
Specifically, a presentation × region cell is declared modelable if it satisfies five joint conditions:
1. Deflated list price coefficient of variation (CV) > 0.03.
2. At least four discrete price changes, defined as |∆ log p| > 0.005 to exclude rounding and deflation noise.
3. Absolute price–promotion correlation < 0.6.
4. Absolute price–competitor correlation < 0.9.
5. Non-frozen shelf status, defined as a sell-out price coefficient of variation CV(P so ) ≥ 0.01 and ≤ 5%
zero-volume weeks. The conjunction is deliberate: a shelf pinned to a round point carries a degenerate
price distribution while volume flows normally, so a disjunctive rule would fail to reject the frozen-shelf
regime.
An additional sample size threshold requires at least 40 usable observations for point estimation. Section 6.6
maps these operating cutoffs onto the minimum detectable effect (MDE) frontier.
Crucially, the diagnostic evaluates real (deflated) prices rather than nominal figures; persistent inflation
generates spurious nominal price changes that would artificially inflate move counts. Units failing the
diagnostic bypass direct point estimation, inheriting a hierarchical elasticity pooled at the brand level
(Appendix B.5) and marked as non-actionable.
The admissibility gate. While the variation diagnostic evaluates data feasibility, the admissibility gate
determines whether an estimated elasticity is statistically fit for decision-making. A presentation is declared
admissible if three conditions hold jointly:
1. The variation diagnostic is passed in at least half of the constituent regions (≥ 1/2, not a strict majority:
the implementation tests ≥ 0.5).
2. The treatment partial R2 after residualization exceeds 0.10. This ensures sufficient residual treatment
variance to prevent variance explosion in the orthogonalized DML estimator [Chernozhukov et al., 2018,
Cinelli and Hazlett, 2020].
3. The bootstrap confidence interval width for the raw elasticity is below 0.6. This operationalizes selective
prediction under estimation uncertainty [El-Yaniv and Wiener, 2010], ensuring downstream optimization
algorithms receive actionable signal rather than wide, decision-neutral bounds.
Architecturally, admissibility is evaluated prior to the decision guards (Appendix B.11). Ineligible presentations
are immediately routed to a WAIT status, bypassing downstream guard evaluations. Furthermore, admissibility
conditions the input set for conformal calibration and QA Gate scoring. This sequential ordering structures the
multi-stage abstention funnel (Section 6.8), systematically isolating data signal feasibility from decision policy
evaluation. Finally, the diagnostic estimates a reduced-form competitor reaction function using log-price
cross-correlograms and a distributed-lag regression. This yields a parameter r̂ capturing the competitor’s
cumulative price reaction and response latency. Although descriptive rather than causal (due to the absence
of a competitor instrument), r̂ serves four functions: ...it makes explicit which of the three estimands of
Section 4.1 the competitive bracket contrasts, enters the internal consistency identity θtotal − θcond ≈ θcross · r̂,
informs competitive scenario analysis (Section 4.4), and detects asymmetric price-following dynamics.

B.4

Confounder architecture, temporal status and the causal extensions

Weekly-Grain Confounder Architecture. Three specific properties of W arise from the weekly panel
structure and require explicit architectural accounting:
1. Non-parametric Seasonality: Week-of-year enters as a continuous integer rather than fifty-two
categorical indicators. Gradient-boosted nuisance estimators split on this feature non-parametrically,

48

absorbing seasonal patterns without imposing explicit degrees-of-freedom penalties on short panels (e.g.,
120 weeks).
2. Collinearity Management: Weekly working days and holidays exhibit deterministic linear dependence
(Working Days = Total Days − Holidays). Admitting both unadjusted destabilizes linear downstream
steps; the framework mandates explicit relation declarations and ingests either a single component or
its orthogonalized residual.
3. Exogenous Demand Drivers: Weather, operationalized as weekly regional mean temperature, drives
demand independently of list prices. Its role is therefore a precision covariate and not a confounder:
a confounder moves both the treatment and the outcome, and weather moves the outcome alone.
Admitting it to W is correct on efficiency grounds, since it absorbs outcome variance that would
otherwise inflate the residual, but it is not part of the identification argument and is not described as
though it were.
Temporal Conditioning and Mediator Diagnostics. A central identification condition dictates that
elements of W must be measured prior to the pricing decision. Conditioning on post-treatment realizations
blocks causal pathways and alters the target estimand [Rosenbaum, 1984, VanderWeele, 2015]. Unlike
structurally bracketed competitor prices, commercial tactics (promotions, distribution, stockouts) adjust
alongside weekly list prices, making them potential mediators. The architecture enforces an explicit declared
temporal status (pre or post) for every covariate in W , rejecting undeclared controls. Elasticities are
reported both with and without post-treatment commercial controls. The resulting differential acts as an
explicit diagnostic rather than an automated pruning mechanism; large gaps signal over-conditioning on
endogenous responses. Under the sign structure assumed for this channel (trade activity increasing when
list price rises), the indirect path through the mediator carries the opposite sign to the direct effect, so
conditioning on post-treatment commercial controls returns the direct effect and inflates the estimate rather
than attenuating it (|θ̂post | > |θ0 |). The consequence for the recommendation follows from the optimizer’s
own iso-elastic form, whose interior optimum P ∗ = Cθ/(1 + θ) carries a markup P ∗ /C = θ/(1 + θ) that
falls toward one as |θ| grows: over-conditioning therefore shrinks the recommended move and generates
systematically conservative recommendations that remain deceptively plausible. The direction is a function
of the assumed sign structure and not of the estimator: were trade activity to contract when list price rises,
the same over-conditioning would attenuate |θ̂| and the failure mode would be an aggressive one.
Implementation Decisions and Inference Rules. Four operational decisions govern the estimation
system:
1. Contiguous Block Cross-Fitting (K = 5): To avoid cross-sectional information leakage within the
same week, cross-fitting folds are partitioned into contiguous temporal blocks [Bergmeir and Benítez,
2012], binding all regional observations of a given week to a single fold. This non-forward-chaining scheme
relies on DGP stationarity and weak temporal dependence [Chiang et al., 2022] rather than Neyman
orthogonality, which pertains to moment conditions, not temporal information sets. Appendix C.4
measures the cost when this assumption is violated. Division of labor delegates strict chronological
requirements to downstream modules (e.g., conformal prediction, backtesting).
2. Shallow Nuisance Learners: Nuisance models utilize shallow gradient-boosted trees (depth 3, 200
estimators, learning rate 0.05, subsample 0.8; expanded to 300 estimators for quantile regressors). A
regression tree is piecewise constant and cannot extrapolate beyond the range of its training data, so it
represents a monotone trend as a staircase and absorbs it only where splits are dense; low-frequency
components can therefore persist into the residuals rather than being partialled out; Appendix C.3
contrasts this choice against regularized trend-harmonic learners.
3. Weekly Moving-Block Bootstrap: Inference is constructed using a moving-block bootstrap clustered by week [Künsch, 1989], accommodating both serial autocorrelation and regional cross-sectional
dependence. Block length is parameterized as L = clip(round(1.5 W 1/3 ), 2, ⌊W/3⌋) (yielding L = 7 for
W = 120, since 1.5 · 1201/3 = 7.40). Replication parameters and coverage properties are detailed in
Appendix A.2 and Section 6.2.
49

4. Median-of-Folds Aggregation: Point estimates are reported as the median across fold estimates
(θ̃ = mediank θ̂k ) rather than the pooled DML2 aggregate. On short panels with five temporal folds, a
single atypical fold materially displaces pooled estimators; the median provides robust finite-sample
protection against that risk. Section 6.2 tests the choice against known truth for both learners and finds
neither paired contrast significant: a paired t of 1.99 for the production learner (sieve) and −1.43 for
the boosted alternative, both short of the conventional threshold. The preference is therefore declared
as a convention rather than as a validated result, and pooled forms remain restricted to diagnostic
layers.
Effective Sample Size Principle. A foundational design principle underpins these choices (Appendix B.14):
the effective sample size is dictated by independent temporal periods, not raw row counts. Multiple regional
observations within a single week represent one draw from the underlying macro shock. All statistical inference
feeding the decision guards (Appendix B.11) evaluates uncertainty over the interval distribution rather than
point estimates.
Limits of Identification and Unobserved Confounding. DML acknowledges three intrinsic boundaries:
functional form choices shift to the outcome model, extrapolation beyond observed support remains unanchored,
and residualization addresses only observed confounders. A fourth is specific to this channel and is not
a property of the estimator: the treatment is the list price, so what is identified is the manufacturer’s
lever composed with the shopkeeper’s response, and separating the two requires the pass-through layer of
Appendix B.7 rather than a better nuisance model. To evaluate unobserved endogeneity, two additional

• Instrumental Contrast (PLIV) and Exclusion Audit:Identification relies on a partially linear

B.5

Hierarchical regularization: partial pooling with a decision-grade elasticity

Estimating hundreds of independent elasticities guarantees overfitting, as most individual presentations carry
little signal of their own. The hierarchy Category > Brand > Presentation allows each estimate to be
shrunk toward its branch via a conjugate normal–normal update, yielding a posterior elasticity that is the
precision-weighted average of a prior and the cycle’s own estimate (see Appendix A.6 for formal equations).
This mechanism relies on three distinct origins for the prior:
1. Hierarchical branch (cross-sectional): The prior mean is the leave-one-out brand mean (preventing
self-shrinkage), with the brand shrunk toward its category. Between-presentation heterogeneity τ 2 is
estimated via the Paule–Mandel estimator [Paule and Mandel, 1982]. To avoid mechanism degradation,
two pitfalls are avoided: using sample variance instead of τ 2 (which inflates heterogeneity with noise)
and summing raw precisions rather than incorporating τ̂ 2 into branch precisions [Efron and Morris,
1975].
2. Previous cycle (sequential): A local-level dynamic linear model with a discount factor δ = 0.90
[West and Harrison, 1997]. The posterior of cycle t − 1, with variance inflated by δ, acts as the prior
for cycle t. At δ = 0.90, information weight halves in roughly seven cycles, establishing the system’s
operational memory decay. Sequential states are versioned per cycle.
3. Cold start and structural breaks: A presentation lacking an own estimate receives the branch
prior with its full predictive variance as posterior. If a structural break is detected by Guard 5
(Appendix B.11), the sequential state resets, expiring the prior with the dead regime and restarting
from the branch. This path is specified and currently inert, since Appendix C.2 reports the break guard
as not operative; a reset mechanism that depends on a detection the guard cannot make never fires,
and saying so is preferable to leaving a dead branch documented as live.
The output is a decision-grade elasticity, θdec , traveling with its posterior variance, own-data weight, and
declared origin. Its role is strictly bounded: the ACT/WAIT decision gate (Appendix B.11) votes solely with
the raw causal estimate θ̂ so that pooling never unlocks movements. Conversely, θdec sets the absolute level
of published price and elasticity curves.

layer estimates in order to set the shrinkage weight also measures something a within-panel bootstrap cannot:
dispersion arising from the realized design itself rather
than from sampling noise around it (Section 6.3

B.6

Cross-elasticities: an identified demand system, not a correlation matrix

Portfolio decisions require knowing how much presentations cannibalize one another. The system estimates a
cross-elasticity matrix through an AIDS-type demand system, the Almost Ideal Demand System [Deaton and
Muellbauer, 1980], in its linear approximation over revenue shares deflated by the Stone index [Stone, 1954].
The identification correction is the central contribution of this layer: the standard practice in the linear
approximation of this system [Deaton and Muellbauer, 1980, Stone, 1954] regresses shares on raw prices by
least squares, which confounds the price effect with everything that moved at the same time and invents
spurious substitutions: cannibalization where there was only co-movement with promotion. Here the system
runs exclusively on the residuals of the same causal model of Section 4.1 (the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell
theorem guarantees that the coefficients coincide with those of the full system including the confounders,
but without the endogeneity of the observational price), reusing the single cross-fitting residualization kernel
that feeds every layer of the system. Because equation-by-equation nonlinear residualization makes Neyman
orthogonality only approximate in a multi-equation system, the theoretical restrictions (homogeneity, Slutsky
symmetry, and adding-up) are not assumed: they are tested explicitly, and the matrix is published only if the
violations are of the order of estimation noise.
Three qualifications keep the claim proportionate to what the layer actually does. The Stone deflator is
built from observed shares, which is the standard linear approximation and carries the simultaneity that
approximation is known to introduce; the correction available for it [Moschini, 1995] is not applied, and
Appendix A.9 states the reason. The confounders are collapsed to the date level by averaging across
presentations before residualization, which is not the same as residualizing each share equation against its own
W , and the simplification is declared rather than absorbed. The third qualification is addressed rather than
merely declared: the block bootstrap of Appendix A.9, applied jointly to the residualization and the demand
stage, supplies standard errors that the standard linear-approximation practice does not, so the restrictions
are contrasted against a null instead of against the dispersion of the estimated coefficients. Appendix C.8
reports the result, and it is unfavourable: homogeneity and Slutsky symmetry are rejected at the 5% level in
most replications. The rule stated above, that the matrix is published only if the violations are of the order
of estimation noise, is therefore testable, and does not clear its own test; the matrix is published with the
rejections attached rather than withheld, and the reason is given in that section. One reading rule matters
more than any of the three for a committee: the Marshallian elasticity divides by the revenue share, so in
small-share presentations the noise in the coefficients is amplified and the matrix can look visibly non-zero
where the underlying coefficients are not. Cross-elasticities of small presentations are to be read as bands,
and the artifact reports them as such.

B.7

Pass-through as a process, not a constant

The elasticity estimated by the causal layer, which measures volume against the sell-in price, is not the
structural consumer elasticity. Instead, it is a convolution of consumer elasticity relative to the shelf price and
shopkeeper pass-through, defined as the derivative of the shelf price with respect to the list price. Treating
that pass-through as an implicit constant, as standard practice does, is flawed: the empirical literature finds
pass-through rates that are neither unitary nor homogeneous across products and retailers [Besanko et al.,
2005], and two presentations with identical observed elasticities under different pass-through regimes require
fundamentally different pricing decisions.
The system estimates pass-through as a process with three components.
First, a pass-through elasticity ρ per presentation, estimated from a single residualized regression of
the log shelf price on the log list price, whose magnitude classifies the shopkeeper’s regime against two
declared cutoffs: below 0.30 the shelf is sticky, anchored at the round point, the shopkeeper absorbs the shock
and the list lever loses traction on the consumer; at or above 0.80 the shopkeeper defends their percentage
margin almost fully and the shelf follows the list; between the two the shock is partially absorbed with
margin compression. On the generator, where the pass-through is injected at ρ = 0.85 in every regime but
52

round-point, the estimator is contrasted on the two presentations of the clean regime over 19 replications and
recovers ρ̂ = 0.911, which classifies the shopkeeper correctly as defending their margin almost fully. This is a
validation result and should be read as one rather than as an observation about any commercial category.
One asymmetry in that estimation is deliberate and is declared, because an attentive reader will otherwise
find it: ρ is residualized against a reduced confounder set, excluding the competitor’s price and the inflation
index, while θ uses the full set. The justification is that the competitor’s list price is not a confounder of the
shopkeeper’s decision about their own margin, which is a different decision by a different agent from the one
θ describes. It is defensible, but “the same residualization kernel” does not mean “the same confounders,”
and the distinction belongs in the text rather than in the code.
The two exclusions are not equally defensible, and separating them is the point of stating both. The
competitor’s price is excluded on the substantive argument above. The inflation index is excluded as well,
and that is a different case: it is a common factor of the list and the shelf price, so dropping it leaves a shared
nominal drift on both sides of the regression and biases ρ̂ toward one, for exactly the reason the exclusion
audit of Appendix A.5 adds an explicit time index to the residualization. The bias is measured rather than
argued: the pass-through experiment estimates ρ with and without the common factor in the reduced set,
and the displacement is 0.061, in the predicted direction and of the predicted kind. The reduced set returns
ρ̂ = 0.911, above the injected 0.85; admitting the inflation index to the reduced set returns 0.844, which
recovers the injected value to within six thousandths. The prediction was that dropping a common factor of
both prices would bias ρ̂ towards one, and it does, by an amount that is small in absolute terms but accounts
for essentially the whole of the error: restoring the common factor moves the estimate by 0.067 and reduces
the miss from 0.061 to 0.006. Two consequences follow. The reduced-set ρ̂ should be read as an upper bound
on the pass-through, as stated, and the bound is now quantified rather than declared. And the specification
this layer should adopt is the augmented one: the substantive argument for excluding the competitor’s price
does not extend to the inflation index, and the experiment shows what the conflation costs. That change
is a one-line one and is recorded here rather than presented as already made, since the figures above were
produced under the reduced set the implementation currently runs.
Second, a Markov chain over the shopkeeper’s margin state, conditioned on the sign of the list shock
and stratified by region, which adds to the pass-through level its dispersion and state dependence: toward
which margin band the shopkeeper gravitates and how long it takes to return after absorbing a shock. On
the five-regime generator the margin is close to a deterministic function of list price and promotion, so this
component was exercised as architecture rather than validated as a measurement. The extended generator
supplies the missing variation, drawing ρ per region and, in one scenario, conditioning it on the margin
state, and the estimator recovers it: a bias of −0.015 under regional heterogeneity and −0.048 under state
dependence, with a regional dispersion of ρ̂ of 0.172 against a bootstrap standard error of 0.016, so the
heterogeneity is an order of magnitude above the noise the estimator carries. What does not follow is a
claim on the response band: Appendix C.7 shows that even with that heterogeneity injected, pass-through
contributes at most 0.6% of the variance of the published band, so the layer is validated as a measurement
and declared as immaterial to the decision on this panel.
A methodological note belongs with that pair, because the two results are easy to read as contradictory
and are not. That an object is estimable and that it matters for a decision are separate questions, and a
system that reports the first as though it settled the second is the failure mode this paper exists to document.
Pass-through is estimable here, well recovered, and heterogeneous; it is also, on this panel, not a material
source of uncertainty in the quantity a committee acts on. Both statements are published.
Third, the deconvolution of θ̂ into consumer elasticity ε̂ = θ̂/ρ̂ and pass-through ρ̂. Because θ̂ is directly
identified by Double Machine Learning, all response bands and decision thresholds rely directly on Var(θ̂)
via the weekly block-bootstrap. Decoupling θ̂ introduces a negative structural covariance (Cov(ε̂, ρ̂) < 0);
omitting this term in a Delta-method expansion would artificially inflate uncertainty bounds. Deconvolution
is thus reported solely as an interpretive diagnostic, while optimization operates directly on θ̂. If ρ̂ < 0.30, ε̂
is reported as undefined.
The system’s honesty requires stating what is not identified in the region×week aggregate: the discrete
rigidity of the shopkeeper’s repricing, that is, the (S, s)-band behavior [Sheshinski and Weiss, 1977, Nakamura
53

and Steinsson, 2008] in which the shelf jump depends on the untransferred cost accumulated since the
last change, averages out and disappears, producing a falsely gradual pass-through [the Caballero–Engel
aggregation bias; Caballero and Engel, 2007]. For when a store-level transactional panel exists, the system
leaves ready a three-layer structural/Bayesian architecture: a data-quality scoring layer that classifies stores
into Gold/Silver/Bronze according to the continuity of shelf-price capture, with repricing detection confirmed
by persistence so as not to confuse capture noise with real jumps; a layer that estimates the (S, s) physics,
that is, the repricing trigger, exclusively on the Gold segment, where individual change events are observable
with dates; and a Bayesian transfer layer that carries that physics, as a prior with a learned hypervariance,
toward the noisy-data segments. The key conceptual correction of this last layer is that reliability is not
sampling precision: shrinkage weights anchor to measurement reliability λ rather than nominal standard
c to prevent corrupt data from appearing spuriously precise. Although attenuation is linear in λ, the
errors (SE)
disattenuated variance Var(ε̂)/λ2 causes effective precision to scale with λ2 [Fuller, 1987]. Final coverage is
guaranteed by construction via quality-stratified conformal prediction: the structural model delivers efficiency,
while the conformal layer maintains validity through wider intervals for low-quality data.

B.8

The budget constraint of the intermediate buyer

The shopkeeper is not only a price setter; they are a liquidity-constrained buyer. Working capital in the
intermediated retail channel is finite and its tightness is not constant across the month, so a store may decline
or truncate an order for reasons that have nothing to do with how well the product sells. This is a distinct
mechanism from pass-through, and it is worth stating precisely why the obvious way of measuring it is worse
than leaving it unmeasured.
The intuitive construction (infer the budget from what the store buys from us) is built from the treatment and
the outcome simultaneously. Introduced as a control it converts the elasticity into within-basket substitution
at constant expenditure: if a list increase on one presentation is absorbed by reallocating toward another, the
model observes little aggregate volume loss and recommends a larger increase precisely where substitution
room exists. There is, moreover, a measurement problem that precedes any econometric one: the manufacturer
does not observe the shopkeeper’s wallet, only its own share of it. A decline in observed purchases is equally
consistent with a contraction of liquidity and with a loss of share to a competitor, and the two call for
opposite responses: the first for patience, the second for a price reaction.
The system therefore decomposes the construct into three objects with three different entry points. A
cash-pressure index, built exclusively from sources exogenous to the firm’s own sales (the semimonthly
payroll cycle taken to govern retail liquidity in the market under study, month-end proximity, staple-basket
inflation, and the real erosion of the minimum wage), is a legitimate pre-treatment control and enters W
with that status declared. Two components of that sentence should not be conflated. That expenditure
responds to the arrival of a paycheck is documented, though for a different population and institutional
setting [Stephens Jr, 2003]; that the relevant cycle here is semimonthly and that it governs shopkeeper
liquidity in this channel is a property of the market under study, asserted on operational grounds and not
transferred from that evidence. The screening test described below is what keeps the assertion from being
load-bearing: the index is admitted to W only if it clears it. Its composition is published together with a
variance decomposition, because the two terms of the index are not equally informative and the aggregate
conceals that. The calendar term carries intra-month variation and is the component that identifies; the
real-income term is, absent a regional price index, a smooth national series that is nearly collinear with the
inflation index already present in W and therefore identifies nothing on its own. On the generator the split is
51.0% of the index’s variance from the calendar and 51.0% from real income, the two summing above unity
because the cross term is negligible and each standardized component contributes its own unit variance.
That split does not, on its own, support the asymmetry just asserted, and saying so is the point of publishing
the decomposition rather than the conclusion drawn from it. By variance the two terms are interchangeable.
What separates them is the second measurement: the real-income term correlates with the inflation index
already in W at 0.884, so almost all of its variance is variance the confounder set already carries, while the
calendar term is orthogonal to it by construction. The correct statement is therefore about incremental
rather than total variation: the two terms contribute equally to the index and unequally to its identifying

54

content, and an index reported as a single number would conceal both facts. The repair is a data one and
mirrors the quota hierarchy of Appendix B.12: regional sources (a regional price index, a harvest calendar, a
measure of informality) enter automatically where the panel supplies them. The construct is admitted to the
full frontier only after a screening test clears it, described below. Total store expenditure belongs in the
demand system of Appendix B.6, where budget shares are defined on it by construction and where it is the
allocation variable rather than an illegitimate control; it must be instrumented, since the error of any single
equation enters the total [Blundell and Robin, 1999], and it yields an expenditure elasticity that the system
does not currently produce and that answers a question the business asks constantly: which references get
restocked first when cash arrives. The binding state of the constraint (whether liquidity binds, rather
than its level) is an effect modifier on both the elasticity and the pass-through, not a control.
Finally, order refusal is not elasticity. The behavioural premise of this whole subsection is that a liquidityconstrained shopkeeper narrows the breadth of the portfolio before reducing depth, and it is stated as a
hypothesis rather than as an established regularity: it is not documented in the literature this paper draws
on, and the screening test below exists precisely to refuse investment in the layer on a panel where it does
not hold. The econometric citations that follow concern the treatment of such an event, not the existence of
the ordering. If the premise holds, the event is an extensive-margin one with an empirical signature distinct
from a smooth volume decline, and it would require a two-part treatment [Cragg, 1971]: the probability that
the order is accepted at the offered price, and the volume conditional on acceptance. If the constraint causes
non-purchase, the observed volume sample is selected on the state of the constraint [Heckman, 1979], and no
adjustment of the observable confounders repairs it.
The status of this frontier in the present system should be stated exactly, because it is an instance of the
paper’s governing principle rather than a gap. One component is implemented: a screening test that regresses
portfolio breadth (the count of presentations with a positive order per region×week) on the cash-pressure
index, controlling for own log price, and requires |t| ≥ 2 before any further construct is built. It is deliberately
cheap, and its purpose is to refuse investment in the full frontier on a panel that shows no cash-constraint
signal at all. On this generator the test returns no statistic: it executes without error in all thirteen replications
and the t is non-finite in every one. That outcome is reported as it is and not rounded into a verdict, because
the two are different. A finite |t| < 2 would be a screening failure and would say that the panel carries no
cash-constraint signal; a non-finite t says the regressor carries no variation against which a signal could be
measured.
The cause of the non-finite statistic is now established rather than conjectured, and it lies on the left-hand
side rather than the right. Portfolio breadth is a count of presentations with a positive order, and on a
generator where every presentation is ordered every week that count is constant: its standard deviation is
exactly zero, there is nothing to regress, and a non-finite t is the correct output of a well-behaved routine
rather than the symptom of a defect. The diagnosis is confirmed by construction. Injecting order refusal gives
the dependent variable variation and the screen then operates: with a weak refusal mechanism the breadth
carries a standard deviation of 1.755 and the screen passes in every one of 5 replications. That |t| runs into
the tens is not evidence of power and is not presented as such, since breadth is generated as a direct function
of the index in those scenarios and the test recovers a relation that was imposed. The informative cell is the
one with regional income variation and no refusal, where breadth is again constant across weeks, the statistic
is finite at t = −0.5, and the screen correctly declines to admit the index. The screen is therefore operative
and correctly calibrated, and what the panel lacked was the phenomenon rather than the instrument. The
frontier remains neither cleared nor refused on this generator, and it is now clear which of the two the
missing ingredient is: an order-refusal signature in the data, not a better regressor. Three components
are specified and not estimated: the two-part selection model, the instrumented total expenditure, and the
binding-state effect modifier. Each is documented in Appendix A.10 as a proposed design with its grain
dependence declared, and each is withheld for the same reason: a region×week panel cannot support them,
and estimating them badly at this grain would smooth a lumpy discrete process into a mild and false elasticity.
This subsection would not be honest if it presented four estimated objects where the system runs one test;
presented correctly, this frontier is a worked example of not estimating what the grain does not support. Two
dependencies are declared rather than worked around. Store-level grain is a prerequisite: the constraint is a
discrete, lumpy phenomenon at the store, and averaging it across a region smooths it into a mild and false

55

elasticity, the same aggregation argument that governs the (S, s) layer of Appendix B.7 [Caballero and Engel,
2007]. And the panel’s imputation policy is a live risk to this measurement: forward-filling gaps can convert
order refusals into apparent continuity, so a concentration of the imputation flag at month-end and among
small stores is evidence of the phenomenon rather than of a capture defect.

B.9

Optimization: contribution as the objective, the Score as the language

The optimization layer is designed on two levels with deliberately separated roles. The optimizer maximizes
contribution in value, (P − C) · Q̂(P ), with minimum margin, volume, market share, and price ladders as
constraints, and with the competitive corridor (competitor price ±10%, the strategic RGM cap) as a hard
constraint. The Score(P), a weighted, normalized combination of margin and volume, is the communication
layer: it ranks scenarios for the committee with readable verdicts (“sweet spot,” “valid,” “marginal”), but
it never defines the optimal price, because maximizing a normalized score is not equivalent to maximizing
contribution and would make the optimum dependent on the normalization window.
The architecture of the decision curve is deliberately simpler than a reader of this literature might expect,
and the simplicity is the point. One hard constraint on the price derives from the competitive environment:
the corridor [P comp (1 − κ), P comp (1 + κ)] with κ = 0.10. It is not the only bound the optimizer respects,
and stating otherwise would misdescribe the feasible set: the operating limit of ±20% on the list price, the
minimum margin, the volume and share bounds, the price ladders, and the absolute range [Pmin , Pmax ] of
Appendix A.11 all bind as well. The precedence between them is explicit, because the ±20% limit can bite
before the corridor does: the feasible set is the intersection of the operating range around the base price with
the competitive corridor, and where the two conflict the tighter of the two governs and the artifact records
which one was binding. The corridor is distinctive only in what it encodes, a strategic RGM position relative
to the competitor rather than an internal accounting floor. The conformal band of Section 4.2 does not
bound it. That band is a predictive interval on the volume response, and it travels with the recommendation
and with each row of the lookup table as the uncertainty attached to the consequence of moving; using it
to trim the corridor would silently mix an interval on a response with a bound on a decision variable, two
objects with different units and different meanings.
The separation is what makes each of them interpretable: the corridor answers where the price is allowed to
go, the band answers how confident the system is about what follows. A third candidate cut, a kink at the
tranche where the elasticity accelerates, is not implemented: the demand model carries no threshold terms,
and introducing them is recorded as future work alongside the multi-item optimizer rather than described as
though it were running.
The choice of optimization method responds to the structure of the problem and not the other way around,
and the argument is worth stating correctly because the intuitive version of it is wrong. For the decision on a
single presentation the feasible space is one-dimensional, bounded by the corridor, and discrete in practice,
since prices are set in whole units of the smallest circulating denomination. That last property is an operating
convention of the channel declared here rather than a documented fact, and the argument does not lean on
the particular denomination: it requires only that the admissible prices form a finite set small enough to
enumerate, which any discrete pricing convention delivers. Exact enumeration of that grid with an arg max of
contribution therefore delivers the global optimum deterministically and without a seed, and the grid is the
feasible set rather than a discretization of it. A metaheuristic would add stochastic dependence to a problem
that has none to spare and would buy nothing in return. What it would not do is rescue the optimizer from a
discontinuous landscape, because the objective is not discontinuous: under Q = Q0 (P/P 0 )ε the contribution
is smooth on each side of the base price, with a single kink at P 0 where the Dual Beta switches slope (a
kink that disappears with the withdrawal of that estimator in Appendix C.6, leaving the objective smooth
throughout, which strengthens rather than weakens the argument for enumeration). The real non-convexity
appears only when optimizing the joint portfolio vector, where cross-cannibalization couples all decisions;
that high-dimensional multimodal landscape is the intended domain of global differential evolution [Storn
and Price, 1997] followed by L-BFGS-B polishing [Byrd et al., 1995], which is specified and reserved for the
multi-item scale-up phase rather than implemented here. The demand curve the optimizer consumes is driven
by the decision-grade elasticity of Appendix B.5: θdec sets the level, whereas the permission to move at all is
granted by the decision layer voting on the raw estimate. In the configuration this paper validates the curve is
56

symmetric, since the asymmetric estimator is withdrawn (Appendix C.6); the scaling of a directional pair by
k = θdec /θ̂ is retained in Appendix A.11 as the form the layer takes on a panel that supports asymmetry. The
Shopkeeper Elasticity (Section 4.3) acts as a traffic light after optimization, not as a term in the objective.
A final translation step closes the gap between what the optimizer computes and what a committee can
adjudicate. The break-even is already implicit in the objective (contribution is maximized against observed
cost), but implicit is not auditable. Every recommendation therefore travels with three additional quantities:
the tolerable volume loss the move can absorb before it stops paying, the critical elasticity at which
the contribution of moving equals that of holding, and a flag stating whether the entire uncertainty interval
falls on one side of that threshold. The status of the first two is worth stating precisely, and in the system’s
favour. The tolerable volume loss is algebraically exact and free of any functional-form assumption: it follows
from the definition of contribution alone, which is what allows a committee to verify it mentally and is the
reason it is published. The critical elasticity is exact conditional on the log-log specification the optimizer
itself uses, so it inherits that assumption and no other. Neither is an approximation, and presenting them as
such would understate the one part of the output a non-technical reader can fully audit. The motivation is
empirical as much as rhetorical: the canonical meta-analysis of price elasticities reports a mean of −1.76 with
a dispersion as wide as the mean itself across 367 estimates [Tellis, 1988], and a later synthesis over 1,851
estimates does not agree with it in the first digit [Bijmolt et al., 2005]. Against that background, publishing
an elasticity to two decimals promises a precision the method cannot deliver; reporting which side of the
break-even the whole range falls on delivers exactly what the decision requires. When the interval straddles
the threshold, the honest output is not a smaller decimal but a smaller move, a store-level test, or abstention.

B.10

The decision layer: ACT, or which kind of WAIT

All the machinery above estimates the causal slope as well as possible with the data at hand. The decision
layer adds no further analysis: it converts what has been estimated into a single verdict per presentation,
drawn from the four states of Definition 1, and makes the optimizer act only where identification holds. The
three WAIT states are not decoration: they separate a block an experiment can lift from one it cannot and
from one that time alone lifts. It is, to the best of our knowledge, the least common component in enterprise
pricing systems, and the one on which organizational trust turns: reluctance to use a model intensifies once it
has been seen to err [Dietvorst et al., 2015], and it is recovered by giving the user visible control over an
imperfect system rather than by improving the model [Dietvorst et al., 2018]. An engine that knows how to
abstain is an engine one can delegate to.

B.11

The eight guards and the ten abstention causes

The ACT decision requires passing every operative guard simultaneously, each tied to a real failure mode and
not to a report metric. The qualifier is load-bearing rather than defensive: Guard 8 is declared non-operative
when the panel carries neither the true incentive fields nor sub-monthly grain, and a guard that cannot be
evaluated is reported as such instead of being counted as passed. Each verdict therefore travels with the
operative status of all eight, so that a presentation reaching ACT on seven evaluable guards is distinguishable
from one reaching ACT on eight. The first five address the quality and the currency of the estimate; the
last three, added after an external audit of the system, address the ways an estimate can be well measured
and still be measuring something other than what it claims. Table 5 summarizes them in compact form (the
question each answers, its mechanism, whether it vetoes, and the verdict type it maps to); the paragraphs
below develop them in turn. The table has ten rows and not nine because Guard 1 evaluates two conditions
on separate thresholds, a regime shift and a coverage share, and Definition 1 classifies causes rather than
guards.
The eight are preceded by the admissibility gate of Appendix B.3, and the order is not incidental: a
presentation that fails admissibility reaches WAIT without a single guard being evaluated, so the guards
are properly understood as the second of two filters rather than the whole of the decision layer. Section 6.8
reports how much of the portfolio each stage removes.
The first guard flags the broken weeks, the contaminated natural experiments, through four observable
proxies whose operating definitions are published because the guard is only as credible as its criteria. A
57

stockout is a week whose volume falls below the presentation’s own fifth percentile without a price fall that
would explain it: volume responded to availability, not to price. Price-matching is a week in which the own
price moved by at least 0.5% in the same direction as a competitor jump of at least 2.5%; that variation
identifies a gap elasticity, not an own-price one. Intense promotion with social diffusion is flagged only above
the 75th percentile of positive promotional intensity, and the restriction is deliberate rather than conservative:
average promotion is already controlled as a confounder, and flagging all of it would change the estimand
instead of protecting it. Clearance is a list fall of at least 4% accompanied by volume at or above one and a
half times the median.
The elasticity is then re-estimated excluding those weeks, subject to a floor of forty usable observations, and
the guard vetoes when the two estimates differ by more than 0.30 or when more than 45% of weeks were
flagged. One limitation of that contrast is declared rather than left implicit: when the clean re-estimation
is left with few observations, the guard compares two estimates that are both noisy, and a large gap may
reflect the loss of precision rather than dependence on the excluded weeks. The artifact therefore publishes
the interval width of the clean estimate alongside the gap, so that a reader can see whether the contrast
discriminates.
The second guard is the Cinelli–Hazlett robustness value [Cinelli and Hazlett, 2020]: a single auditable
number answering how strong an unmeasured confounder (one moving price and volume at the same time)
would have to be to drive the elasticity to zero. A low value means the sign of the effect is fragile with respect
to what is not observed, and no price is moved on that basis. The operating threshold is 0.12.
Two refinements make this guard say what it is supposed to say. The first is the unit of inference, and
it is a correction rather than an extension. The robustness value depends on the degrees of freedom, and
feeding it a count of panel rows inflates those degrees of freedom by approximately the number of regions,
since six regions observed in one week are not six independent observations. The consequence is systematic:
an inflated count shrinks the statistic that enters the calculation and displaces the robustness value, so a
fixed threshold is miscalibrated against the very unit of inference the rest of the system treats as honest. The
guard therefore consumes the effective number of independent periods, consistent with the principle stated in
Appendix B.14, and the threshold is recalibrated against the generator. Section 6.8 reports the veto rate
before and after the correction, since a change in the veto rate of a guard is exactly the kind of consequence
a methodological paper exists to document.
The second refinement is presentational and matters for adoption. A bare robustness value is close to
unreadable for a committee, and the device that makes it interpretable is the benchmark against an observed
covariate that Cinelli and Hazlett propose. Since every column of W is already declared, promotional intensity
is the natural reference, and the guard reports the robustness value alongside a sentence of the form: an
unmeasured confounder would have to be some multiple of the strength of promotional intensity to nullify
the estimate. That formulation is adjudicable in a meeting; a bare value is not.
The third guard evaluates the stability of the recommendation across the width of the uncertainty:
the optimizer is run at the endpoints of the elasticity’s confidence interval, and if the recommended price
swings between them by more than a tolerance (half the corridor width), the decision is not robust to the
estimate’s own uncertainty.
As implemented, that test interrogates the raw estimate while the price actually emitted is computed at the
regularized level, which means the guard verifies the stability of a price different from the one published.
The division of roles behind this is sound and is retained (the gate votes with the raw evidence, the pooled
estimate sets the level), but the gap it leaves is closed by evaluating stability twice and publishing both.

The fourth guard is the instrumental contrast described in Section 4.1: if the cost instrument is strong

The fifth guard monitors not the quality of the estimate but its validity over time: two structural-break
tests on the residual of the causal model: an OLS-CUSUM [Ploberger and Krämer, 1992] that detects
breaks without knowing the date [Andrews, 1993], and Chow tests [Chow, 1960] with Bonferroni correction
at candidate dates known a priori (own list changes and competitor jumps above 2.5%), both on weekly
aggregates and with a Bartlett long-run variance estimator [Newey and West, 1987], which gives three layers
of protection against false positives. The full operating parameters are given in Appendix A.14; two of them
carry an argument that belongs in the main text. The aggregation to weekly totals before testing is not a
convenience: the Brownian-bridge limit of the CUSUM and the F distribution of the Chow statistic both
assume approximately independent observations, and running them on a panel with six regions per week
inflates the sample size and makes both tests liberal. And “recent” is defined as twelve weeks, which is the
window that determines whether a detected break vetoes or is merely recorded.
Three layers of protection against false positives is a statement about the size of these tests and says nothing
about their power, which is the property that matters when the guard’s job is to expire an estimate. A
test that never fires spuriously and never detects a real break would satisfy the description and be useless.
Appendix C.2 therefore reports the detection rate against the magnitude of an injected break. That section
also verifies the size claim itself rather than assuming it: the three devices control multiplicity and dependence,
but none of them protects against a non-stationary residual, and the realized false-positive rate on residuals
of a boosted nuisance model does not match the nominal one: with no break injected the pair rejects at 0.94,
and neither device is innocent, the CUSUM alone firing at 0.71 and the Bonferroni-corrected Chow family at
0.84 (Appendix C.2). The sentence above should therefore be read strictly as a description of what the three
devices are designed to do, and as a measured refutation of the claim that they achieve it here. Calibrating
the critical value by simulation rather than taking it from the asymptotic distribution restores the size on
every residual path tested and leaves the guard with no power at the largest break the generator can inject,
so the outcome of that section is that Guard 5 is not operative and both its veto and its reset are withdrawn
from the decision layer. The description that follows is therefore of the design as specified, and the measured
status of each element is given in Appendix C.2. As specified, a recent break vetoes action: the elasticity was
estimated mostly in the previous regime and has expired. The same break also expires the sequential prior of
the pooling layer: the state is marked as reset, so the next cycle restarts from the hierarchical branch instead
of a posterior learned in the dead regime. An old break does not veto, because the re-estimation already lives
in the new regime, but it is recorded.
The sixth guard tests over-conditioning on mediators. Following the declared temporal status of every
control in Section 4.1, the elasticity is re-estimated with the post-treatment commercial controls removed,
and the two estimates are compared. A material gap means the production estimate is partly conditioned on
activity that itself responds to price. The competitor’s price is deliberately excluded from this pruning, since
its ambiguity is already resolved by the competitive bracket and merging the two would confound distinct
questions.
The seventh guard tests the exclusion restriction of the cost instrument by the residual-correlation
procedure of Section 4.1. Like the fourth guard, it vetoes only where the instrument is strong: with a weak
instrument it records the state and defers to the remaining guards. Its practical effect is to prevent the
instrumental contrast from being read as an endorsement when both estimators are displaced by the same
common shock.
The eighth guard addresses cross-system interference and is developed in Appendix B.12: the elasticity is
re-estimated with controls for the commercial incentive cycle, and a material change indicates that part of
what was being read as price sensitivity was salesforce effort.
Its inputs follow a declared hierarchy of sources, and the hierarchy is part of the guard rather than an
implementation note. Where the panel carries the true incentive fields (quota, attainment against target,
59

effective discount), they enter directly and the guard runs on them. Where it does not, the guard falls back
to a calendar proxy built from the day of the month, which requires sub-monthly grain to have any variation
at all. And where neither is available, the guard is declared non-operative rather than run on a constant
and reported as passing, since a contrast between two identical specifications returns zero and would be
indistinguishable from a clean verdict. This is the one component of the system whose identification depends
on the temporal resolution of the panel, which Appendix B.2 treats as a result worth reporting rather than a
limitation to be apologized for.
Two mechanisms complement the guards. First, the elasticity is treated as a snapshot with an expiration
date, not a structural truth: it is re-estimated on expanding windows at quarterly cuts, a drift alert fires
when the estimate of one cut falls outside the interval of the previous cut, and an estimate with active drift or
not revalidated in three cycles does not feed decisions until re-estimated. The same discounted sequential filter
of Appendix B.5, run over this history, quantifies for the committee that the decision-grade elasticity does
not bounce between cycles with pure estimation noise while the raw estimate does, without changing a single
ACT/WAIT verdict. Second, every ACT-class recommendation is re-evaluated under three competitive
scenarios built on the bracket of Section 4.1: the competitor ignores the move, reacts with their historical
slope, or matches it fully. The verdict distinguishes moves that are robust to the game (the contribution gain
survives all three scenarios, typical of increases: if the competitor matches, the whole category moves up),
conditioned moves (they pay only if the competitor does not match, typical of cuts that degenerate into a
price war), and cases where holding dominates. This is not structural game theory: the reaction is taken as
constant and one round, and it is declared as such: it is the deliberate refusal to optimize with false precision.
One risk remains that no econometrics resolves: performativity [MacKenzie, 2006], the pricing instance of
the general point that a relation estimated under one policy regime need not survive the adoption of a new
one [Lucas, 1976]. Once the engine starts moving prices, future variation will be the child of its own estimates
and observational identification will degrade on its own. The defense is not statistical but experimental,
and this system instruments it rather than merely recommending it: Appendix B.19 describes the holdout
protocol, the synthetic control contrast, and the contamination test that operationalize it. It remains the
single investment worth more than any additional sophistication of the estimator.

B.12

Cross-system interference and the endogeneity of commercial effort

No decision model in an enterprise operates alone, and treating adjacent systems as inert background is
an unstated identifying assumption. In this setting, a primary structural neighbor is a commercial orderrecommendation system that functions as a quota allocator rather than a demand forecast, predicting what
the enterprise aims to place in the channel rather than what final demand will absorb. When downstream
models mistake these push-driven placement targets for true consumer demand, historical sales become
artificially capped by past allocation policies. Conflating supply allocation with unconstrained point-of-sale
demand induces structural endogeneity. As a result, optimization models inadvertently capture past enterprise
policies rather than true underlying market responsiveness.
The consequence for causal identification runs through three channels, none of which was instrumented
before. First, quota pressure is an unobserved confounder with a temporal signature. When a
salesperson runs behind target, discounts, free-goods allowances, and portfolio pushes appear; the effective
price falls and volume rises. A model without a quota variable records that co-movement as elasticity when it
is quota compliance, and because the incentive cycle is monthly the contamination concentrates in the closing
weeks, precisely where a weekly panel carries the most price movement [Oyer, 1998, Larkin, 2014]. Second,
forward buying reverses. A month-end push leaves the store holding inventory above its rotation, and the
following week it purchases less for reasons of stock rather than price [Hendel and Nevo, 2006], producing an
overstated sell-in elasticity in the push week and a negative one after it, a pattern a model without the cycle
variable reads as noise or as a structural break. This constitutes an additional and independent argument
for the system’s choice of sell-out rather than sell-in as the outcome: the consumer does not stockpile to
meet a salesperson’s target. Third, and most consequentially for this system, over-inventoried stores are
conjectured to discount on the shelf to rotate, so the shelf price would fall for inventory reasons and
the pass-through layer would record the resulting volume response as consumer behavior. The asymmetry
with the two channels above is deliberate and is declared: those rest on documented evidence [Oyer, 1998,
60

Larkin, 2014, Hendel and Nevo, 2006], whereas this one is an operational conjecture from the channel with
no source behind it, and it is the channel on which the most consequential of the three claims rests. The
broken-weeks guard already flags the terminal symptom (clearance), but only after the fact and without the
cause; with the quota variable the cause becomes observable in advance.
The loop closes uncomfortably: the push responds to the target, the target is set by looking at history, and
the pricing engine learns from that same series. Part of what the system measures is the consequence of its
own company’s commercial policy. The correction is to admit the incentive cycle into the confounder set and
to test its influence explicitly, which is the eighth guard.
There is also a direct operational conflict, and it is not resolved by econometrics. The two systems optimize
objectives that cannot be maximized jointly, and adherence to the suggested order will fall in response to
the very price increases this engine recommends. Arbitrating each collision bilaterally lets the outcome be
decided by the relative standing of the areas involved and leaves nothing learned for the next one, and there
will be a next one, since production planning, territory targets, and trade investment optimize objectives that
are equally legitimate and equally unaware of each other. The mechanism adopted instead is a reporting
convention: every alternative that reaches the committee declares its cross-system cost: the
expected volume loss, and hence the expected adherence loss, implied by the proposed move, on a three-level
scale. The committee stops choosing between two systems and chooses an option that already carries the
cross-cost inside it, and the same format extends to the next collision. A secondary benefit is at least as
valuable: the convention forces each model to state in writing what it predicts. Much of the friction did not
arise from the objectives differing (that is normal and healthy) but from both figures being presented as
though they were the same kind of object.

B.13

System architecture and artifacts

The layers above are a specification; this subsection states how the specification is enforced in code and what
the cycle leaves behind. Three elements complete the methodology: the architectural principles that prevent
two layers from diverging on the same operation, the single quality checkpoint that halts the system rather
than degrading it, and the versioned artifacts that make a cycle auditable ex post. The validation protocol
that measures whether all of this works is deliberately separated into Section 5.

B.14

Architecture principles

The system is implemented as a modular, auditable computational architecture, with the more opaque
production components for gradient boosting, automated causal estimation, and Bayesian updating replaceable
by transparent implementations that are contrasted against the production estimator where available. Five
principles organize the design, and each is enforced in code rather than recommended in documentation.
First, a single residualization kernel: the same cross-fitting function feeds the causal estimator, the
cross-elasticity system, and the pass-through deconvolution, so that no layer can diverge in folds, estimators,
or resampling policy. Second, a single temporal resampling policy across the entire system (contiguous
weekly blocks, moving-block bootstrap, chronological splits), because a single component that shuffles rows
contaminates with optimism everything that consumes it downstream. Third, labeled placeholders, never
disguised ones: where a quantity is provisional (for example, the long-run elasticity derived by a factor from
the short-run one), the artifact carries it with an explicit label of its computational basis, visible all the way
to the committee dashboard. Fourth, no undeclared control: every column admitted to the confounder set
carries its temporal status relative to the pricing decision, and the estimation refuses to run on an undeclared
one; this is the same discipline of declaring estimands in Section 4.1, applied at the level of the individual
variable.
Fifth, stated as its own principle because its violation was found in a place where the rest of the system had
already got it right, one effective unit of inference: the number of observations that enters any statistic
whose distribution depends on a sample size is the number of independent periods, never the number of panel
rows. Six regions observed in the same week are one draw from the weekly shock, and treating them as
six inflates degrees of freedom, deflates standard errors, and makes tests liberal. The principle was already
respected by the bootstrap, which clusters on the week, and by the break tests, which aggregate to the week
61

before testing. However, it was violated by both the robustness value and the first-stage F statistic of the
instrumental contrast, each of which consumed a raw row count and thereby rendered the weak-instrument
screens (Guards 4 and 7) effectively non-binding (Appendix B.11). That both instances survived a build in
which every other component got the unit right is the strongest evidence for stating the requirement as a
principle rather than an ad-hoc, per-component correction. It is the concrete form that the paper’s general
thesis takes inside its own implementation: the failure that survived a careful build was a tacit assumption
about the unit of analysis, not an error of estimation.

B.15

The QA Gate: the system halts, it does not degrade

A single checkpoint operationalizes the first layer of the validation protocol before the decision layer and
the artifacts consume the model outputs. The system halts with an explicit exception (it does not degrade
silently) if the out-of-fold fit of the demand model falls below 0.65 on the modelable set, or if mean conformal
coverage falls below the nominal level minus a tolerance of 0.03, which at α = 0.10 places the floor at 0.87.
The fit is computed out of fold on temporal blocks, since evaluating it on shuffled folds would exploit the
serial leakage the gate exists to police. The nuance matters: the gate is evaluated on the modelable set; that
presentations diagnosed as non-modelable fail thresholds is the desired behavior of the routing, not a failure.
The same control protects, as a decorator, the artifact-construction stage: no curves are published from a
model that did not pass health.
One deliberate correction to that design is proposed here and stated as such, because the gate as built
answers one question and is asked to guarantee two. The out-of-fold fit of a log-volume model is dominated
by seasonality and level, not by price: a demand model can clear 0.65 comfortably while carrying almost
no identifying price variation, so a fit threshold protects the conformal band (which predicts volume) but
does not protect the elasticity (which requires the treatment to be identified). The quantity that protects
the second is already computed and already used, in the admissibility gate of Appendix B.3: the partial R2
of the treatment after residualization, with its cutoff at 0.10. The gate is therefore best presented as two
thresholds answering two questions: predictive health for the band, and effective identification for the
slope. The system currently reports the first and applies the second without naming it, and naming it costs
nothing and closes a real gap in the argument.

B.16

Versioned artifacts

The deliverable is not a table: it is an auditable repository per cycle. The lookup table discretizes the
±20% operating range into 1% steps (41 rows) with three non-negotiable rules: the percentage is applied once
on the base price (in log-log, tranches compose, they do not add, and a tranche-by-tranche chaining would
be misleading), each row stores the volume effect against the base as the single decision column, and each
row declares whether it falls within the observed support, computed per region and taking the intersection,
because pooled support across regions with different levels declares “inside” tranches that no region ever
traversed. When an asymmetric estimator is in use the table exposes the effect band per direction; in the
configuration validated here it is not, so each row carries a single band (Appendix C.6). It is accompanied by
the elasticity curves by tranche, whose level is driven by the decision-grade elasticity of Appendix B.5
and whose confidence score is continuous in the weight of the cycle’s own data, the cross-elasticity matrix
in long format, and the dashboard feed under schema validation with rollback; the data contract is sacred:
a change of key or unit silently corrupts the downstream reporting. Everything is persisted versioned by cycle
and presentation, with quality flags, confidence intervals, and conformal intervals. To these are added the
versioned elasticity history per cycle and the versioned sequential state of the pooling layer, which together
materialize the validity governance of Appendix B.11, and a versioned report payload persisted centrally
that composes the already-computed outputs (it re-estimates nothing) into two summary views, one for data
science and one for the pricing committee.

B.18

Four-layer validation protocol

Validation follows four layers of robustness. The statistical layer requires significance, a negative sign on the
own elasticity, dominance of the own elasticity over the cross ones, and the QA Gate fit threshold. The sign
requirement carries a selection cost that the system measures rather than assumes away: retaining estimates
conditional on their own realization truncates the published distribution, and that truncation composes with
the admissibility gate and with the shrinkage, so the elasticities a committee sees are filtered at three points.
Appendix C.5 quantifies the displacement against known truth and states the condition under which the
filter should be replaced by an a priori criterion. The commercial sanity layer confronts the results
with category managers: the estimated substitution hierarchy must match the consumer decision tree the
business knows. The back-testing layer introduces a deliberate methodological correction: it decouples
prediction (base volume, evaluated via conformalized quantile regression with out-of-time coverage and a
target percentage error below 15%) from inference (the stability of the elasticity between the training window
and the full panel), because a single direct predictive model conflates both questions and answers both poorly.
The stress layer verifies the system’s behavior at the extremes: competitor tending to infinity (the corridor
shifts but the optimal price stays bounded by margin and volume), a 20% cost shock, and a price war with
no destructive recommendations.

B.19

The experimental layer: holdouts, synthetic controls, and contamination

The four layers above interrogate the estimate; none of them can establish that an executed price change
caused the outcome that followed it. That requires a comparison group, which the system implements directly
rather than merely prescribing. Three design decisions define the protocol. First, the retained regions are
drawn by a deterministic function of the cycle identifier, not resampled at each run: the same cycle
always yields the same holdout, and the assignment remains auditable months later, a property that matters
more for governance than for statistics. Second, the counterfactual is not a raw group average but a synthetic
control with unit weights: the retained regions are reweighted, with non-negative weights summing to one,
to reproduce the pre-treatment trajectory of the treated group, following Abadie et al. [2010], and the quality
of that pre-treatment fit is reported alongside the effect, without which the estimate is not interpretable. The
name is chosen precisely: the estimator carries unit weights only, without the time weights and the intercept
term that define the synthetic difference-in-differences of Arkhangelsky et al. [2021], which is specified as the
natural upgrade of this layer rather than claimed as its current content.
Inference for that estimator is supplied by permutation, which the design makes natural. With six regions
the sampling distribution of the effect is not available analytically, but the assignment mechanism is a
deterministic hash of the cycle identifier, so re-drawing it under alternative seeds enumerates the assignments
that could have occurred and yields the distribution of the placebo effect under the null of no intervention. The
protocol reports the observed effect against that distribution as a permutation p-value, which is what converts
a placebo departure from zero into evidence of an unbalanced assignment rather than an impression of one.
Third, the protocol tests its own key assumption. Synthetic controls presume the donor pool is unaffected
by the intervention (the no-interference half of SUTVA; Rubin, 1980), an assumption a competitor who
reads regional price movements will violate by construction; the system therefore compares the competitor’s
price movement across treated and retained regions after the intervention. The threshold for declaring
contamination is not chosen from outside: it is the placebo of the panel itself, the same computation
performed on false cuts inside the pre-period, which supplies the natural noise scale of these data, augmented
with a minimum economic magnitude of 0.005 in log price, without which the test fires on differences of
thousandths.
The construction of that noise scale is upgraded here from a single comparison to a test. Contrasting the
observed discrepancy against one false cut and a multiplicative factor makes the verdict depend on where
the single cut happened to fall; sweeping the pre-period yields a distribution of placebo discrepancies and
therefore a permutation p-value, with the economic floor retained as a declared minimum magnitude below
which no discrepancy is acted on regardless of significance. The two conditions remain complementary for

63

the reason they were both introduced: the placebo supplies a noise scale but no notion of materiality, and a
fixed threshold supplies materiality but is arbitrary across panels.
Two properties of this layer deserve emphasis because they are easy to lose. Absent a real intervention the
effect this block returns is a placebo and should be near zero; a departure from zero indicates that the holdout
assignment is not balanced for the panel at hand, which is a design defect worth discovering before a pilot
rather than during one. At six regions, over 300 replications, the placebo effect averages 0.081 in log volume
(Appendix C.9), and the assignment is exhaustively enumerated rather than drawn at a single seed, so this
is a rate rather than one realization. The assignment is balanced on this panel. One quantity published
alongside it bounds what that reassurance is worth: the pre-treatment fit of the synthetic control has a root
mean squared error of 0.189 in log volume, roughly 2.34 times the placebo effect it is being used to certify. A
comparison group that cannot reproduce the treated trajectory before the intervention cannot license an
inference about what follows it, so the correct reading of this block is that the assignment shows no imbalance
the layer is powerful enough to see, rather than that it shows balance.
The resolution of that p-value is bounded by the panel and the bound is declared, since a reader is entitled to
know the smallest value the test can return before reading the value it did return. With |R| = 6 regions

Because that is a property of the panel width, it is testable by varying it, and the run does. Appendix C.9

C.1

Sell-in against sell-out under injected forward buying

The generator inflates sell-in by 30% in the two weeks before a list increase while leaving sell-out untouched,
which makes the cost of the wrong outcome variable directly measurable rather than merely arguable. Over
50 replications, estimating θ on sell-in volume returns −1.025 against the injected list-price elasticity of
−1.105 in the clean regime; estimating it on sell-out returns −0.937, and the gap between them is −0.088.
The gap is not merely small, it carries the sign opposite to the one the mechanism predicts: the contaminated
series is the closer of the two to the truth, and not by a small margin: its bias is 0.080 against 0.168 for the
clean one, less than half as large.
This experiment is reported as a null result, and the distinction between the mechanism and its magnitude
is the whole of what it establishes. The mechanism is not in doubt: a purchase pulled forward into the weeks
before an increase inflates sell-in without any consumer having responded. What the generator shows is that
the injected contamination does not displace θ̂ materially on this panel, and the reason is structural: the
inflation is confined to a two-week window before increases above 2%, those events are few in a hundred
and twenty weeks, and the residualization against W absorbs a share of a contamination that is itself
a deterministic function of an observable price movement. The choice of sell-out over sell-in retains its
argumentative support, through the reversal of forward buying after a month-end push (Appendix B.12), and
loses its quantitative one here.

C.2

The structural-break guard: size, critical values, and power

A test’s size says nothing about its ability to expire an estimate, and the three layers of protection against
false positives described in Appendix B.11 are statements about size alone. The run measures both, and it
does so twice, because the first measurement diagnosed a configuration defect and the second asks whether
repairing it recovers a usable guard.
The size failure, and its cause. With the asymptotic critical value of 1.358 on residuals from the boosted
nuisance learner, the union of the two tests rejects at 0.94 with no break injected, over 31 replications:
the CUSUM alone at 0.71 and the Bonferroni-corrected Chow family at 0.84. The condition was declared
in advance, the guard does not pass it, and the planned power figure is withdrawn: a detection rate that
begins at 0.94 under the null cannot be crossed by a power criterion, and the aggregator’s value of zero for
the smallest detected break is the arithmetic of a threshold already exceeded at the origin rather than a
measurement. The failure traces to a low-frequency component surviving residualization: the learner matrix
of Appendix C.3 confirms the mechanism directly, with the false-positive rate of the CUSUM under no
break at 0.810 on residuals from the boosted learner, 0.619 from a histogram-based one, 0.476 from a radial
basis kernel, and exactly 0.000 from the lasso, ridge and sieve learners, whose residuals pass an augmented
Dickey–Fuller test at t ≈ −4.81. The size failure is a property of the residual the guard is fed, not of the tests.
Repairing the critical value does not repair the guard. Table D.7 reports the second measurement,
over four residual paths crossing two learners with and without explicit detrending, at 14 replications per cell
and 672 evaluations in total. Calibrating the critical value by simulation under the null, rather than taking it
from the asymptotic distribution, moves the boosted learner’s size from 0.607 to 0.035 at a calibrated critical
of 1.720, itself above the asymptotic 1.358; the other three paths already sit at zero size under the asymptotic
critical, and calibration leaves them between 0.000 and 0.107, at calibrated criticals of 0.733 to 1.123, all
below the asymptotic value. The asymptotic critical is therefore mis-specified in both directions: too low for
the boosted learner’s residuals and too high for the other three.

C.3

Nuisance learners: a trade-off, not a dominance

Two panels test whether the production nuisance learner, a gradient-boosted tree, is dominated by a regularized
alternative carrying seasonal harmonics and an explicit trend, and the two panels do not agree with each
other, which is itself the finding.
On the five-regime generator, over 38 replications, the regularized learner dominates: bias 0.080 against
−0.003, root mean squared error 0.173 against 0.091, and partial R2 of the treatment 0.446 against 0.652,
with the fit of the treatment nuisance itself nearly equal at 0.349 against 0.361. That near-equality is the
informative part: the two learners explain almost the same share of the treatment and differ almost entirely in
which share, the boosted tree leaving a low-frequency component in the residual that the regularized learner
removes by construction.
On the extended generator, over 42 replications, the ordering does not carry over. Table D.6 reports six
learners on that panel. The paired contrast in absolute error between the best-performing rival, a radial
basis kernel, and the production learner is −0.0185 with a standard error of 0.0615 (t = −0.30, n = 42): the
rival’s error is nominally smaller but the contrast is not distinguishable from noise. No rival dominates the
production learner on this panel, and the production learner does not dominate either.
The table settles a question the five-regime panel could not raise on its own. No single learner wins on
all three quantities that matter. The radial basis kernel has both the lowest bias, at −0.009, and the best
coverage, at 0.690; the boosted learner has the lowest root mean squared error, at 0.457. The choice is a
trade-off between two learners rather than a search for a dominant one, and the paper declares it as such.
The column that connects this table to the rest of the paper is the last one: the false-positive rate of the
break test under each learner, which is exactly 0.000 for the three linear learners (lasso, ridge, sieve), 0.476
for the radial basis kernel, 0.619 for the histogram-based learner, and 0.810 for the boosted one, and which
locates the size failure of Appendix C.2 in the choice of learner rather than in the tests. A reader weighing
bias, coverage and error against each other should weigh that column too: the learner with the lowest root
mean squared error is the one that breaks the break test.
The paper’s organizing claim survives both panels and is sharpened by the pair. The claim is that the value
of the system does not lie in the sophistication of its estimator. What the two contrasts show together is
stronger: which estimator is better is not stable across panels that differ only in how pass-through is drawn,

C.4

What the cross-fitting scheme costs

Block cross-fitting predicts each block from other blocks, past and future, which relies on weak temporal
dependence under stationarity. Injecting a break makes the cost of that tolerance measurable: under a
break at week 80 that moves θ from −1.105 to −0.510, the scheme returns θ̂ = −0.705 against −0.769 under
forward-chaining, over 38 replications. The difference is 0.063.
This is the second null of the block, and it is a narrower null than it looks. What the contrast establishes is
that using future blocks to fit the nuisance models costs nothing detectable here; what it does not establish
is that either estimator is doing well. Both return roughly −0.74 against a panel whose true θ is −1.105
before week 80 and −0.510 after it, so both are recovering a blend of two regimes rather than either one,
and they blend it almost identically. The cost of the tolerance is nil because the tolerance is not what binds:
the binding problem under a break is that a single elasticity is being fitted to two, and no partition of the
folds repairs that. The implied policy, re-estimating within the regime that follows the most recent break, is
unchanged and blocked at a different point: Appendix C.2 reports that no configuration tested locates the
boundary.

C.5

The sign filter and its replacement

The statistical validation layer of Appendix B.18 retains only estimates that are significant and negative,
and that filter composes with the admissibility gate and with the shrinkage, so the published distribution of
elasticities is truncated at more than one point. The measurement is made on a low-variation configuration
where the filter bites, with four movements of 1.0 to 2.5% at a consumer elasticity of −1.0, yielding θ = −0.85.
Over 125 replications the filter retains 0.51 of the estimates and the distribution of retained θ̂ sits at −0.682
against a mean of −0.452 over all estimates, so the published mean is displaced from the truth by 0.168 while
the unfiltered mean is displaced by 0.398.
The underlying distribution is attenuated before any filtering occurs: the filter discards the estimates nearest
zero, and two errors partially cancel, which is why the filtered displacement looks smaller than the unfiltered
one despite the filter selecting on the estimate’s own realization. A second experiment contrasts the sign
filter against a priori criteria that do not condition on the estimate’s own realization, over 25 replications on
the same configuration. Under the boosted learner, retaining on the partial R2 keeps 0.96 of estimates and
moves the published mean by 0.022 against the unfiltered mean; adding a variation criterion changes nothing
further; the sign filter keeps 0.20 and moves it by −1.293. The selection bias of the a priori criterion is smaller
than that of the sign filter by a factor of roughly sixty, and it is small for a structural reason rather than by
luck: a criterion evaluated before the estimate is realized cannot truncate the distribution of that estimate.
Replacing the sign filter with the a priori criterion is the change this section makes, and what the a priori
criterion exposes is the attenuation the sign filter had been concealing: with the a priori criterion in place
the published mean sits at 0.037 against a truth of −0.857, a displacement of 0.894, against an unfiltered
displacement of 0.872 on this configuration – nearly the same, because a criterion evaluated before the
estimate is realized retains almost all of it (0.96) rather than the sign filter’s 0.20. Removing a selection rule
that flattered the output is the correct change and it makes the output look worse, which is the expected
consequence of removing a selection rule that flattered the output.

C.6

The Dual Beta is retired

The asymmetric-response estimator carries an unresolved marker in its specification: Appendix A.3 specifies
a decomposition on the deflated price while the validation below exercises it on the nominal one as well, so
the choice of which the production implementation should run is not yet settled by specification alone. This
section settles it by retiring the estimator rather than by choosing between the two specifications.
Over 28 replications per cell the experiment injects an asymmetry of zero and then of 0.25, and runs both

67

specifications against each. With no asymmetry injected the detection rate is the estimator’s size: 0.321 on
the deflated specification and 0.286 on the nominal. With an asymmetry injected that implies a true gap of
0.557, the detection rate is its power: 0.357 deflated and 0.321 nominal. Power exceeds size by 0.036 and
0.035 respectively. The estimator declares an asymmetry in roughly a third of panels that contain none, and it
is barely more likely to declare one when an asymmetry is present than when it is absent. The estimated gap
under the null is 0.262 on the deflated specification and 0.237 on the nominal, against mean interval widths
of 1.311 and 1.389; the event counts and collinearity are not what limit it on the deflated specification, at
24.5 upward and 25.4 downward events on average against a declared minimum of 20 and a variance inflation
factor of 1.26, though the nominal specification’s event counts split far more unevenly between directions, at
99.1 upward against 32.4 downward, and still returns the same verdict.
That the two specifications return the same verdict is what settles the question the specification left open.
Which of the two the implementation runs is not a question the paper needs to resolve, because resolving it
would not rescue an estimator whose power and size are indistinguishable on this panel. The Dual Beta is
therefore withdrawn from the published output on this evidence. The lookup table loses its per-direction
effect band and carries the symmetric response only; the asymmetry ratio that scaled it in the pooling layer
is removed with it; and a noise floor of roughly 0.25 is recorded as the smallest gap a panel of this length and
event count could establish, which is larger than any asymmetry a committee would act on. The architecture
remains specified in Appendix A.3 for a panel that can support it, and it is not run on one that cannot.
A further experiment asks what kind of panel would support the estimator rather than retire it outright.
Over 300 replications, running the identical decomposition against a design where shocks are blocked and
of fixed magnitude, half increases and half decreases, one per temporal block, rather than drawn from the
observational panel, changes the picture entirely: the size falls from 0.353 under the observational design
to 0.027 under the blocked design, and at an injected asymmetry the blocked design recovers 99.6% of the
true gap against 53.9% for the observational one, with detection power of 0.797 against 0.297. The variance
inflation factor moves the other way, from 1.14 to 3.10, because blocking the shock’s timing correlates the upand down-movement regressors more tightly; the recovery gain dominates that cost. The estimator’s problem
was therefore never the decomposition itself, which recovers an injected asymmetry cleanly once the design
assigns shocks rather than merely observing them; it is the observational panel’s shock timing, which the
retirement in this section leaves to a genuinely randomized or blocked price experiment to supply.

C.7

Two nulls that were re-run under conditions designed to break them

Two of the system’s layers return a null result on the production generator, and a null whose only support
is that nothing happened on one generator is weak evidence on its own. Both are therefore also run on
generators built specifically to produce the effect they otherwise fail to find.
Pass-through as a source of decision-relevant variance. The system reports pass-through as a process;
on the production generator its contribution to the variance of the published response band never rises above
a fraction of a percent, which makes the elaborate treatment hard to justify on that panel alone. Injecting
the heterogeneity whose absence is the obvious explanation, a ρ that varies across regions with a standard
deviation of 0.1715, an order of magnitude above the bootstrap standard error of 0.0156, and in one scenario a
ρ that depends on the shopkeeper’s margin state, does not move the attribution. At a 20% list movement the
pass-through share of the response band’s variance is 0.0059 under state-dependent heterogeneity and 0.0007
under regional heterogeneity alone; at 10% it is 0.0016 and 0.0002. The null holds under conditions designed
to break it, a considerably stronger result than a null measured on the production panel alone, and the
implication for the artifact is to publish a single pass-through number with its regime classification rather than
a variance decomposition that attributes nothing. The estimator’s own recovery is worse under the injected
heterogeneity than on the unperturbed panel: a bias of −0.048 under state-dependent heterogeneity and
−0.015 under regional heterogeneity alone, against −0.007 on the homogeneous baseline, over 35 replications
per scenario – still small relative to a true ρ near 0.85, but not the flat recovery a single-scenario report would
suggest.

68

The cash-pressure screen. The screening test for a liquidity constraint returns no statistic on the
production panel: it executes in all thirteen replications and the t is non-finite in every one. This is not a
defect of the test. The dependent variable is portfolio breadth, the count of presentations with a positive
order in a region-week, and on a generator where every presentation is ordered every week that count is
constant: its standard deviation is exactly 0, the regression has nothing to explain, and a non-finite t is the
correct output. Re-running with order refusal injected gives the variable variation, and the test then works:
in the weak-refusal scenario, over 5 replications, the breadth has a standard deviation of 1.755 and the screen
passes in every replication at a mean |t| of 45.58. That figure is not evidence of power and is not presented as
such, because breadth is generated as a direct function of the index in these scenarios, so the test recovers a
relation that was imposed. The informative cell is the one with regional income variation and no refusal:
there the breadth is again constant, the t is −0.5, and the screen correctly declines to admit the index. The
screen is therefore operative and correctly calibrated, and what the production panel lacks is the phenomenon
rather than the instrument.

C.8

The demand system, with inference

The cross-elasticity layer travels with standard errors from a block bootstrap specified in Appendix A.9,
run jointly over the residualization and the demand stage, over 8 replications on eight presentations, so its
theoretical restrictions are tested against a null rather than merely contrasted against the dispersion of the
estimated coefficients, and the layer can be reported as a demand system rather than as a matrix.
The matrix carries statistical content, but less than the validation layer requires: only 18.5% of the cross
coefficients have |t| > 1.96 against bootstrap standard errors, short of the declared minimum of 20%. The
mean own-price coefficient is −4.167 against a mean absolute cross coefficient of 0.586, so the own effects
still dominate in magnitude, and the smallest revenue share is 0.122, which bounds the amplification the
Marshallian conversion applies to the noisiest row.
Table D.12 reports the three restrictions against their null distributions. Adding-up is satisfied almost exactly,
at a mean violation of 0.0000 and a Wald statistic of 0.160 on one degree of freedom (p = 0.759), rejected
in none of the replications: this is the restriction the estimator imposes by construction, and its behaviour
confirms the bootstrap is doing what it claims. Homogeneity carries a mean violation of 0.856 with a Wald
statistic of 21.39 on seven degrees of freedom (p = 0.079), rejected at the 5% level in 0.625 of replications.
Symmetry carries a mean violation of 0.241, a Wald statistic of 59.60 on twenty-eight degrees of freedom
(p = 0.133), rejected in 0.625. Both are rejected in most of the eight replications, and the honest reading is
that on this panel the demand system does not satisfy the restrictions economic theory places on it. The
published matrix therefore travels with its bootstrap standard errors, with the three Wald statistics, and with
the statement that two of the three restrictions are rejected in most replications and that the cross-coefficient
significance rate itself falls short of the validation layer’s own bar.

C.10

Implementation challenges, governance and the pilot

Three challenges dominated the implementation. The first is the internal coherence of the system itself: in
a system with resampling at multiple points, a single component that shuffles rows (an i.i.d. bootstrap, a
random K-fold, a conformal bridge that mixes residuals) suffices for optimism to leak into everything that
consumes its outputs; the audit that unified the temporal resampling policy across all components was as
valuable as any estimator improvement. The second is the discipline of declaring estimands: the difference
between the competitor-conditional elasticity and the equilibrium response, or between the list elasticity
and the consumer elasticity, is invisible in a report that only shows “the elasticity,” and making it explicit
(with labels traveling all the way to the dashboard) changed committee conversations that were previously
inconclusive.
One finding emerged only from executing the newly instrumented controls, and it argues for building such
controls before they are needed rather than when they are. The false-positive rate of the exact permutation
test is mechanically zero at six regions, an arithmetic consequence of the combinatorial floor rather than
evidence the test is well calibrated, and only becomes informative once the panel widens enough for the floor
to stop binding (Appendix C.9); a team that read the six-region number at face value could mistake the
test for conservative when it is merely unable to reject anything. Sizing a holdout against the measured
resolution in Table D.13, rather than assuming a construction usable at thirty regions is equally usable at six,
is the operational lesson, and it is one that only surfaces by running the width sweep rather than a single
configuration.
The third challenge is organizational, and the validation run has changed what it requires. Planners accustomed
to experience-based decisions rightly distrust recommendations they cannot interrogate, a reluctance that
intensifies once the model has been seen to err [Dietvorst et al., 2015]. The design answer was the same one
the enterprise-systems literature reports [Llenas et al., 2026]: transparency and interactivity (scenarios, visible
bands, verdicts with readable reasons, and the ability to override while seeing the quantified consequences),
which is precisely the lever experimental work identifies as restoring willingness to use an imperfect model
[Dietvorst et al., 2018], and, distinctively here, explicit abstention. The conversation a committee must
be prepared for is harder than a simple decline-to-recommend framing suggests. It is not “the system will
sometimes decline to recommend” but “the system cannot, on the evidence this channel supplies, publish a
per-presentation elasticity with an interval narrow enough to act on, and here is the level at which it can.” A
system that says the first earns trust; a system that says the second earns it and changes the operating unit,
which is a larger organizational ask and the honest one.

C.11

From artifact to process: governance, adoption, and expected impact

A critical design factor is that the artifacts feed a human-in-the-loop operating process, not an autopilot.
The target flow chains a periodic trigger at fixed cadence, the data system, the recommendation layer with
its corridor, commercial validation with a documented override, the committee with minutes linked to the
analysis, and execution with ex-post measurement. Governance assigns explicit roles (the RGM analyst
operates the application and documents exceptions; the pricing manager approves and owns the cycle; the
committee decides) and typifies valid overrides (strategic, commercial, model-based, always documented)
against the single invalid one: “we have always done it this way.” To these is added the cross-system convention
of Appendix B.12: each alternative submitted to the committee declares its expected cost on the adjacent
decision systems, so that conflicts between models are adjudicated on the same sheet as the price.
One governance risk is created by the system’s own discipline and should be anticipated rather than discovered.
An engine designed to halt rather than degrade will return WAIT for a substantial share of a real portfolio,
which is the intended behavior, and which reads to a committee expecting a price list as a malfunction. The
pressure that follows is predictable in any organization: relax a threshold for one category, waive a guard
for a strategic presentation, publish the midpoint because a number is required. The countermeasure is not
technical. It is to publish an explicit degraded mode (“no recommendation, reason, date of next review”)
rather than silence, and to accompany every abstention with the decision arithmetic of Appendix B.9, so that
holding is legible as discipline rather than as incapacity.
A second governance item follows directly from this validation, and it is the more consequential of the two.
Two of the system’s calibrations, the admissibility width threshold of 0.6 and the construction of the interval
it reads, were set independently, and Section 6.5 reports that, across the levels of aggregation tested, intervals
wide enough to cover tend to run one and a half to three and a half times wider than the threshold admits.
A committee cannot be asked to adjudicate that as two separate switches. It is one decision, and it has
three defensible resolutions, each of which the committee should see costed: publish at a coarser unit, where
the interval is narrowest; publish per presentation with an interval that does not cover, and say so on the
artifact; or supplement the observational panel with experimental variation, the path Appendix B.19 develops
empirically and companion methodological work takes further. The first is available now and the second is
what the system does today without declaring it. Presenting those options with numbers attached is what
this validation makes possible and what a recalibration exercise should start from.
The ex-post loop remains the most valuable asset of the system. Every real price change is a natural
experiment: measuring with discipline the actual versus predicted volume at 6–12 weeks and feeding that
data back into the next cycle’s training set is what makes the system improve on its own; if that loop is
not closed, everything else degrades silently. The residual uncertainty this validation isolates behaves like
dispersion across designs rather than sampling noise within one, a distinction companion methodological
work [Delgado, 2026] develops formally; if that description is right, better estimation of the same panel would
narrow it only partially, and generating new designs would narrow it further.
For the pilot that would follow this work, a set of proposed success criteria is stated in advance rather
than reported as results, since none of them has been observed and the distinction between a target and
a measurement is exactly the kind of slippage this paper argues against. They are staged across process
(full coverage of priority items with a recommended price by month six; fewer than five days from trigger
to deliverable by month four), decision (100% documented overrides by month three; concordance between
the approved and recommended price above 0.80 by month twelve), model (forecast error below 15% by
month nine, together with the QA Gate’s statistical thresholds), and business (a revenue increase of 2–5%
by month nine with no share deterioration). The provenance of that range is stated exactly, because it is a
target and not an estimate: the classic evidence establishes that price carries high leverage on profit [Marn
and Rosiello, 1992] and the documented deployments establish that analytics-enabled pricing has delivered
material gains at scale [Deng et al., 2023, Llenas et al., 2026], but neither supplies an expected uplift for
this system in this channel. The 2–5% figure is a commercial ambition set by the sponsoring organization,
and it is recorded here as the bar the pilot will be judged against rather than as a forecast the evidence
supports. One further criterion is added by the run: rather than assume a holdout of any size is adequate,
size it against the experimental layer’s own measured resolution, for which Table D.13 gives a concrete range

to plan against, between twelve and thirty independently assigned units in this panel. The roadmap is
otherwise incremental by design: a directional phase of contribution and communication, the phase this work
documents, the scale-up of the multi-item portfolio optimizer, and only at the end dynamic pricing.

from six to thirty regions. Two regions are treated at every width, and the assignment is always enumerated
