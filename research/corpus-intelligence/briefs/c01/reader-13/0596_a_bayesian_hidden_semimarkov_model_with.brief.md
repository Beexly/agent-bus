# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0596-a-bayesian-hidden-semimarkov-model-with.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A Bayesian hidden semi-Markov model (Rojas-Salazar, Schliep, Wikle & Hawkey, 2020) with two innovations: state duration parameters regressed on covariates observed before each transition, and random within-MCMC data subsampling to restore nominal coverage when high-frequency observations violate conditional independence — demonstrated on wearable heart-rate data from an MLS referee. Verdict in the file: ADAPT — not the wearable application, but the covariate-dependent duration regression (regime-duration modeling) and the subsampling-for-coverage fix, which apply to autocorrelated sports time series.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- HSMM: segments q=1…Q with states S_q, durations τ_q ~ zero-truncated Poisson h_{S_q}(τ_q|φ_{S_q}); Gaussian emissions y ~ N(μ_{S_q}, σ²_{S_q}); Markov transitions P between segments.
- Duration regression (4): φ_{S_{q+1}}(X_{1:T_q}, β_{S_{q+1}}) = g(β_{0,S_{q+1}} + β_{1,S_{q+1}} f_1(x_{1,1:T_q}) + … ), g ensuring positivity.
- Application form (7): φ = exp[β_0 + β_1 x̃_{1,T_q} + β_2 x_{2,T_q} + β_3 x_{3,T_q} + β_4 h_{T_{q+1}} + β_5(x̃_1·h) + β_6(x_2·h) + β_7(x_3·h)], with 20-s pre-transition acceleration average x̃_1, cumulative distance x_2, distance-from-center x_3, second-half indicator h (8 coefficients per state).
- Inference: Metropolis-within-Gibbs (Gibbs for ρ, P rows, μ, σ²; random-walk Metropolis for duration coefficients β_j; Yu 2016 Viterbi extension to sample the state sequence); at each MCMC iteration a fresh random subsample of observations is used for emission updates (sampling rate tuned per application).
- Priors: μ ~ N(0,10000), σ² ~ IG(3,3), Dirichlet(1,…,1) for ρ and P rows, β ~ N(0,10000).
- State count selection: MPSRF ≤ 1.2 convergence diagnostic across 2–5-state chains (DIC always selected the largest model); 100,000 MCMC iterations (30,000 adaptive discarded, 70,000 retained).
- GSE implementation spec: port duration-regression HSMM to game-state regimes (pass-heaviness/tempo states from play-by-play), durations as zero-truncated Poisson regressed on pre-transition covariates (score differential, time remaining, timeouts, weather, QB); fit nflverse 2018–2025 with subsampling rate tuned by segment-scale autocorrelation (paper's Table B.2 procedure); use for expected-regime-duration forecasts as live-model features.
## Data sources named
Catapult minimax wearable data, one MLS referee, one 2017 game: raw 10 Hz ~150 min, truncated to 90-min game, thinned to 1 Hz (5,400 time points); covariates: instantaneous heart rate (mean 150 bpm), acceleration (mean 0.03 m/s², range −5.97 to 8.08), cumulative distance (~10 km), distance from field center (0.4–47.4 m, mean 19 m). Simulation: AR(1)-correlated Gaussian emissions, M ∈ {2,3,4} states, ψ ∈ {0.3, 0.6, 0.86, 0.95}, n ≈ 1000/2600/4000, 45 scenarios × 100 realizations. GSE-side target data named: nflverse play-by-play 2018–2025 (drives as segments).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Simulation (n≈4000): with autocorrelation ψ=0.86 and no subsampling, empirical coverage of 90% CIs collapses to 35–38% (M=2: 36%; M=3: 35%; M=4: 38%); at ψ=0.95, 24–28%. Subsampling restores coverage: at ψ=0.86, 20% sampling → 82–86%, 10% → 94–96%; at ψ=0.3, 70% sampling already ~nominal. Consistent across M=2,3,4 and n=1000/2600/4000.
- Application (3 states, 15% subsampling rate matched to observed autocorrelation 0.83–0.89): emission means S1=133.6 (132.3,134.9) var 59.7; S2=150.5 (149.8,151.1) var 14.2; S3=165 (164,166) var 28.1 — non-overlapping intervals (mild/moderate/strenuous).
- Transitions: 1→2 0.91 (0.81,0.98), 2→1 0.56 (0.45,0.68), 2→3 0.44 (0.32,0.55), 3→2 0.97 (0.88,1) — adjacent-state moves dominate (cardiac lag). Segments: 41/69/32; mean duration parameters S1=45.9 (24.1–121), S2=27.8 (11.6–70.4), S3=48.1 (15.2–114.0).
- Duration coefficients (Table 4, significant): 1st half — distance traveled lengthens S1 (0.21 [0.12,0.31]), shortens S2 (−0.27 [−0.36,−0.19]) and S3 (−0.64 [−0.74,−0.55]); large decelerations lengthen S3 (−0.74 [−1.32,−0.23]); distance from center shortens S1 (−0.11) and S3 (−0.09). 2nd half — distance traveled lengthens S1 (0.09 [0.02,0.17]) and S2 (0.34 [0.21,0.49]); large accelerations shorten S2 (−1.85 [−2.55,−1.18]); distance from center lengthens S1 (0.34 [0.26,0.42]) and shortens S2/S3.
- Limitations: single referee, single game (no replication); zero-truncated Poisson durations are a strong parametric choice (no NB comparison); subsampling effect on duration/transition inference not studied; no predictive validation.
