## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- American football is "structurally the hardest mainstream sport for CV": 22 players, coordinated movement from standing start, LOS pileups ("densest occlusion scenario in mainstream sport"), helmets defeating face-based re-ID, route/formation classification requiring strategy understanding. No open equivalent of SoccerNet exists for NFL broadcast (labeled routes/formations/play boundaries from TV footage); NFL's own tracking is RFID/Zebra (Next Gen Stats) — positional, not tactical. CV is the layer that adds formation/route/blocking classification, and that labeled data is the moat.
- Expired patent ideas G1/G2/G4 (field-anchored telestration, classical prefilter, vanishing-point calibration) describe precisely the architecture the 2026 field converged on — the patents' value is as a design spec, not IP.
- Automated 2–4 s field-anchored clips and play segmentation are feasible internal tooling; full automated charting (formations, routes, personnel) from broadcast remains research-grade without a labeled dataset — and that dataset, built as a byproduct of the content pipeline, is the actual long-term asset.
- Bettors care about verified records (Betstamp's verified-capper social layer used by handicappers on Whop; Action's expert tracking), NOT demonstrably about the cryptographic mechanism — no evidence bettors choose a capper over hash chains vs. app-enforced immutability.
- Trust play for GSE: "tamper-evident public record" differentiates against self-reporting touts, but Betstamp already occupies "can't be edited or deleted" as a shipping feature; hash-chained daily digests are cheap to build and make the record provably unedited — brand infrastructure, not a marketing claim; market it as "independently verifiable record," not "blockchain."
- Public/private doctrine check: nothing in the doc contradicts it — all GSE items remain internal capability; the pick-trail is the one legitimate public artifact (it publishes outcomes, which the doctrine explicitly allows).
