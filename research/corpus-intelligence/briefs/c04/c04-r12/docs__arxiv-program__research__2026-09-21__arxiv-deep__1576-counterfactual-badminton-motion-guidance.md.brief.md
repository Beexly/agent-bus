# docs/arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1576-counterfactual-badminton-motion-guidance.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Ledger of arXiv:2405.11802 (Seong et al., MIT CSAIL/GIST 2024): LatentCF — latent-space counterfactual optimization that computes the minimal personalized joint-motion edit flipping a stroke-quality classifier to expert level, from wearable-sensor badminton data, visualized as a ghost-avatar overlay. Verdict in the ledger: ADAPT — a genuine causal-explanation method for motion data (morphological counterfactuals, complementing 1572's temporal counterfactuals), portable to NGS tracking for "what should the player have done differently" analysis.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Algorithm 1 (LatentCF): z ← AE-Encode(x); loop: y_pred ← C(AE-Decode(z)); loss ← CE(y_pred, y′); z ← Adam(z, loss, α) until y_pred ≥ τ or max_iter; decode x′.
- (1) Stroke-quality classifier on joint positions — Transformer selected over Conv1D/ConvLSTM/LSTM via 5-fold CV. (2) LatentCF explainer. (3) Unity visualizer overlaying CF motion as ghost avatar.
- CF quality metrics: validity (fraction achieving target class), proximity (L1/L2/L∞), plausibility (LOF, Isolation Forest, OCSVM), motion-specific: DTW closeness, Fréchet Pose Distance (FPD), Fréchet Motion Distance (FMD).
- Assumptions: autoencoder latent space smooth enough that gradient steps stay plausible; classifier boundary approximates true quality differences; minimal latent edit ≈ minimal biomechanical edit.
## Data sources named
MultiSenseBadminton (Seong et al., Scientific Data 2024): 7,763 swings from 25 players (12 beginners, 8 intermediate, 5 experts), 2 stroke types (forehand high clear, backhand drive); multimodal wearables (eye tracking, EMG, foot pressure, joint data); per-swing annotations: ball landing location, hit point, stroke quality. Paper uses joint global positions. No training-code link; demo video https://youtu.be/o7bDM5yRbtw.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Classifier 5-fold: forehand — Transformer acc 85.12 (1.62), BalAcc 85.67 (1.57), F1 85.22 (1.60) vs LSTM 84.29/84.84/84.40, ConvLSTM 80.83/78.41/79.74, Conv1D 79.97/76.82/78.94, majority baseline 59.17/50.00/44.01; backhand — Transformer 87.89 (0.89)/88.93 (0.76)/88.22 (0.87).
- LatentCF vs 1NN (forehand): validity 1.0 (all); L1 3.41 vs 5.43–5.54; L2 0.58 vs 0.91–0.92; L∞ 0.23 vs 0.34–0.35; LOF 0.36 vs 0.62–0.63; IF 0.91 vs 0.97–0.98; OCSVM 0.55 vs 0.68–0.69; DTW 3.39 vs 5.36–5.42; FPD 0.33 vs 0.69–0.71; FMD 1.02 vs 2.09–2.16. LatentCF wins on every proximity/plausibility/motion metric; backhand shows the same pattern.
- Ledger's limitations: only 5 experts (thin expert manifold); validity = 1.0 for all methods suggests an easy-to-satisfy classifier; no user study (deferred); no evidence athletes improve from ghost guidance.
