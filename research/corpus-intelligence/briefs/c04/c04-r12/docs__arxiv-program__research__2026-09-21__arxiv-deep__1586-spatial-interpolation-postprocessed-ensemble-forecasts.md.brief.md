# docs/arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1586-spatial-interpolation-postprocessed-ensemble-forecasts.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Ledger of arXiv:2609.07512 (Lakatos 2026), benchmarking statistical (EMOS-R/C/L, boosted EMOS) vs ML (DRN/MLP, GraphSAGE GNN, Transformer) post-processing methods for 50-member ECMWF 2-m temperature and 10-m wind-speed forecasts at observed vs unobserved (held-out) German weather stations, plus an altitude-aware linear pool (ALP) for forecast combination at new locations. Verdict in the ledger: ADAPT — the core transferability rule (best local model at observed stations is worst at unobserved; regional/semi-local/ML models transfer) applies directly to GSE's 30-stadium weather problem, and ALP offers altitude-aware combination at new venues.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- EMOS links: μ = a + b²f̄, σ = c² + d²S (Eq. 1), Gaussian (T2M) / truncated normal (WS), minimum-CRPS estimation; semi-local = k-means on climatology + error features (8–10 clusters). Boosted EMOS (R crch) for automatic feature selection.
- DRN (MLP), GraphSAGE GNN (Haversine ≤ 50 km edges, mean aggregation, fixed graph), Transformer (self-attention across stations, no fixed graph) — output Gaussian/TN parameters minimizing closed-form CRPS; 10 replications.
- SLP: F^(w) = wΦ((y−μ1)/σ1) + (1−w)Φ((y−μ2)/σ2), w by minimum-CRPS on 60-day rolling window. ALP: separate w per altitude group (<300 m, 300–700 m, ≥700 m), each unobserved station gets its group's weight: F_ALP,t,s(y) = ŵ_t,g(s)F_1,t,s(y) + (1−ŵ_t,g(s))F_2,t,s(y).
- Metrics: CRPSS, MAES, RMSES vs DRN baseline; coverage + width of 96.08% intervals; rank histograms + reliability index RI = Σ|ρ_r − 1/(K+1)|; Diebold–Mariano tests: t_N = √N(S̄_F − S̄_G)/σ̂.
## Data sources named
50-member ECMWF ensemble (TIGGE archive, 0.5° Europe), 48 h lead, daily 00 UTC; observations from DWD (Germany); 3 Jan 2007 – 31 Dec 2016; 99 temperature / 182 wind-speed stations with complete records; 70% observed (train), 30% unobserved (test). Predictor sets: non-extended (ensemble mean/SD + station metadata + day-of-year; 41 T2M / 46 WS variables in extended) vs extended (all Chen et al. 2024 covariates). No code link in the paper.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- All post-processing beats the raw ensemble by wide margins (raw coverage ~59% → 92–98%).
- T2M: EMOS-L best at observed (CRPSS 14.7 vs DRN) but worst at unobserved (negative skill, largest generalization gap); EMOS-R best at unobserved (lowest mean CRPS); Transformer the only ML model positive at unobserved on the non-extended set; extended set: Transformer best at observed, DRN/Transformer competitive at unobserved; GNN-Geo degrades badly at unobserved (oversmoothing; Feldberg at 1,490 m is a failure case — geographical graph ignores elevation).
- WS: EMOS-C best at unobserved (small but significant CRPSS; better on 59.2% of days, 86.0% with extended set); boosted EMOS wins in "difficult situations with larger raw ensemble errors"; EMOS-L again fails to transfer; GNN-Geo gets lowest RI on extended-set unobserved.
- Coverage (96.08% nominal): T2M EMOS-R unobserved 94.97%; WS unobserved: DRN 95.40% (non-extended), GNN-Geo 92.57% / EMOS-C 97.91% (extended); ML intervals sharper (width ~4.3–5.7 m/s vs EMOS ~6.3–6.5).
- ALP vs SLP (T2M): ALP best in all three component combinations at observed; lowest CRPS in 2/3 at unobserved; significantly beats SLP in all three at unobserved.
- Ledger notes: ALP tested only for temperature (TN-mixture CRPS has no closed form for wind speed — skipped); single model/country/lead time; no extreme-event analysis.
