# docs__arxiv-program__research__2026-09-21__arxiv-deep__0720-cost-sensitive-conformal-prediction-abstention
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Singh, Srikantha & Lakhanpal (2026), arXiv:2607.27143v1 — a 3,150-run benchmark showing standard marginal conformal prediction collapses on rare classes under imbalance (30.5% average minority coverage at a nominal 90% target) and that Mondrian (class-conditional) conformal with a cost-controlled abstention/deferral rule restores minority coverage and beats Bayes thresholding on expected cost, with a derived economic break-even for human review. Ledger verdict: ADAPT — directly operational for GSE's conformal lane and Garrett's manual-review economics.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Nonconformity score S(X,y) = 1 − P̂(Y=y|X). Marginal CP: single quantile q_marg → Ĉ_marg(X) = {y: S(X,y) ≤ q_marg}, global coverage ≥1−α. Mondrian CP: separate quantiles q_0, q_1 per class → Ĉ_Mondrian(X) = {y: S(X,y) ≤ q_y}, guaranteeing P(Y ∈ Ĉ | Y=y) ≥ 1−α per class.
- Action rule: singletons (|Ĉ|=1) auto-execute; multi-label (|Ĉ|=2) and empty sets abstain → human review.
- Cost matrix C = [[0, C_FP],[C_FN, 0]]; Bayes cost-tuned threshold τ* = C_FP/(C_FN + C_FP). Noisy-human model: deferral cost C_rev + ε_hum(C_FN·1(Y=1) + C_FP·1(Y=0)).
- Deployment: 4-tier architecture (inference, Mondrian quantile server, cost-optimal routing engine, audit/recalibration); M/M/k queue stability ρ = λ·r_abs/(k·μ) < 1; dynamic α(t) caps abstention under review budgets.
- Assumptions: exchangeability of calibration vs test (drift needs online conformal); calibration n₁ ≥ ~50 minority samples for stable q₁; human error ε_hum constant.
## Data sources named
- 15 OpenML tabular datasets, binary classification, imbalance ratios 2.3:1 (credit_g) to 580:1 (fraud): aps_failure (58:1, n=76k), seismic_bumps (14.2:1), pc1 (13.5:1), oil_spill (17.9:1), mammography (42.5:1), sick_numeric, wilt, ozone_level_8hr, miniboone, fraud (0.17% prevalence), uci_default (n=30k), diabetes130us (n=101,766), credit_g, +2 more. No sports data.
- Code: https://github.com/physics-vibes15/cost-sensitive-conformal. Design: 15 datasets × 7 classifiers (HistGradientBoosting, LogReg, RF, Extra Trees, GB, AdaBoost, Gaussian NB) × 3 calibrations (none, Platt/sigmoid, isotonic) × 10 seeds {7,19,31,42,101,202,303,404,505,606} = 3,150 runs; paired Wilcoxon + 1,000 bootstrap CIs; error level α=0.10 throughout.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Marginal CP minority coverage: 30.5% average; aps_failure 0.8% (<1%) under GB; seismic_bumps 4.0%; pc1 7.6% — while meeting the global 90% target; degrades monotonically with imbalance ratio.
- Mondrian CP: 92.2% average minority coverage; +61.71 pp gain over marginal (95% CI [58.20%, 65.22%], paired Wilcoxon p=1.52×10⁻⁸²). Nemenyi diagram: marginal CP ranks last by >1 critical difference (CD=2.71).
- Full decision comparison (averaged): Bayes τ* — minority 80.0%, set 1.00, abst 0%, cost 0.340; Marginal CP — 30.5%, 1.04, 12.5%, 0.413; APS — 55.6%, 1.09, 19.8%, 0.380; RAPS — 48.7%, 1.07, 15.7%, 0.444; Mondrian raw — 92.2%, 1.36, 38.3%, 0.143; Cost-controlled Mondrian — 97.7%, 1.71, 71.9%, 0.021.
- Cost-controlled Mondrian at C_rev=0.5: mean cost 0.313 — 54.1% below default point classification (0.682), 38.6% below Bayes thresholding (0.510); vs Bayes p=2.93×10⁻⁴⁰, vs confidence rejectors p=1.18×10⁻⁷², vs risk-controlled p=5.12×10⁻⁵⁴.
- Isotonic calibration: GB minority coverage 94.3%, set 1.44, abstention 46.0%, cost 0.109; isotonic cut RF set size 1.42→1.35 and abstention −6.8 pp — but on linear models isotonic raised cost to 0.351 (calibration interacts with model family).
- Break-even: C_rev* rises 0.82→4.35 as C_FN goes 5→50; net savings persist while noisy-human error ε_hum < 0.18.
- Case studies: aps_failure — 65.3% cost reduction vs Bayes, 34.1% of ambiguous cases routed to technicians; uci_default — 13.6% reduction; diabetes130us — 94.9% minority coverage at 24.1% abstention.
- File's own five threats: (1) static quantiles break under drift — needs online adaptive Mondrian; (2) binary-only; (3) sparse minority calibration (n₁<50 → clustered Mondrian); (4) expert fatigue; (5) tabular only. Benchmark is offline; no randomized deployment experiment.
