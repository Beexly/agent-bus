# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0767-predictive-modelling-of-football-injuries.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A 120+ page PhD thesis (Kampakis, UCL 2016, arXiv:1609.07480v1) running three real-data injury-prediction investigations on Premier League clubs (Tottenham Hotspur, Wolverhampton Wanderers): UEFA-record recovery-time modeling, GP regression with a dynamic-time-warping kernel for days-to-first-injury from variable-length exposure records, and supervised PCA for weekly intrinsic-injury classification from GPS data. Ledger verdict: ADAPT — the GP–DTW kernel construction and the forecast-horizon landmark evaluation protocol are directly portable to GSE's player-availability and workload-risk work.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Inv. 1 (recovery time): Poisson log-link with White (1980) robust SEs (E[y] ≠ Var(y) everywhere), negative binomial, cumulative-logit ordinal regression; ML arm: k-NN, SVM (poly degree 2–3), GP (poly kernel), MLP (45 hidden, 2500 epochs), RF; 10 rounds of 10-fold CV on RMSE, Pearson correlation, concordance correlation coefficient ccc = 2ρσₓσᵧ/(σₓ²+σᵧ²+(μₓ−μᵧ)²), Cohen's kappa; feature selection: RF node-impurity ranking (500 trees) + CFS via genetic algorithm (pop 50, 1000 generations, crossover 0.7, mutation 0.05, roulette selection).
- Inv. 2 (exposure): GP regression on log(days-to-first-injury) with composite DTW kernel: k(xᵢ,xⱼ) = exp(−γ·DTW(xᵢ,xⱼ)), K = ½[k_train + k_match]; DTW admits different-length exposure histories (acknowledged not necessarily positive semi-definite — empirical variance-positivity checks instead). Evaluation: leave-one-out CV with test record truncated at T, T−1, …, T−12 days before injury — a forecast-horizon/landmark protocol measuring skill decay vs horizon; grid γ ∈ [0.00002, 0.2], noise ∈ [0.0001, 0.01].
- Inv. 3 (GPS): weekly binary classification with supervised PCA (Bair et al. 2006; screen by univariate |xⱼᵀy|, α ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.5}, 2–50 components), ridge logistic, RF, naïve Bayes, SVM/GP, MLP, k-NN; 10 rounds of 10-fold CV; primary metric Cohen's kappa (imbalanced classes).
- Assumptions: injuries ≤3 days into pre-season attributed to pre-existing conditions (removed); "intrinsic" = non-contact, medic-judged; days off = 0-exposure imputation; log(days) approximately normal.
## Data sources named
- Inv. 1: three UEFA injury-recording datasets — THFC (152 injuries), WW, integrated merged; UEFA fields: recurrence, activity, position when injured, phase of play/mechanism, body part, diagnosis, side, stage of season, age, footwear, strapping, surface, referee's decision, date. THFC mean 15.5 days (variance 1298.8); WW mean 10.8 (variance 341.3); integrated mean 13.8 (variance 975.8).
- Inv. 2: daily training+match exposure minutes for 35 THFC first-team players, 2012–13 pre-season; cleaned to 29 players (21 injured, 9 right-censored); days-to-first-injury range 14–312, mean 91.73, median 49.5; mean training exposure 35.83 ± 42.75 min/day.
- Inv. 3: STATSports Viper10 training GPS for 29 THFC players, July–Dec 2014: 1,310 session rows × 68 GPS variables + session duration (69 total), 6 speed zones (Z1 <35% max … Z6 >75%), accelerations/decelerations, metabolic power (EMD, HML distance, AMP), impacts, dynamic stress load; Approach A: 206 player-weeks × 69 features, 11 injury-weeks; Approach B: 426 player-weeks, 70 variables incl. class.
- All three datasets club-confidential, not released; no code repository.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Inv. 1 statistical (in-sample fit, not CV): Poisson THFC RMSE 11.4/corr 0.94/ccc 0.94; WW 4.9/0.96/0.96; integrated 19.0/0.79/0.75. Negative binomial worse everywhere (THFC RMSE 43.45/corr 0.78/ccc 0.65; WW 9.38/0.85/0.85; integrated 26.22/0.55/0.51). Ordinal regression accuracy 71.0–75.0%, kappa 0.31 (THFC)/0.40 (WW)/0.37 (integrated) — author rejected it as worse than regression. Significant covariates (>50% of tests at 5%): recurrence 6/6, phase of play 6/6, body part 5/6, injury diagnosis 5/6, stage of season 3/4; position when injured 2/2.
- Inv. 1 ML (10×10-fold CV, THFC): RF best — RMSE 30.1, corr 0.471±0.341, ccc 0.406; SVM poly3 — RMSE 28.8, corr 0.511, ccc 0.269; all correlations <0.5 with very large variance (k-NN corr 0.225±0.387). Classification: every classifier ≈ 66.4% majority-class baseline; best kappa = RF 0.256 (THFC), SVM 0.225 (WW). CFS selection: SVM THFC corr 0.511→0.584, ccc 0.269→0.455.
- Inv. 2: GP–DTW LOOCV: ccc 0.95–0.98 from T down to T−12; best MAE ≈ 11 days (response mean 91.7, median 49.5); MAE minimized at T−3.
- Inv. 3: supervised PCA best by kappa in both approaches — Approach A: kappa 0.21±0.04 (acc 88.8%, precision 0.55, recall 0.33; α=0.01, 41 components); ridge logistic second (kappa 0.16±0.33); RF/SVM/GP/MLP/k-NN all ≈ 0 (RF 0.06, k-NN 0.0). Approach B: supervised PCA kappa 0.14±0.09 (α=0.09, 40 components); GP second (0.12). Approach-A PC1 = "absence of high-speed running" (DistanceZone5 r=−0.764), PC2 = "medium-speed sprinting".
- Thesis lessons: kappa-over-accuracy discipline for imbalanced injury classification; regression beats medically-motivated severity discretization; RF's failure on all-numeric high-dimensional data; single-club generalizability flagged by author.
