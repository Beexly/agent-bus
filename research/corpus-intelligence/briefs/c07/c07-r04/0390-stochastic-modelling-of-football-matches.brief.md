# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0390-stochastic-modelling-of-football-matches.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
General Cox-process (doubly stochastic Poisson) framework for jointly modeling soccer match dynamics — goals, red cards, and random referee stoppage-time lengths — with state-dependent intensities and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the log-likelihood is strictly concave, making MLE a convex optimization problem; produces calibrated in-game (live) forecasts of results and exact scores via forward simulation. Verdict recorded: ADAPT — ports directly to NFL live win-probability and live spread/total surfaces; adapt regressors from soccer state (score, red cards, stoppage time) to football state (down, distance, field position, clock, timeouts, penalty shocks).

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Event processes: types J = {home goals, away goals, home red cards, away red cards}, each a Cox process with intensity λ^j(t); conditional on λ^j, event times follow an inhomogeneous Poisson process. Single-match likelihood (Eq. 1): ℒ = Π_k π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k! × Π_j exp(−Λ^j(0,T)) Π_l λ^j(t^j_l), T = 90 + U_1 + U_2. Log-linear intensities (Eq. 2): ln λ^j(t) = Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t); log-linear stoppage (Eq. 3): ln π_k = Σ_i ξ_i φ_i; parametric log-likelihood ℓ(ξ) = −Σ_j[∫_0^T exp(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(r))dr − Σ_l(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t^j_l))] + Σ_k[U^k Σ_i ξ_i φ_i − exp(Σ_i ξ_i φ_i) − ln(U^k!)]. Theorem 1: ℓ is concave on ℝ^I; strictly concave when regressors are linearly independent → unique MLE, standard convex optimization (attack/defence normalization constraints Σ_k ln α_k = Σ_k ln β_k preserve convexity). Regressors: Maher attack/defence/home indicators; team market-value log-ratio (ln V^h − ln V^a); score difference; red-card difference; second-half indicator; ln t for red cards (power law λ^j(t) = e^{ξ} t^{ξ}); stoppage regressors: goals/red cards in half + close-game indicator 1_{|N^h − N^a| ≤ 1}. Five nested goal models G0–G4 (67→71 params), six stoppage models S0–S5; fitted via MOSEK primal-dual interior point through R CVXR — 78 params on 3,039 matches in <10 s (i7-10700F, 32 GB). Forecasting: per match simulate 100,000 scenarios from minutes 0/15/30/45/60/75 (earliest-event simulation + state update; stoppage time simulated at minute 45; no lookahead), evaluated by predictive log-likelihood of results and exact scores.

## Data sources named
Campeonato Brasileiro Série A 2015–2022: 3,039 matches, 7,126 goals (4,248 home), 717 red cards; outcomes: home 48.3%, away 24.5%, draw 27.1%. Sources: Flashscore (match/goal/red-card), CBF official match reports (stoppage times), Transfermarkt (starting-11 market values). Event times minute-granularity; stoppage in whole minutes. Forecasting exercise: 2,611 matches (first 4 matches per team per season excluded). No code or downloadable dataset released; solver details given (MOSEK/CVXR) — reproducible in principle.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Red card reduces the receiving team's goal intensity by 30.48% (opponent intensity ×1.4385)
- Losing by one raises goal rate ×1/0.9082 = 1.1011 (+10.11%); losing by two ×1/0.9082² = 1.2124 (+21.24%); leading by one ×0.9082, by two ×0.8248
- Second-half stoppage time on average >1 minute longer when score difference ≤1 (S5 goal-difference coefficient 1.2814)
- Home advantage: home goal intensity +51.40% vs away; second-half goal rate +20.62% (1.2062); doubling squad value multiplies goal rate ×1.1454^{ln 2} = 1.0987; 10× value → ×1.3670
- Stoppage base rates (S0): first half 2.6680 min, second half 4.8066 min; goals-per-half have ~no effect on stoppage length (coefficients ~1.0)
- Red cards: home intensity at min 15 = 1.6010×10⁻⁴/min, at min 75 = 1.5929×10⁻³/min (~10× higher); away at min 75 = 2.5092×10⁻³/min (~1.5× home); away exceeds home after minute 8
- Draw calibration: G4S5R predicts 27.48% draws vs 27.42% observed (far better than Maher static 24.46%/27.32% on 27.92% observed)
- G4S5R beats simpler models on both result and exact-score likelihood from minute 30 onward; team-value regressor's value decays by minute 75 (stale after substitutions — paper's own interpretation)
- Nested comparison: all G0→G4 LRT p-values ≈ 0; G4 preferred by AIC (76,523) and BIC (77,011)
- Limitations recorded: minute-granularity event times; §7.1 only is rolling-window out-of-sample (calibration tables are in-sample); 33 teams with relegation churn (some on 1 season); red-card intensity a pure function of time (no match state or referee effects)
