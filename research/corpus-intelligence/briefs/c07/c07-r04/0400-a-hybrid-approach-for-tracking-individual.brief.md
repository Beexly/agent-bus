# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0400-a-hybrid-approach-for-tracking-individual.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Engineering-integration study on tracking an individual player through unedited broadcast soccer video in real time, unsupervised (no human operator selecting the initial position), under camera motion, occlusions, identical-appearance teammates, and off-camera intervals — combining a costly-but-accurate deep detector with fast-but-fragile classical trackers into a hybrid that is simultaneously fast and accurate. Verdict recorded: ADAPT — do not build as a tracking-data source (cannot replace NGS, doesn't scale across rosters), but port the architectural pattern — a slow accurate detector run every *jump* frames supervising a fast cheap per-frame tracker — to GSE's NFL clip/telestration video pipeline, where per-clip single-player auto-tracking is exactly the use case.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Per-frame precision: average overlap via Intersection over Union, Φ(Λ^G, Λ^T) = {Φ_t}_{t=1}^{N}, φ = (R_t^G ∩ R_t^T) / (R_t^G ∪ R_t^T); success threshold IoU > 0.5; OPE (One Pass Evaluation) curve = % frames with IoU above threshold across thresholds; AUC = integral of OPE curve in [0,1]. Architecture (three models at different frequencies): Faster-RCNN (player-specific detector) runs once every `jump` frames as the supervisor — auto-initializes tracking and periodically corrects drift; its box crops an ROI; SSD (player-specific) runs on the remaining jump−1 frames on the ROI to refine; KCF (Kernelized Correlation Filter) runs every frame inside the ROI for fine-grain per-frame output. Drift correction: KCF's box adjusted only when it diverges from SSD for more than 3 consecutive frames (first-disagreement adjustment found unstable; 3-frame rule experimental). Design evolution: Faster-RCNN every frame infeasible (~45 ms/frame, detection in only ~20% of frames); KCF alone drifted after seconds; Kalman filters failed on camera motion/direction changes; Meanshift/Camshift lasted seconds. Assumptions: rectangular bbox output; motion locality (tight ROI contains the player); one detection box per frame; detector confidence <0.6 treated as no detection.

## Data sources named
SoccerNet dataset (Giancola et al. 2018) segments, full HD 1920×1080 at 60 fps broadcast soccer. Test: 4 individual players from 2 teams, each with a "specially challenging" sequence; accuracy vs hand-labeled frames on challenging segments only (e.g., 200-frame subset for the Faster-RCNN baseline). Training: 1,500 labeled frames per player via a semi-automated OpenCV-tracking tool (not fully automatic). Per-player training sets and hand-labeled eval sequences proprietary (no URL); no code released. Inference: GTX 1080 Ti + i7-8700K + 16 GB. Training: Tesla K20m 5 GB + 2× Xeon E5-2660.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Faster-RCNN alone: ~45 ms/frame (insufficient for 30 fps = 33 ms/frame); IoU > 0.5 in only ~45% of 200 challenging frames; AUC 0.32
- Hybrid tracker (per player): avg overlap 0.620/0.653/0.650/0.600; 91.75/84.98/86.65/87.36 fps; AUC 0.610/0.651/0.660/0.600; lost frames 2/0/0/0
- IoU ≥ 0.5 in 80–90% of frames; AUC "around 0.6" vs literature SOTA SiamVGG 0.610–0.654 on OTB (cross-dataset comparison) at ≤35 fps vs hybrid's 80+ fps; abstract claims 80 fps average on 1920×1080
- Jump-size study: jump = 3 gives highest AUC (0.64) but large/unstable per-frame time; larger jumps reduce AUC, frame time falls and stabilizes
- Crop-size study: varying SSD crop has no significant influence on accuracy or frame time
- Training cost per player: Faster-RCNN 15–21.19 GPU-hours (164,166–200,000 steps, loss 0.0014–0.0065); SSD 21.71–46.9 GPU-hours (93,768–200,000 steps, loss 1.0371–1.3549)
- Limitations recorded: accuracy computed only on hand-selected challenging segments (no full-sequence error rates); 4 players, 2 teams, one sport (no cross-team/league/sport validation); per-player training is a scalability wall (NFL: 53 × 32 players infeasible); no identity resolution (no jersey-number OCR or re-ID — tracks one pre-specified player); broadcast 2D can't recover 3D positions/velocities (not an NGS substitute); the 0.6 detector-confidence cutoff unexplained
