# docs/arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0587-gnnrank-learning-global-rankings-from-pairwise.md

## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A full-text read of arXiv:2202.00211v3 (He, Gan, Wipf, Reinert, Yan, Cucuringu 2022) introducing GNNRank, an unsupervised framework that embeds a directed pairwise-comparison graph with a directed GNN (DIMPA or Inception Block), converts embeddings into a learned similarity/Laplacian, and computes a Fiedler-style ranking through differentiable unrolled proximal/projected-gradient steps, trained end-to-end on upset losses. Evaluated on 78 real-world digraphs (NCAA basketball 1985–2014, Premier League 2009–2014, tennis, finance, etc.) and ERO synthetic graphs. The file's verdict is ADAPT — but only with time-forward held-out game validation replacing the paper's in-sample upset fitting.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Learned similarity and Laplacian: S_ij = exp(−‖z_j − z_i‖²_2 / (σ²d)), L = D − S, D = degree matrix of S.
- Fiedler ranking problem: min_r rᵀLr subject to ‖r‖²_2 = 1 and rᵀ1 = 0; orthogonal Q rotates this to a sphere constraint in n−1 dimensions; unfolded into differentiable projected/proximal gradient steps, default Γ = 5 steps.
- Convergence theorem (Appendix C): if the Fiedler vector is a strict local minimizer and step size 0 < α < 1/(4(n−1)), Algorithm 1 converges locally uniformly.
- Differentiable upset losses: L_upset,ratio = ‖T̃ − M‖²_F / t(M); L_upset,margin = Σ_ij (M_ij + |M_ij|) ReLU(r_j − r_i + ε) / t(M), default margin ε = 0.01.
- Variants: GNNRank-N ("innerproduct", "dist" readouts) trained directly on upset losses; GNNRank-P proximal variants ("proximal innerproduct", "proximal dist", "proximal baseline" — the latter refines any arbitrary baseline ranking vector r′, e.g., SpringRank/SyncRank/SerialRank/BTL, with Γ proximal steps).
- Training protocol: all graph data used for training (no held-out split for real data); up to 1,000 epochs with early stopping after 200 epochs without loss decrease; 50 epochs of proximal pretraining; Adam/SGD; weight decay 5×10⁻⁴; learning rate fixed at 0.01 in the selection study; 10 repeated runs on real data; synthetic: 5 networks × 2 runs. Whole study: 167,040 runs on 8 Tesla T4 GPUs, 96 Xeon Platinum 8259CL CPUs, 378 GB RAM.
- Code: https://github.com/SherylHYX/GNNRank. 11 baselines compared: SpringRank, SyncRank, SerialRank, BTL, David's Score, eigenvector centrality, PageRank, Rank Centrality, SVD_RS, SVD_NRS, MVR (MVR frequently failed to finish after one week and was omitted from several tables).

## Data sources named
- Real-world digraphs (78 total): NCAA men's basketball seasons 1985–2014 ("Basketball(year)" binary win/loss graphs and "Basketball finer(year)" score-differential graphs; 30 seasons each); English Premier League seasons 2009–2014 ("Football(year)" binary plus "Football finer(year)" variants; 6 seasons each); Animal (dominance hierarchies); Faculty (Business, CS, History hiring networks); HeadToHead (tennis); Finance (1,315 stocks lead-lag network, 2001–2019); Halo 2 (game-result network).
- Synthetic: ERO (Erdos–Renyi Outlier) model, n=350 nodes, sparsity p ∈ {0.05, 1}, noise/outlier η ∈ {0, 0.1, …, 0.8}, uniform and gamma score styles, five random networks per (p, η, style) × 2 runs.
- Data schema: directed adjacency/comparison matrix with entries in {−1, 1} (win/loss) or real-valued weights (finer variants); no node-level covariates; initial node features not stated in the paper.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Average L_upset,simple (means over 10 runs; in-sample — see cautions): Basketball 1985–2014: GNNRank-P 0.73 vs SpringRank 0.78 vs BTL 0.91; Basketball finer: GNNRank-P 0.74 vs SpringRank 0.81; Football 2009–2014: GNNRank-P 0.78 vs SpringRank 0.91; Football finer: GNNRank-P 0.82 vs David's Score 0.93; Animal: GNNRank-P 0.25 vs David's Score 0.33. [TRUST-SIGNAL — headline numbers are in-sample reconstruction, not predictive skill]
- Synthetic ERO Kendall τ for GNNRank-P: sparse (p=0.05), uniform scores, η=0.1: 0.79; sparse, gamma, η=0.2: 0.77; sparse, uniform, η=0.3: 0.70; sparse, gamma, η=0.4: 0.66; dense (p=1), uniform, η=0.5: 0.92; dense, gamma, η=0.6: 0.89. Performance degrades smoothly as noise η grows; dense graphs recover truth much better than sparse ones. [OTHER]
- Inductive transfer (IB proximal baseline trained on Basketball-finer 1985, applied to other seasons without retraining): L_upset,simple = 0.75 ± 0.02 vs season-specific retraining 0.74 ± 0.00; L_upset,naive = 0.19 ± 0.01 both; L_upset,ratio = 0.01 ± 0.00 both — paper concludes the general model produces "almost the same superior performance" as retraining. [OTHER]
- Proximal refinement of baselines (avg L_upset,simple improvement across all datasets): SyncRank-as-initialization improves by 1.02; SpringRank 0.07; SerialRank 0.82; BTL 0.22; eigenvector centrality 0.19; PageRank 0.21; SVD_NRS 0.12. Avg L_upset,naive improvement with SyncRank init: 0.24. [OTHER]
- Ablations: adding the margin loss generally harms GNNRank-N but generally helps GNNRank-P; pretraining and trainable proximal learning rates help; Γ = 3, 5, 7 proximal steps perform similarly (5 retained); directed GNNs (DIMPA/IB) beat a two-layer MLP by ~2% on L_upset,simple, supporting the directed architecture. [OTHER]
- File's decisive adversarial notes: every real-data edge is used for unsupervised fitting AND in-sample upset evaluation — the task is ranking reconstruction, not forward prediction; a model can drive in-sample upsets toward zero by memorizing the graph. [TRUST-SIGNAL]
- Hyperparameter/variant selection (Appendix G) picks the lowest in-sample L_upset among many variant×pretraining×loss-weight combos with no held-out set — reported numbers are best-of-category, inflating the apparent margin over baselines; the margin-loss default ε=0.01 and per-dataset loss coefficients are themselves tuned on the test metric. [TRUST-SIGNAL]
- ERO synthetic assumes a fixed latent order with i.i.d. flip noise — NFL strength is dynamic within a season, so the synthetic recovery results overstate applicability to a nonstationary league. [TRUST-SIGNAL]
- The method uses no home-field, no injuries, no market information, and no score differentials beyond "finer" weights; the evaluation metric (upset fraction) rewards getting the sign right, not calibrated probabilities or ATS performance. [TRUST-SIGNAL]

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- Directed-GNN strength embeddings (DIMPA/Inception Block) learned from matchup-graph topology rather than scalar win/loss updates: SCHEME (new algorithmic family — directed message-passing team representations — as nonlinear strength features for the learning-to-rank/state-space stack; corpus has Elo/BTL/Massey/Colley/state-space but no graph-neural strength embeddings).
- Differentiable proximal-unrolled Fiedler refiner ("proximal baseline") that refines ANY baseline ranking (e.g., GSE's dynamic Elo / state-space posterior mean): SCHEME (a "rank refiner" capability the corpus lacks; largest gains observed when refining SyncRank-style initializations).
- Training directly on differentiable upset loss rather than log-loss: TRUST-SIGNAL (portable objective idea, but must be replaced with a supervised time-forward log-loss objective — the paper's unsupervised in-sample upset objective is the exact failure mode to avoid).
- In-sample selection inflation (best-of-category variant selection, tuning on test metric, memorizing observed graphs): TRUST-SIGNAL (any GNNRank port must use strict time-forward walk-forward training — train ≤2021 seasons, validate 2022–2023, test 2024–2025 — and be judged only on held-out future-game log-loss/Brier/ATS, never in-sample upset).
- Inductive transfer of a 1985-trained model to other seasons at near-parity with retraining: TRUST-SIGNAL (evidence that a once-trained directed-GNN refiner may generalize across seasons, reducing weekly retrain cost — but unproven under NFL nonstationarity).
- No probability output / no ATS evaluation in the paper: TRUST-SIGNAL (GSE port must add a Platt-style calibration layer mapping score gaps to win probabilities; acceptance gate: log-loss beats best baseline by ≥0.005 nats/game, Brier ≥0.002 better, ATS ≥52.4% over n≥500 picks, ECE within 0.01 of baseline).

## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
Yes — port the directed-GNN embeddings plus the Γ=5-step proximal Fiedler refiner onto weekly NFL matchup graphs (edge weights = point/EPA differential) with a strict time-forward walk-forward protocol and calibration layer, keeping only the components that pass the log-loss/Brier/ATS acceptance gate on the 2024–2025 test window.
