# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/2148-long-run-reward-cvar-maximization-mdps.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Research ledger for "On the Maximization of Long-Run Reward CVaR for Markov Decision Processes" (arXiv:2312.01586) — the risk-seeking side of CVaR-MDPs, grounded in prospect theory's loss frame: it proves the long-run right-tail CVaR optimum is attained by a stationary randomized policy requiring at most one randomization (mixing ≤2 actions), computed via two linear programs. Ledger verdict: ADAPT — the only paper in this lane treating the risk-seeking side; gives GSE a principled "drawdown regime" rule: when the bankroll is underwater, the rational risk-seeking response is a computed mixture of exactly two stake/action levels, not ad-hoc chasing.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Right-tailed CVaR_α(ξ) = (1/(1−α))∫_α^1 VaR_q(ξ)dq (1), α small (e.g., 0.7 in examples → focus on top 30% of outcomes). (1−α)CVaR_α + α·CVaR̂_α = E[ξ] links the two tails.
- Rockafellar–Uryasev convex representation: CVaR_α(ξ) = min_{y∈[L,U]} {y + (1/(1−α))E[ξ−y]^+} (2); y* = VaR_α(ξ).
- Long-run definitions via limsup/liminf CVaR over history-dependent randomized policies (limit need not exist — Example 1: limsup +2, liminf −2).
- Structural results: (a) limsup optimum = liminf optimum via two opposing optimality inequalities, attained within stationary randomized policies; (b) counterexample (3-state/3-action) where NO stationary deterministic policy is optimal — contrary to risk-neutral and risk-averse MDPs; (c) Theorem 2: optimal stationary randomized policy exists requiring at most one randomization (mixing ≤2 actions).
- Computation: max-min saddle point via von Neumann minimax → solved by two LPs (Eqs. 38/39) — one for optimal VaR y*, one dual LP for the optimal stationary randomized policy x*(i,a) with flow-balance constraints.
- Extension (Sec. 5): mean-CVaR maximization J*_β = max_d [CVaR^d + βη^d] (42) — same two-LP structure; α=0 degenerates to ordinary long-run average MDP.
- Assumptions: finite S, A; bounded rewards [L_r, U_r]; infinite-horizon average-reward (unichain-ish) setting; known transition kernel; fixed risk level α.
## Data sources named
- No real datasets — three constructed numerical MDP examples: toy 2-state, 3-state/3-action counterexample, university-endowment allocation (2 economic states × 3 allocation levels, rewards 1000[(1−a)r_0 + a r_1(x') − b|a−ω|]). GSE test: engine weekly P&L 2022–2025 (unit stakes).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Example 2 (α=0.7): optimal randomized CVaR* = 93.24 vs best deterministic 92.6675 (gap ≈0.57, ~0.6%); policy d*(3|1) = d*(1|2) = 1, d*(1|3) = 0.0255, d*(3|3) = 0.9745 — single randomization suffices.
- Example 3 (endowment): two LPs compute the optimal mean+CVaR allocation policy (no numeric optimum quoted in extracted text).
- Example 1: limsup CVaR = +2, liminf CVaR = −2 for a history-dependent policy — limit need not exist.
- Limitations from file: requires a known tabular MDP (GSE must estimate the kernel; no learning theory given, contrast 2146's regret bounds); toy-scale numerics; right-tail CVaR maximization is the opposite of what a bankroll usually wants — misapplication would be catastrophic, use must be fenced to the drawdown regime; LP size grows with |S×A×S|; no robustness analysis for MDP misestimation; no empirical comparison against heuristic drawdown rules.
