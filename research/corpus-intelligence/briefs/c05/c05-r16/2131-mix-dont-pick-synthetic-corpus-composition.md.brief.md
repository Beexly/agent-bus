# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/2131-mix-dont-pick-synthetic-corpus-composition.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Research ledger for "Mix, Don't Pick" (arXiv:2606.09912), which shows that under a fixed pretraining budget, the choice of synthetic time-series generator is first-order for time-series foundation model (TSFM) zero-shot performance — and that a simple equal-weight mixture over generator families matches or beats picking the best single generator. Ledger verdict: ADAPT the equal-weight-mixture recipe for GSE's synthetic sports pretraining corpus, but GSE must first design sports-native generators (generic fBm/chaotic systems have no sports semantics).
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Metrics: nCRPS = CRPS/CRPS_seasonal-naive; nMASE likewise; aggregated by geometric mean over 97 dataset–horizon tasks (lower better; <1 beats naive).
- Design: fix corpus size (1M×1024 windows) and training budget; vary only the generator (11 single-generator corpora) or the mixture (Mixed11, real+synthetic ratios 75/25, 50/50, 25/75); train Chronos-T5-Mini and Moirai-Small from scratch on each; compare zero-shot on GIFT-Eval.
- Statistics: paired bootstrap over the 97 dataset–horizon tasks as resampling units.
- GSE transfer: sports generator families to design — score-dynamic generators (Poisson/binomial play-by-play → margins with key-number effects), possession/pacing generators, usage generators (target-share random walks), injury-shock generators (mid-season regime shifts), volatility-regime generators (weather-game variance), market-line generators (synthetic spreads with known inefficiencies). Code: https://github.com/birla-ai-labs/mix-dont-pick.
## Data sources named
- 11 synthetic generator families: ARIMA, ETS, fractional Brownian motion (fBm), SDEs, GARCH, chaotic systems, TimeSynth, TSI (trend-seasonality-irregularity), StepFunction/Waveform-inspired, KernelSynth (GP kernel compositions). Total pool: 11.2B time points; each single-generator corpus: 1M univariate windows × length 1024.
- Real reference: GIFT-Eval pretraining reference corpus. Evaluation: zero-shot on 28-dataset GIFT-Eval → 97 dataset–horizon tasks. Models: Chronos-T5-Mini, Moirai-Small.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Generator choice is first-order: CRPS varies 1.6× across generators for Moirai-Small and 2.1× for Chronos-T5-Mini under identical budgets.
- Rankings don't transfer: KernelSynth best for Moirai-Small (CRPS 0.734 / MASE 1.049); for Chronos-T5-Mini the best single generators differ (SDE second-best; ETS catastrophic: CRPS 1.976 / MASE 2.694). Real reference: Moirai 0.814/1.149, Chronos 0.791/1.061.
- Table 1 (Moirai-Small CRPS): KernelSynth 0.734/1.049; ETS 0.820/1.154; Waveform 0.870/1.240; SDE 0.910/1.306; StepFunction 0.958/1.391; TSI 0.969/1.369; ARIMA 0.988/1.430; fBm 1.036/1.419; GARCH 1.055/1.466; Chaotic 1.154/1.636; TimeSynth 1.194/1.659; Real Reference 0.814/1.149.
- Equal-weight mixture (Mixed11) matches or beats the best single generator for both architectures — "don't pick."
- Real+synthetic is strongest overall, but the optimal ratio is architecture-dependent. Limitations: 1024-length windows vs. 17-game NFL seasons (untested); GIFT-Eval has no sports datasets; 11.2B-point pool is large — small-budget scaling untested.
