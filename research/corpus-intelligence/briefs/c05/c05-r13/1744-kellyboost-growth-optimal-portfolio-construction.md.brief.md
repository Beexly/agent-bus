# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1744-kellyboost-growth-optimal-portfolio-construction.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Ledger verdict: ADAPT. Full read of Jiayu Li (2026), arXiv:2608.23393 — KellyBoost: trains gradient-boosted trees directly on the growth-optimal (Kelly) decision objective instead of a prediction/classification surrogate, with closed-form gradients and a CRRA risk-aversion dial; directly portable to GSE's multi-pick simultaneous portfolio sizing — but only with the fractional-Kelly tempering dial engaged, per the paper's own boundary finding.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
One multi-output XGBoost emitting logit vector z(x_t) ∈ R^K; portfolio weights w_t = softmax(z) on the simplex (long-only, fully invested; cash leg makes this unrestrictive). Loss (Eq. 1): ℓ_t = −log(1+S_t), S_t = w_t^T y_t — negative log growth rate, the Kelly criterion as training objective. Population proposition: minimizer satisfies σ(z(x)) ∈ argmax_w E[log(1+w^T y) | x] a.e. — the conditional Kelly portfolio. Gradient: logit k pushed up when y_k > S (outperforms competing portfolio), force ∝ σ_k · 1/(1+S). Hessian diagonal h_k can be negative (non-convex in z); safeguard uses |h_k|. CRRA dial: φ_γ(S) = ((1+S)^{1−γ} − 1)/(γ−1), γ→1 recovers −log(1+S) — "the utility-side analogue of fractional Kelly." Stabilizers: K_ens=4 leave-one-out ensemble (single tree fits unstable to one-row perturbations). Comparison 2×2: {boosted trees, MLP} × {growth objective, classification surrogate}, plus two-stage LGBM+optimizer and unconditional-Kelly baselines. Dependency-free reference engine with finite-difference derivative tests. Assumptions: E|log(1+w^T y)| < ∞ on simplex; i.i.d.-ish rows; simplex constraint.
## Data sources named
Eight-asset-leg public-data equities testbed, 23 years; 7,871-column "searched" feature pipeline and 174-column hand-built pipeline; selection segment ending 2012, evaluation 2013-01 → 2026-07 with 163 monthly (20-trading-day) decisions; deployment refits every 21 trading days on expanding windows; one committed CSV, no API key, every number regenerable.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
OOS 2013-01 → 2026-07 (163 monthly decisions; log G bar = mean realized log growth per 20-day decision ×100; Sharpe daily ann. vs T-bills): KellyBoost log G 0.47, ann. return 6.2%, vol 23.9%, Sharpe 0.30, max DD −46.2%; MLP same loss 0.56/7.1%/20.5%/0.35/−55.5% (Δ log growth vs KellyBoost −0.9 [−9.2,+8.9]); two-stage LGBM+optimizer 0.82/8.2%/20.9%/0.40/−40.4% (Δ −1.9 [−12.3,+10.0]); multiclass surrogate 0.35/4.0%/19.2%/0.21/−42.7% (Δ +2.0); unconditional Kelly 0.42/4.1%/19.3%/0.22/−50.9% (Δ +2.0). Every pairwise bootstrap interval straddles zero — 13.6 years of monthly decisions can't separate any two methods at conventional significance, so the paper argues from consistency. Growth objective beats the classification surrogate in all four 2×2 cells (trees 0.47 vs 0.35 searched; 0.39 vs 0.32 hand-built; MLPs 0.56 vs 0.20; 0.67 vs 0.49); pooled paired per-decision difference +0.18, bootstrap Pr(Δ>0) = 0.89 (0.84–0.96). Growth objective trades less in every cell, so transaction costs widen the gap. Boundary finding: end-to-end learners trail the two-stage pipeline (0.82 log G) because estimated full-Kelly concentrates too aggressively while two-stage squared-error forecasts shrink toward zero — "the optimizer is not the problem; the estimated full-Kelly target is." Exact Hessian beats first-order substitutes 3× on selection but loses deployed. Selection protocol committed γ=1.14 (essentially logarithmic).
