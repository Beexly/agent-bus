# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0489-decoding-defensive-coverage-responsibilities-in-american.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Deep-read ledger of arXiv:2603.25901v1 (Song et al. 2026), which trains factorized-attention transformers on NGS tracking trajectories to predict per-defender coverage assignments (20 classes), receiver–defender matchups, and the targeted defender on every pass play at any frame from pre-snap through pass arrival. Verdict: ADAPT — port the "disguise rate" (pre-snap vs post-snap assignment divergence) and per-defender matchup-attribution concepts to GSE's matchup model using Big Data Bowl 2025 tracking; the proprietary NGS/PFF labels cannot be replicated.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Factorized attention: temporal attention per agent + agent ("social") attention per timestep (Scene Transformer / HoopTransformer style) instead of full spatio-temporal attention; learnable embeddings for categorical metadata (position, offense/defense) fused with transformer output → per-player latent vectors → feed-forward head; temporal pooling (average over time) → play-level per-player representations. Three cross-entropy losses: matchup L = −(1/D) Σ_d Σ_r z*_{d,r} log(exp(Z_{d,r})/Σ_{r'} exp(Z_{d,r'})) with "no matchup" class; coverage L over C=20 classes (19 responsibilities e.g. hook curl left, deep right + "no assignment"); target-defender L over N players (receiver logit masked −1e9 pre-softmax) plus post-processing rules (confidence <50% in prevent → "no target defender"). Training augmentation: random trajectory truncation — 11 fixed strategies (start ∈ {−30,−20,−10,0}, end ∈ {snap, pass forward, pass arrival}) at 60% + fully random at 40%. Hyperparameters: batch 16; epochs 100/200/150 (target/matchup/coverage); AdamW (wd 1e-4, betas 0.9/0.999); OneCycleLR max_lr 2e-4 (target/matchup), CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts init 2e-5 (coverage); heads 4/4/8; layers 3/3/6; dropout 0.1/0.2/0.1.
## Data sources named
NFL Next Gen Stats tracking, 2020–2024 (2020–2023 train, 2024 test); 10 Hz (x,y), orientation, movement direction; play events (snap, pass forward, pass arrival); participation/roster data; Pro Football Focus ground-truth labels (coverage assignment, matchups, target defender) — proprietary, not publicly replicable; play data. Filtered to passing plays with valid snap + pass-forward events. Public proxy: NFL Big Data Bowl 2025 tracking release. 21,567 eligible pass plays in 2024. Models deployed for NFL broadcast production; no public code/weights/labels.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Receiver–defender matchup: up to 0.894 accuracy / 0.894 F1 full context (−30 → pass arrival); post-snap windows 0.872–0.873; pre-snap-only ~0.750–0.752 accuracy, 0.745–0.746 F1.
- Coverage assignment: ~0.918–0.919 accuracy / 0.877–0.879 F1 with post-snap context; pre-snap-only ~0.882–0.883 / 0.825–0.827; ~70% of 2024 plays had all coverage defenders correct at pass arrival; ~20% had 1–3 errors ("judgment calls"); Top-2 accuracy 97% across 20 classes.
- Target defender: rule-based nearest-defender 0.764; transformer 0.862; transformer + post-processing 0.882 (~12pp lift).
- Double coverage: 1,179 instances across 21,567 eligible 2024 pass plays (5.5% rate); top-5 model-ranked double-covered receivers: Ja'Marr Chase, Garrett Wilson, CeeDee Lamb, Justin Jefferson (plus one more, aligned with human labels); model flagged 9 double coverages on Tre Tucker vs 0 in human labels (paper suggests human "reputation bias"); Mike Evans only 10 all season.
- Disguise proxy: Kansas City (Spagnuolo) lowest pre-snap prediction accuracy (most deceptive); Tampa Bay and San Francisco most predictable.
- Limitations: play-level end-of-play labels vs frame-by-frame prediction (accuracy likely understates true frame-level performance; label-noise distortion both ways); inference substitutes a predicted team scheme for the ground-truth scheme used in training (error cascade; Cover 4 vs Cover 2 disagreement → 4 "wrong" assignments); PFF labels have charting-guideline violations; inter-annotator agreement never reported; scheme symmetry bias; rare schemes concentrate errors (Prevent: 3% of plays with 6+ errors); quick-game passes (<2s) unassignable.
