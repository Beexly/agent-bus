## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Full-paper research brief on Király & Qian (2017), arXiv:1701.08055v1, unifying Bradley-Terry and Élő as one structured-logodds model (Élő = single-sample gradient ascent on the Bradley-Terry log-likelihood) and testing extensions plus two-stage training on 8,524 EPL matches (1993–2015). Verdict: ADAPT the framework and two-stage training recipe; reject the fancier two-factor/rank-four parameterizations (they failed to beat vanilla Élő on real data).
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Core equations (quoted from paper): L_ij = θ_i − θ_j; matrix form L = θ·1ᵀ − 1·θᵀ (antisymmetric, rank 2); Élő with home advantage p_ij = σ(θ_i − θ_j + h); two-factor L = u·vᵀ − v·uᵀ; rank-four L = u·vᵀ − v·uᵀ + θ·1ᵀ − 1·θᵀ; features L_ij = ⟨λ,X_ij⟩ + ⟨β,X_i⟩ + ⟨γ,X_j⟩ + α_ij; ternary Rao-Kupper: P(win)=σ(L_ij), P(lose)=σ(−L_ij−φ), P(draw)=σ(−L_ij)−σ(−L_ij−φ); score diff Y_ij ~ Skellam(exp(L_ij), exp(L⁰_ij)); gradient ∂ℓ/∂θ = (Y_ij − p_ij)·∂L_ij/∂θ; batch update θ_i ← θ_i + K·Σ(Q_i − p_ij); empirical log-odds MLE L̂ = log W − log Wᵀ. Training: four variants — single-batch MLE, repeated retraining, pure online (classical Élő), two-stage (large initial batch to convergence, then online). Validation: time-ordered train (from 1993-08-04 Arsenal vs Coventry) / tune (from 2005-01-01) / test (2010-01-05 to 2015-05-24, 2,048 matches); grid search maximizing validation log-likelihood; metrics = accuracy with Clopper-Pearson 95% CIs, mean out-of-sample log-likelihood with 5,000-bootstrap CIs, paired t-tests with Holm correction.
## Data sources named
EPL: 8,524 matches 1993–94 to 2014–15, 47 teams, from http://www.football-data.co.uk/ (public, reconstructable). Bookmaker (Bet365) odds from same source as benchmark (vig removed by normalization). Synthetic datasets generated from structured-logodds assumptions (procedure described, no code published). Brief's GSE spec adds: nflverse schedules/scores 1999–2025; The Odds API for bookmaker benchmark.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Accuracy on 2,048 test matches: Bet365 odds 54.13% [51.96%, 56.28%]; Élő two-stage 52.40% [50.23%, 54.56%]; Élő with covariates two-stage 52.78% [50.61%, 54.95%] (best model); score-difference two-stage 52.59%; naive home-win 46.07%; trace-norm batch 45.89% (below naive baseline). Two-factor/rank-four two-stage ≈ 51.30–51.34% (no better than Élő).
- Mean out-of-sample log-likelihood: Bet365 −0.9669 [−0.9877, −0.9460] (best); Élő two-stage −0.9854; Élő with covariates two-stage −0.9807; Élő online −1.0003; batch-learning best GLM1+MA −0.9797.
- Hypothesis tests: H1 two-stage > online — Élő p=7.8×10⁻⁵, two-factor p=4.4×10⁻¹⁴, rank-four p=9.8×10⁻⁹, score-diff p=2.2×10⁻¹⁶ (all highly significant). H2 new model > Élő — two-factor p≈1, rank-four p≈1 (FAILED), score-diff p=0.235 (not significant), Élő with covariates p=0.002 (significant).
- Authors' conclusion: everything lost to Bet365; only three observable influential factors confirmed — a general "good vs bad" skill quantifier, home advantage, and newly-promoted status.
- Fairness analysis: 10,000 Monte Carlo season replays — only Manchester United ended with the same rank >50% of cases; middle 50% of teams spread over 5+ ranks; Arsenal predicted top-3 with high confidence but finished 4th.
- The two-stage advantage and the gradient-residual unification (same (Y−p) term across all variants) are the paper's two durable contributions per the brief.
