## What it is (1-2 sentences)
PETSA (Borealis AI, arXiv 2506.23424): parameter-efficient test-time adaptation for time-series forecasting — freeze the big forecaster, update only tiny gated low-rank input/output calibration modules on delayed partial ground truth with a Huber + patch-wise-structural + frequency loss; beats full-model TTA (TAFAS) on 6 datasets × 6 backbones with up to 33.6× fewer parameters. GSE verdict: ADAPT.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Calibration (Eq. 1): X̂^cali = X + (tanh(α⊙X)·W + b), Ŷ^cali = Ŷ + (tanh(α⊙Ŷ)·W + b); α ∈ ℝ^V per-variable gating (DyT-inspired); W = A·B low-rank (A ∈ ℝ^{L×r}, B ∈ ℝ^{r×L×V}); A Xavier-init, B zero-init; forecaster frozen. PETSA loss (Eqs. 2–6): ℒ_PETSA = ℒ_T + ℒ_pt (delayed-full + partial labels); each = ℒ_Hub + ℒ_pw + β·ℒ_freq. Huber (Eq. 2): 0.5e² if |e|<δ else δ(|e|−0.5δ), δ=0.5. ℒ_freq = ‖ℱ(Ŷ^cali) − ℱ(Y)‖_1 (Eq. 3). ℒ_pw = Σ_{k∈{corr,mean,var}} ℒ_k (Eq. 4). Metric: MSE; row-winner/column-winner counts; input 96, horizons {96,192,336,720}; adaptation window from dominant FFT period (TAFAS convention).
## Data sources named
ETTh1, ETTm1, ETTh2, ETTm2, Exchange, Weather (standard multivariate TSF benchmarks). Backbones: iTransformer, PatchTST (transformer); DLinear, OLS (linear); FreTS, MICN (MLP). Code: https://github.com/BorealisAI/PETSA (PyTorch, built on TAFAS).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
PETSA 127 best-MSE wins vs TAFAS 88; wins row count on nearly every dataset/window; gains hold across transformer, linear, and MLP backbones. Parameter efficiency: at window 720, 0.21MB vs TAFAS 3.70MB (OLS/ETTh1) — up to 33.6× fewer parameters at equal-or-better MSE. Ablations: MSE-only or Huber-only underperform the full loss; frequency term sometimes hurts (β=0 optimal for ETTh1/OLS; β=0.1 best for FreTS) — β needs per-dataset tuning. Harder datasets need more adapter memory. Limitations: MSE ≠ Brier on NFL games; ℒ_freq designed for periodic physical signals (NFL "period" far less regular); NFL partial labels = early-slate results before late games (~3-hour gap, ~10 games, thin); freezing assumes core dynamics stable — under true regime change the frozen core is the problem; no uncertainty/calibration metrics.
## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- TRUST-SIGNAL: frozen-season-model + tiny weekly calibration overlay is a principled cheap alternative to full weekly refits; ECE-neutral-or-better gate on Brier.
- OTHER: composes with 1890's teacher-student framing (student = this calibration adapter) and 1889's frozen experts (adapters per expert); hierarchical-shrinkage improvement (division/league priors, weeks 1–4 ≥0.003 Brier hypothesis) is the Bayesian upgrade of flat gating.
- COACHING: the frozen-core assumption breaks under coaching regime change (2020 COVID cited as failure case) — overlay alone can't compensate.
## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
Yes — freeze GSE's preseason win-prob model and update only a ~64-param per-team + Platt-style calibration overlay weekly (Huber on log-loss residuals, mean-zero structural term, gated step), with a Sunday partial-label nudge for late games; adopt if overlay captures ≥80% of full-refit's Brier gain.
