## Data sources named
RacketVision (new): 1,672 clips, 435,179 frames, 12,755 s from 942 top-level professional game broadcasts on YouTube; 64,042 ball + 24,621 racket annotations, 1080p, sparse (20% of frames sampled). Per sport — table tennis: 50 games/780 clips/170,027 frames/3,878 s; tennis: 431/431/150,399/4,285; badminton: 461/461/114,753/4,592. No code or dataset download link stated in the extracted text.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Ball tracking (best: MS-TrackNetV3 w/ background modeling, 4 frames): tennis Prec 0.945/Rec 0.880/MDE 1.96/mAP 81.9; badminton 0.915/0.865/1.54/83.1; table tennis 0.924/0.762/3.41/71.1. Multi-sport gains: mAP +19.2% tennis (81.9 vs 68.7 single-sport), +14.6% badminton (83.1 vs 72.5). Background modeling cuts MDE 54.0% (table tennis), 61.4% (tennis), 54.8% (badminton). Racket pose multi-sport PCK@0.2: table tennis 81.8 (vs 75.6 SS, +6.17%), badminton +6.36%, tennis 89.6–89.69% (+5.97%); side keypoints (left/right) 64.8–80.1% vs top/bottom/handle 92.6–99.7%. Trajectory: **naive concat fusion consistently underperforms the ball-only baseline** across both backbones and all sports; cross-attention LSTM best overall. Long-horizon ADE/FDE (px): tennis ball-only 62.5/108.7 → concat 76.8/125.0 → cross-attn 55.5/94.7; table tennis 113.9/184.3 → 139.9/198.9 → 101.3/161.3; badminton 118.7/194.7 → 134.5/203.3 → 114.6/187.6. Fusion gains concentrated at rare hit/turning-point frames; average improvements modest.
