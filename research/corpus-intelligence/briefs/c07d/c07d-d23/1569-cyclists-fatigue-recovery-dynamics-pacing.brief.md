# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1569-cyclists-fatigue-recovery-dynamics-pacing.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Ledger brief of arXiv:2007.05507 (Ashtiani et al., 2020): experimentally fitted Critical Power / Anaerobic Work Capacity (CP/AWC) two-tank fatigue model with an asymmetric, experimentally measured recovery law (recovery slower than expenditure, power-level-dependent) and a quadratic max-power-vs-remaining-energy curve, used to compute an optimal pacing policy via dynamic programming. Verdict ADAPT — gives GSE the canonical quantitative fatigue/recovery dynamics for a player work-capacity state; validated by a 3-minute improvement on a 10.3 km time trial.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- AWC = (P − CP)·Δt (Eq. 1); 3-min all-out test: CP = mean power of last 30 s, AWC = area above CP.
- Two-tank state W = remaining AWC energy. Switching dynamics: dW/dt = −(P−CP) when P > CP (expenditure); dW/dt = −(P_adj−CP) when P < CP (recovery) (Eq. 3).
- Adjusted recovery power: P_adj = CP − W_rec/T_rec (Eq. 2); fitted law P_adj = aP + b (Eq. 4, linear in P, Fig. 5) — fitted per subject from interval tests. Recovery capped so P_adj can't imply recovery while P > CP (added constraint).
- Max power capacity: P_max(t) = a₁W²(t) + a₂W(t) + CP (Eq. 5, Fig. 6b) — quadratic in remaining energy, fitted from 3-min all-out data; replaces the constant-800 W assumption of prior work.
- Bicycle dynamics: F(t) = m_t dv/dt + m_t g(sin θ + μ cos θ) + 0.5C_dρAv² (Eq. 6); P(t) = F(t)v(t) (Eq. 7).
- Optimal control: min J = ∫₀^{tf} dt = ∫₀^{xf} dx/v (Eq. 8) subject to constraints (Eq. 9): 0 ≤ P_rider ≤ P_max(W), 0 ≤ W ≤ AWC, 0 ≤ v ≤ v_max, P_rider = P_req feasibility; states z = [v, W], control P_rider. Solved by dynamic programming with distance as independent variable (dt → 2ΔX/(v_i+v_{i+1})), grid 32 velocity × 100 energy nodes × 100 m intervals, discrete DP Eqs. 10–12; <60 s runtime on desktop i5-4460/12 GB. Software-in-the-loop real-time controller planned: backward-DP matrices as lookup tables, forward pass with CompuTrainer feedback.
- Assumptions: 100% drivetrain efficiency; constant air density; cadence fixed 80 rpm in tests; drag term removed for lab (no air resistance on CompuTrainer); models fitted on one subject (Sub 9) — generalizability across subjects untested.

## Data sources named
- Human-subject lab experiments (Clemson/Furman Universities), 9 subjects recruited from local cycling communities; full models presented for "Sub 9". Protocol per subject: 13 lab visits — VO₂ ramp test (gas exchange threshold power P_GET), 3-min all-out test ×2 (CP = mean power of last 30 s; AWC = area above CP), and 9 interval tests (3 recovery power levels × 3 durations: 2/6/15 min) where subjects burn half their AWC at CP4 (the 4-min sustainable power = AWC/240 s) then recover, then a final 3-min all-out to measure recovered energy. CompuTrainer ergometer (power control), Moxy NIRS sensor for muscle oxygenation SmO₂. Sub 9: CP = 234 W, AWC = 9758 J. Test course: 10.3 km Caesars Head State Park route (two long uphills), simulated on CompuTrainer via PerfPro.
- No public code or data stated; methods fully specified (equations + grid sizes + protocol), reproducible in principle; raw subject data not shared.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Interventional validation: Sub 9 rode the simulated 10.3 km course with own strategy (baseline 41:17, verbally encouraged, familiar course) vs DP-optimal policy (38:15) — 3 min 2 s (≈7.3%) improvement. Mechanism: optimal policy inserts low-power recovery intervals that regenerate W, enabling higher sustained power late (especially final km); the subject's own strategy never recovered at very low power.
- Sub 9 parameters: CP 234 W, AWC 9758 J.
- SmO₂ data: recovery plateaus at a power-dependent level regardless of duration — so a duration-based recovery model would be non-causal; hence the power-level-dependent P_adj law (causality argument).
- Experimental validation: interval-test protocol directly measures recovered energy vs power level/duration → fitted P_adj law; quadratic P_max(W) fitted to 3-min all-out traces.
- UNCERTAIN/limitations: single-subject models (9 recruited, Sub 9 reported) — fitted a, b, a₁, a₂ are individual, not population; GSE needs per-athlete calibration. Lab ergometer ≠ field (no drag, no tactics, no competitors). Baseline is one ride by the same subject (motivation/familiarity confounds; still, 3 min is a large margin). Recovery linear in P only over tested range — extrapolation beyond tested power levels unjustified. No injury outcome — W is a performance reservoir, not an injury predictor. Cycling-specific (power-meter-based); translating "power above CP" to football requires an NGS-derived exertion proxy.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- **OTHER (total-signal program / injury-risk lane):** Complements 1568 (trail-running fatigue ODE) with the other canonical fatigue formalism: the CP/AWC two-tank model with *asymmetric, experimentally measured* recovery — 1568's dQ/dt had no recovery term at all. Together they give GSE both candidate dynamics for a player work-capacity state. The quadratic P_max(W) (max output degrades with depleted reserves, concave with a₁ < 0) is the direct analogue of "player explosiveness declines with accumulated load" — usable for snap-count/load-management features. No existing corpus work uses CP/AWC.
- **COACHING (load management):** The implementation spec is concrete: per-player anaerobic-reserve state W updated per play — dW = −(P−CP) when exertion proxy P > CP, dW = −(P_adj−CP) when P < CP, with P = NGS-derived exertion (e.g., normalized high-speed distance + accelerations per play), CP = position-group aerobic threshold, P_adj = aP + b fitted per position group (mirroring the interval-test protocol: hard-effort bouts followed by low-load periods). P_max(W) = a₁W² + a₂W + CP gives a per-game "explosiveness ceiling" feature — a coaching-input signal: players with depleted W project below market, informing snap-count decisions and late-game substitution value.
- **OTHER (DFS lane):** W and P_max(W) serve the DFS value model as fatigue features — players with depleted reserves project below market pricing. Backtest gate: injury-risk model with (W, P_max(W)) features beats rolling-average-load baseline by ≥2 AUC points on 2024 soft-tissue injuries; fitted recovery asymmetry must hold (recovery rate < expenditure rate, a < 1); P_max(W) must show concave-degrading shape (a₁ < 0) — convex fit fails the gate.
- **OTHER (improvement experiment):** The paper's single-subject lab calibration should be replaced with hierarchical partial pooling across NFL position groups (group-level a, b, CP with player random effects) estimated from NGS exertion + injury data. Add stochastic injury-hazard layer λ(t) = λ₀·exp(γ·(AWC−W(t))) linking depleted reserves to soft-tissue injury risk, tested head-to-head against the 1568-proposed hazard λ(t) = λ₀·exp(γ·Q(t)) to select the better fatigue dynamics for the injury model.
- The SmO₂ causality argument (recovery driven by power level, not duration) is a modeling lesson for any GSE recovery feature: parametrize recovery by intensity of the recovery period, not its length.
