# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1557-regularized-ensemble-forecasting.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Ledger brief of arXiv:2602.11379 (Su, Guo & Zhang, 2026): Regularized Ensemble Forecasting (REF) — an ensemble weighting objective that jointly minimizes current-forecast-set dispersion while shrinking weights toward historical track-record priors, with λ tuned by rolling validation, derived from a Bayesian hierarchical model. Verdict ADAPT — a principled answer to the "history vs. this-week" weight problem every GSE ensemble faces; rank 1 in both the M5 competition study and the Survey of Professional Forecasters study.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- REF objective: w* = argmin_w f(Σᵢ wᵢ²(µᵢ−E[µ])²) + λΦ(w), s.t. Σwᵢ=1, wᵢ≥0 (Eq. 3). Variance term = current-forecast dispersion around consensus (down-weights outliers); Φ = historical regularization (L2: Σ(wᵢ−sᵢ)²; Entropy: Σsᵢlog(1/wᵢ)); f ∈ {identity, log, shifted-log}.
- Eq. (1)→(2): min Var(Σwᵢµᵢ) with independent common-mean forecasts → min Σwᵢ²(µᵢ−E[µ])².
- Bayesian derivation: hierarchical w∼π(·|α), β|w,µ∼h, y|β∼g; unobserved y treated as missing data, EM-style E-step Q(w|µ)=E_y[log p(y|w,µ)π(w|α)], M-step maximizes Q → exactly the REF objective under six specs (Table 1): {Shifted Log, Log, Identity} × {Entropy (Dirichlet prior), L2 (normal prior centered at s)}; also yields the full posterior predictive distribution of the ensemble forecast.
- Prior weights s: Common Correlation Weights (Soule et al. 2024) — sᵢ ∝ [(1+(k−1)ρc)vᵢ⁻² − ρc vᵢ⁻¹Σⱼvⱼ⁻¹]/normalization (Eq. 17); ρc estimated by maximum-posterior on complete-case subset, vᵢ² per-expert from available history; new experts imputed with cross-sectional average precision. Robustness check with plain variance weights.
- RMSSE (Eq. 18) vs. naïve in-sample RMSE; PS (penalty share) measure §5.4 — diagnostics how much REF leans on history vs current consensus.
- Theorem 1: MSPE of REF = σ_y² + O(1/k) — same asymptotic rate as the simple mean (needs proper λ order); Proposition 1: simple-mean baseline O(1/k)+σy²; Remark 1: assumptions imply common correlation ρ=σy²/(σ₀²+σy²) on forecast errors; closed-form Identity-L2 weights (Eq. 19, Appendix A.1).
- Assumptions: µᵢ independent draws with common mean E[µ] (errors may still be correlated, Remark 1); convex weights (interpretability, Theorem 1 requirement); wᵢ>0; expert pool known at forecast time; imputation scheme for rotating pools.
- Implementation: softmax reparameterization (unconstrained optimization), σ² estimated from recent simple-mean errors, λ selected by rolling-window validation minimizing validation RMSSE (fixed λ* for M5; per-quarter re-tuning for SPF). Final REF = average of the six specifications (best-spec selection gives similar results).

## Data sources named
- Study 1 — M5 competition: publicly released submissions of 50 teams (accuracy track), 28-day point forecasts of daily unit sales; first 9 hierarchical levels (154 series, levels 1–9; levels 10–12 excluded — sparse daily sales). Fixed expert pool = top-k teams by official M5 leaderboard, k ∈ {5,10,15,20,50}. Days 1–21 historical forecasts (T=21), Days 15–21 rolling validation (l=14), Days 22–28 testing.
- Study 2 — SPF: Philadelphia Fed Survey of Professional Forecasters, NGDP growth, UNEMP change, CPI inflation; 2000Q1–2025Q2 test quarters; horizons h=0 (nowcast) and h=1; rotating expert pool (forecasters enter/exit); realizations from RTDSM initial-release vintages. Per test quarter: preceding 16 quarters history (T=16), prior-weight window l=8 → 8 rolling validation quarters, λ re-tuned per quarter; pool restricted to forecasters submitting at t with ≥1 appearance in the window; missing validation forecasts imputed by cross-sectional mean.
- Simulation (A.5): k=10 experts, y_t = m+u_t, µ_{t,i} = m+a_{t,i}+r_{t,i} with a_{t,i} AR(1) (φ=0.5) persistent bias; ρ ∈ {0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8}; 100 Monte Carlo reps; 40-period history, 40-period rolling window, 20 validation, 20 test.
- Benchmarks: Simple/Trimmed/Winsorized means; Variance Weights; CWM; CCR; StackingRegressor/RidgeCV; ES_bu; Best Expert. CCR benchmark code adapted from Soule et al. 2024's released code. No public REF code/package stated.
- Full text read: main text §§1–6 + refs + Online Appendix A.1–A.9.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- M5 (Table 2): REF rank 1 at every pool size k; improvement over Simple Mean: 2.26% (k=5) → 6.22% (k=50); best absolute RMSSE 0.335 at k=15. CCR second (2.27% at k=15). StackingRegressor catastrophically worst (e.g., −553.6% at k=20; unconstrained ridge weights overfit with #coefficients ≈ #observations). All REF-vs-benchmark p-values <0.001 (except CCR at k=20/50: 0.016/0.012; one-sided bootstrap, 1,999 reps). Table 3: REF best at all 9 hierarchy levels.
- SPF (Table 4, avg of h=0/1): REF rank 1 for all three indicators: NGDP growth 1.054 (+4.08%), UNEMP change 1.662 (+5.73%), CPI inflation 0.954 (+2.53%). Significant vs. nearly all benchmarks. History-only methods underperform the Simple Mean in 8/9 cases here (rotating pool makes history unreliable) — yet REF still wins by balancing.
- Penalty-share analysis (§5.4): REF beats current-only benchmarks most when PS high, beats history-only benchmarks most when PS low; strong even at medium PS (balanced regime). In M5, REF's edge over the Simple Mean grows with expert pool size; historical influence (PS) grows as k grows.
- Simulation (A.5, Fig A.1): positive average RMSE gains over both Winsorized Mean and CCR at all ρ ∈ {0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8}; gain over Winsorized Mean larger at small ρ (dispersed current forecasts).
- Appendix confirmations: REF vs simple mean 2.26% (k=5, Table A.4) to 6.22% (k=50, Table A.7); best RMSSE 0.335 at k=15 (Table A.2); validation-selected spec rank 1 at all pool sizes (Table A.9); variance-based priors beat CCR-based priors (GDP 6.81%, UNEMP 6.27%, CPI 2.85%, Tables A.14–A.15).
- Limitations: no NFL/sports data (retail sales + macro — transfer to weekly sports forecasts is the core assumption to test); M5 test window only 7 days; SPF validation windows 8 quarters (λ selection noisy, though re-tuned per period); new experts imputed at cross-sectional average precision (slow to promote a genuinely better model); point forecasts only (M5 uncertainty-track and Beta–Bernoulli win-probability extensions are future work); six-spec averaging is the reported REF (one spec blind may do less); λ grid search per series/quarter compute-heavy; assumes common-mean current forecasts; with k=2–3 experts the variance term is a noisy consensus gauge (Theorem 1 asymptotic in k); absolute RMSSE differences small (0.335 vs 0.351) — percentage gains relative to Simple Mean within same scaling.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- **OTHER (ensemble program — the most direct engine hit in this batch):** This is the principled joint answer to GSE's standing "history vs. this-week" sub-model weighting problem — no REF-style dual objective exists in the current research map (ledgers cover current-only like equal weights/trimming and history-only like variance-weight-style and OLS/stacking analogues [1555], nothing joint). The weekly NFL implementation is fully specified in the ledger: current forecasts µᵢ (per market: spread, total; probability outputs in log-odds), priors s = CCR (Eq. 17) from rolling l=8 weeks with new sub-models imputed at average precision (directly answers GSE's sub-model churn problem, mirroring the SPF rotating-pool handling), λ from rolling validation, average the six specs' softmax-solved weights, publish ensemble + posterior predictive credible bands for the pick. Complements [1548] (adaptive weights), [1552] (diversity features), [1556] (error correction downstream).
- **TRUST-SIGNAL (calibration/sizing):** The penalty-share (PS) diagnostic is new instrumentation the engine lacks — it tells us *when* the ensemble is leaning on track record vs. this-week consensus. The PS trajectory through the season (proposed as a test metric) would be a trust signal on whether weekly ensemble weights are earned or habitual. The Bayesian posterior predictive distribution is a bonus: credible bands around each pick.
- **OTHER (calibration/sizing):** The StackingRegressor catastrophe (−553.6% at k=20) is a CONTRADICTION-adjacent warning to any unconstrained OLS/stacking weighting in the engine — with coefficients ≈ observations it overfits disastrously; REF's convex-weight constraint is the safeguard. Also note variance-based priors beat CCR-based priors in SPF robustness checks (GDP 6.81%, UNEMP 6.27%, CPI 2.85%) — the simpler prior may win in NFL too, an empirical question for the test.
