# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1849-iife-interaction-information-feature-engineering.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
IIFE (arXiv:2409.04665, Northwestern/Allstate): automated feature engineering that pre-selects which feature pairs to combine using interaction information (synergy beyond individual contributions), iteratively building arbitrarily high-order features; it also audits the AutoFE literature and quantifies how much published gains are inflated by bad experimental hygiene (CV-as-metric, transductive leakage).

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Interaction information (Algorithm 1): τ_ij = I(F_i; F_j; Y) = I(F_i; F_j | Y) − I(F_i; F_j) = H(F_i,F_j) + H(F_j,Y) + H(F_i,Y) − H(F_i) − H(F_j) − H(Y) − H(F_i,F_j,Y). Symmetric in all three variables; interpretable as I(F_i; Y | F_j) − I(F_i; Y): the information F_i shares with Y *given* F_j minus without — synergy beyond isolation.
- IIFE loop (Algorithm 3): (1) compute II for all pairs (O(|F|²); prefilter to F̃ = top-50 by RF impurity importance if |F| too large); (2) loop: take K highest-τ pairs, apply all bivariate functions B, evaluate each candidate by cross-validated downstream-model performance V_M in parallel, keep the argmax; (3) apply univariate functions U to the new feature, keep argmax; (4) add to pool; next iteration computes II only for (new feature × existing) — O(|F|).
- Stop (Algorithm 2): patience P — stop if mean of recent P/2 CV scores ≤ mean of previous P/2. Order cap optional for interpretability.
- II as accelerator (§IV-D): restrict expand-reduce methods' expansion (OpenFE, AutoFeat) to high-II pairs → "OpenFE-II"/"AutoFeat-II": pair set reduced ~5×, similar-or-better test scores, much shorter runtimes.
- II computed via nearest-neighbor CMI estimator (Mesner & Shalizi, ref [14]) on a random 3,000-sample subset.
- Named statistics: F1-micro (classification), 1 − relative absolute error (regression); RF impurity importance for prefiltering.
- Operators used: num-num (+,−,×,min,max,x1/(|x2|+1),%,reverse), num-cat/cat-num groupbythen{min,max,mean,median,std}, univariate (x²,|x|,x,1/(1+e^(−x)),1/x); Lasso drops ÷ and 1/x (division-by-zero).
- Implementation parameters: K = 3, stop patience P = 20 (P = 40 for cal housing/openml586/jm1); RF on large sets n_estimators=50, max_samples=0.25; unseen-category encoding = train max|value| + 10%·i (or 0); hardware Intel Xeon Silver 4108 @ 1.80GHz, 8 cores, Ray-parallel. LightGBM retune ranges: learning_rate loguniform(0.001,1), subsample/colsample uniform(0.1,0.9), reg_lambda loguniform(0.001,100), num_leaves randint(8,64).
- Assumptions (stated): high-II pairs are good combination candidates; entropy estimation on discretized/continuous features is reliable; patience stop detects convergence; prefiltering to top-50 doesn't drop synergistic pairs.

## Data sources named
- Public datasets "randomly chosen from the EAAFE, DIFER, and OpenFE papers" (Table III): Airfoil (1,503×5, reg), Credit Default (30k×23, clf), Bikeshare (17,389×13, reg), Wine Quality Red (999×12, clf), California Housing (20,640×8, reg), OpenML 586 (1k×25, reg), JM1 (10,885×22, clf), Jungle Chess (44,819×6, clf).
- Proprietary Allstate-scale regression dataset (100s of thousands of samples, ~1,000s of features).
- Synthetic verification: targets y_ij = f(F_i, F_j) for 105 credit-approval pairs, f ∈ {+, ×, sin(+), exp(|max|)}.
- Downstream models: LR/Lasso, RF, LightGBM — hyperparameter-tuned (random search, 100 iterations, 5-fold CV) *before* AutoFE and *after* AutoFE. 25 runs per dataset/model (5 train/test seeds × 5 algorithm seeds).

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Table I (all public datasets/models): IIFE **10 top ranks**, average rank **2.50 (1.64)** vs OpenFE 3.38, EAAFE 3.13, AutoFeat 3.08, DIFER 3.75, baseline 4.46. Average % change over baseline: **26.88% (90.6)** vs AutoFeat 20.62%, DIFER 9.02%, OpenFE 8.26%, EAAFE 7.11%. Excluding the OpenML-586 outlier: 8.74% vs 5.63–7.16%. By model: linear-only **72.42%** (AutoFeat 59.44%), RF-only 5.20%, LGBM-only 3.02% (OpenFE marginally better at 3.11%).
- Linear-model gap closure (Fig. 3): IIFE+LR reduces the gap to RF* to **4.99%** and to LGBM* to 5.35%; IIFE+Lasso → RF* 4.94%, → LGBM* 10.15% — linear models reach near-nonlinear performance while staying explainable.
- Standout wins (Table IV, 25 runs per cell): OpenML 586 (Lasso) IIFE 0.7494 vs OpenFE 0.2150 vs AutoFeat 0.6235 vs DIFER 0.3261 (baseline 0.1383); Jungle Chess (LR) 0.7988 vs OpenFE 0.7543; Jungle Chess (RF) 0.9396 vs OpenFE 0.8391; Airfoil (Lasso) 0.6468 vs AutoFeat 0.6349; Bikeshare (RFR) 0.9955 vs OpenFE 0.9898. OpenFE occasionally beats IIFE (Cal Housing Lasso 0.5260 > 0.5076; Jungle Chess LGBM: EAAFE 0.9873 > IIFE 0.9701 > OpenFE 0.9481).
- Large-scale proprietary (Table II): IIFE **+6.21% in 5.33h** vs OpenFE-II +1.16% (4.17h) vs AutoFeat-II +4.23% (5.75h).
- Literature-hygiene quantifications (§IV-C): CV-as-final-metric inflates gains 21.03% → 15.33% on holdout (a 5.7pp inflation); OpenFE's transductive groupby-then-* (test samples in aggregates) inflates airfoil by **+14.01%** and jungle chess by +1.95% vs the inductive fix.
- Combining methods (§IV-E): IIFE+AutoFeat (LR) +1.51%, IIFE+EAAFE (RF) +0.31%, IIFE+OpenFE (LGBM) +0.20% over the second-best single method.
- Synthetic verification: II ranks the true pair at/near rank 0 across all f (Fig. 5 histograms).
- Key property: builds increasingly complex features iteratively (Jungle Chess: best feature reached order 10 — beyond any competitor's cap) while stopping at low order when the model doesn't benefit (Airfoil/RF case).
- Runtimes (Table V): TOTAL avg — IIFE 1.58h, OpenFE 1.45h, AutoFeat 1.53h, EAAFE 1.31h, DIFER 4.55h (slowest everywhere; LGBMclass avg 7.07h). On large data, OpenFE/AutoFeat without II-acceleration "cannot complete within several weeks."
- Appendix C (Wine Quality-Red): all AutoFE methods score below baseline as classification; reframing as regression gives +4.43% over baseline — the deficit is the modeling choice, not AutoFE.
- Limitations (adversarial read in file): (a) IIFE's internal selection still uses CV scores on train for the keep decision (V_M), holdout only at end — the 26.88% headline still carries selection bias; (b) the transductive fix (train-only aggregates applied to test) is exactly what ledger 1842's OpenFE implementation must enforce — cross-ledger dependency; (c) the 72.42% linear-only gain is dominated by datasets where linear baselines were terrible (huge std 153) — gap-closure framing is fairer; (d) II estimation via nearest-neighbor MI is noisy at small N, no discretization sensitivity analysis; (e) prefiltering to top-50 by RF importance can drop pure-interaction pairs (XOR-type) — the exact pairs II is supposed to find; untested failure mode; (f) proprietary dataset unverifiable; (g) anonymous code link (github.com/2oppy67zj4ky/Appendix) in v1 may be dead.
- GSE implementation spec in file: "GSE-IIFE" — (1) pair screening: compute τ_ij for all candidate feature pairs on training seasons only (2020–2022) via Mesner–Shalizi nearest-neighbor CMI; skip RF-importance prefiltering (the XOR-blindspot — NFL feature counts in the hundreds, O(|F|²) affordable); (2) iterative construction: top-K pairs × bivariate set {+, −, ×, ÷, diff-vs-opponent, ratio-to-league-avg} → LightGBM CV on 2023 → keep argmax; univariate {log, sqrt, z-score, sigmoid} on winner; patience P = 5; (3) inductive enforcement: every groupby-then-* aggregate computed on training seasons only, mapped onto validation/test; (4) run separately for public explainable LR model and LightGBM engine model. Effort: ~4–5 days.
- Reproducible test in file: nflverse 2020–2024 game-level, kickoff cutoffs; IIFE loop on 2020–2022 (II) / 2023 (V_M selection); frozen feature set; evaluate LR and LightGBM on held-out 2024 (log-loss) vs no-FE baseline. Controls: (a) transductive-aggregates arm to replicate the +14% inflation finding in sports; (b) random-pair baseline (same K) to prove II prioritization beats chance; (c) leakage audit on all groupby features.
- Acceptance gate in file: ADAPT iff (a) 2024 held-out log-loss improves ≥ 0.003 for LightGBM, AND (b) II-prioritized arm beats random-pair arm by ≥ 0.002, AND (c) the transductive-control arm demonstrably inflates, AND (d) LR arm closes ≥ 50% of the LR→LightGBM gap. REJECT if II doesn't beat random pairs, any inductive violation, or >8h per model arm.
- Improvement experiment in file: fix the XOR-blindspot — two-stage screen (stage 1: II for all pairs, no prefilter; stage 2: re-estimate II conditioned on the current pool, I(F_i; F_j; Y | pool)) to find XOR-type sports interactions (e.g. blitz-rate × man-coverage-rate); plus sports-native bivariate set (rest-differential interactions, weather × dome/outdoor, divisional-rematch adjustments).

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- TRUST-SIGNAL (pair-prior as search pruner): computing τ_ij for all NFL candidate feature pairs (team stats × situational splits × market features) is O(|F|²) entropy work — orders of magnitude cheaper than LightGBM retrains per candidate — and the paper proves it finds synergistic pairs (synthetic rank-0) while accelerating expand-reduce methods 5× without score loss. Full sentence: this slots directly before the GSE OpenFE/DIFER-style expansion as the cheapest principled way to select which pairs deserve combination, serving the trust-target intake program by surfacing interactions like blitz-rate × man-coverage-rate that neither feature reveals alone.
- OTHER (experimental hygiene as GSE law): the §IV-C quantifications are methodological findings, not modeling ones — CV-as-metric inflates 5.7pp (21.03% → 15.33%), transductive groupby-then-* inflates +14.01% on airfoil — and they transfer directly to GSE: any groupby-then-* NFL feature like "team's average EPA" computed over train+test carries the identical transductive bug, so inductive-only aggregates become a hard rule for the calibration/sizing program. Full sentence: the file proposes a hygiene ablation arm (transductive aggregates on purpose) to *replicate* the +14% inflation in the sports domain as a calibration of the audit itself.
- QB-BEHAVIOR: the linear-model gap closure (IIFE+LR within 4.99% of RF*) is strategically valuable for an explainable public model — Garrett's "show their work" doctrine means a linear model at near-GBM performance is what can be shown publicly while the engine stays private; the LR arm targets this for the public rankings surface. Full sentence: if IIFE-generated features close ≥50% of the LR→LightGBM gap on held-out 2024, the public-facing model can be an explainable linear model instead of a black box, which serves the public/private surface doctrine.
- SCHEME: the proposed conditional-II improvement (I(F_i; F_j; Y | pool)) finds XOR-type scheme interactions — individually weak features jointly predictive (e.g. blitz-rate × man-coverage-rate for defensive EPA) — which serves the scheme-analysis program with discovered rather than stipulated interaction structure.
- UNCERTAIN: whether nearest-neighbor CMI estimation is reliable at NFL sample sizes (weekly game-level rows are far fewer than the 1,503–44,819-row public sets); the paper ran on a 3,000-sample subset but did no discretization sensitivity analysis.
- CONTRADICTION (methodological): the paper's own RF-importance prefiltering (top-50) can drop exactly the XOR-type pure-interaction pairs II is designed to find — the paper doesn't test this failure mode, and the file's GSE spec explicitly rejects the prefilter for this reason.
