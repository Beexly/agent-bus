# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/2203-multi-camera-asynchronous-ball-localization.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
arXiv:2401.17185 (Xiao, Zaidi & Gombolay, 2024): an incremental factor-graph system (GTSAM + ISAM2) for asynchronous multi-camera 3D ball localization and trajectory prediction with physics-based aerodynamic/bounce factors, plus a TCN that estimates early-flight spin (hidden, unobservable from early detections) from human pose (kicker/hitter posture) as a spin-prior factor. Ledger verdict ADAPT: the pose→spin-prior idea is the gem (kicker/punter body mechanics predict ball flight); NGS gives no spin data for kicks, and this paper shows how to infer spin as a hidden state and how much it matters.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Formulas copied verbatim from the file (eqs. numbered as in the paper):
- Graph factorization (eq. 1): `G(η)=∏_{i=0}^{M−1} f_i(ξ_i)`; potentials (eq. 2): `f_i(ξ_i)=exp(−½‖err_i(ξ_i)‖²_{Σ_i})`.
- Projection (eqs. 3–4): `π^proj(L)=K[R T]L`; `err^proj=u_detect−π(L)`.
- Location (eqs. 5–6): `π^loc(L_s,V_s)=L_s+V_s[t(s+1)−t(s)]`; `err^loc=L_{s+1}−π^loc`.
- Aerodynamics (eqs. 7–11): drag `F_D(V)=−½ρ_air A C_D‖V‖V`, lift `F_L(V,W)=½ρ_air A C_L‖V‖²U (U=W×V)`; `acc=(F_D+F_L)/m+g`; `π^aero(V_s,W_j)=V_s+acc·Δt`; `err^aero=V_{s+1}−π^aero`.
- Bounce (eqs. 12–19): `V_{x,t}=[RW_{y,j−1}+(1.5R²/R_1²)V_{x,t−1}]/[1+1.5R²/R_1²]`; `V_{y,t}=[−RW_{x,j−1}+(1.5R²/R_1²)V_{y,t−1}]/[1+1.5R²/R_1²]`; `V_{z,t}=−e_z V_{z,t−1}`; `W_{x,j}=−V_{y,t}/R`; `W_{y,j}=V_{x,t}/R`; `W_{z,j}=W_{z,j−1}`; `err^bc=[V_{s+1},W_{j+1}]ᵀ−π^bc(V_s,W_j)`.
- Flight ODE + RK4 prediction (eq. 21): `[L̇, V̇, Ẇ]=[V, acc(V,W), 0]` propagated with 4th-order Runge-Kutta; on ground contact (`V_z<0, L_z<z_ground`) terminate integration, apply bounce dynamics (eq. 12), restart.
- Incremental inference: each new detection triggers relinearized incremental optimization (ISAM2): `argmin_η −log G(η) ≈ argmin_η ‖Jη−b‖²` (eq. 20). No camera synchronization needed — every single detection updates the graph.
- Variables: camera poses X_i (priors from extrinsic calibration), ball 3D location L_t, 3D velocity V_t, 3D spin W_j (constant between bounces — spin only changes at ground contact).
- TCN spin-prior network (§IV): temporal convolutional network regressing ball spin from a 100-frame human-pose sequence (hip-centered, right-arm shoulder/elbow/wrist keypoints); authors report TCNs beat RNNs/LSTMs for this task. Spin labels generated by the authors' iterative procedure: factor-graph inference with evolving initial spin estimates, iterated until the final-detection spin estimate converges, then used as labels (self-distillation).
- Stated assumptions: spin constant during flight, changes only at bounce; ball is a sphere (radius R); detections arrive with known timestamps; camera poses known from calibration; hip-centered arm poses carry spin information.

## Data sources named
- Proprietary hardware setup (not public, not downloadable): indoor tennis court, 6 Blackfly S cameras at 5.5 m height on back walls (3 per side, overlapping coverage), each with a Jetson Xavier; intrinsic calibration via checkerboard, extrinsic via AprilTag + court keypoints; ball detection via background subtraction + YOLO-tiny trained on tennis balls, ~140 Hz detection per camera, ~1 ms Ethernet latency to central computer. Human poses via AlphaPose; per trajectory, 100 frames of pose keypoints centered at the hip, emphasizing right arm.
- Dataset split 80/20 train/validation for the TCN (no time-ordered splits; no cross-validation reported; no blind test set described).
- Tools: GTSAM (open-source factor-graph package), AlphaPose. No author code or dataset link stated in the paper. Work supported by a Google grant.
- Baselines: prior EKF system (Zaidi et al. [14]), adaptive EKF with spin as hidden state (AEKF), ablation without spin priors, and oracle labeled-spin variant.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- TCN spin prior: training RMSE **2.95 Hz**, validation RMSE **5.27 Hz**; Pearson R **0.807 (novice) / 0.815 (competitive)**, p<0.001 (V-B).
- Headline claim: "reduces the prediction error of landing positions by **over 63.6%** compared to a baseline method that utilized an adaptive extended Kalman filter" (Abstract); §IV: "63.6% reduction in the RMSE for the ball's landing position vs. baseline methods" via spin-prior integration.
- Trajectory-prediction evaluation: landing-position RMSE binned by observation stage (quartiles of detection count per trajectory — Stage 1 = first 25% of detections, etc.), mean ± std per stage, for first and second landing positions.
- Stage-binned RMSE curves (Fig. 6, qualitative from text): error drops most from Stage 1→2 (first 25% of observations); TCN-spin variant (green) beats EKF (red), AEKF (purple), and no-spin (blue), approaching the labeled-spin oracle (orange); spin advantage appears mainly in second-bounce prediction (spin affects first bounce, then propagates).
- Heavy-spin pro shots (Fig. 7): TCN variant consistently beats EKF/AEKF but underperforms labeled-spin oracle — authors attribute TCN gap to limited training data and missing racket pose.
- Limitations stated: (a) spin labels are self-distilled from the same factor-graph inference the method evaluates — circularity risk in the TCN's training signal; (b) small, proprietary, single-venue dataset; no public benchmark; (c) bounce-model coefficients required "ad hoc modifications" for high-spin ball-ground slip — authors propose a future *learnable* bounce factor (§VI); (d) tennis ball ≠ football: prolate spheroid with orientation dynamics; the bounce equations are sphere-specific; (e) no real-time latency numbers reported for the central factor-graph optimization (detection runs at 140 Hz, but ISAM2 update cost per detection is unstated).
- External validity to NFL (from the ledger): the asynchronous multi-view factor graph is overkill for NFL where broadcast is monocular, but the *factor-graph-as-learned-simulator* framing (physics factors + learned factors, incremental inference) is a portable design pattern. Complements ledger 2202 (learned 3D trajectory from monocular 2D — here the emphasis is *online* estimation + spin physics + multi-view fusion). Per the existing-research map, no factor-graph ball-physics work is in the corpus.
