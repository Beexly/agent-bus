## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Patent forensics on the six-patent Sportvision/FOX telestration family (yellow first-down line, glowing puck, bat-speed radar, etc.), researched 2026-09-29 with all facts verified on Google Patents and contemporaneous press. Bottom line: all six patents expired by normal 20-year term completion (2017–2019), not by fee lapse or abandonment; the yellow-line tech was a commercial success acquired by SMT in 2016 and still in active use on NFL broadcasts.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
### Per-patent claim coverage (all expired by 20-year term; none lapsed or abandoned)
1. **US7075556B1 "Telestrator system"** — Filed 1999-10-21, granted 2006-07-11, expired 2019-10-21. Inventors: Kevin R. Meier, Walter Hsiao, James R. Gloudemans, Marvin S. White, Richard H. Cavallaro, Stanley K. Honey. Covers: human operator draws on live video; system smooths drawing, converts to 3D field coordinates so it appears painted on the field even when camera moves/switches (claims 1–5, 12–15); blends without covering players (claim 3); color-map keyer + flicker filter compositing (claims 9–11, 24–32); dependent claims: tessellation when zoomed past threshold (claim 2), Bezier-spline stroke smoothing (claim 23). Does NOT cover: pre-drawn graphics not manually created by a human during the live event (claim 1 is human-drawing-triggered); pure 2D screen-space graphics; non-color-map compositing (depth buffers, segmentation masks, neural segmentation); automatic drawing generation from tracking data without human touch-screen input. Limitations: requires camera sensors (optical shaft encoders, inclinometers, fiber-optic gyros, lens/zoom/2x-extender telemetry) and camera attitude calibration; pattern-recognition/image-beacon alternatives only sketched, not enabled.
2. **US5953077A "System for displaying an object that is not visible to a camera"** (FoxTrax glowing-puck lineage) — Filed 1997-01-17, granted 1999-09-14, expired 2017-01-17. Assignee: Fox Sports Productions LLC → Sportsmedia Technology Corp. Inventors: Stanley K. Honey, Richard H. Cavallaro, Terence J. O'Brien, Timothy P. Heidmann, Patrick W. Olsen, James R. Gloudemans. Covers: obstruction detection (build a line-of-position from camera to target, check whether it passes through a barrier surface — claims 28–29); show target location by keying in a second image or adding a highlight (claims 1, 6–8); target position from a separate sensor plus field-of-view sensors (claims 20–23). Does NOT cover: enhancing an already-visible object (claim 1 requires obstruction); camera-only sensing. Limitations: requires co-located IR/detection sensors separate from broadcast cameras plus field-of-view sensors (per-arena installs); the glowing puck was a viewer-rejected product — core mechanic sound, product wrapped around it not.
3. **US6133946A "System for determining the position of an object"** — Filed 1998-03-11 (priority 1998-01-06), granted 2000-10-17, expired 2018-03-11. Covers: operator points at object in images from two cameras; rays through pointed pixels to common coordinate system; point of closest approach (claim 1); single-camera pre-calibrated pixel→height lookup table (claim 37); subtract player height for jump height (claims 2, 9); overlay graphic on replay video (claim 5). Does NOT cover: automatic detection — independent claims are operator-in-the-loop; anything not measuring vertical position. Limitations: post-hoc replay tool, not real-time tracking; accuracy bounded by camera registration quality, operator pointing precision, frame synchronization between cameras (time-code readers); spec includes full camera-mapping/registration flowcharts (FIGs. 7–9) and FIG. 10 "minimizing registration error" — the math only converges after calibration.
4. **US6292130B1 "System for determining the speed and/or timing of an object"** — Filed 1999-04-09, granted 2001-09-18, expired 2019-04-09. Covers: bat-swing speed and pitch timing via multiple radar units (apparatus claim 40: five at batter level behind home plate plus one above); FFT of returns; pitch-start from peak intensities meeting pitch criteria; end-of-pitch via predicted end-of-flight + "end of line" points in a window; "bat flashes" in radar returns for max bat speed (claims 1–8, 19–22); 3D velocity vectors and trajectory estimation (claims 13, 27–28). Does NOT cover: radar-free speed measurement (optical/CV tracking, RFID, wearables); single-radar with a different scheme; true real-time display. Limitations: claim 9 — detection + determination "within 5 seconds" (near-real-time, designed for between-pitch broadcast insertion, not in-play); six-radar behind-the-plate installation per stadium (heavy fixed infrastructure); bat-flash identification in noisy Doppler returns is heuristic-based (predefined pitch criteria, predicted windows) — where accuracy and false-positive risk live.
5. **US6229550B1 "System for blending a graphic"** (the yellow-line compositing engine) — Filed 1998-09-24, granted 2001-05-08, expired 2018-09-24. Covers: operator picks a video region; system records its colors; per-frame pixel-color comparison blends the graphic via "inclusions" (colors to draw over) and "exclusions" (colors never to draw over, e.g., uniforms) with taper zones and per-pixel or per-polygon alpha blending coefficients (claims 1, 16–26); sampled region can differ from target region (claims 3–4); color table re-samplable mid-broadcast (claim 15). Does NOT cover: blending without live color comparison (pre-computed/learned blending); non-color-comparison segmentation (semantic masks, depth maps); single hard-edged binary chroma key — claims require degrees of blending via coefficients. Limitations: colorimetry breaks under snow, glare, team-color collisions, uniforms with grass-like colors; changing weather/light forces operator re-sampling during the event. Litigation: Sportvision/Fox sued PVI (Oct 1999, C.D. Cal.); PVI counterclaimed (Aug 2001); settled with cross-licensing 2002 (SBJ) — PVI's L-VIS (image-recognition, no stadium hardware) was never suppressed.
6. **US6466275B1 "System for enhancing a video presentation of an event with event information"** — Filed 1999-04-16, granted 2002-10-15, expired 2019-04-16. Covers: split graphics pipeline — sensors at stadium, heavy processing at a remote studio; camera-view data (pan/tilt/zoom/roll/focus/extender sensors, 40,000-count optical encoders) time-code-synced at venue, embedded in vertical blanking interval or sent over phone/cable/satellite lines, frame-matched to program video remotely (claims 1–8, 13–14, 25–31); one central facility serving many simultaneous events (claim 13). Does NOT cover: all-at-venue processing (the remote-location limitation is the claims' defining feature); enhancement without camera-view sensor data (pure image-based tracking). Limitations: per-event equipment expensive ("four sets of equipment... trucks to house/transport"); claims repeatedly require receiving registration information multiple times/periodically (claims 5, 10, 30) — camera-sensor drift is a known recurring failure mode requiring continuous correction; any backhaul latency breaks the frame→sensor-data match.
### Company verdict
- Expiry mechanism: normal 20-year term completion on all six (anticipated-expiration dates exactly 20 years post-filing, 2017–2019). No fee lapse, no abandonment. Assignments diligently maintained; Sportvision repeatedly pledged the portfolio as collateral to lenders 2004–2015 (Comerica, Hercules Technology Growth Capital, Escalate Capital, Velocity Venture Holdings, Multiplier Capital) — financing pressure, not IP abandonment.
- Yellow first-down line won commercially: Emmy-winning, adopted across NFL broadcasts; SMT's Super Bowl LII coverage (SVG, 2018) still ran the 1st & Ten line on NBC's Sunday Night Football, Thursday Night Football, SkyCams, goal-line cameras, and the in-stadium videoboard, plus new real-space graphics (backwards-pass line, route painting, Next Gen Stats overlays) — active development, not maintenance.
- Acquisition: SMT (SportsMEDIA Technology), Durham NC, acquired 100% of Sportvision (Chicago), deal closed October 4, 2016 (unanimously approved; financed via minority investments by Eldridge Industries, Vicente Capital Partners, CEO Gerard J. Hall); rebranded as SMT; patents assigned to Sportsmedia Technology Corp March 2017. Reported price >$25M (Bleacher Report). SMT held ~70 Sportvision patents, including PITCHf/x, RACEf/x, the 1st & Ten line.
- Continuations show R&D ran until ~2010, not 1999: US7075556 spawned continuations US7492363B2 (filed 2005), US7750901B2 (2009), US7928976B2 (2010) — telestrator line kept filing for a decade, then stopped, consistent with a company in financial restructuring heading toward sale.
- Litigation settled, not lost: Sportvision/Fox vs. PVI (1999–2002) ended in cross-licensing (SBJ, 2002) — sensor-free image-recognition implementations were never blocked by this portfolio.
- Supersession, lane by lane: PITCHf/x replaced by Statcast (TrackMan + ChyronHego) in 2017; SMT later sued MLB Advanced Media over alleged misappropriation/infringement of U.S. Patent 7,341,530 in the PITCHcast/Statcast buildout (Harvard JOLT digest); glowing puck (FoxTrax) was a viewer-rejected product dead-end; radar bat-speed and jump-height tools were always secondary products.
- Current broadcast-graphics landscape: SMT remains incumbent (live 1st & Ten lines, Next Gen Stats data integration, ISO Track image-based tracking — Indy 500, Kentucky Derby, Tour de France); competitors: ChyronHego (tracking/integration, e.g., Statcast); leagues' own data arms (NFL Next Gen Stats via RFID chips — a sensor paradigm this patent family never covered).
- The single most important technical limitation for a re-implementer: calibration/registration fragility. Sportvision's approach requires instrumented cameras (pan/tilt/zoom encoders, inclinometers, gyros) with repeated, continuous re-registration, and its blending engine is colorimetry-based, degrading under weather, lighting shifts, and uniform/field color collisions. Modern re-implementations avoid both with image-based match-moving/segmentation needing no stadium hardware — the approach PVI shipped in the 1990s and SMT's ISO Track uses today.
