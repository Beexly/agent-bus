# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0390-stochastic-modelling-of-football-matches.md

## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Maia, Pennanen, da Silva & Targino (2023, arXiv:2312.04338v1) build a Cox-process (doubly stochastic Poisson) framework for jointly modeling soccer goals, red cards, and random stoppage-time lengths, with state-dependent intensities and a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the log-likelihood is strictly concave — making MLE a convex optimization problem — and produce calibrated in-game forecasts of match results and exact scores. Verdict recorded in the file: ADAPT — the framework with state-dependent intensities and concave log-likelihood ports directly to NFL live win-probability and live spread/total surfaces; adapt the regressors from soccer state to football state (down, distance, field position, clock, timeouts, penalty shocks).

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Event processes (§3.1): event types J = {home goals, away goals, home red cards, away red cards} each follow a Cox process with intensity λ^j(t); conditional on λ^j, event times follow an inhomogeneous Poisson process. Event-time CDF/density: **Φ^j_{λ^j}(s;t) = 1 − exp(−Λ^j(s,t)); φ^j_{λ^j}(s;t) = λ^j(t) exp(−Λ^j(s,t)); Λ^j(s,t) := ∫_s^t λ^j(r) dr.**
- Full single-match likelihood (§3.3, Eq. 1): **ℒ = Π_{k=1,2} π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k! × Π_j exp(−Λ^j(0,T)) Π_l λ^j(t^j_l)**, with T = 90 + U_1 + U_2.
- Log-linear intensity (Eq. 2): **ln λ^j(t) = Σ_{i∈I^j} ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t)**. Log-linear stoppage parameter (Eq. 3): **ln π_k = Σ_{i∈I^k_s} ξ_i φ_i**.
- Parametric log-likelihood (§4.3): **ℓ(ξ) = −Σ_j[∫_0^T exp(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(r))dr − Σ_l(Σ_i ξ^j_i ψ^j_i(t^j_l))] + Σ_{k=1,2}[U^k Σ_i ξ_i φ_i − exp(Σ_i ξ_i φ_i) − ln(U^k!)]**.
- **Theorem 1:** ℓ is concave on ℝ^I; strictly concave under linear independence of regressors → unique MLE, solvable with standard convex optimization (linear equality constraints, e.g. attack/defence normalization Σ_k ln α_k = Σ_k ln β_k, preserve convexity).
- Stoppage time: T = 45 + U_1 + 45 + U_2; U_k ~ Poisson(π_k); ℒ(π_k|U_k) = π^{U_k} e^{−π_k}/U_k!. Red-card power law: **λ^j(t) = e^{ξ^j_{i_0}} t^{ξ^j_{i_1}}**.
- Regressors (§5): static — Maher-style attack/defence/home-advantage indicators; team market-value log-ratio (ln V^h − ln V^a); dynamic — score difference N^h(t) − N^a(t); red-card difference N^{ar}(t) − N^{hr}(t); second-half indicator 1_{t≥45+u}; stoppage-time regressors: goals and red cards in the half + close-game indicator 1_{|N^h − N^a| ≤ 1}; red-card intensity: constant + ln t.
- Fitting: five nested goal models G0–G4 (static Maher → +values → +half-time → +goals → +red cards; 67→71 parameters); six stoppage models S0–S5; MOSEK primal-dual interior-point solver through R CVXR. Fitting 78 parameters to 3,039 matches took <10 s on an Intel i7-10700F with 32 GB RAM.
- Forecasting (§7): for each match simulate 100,000 scenarios from minutes 0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 — simulate the four processes, take the earliest event, update state, repeat; simulate stoppage time at minute 45. Nonhomogeneous red-card processes simulated by the inversion method. No lookahead (only information available at forecast minute).

## Data sources named
- **Campeonato Brasileiro de Futebol Série A, 2015–2022:** 3,039 matches, 7,126 goals (4,248 home), 717 red cards (434 to away players; cards to coaches/bench players excluded). Outcomes: home win 1,468 (48.3%), away win 746 (24.5%), draw 825 (27.1%).
- Sources: match/goal/red-card info from **Flashscore**; stoppage-time data from official match reports on the **CBF website**; starting-lineup player market values from **Transfermarkt** (Table 1: mean starting-11 market value in millions of euros, e.g., Flamengo €49.08M/8 seasons/4.00, Paraná €5.08M/1 season/20.00).
- Event times at minute granularity (discrete); stoppage times in whole minutes per half. No code repository and no downloadable data files stated.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Nested-model comparison: all nested LRT p-values ≈ 0 (G0→G4). G4 preferred by both AIC and BIC despite rising parameter count (Table 4: G0 LL −38,290 / AIC 76,713 / BIC 77,174 vs G4 LL −38,191 / AIC 76,523 / BIC 77,011).
- Headline effects (Abstract/§6.1): (a) a red card reduces the receiving team's goal intensity by **30.48%** (exp ξ = 1.4385 for the *opponent's* intensity increase); (b) losing by one goal raises a team's goal rate by **1/0.9082 = 1.1011 (+10.11%)**, losing by two by **1/0.9082² = 1.2124 (+21.24%)**; a team leading by one has its rate ×0.9082, by two ×0.8248; (c) second-half stoppage time is on average **>1 minute longer** when the score difference ≤1 (S5 goal-difference coefficient 1.2814; tied vs 3–0 example: 1.2935 fewer extra minutes when 3–0).
- Home advantage: 1.5140 → home goal intensity +51.40% vs away, all else equal; second-half goal rate +20.62% (1.2062); team value: doubling squad value multiplies goal rate by 1.1454^{ln 2} = 1.0987; 10× value → ×1.3670.
- Stoppage-time base rates (S0): first half 2.6680 min, second half 4.8066 min expected. Goals-per-half have essentially no effect on stoppage length (coefficients ~1.0, S1/S2).
- Red cards (§6.3): home red-card intensity at minute 15 = e^{−12.6054}·15^{1.4275} = 1.6010×10^{−4}/min; at minute 75 = 1.5929×10^{−3}/min (~10× higher). Away rate at minute 75 = 2.5092×10^{−3}/min (~1.5× home). Away exceeds home after minute 8.
- Descriptives: 3.4% of goals in first-half stoppage time, 6.7% in second-half stoppage time; >7% of total match duration is stoppage time; goal rates higher and increasing in the second half; red-card rates also increase over time.
- Draw calibration: G4S5R predicts 27.48% draws vs 27.42% observed — far better than Maher's original static models (24.46% / 27.32% on 27.92% observed). G4S5R beats simpler models on both result and exact-score likelihood from minute 30 onward (Figures 10–11, normalized by 2,611); the team-value regressor's value decays by minute 75 (starting-lineup values become stale after substitutions — paper's own interpretation).
- Team strength: Palmeiras highest attack/defence ratio (1.4872), then Flamengo (1.3420); ratio correlates with mean league placement (Figure 9).
- Forecasting exercise: 2,611 matches × 100,000 simulated scenarios at 6 in-match time points; scoring by predictive log-likelihood of results and exact scores (rolling-window out-of-sample for §7.1 likelihood comparison; the calibration checks in Tables 11–13 are in-sample fits — noted as a limitation in the file).
- Minute-by-minute probability paths for two illustrative matches (Figures 12–13); empirical-probability calibration: simulated vs observed outcome proportions (Tables 11–13).
- Limitations recorded: minute-granularity collapses multiple events per minute; team-value regressor stale after substitutions; 33 Brazilian Série A teams, 8 seasons — no cross-league validation (Paraná: attack 0.0454 on 1 season); red-card intensity is a pure function of time with no dependence on match state (score, fouls, referee identity — a known GSE gap for NFL crew effects); AIC/BIC comparisons between non-nested stoppage models (S2 vs S5) not strictly valid (authors concede); soccer-specific regressors don't transfer to NFL (no stoppage-time concept, no sendings-off equivalent, discrete play structure).

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- **OTHER (calibration/sizing lane — the flagship connection):** This is the methodological seed for the research-map gap list item 7 ("In-play / live NFL spread & total modeling — iWinRNFL covers in-game WP; live spread/total probability surfaces are thin"). Build an NFL live-game Cox/Hawkes-style event model as GSE's live probability engine: events J = {home TD, away TD, home FG, away FG} + intensity shocks {key-player injury/ejection, weather change, referee flags}; regressors = score differential, possession state (down/distance/field position), time remaining, timeouts, pregame spread-implied strength, turnover/injury shock indicators, pace. The strict-concavity guarantee (Theorem 1) gives fast, globally-optimal fitting of live models.
- **COACHING (game-state coaching effects):** The paper's quantified state effects (trailing raises scoring rate +10%/+21% at −1/−2 goals; leading depresses it ×0.9082/×0.8248) are the soccer analog of in-game aggression/coaching-behavior shifts — the NFL port should estimate the analogous down/distance/time/score state effects, which are directly coaching-tendency parameters (4th-down aggression, hurry-up usage, etc.).
- **OTHER (serving architecture):** The paper's §7 simulation protocol (from any live state, simulate 100k forward paths, earliest event, update, repeat → live WP, live spread/total distributions) is the serving template; inference is embarrassingly parallel; pre-match fit weekly; stream nflverse live play-by-play as the state updater.
- **OTHER (momentum / discovery lane):** The file's improvement experiment adds **Hawkes self-exciting terms for momentum bursts** — the paper assumes intensities depend on state but not recent event *rate*; adding a decaying excitation term tests the momentum question the GSE discovery lane has been probing (Koopman/DMD momentum was REJECTED; a Hawkes test is the clean next attempt). Also: **market-implied regressors** — replace Transfermarkt values with live market-implied team strength (de-vigged moneyline/spread) as a dynamic regressor, fusing with GSE's market-relative learning lane.
- Not duplicate of iWinRNFL (1704.00197): different method; this adds convex fitting + event-shock dynamics.
- File's acceptance gate: mechanism check reproducing the three headline effects within ±2 pp, NFL transfer beating pregame-spread-carry baseline on mean per-game log-likelihood (paired t-test, p < 0.05) over 2024–2025, and final-margin distribution calibration passing χ² goodness-of-fit at α = 0.05.
