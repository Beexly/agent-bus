# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0400-a-hybrid-approach-for-tracking-individual.md

## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Castro, Andrade & Fraguela (2020, *Pattern Recognition*, arXiv:2003.03271v2) combine a costly-but-accurate deep detector with fast-but-fragile classical trackers into a hybrid that tracks one individual player through unedited broadcast soccer video in real time, unsupervised — a slow player-specific Faster-RCNN run every `jump` frames supervises a per-frame KCF tracker with SSD refinement, correcting drift only after >3 consecutive frames of disagreement. Verdict recorded in the file: ADAPT — port the architectural pattern (slow accurate detector supervising a fast per-frame tracker) to GSE's NFL clip/telestration video pipeline; do not build it as a tracking-data source (it cannot replace NGS).

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Three-model hybrid at different frequencies: (1) **Faster-RCNN** (player-specific) runs once every `jump` frames — its output box initializes tracking automatically and periodically corrects drift; (2) the Faster-RCNN box crops a reduced **region of interest (ROI)**; (3) **SSD** (player-specific) runs on every remaining `jump−1` frames on the ROI to refine/update it; (4) **KCF (Kernelized Correlation Filter)** runs every frame inside the ROI for fine-grain tracking, producing the per-frame output.
- Drift correction rule: KCF's box is adjusted only when it **diverges from SSD for more than 3 consecutive frames** — adjusting on first disagreement was found to make the solution unstable; the 3-frame rule was obtained experimentally.
- Design evolution: Faster-RCNN on every frame was infeasible (45 ms/frame; successful detection in only ~20% of frames in development tests); KCF alone required human supervision and drifted after seconds; Kalman filters "failed to capture camera movements, or situations where players change of direction or speed up suddenly"; Meanshift/Camshift lasted only seconds.
- Per-frame precision: average overlap via **Intersection over Union, Φ(Λ^G, Λ^T) = {Φ_t}_{t=1}^{N}, with φ = (R_t^G ∩ R_t^T) / (R_t^G ∪ R_t^T)**, where R_t^G = ground-truth box, R_t^T = tracker box. Success threshold: **IoU > 0.5**.
- **OPE (One Pass Evaluation) curve**: percentage of frames with IoU above a threshold across thresholds; **AUC** = integral of the OPE curve, in [0,1], higher is better.
- Explicit assumption: rectangular bounding box is the tracking output. Implicit: motion locality (tight ROI contains the player); sudden edge-to-edge jumps rare; one detection box per frame; Faster-RCNN confidence <0.6 treated as no detection (assigned overlap 0 in baseline figure).
- No other equations stated — engineering-integration study, not mathematical.

## Data sources named
- **SoccerNet dataset** (Giancola et al. 2018), full HD 1920×1080 at 60 fps, broadcast soccer. Test set: 4 individual players from 2 teams, each with a "specially challenging" sequence balanced for on-camera time. Ground truth: hand-labeled frames on challenging segments (e.g., a 200-frame subset for the Faster-RCNN-only baseline).
- Training: 1,500 labeled frames per player (4 players), generated semi-automatically with an OpenCV-based tool (KCF on short sequences, stopped when it lost the player).
- Hardware: inference — NVIDIA GTX 1080 Ti, Intel i7-8700K, 16 GB RAM; training — NVIDIA Tesla K20m 5 GB, 2× Intel Xeon E5-2660, 64 GB RAM.
- No code repository, no link to per-player training sets or hand-labeled evaluation sequences (proprietary to the study).

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Faster-RCNN alone: ~**45 ms/frame** (insufficient for 30 fps at 33 ms/frame or 60 fps at 16 ms/frame); IoU > 0.5 in only ~**45%** of the 200 challenging hand-labeled frames; **AUC 0.32**, "far from the state of the art."
- Hybrid tracker (Table 2): Player 1 — avg overlap 0.620, **91.75 fps**, AUC 0.610, 2 lost frames; Player 2 — 0.653, 84.98, 0.651, 0; Player 3 — 0.650, 86.65, 0.660, 0; Player 4 — 0.600, 87.36, 0.600, 0.
- IoU ≥ 0.5 achieved in **80–90%** of frames; AUC "always around 0.6," described as comparable to SOTA (SiamVGG 0.610–0.654 on OTB benchmarks, but at ≤35 fps — vs the hybrid's 80+ fps on harder material).
- Abstract claims **80 fps** average on 1920×1080 video ("more than 60 times... 80 times on average per second").
- Jump-size study: `jump` = 3 gives the highest AUC (**0.64**) but large and unstable per-frame time; larger jumps reduce AUC while frame time falls and stabilizes.
- Crop-size study: varying the SSD crop has **no significant influence** on accuracy or frame time in practice.
- Training cost per player (Tables 3–4, Tesla K20m): Faster-RCNN 15–21.19 GPU-hours (164,166–200,000 steps; loss 0.0014–0.0065); SSD 21.71–46.9 GPU-hours (93,768–200,000 steps; loss 1.0371–1.3549).
- UNCERTAIN / limitations recorded: accuracy computed only on hand-selected "specially challenging" segments and 200-frame subsets — no full-sequence error rates (cherry-picking risk); tiny test sample (4 players, 2 teams, one sport, one broadcast style); **scalability wall** — per-player networks at ~15–47 GPU-hours each on 1,500 semi-automatically labeled frames; tracking an NFL roster (53 players × 32 teams) is infeasible this way; **no identity resolution** (no jersey-number OCR, no re-identification — useless for full-field multi-player broadcast tracking); single 2D camera view cannot recover 3D positions/velocities/separations the way multi-camera NGS optical tracking does — not a substitute for tracking data; baseline asymmetry (SiamVGG SOTA numbers measured on different, easier OTB data — cross-dataset comparison); detector confidence 0.6 threshold unexplained; NFL broadcast has more frequent occlusions (LOS pile-ups) and faster direction changes than soccer.
- File's port design: replace per-player Faster-RCNN/SSD with a **generic person detector + jersey-number/OCR re-ID** (e.g., modern YOLO + jersey-number classifier) every `jump` = 5 frames; fast KCF-equivalent (or ByteTrack-style association) per frame inside ROI; keep the >3-consecutive-frame disagreement rule; zero per-player training; feeds telestration overlays for GSE's seconds-long transformative highlight clips (per the standing video rule).
- File's acceptance gate: adopt into the clip pipeline only if hybrid achieves **AUC ≥ 0.55** AND beats tracker-alone baseline by **≥0.10 AUC** AND sustains **≥50 fps** at 1080p; reject if AUC < 0.45, supervision adds <0.05 AUC, or identity switches (>1 s wrong player) occur in >2 of 20 test clips.
- File's improvement experiment: add a **jersey-number + team-color re-identification head** on the slow detector so the system tracks "the player wearing #17 in white" rather than a learned appearance embedding; test on clips with two same-team players in frame simultaneously; adopt if identity switches cut by ≥50% — this fixes the scaling wall (no per-player training ever needed).
