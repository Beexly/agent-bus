# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1357-online-learning-in-betting-markets-profit.md

## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Deep-research ledger (full 32-page read incl. appendices) of arXiv:2406.04062v1 (Zhu, Soen, Cheung, Xie, 2024, cs.GT): "Online Learning in Betting Markets: Profit versus Prediction" — proves profit-maximization and information-elicitation are fundamentally incompatible in binary betting markets with Kelly bettors, and gives online pricing algorithms with regret guarantees for both unfair-odds (bookmaker profit) and fair-odds (prediction market) regimes. Verdict ADAPT — but inverted, since GSE is the bettor/information platform, not the house.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Bettors: Kelly bettors arriving one per round with belief p_t; p̄_t is an unbiased estimator of E[p_t] (eq. 2); profit function u_t(a, b) (eq. 3); key quantity Υ^R(x) = x + G(x) − E[p_t | p_t ≥ x], roots are critical points.
- Algorithm 1 (unfair odds, Sec. 4): stochastic approximation `a_{t+1} = a_t + η_t(p̄_t − a_t − G(a_t))` (similarly b_t); stays in [g, 1]; converges to a local maximizer of profit w.p. 1 (Thm. 4.3); local minimizers avoided via Pemantle (1990, Thm. K.5), boundary points {0,1} avoided via Renlund (2010, Thm. K.6) with Lemma K.7/K.8.
- Algorithm 2 (fair odds, Sec. 5): Follow-the-Leader-type (after Frongillo et al. 2012); fair-odds profit u(a, 1−a) concave in a with closed-form maximizer (eq. 27): `a⋆ = √(g·E[p_t]) / (√(g·E[p_t]) + √((1−g)(1−E[p_t])))`; empirical mean p̄_T plugged in, clipped to (τ, 1−τ).
- Regret: Thm. 4.4 (unfair): `E[u_T(a_T,b_T)] ≥ u_t(a♯,b♯) − 7L_u·T^{−1/2}`, (a♯,b♯) the worst local maximizer — O(√T) regret vs worst local max. Thm. 5.1 (fair): `u_t(a_T,b_T) ≥ u_t(a⋆,b⋆) − L·T^{−1/2}·√log(1/δ)` with probability ≥ 1−δ. Cor. 5.2: `REGRET(T, a⋆, b⋆) = O(√(T log T))`.
- Core economic insight: bookmaker profit hinges on the deviation between bettor-belief distribution and true beliefs; heavier tails in the bettor belief distribution ⇒ higher profit (abstract, Sec. 2.2).
- Regret machinery: descent lemma (L.2), Chung (1954) recurrence lemma (L.3), noise-control via Mertikopoulos et al. (2020) (App. M), Azuma–Hoeffding for the fair-odds martingale (App. N).
- Assumptions: bettors are exact Kelly optimizers with independent beliefs; one bettor per round; bookmaker knows its own belief g; fair-odds analysis needs g, E[p_t] ∈ (τ, 1−τ) and bounded importance weights w_t; Thm. 4.3 needs Υ^R to have finitely many roots and no saddle points.

## Data sources named
- No real-world data — theory + simulations. Simulations (Sec. 6): 10^5 = 100,000 sequential Kelly bettors with mixture beliefs p_t = sigmoid(s_t), s_t ∼ 0.25·N(2,1) + 0.75·N(−1,1) (bimodal, one mode each side of bookmaker belief g = 0.5, asymmetric); learning rate η_{t+1} = 300/(t+5000); four price initializations ((0.9,0.9), (0.6,0.6), (0.9,0.6), (0.6,0.9)); baseline = risk-balancing heuristic (Levitt 2004: equalize dollars wagered per outcome, Appendix F.I).
- Code: https://github.com/haiqingzhu543/Betting-Market-Simulation-2024.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Algorithm 1: under all four initializations, regret stays ≤ 10² over 10^5 iterations; risk-balancing regret is larger by more than an order of magnitude and keeps increasing (Fig. 2 middle, log-scale y-axis).
- All SA price trajectories converge to the global maximizer when it is unique; for t ≥ 10⁴ trajectories stay within the innermost profit contour (Fig. 2 right) — verifying Thms. 4.3/4.5 empirically.
- FTL (Algorithm 2) converges to a point between the bookmaker's belief g = 0.5 and the crowd's average belief; LMSR price dynamics approximately converge to the crowd's average belief — "the bookmaker exploits the bias of bettors to maximise the profit."
- Authors note: with multiple modes on each side of g, Algorithm 1 can get stuck at local maximizers (the Thm. 4.4 "worst local maximizer" guarantee bites).
- Profit-vs-information incompatibility: the paper proves profit-maximization and information-elicitation are fundamentally incompatible.
- Limitations: bettors modeled as exact Kelly optimizers (real bettors noisier, stake-constrained, heterogeneous — profit theorems may overstate bookmaker power against real flow); one-bettor-per-round ignores simultaneous correlated flow (steam moves, syndicate action) that dominates real line movement; "heavier tails ⇒ higher profit" is distributional/qualitative with no mapping to observable order-flow statistics; single synthetic belief mixture, no sensitivity analysis over tail thickness despite it being the headline insight; GSE is not a bookmaker — direct deployment would mean building a sportsbook, not a prediction product.
- Acceptance gate (ledger §13): ADAPT the shading-monitor if, on one full NFL season, games flagged "book already shaded against informed flow" show GSE's realized CLV ≥ 2 points worse than unflagged games; otherwise REJECT the defensive application, keep only the FTL-consensus benchmark.
- Improvement experiment: invert the model — estimate the bookmaker's belief g per market from the observed opening→closing price path (inverse SA), compare implied g against GSE's model probability; systematic gaps conditioned on the tail-heaviness profit theory become a direct edge-detection signal.
