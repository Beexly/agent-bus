## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
**Thesis verdict: MIXED — run-identity half supported, rotation half refuted.**
- 2024 Week 1: only 2 QBs topped 300 yards (vs 6 in 2023 W1); 8 QBs hit 250+ (vs 13 in 2023). Running was more efficient than passing; two-high shells forced teams to run. Sub-claim "run teams run more in Week 1": SUPPORTED.
- PFF 2021–25 opening-play run rates for run-heaviest teams: Chiefs 73.0%, Patriots 66.7%, Giants 62.5%, Lions 60.4%, Falcons 60.0%. "Offenses are noticeably more likely to run the ball to begin games."
- 2025 neutral pass rates are structural: ARI 65.3%, KC 63.1%, CIN 61.9%, LAC 61.2% vs BAL 50.1%, NYJ 49.9%. "These don't flip week to week." Pass teams stay pass-heavy: SUPPORTED (by stability).
- "Use two running backs when they run": REFUTED for Week 1. Week 1 CONCENTRATES backfields: 2025 W1 — Chase Brown 91.3% of team rush attempts, Tony Pollard 85.7%, Kyren Williams 91% snap share (PFF). Coaches trust veterans/lead backs in openers; committees settle later.
- "Spread it around when passing": UNVERIFIED — no Week-1-specific target-concentration data found in free sources.
- "Heavy-TE teams lean on TEs": PLAUSIBLE, unverified — no Week-1 TE-target-share study found free; directionally consistent with run-identity logic (play-action + TE usage correlate) but marked as hypothesis.
- Identity caveat — new schemes on this slate: BAL (Jesse Minter era begins), ATL (Kevin Stefanski era), LAC (Mike McDaniel now OC). "Revert to identity" is weakest for these three — their 2025 pace numbers describe the OLD regime.
