# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0582-developing-a-ranking-problem-library-rplib.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A methods paper (arXiv:2206.11258v1, Anderson et al. 2022) presenting RPLIB — a living, searchable successor to the static LOLIB linear-ordering library — as a shared database of ranking-problem instances (dominance matrices + JSON model cards + ranking algorithms), and introducing a fourth rankability measure β that locates *where* in a ranking the ambiguity among multiple optimal solutions sits. Deep-read verdict: ADAPT — adopt the rankability framework (k, |P|, τ, β) as a diagnostic for GSE's own NFL team-ranking instances, not the library's data.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Rankability extended from r = f(k, |P|, τ) to r = f(k, |P|, τ, β), where: k = distance of D̄ from a perfectly rankable matrix; |P| = number of optimal rankings; τ = diameter of the optimal set (Kendall-tau distance between the two farthest optimal rankings); β = new measure of the *location* of indecision among multiple optimal rankings.
- β: a weighted function of the number and location of fractional entries of the X̄* matrix from the linear ordering model — penalises fractional entries that are further from the diagonal and lower in the ranking. Lower β is better. (The exact closed-form formula is presented as a figure, not typeset text, in the read extract; the read reports the paper's verbal definition rather than inventing the formula.)
- Demonstration construct: a dominance matrix built with beginning/ending row indices 6 and 10 has (10−6+1)! = 5! = 120 multiple optimal rankings.
- RPLIB classes: (a) optimization — linear ordering problem (LOP) and hillside variant, outputting optimal objective value, the set P of (all/many) optimal rankings, diameter τ, centroid-nearest/farthest rankings, the X̄* matrix, and the indecision-location measure; (b) linear algebra — Massey and Colley systems, outputting rating vector, ranking vector, and a Ȳ*(i,j) certainty matrix (certainty i is ranked above j), plus pseudo-optimal ranking sets. Outputs stored as JSON model cards with Colab notebooks (pixel plots of reordered X̄*, spaghetti plots between farthest optimal rankings). Elo and learning-to-rank listed as future additions.
- D̄(i,j) = # times i beat j (square dominance matrices). Rectangles converted to pairwise dominance counts.

## Data sources named
RPLIB at https://igards.github.io/RPLib/ (public library, code downloadable, Colab notebooks for artificial-data generation, user contributions via form/GitHub PR). Static real data: U.S. college basketball season game scores incl. March Madness, Japan economic input-output matrices (1995, 2005), cleaned LOLIB/XLOLIB instances, US News & World Report liberal-arts college features; sizes n=10 to hundreds. Dynamic artificial data: generated in Colab with tunable structure — empty matrix, empty+noise, fully connected ± noise, perfect-ranking hillside/dominance + noise, cyclic matrices, matrices engineered to have c! multiple optimal rankings with ambiguity at top/middle/bottom, upset-simulated game matrices (example call: emptyplusnoise(5,20,2,4)). Prior work: Anderson et al. 2021 "The rankability of weighted data from pairwise comparisons," Foundations of Data Science (source for the β formula); [1–4] on k/|P|/τ → March Madness predictability correlation.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- 98% of the 50 instances in the LOLIB IO folder have more than one optimal ranking, though LOLIB stores only one — the paper's motivating statistic for storing full optimal sets.
- Example model card (dataset_id 363): 1,095 optimal rankings, objective value 204, D is 65×65 (Gonzaga row shown).
- Four artificial D̄₁…D̄₄ matrices engineered to have identical k, |P|, τ but different β — β separates them while the other three measures cannot (Figure 11).
- March Madness 2002 vs 2008 X̄* matrices: 2002 shows little top-of-ranking disagreement, 2008 shows top-of-ranking disagreement, consistent with prior finding that rankability correlates with tournament predictability (fewer upsets in more rankable years). Exact β numbers are figure-rendered, not stated in text; 2002 lower (better) than 2008 at the top.
- UNCERTAIN / limitations: β's exact formula is figure-presented; the rankability→predictability link is correlational on March Madness only; LOP optimal-face enumeration is exponential — for large n only a partial set P is stored, so |P|, τ, β are lower bounds on true ambiguity; the library's NFL-relevant content is nil (college basketball only); β's top-vs-bottom weighting is a value judgment, not derived; no guidance on combining (k,|P|,τ,β) into a scalar r — an open question.
- NFL transfer caveat: NFL seasons produce tiny dominance matrices (32 teams, 17 games each, sparse) — LOP on such sparse D̄ will have enormous optimal sets, making |P|/τ uninformative without regularisation; measures are most useful on dense comparison graphs (pairwise unit-matchup win rates across many games, or model-vs-model comparisons).

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER (calibration/sizing — the strongest link): the read states that rankability as a diagnostic "does not exist anywhere in Garrett's corpus" — a new diagnostic layer. Mechanism: compute (k, |P|, τ, β) on weekly NFL power-ranking instances; β's top-vs-bottom indecision weighting maps directly onto what matters (top of the ranking = playoff teams, MVP candidates, the bets that matter). A high-β week means the top-8 ordering is ambiguous → shrink edge estimates in bet sizing. This is a risk-management input, not a prediction input.
- OTHER (tracking lane / model selection): apply rankability to model-vs-model comparison matrices (which of GSE's candidate models beats which on backtests) to test whether "model A > model B" is even a rankable claim before investing engineering.
- OTHER (composes with 0572): spectral ranking (0572) produces the ranking; rankability (0582) reports whether the underlying dominance matrix supports one meaningful ranking. Both serve the team-ratings lane.
- TRUST-SIGNAL (indirect): per-team ambiguity location (top vs bottom) is exactly the kind of uncertainty disclosure that a trust-target intake consumes — if the rank order at the top is fragile, the published number should carry that caveat.
