# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0602-measuring-spatial-allocative-efficiency-in-basketball.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A framework (arXiv:1912.05129v2, Sandholtz, Mortensen & Bornn 2020) for measuring spatial allocative efficiency in basketball: a lineup is allocatively efficient at a court location if players' FG% ranks match their FGA-rate ranks (best shooter takes the most shots), quantified via Lineup Points Lost (LPL) and player LPL Contribution (PLC), with a game-level regression showing each point of misallocation costs 0.62 actual points. Deep-read verdict: ADAPT — port the rank-matching allocation framework to NFL target/touch allocation (receiver target share vs. per-route efficiency; play-call mix vs. situational EPA), dropping the Bayesian spatial machinery for empirical zone/route splits.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Verbatim equations:
- FG% surfaces (Bayesian hierarchical, Cervone et al. 2016 adaptation): logit(π_j(s)) = β'x + Z_j(s) (eq. 1), x = [intercept, player position, shot distance, position×distance]. Z_j(s) = w_j'ΛΨ(s) (eq. 2): Gaussian process with deterministic basis Λ (D=16 bases) from NMF of per-player LGCP intensity fits of made-shot locations (Miller et al. 2014; P ≈ BΛ, eq. 3), and Ψ(s) linear-interpolation mesh basis (Lindgren et al. 2011). Player weights regularized by CAR prior shrinking toward 5 nearest neighbors in NMF loading space B (symmetrized adjacency H): (w_j|w_{−(j)},τ²) ~ N(mean of neighbors, τ²/n_j·I_D), τ² ~ InvGam(1,1) (eq. 4); β ~ N(0, 0.001·I); fit by INLA. Court gridded 1ft×1ft (M=2350 cells); ξ_ij = player j's FG% at cell i centroid.
- FGA-rate surfaces: per-player, per-lineup log-Gaussian Cox process log λ(s) = β₀ + Z(s), INLA (Simpson et al. 2015); rescaled to exactly match observed attempts; normalized per 36 minutes → A_ij (deterministic, no uncertainty modeled).
- Rank correspondence: R^ξ_ij (FG% ranks 1–5 within lineup, MAP estimate) and R^A_ij (FGA ranks, deterministic) per cell (eqs. 5–6). Rank correspondence R^A − R̂^ξ ∈ [−4,4]; negative = over-usage, positive = under-usage.
- Lineup Points Lost: redistribute shot vector A_i to A*_i via permutation g(·) matching FGA ranks to FG% ranks (eq. 7–8): LPL_i = Σ_j v_i·ξ_ij·(A*_ij − A_ij); per-shot version LPL^Shot_i = LPL_i/Σ_j A_ij (eq. 9). Constraint: A*_i is a permutation of A_i (total attempts per cell preserved).
- Player LPL Contribution: PLC_ij = LPL_i × (A*_ij − A_ij)/Σ_j|A*_ij − A_ij| (eq. 10); positive = undershooting, negative = overshooting; per-shot version (eq. 11). No whole-lineup over/under-shooting by construction.
- Game-level regression (§4.2): court collapsed to 3 regions (restricted area, mid-range, 3pt); TGLPL_ag = Σ_ℓΣ_c GLPL^ℓ_c (eqs. 15–16); Score_abg = μ + α_a + β_b + γ·I(Home_ag) + θ·TGLPL_ag + ε_abg (eq. 17), ε ~ N(0,σ²) (eq. 18); Bayesian HMC in Stan; priors μ~N(100,10²), α,β,γ,θ~N(0,10²), σ~Gamma(2,0.2).
- Permutation test (§4.1): test statistic T = Σ_iΣ_j v_i·ξ̃_ij·(A_ij − A†_ij) (eqs. 12–14) vs. 500 random permutations; one-sided p̂ = fraction < 0.
- Toy example: Cavs 3-pt region LPL = 0.84 expected points. Cavaliers starting lineup total LPL = 0.68 per 36 min (cell values 0–0.008).
- Empirical appendix variant: 12 discrete regions; ad hoc +1 make / +4 misses anchor (FG% → ~20%) for low-sample cells.

## Data sources named
2016–17 NBA regular season: 224,567 shots by 433 players, from the NBA stats API (shotchartdetail endpoint for shooter + shot (x,y); playbyplayv2 for lineup construction). Lineup code: https://github.com/jwmortensen/pbp2lineup. Empirical demo: https://github.com/nsandholtz/lpl. Players with <5 shots treated as "replacement players." No train/test split (descriptive framing). No model code released for the INLA/CAR pipeline (method described only).

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Permutation test (Table 1, starting lineups): GSW and POR p̂ = 0.000 (no random allocation beat observed in 500 draws — best allocative efficiency); CLE p̂ = 0.002; SAC p̂ = 0.442 (worst); league-wide, most lineups beat random with p̂ < 0.15 — offenses minimize LPL beyond the defense's ability to prevent it.
- Game regression: θ posterior mean = −0.62, 95% HPD (−1.08, −0.17) — each additional lineup point lost costs 0.62 actual points; shaving ~3 TGLPL points ≈ +2 game points. Houston lost only ~1 point/game to misallocation (most efficient); Washington lost >3 points/game (least). 10% of 2016–17 games were decided by ≤2 points — playoff-bubble relevance.
- Cavs specifics: Kyrie Irving under-utilized from 3 (positive rank correspondence at the arc); LeBron over-shooting mid-range top-of-key — but mid-range LPL density is negligible for CLE (they barely shoot there). LPL surfaces peak around the rim and 3-point line (attempt density dominates).
- Utah Jazz case (§4.3): Derrick Favors' mid-range baseline/elbow shots (1500+ shots, 0.76 PPS over 2013–17) flagged by PLC; the Jazz's "stretch four" fix (21 threes in 4 seasons → 141 in 2017–19, at 0.66 PPS) was misguided; he wasn't re-signed after 2019 — a six-year decision LPL/PLC might have expedited.
- Westbrook OKC (§4.4): four Thunder elimination games 2017–2019 — 30+ attempts each, 45.5% average usage; extreme case 46 points on 43 attempts in a 96–91 Game 6 loss to Utah (2017–18 R1); OKC lost 3 of 4 close-out games, never escaped the first round. Positive PLC in corner threes for Westbrook looks like under-usage, but those shots are created by his drives — LPL ignores shot creation and game-theoretic predictability (D'Amour et al. 2015): minimizing LPL is diagnostic, not prescriptive.
- Simpson's-paradox finding: aggregate FGA-rate vs PPS looks flat/slightly negative, but conditioning on court region flips it positive everywhere — motivating the spatial treatment.
- UNCERTAIN / limitations: FG% lineup-independence assumption (flagged as likely violated — gravity/open shots); FG% held constant when redistributing volume (no usage/skill curve — Oliver 2004; Goldman & Rao 2011 acknowledged); A*_i constrained to permutation of A_i; rank matching (not marginal-efficiency equalization) defines the optimum; defensive pressure/shot clock unobserved; shot creation not credited; game regression is observational — θ = −0.62 likely absorbs unobserved quality, so the conversion factor is descriptive; no uncertainty on FGA side; no out-of-sample prediction.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- SCHEME + COACHING (primary mechanism): the transferable skeleton is **allocation rank vs. efficiency rank within a unit, with deviation scored in expected points**. NFL ports: (a) receiver target allocation — rank WR/TE target share (TPRR) against per-target efficiency (EPA/target, YPRR) stratified by route-depth × field zone, computing expected EPA lost = Σ_j (EPA/target)_j × (optimal_targets_j − actual_targets_j) with optimal targets = rank-matched permutation of the actual target vector; (b) play-calling — rank run/pass mix or target distribution across downs/distances/field zones against situational EPA efficiency (coaching content). Serves the props lane (weekly over/under-targeted lists) and coaching/team-ratings program.
- OTHER (tracking lane — new capability): the read states no paper in the corpus jointly evaluates allocation vs. efficiency or quantifies "points lost" to misallocation — GSE has TPRR, YPRR, target share, EPA/play, xFP/FPOE, but the rank-matching allocation framework is new. The 3×3 (route-depth × field-zone) stratification is the NFL "spatial" analog replacing the paper's 1ft×1ft court grid and INLA machinery (empirical estimates only, per the paper's appendix).
- TRUST-SIGNAL: per-player TPC (target points contribution) sign convention (positive = under-targeted, negative = over-targeted) is a directly publishable, human-interpretable diagnostic for props content — though the paper's Westbrook caution applies: flag, don't prescribe.
- CONTRADICTION / NFL-specific caveat from the read: the NFL analog of the paper's caveats is worse — a receiver's efficiency depends on coverage concentration; redistributing targets assumes efficiency holds at volume (no usage curve — in NFL, target concentration invites bracket coverage); a QB creating open targets via scrambles/play-action is the Westbrook analog — a naive port would punish the offense's engine. The read's improvement experiment (usage-curve-aware constrained optimization) is the fix: maximize Σ_j targets_j·eff_j(targets_j) subject to Σ targets_j = total team targets, estimated per-receiver by archetype.
