# data-sources/research/2026-09-17/dossiers/advanced-metrics-data-source-catalog.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A 392-line engine gap-analysis (compiled 2026-09-17) benchmarking GSE's Elo-based engine (v5.2.7, moneyline-focused, thin NFL sample) against the advanced metrics the sharpest public NFL analysts use: Part 1 catalogs ~27 metrics with definitions, predictive evidence, limitations, and Engine-relevance tags; Part 2 maps the free/paid/scraped data-source landscape with prices and license terms; Part 3 states the licensing reality (facts vs. databases, nflverse as legal foundation); and a 12-item ranked gap list closes it. Every formula, price, and license claim comes from a cited source or is flagged **unverified**.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Verbatim formulas/parameters stated in the file:
- **EPA per play (team):** EPA = change in Expected Points from before a play to after it; Expected Points = historical average net point value of a game state defined by down, distance, and field position. EPA/play = total of those values ÷ play count.
- **Success rate (three conventions):** (1) nflfastR convention: play is successful if **EPA > 0**; (2) Football Outsiders convention: gain ≥40% of line-to-gain on 1st down, ≥60% on 2nd, 100% on 3rd/4th; (3) Bill Connelly/college convention: 50%/70%/100%.
- **CPOE:** actual completion outcome (or rate) minus the model's estimated completion probability; expected rate reflects throw difficulty (air yards, field placement, other variables).
- **DAVE decay schedule (verified from FTN live tables):** after Week 1 of 2026, DAVE was **83% preseason forecast for offense and 98% for defense/special teams**; by Week 11 of 2025 it was **12% offense / 35% defense+ST**.
- **EPA+CPOE composite:** **canonical weighting/formula unverified** — no primary (Ben Baldwin) source for the exact weights was found; flagged, not specified.
- **PRWR / PBWR (2026 methodology):** PRWR = how often a rusher beats his blocker within **2.5 seconds**; PBWR = how often a blocker sustains for at least **2.5 seconds**. (2026 revision added/refined bull rushes, chips, stunts.)
- **RBWR / RSWR:** defender wins by defeating/displacing a blocker, forcing the runner to alter the lane, or making a tackle within **three yards**; responsible blocker receives the inverse result.
- **Situation-neutral pace (Football Outsiders definition):** exclude 4th quarter and final five minutes of the first half, require score within **six points**; lower seconds/play = faster.
- **Explosive-play rate (4for4 convention):** pass gains ≥15 yards and rush gains ≥10 yards.
- **Havoc rate (traditional formula):** defensive plays with a tackle for loss, forced fumble, or defended pass, divided by total defensive plays (family — no NFL standard).
- **Stuff rate:** share of rushes stopped for no gain or negative yardage (boundary varies: providers differ on whether exactly-zero-yard runs count).
- **Fourth-down aggressiveness:** observed go-for-it rate vs. a WP/EP model's recommended action, controlling for field position, yards to go, score, clock, timeouts.
- **FPI preseason prior (ESPN):** anchored on Vegas expectations (win totals, moneylines) plus prior-year unit efficiencies, returning starters, coaching/coordinator/QB changes, and QB injuries; adjusts for altitude, seasonal effects, QB injury/suspension/absence probability.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Predictive-evidence numbers, verbatim from the file:
- Dropback EPA: Advanced Football Analytics expected-wins model shows offensive passing efficiency correlation ~**0.53–0.61** with wins vs. rushing efficiency correlation ~**0.13–0.19**. A 2026 early-season study found passing EPA predicts future point differential at **r ≈ 0.42 at 6 games**, better than success rate despite stabilizing later.
- Success rate (same study): offensive success rate reaches ~**r = 0.60** stability by game 6, but is less predictive of future scoring than passing EPA.
- ESPN FPI: in 2016, FPI favorites won **73%** of regular-season games, beating the Vegas closing line's hit rate that year.
- Special-teams EPA: the Wharton study's Special Teams Performance Index cut prediction RMSE by **1.9%** when added to offense/defense ratings; special teams are ~**15%** of plays.
- Turnover luck: season-to-date turnover differential regresses toward zero (PMC meta-review); the file calls turnover regression "the single biggest upgrade available to an Elo-based engine" because record-based ratings like Elo systematically undervalue efficient teams with bad turnover records.
- Fourth-down aggressiveness: aggressive coaching captures **0.5–1.5 points/game** of systematic value the market is slow to price.
- Red-zone: Aaron Schatz's critique — red-zone *efficiency* is close to noise; red-zone *trip rate* (opportunity) is the stickier part. Schatz questioned red-zone conversion's predictive persistence (ESPN id/7170232).
- Third-down trap: a 2012 hierarchical-Bayes study found raw third-down conversion rate "a nearly meaningless measure of the efficiency of an offense"; all-downs efficiency (first downs per play) has 8-game predictive correlation with wins of ~**0.36**, the highest of the common offensive stats tested.
- Rest: the 2024 arXiv study estimates the historical bye-week advantage largely disappeared after the 2011 CBA; mini-byes showed no obvious benefit; Monday-rest differential was small.
- Pressure: PFF research — pressure *creation* is more repeatable and predicts future sacks better than prior sack totals; pressure-to-sack conversion is noisy and heavily QB/scheme-influenced.
- ESPN 2026 win-rates update claims improved year-over-year reliability and EPA correlation — use the current methodology, not the legacy description.
- DVOA: proprietary formula; expressed as percentage with league average 0%; FTN implementation canonical now; weekly numbers move retroactively with opponent adjustments; no public API.
- NGS SEP/CUSH: Average Separation = distance between WR/TE and nearest defender at catch/incompletion; Average Cushion = same distance at the snap; ESPN's all-routes (untargeted included) Open/Catch/YAC approach is the most predictive-flavored; NGS SEP conditions on targets (selection bias toward open receivers).
- Time to throw: descriptive, not normative — no "good" time to throw; must be paired with pressure data.
- Wind/weather: clearest weather relationship is wind vs. passing and kicking production and totals; effects nonlinear, interacting with stadium/roof, direction, precipitation, offensive style; avoid fixed rules like "X mph = Y points."
- Altitude: no robust NFL-specific coefficient verified; ESPN FPI includes an altitude adjustment (weak evidence the effect is nonzero). Assigning a number without backtesting is fabrication.
- CPOE limitation: model-dependent; the public nflfastR CPOE is proprietary to that model's specification.
- SIS Total Points: EPA-denominated cross-position player valuation; allocation choices are judgment calls; inaccessible without an SIS relationship.
- SIS blown block: **primary SIS glossary not located — treat exact wording as unverified** (definition from secondary sources: Mile High Report, Silver and Black Pride).
- Licensing reality: facts aren't copyrightable; compiled databases and proprietary grades/metrics (PFF grades, DVOA, FPI, Total Points) are protected IP. Computing your own EPA from nflverse play-by-play is clean; republishing PFF's grades table is not. nflverse is the engine's legal foundation — the only source that affirmatively grants reuse. Scraped tables used internally for research are low-risk; publishing or serving them via an API is not.
- The 12-item ranked gap list for v5.2.7 (expected lift for moneyline/spread/total, thin sample): 1. Opponent-adjusted efficiency (HIGH). 2. Turnover regression (HIGH). 3. Dropback vs. rush EPA split (HIGH). 4. Explosive-play differential (HIGH). 5. Early-season shrinkage (HIGH) — DAVE's lesson: blend observed efficiency toward a Vegas-anchored preseason prior with decaying weight. 6. Pressure/win-rate matchup features (HIGH). 7. Special-teams EPA adjustment (MEDIUM). 8. Pace + weather for totals (HIGH for totals). 9. Fourth-down aggressiveness coaching prior (MEDIUM). 10. CPOE QB priors (MEDIUM–HIGH). 11. Rest/travel/altitude (LOW). 12. PFF grades / SIS charting (MEDIUM, paid) — first paid upgrade once the free stack is exhausted; PFF Pro $199.99/yr is the cheapest legitimate path to grade-level data.
- **Caveat that applies to everything:** predicting *team quality* ≠ predicting *covers*; efficient markets already price public info (open EPA, FPI, PFF grades), so metrics enter as feature candidates for a spread/total model — the edge is implementation quality (opponent adjustment, regression of luck, timeliness), not knowing the metric exists.
- **CONTRADICTION to note:** the alignment synthesis (2026-09-28) records NGS metrics as internal-reasoning-fuel-only under a HARD internal-only doctrine — this catalog's "free via nflverse `load_nextgen_stats`" framing for SEP and time to throw is compatible with internal use, but the doctrine adds the constraint that no NGS data/metric names reach the public site. No contradiction on values, only on surface handling.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- **QB-BEHAVIOR — dropback EPA vs. rush EPA split is the QB program's #1 feature.** With passing efficiency correlated ~0.53–0.61 with wins vs. rushing at ~0.13–0.19, the QB-behavioral profiles lane must decompose every QB rating into dropback efficiency (weighted heavily) and rushing efficiency (weighted lightly). Mechanism: scrambles-classification convention must be stated first (4for4 convention includes sacks and scrambles in dropback EPA; rush EPA excludes scrambles) so provider numbers are comparable.
- **QB-BEHAVIOR — CPOE is the cleanest public QB-accuracy prior.** It isolates throw-difficulty-adjusted accuracy (air yards, placement) from scheme/depth-of-target, and QB accuracy persists — making it the QB-behavioral lane's team-strength prior for moneyline modeling. Build the EPA+CPOE composite in-house with explicitly defined and backtested weights (the Baldwin canonical weights are unverified).
- **COACHING — fourth-down aggressiveness as a coaching prior worth 0.5–1.5 pts/game.** The coaching-tendencies program gets a concrete adjustment variable: observed go-for-it rate vs. WP/EP prescription, controlling for field position, yards to go, score, clock, timeouts. Free to implement via nflverse + nfl4th-style decision models; it captures systematic value the market is slow to price.
- **COACHING — situation-neutral pace feeds the game-management coaching model.** Pace defined with the Football Outsiders filter (exclude 4th quarter, final 5 minutes of H1, score within 6) isolates coaching *choice* from game-script trailing/hurrying; pairs with pace matchups (fast vs. slow) for game-flow and totals modeling.
- **OL — PRWR/PBWR and pressure-rate as trench features.** Pressure *creation* is more repeatable and predicts future sacks better than sack totals (PFF) — the OL/trench program should use pressure rate and ESPN 2.5-second win-rate thresholds as matchup features rather than outcome stats; the file notes FTN charting in nflverse (free, CC-BY-SA) gives pressure data now, while PRWR/PBWR have no public feed (manual ESPN-table ingestion only).
- **OL — SIS blown-block and RBWR/RSWR as internal-only line-play reads.** Blown blocks (adjusted for opponent ability) are the attributable cause of pressures and stuffed runs; RBWR/RSWR isolate run-game line play from RB talent. Both are proprietary/charting-bound — approximate with FTN charting pressure-allowed data from nflverse.
- **TRUST-SIGNAL — family labeling as a data-integrity protocol.** Any metric labeled "family (definition varies by provider)" — dropback/rush EPA, success rate, separation/open rate, pressure rate, explosive-play rate, havoc rate, stuff rate, situation-neutral pace, SIS blown block, SOS — must carry its convention label in the engine and never be silently swapped. This is directly the trust lane's "source reliability and model accountability" proof artifact.
- **SCHEME — separation/open rate as the QB-independent passing-game read.** ESPN's all-routes Open/Catch/YAC scores don't condition on the QB's target choice (unlike NGS SEP, which selects on targets toward open receivers) — the scheme lane's receiver-corps quality signal should prefer the all-routes framing for matchup modeling.
- **OTHER — turnover regression is the calibration lane's largest documented upgrade.** Because turnovers regress toward zero, a team with great efficiency but a bad turnover record is systematically undervalued by record-based ratings like Elo — model the process (turnover-worthy throws, interception opportunities, fumble rates/recovery rates, INT rates vs. expected) rather than carrying raw margin forward. Computable from free data.
- **OTHER — early-season shrinkage operationalized by DAVE's decay schedule.** Blend observed efficiency toward a Vegas-anchored preseason prior: start at 83% prior/2% observed-defense weight shape (Week 1 2026: 83% offense / 98% defense+ST) and decay to ~12% offense / 35% defense+ST by Week 11 — adaptive decay preferred over the fixed schedule. Critical for the thin-sample engine.
- **OTHER — the calibration gate alignment.** The catalog's backtest-not-narrative posture (state conventions, backtest composites in-house, never ship a coefficient without backtesting) matches the overnight queue's `|r| >= 0.08`, `|slope| > se`, `g = 0` LIVE gates.
