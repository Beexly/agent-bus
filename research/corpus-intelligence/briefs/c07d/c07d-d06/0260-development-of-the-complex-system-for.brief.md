## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A build log (A. A. Kramov and O. S. Bauzha, underlying article 2016, arXiv:2010.13629v1) describing a low-cost (~$20 small-series) hobbyist hardware chain for real-time remote heart-rate monitoring: PPG pulse sensor → ATmega8 → Bluetooth → Arduino Uno → Ethernet → WebSocket/JSON browser display, with crude threshold-based tachycardia/bradycardia flagging. It is an engineering prototype description, not a hypothesis-driven study — no dataset, no equations, no validation, no quantitative results.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Not specified — no formulas, no equations, no statistical method. Hardware/method chain (verbatim parameters): UART serial at "38,600 baud" (sic — 38,400 is the standard rate); ATmega8 clock adjusted to 16 MHz to match Arduino timing; HC-05 Bluetooth 2.0 modules (paired); ENC28J60 Ethernet controller at 3.3 V supply with 25 MHz crystal, fed via SPI; JSON messages over WebSocket to browser client. Detection logic: threshold deviation only — measured heart rate above/below set bounds reported as tachycardia/bradycardia. Stated small-series production cost: ~$20. Photoplethysmography (PPG) defined as optical measurement of blood-volume change in microvascular tissue. No signal-processing formulation, no filtering equations, no error model, no accuracy metric defined.
