# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0280-forecasting-soccer-matches-through-distributions.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Mendes-Neves et al. (2025, arXiv:2501.05873v1) entered the 2023 Springer Soccer Prediction Challenge with a generative pipeline: compute team ELOs, regress ELO pair → per-team shot-quantity and shot-quality distributions (mean + residual-fitted σ), then Monte Carlo-simulate games to derive outcome/scoreline probabilities — finishing within 5% of the top challenge entries, with a marginal (+1.1% absolute under one betting strategy) profitability claim.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Formulas (verbatim):
- Eq. 1 (quantity distribution): Quant_H = N(μ_quant, σ_quant²), where μ_quant, σ_quant = f(ELO_H, ELO_A).
- Eq. 2 (quality distribution): Qual_H = N(μ_qual, σ_qual²), same inputs.
- Eq. 3 (simulation): hs = [S_1..S_N], S ~ Qual_H, N ~ Quant_H.
- Eq. 4 (goal conversion): Home Goals = Σ_{i=1..N} I(hs_i > X_i), X_i ~ U(0,1).
- Eqs. 5–6 (ELO update): ELO_{i(t+1)} = ELO_{it} + K(O_{ijt} − P_{ijt}); P_{ijt} = 1/(1 + 10^{−(ELO_{it}−ELO_{jt})/a}), with K=32, a=400, initial rating 500, O ∈ {1, 0.5, 0}.
Pipeline steps: Step 1, compute ELOs (K=32, a=400, initial 500). Step 2, train two regressors from (ELO_home, ELO_away) → mean of shot quantity (total shots) and mean of shot quality (goals per shot); then train second-stage models on the first model's training-set residuals to predict σ (uncertainty), assuming normal distributions. Algorithms tested: Linear Regression, KNN, Decision Tree, Random Forest (competition submission used Linear Regression due to time constraints; Random Forest performed best in development). Step 3, simulate: draw team ELO from last-10-games mean/std distribution; fill an (n_simulations × 50) matrix sampling shot quality; per-row sample shot count from quantity distribution (zero out beyond shot count); per shot draw X ~ U(0,1), goal if X < shot-quality sample; sum goals per team per simulation; outcome/scoreline probabilities from simulation frequencies. Correct-score predictions: mean-rounded, median, or mode of simulated scores (authors chose mode on MAE grounds). Validation: last 300 games; metrics RPS, RMSE, MAE; baselines: 1-1 draw for every game (RMSE 1.68, MAE 1.73 on validation), plus bookmaker baseline on test (margin ≈ −5.6%, bookmaker RPS ≈ 0.198). Betting evaluation with two flat strategies: Strategy 1 = bet 1 unit whenever bookmaker odds > model estimate; Strategy 2 = bet 1/(forecasted odds) units when bookmaker odds > model estimate.
Assumptions: shot quantity and quality follow normal distributions; quality samples i.i.d. across shots; team ELO uncertainty captured by last-10-game mean/std; non-negative integer shot counts enforced post-hoc. Not stated: dependence between quantity and quality (treated independent).

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Validation set (Table 2): RPS — LR 0.215, KNN 0.213, DT 0.215, RF 0.213; RMSE (median variant) — LR 1.76, KNN 1.73, DT 1.77, RF 1.74; MAE — LR 1.87, KNN 1.79, DT 1.83, RF 1.80. Competition submission (LR): RPS 0.216, RMSE 1.70. Challenge finish: within 5% of top entries.
- Test set (Table 3, averages of 50 runs): RPS 0.201 vs bookmaker 0.198 — the model does NOT beat bookmaker probabilities. RMSE 1.58 (median) / 1.64 (mode); MAE 1.65 / 1.66.
- Betting rentability (Table 3): Strategy 1: −0.8% absolute (+4.8% over the −5.6% bookmaker-margin baseline); Strategy 2: +1.1% absolute (+6.7% over baseline). Claims positive return with Strategy 2 only.
- Bias analysis (Figure 4): systematically underestimates draws — boosting draw likelihood by 27% improves RPS marginally.
- Limitations flagged by the authors themselves: normal-distribution assumption for shot counts is mis-specified (counts, small, skewed) — negative binomial would be more appropriate; quantity/quality independence is assumed, not tested; shot quality likely correlates with quantity (team dominance); linear models knowingly underfit; RF won in development but LR was submitted — published numbers mix the two; no date discipline or significance testing on the profitability window; no stated number of test-set games; Strategy 1 is −0.8% absolute (i.e., losing); no commission/limits realism.
## Intelligence connections
- (OTHER / TRUST-SIGNAL) The two-stage mean+residual variance estimator (mean model on labels, then a model trained on |errors| to get σ) is a cheap uncertainty estimator without Bayesian machinery — novel-to-GSE relative to its calibration stack and directly portable to NFL per-team play-count and EPA-per-play distributions; the NFL analogue should use negative binomial for play counts and normal/Student-t for EPA-per-play, with quantity/quality modeled jointly via copula or conditional EPA|pace-decile model since the paper's independence assumption is untested and likely wrong (fast high-play games tilt efficiency). Serves the calibration/sizing program (per-game σ feeds simulation-derived moneyline/spread/total probabilities).
- (COACHING / SCHEME) The pipeline's explicit structure — team strength → latent process distributions (quantity, quality) → simulated games — maps to modeling offensive play volume and per-play efficiency separately, letting scheme tendencies (pace, run/pass mix) live in the quantity distribution and execution quality in the quality distribution, which is more compositional than a single scoreline regressor.
- (TRUST-SIGNAL) CONTRADICTION-adjacent caution: the paper's RPS 0.201 vs bookmaker 0.198 means a generative pipeline that doesn't out-calibrate the market adds complexity without edge — any GSE port must beat the bookmaker-consensus-implied Brier (proposed gate: ≥0.002 better on walk-forward moneyline Brier) before adoption; the positive betting return on an unaudited window does not clear the bar alone. Also the draw-underestimation bias (fixed by a 27% draw-boost) is the NFL analogue of tail miscalibration (blowouts) — joint quantity/quality sampling is the proposed experiment to fix it.
## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
Yes, as a low-cost prototype pattern — port the two-stage mean+residual-σ estimator and the "forecast latent process distributions, then Monte Carlo-simulate games" architecture to NFL play-count/EPA-per-play distributions, gated on beating bookmaker-consensus Brier by ≥0.002 on walk-forward 2024.
