# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/2123-chronos-learning-language-time-series.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A full-paper read (ar5iv HTML, arXiv:2403.07815v2, Ansari et al., 2024 — 17 authors) of Chronos: a time-series foundation model that reframes forecasting as next-token prediction on quantized, mean-scaled series, training T5/GPT-2 architectures with categorical cross-entropy so a single pretrained model is a probabilistic zero-shot forecaster. Lane: `timeseries_foundation`. The file's verdict is ADAPT — probabilistic by construction (sampled paths, WQL-native), mapping directly onto GSE's calibration needs; adapt means borrowing the tokenization + T5 recipe for a sports model and pairing it with covariate extensions (ChronosX, 2127), not deploying univariate-only Chronos. No Chronos/TSFM references found in the GSE corpus (checked benchmark-audit-2026-09-17, AGENTS.md, docs/research tree — only unrelated "momentum" hits).

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Formulas copied verbatim:
- Scaling: s = (1/C)Σ_{i=1}^C |x_i|; x̃_i = x_i / s (mean scaling; paper also writes preprocessing as x_i / ((1/C)Σ|x_i|)).
- Quantization: q(x̃) = argmin_b |x̃ − c_b|, c_b uniformly spaced over [−15,15], B=4096; values outside [c_1,c_B] clipped.
- Objective: θ* = argmin_θ −Σ_{t=1}^{H} log p_θ(z_{C+t} | z_{1:C+t−1}) — categorical cross-entropy with label smoothing.
- Modeling: series → sequence of tokens z_{1:C} (from quantized values); train T5 encoder-decoder (sizes 20M, 46M, 200M, 710M — "Chronos-T5 Mini/Small/Base/Large") or GPT-2 (90M "Chronos-GPT2").
- Inference: sample autoregressive paths (temperature or categorical samples) from the token distribution, map tokens back to values, unscale — empirical predictive distributions; point forecast = median path.
- Point/probabilistic metrics: WQL over quantiles {0.1,…,0.9}; MASE vs seasonal-naive; aggregated by geometric mean relative to seasonal naive.
- Training config: 200K steps, batch 256, AdamW, LR 0.001 → 0 (linear decay), weight decay 0.01, dropout default. Context length 512, horizon 64 at train time; inference context C=512 (any length in practice).
- Training data: 28 datasets, ~890K univariate time series, ~84B observations (energy, transport, nature, web, sales, economics/finance, healthcare, web/cloud ops, synthetic).
- Augmentation: TSMixup — 10M mixup augmentations combining k∈{2,3} series with Dirichlet weights; 1M synthetic series from Gaussian processes (kernel-combination "KernelSynth" recipe, 1024 length, 9:1 real:synthetic sampling).
- Evaluation: (i) 15 datasets seen in training (in-domain), (ii) 27 datasets NOT used in training (zero-shot) — spanning Monash, GluonTS, LibCity, etc.
- Baselines: local statistical — SeasonalNaive, AutoETS, AutoARIMA, Theta, CES, CrostonSBA, NPTS, DynamicOptimizedTheta; deep — DeepAR, PatchTST, TFT, TiDE; pretrained LLMs-for-time-series — llmtime via GPT-3.5, GPT-4.
- Fine-tuning study: Chronos-T5 (Small) fine-tuned on the single training split of each evaluation dataset vs. training from scratch.
- Assumptions: mean-scale statistics make series dimensionless and comparable across domains (fails if a series is constant → degenerate s=0, handled by fallback); 4096-bin quantization sacrifices sub-bin precision (acceptable at 64-step horizons); autoregressive sampling accumulates error; no cross-series or covariate information.
- GSE implementation spec: (1) baseline experiment — zero-shot Chronos-T5-Small/Base on weekly NFL team-margin series (2002–2024); score WQL/MASE vs. GSE engine's current margins; ~1 engineer-day, no code; (2) sports adaptation — fine-tune Chronos-T5-Small on GSE's sports corpus (team margins, totals, player usage) via the paper's recipe (AdamW, low LR, early stopping on seasonal holdout); use sampled paths as the probabilistic engine input for spread/total pricing; (3) covariate extension — adopt ChronosX (2127) adapters on top of fine-tuned Chronos to inject weather, rest, injuries; (4) serving — batch-infer quantile paths per game; feed WQL-calibrated distributions to the existing moneyline/CLV pricing layer; sampling cost ~100 paths × 32 games ≈ trivial. Effort: 1 day baseline → 2–3 weeks fine-tune + serving integration.
- Reproducible test: NFL margins 2002–2024, weekly team sequences; expanding walk-forward, test = 2021–2024 seasons; horizon = next 1 game per team (H=1; then H=4 variant). Metric: mean WQL (quantiles 0.1–0.9, 100 sample paths) relative to SeasonalNaive + MAE of median. Baselines: SeasonalNaive, AutoETS, GSE engine v5.2.7 margins. Leakage audit: pretraining corpus excludes NFL data (stock Chronos has never seen sports series — pure zero-shot); fine-tuning split strictly season-ordered.
- Acceptance gate: ADOPT into the engine's probabilistic path if fine-tuned Chronos beats GSE's current margin MAE by ≥0.3 points AND WQL ≤ 0.95× SeasonalNaive on 2021–2024; ADAPT (keep researching) if it only matches; REJECT the covariate-free variant for live pricing if WQL does not beat naive — univariate alone cannot ship. Hard reject if any sample-path distribution fails calibration (empirical coverage of 80% intervals deviates >5 pts from nominal).
- Improvement experiment: tokenization resolution study — re-quantize with finer vocab (8192 bins) or learned quantization on sports series; replace mean scale with robust scale (median absolute deviation) for constant/degenerate sequences. Hypothesis: NFL-margin-optimal tokenization beats the paper's generic 4096-bin recipe on WQL while preserving zero-shot transfer.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Zero-shot: Chronos models occupied 2nd–4th in probabilistic (WQL) rankings; Chronos-T5 (Large) ranked 2nd on point forecasting (MASE) — all zero-shot against per-dataset trained models. SeasonalNaive normalized to 1.0 baseline.
- In-domain: Chronos models beat almost all task-specific models on both WQL and MASE; the largest (710M) was best.
- Fine-tuning: fine-tuned Chronos-T5 (Small) was THE BEST method on average across both metrics, beating supervised PatchTST/DeepAR — with far cheaper training (fewer steps) than training from scratch. File flags this as directly relevant to GSE's per-season adaptation strategy.
- llmtime (GPT-3.5/4): far behind Chronos on both metrics despite being much larger language models — text-tokenization is a poor time-series tokenizer.
- TSMixup ablation: removing mixup/synthetic hurt zero-shot performance; synthetic GP data with realistic kernels helped most on small-data regimes.
- Leakage/limitations in file: uniform quantization to 4096 bins caps precision — for tight-margin NFL games (±3) the grain relative to spread-market resolution (~0.5 pt) is acceptable but must be validated against market granularity; mean scaling is fragile on constant/near-constant sequences (e.g., a team's kickoff counts) — degenerate denominators; zero-shot results confound pretraining overlap (in-domain/zero-shot split partially addresses this, but Wikipedia-style familiarity effects apply to its web/econ data — cf. 2609.10357); NO covariates — cannot ingest weather, injuries, rest, line movement, "the very inputs that separate sportsbooks' models"; autoregressive sampling is slow for many series × many paths (latency matters for Sunday-morning odds refresh).
- GSE-positioning: current calibration work (Mimo CQR lane) is conformal post-hoc on point forecasts; Chronos-style generative distributions give NATIVE probabilistic outputs and a direct WQL/CRPS training signal — a new, complementary lane.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER (calibration/sizing): Chronos gives NATIVE probabilistic outputs (100 sample paths → quantiles/intervals) vs. the current Mimo CQR conformal post-hoc approach — the mechanism feeds WQL-calibrated margin distributions directly into the existing moneyline/CLV pricing layer instead of post-hoc-ing intervals onto point forecasts. The acceptance gate is quantitative: fine-tuned Chronos must beat the current engine margin MAE by ≥0.3 points with WQL ≤ 0.95× naive, and 80%-interval empirical coverage must stay within 5 pts of nominal — a hard reject otherwise. Relevant program: calibration/sizing (probabilistic pricing path).
- QB-BEHAVIOR: the fine-tuning result (small Chronos fine-tuned beats big supervised models, cheaper) is the mechanism for per-season adaptation of QB/player-level series — the improvement experiment's tokenization study (8192 bins or learned quantization; MAD robust scaling) is aimed at sub-point precision at key numbers 3/7, which is where player/team margin series live. The 1-day zero-shot baseline experiment (stock Chronos on 2002–2024 weekly margins vs. engine v5.2.7 margins) is the cheapest trust-gate: pretraining never saw sports series, so it's a pure zero-shot read. Relevant program: QB-behavioral profiles (weekly series modeling) + calibration/sizing.
- OTHER (engine architecture, cross-file): pairs with 2070 (TimeGrad) — Chronos is univariate-without-covariates, TimeGrad is multivariate-with-known-covariates; the file prescribes ChronosX (2127) adapters for weather/rest/injuries on top of fine-tuned Chronos, which is the cheaper covariate path vs. TimeGrad's full per-step conditional diffusion. The two are complementary (not competitive) lanes: Chronos for per-series probabilistic paths, TimeGrad for joint multivariate synthesis. Relevant program: calibration/sizing + tracking lane.
- UNCERTAIN: whether 4096-bin uniform quantization preserves edge at spread-market granularity (~0.5 pt) for tight-margin games (±3) — the file states it "must be validated against market granularity," and the improvement experiment is pre-registered to test finer vocab. Also UNCERTAIN: sampling latency for Sunday-morning odds refresh — autoregressive path sampling is slow for many series × many paths.
- CONTRADICTION-risk (flag, not verified): the paper's zero-shot strength vs. the file's hard claim that the covariate-free variant cannot ship — both can be true (zero-shot WQL ranks well against naive baselines while still lacking the weather/injury/line-movement inputs that separate sportsbook models), but any deployment must satisfy BOTH the zero-shot benchmark and the covariate gate before live pricing.
