# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/2070-timegrad-autoregressive-diffusion-time-series.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A full-paper read (ar5iv HTML, arXiv:2101.12072, Rasul, Seward, Schuster, Vollgraf — Zalando Research, 2021) of TimeGrad: an autoregressive denoising-diffusion model that replaces the restrictive emission distributions (Gaussian, low-rank copula, normalizing flows) in multivariate probabilistic time-series forecasting with a per-time-step conditional DDPM conditioned on an RNN hidden state, and achieves SOTA on 5 of 6 benchmarks on CRPS_sum. The file's verdict is ADAPT — not as a forecaster but as a *generator* of synthetic multivariate stat trajectories (team-week EPA/success/pace panels, within-game state sequences), exploiting the fact that the paper's "covariates known for the whole forecast horizon" design maps exactly onto the NFL's fully-known schedule. Verdict: ADAPT.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Formulas copied verbatim from the paper (Eqs. 1–7, following Ho et al. 2020):
- Forward: q(x^n|x^{n−1}) = N(x^n; √(1−β_n)x^{n−1}, β_n I)
- Closed-form: q(x^n|x^0) = N(x^n; √ᾱ_n x^0, (1−ᾱ_n)I) (Eq. 3)
- Reverse: p_θ(x^{n−1}|x^n) = N(x^{n−1}; μ_θ(x^n,n), Σ_θ I) (Eq. 1), with μ_θ(x^n,n) = (1/√α_n)(x^n − (β_n/√(1−ᾱ_n))ε_θ(x^n,n))
- Training objective: E‖ε − ε_θ(√ᾱ_n x_t^0 + √(1−ᾱ_n)ε, h_{t−1}, n)‖²
- Sampling (Algorithm 2): x_t^{n−1} = (1/√α_n)(x_t^n − (β_n/√(1−ᾱ_n))ε_θ(x_t^n, h_{t−1}, n)) + √Σ_θ z (annealed Langevin sampling per step, autoregressive rollout, S=100 trajectories for empirical quantiles)
- Model factorization (Eq. 8): q(x_{t0:T}^0 | x_{1:t0−1}^0, c_{1:T}) = Π_{t=t0}^{T} q(x_t^0 | x_{1:t−1}^0, c_{1:T})
- Temporal encoder (Eq. 9): h_t = RNN_θ(concat(x_t^0, c_t), h_{t−1}) — 2-layer LSTM, h_t ∈ ℝ^40
- ε_θ: conditional 1-D dilated ConvNet (WaveNet/DiffWave style) — 8 residual blocks, 8 residual channels, gated activation σ(·)⊙tanh(·), bidirectional dilated convolution with dilation 2^{i%2}, 8 skip connections summed; noise index n encoded by Transformer Fourier positional embeddings into ℝ^32 (N_max=500). Σ_θ = β̃_n.
- N=100 diffusion steps, linear β schedule 1e−4→0.1; training: random context+prediction windows, batch 64, Adam 1e−3, early stopping on validation; single Nvidia V100 16GB.
- Scaling (§3.3): each series divided by its context-window mean (or 1 if zero) — the Salinas et al. 2019b trick, "reported as significantly improving performance".
- Covariates: time-dependent (day-of-week, hour-of-day), time-independent embeddings, lag features by frequency — ALL KNOWN for the forecast period (§3.4 categorical-embedding trick).
- Metric: CRPS(F,x) = ∫(F(z) − 1{x≤z})²dz; CRPS_sum = E_t[CRPS(F̂_sum(t), Σ_i x_{i,t}^0)] — a proper scoring rule (de Bézenac et al. 2020), used instead of likelihood since not all baselines yield analytic distributions.
- NFL translation in file: entities i = teams (D=32) or team-game stat streams; time steps = weeks or plays/drives; covariates c_t = opponent, home/away, rest days, dome, week number, bye + learned team-identity embeddings; x_t^0 = per-team weekly EPA margin, success rate, pace (32-dim joint distribution per week).
- Baselines (11): classical — VAR, VAR-Lasso, GARCH, VES; deep — KVAE, Vec-LSTM-ind-scaling, Vec-LSTM-lowrank-Copula, GP-scaling, GP-Copula, Transformer-MAF.
- Ablation (Fig. 3): diffusion steps N ∈ {2,…,256} on Electricity — N≈10 already near-optimal, N≈100 optimal, larger not beneficial.
- GSE implementation spec: 2-layer LSTM (h=40 or scaled up) + conditional dilated-ConvNet ε_θ; N=100 steps (ablate to ~10 per Fig. 3); context-mean scaling per team-metric; random context+prediction windows across seasons (season-level splits only, never split a season across train/test); seed with real weeks 1–12, roll out 100 synthetic weeks-13–18 trajectories per team; effort ~5 engineer-days.
- Reproducible test: nflverse team-week panel 2015–2022 train, 2023–2024 held-out; metrics (a) CRPS_sum over the 32-team joint weekly vector on 2023–2024 (paper's own metric), (b) downstream engine spread-model log-loss on weeks 13–18 trained on real vs real+synthetic, (c) calibration of 50%/90% intervals on held-out weeks.
- Acceptance gate: ADOPT if (a) CRPS_sum on 2023–2024 weeks 13–18 beats the copula baseline by ≥5% relative, AND (b) real+synthetic spread log-loss beats real-only by ≥0.003 on held-out late-season weeks, AND (c) 90% empirical intervals achieve 85–95% coverage. REJECT if autoregressive rollout error compounds (CRPS_sum degrades vs baseline by week 18 by >20% relative to week 13) or N=100 sampling makes generation >10× tabular-backbone cost without meeting (a). 10 retrainings for se, per the paper.
- Improvement experiment: graph-conditioned emission — replace shared-h_{t−1} cross-team dependence with a per-week matchup-graph attention layer inside ε_θ (each team's denoising step attends to its opponent's noisy state and division rivals' states at the same diffusion step); test whether matchup-graph conditioning beats shared-hidden-state conditioning on CRPS_sum for weeks 13–18, especially head-to-head-correlated metrics (margin, total).

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Table 2 (CRPS_sum, lower better; TimeGrad 10-run mean±se):
  - Exchange: TimeGrad 0.006±0.001 (NOT best — VES/VAR/Transformer-MAF at 0.005; only dataset not won)
  - Solar: TimeGrad 0.287±0.02 (best; next Transformer-MAF 0.301)
  - Electricity: TimeGrad 0.0206±0.001 (best; next Transformer-MAF 0.0207)
  - Traffic: TimeGrad 0.044±0.006 (best; next Transformer-MAF 0.056 → −21%)
  - Taxi: TimeGrad 0.114±0.02 (best; next Transformer-MAF 0.179 → −36%)
  - Wikipedia: TimeGrad 0.0485±0.002 (best; next Transformer-MAF 0.063 → −23%)
- SOTA on 5/6 benchmarks. Paper's claimed mechanism: EBMs don't assign spurious density between disconnected modes (flows do) and don't need continuous-to-disconnected maps (VAEs do).
- Ablation: N can drop to ~10 without significant loss (10× sampling speedup available); N≈100 optimal.
- Leakage/adversarial notes in file: (1) forecasting ≠ synthesis — the paper never generates unconditional trajectories; NFL synthesis requires re-purposing (sample with teacher-forced history from real seasons, then roll forward); (2) autoregressive rollout compounds error over long horizons (30 steps tested; an 18-week NFL season is within range, full play-by-play is not); (3) no discrete/categorical emission modeling — counts treated as continuous; NFL needs discrete outcomes (scores, covers) → combine with a discrete head or 2067's categorical machinery; (4) cross-entity dependence only via shared h_{t−1} + joint per-step noise — no explicit team-interaction structure (§6 suggests GNNs as future work); (5) sampling cost N=100 × horizon × S=100 trajectories is heavy (N≈10 ablation mitigates); (6) Exchange (smallest, 8 series) is the one loss — small-D regimes are not its strength; D=32 NFL is closer to Solar/Electricity where it wins.
- External-validity note: high for trajectory synthesis — known-covariate design matches the NFL's fully-known schedule; CRPS_sum directly reusable as the proper scoring rule for GSE's own multivariate probabilistic forecasts (e.g., joint distribution of all Week N spreads).
- Lane position: TimeGrad handles the TIME dimension every other ledger (2062–2069, i.i.d. row generators) ignores; composes as: tabular ledgers synthesize the cross-sectional game table, TimeGrad synthesizes temporal evolution. The lane's standing evaluation metric is already CRPS — adopt CRPS_sum for any multivariate probabilistic GSE output.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER (tracking lane / temporal synthesis): TimeGrad is the first sequential-synthesis capability in the research lane — it fills the time dimension for the tracking/temporal lane. Mechanism: a 32-team joint weekly vector synthesized per week preserves correlated outcomes (if KC blows out LV, both teams' stat vectors move jointly), so it can generate synthetic late-season team-week panels (weeks 13–18 from weeks 1–12 context) as augmentation for the engine's late-season spread model. The file's reproducible test quantifies the downstream effect: real+synthetic spread log-loss must beat real-only by ≥0.003. Relevant program: tracking lane / engine data augmentation.
- OTHER (calibration/sizing): CRPS_sum is a proper scoring rule the paper uses in place of likelihood — the file recommends adopting it for ANY multivariate probabilistic GSE output (e.g., the joint distribution of all Week N spreads), and the acceptance gate requires 85–95% coverage on 90% empirical intervals, tying the method to calibrated probabilistic pricing rather than point forecasts. Relevant program: calibration/sizing (native probabilistic outputs, complementary to Mimo's conformal post-hoc CQR).
- SCHEME: the matchup-graph improvement experiment (per-week opponent attention inside the denoising step) is a SCHEME-facing mechanism — it makes the generative model aware of who-plays-whom each week rather than treating teams as anonymous series, which is where head-to-head-correlated outputs (margin, total) get their joint structure. If it beats shared-hidden-state conditioning, it becomes the standard architecture for any multi-team joint generative model at GSE. Relevant program: SCHEME (matchup-graph structure) and the tracking lane.
- UNCERTAIN: the discrete-head requirement — NFL outcomes are discrete (scores, covers) and the paper treats counts as continuous; whether a bolted-on discrete head preserves the diffusion's CRPS_sum advantage on discrete targets is unverified in the file (flagged, not tested).
