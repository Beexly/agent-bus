# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0174-beating-the-market-with-a-bad.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A market-microstructure-meets-ML paper (Hubáček & Šír, arXiv:2010.12508v1, 2020) proving and empirically testing the thesis that a strictly *inferior* predictive model can systematically profit as a market taker by being trained to **decorrelate its estimation errors from the market maker's** — formalizing the taker-vs-maker game, translating decorrelation into an ML loss function (MSE*), and validating it on real NBA betting data. The ledger's verdict is **ADOPT** — the decorrelation training objective ports directly into GSE's model training and complements paper 0171's Kelly/bet-sizing findings.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- **Kelly wealth growth decomposition (Eq. 32):** W_G = D_KL(R||M) − D_KL(R||T) — trader's wealth growth equals the difference in quality of her estimates T over market prices M in KL divergence. Positive Kelly returns ⟺ XENT_Ω(R,T) < XENT_Ω(R,M).
- **Ideal decorrelation loss (Eq. 59):** MSE*_Ω(R,M,T) = (1/|Ω|) Σ_{ω_i ∈ Ω} (t_i − r_i)² + γ·(t_i − r_i)(m_i − r_i), γ > 0.
- **Betting-adapted loss (5.4):** MSE*_Ω(R,M,T) = E[(T − R)² − γ·(T − M)²], where m_i = bookmaker probabilities from odds o_i; γ trades accuracy vs decorrelation.
- **Essential profitability (Definition 3.1):** opportunities exist with (m_i < r_i ∧ t_i > m_i) or (m_i > r_i ∧ t_i < m_i).
- **Buy-low-sell-high rule (Eq. 33):** m_i < t_i ⟹ α = buy (bet home); m_i > t_i ⟹ β = sell (bet away).
- **True expected returns:** stocks (Eq. 34) — E_R[ρ_i] = (r_i − m_i)/m_i if m_i < t_i; (m_i − r_i)/m_i if m_i > t_i. Betting (Eq. 35) — E_R[ρ_i] = r_i/m_i − 1 if m_i < t_i; (1 − r_i)/(1 − m_i) − 1 if m_i > t_i [rendering UNCERTAIN in extraction].
- **Unbiasedness (Eqs. 40–41):** E_P(M) − R = 0; E_{P(M|R=r)}(M) = r; empirically verified "∀r ∈ [0,1]: E_{P(M|r)}(M) = r" (5.3.1).
- **Variance/covariance identities (Eqs. 42–43):** Var[T] = E_R[Var[T|R]] + Var_R(E[T|R]); Cov[M,R] = Var[M|R] under pointwise unbiasedness.
- **Market taker advantage under uniform PU (Eqs. 38–39):** m_i < r_i ⟹ P_U(m_i < t_i) > P_U(t_i < m_i), and vice versa — with zero information the taker identifies 2/3 of profitable opportunities.
- **Expected ROI (Eqs. 12–13)** [UNCERTAIN — extraction garbled]: E_t[ρ_i^α] = (t_i − m_i)/m_i for stocks; t_i/m_i − 1 and (1−t_i)/(1−m_i) − 1 for betting sides α, β.
- **Investment strategies:** (i) unif — uniform unit stakes on positive-expected-return opportunities; (ii) sharpe — Markowitz MPT portfolio maximizing the Sharpe ratio (Eqs. 20–21) with E_R[w_i] = (r_i/m_i − 1)·f_i (Eq. 61) and Var_R[w_i] = (1−r_i)r_i f_i²/m_i² (Eq. 62), model estimates t_i substituted for unknown r_i, matches assumed independent, solved by sequential quadratic programming; only positive-estimated-expected-return opportunities bet (one side per game).
- **Theory:** market taker vs maker via joint distribution P_Ω(R,M,T) (Definition 2.1); taker's advantage — must estimate only the *direction* of the market's error, not the price (3.3); decorrelation = decreasing partial Corr[T,M|R] (Definition 4.1); Theorem 4.1 — for unbiased estimators, Corr[T,M|R] = −1 maximizes essential profitability; fractional Kelly benefits from decorrelation while full Kelly is blind to it (4.4–4.4.1, Examples 4.1–4.2; Example 4.2: half-Kelly on decorrelated estimates W_G = 0.038 > 0 vs 0 on coincident estimates).
- **Assumptions:** partially (in)efficient market; two-player zero-sum, roles never switch; spread/margin omitted from theory (present in experiments); short selling available for stock correspondence; both estimators unbiased; opportunities independent; Kelly analysis assumes true distribution known; MSE* "rooted merely in an intuition rather than formal mathematical derivation" (7.1).
## Data sources named
- NBA box-score data, seasons 2000–2014 (official NBA box scores); features = aggregated basic player stats (shots, passes, steals, etc.) for home and away teams from all preceding matches since season start + seasonal aggregates from preceding seasons. Deliberately "clearly inferior data sources."
- Odds: Pinnacle **closing** odds 2010–2014 (market-adjusted, margin ≈ 2.5%); multiple bookmakers 2000–2010 (margins ≈ 4.5%, aggregate of many makers makes decorrelation harder).
- Evaluation window: seasons 2006–2014, **9,093 games**; chronological protocol — train on all preceding seasons (2000–2005 → predict 2006, …, 2000–2013 → predict 2014).
- Simulated data (Appendix A.1): triples (R,T,M) from multivariate Beta, means 1/2; Var(M) = 0.054 (real bookmaker data), Var(T) same, Var(R) = 0.08 ("glass ceiling" thesis: ≤ 75% predictability); correlations {0.85, 0.90, 0.95}; n = 30 bets/round, 10,000 rounds; odds o_i = 1/m_i.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- **Table 3 (real data, means ± SE over 10 runs, 2006–2014):**
  - Without odds as feature (γ | W_sharpe | W_unif | Accuracy): 0.0 | 0.38 ± 0.10 | −5.12 ± 0.11 | 67.62 ± 0.03; 0.2 | 1.05 ± 0.12 | −3.31 ± 0.13 | 67.47 ± 0.03; **0.4 | 1.74 ± 0.14 | −1.73 ± 0.18 | 67.15 ± 0.10**; 0.6 | 1.32 ± 0.14 | −0.61 ± 0.28 | 66.19 ± 0.09; 0.8 | 1.10 ± 0.29 | −0.39 ± 0.22 | 64.93 ± 0.35; 1.0 | −1.92 ± 0.81 | −2.59 ± 0.57 | 61.30 ± 0.48.
  - With odds as feature: 0.0 | −0.12 ± 0.24 | −3.83 ± 0.22 | 68.80 ± 0.06; 0.2 | 0.72 ± 0.13 | −2.50 ± 0.14 | 68.37 ± 0.04; **0.4 | 1.49 ± 0.10 | −1.30 ± 0.12 | 67.48 ± 0.10**; 0.6 | 1.02 ± 0.20 | −1.15 ± 0.22 | 66.55 ± 0.10; 0.8 | 1.00 ± 0.35 | −0.45 ± 0.28 | 65.19 ± 0.27; 1.0 | −1.22 ± 0.51 | −2.25 ± 0.30 | 61.77 ± 0.44.
  - Paper's reading: decorrelation term "generally helps both the basic unif as well as the standard sharpe strategies to generate more profits, while there is a sweet spot in tuning the tradeoff parameter γ"; sharpe "consistently dominates" unif; "consistently able to generate statistically significant positive returns, despite the clearly inferior accuracy" (model ≤ 68.80 vs bookmaker 69 ± 2.5).
- **unif never profitable** on real data (best −0.39 ± 0.22) — the "bad model wins" claim rests entirely on sharpe/MPT, which uses the model's own t_i in variance terms (estimation-error feedback under-discussed).
- Model–market Pearson correlation: 0.87 (no-odds models) vs 0.95 (with-odds models) — the odds feature raises accuracy but raises market correlation (the tradeoff under test).
- **Table 4 (simulation, profits in %):** at Corr(T,R) = 0.85: Corr(T,M) = 0.85 → W_sharpe 11.15%, W_unif 3.14%, accuracy 70.11%; Corr(T,M) = 0.90 → 6.14%, 0.52%, 70.05%; Corr(T,M) = 0.95 → −1.73%, −5.46%, 70.08%. Profits decay monotonically as Corr(T,M) rises at fixed accuracy — the cleanest demonstration of the thesis. "Spotted" (bettor right, bookmaker wrong) proportion higher at lower Corr(T,M) (e.g., 8.12% at 0.85/0.85).
- γ = 0.4 is the sweet spot in both feature settings; γ = 1.0 destroys profit (−1.92/−1.22 sharpe) and accuracy collapses to ~61–62.
- Limitations: closing odds (2010–2014) are the hardest benchmark; pre-2010 odds mix bookmakers with 4.5% margins — inconsistent market definition across the sample; including odds as a feature hurts decorrelation yet is the realistic production choice; decorrelation can *hurt* if the trader's model is actually superior (4.3, Figure 5) — no online test for which regime you're in; profit units in Table 3 not normalized (unlike Table 4's %); unif stake size is a free hyperparameter; external validity to NFL — NBA two-way maps cleanly, NFL moneylines similar, but spreads/totals need the n-way extension (claimed "directly applicable," not demonstrated).
## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER (calibration/sizing lane — the training layer): this supplies the missing *training* complement to paper 0171's fractional-Kelly/drawdown-constrained staking: change the loss itself with L = XENT − γ·(t_i − m_i)² (m_i = de-vigged multi-book consensus from the Odds API), sweep γ ∈ {0.1,…,1.0}, and keep the fractional-Kelly layer — Example 4.2 shows fractional Kelly is exactly the strategy class that benefits from decorrelation. Serves the calibration/sizing program directly.
- OTHER (regime check, trust-signal lane): before deploying γ > 0, verify the model is *not* already XENT-superior to market consensus (paper 4.3: decorrelation hurts superior models) — this becomes an explicit gate in the calibration lane: if GSE beats consensus XENT, use γ = 0.
- TRUST-SIGNAL (indirect): the sharpe strategy only bets positive-estimated-expected-return opportunities, one side per game — i.e., the staking layer is a selective trust/intake filter on model outputs, consistent with treating model probabilities as directional signals against the market rather than absolute truths.
- OTHER (feature design): the odds-as-feature result (correlation 0.95 vs 0.87) is a caution for GSE's feature set — any market-derived feature raises the correlation the loss term must then fight; the "with and without market-odds features" audit becomes part of the γ sweep.
## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
Yes — add the decorrelation penalty L = XENT − γ·(t_i − m_i)² to GSE's win-probability training with a γ sweep on a 2020–2024 chronological NFL holdout, adopting only if some γ ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6} beats γ = 0 by ≥ 2 pp ROI under identical sharpe staking and model–market correlation decreases monotonically in γ (~1–2 days to add the loss; ~1 week for the sweep).
