# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0190-pref-mh-exact-mcmc-preference-voting.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A theoretical machine-learning paper (Sairien, Michaeli, Sochen, Gandelsman, Shocher, arXiv:2609.00905v1, 2026) that derives an *exact* Metropolis–Hastings acceptance rule from N stochastic pairwise-preference votes: with a fixed sampling budget no exact oracle-based BT-MH implementation exists (Theorem 1), but with a random unbounded-but-finite budget, the N-vote rule is exact (Theorem 2) and Peskun–Tierney optimal among exact rules (Theorem 3) — provided judge votes follow the Bradley–Terry model. The ledger's verdict is **ADAPT** — port the rule as a sampler for GSE's scenario/ensemble generation (lineup slates, betting-card candidates), replacing LLM/human judges with a Bradley–Terry-consistent scoring function.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Target: π(x) ∝ p₀(x)e^{s(x)} — base distribution tilted by latent preference score s(x), never observed directly.
- BT pairwise probability: p_J(x≺x′) = σ(s(x′)−s(x)) (logistic).
- Ideal oracle acceptance: α*(x,x′) = min{1, r₀(x,x′)·p/(1−p)}, where r₀(x,x′) = p₀(x′)q(x|x′)/[p₀(x)q(x′|x)] and p = p_J(x≺x′).
- Pointwise-MH (baseline): α = min{1, r₀(x,x′)·e^{ŝ(x′)−ŝ(x)}} — biased because absolute scores miscalibrate.
- **N-vote rule (proposed):** **α_N(x,x′;K) = min{1, r₀(x,x′)·K/(N−K+1)}**, where K = number of N judge votes favoring x′; K/(N−K+1) is the unbiased-in-ratio estimator of the ideal odds p/(1−p).
- Multi-judge: α = min{1, r₀(x,x′)·∏_i K_i/(N−K_i+1)}.
- MCMC loop: propose x′ ~ q(·|x), run the N-vote rule, accept/reject; chain targets π exactly under BT judges; Corollary 1 — convergence under standard MH regularity.
- Optimality: Peskun–Tierney dominance among exact acceptance rules using the same q and N.
- Stated assumptions: judge votes follow the **Bradley–Terry model** (transitive latent scores, independent votes conditional on the pair); the judge is stochastic but stationary; N is the expected (not fixed) number of queries — exact for every integer N ≥ 1.
## Data sources named
- 2D toy problems: banana-shaped and ring-shaped target densities for convergence diagnostics (R̂, ESS).
- Image generation: prompts from **MS COCO**; base sampler is a diffusion model; judge is an LLM-based aesthetic comparator (pairwise).
- Molecule generation: **ZINC** database molecules; base sampler trained on ZINC; properties evaluated: QED, SA (synthetic accessibility), diversity, and **MolSkill** (a learned skill predictor; lower = better).
- Code: https://github.com/Ariels34/Pref-MH (judge implementations in repo).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- **Image generation:** Pref-MH achieves **63.6%** preference success vs. **7.4%** for Pointwise-MH vs. **4.5%** base sampler; aesthetic scores 6.395 (Pref-MH) / 6.289 (Pointwise) / 6.287 (base). Paper's interpretation: absolute-score judges miscalibrate, so Pointwise-MH barely beats base; the 63.6% vs 7.4% gap reflects the pairwise vote rule capturing the preference signal.
- **Molecule design (Table 1):** Pref-MH — QED **0.698±0.002**, SA **0.781±0.001**, diversity **0.899±0.001**, MolSkill mean **−1.432±0.398**, median **−1.116±0.396**; base sampler and Pointwise-MH worse on the preference-tilted objective.
- **Toy diagnostics:** R̂ → 1 and ESS grows with chain length for all N ≥ 1; larger N gives higher ESS per step at higher query cost (the cost/quality trade-off).
- Limitations (adversarial read): exactness is conditional on BT judges — real LLM/human judges routinely violate transitivity, independence, stationarity (position bias, prompt-order effects, drift); under a non-BT judge the chain targets an undefined distribution and the "exact" guarantee evaporates silently. The paper reports no BT-consistency diagnostic for its own LLM aesthetic judges. Cost: N votes per proposal × chain length — expensive with LLM judges; total judge-query budgets for the image/molecule runs not reported. Peskun–Tierney optimality is among exact rules with the same q and N — says nothing about choosing q or N, which dominate practical mixing. Pointwise-MH is arguably a weak baseline (fixed-scale absolute scoring is known to miscalibrate); the 63.6% vs 7.4% gap flatters the comparison. For GSE: lineups/betting cards have no natural pairwise judge — one must be constructed and its BT consistency verified before any guarantee applies.
