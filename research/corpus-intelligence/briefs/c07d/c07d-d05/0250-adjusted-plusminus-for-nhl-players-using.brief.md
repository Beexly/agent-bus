# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0250-adjusted-plusminus-for-nhl-players-using.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Macdonald (arXiv:1201.0317v2, 2012): regression-based adjusted plus-minus (APM) for NHL skaters — replaces OLS with ridge regression and fits on higher-frequency proxy outcomes (shots, Fenwick, Corsi) rescaled to expected goals/60 to stabilize player ratings for decisions like trades and salary negotiation. File verdict: REJECT — the shift-stint design cannot transfer to NFL (no per-play full-lineup data in nflverse; APM's collinearity fix is weakest exactly where football lineups are most collinear), and its portable sub-ideas are already standard in GSE's ML lanes.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Model (Eq. 10): y = β_0 + β_1X_1 + ⋯ + β_JX_J + δ_1D_1 + ⋯ + δ_JD_J + ζ_offZ_off + ζ_defZ_def; matrix form y = Xβ (Eq. 11). Design: offense indicator X_j (1 if skater j on offense), defense indicator D_j, zone-start faceoff dummies Z_off, Z_def; goalies included as defensive variables in goals models. One model per situation (even strength / special teams) × per response (4 outcomes) = 8 models.
- OLS objective (Eq. 12): Q = Σ_i (y_i − ŷ_i)²; normal equations XᵀXβ̂ = Xᵀy.
- Ridge objective (Eq. 16): Q = (y − Xβ)ᵀ(y − Xβ) + λβᵀβ; solved via (XᵀX + λI)β̂ = Xᵀy (Eq. 18).
- HKB λ choice (Eq. 23): λ_HKB = p·MSE/(β̂ᵀβ̂).
- Ridge VIF (Eq. 24): diagonal elements of (XᵀX + λI)^{-1}XᵀX(XᵀX + λI)^{-1}; target: all VIF < 10.
- λ chosen per model as the MAXIMUM of four candidates: (1) randomized generalized cross-validation (Girard 1991 trace estimator, since n ~ 10^6, p ~ 10^3), (2) trace-curve stabilization point, (3) Hoerl–Kennard–Baldwin λ_HKB, (4) the λ needed to bring all VIF below 10. Displayed models: λ ≈ 0.5 (vertical line in Figure 1 trace curve).
- Proxy outcomes: goals, shots, Fenwick (shots + missed shots), Corsi (shots + missed shots + blocked shots), all per 60 minutes of ice time. Rescaled to expected goals/60 by multiplying by league-average goals-per-shot, goals-per-Fenwick, goals-per-Corsi, computed separately for even strength, power play, short-handed from four seasons (~10 shots per goal).
- Year-to-year stability measured via Pearson correlation of per-60 estimates across seasons (≥500 EV minutes / ≥150 special-teams minutes thresholds).
- Outputs: 36 per-player estimates (offense/defense × EV/PP/SH × 4 outcomes) in goals or expected-goals per 60 minutes; 48 expanded to per-season + all-situations totals; trophy finalists from G, G_def, G_off aggregations (Selke/Norris/Hart tables).

## Data sources named
- Every shift of every NHL game, four seasons (2007-08, 2008-09, 2009-10, 2010-11): shift start/end times, players on ice per shift, zone of starting faceoff, goals/shots/missed/blocked shots per shift; empty-net situations removed. Each shift → two observation rows (home and away), weighted by shift duration. N = 2,324,528 observations at even strength; N = 461,022 for special teams. Data source not stated as public (era-analogous to NHL official API / Evolving-Hockey). No code or data link in the paper.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Four-season offensive leaders (G_off, goals/season): Sidney Crosby 23 (S_off 12, F_off 13, C_off 14), Jonathan Toews 18, Alex Ovechkin 17 (S 17, F 20, C 24), Daniel Sedin 16, Joe Thornton 16.
- Per-60 EV offense (SE): Crosby 0.83 (0.20), Ovechkin 0.46 (0.18), Datsyuk 0.53 (0.19); shots-based SEs ~0.05–0.08 — roughly 2.5–3× smaller than goals-based SEs. Ovechkin EV offense: G 0.46 (0.18), S 0.45 (0.07), F 0.53 (0.06), C 0.63 (0.05) — the SE collapse with proxy outcomes is the paper's central numerical result.
- Year-to-year correlations: ridge > OLS for EV offense (goals) and for shots/Fenwick/Corsi in all three displayed panels (EV offense ≥500 min, PP offense ≥150 min, SH defense ≥150 min), EXCEPT SH defense where ridge-goals ≈ OLS but shots/Fenwick/Corsi still higher.
- Chosen λ ≈ 0.5 for the illustrated PP-offense model (Figure 1); Datsyuk's PP offense flips from negative (λ=0, OLS) to elite-positive (λ=0.5); Lidstrom's OLS estimate ≈ 4.0 goals/60 collapses under ridge.
- Award agreement (face validity): Datsyuk ranked #1 defensive forward (G_def 12) and #1 overall (G 27); actual Selke wins 2007-08–2009-10 confirmed; Lidstrom/Chara Norris and Ovechkin/Sedin Hart wins consistent.
- Assumptions: additivity of player contributions within a shift; shift-level outcomes independent given lineups; duration weighting corrects exposure; zone-start dummies fully capture deployment; league-average shooting % valid for rescaling (i.e., all players have league-average finishing); empty-net removal unbiased.
- Limitations: NO predictive validation — year-to-year correlation + award-vote face validity are not out-of-sample tests; never predicts future performance or team outcomes. Rescaling by league-average shooting % assumes away the exact skill (finishing talent) separating elite scorers from shot compilers — Ovechkin's C_off (24) vs G_off (17) spread shows how much rescaling moves conclusions. λ = max of four heuristics is ad hoc/conservative-by-design; trace-curve inspection is subjective and could rationalize any λ (Datsyuk "stabilizing" to elite). Shift-level i.i.d. ignores within-game state (score effects massively distort Corsi), goalie quality (acknowledged only for goals models), opponent-line matching. Fenwick/Corsi credit blocked shots and misses equally to on-net shots; zone-start dummies crude. Estimates condition on observed deployment (coach usage confounds; QoC/QoT only via opponent indicators in goals model).
- File's rejection rationale: NFL needs per-observation full lineups with frequent partial rotation (hockey shifts); NFL plays are 11 fixed starters with near-total collinearity, and nflverse play-by-play does not record which 22 players are on the field per play — the design matrix cannot be built. Player-level EPA attribution already exists in GSE's inventory (RYOE, YPRR, TPRR). Portable sub-ideas are duplicates: ridge/L2 shrinkage routine in GSE's ML lanes; "noisier high-frequency proxy rescaled to target unit" = same principle as EPA per-play vs points, already the engine's core.
- File's notional port (if per-play full NFL lineups ever exist, e.g., FTN charting expanded to all-22 presence): observations = drives or play-blocks (~40k drives/season), X_j = offensive snap indicators for skill players (QB/RB/WR/TE), D_j = defensive starters, y = EPA per 60 plays, ridge with the paper's max-of-four λ rule. Effort ~1–2 weeks — unjustified now.
- File's acceptance gate: REJECT stands unless (a) a per-play full-lineup NFL data feed becomes available AND (b) pilot ridge-APM on one NFL season shows year-to-year QB/WR EPA-contribution correlations exceeding current snap-weighted EPA stability by ≥0.05 — neither holds today.
- File's improvement experiment (within NHL frame): replace ad hoc λ = max(GCV, trace, HKB, VIF<10) with time-series CV choosing λ to minimize next-season player goals/60 prediction error; add shot-quality-weighted response (Krzywicki/Schuckers weighted shots, the paper's own "future work") — tests whether the proxy-outcome trick survives player-specific rather than league-average rescaling.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER — rejection with one salvaged method note: the max-of-four λ rule (GCV trace estimator, trace-curve stabilization, HKB, VIF<10) is a usable ridge-tuning heuristic for any high-dimensional collinear design the engine encounters, even though the APM application itself doesn't port. Tagged OTHER since it's a generic ML-calibration sub-idea — the file's own map check confirms GSE's ML lanes already cover ridge/L2 shrinkage, so this is a duplicate, not a gap-fill.
- TRUST-SIGNAL — negative evidence for rating-metric QC: the paper's own Ovechkin spread (C_off 24 vs G_off 17, goals/season) demonstrates how proxy-outcome rescaling by league averages can move conclusions substantially — a cautionary data point for ANY GSE metric that rescales a high-frequency proxy by a league-average conversion rate (e.g., turning shot/adot volume into expected fantasy points). Serves calibration/sizing as a "check your rescaling assumptions" flag.
- OTHER — domain non-transfer: serves the corpus as a documented dead-end so future sweeps don't re-open the APM-for-NFL question without new data (per-play full lineups). The file's collinearity argument is the key mechanistic reason: ridge's shrinkage helps exactly when lineups partially rotate; with 11 fixed starters and no rotation, the design matrix is near-singular and the signal is absorbed by the intercept.
- CONTRADICTION (methodological): the paper claims ridge stabilizes player ratings for decision-making, yet performs zero out-of-sample predictive validation — stability (year-to-year correlation) is presented as validity. Any GSE use of this pattern must add the missing forecasting test (next-season prediction), which the file's own improvement experiment concedes.

## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
No — shift-stint APM cannot be built without per-play full NFL lineups (data gap), and its portable ideas (ridge shrinkage, proxy-outcome rescaling) are already in GSE's ML/calibration lanes; file gate reopens only if a full-lineup feed appears AND pilot beats snap-weighted EPA stability by ≥0.05 year-to-year correlation.
