# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0210-nfl-stepandturn-a-generative-framework-for.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Nguyen & Yurko (CMU, arXiv:2603.17866v3, 2026): a Bayesian generative framework for evaluating NFL ball-carrier movement — step-length + turn-angle multilevel models fit in Stan produce posterior-predictive "ghost" simulations of an average player, and observed plays are valued against the ghost distribution at the frame level. File verdict: ADAPT — closest paper in its batch to a drop-in GSE tracking-evaluation module ("GSE Ghost Score").

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Step-and-turn representation (from animal-movement literature): each frame's movement = step length s_t and turn angle φ_t. s_t = √((x_{t+1}−x_t)² + (y_{t+1}−y_t)²); bearing b_t = atan2(y_{t+1}−y_t, x_{t+1}−x_t); φ_t = b_t − b_{t−1}.
- Step-length model (Eq. 2): transformed response s̃_ijt (scaled arcsin of min-max-normalized step length) ~ N(μ^(SL)_ijt, σ²), with μ^(SL)_ijt = α^(SL)_0 + X^(SL)_ijt β^(SL) + u_j + v_k; ball-carrier random intercept u_j ~ N(0, τ²_u), defensive-team random intercept v_k ~ N(0, τ²_v). Covariates: ball carrier + closest defender features, player counts per direction, previous-frame step length.
- Turn-angle model (Eq. 3): φ_ijt ~ von Mises(μ^(TA)_ijt, κ_ijt); mean via tanh-half link on α^(TA)_0 + X^(TA)_ijt β^(TA) (includes previous-frame turn angle = directional persistence); concentration log κ_ijt = γ_0 + γ_1 s_ijt + w_j, ball-carrier random intercept w_j ~ N(0, τ²_w) — step length dictates turn concentration; players differ in turn-angle variability.
- Fit in Stan via brms: 4 chains × 5,000 iterations (2,500 warmup) → 10,000 posterior draws; half-t_3 priors on variance components; R̂ ≈ 1, no ESS issues; prior-sensitivity check in Appendix C.
- Posterior-predictive simulation: one step ahead per frame, other 21 players held fixed; draw a NEW player random effect from N(0, τ²) to simulate an "average player" ghost; H = 100 hypothetical steps per frame; (s, φ) pairs converted to hypothetical locations; features recomputed; ending yard line estimated.
- Play value (Eq. 1): ℓ_ijt = E[L_ijt | X_ijt]. Estimated with CatBoost multinomial classifier over 1-yard ending-yardline bins (10–110): ℓ̂_ijt = Σ_{l=10}^{110} l·P(L_ijt = l | X_ijt) (Eq. 5). Framework is modular — any valuation model can substitute.
- Evaluation vs ghost (Eq. 6): δ^(h)_ijt = ℓ̂_ijt − ℓ̂^(h)_ijt; frame-average (Eq. 7): δ̄_ijt = (1/H)Σ_h δ^(h)_ijt with quantile-based intervals.
- Yards success rate (Eq. 8): (1/H)Σ_h 1(δ^(h)_ijt > 0), averaged over frames.
- Explosiveness (Eq. 9): 1(ℓ̂_ijt > q_0.95(ℓ̂^(h)_ijt)), averaged over frames.
- Anchoring strategy (Horton 2020; Yurko et al. 2020): ball carrier as anchor, other players ordered by Euclidean distance.
- Posterior-predictive integral form (Eq. 4): ∫ p(s̃_new | θ)p(θ | D) dθ.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Model choice via posterior predictive checks: Gamma and lognormal step-length models both FAIL (overestimate density near short steps, underestimate tail); the scaled-arcsin Gaussian wins (Appendix A, Figure 8).
- Yards success rate (fraction of frames beating the ghost; all ≥70 attempts, weeks 1–9 2022): top 5 — Josh Jacobs 0.542, Miles Sanders 0.539, Travis Etienne 0.527, Dameon Pierce 0.512, James Robinson 0.507; bottom 5 — Michael Carter 0.446, A.J. Dillon 0.448, Cordarrelle Patterson 0.450, Raheem Mostert 0.453, Christian McCaffrey 0.457.
- Explosiveness (fraction of frames above ghost's 95th percentile): top 5 — Travis Etienne 0.118, Aaron Jones 0.095, Kenneth Walker 0.093, Miles Sanders 0.092, Antonio Gibson 0.089; bottom 5 — Tyler Allgeier 0.035, Jamaal Williams 0.044, A.J. Dillon 0.050, David Montgomery 0.054, Michael Carter 0.054.
- Movement-profile insight: correlation r = 0.279 between step-length and turn-angle random effects; Jonathan Taylor = least variable turn angle (matches scouting "straight-ahead runner"); Taylor = long strides/low turn variability; McCaffrey = short steps/high turn variability.
- Discriminative power: credible intervals at top vs. bottom of the leaderboard do not overlap.
- Case study: Javonte Williams week-2 play (TEX @ DEN, 17-yard run) — accumulated +11.4 yards vs. hypothetical baseline; at first contact, observed ending yard line above the 95th percentile of the ghost distribution; post-contact peak ≈ +3 yards/frame differential; interval bands widen post-contact.
- Assumptions flagged in paper: other 21 players held fixed during hypothetical simulation — acknowledged as a simplification ("hypothetical individual movement behavior for a single step, rather than a fully dynamic recreation"); full-trajectory simulation would need a tackle-probability termination model + multi-agent movement models (explicit future work). One-step-ahead ghosting only, not trajectory ghosting. RBs on run plays only (no RAC receivers, QBs, defenders). 2022 weeks 1–9 only (roster/season drift).
- No leakage identified: simulation is one-step-ahead at observed frames; no future information leaks into the ghost.
- PDF extraction garbled superscripts in Eqs. 2–3 — notation partially reconstructed from prose (structure verified, not uncertain substance).
- GSE implementation spec in file: port as "GSE Ghost Score" — re-fit on full 2022–2025 nflverse/BDB tracking with GSE's CatBoost/EP valuation swapped in; batch-compute frame-level δ̄, yards success rate, explosiveness per player-week; weekly RB content ("which backs are creating yards the blocking didn't") + engine feature for rushing prop markets. Effort: re-fit + pipeline ~2 weeks; weekly content table ~2 days after pipeline exists.
- Reproducible test: nflverse 2022 weeks 1–9 RB runs; reproduce yards-success-rate leaderboard ordering (Jacobs/Sanders/Etienne top 3) within ±0.02 per player; Spearman ≥ 0.80 vs paper's Table 3; split-half stability refit on weeks 10–17, require Spearman ≥ 0.60.
- Acceptance gate: ADOPT ghost-score pipeline if refit reproduces Table 3 (Spearman ≥ 0.80) AND shows split-half stability (Spearman ≥ 0.60); otherwise REJECT.
- Improvement experiment: joint multi-agent forward simulator — train the 0205 heteroscedastic diffusion trajectory model as opponent simulator + step-and-turn as ball-carrier proposal, terminate with learned tackle-probability model → full-trajectory ghosting ("what if he cut left" content).
