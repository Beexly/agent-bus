# arxiv-deep/0767-predictive-modelling-of-football-injuries.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Read-and-ledger of Kampakis (2016), "Predictive modelling of football injuries" — a UCL PhD thesis (arXiv:1609.07480v1) with three real-data investigations on Premier League club data (Tottenham Hotspur, Wolverhampton Wanderers): (a) recovery-time modelling from UEFA-standard injury records, (b) GP regression with a dynamic-time-warping kernel for days-to-first-injury from variable-length exposure records, (c) supervised PCA for weekly intrinsic-injury classification from 69 in-training GPS variables. Verdict: ADAPT — the GP–DTW kernel and the forecast-horizon landmark evaluation protocol are directly portable to GSE's player-availability work.

## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Poisson log-link regression with White (1980) robust SEs because E[y] ≠ Var(y) in every dataset (e.g., THFC recovery mean 15.5 days vs variance 1298.8).
- Negative binomial and cumulative-logit ordinal models; ordinal ("severity" discretization) abandoned — kappa 0.31–0.40 showed the medically-motivated discretization was worse than plain regression.
- DTW–RBF kernel: `k(xᵢ, xⱼ) = exp(−γ · DTW(xᵢ, xⱼ))`, γ > 0 constrained so predictive variances stay positive; composite kernel over two exposure series: `K(xᵢ,xⱼ) = ½[k_train(xᵢ,xⱼ) + k_match(xᵢ,xⱼ)]`. DTW distance: `DTW(X,Y) = min over warping paths of Σ d(xᵢ, yⱼ)` — admits different-length histories. Thesis acknowledges DTW is not necessarily positive semi-definite; relies on the GP closed-form solution (exists unless K + σ²I is non-invertible) plus empirical variance-positivity checks.
- Concordance correlation coefficient: `ccc = 2ρσₓσᵧ/(σₓ² + σᵧ² + (μₓ−μᵧ)²)`; Cohen's kappa as the primary metric for imbalanced classification.
- Supervised PCA (Bair et al. 2006): screen features by univariate regression |xⱼᵀy|, PCA on survivors with α-thresholded selection (α ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.5}, components ∈ {2..50}), then classification on retained components.
- ML comparison arm: k-NN, SVM (polynomial kernels degree 2–3), GP (polynomial kernel), MLP (45 hidden, 2500 epochs), random forest — 10 rounds of 10-fold CV, mean ± std of RMSE, Pearson correlation, ccc; classification variant with accuracy + kappa.
- Feature selection: random-forest node-impurity ranking (500 trees, RSS impurity) and CFS via genetic algorithm (population 50, 1000 generations, crossover 0.7, mutation 0.05, roulette selection).
- Inv. 2 protocol: leave-one-out CV with the test subject's record truncated at T, T−1, …, T−12 days before injury (T−12 chosen because minimum response was 14 days) — a forecast-horizon/landmark evaluation; grid 1000 γ values ∈ [0.00002, 0.2] × noise ∈ [0.0001, 0.01], discarding parameter sets with negative predictive variance.
- Assumptions: injuries ≤3 days into pre-season attributed to pre-existing conditions, not workload (removed); "intrinsic" = non-contact, medic-confirmed; days off = 0-exposure imputation; log(days) approximately normal for the GP; ordinal severity categories presumed ordered correctly.

## Data sources named
- Inv. 1 (recovery time): three UEFA injury-recording datasets — THFC (152 injuries), WW, integrated merged. UEFA fields: recurrence, activity, position when injured, phase of play/mechanism, body part injured, type, injury (specific diagnosis), side, stage of season, age, footwear, strapping, surface condition, referee's decision, date of injury. Target: days lost — THFC mean 15.5 (variance 1298.8); WW mean 10.8 (variance 341.3); integrated mean 13.8 (variance 975.8). NOT public (club-confidential).
- Inv. 2 (exposure): daily training + match exposure (minutes) for 35 THFC first-team players, 2012–13 pre-season (reference day 8 July 2012; days off = 0). After cleaning (removed 3 goalkeepers, 2 players injured within first 3 days, 1 collision injury, 5 transient injuries replaced by next injury; 9 right-censored): 29 players, 21 injured; response = days to first injury (range 14–312, mean 91.73, median 49.5); mean training exposure 35.83 ± 42.75 min/day. NOT public.
- Inv. 3 (GPS): STATSports Viper10 training-session GPS, 29 THFC players, July–Dec 2014: 1,310 session rows × 68 GPS variables + session duration (69 total). Six speed zones (Z1 <35% max speed … Z6 >75%): distance, high-speed running, speed intensity, accelerations/decelerations (>0.5 m/s², >0.5 s), metabolic power variables (EMD, HML distance, AMP), impacts, dynamic stress load, steps, total loading. Weekly aggregation (mean): Approach A = 206 player-weeks × 69 features, 11 injury-weeks (injured players only, intrinsic); Approach B = 426 player-weeks, 70 variables incl. class (all players). NOT public.
- No code repository stated (thesis-era Weka/R-style analysis); methods fully described and reimplementable. Note: the local text cache held only the 37-line abstract; the full 120+ page PDF was fetched and read end-to-end.

## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Inv. 1 statistical fits (IN-SAMPLE fit quality, not CV-validated): Poisson — THFC RMSE 11.4/corr 0.94/ccc 0.94; WW 4.9/0.96/0.96; integrated 19.0/0.79/0.75. Negative binomial worse everywhere (THFC RMSE 43.45/corr 0.78/ccc 0.65; WW 9.38/0.85/0.85; integrated 26.22/0.55/0.51); one 256-day meniscus-lesion outlier (Cook's D 0.006, 96th of 152 — low influence).
- Ordinal regression: accuracy 71.0–75.0%, kappa 0.31 (THFC) / 0.40 (WW) / 0.37 (integrated) — author rejected as worse than regression.
- Significant covariates (>50% of tests at 5%): recurrence 6/6, phase of play 6/6, body part injured 5/6, injury diagnosis 5/6, stage of season 3/4; position when injured 2/2.
- Inv. 1 ML regression (10×10-fold CV, THFC — the honest numbers): random forest best overall — RMSE 30.1, corr 0.471±0.341, ccc 0.406; SVM poly deg3 — RMSE 28.8, corr 0.511, ccc 0.269; all correlations <0.5 with very large variance (k-NN corr 0.225±0.387). Classification: every classifier ≈ baseline accuracy (THFC baseline 66.4%); kappa best = RF 0.256 (THFC), SVM 0.225 (WW).
- After CFS feature selection (e.g., THFC kept: phase of play, injury, body part, recurrence, age): SVM THFC corr 0.511→0.584, ccc 0.269→0.455; RF robust to noisy features throughout.
- Inv. 2 (GP–DTW, LOOCV): ccc 0.95–0.98 from T down to T−12; best MAE ≈ 11 days (response mean 91.7, median 49.5). Skill deteriorates with truncation; MAE minimized at T−3. Author: predictions reliable to 12 days ahead on this data; no longer horizons tested.
- Inv. 3: supervised PCA best by kappa in both approaches — Approach A: kappa 0.21±0.04 (acc 88.8%, precision 0.55, recall 0.33; α=0.01, 41 components); ridge logistic second (kappa 0.16±0.33); RF/SVM/GP/MLP/k-NN all ≈ 0 (RF kappa 0.06, k-NN 0.0). Approach B: supervised PCA kappa 0.14±0.09 (α=0.09, 40 components); GP second (0.12).
- SPCA component interpretation (Approach A): PC1 = "absence of high-speed running" (dominated by DistanceZone5, r=−0.764); PC2 = "medium-speed sprinting".
- Author notes: RF's failure on all-numeric high-dimensional data; k-NN's curse-of-dimensionality collapse; Approach B supervised PCA reached 97.07% accuracy but kappa only 0.14 — accuracy alone is misleading.
- Limitations: Inv. 2's ccc 0.95–0.98 on n=29 single-club first-injury-only data — author flags single-club generalizability; training subjects contribute full records while test subject is truncated, so the model has population-level "future" schedule information; Inv. 3 Approach A discards uninjured players (biased sample); the 6-zone expansion creates 68 highly collinear variables from ~20 underlying quantities; no deep learning, no calibrated injury probabilities, no conformal coverage; intrinsic-injury definition is medic-judged, not data-verified.

## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- **TRUST-SIGNAL + CALIBRATION/SIZING (OTHER — player-availability lane):** Injury/availability prediction feeds the trust surface only through availability-adjusted picks; the thesis's two disciplines port directly: (a) **kappa-over-accuracy discipline** for imbalanced injury classification (97.07% accuracy with kappa 0.14 is the cautionary tale — any GSE injury classifier reported on accuracy alone is theater), and (b) the **landmark horizon protocol** (predict-then-truncate at h ∈ {0,3,7,10,14} days, report ccc/MAE vs h, ship only horizons where skill beats the no-information baseline) — this answers "how far ahead can we trust the injury call," which is a trust-signal intake question about availability claims on the public surface.
- **COACHING (OTHER — workload/rest management):** The GP–DTW kernel on variable-length workload histories (k=exp(−γ·DTW), γ ∈ [2e-5, 0.2]) gives a rest-management flag for questionable/tagged players: compare a player's recent practice-participation/snap-count/travel trajectory against injury-preceding trajectories via DTW similarity. NFL translation per ledger: daily workload series (practice participation % × session length, snap counts, travel distance) → GP regression on log(days-to-next-missed-game). This serves the **tracking lane** (workload tracking → availability risk).
- **QB-BEHAVIOR (OTHER, secondary):** Return-to-play regression evidence — recurrence 6/6, phase of play 6/6, body part 5/6, diagnosis 5/6 significant — maps to modeling expected games missed for questionable/doubtful QBs with count regression on injury type + body part + recurrence + mechanism, preferring regression over discretized severity classes (the thesis's own ordinal failure). QB availability is the single highest-leverage availability input, so this serves the QB-behavioral profiles program's injury side.
- **SCHEME/OL (OTHER, indirect):** SPCA components ("absence of high-speed running" r=−0.764, "medium-speed sprinting") are interpretable injury-correlated workload factors; the Bair-style supervised-PCA pattern (screen on injury label, keep α<0.05 components, feed 3–4 into the availability model) is the recipe for any high-dimensional tracking/GPS feature block (including OL workload proxies) — serves the tracking lane.
- UNCERTAIN: Inv. 2's ccc 0.95–0.98 is almost certainly optimistic (n=29, single club, population-future schedule leakage noted by the author); the ledger's discounted bar (ccc ≥ 0.60 at 7-day horizon on cross-club NFL data) is the honest test.
- CONTRADICTION: the Poisson-in-sample fit quality (corr 0.94) contradicts the honest ML CV results (corr ≤ 0.5) — the ledger explicitly flags this as fit-vs-generalization, and the brief should be read with the CV numbers as the real ones.
