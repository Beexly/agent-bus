## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Standing rules (from the prompts themselves): never guarantee a result; follow the usage, not the name; judge the situation, not just the talent; a lead is not a signal until the data supports it.
- Prompt 1 — THE GOAL LINE (who gets the ball inside the 5): inputs team, player(s), recent goal-line carries/targets/short-yardage package notes; steps: identify recent goal-line/short-yardage handler by role, flag short-yardage back / package TE / QB sneak, determine split division, classify player as finisher vs setup man; verdict: real goal line role / shared / not the guy. Rule: "The best player on the field is often not the one who punches it in. Follow the usage, not the name."
- Prompt 2 — THE RED ZONE (real share of work inside the 20): inputs player+team, opponent, red-zone targets/carries/snap share/role notes; steps: real red-zone share (targets/carries inside the 20, not overall volume), does role grow/shrink/stay flat near end zone vs general workload, how often offense reaches red zone, opponent red-zone defensive strength; verdict: real scoring role / situational only / volume without finishing work. Rule: "A player can lead a team in yards and never see the ball in scoring position. Those are two different jobs."
- Prompt 3 — THE SCRIPT READ (game flow creating chances): inputs player+team, matchup, expected shape (favored/underdog, pace, closeness); steps: most likely script (comfortable lead / close / playing from behind), what script does to role (leading teams run, trailing teams throw), increase/decrease scoring chances, the one script that kills the angle entirely; verdict: script helps / hurts / neutral. Rule: "A great player in the wrong script gets fewer chances than an average player in the right one. Judge the situation, not just the talent."
- Prompt 4 — THE LONG SCORE (goal-line scorers vs distance scorers): inputs player+team, aDOT/run depth, speed profile, how past scores happened; steps: where TDs come from (inside the 5 / red zone / beyond 20 yards), speed/route profile for a long one vs needing short work, flag players buried on depth chart near goal line but still scoring ("scoring the hard way"), one-sentence requirement for a score; verdict: goal line scorer / distance scorer / both. Rule: "A player who only scores from distance needs a broken play, not a play call. That is less repeatable and it is priced differently."
- Prompt 5 — THE SOFT SPOT (which position the defense gives up scores to): inputs defense, red-zone defense/run defense/coverage weaknesses/secondary-front injuries, player and position; steps: scores allowed on ground vs air, position group struggled with near end zone (backs / tight ends / outside receivers / slot), is weakness personnel/scheme/small-sample noise, does player's position match weakness or strength; verdict: target for his position / neutral / bad matchup. Rule: "A defense that gives up a lot of yards can still be stingy in the red zone. Judge them on scores allowed near the end zone, not total yardage."
- Prompt 6 — THE QB VULTURE (how many short scores the QB takes himself): inputs QB+team, player and position, QB rushing attempts near goal line / designed runs / sneak usage / red-zone play calling; steps: how often QB runs it in himself near goal line, sneak usage on short yardage (removes goal-line carries from backs entirely), which skill player loses the most scoring work because of this, whether the player's role is affected or insulated; verdict: QB is a real threat / minor factor / not an issue. Rule: "A rushing quarterback is the most overlooked competitor for short touchdowns on the roster. He does not appear on any depth chart as a running back."
