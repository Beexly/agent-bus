# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/1617-adaptive-liquidity-in-prediction-markets-via-online-learning.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Deep read of a 2026 theory paper (arXiv:2605.09599) that makes prediction-market liquidity itself adaptive by mixing a family of LMSR cost-function markets via online learning, preserving no-arbitrage and bounded worst-case loss with only a (1/β)log M overhead, driven by a hybrid slippage+liability structural signal.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Mixture: C^mix(q;w) = (1/β)log(Σ_k w(k)e^{βC_k(q)}), β>0 (small β → near-averaging, large β → soft maximum). Prices: ∇C^mix(q;w) = Σ_k π_k(q;w)∇C_k(q) with posterior weights π_k(q;w) = w(k)e^{βC_k(q)}/Σ_j w(j)e^{βC_j(q)}. Fee: fee_t = [Δ_t^*]_+ = max{sup_q(C^mix(q;w_t) − C^mix(q;w_{t+1})), 0}. Worst-case loss: sup_{T,(r_t),o} Loss_T(o) ≤ max_k B_k + (1/β)log M + C^mix(q_0;w_1) − min_o ρ(o)·q_0. Hessian decomposition: ∇²C^mix = Σ_k π_k∇²C_k + β·Cov_{k∼π}(∇C_k(q)) — curvature = expected expert curvature + β × expert disagreement variance. Hybrid signal: Γ^hyb_{k,t} = a(S_{k,t} − (1/M)Σ_j S_{j,t}) + bL_k(q_t); slippage S_{k,t} = D_{C_k}(q_t,q_{t−1}); liability L_k(q) = max_o ρ(o)·q − (C_k(q)−C_k(0)). Regret: O(√(T log T)) vs best ≤J-switch regime sequence. Effective liquidity: b_eff,t = (Σ_k π_t^{(b)}(k;q_t)η_k^{−1})^{−1}. Validation: pure theory + stylized 2-outcome/2-LMSR-expert simulation over four designed regimes (R1/R4 directional flow → inventory; R2 oscillatory neutral; R3 large alternating trades), Fixed-Share learner.
## Data sources named
No empirical data — theory plus author's simulation; code at github.com/EnriqueNueve/Adaptive-Cost-Function-Liquidity-. Only LMSR experts demonstrated; permutation/pair-betting sketched in Appendix G only.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Adaptivity overhead: worst-case loss adds only (1/β)log M over the most conservative expert — negligible for moderate M and non-tiny β. Simulation: in R1 (sustained directional flow) effective liquidity drops sharply to low as inventory accumulates; in R2 (oscillatory, neutral inventory) it jumps up and plateaus high; in R3 (large alternating trades) adaptive curve sits between fixed benchmarks with damped slippage spikes; in R4 liability dominates and liquidity collapses. Slippage governs short-term responses; liability governs long-term regime positioning. Key qualitative result: the learner switches regimes state-dependently rather than smoothing — liquidity aligns with whichever risk (price impact or inventory) dominates. Guarantees are on the hybrid surrogate signal, not on trader surplus, price accuracy, or information aggregation; equilibrium behavior under adaptive liquidity is unstudied (strategic traders could exploit predictable switches).
