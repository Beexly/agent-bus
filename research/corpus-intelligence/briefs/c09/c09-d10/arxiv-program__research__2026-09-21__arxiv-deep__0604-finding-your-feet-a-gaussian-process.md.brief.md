# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0604-finding-your-feet-a-gaussian-process.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A Bayesian Gaussian-process model of Test-cricket batting careers (40,273 innings, 1,018 players) that estimates per-innings ability ν(t) as a powered-exponential GP over career-innings time layered on a hazard/eye-in scoring likelihood, with multiplicative home/away and innings-order context effects. The file's own verdict: ADAPT — port the powered-exponential GP career-trajectory framework to NFL player aging/form curves (with opposition adjustment added); cricket-specific likelihood (not-out censoring, eye-in) does not transfer.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Likelihood: hazard H(x) = P(X=x|X≥x); effective average H(x) = 1/(μ(x)+1); μ(x) = μ₂ + (μ₁−μ₂)exp(−x/L). Between-innings: μ(x,t) = μ_{2t} + (μ₁−μ_{2t})exp(−x/L). GP prior: log{μ_{2t}} ~ GP(λ, K), K(tⱼ,t_k) = σ²·exp(−|j−k|^α/ℓ^α), α ∈ [1,2]. Context: μ(x,t) = [·] × ψ^{v_t} × φ^{i_t}, v_t ∈ {1,0,−1} home/neutral/away, i_t ∈ {1,−1} first/second innings. Priors: log σ ~ N(log 0.2, 1²) (restrictive anti-overfit: median player varies ~20% around λ over career); log ℓ ~ N(log 20, 1²); α ~ U(1,2). Fitting: C++ nested sampling (1000 particles × 1000 MCMC steps/iteration), marginal likelihood Z free for Bayes-factor comparison. Validation: LOOCV predicting each player's most recent out innings (913 players), MSE vs. simple moving averages.
## Data sources named
Statsguru (ESPNcricinfo): Test-match career scores of all 1,018 players who batted in a Test innings in the 21st century, 40,273 innings. Interactive trajectories: oliverstevenson.co.nz/cricket-visualisation/. Fitting code not released.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
LOOCV MSE: GP 544.0 vs SMA(10%) 633.1, SMA(25%) 588.4, SMA(50%) 608.2, SMA(100%) 589.1 — GP wins at all minimum-innings thresholds (649.6/616.8/785.8 at 10/20/50). SMA(10%) is worst of all: "predicting a player's ability purely on recent scores is unwise." Average log Bayes factor GP vs constant-ability: 1.4 (−152,071.8 vs −153,473.4); more innings → stronger GP preference. Top individuals: Kohli 6.9, Williamson 6.3, Warner 6.0, Sharma 5.8, Azam 4.4, Smith 3.0, Agarwal 0.9, Root 0.4, Labuschagne 0.2, Mathews −3.1 (constant model wins for Mathews). Williamson deep-dive: career avg 50.99; C=0.30, D=0.12, λ=56.6, σ=0.27, ℓ=36.7, α=1.50; ψ=1.11 (95% CI 0.93–1.32), ψ²=1.25 (0.87–1.75) home-vs-away; φ=1.03, φ²=1.07 (0.74–1.48) first-vs-second innings; career low ν=34.2, high 73.6, next-innings prediction 47.1 (95% CI 25.3–72.0). Form finding: σ posterior shifted from zero → long-term ability variation is real; but ℓ and α posteriors barely move from priors → data cannot distinguish smooth vs. ragged short-term trajectories. "Finding your feet" supported: most players' ability lowest early, peak after many innings (Williamson's peak estimated after his 60th innings). Rankings vs ICC (1 Dec 2020): GP #1 Smith 57.9 (47.9,68.8) vs ICC 911; Sharma GP #8 vs ICC #16 (ICC mishandles not-out-heavy careers); Stokes GP #17 vs ICC #8 (ICC overweighting recent 10 innings). Empirical regularities: batting averages ~17% higher at home than away; ~20% higher in team's first innings than second. Opposition strength NOT modeled (acknowledged gap).
