# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0747-conformal-predictive-portfolio-selection.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A paper proposing HR–LR portfolio selection: build conformal prediction intervals over candidate portfolios' future returns, filter to m "low-risk" candidates by lower bound r̲, then pick the max upper bound r̄ (highest upside), with the claim that uncertainty-aware selection beats return-maximizing baselines on 3 US and 3 Japanese assets. The file's own verdict: ADAPT — the uncertainty-aware slate-selection rule transfers to GSE's DFS lineup / posted-pick selection, but the as-written "lowest r̲ = low risk" orientation looks like a sign error and the empirical basis is thin (3 assets, figures only, no cost model).
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Interval per candidate w: [r̲(w), r̄(w)] from conformalized residuals of the return model (AR(3) or 100-hidden-unit feedforward NN, refit sequentially). Selection: S = argmax_{w ∈ M_m} r̄(w), where M_m = {m portfolios with smallest r̲}. Assumptions: exchangeability of residuals over time; candidate set covers the optimum; transaction costs ignored. Validation: sequential out-of-sample 2012–2018 (learn through 2011), cumulative return curves vs. baselines Mean[1], Mean[3], AR(1–3), Uniform.
## Data sources named
Apple, Microsoft, Amazon (US); Toyota, SoftBank, Keyence (Japan); monthly returns, main window 2009-01-01–2018-12-31 (paper inconsistently also says 2008–2019 — flagged). No code released; asset data public (Yahoo Finance-type).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Results are FIGURES ONLY (cumulative-return plots, Figures 1–2): Conformal (AR) and Conformal (NN) curves lie above Mean[1], Mean[3], AR(1–3), and Uniform for both US and Japan asset sets, avoiding sharp drawdowns. No exact cumulative-return values, Sharpe ratios, or significance tests are printed. No coverage level α stated for the experiments. Limitations flagged in file: only 3 assets per market (trivial diversification, untested at scale); transaction costs ignored (monthly rebalancing would eat the visual edge); the "low risk = lowest lower bound" ranking is conceptually odd — a lower r̲ means a WORSE worst case, so the rule as written picks the m portfolios with the worst worst-cases and maximizes upside among them (possible sign error in the paper; must be re-derived, not copied); exchangeability on financial returns is false.
