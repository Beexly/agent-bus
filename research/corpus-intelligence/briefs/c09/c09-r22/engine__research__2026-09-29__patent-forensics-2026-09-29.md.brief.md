## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- **Sportvision/FOX telestration family (6 patents, expired 2017–2019 on normal 20-year term):** commercial success + acquisition — the yellow first-down line won (Emmy, still live on NFL broadcasts per SMT's 2018 SBLII coverage), patents maintained diligently then aged out; SMT acquired 100% of Sportvision Oct 4 2016 (reported >$25M); new filings tapered after telestrator continuations through 2010 (US7492363B2/2005, US7750901B2/2009, US7928976B2/2010) consistent with lender-restructuring years (portfolio pledged as loan collateral 2004–2015); PVI's sensorless image-recognition approach (L-VIS) was never blocked — sensor-free implementations were always legal. Claims cover: human-drawn telestration smoothed into 3D field coords; auto-reveal via separate sensor + barrier geometry; two-camera triangulation; six-radar Doppler; color-sampling compositing so the line passes under players; remote-studio processing split. They do NOT cover: automatic drawing generation without a human hand, neural-segmentation/non-color compositing, radar-free CV speed measurement, all-at-the-venue processing. **An automatic, no-stadium-hardware, segmentation-based pipeline does not read on these claims.**
- **Sharp Labs football-video family (7 patents, all Expired — Fee Related 2022–2026, deliberately unpaid):** commercial orphaning — Sharp Labs of America (Camas, WA; 275 employees, 286 patents) built summarization ("a game to every play in about 45 minutes") to differentiate Sharp LCD TVs; founder Jon Clemens retired and formed Sharp Technology Ventures to license "languished" tech; sports work branded HiMpact Sports (3-hour game → 45 min, automatic indexing, random-access play-by-play), ESPN described as likely first licensee, no durable commercial traction after. Sharp's 2012–2016 collapse ($1.2B quarterly loss Aug 2012, ~$13B losses over four years, Foxconn 66% acquisition 2016) gutted the US lab (ex-employee review: "R&D discontinued"); lab-to-lapse causal link flagged unverified. Key methods claimed: vanishing-point start detection, 70% voting sliding window (US7639275), 5–6σ histogram scene-change end detection (US7474331), full-speed vs. slow-motion classification (US8018491), logo-transition replay detection (US7474698/US7653131). Claims locked to handcrafted color/frame-difference heuristics — do NOT cover learned/classifier boundaries, scoreboard/clock OCR, audio cues (whistle/crowd), broadcast metadata, or league data feeds. Sharp's own engineers concluded deterministic rules were "too difficult to write rules for that cover every situation," already moving to probabilistic (HMM) methods.
- **Object Prediction Technologies LLC US7609855B2** (NOT Sharp — corrected from earlier report; filed by Sada/Tsai/Meijome, granted Oct 27 2009, expired fee-related 2027): vanishing-point moving-object analysis.
- **Consolidated verdict:** neither family stopped because the problem was unsolvable — the field has since validated both architectures with modern tools (nflgsplat: classical geometric front end → deep refinement on consumer GPUs; soccer charting real-time on RTX 4060).
- **Two design-around mandates:** (1) registration/calibration fragility → image-based match-moving + neural segmentation, zero stadium hardware, continuous self-recalibration, never encoder telemetry; (2) single-cue brittleness → multimodal learned boundaries (OCR/clock, audio, data feeds, visual models), explicit uncertainty, no single broadcast cue as ground truth.
- Build recommendation: prototype G2's classical prefilter first, then G1's field-anchored telestration on real NFL frames — both marked UNTESTED — QUEUED FOR EVALUATION.
- Go-to-market caution: Sharp had working tech + an ESPN relationship and still failed to commercialize; GSE's use is internal capability (film pipeline → content + training data), which sidesteps the trap that killed HiMpact.
