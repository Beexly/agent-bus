# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0232-flexbart-flexible-bayesian-regression-trees-with.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A full-text read of flexBART (Deshpande 2023, arXiv:2211.04459v3), a Bayesian regression-tree method that replaces one-hot encoding of categorical predictors with a multi-level split prior (each level assigned to a branch with probability 1/2), plus network-structured split priors (gs1–gs4) for adjacency data; motivated and validated on MLB pitch framing and Philadelphia crime. The corpus reader's verdict is ADOPT: benchmark and sports results show the categorical-encoding choice is first-order, and the R package is open source.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
BART: y ~ N(f(x), σ²), f(x) = Σ_m g(x; T_m, D_m, μ_m); tree growth P(grow at depth d) = 0.95(1+d)^{−2}; leaf posterior μ_ℓ | … ~ N(P_ℓ^{−1}θ_ℓ, P_ℓ^{−1}), P_ℓ = σ^{−2}|I(ℓ)| + τ^{−2}, θ_ℓ = σ^{−2}Σ_{i∈I(ℓ)} r_i + τ^{−2}μ_0; closed-form marginal tree likelihood (eq B9); MH acceptance α = min{1, [p(T*,D*|…)q(T,D|T*,D*,…)] / [p(T,D|…)q(T*,D*|T,D,…)]}. Core result: one-hot encoding of a K-level categorical yields only 2^K − K reachable partitions of levels vs the Bell number (K=5: 27 of 52; K=10: 1,014 of 115,975, under 1%) — crippling partial pooling across groups of levels. flexBART decision rule: assign each available level to C independently with probability 1/2 (no one-hot). Network strategies: gs1 = deterministic Fiedler partition; gs2 = uniform spanning tree (Wilson's algorithm) + delete uniform edge; gs3 = spanning tree + delete edge with probability ∝ smallest resulting cluster size (balanced bias); gs4 = Fiedler partition of uniform spanning tree; defaults recommended: gs2 or gs3. Gibbs: Bayesian backfitting on partial residuals; suff_stat_map + running residuals for efficiency. Benchmarks vs one-hot BART, targetBART, oracleBART, ASE-embedded BART. Pitch framing uses probit link P(y=1) = Φ(f(x)). Synthetic: 4 DGPs × n ∈ {1,000, 5,000, 10,000}, 50 replications; 16 UCI/JAE/R benchmark datasets, 50 random 75/25 splits; pitch framing 10 random 90/10 splits/season; crime 100 random 75/25 splits.
## Data sources named
baseballr-scraped MLB pitch-by-pitch 2013–2019 (~350,000 called pitches/season; ~100 umpires, ~1,000 batters, ~100 catchers, ~700 pitchers/season); Philadelphia monthly crime density per census tract Jan 2006–Dec 2021 from opendataphilly.org (inverse hyperbolic sine transform); 16 public benchmark datasets (UCI: abalone, cpu, mpg, servo; JAE: Alcohol, amenity, Engel, spouse; R: ais, attend, cane, Caschool, fuelEcon, Insur, Medicare, strike). Code: https://github.com/skdeshpande91/flexBART.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Pitch framing (7 seasons): misclassification 8.6% (flexBART) vs 8.8% (targetBART) vs 18.4% (one-hot BART) vs 10.6% (Deshpande & Wyner 2017 hierarchical model); significance tests flexBART vs BART p = 4.5×10^{−26}, vs targetBART p = 2.7×10^{−22}. Synthetic (n = 5,000, MSE relative to BART): flexBART 18% lower (DGP1), 13% lower (DGP3), 20% lower (DGP4); worse on DGP2 (singleton-outlier partition — flexBART's balanced-partition bias bites). Benchmarks (SMSE, flexBART vs BART): cpu 0.154 vs 0.222; servo 0.153 vs 0.189; Insur 0.021 vs 0.297; Caschool 0.002 vs 0.005; attend 0.212 vs 0.262; cane 0.614 vs 0.691 — better on 12/16 datasets, significant on 10 after Bonferroni; runtimes 2–4× faster (Engel: 88.9s vs 253.4s). Network crime RMSE: gs2 1.21, gs3 1.21 vs BART 1.32, targetBART 1.33, gs1 1.93, BART_ase1 4.05 (SD of y ≈ 4.65). Synthetic network piecewise-constant RMSE: gs2 3.708 vs BART 39.036. Runtime pitch framing: flexBART 48 min vs BART 2 hours per 2,000 MCMC samples.
