## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- NFL Access Pass (opened 2023, "tight parameters") licenses Kollmann/O'Sullivan/Benkert footage — their survival is not proof an unaffiliated account faces the same enforcement; enforcement history: NFL objected to 20 seconds of game footage (2007 Seltzer case); 3,000+ YouTube clips removed in 2006.
- No safe "under X seconds" rule; fair-use frame = footage subordinate to analysis, new purpose (teaching), minimum necessary, attributed.
- PFF §1.9–1.10 explicitly permits monetized creators to reference/quote/display individual grades, stats, or free-article excerpts with attribution + link, non-bulk, non-substituting; paid content, API data, AI output excluded.
- Facts/game results are not copyrightable (NBA v. Motorola); The Athletic/NYT has no share-with-credit provision (permission-first); ESPN/Disney terms bar reproduction for commercial use; X ToS licenses X, not third-party re-uploads; US Copyright Office: no word/percent safe harbor, "heart of the work" doctrine (Harper & Row).
- Zero-cost options: YouTube embeds (licensed via the service, player intact — not for cropping into X videos); Getty Embed (70M+ photos, noncommercial editorial only, own player); Wikimedia Commons NFL game photos (verify per-file license, e.g. CC BY-SA); quote official @NFL/team posts rather than downloading video; GIPHY embed of official NFL GIFs (2016 NFL–GIPHY agreement covered ancillary, not gameplay, GIFs).
- NFL+ "NFL Pro" offers All-22 coaches film back to 2022 (viewing only, no re-upload rights); no official fan clip-sharing tool exists (Replay Re-Cutter discontinued); pre-1964 public-domain path requires per-item renewal search — archive.org hosting proves nothing.
