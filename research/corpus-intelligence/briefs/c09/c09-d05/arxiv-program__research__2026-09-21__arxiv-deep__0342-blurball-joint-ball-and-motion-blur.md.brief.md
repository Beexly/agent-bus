# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0342-blurball-joint-ball-and-motion-blur.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
Deep-read of arXiv:2509.18387v3: BlurBall, a table-tennis ball tracker that jointly estimates ball position AND its motion-blur vector (orientation θ, half-length l), showing blur vectors improve downstream trajectory fitting by ~37%. The file's ledger verdict is **ADAPT** — the midpoint-plus-blur-vector label schema and blur-aware trajectory fitting are portable to NFL ball tracking (passes, punts, kicks), but the detector must be rebuilt for football geometry and broadcast cameras.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
Label schema: ball at midpoint p_b of blur streak plus orientation θ and half-length l; streak endpoints p_{1,2} = p_b ± (l cosθ, l sinθ). Detector: WASB (HRNet backbone) with squeeze-and-excitation attention; heatmap trained to cover full line blur; quality focal loss; at inference PCA of thresholded blob → blur angle, projected extent → half-length. Training: 30 epochs, Adam, batch 8, 288×512 input, d=2.5, cmin=0.7, hard-label self-mining from epoch 20, RTX 2080 Ti. Trajectory fitting: quadratic fit on first three observations; compare position-only vs position+blur inputs.
## Data sources named
New dataset: 26 online table-tennis recordings, 64,119 frames (train 51,423 / test 12,696, game-disjoint); blur present in 62% of frames; max blur half-length 73 px; 95 manually segmented trajectories for fitting. Project/data: cogsys-tuebingen.github.io/blurball/ with camera calibration.
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
Midpoint labels improved every detector: WASB steps=1 F1 front-point 95.58 → midpoint 96.00. BlurBall SE one-step: 96.52 F1, 93.47 accuracy, 98.23 AP, length MAE 1.5±1.2 px, angle MAE 6.5±18.9°. At δ=0.7: 97.17 F1, 94.75 accuracy, 97.34 AP. Trajectory fitting (95 trajectories): position-only MAE 84.4±136.6 px vs position+blur 53.0±87.1 px; median 28.4 vs 19.9 px; upper whisker 224.6 vs 140.2 px — ~37% mean-MAE reduction. Stated limits: straight-blur assumption fails at bounce; dynamic cameras increase misses; false positives on white blobs; angle MAE has huge variance (6.5±18.9°) — blur orientation unreliable when blur is short.
## Intelligence connections (tag each: QB-BEHAVIOR, COACHING, OL, TRUST-SIGNAL, SCHEME, OTHER)
- OTHER: upstream perception capability for GSE's tracking lane — blur-aware ball detection complements NGS downstream metrics (air distance, completion probability, catch-point models). Best use case: first frames after pass release where blur is worst and velocity inference matters most for completion-prob/air-yard models. Note: the bounce/deflection failure point (straight-blur assumption) maps to NFL catch/tip/deflection frames — exactly where completion-probability models are most uncertain; a discontinuity-aware piecewise trajectory model is the proposed improvement.
## Engine-actionable? (yes/no + one-line what)
Yes — build a GSE football blur dataset (500+ pass/punt/kick broadcast clips with midpoint+θ+l labels, incl. bounces/tips/occlusions), retrain the detector with camera-motion compensation (homography global-motion subtraction), and run the position+blur vs position-only quadratic-fit test against NGS ball tracking as ground truth (adopt gate: ≥2 F1 gain + ≥25% catch-point MAE reduction; gate usage on blur length not angle when blur is short); est. 5–7 engineer-weeks.
