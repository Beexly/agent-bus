# arxiv-program/research/2026-09-21/arxiv-deep/0513-finegrained-activity-recognition-in-baseball-videos.md
## What it is (1-2 sentences)
A computer-vision benchmark paper (Piergiovanni & Ryoo, 2018, arXiv:1804.03247v1) introducing the MLB-YouTube dataset (20 games of 2017 MLB postseason broadcast footage) and comparing temporal feature-aggregation methods — learned sub-event Gaussian filters and super-event Cauchy filters winning over LSTM, temporal convolution, and pooling — for fine-grained baseball activity recognition (swing, pitch type, pitch speed, etc.). The worker ledger marked it REJECT: GSE operates no video pipeline and the methods have no transfer path to NFL betting, DFS, or tracking-data modeling.
## Key metrics/methods (formulas where given, else "not specified")
- Base CNNs: I3D (pretrained ImageNet + Kinetics) and two-stream InceptionV3; frames at 25 fps, TV-L1 optical flow clipped to [−20, 20]; InceptionV3 features every 3 frames (8 fps), I3D temporal stride 8 (3 fps); pose features via OpenPose heatmaps for pitch-type classification.
- Temporal aggregation compared: (a) temporal max/mean pooling; (b) temporal pyramid pooling (intervals 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, max-pooled and concatenated → K×D); (c) temporal convolution (L×1 kernels) + max-pool; (d) learned sub-events: N strided Gaussians with learned center g, width σ, stride δ; (e) bi-directional LSTM (512 hidden units, last hidden state to classifier).
- Sub-event filter equations (stated faithfully): g_n = 0.5·T·(g̃_n+1); δ_n = (T/(N−1))·δ̃_n; μ_n^i = g_n + (i − 0.5N + 0.5)·δ_n (Eq. 1). Filters: F_m[i,t] = (1/Z_m)·exp(−(t−μ_m^i)²/(2σ_m²)), i∈{0..N−1}, t∈{0..T−1} (Eq. 2).
- Losses: segmented L(v) = Σ_c [z_c·log p(c|G(v)) + (1−z_c)·log(1−p(c|G(v)))] (Eq. 3); continuous L(v) = Σ_{t,c} [z_{t,c}·log p(c|H(v_t)) + (1−z_{t,c})·log(1−p(c|H(v_t)))] (Eq. 4).
- Super-event filters: x̂_n = (T−1)(tanh(x_n)+1)/2; γ̂_n = exp(1−2|tanh(γ_n)|); F[t,n] = 1/[Z_n·π·γ̂_n·((t−x̂_n)/γ̂_n)²] (Eq. 5). S_c = Σ_m A_{c,m}·Σ_t F_m[t]·v_t (Eq. 6).
- Training: PyTorch, Adam, lr 0.01 decayed ×0.1 every 10 epochs, 50 epochs; multi-label BCE (segmented), per-frame BCE (continuous).
## Data sources named
- MLB-YouTube (new dataset introduced by the paper): 20 games from the 2017 MLB postseason sourced from YouTube, 42+ hours of footage; segmented set: 4,290 multi-label annotated clips (activity counts: No Activity 2,983; Ball 1,434; Strike 1,799; Swing 2,506; Hit 1,391; Foul 718; In Play 679; Bunt 24; Hit by Pitch 14); pitch clips labeled with pitch type (fastball, sinker, curveball, changeup, slider, knuckle-curve) and pitch speed; continuous set: 2,128 clips of 1–2 minutes, per-frame annotated, averaging 7.2 activities each → 15,000+ activity instances.
- Code/dataset/models stated as available at https://github.com/piergiaj/mlb-youtube/ (not verified by the original worker).
## Findings (numbers and facts, not vibes)
- Pitch/non-pitch binary accuracy (Table 2): InceptionV3 97.46/98.44/98.67 (RGB/Flow/Two-stream); +sub-events 98.67/98.73/99.36; I3D 98.64/98.88/98.70; I3D+sub-events 98.42/98.35/98.65 — task near-saturated.
- Multi-label mAP (Table 4, two-stream): InceptionV3+mean-pool 45.3; max-pool 54.4; pyramid 55.3; LSTM 57.7; temporal conv 56.1; sub-events 62.6 (best). I3D: 52.7/57.2/58.7/53.1/58.4/61.3 (sub-events best).
- Parameter cost (Table 3, added params): max/mean pool 16K; pyramid 115K; LSTM 10.5M; temporal conv 31.5M; sub-events 36K — paper's interpretation: LSTMs/temporal conv overfit and are sequential/non-parallelizable; sub-events win with far fewer parameters.
- Per-class AP (Table 5, two-stream): sub-events especially help strike, hit, foul, hit-by-pitch (e.g., InceptionV3 foul 40.3→60.7; hit-by-pitch 15.7→29.2). Bunt remains poor (10.2→12.4; only 24 examples).
- Pitch speed RMSE (Table 6): best = InceptionV3+sub-events at 60 fps, 3.6 mph (I3D+sub-events 3.9; I3D 4.3; InceptionV3 5.3); 8/3 fps features gave only 1–2 pitch frames — insufficient, requiring 60 fps re-extraction.
- Pitch type accuracy (Table 7): I3D+sub-events 34.5%; pose+sub-events 36.4% (best); random 17.0%; fastball easiest (68%), slider 45%, sinker hardest (12%). LSTM underperforms baseline (18.5% vs 25.8%) — attributed to overfitting.
- Continuous per-frame mAP (Table 8, two-stream): best = sub+super-events: I3D 40.4, InceptionV3 40.9 (vs I3D baseline 34.2 / InceptionV3 baseline 31.9).
- Limitations: train/test split protocol unstated — clips from the same 20 games could leak across splits; Bunt (24) and hit-by-pitch (14) sample sizes trivial; no ablations of N (number of filters) or initialization sensitivity; paper is a 2018 dataset+benchmark contribution, now dated vs modern video architectures.
